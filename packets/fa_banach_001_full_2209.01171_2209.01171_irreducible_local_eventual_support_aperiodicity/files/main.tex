\begin{abstract}
Gl\"uck asked whether the local irreducible version of his aperiodicity
theorem remains true when one-step support growth is replaced, separately for
each positive function, by growth at some pair of consecutive iterates.  We
give an affirmative answer.  If $Tz=\lambda z$ with $|\lambda|=1$, then
$e=|z|$ is a strictly positive fixed vector.  Normalizing by $e$ produces a
unital positive map $R$ on $L^\infty$ and a unitary $u=z/e$ satisfying
$Ru=\lambda u$.  The multiplicative-domain identity forces narrow phase
slices of $u$ to move into disjoint consecutive phase slices when
$\lambda\ne1$.  A nonzero slice inside the prescribed local set therefore
violates every possible consecutive support inclusion.
\end{abstract}

\section{The source question and result}

Let $(\Omega,\mu)$ be a $\sigma$-finite measure space, let
$1\le p<\infty$, and let $T:L^p(\Omega,\mu)\to L^p(\Omega,\mu)$ be positive,
power bounded, and irreducible.  The source proves that if there is a
measurable $S\subseteq\Omega$ of positive measure such that
\[
 \suppop(Tf)\supseteq\suppop(f)
 \quad\text{for every }f\ge0\text{ with }\suppop(f)\subseteq S,
\]
then $\spoint(T)\cap\Torus\subseteq\{1\}$.  It asks whether the same conclusion
follows from the apparently weaker condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eventual}
 \text{for every such }f\text{ there is an }n=n(f)\ge1\text{ such that}
 \quad
 \suppop(T^nf)\supseteq\suppop(T^{n-1}f).
\end{equation}
All support statements are modulo null sets.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Under the assumptions above, condition \eqref{eq:eventual} implies
\[
 \spoint(T)\cap\Torus\subseteq\{1\}.
\]
Thus the source question has an affirmative answer, with the integer $n$
allowed to depend on $f$ exactly as in Theorem~1.1 of the source.
\end{theorem}

\section{Idea of the proof}

A unimodular eigenvector has a pointwise phase.  Irreducibility and power
boundedness first force its modulus $e$ to be a strictly positive fixed
vector.  After dividing by $e$, the operator becomes a unital positive map
$R$ on $L^\infty$, while the phase $u=z/e$ is a unitary eigenvector of $R$.
Equality in the unital Schwarz inequality then makes $u$ multiplicative for
$R$: a function localized to one phase arc is carried to the corresponding
$\lambda$-rotated phase arc.

If $\lambda\ne1$, take an arc narrow enough to be disjoint from its rotation
and choose a nonzero piece of that phase arc inside $S$.  All consecutive
iterates of this positive piece lie in disjoint rotated arcs.  A strictly
positive fixed dual functional prevents any iterate from vanishing, so none
of the proposed support inclusions can ever occur.

\section{Two elementary reductions}

We first record why a peripheral eigenvector supplies the order unit needed
below.  The argument is included because it also guarantees that none of the
phase slices can die under iteration.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:fixed-modulus}
Suppose $Tz=\lambda z$ for a nonzero $z\in L^p$, where
$|\lambda|=1$.  Then $e:=|z|$ is a fixed vector of $T$, is strictly positive
almost everywhere, and there is a strictly positive functional
$\varphi\in(L^p)'_+$ with $T'\varphi=\varphi$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Positivity gives $Te\ge |Tz|=e$.  Hence $(T^ne)_{n\ge0}$ is increasing.
It is norm bounded because $T$ is power bounded.  The space $L^p$ is a
KB-space for $1\le p<\infty$, so $T^ne$ converges in norm to some $y\ge e$;
continuity gives $Ty=y$.

Choose $\psi\in(L^p)'_+$ with $\langle\psi,y\rangle>0$.  The Ces\`aro means
\[
 \varphi_N=\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(T')^k\psi
\]
are norm bounded.  A weak-* cluster point $\varphi$ is positive,
$T'\varphi=\varphi$, and
$\langle\varphi,y\rangle=\langle\psi,y\rangle>0$.  Its absolute kernel
\[
 I_\varphi=\{x\in L^p:\langle\varphi,|x|\rangle=0\}
\]
is a closed ideal and is $T$-invariant, since
$|Tx|\le T|x|$.  Irreducibility forces $I_\varphi=\{0\}$, so $\varphi$ is
strictly positive.  Finally,
\[
 0\le Te-e,
 \qquad
 \langle\varphi,Te-e\rangle=0,
\]
and strict positivity imply $Te=e$.

The closed ideal generated by $e$ is nonzero and $T$-invariant: if
$|x|\le ce$, then $|Tx|\le cTe=ce$, and the same remains true after taking
the closure.  Irreducibility therefore makes this ideal all of $L^p$.
Equivalently, $e>0$ almost everywhere.
\end{proof}

We will also use the following standard multiplicative-domain fact.

\begin{lemma}[unitary multiplicative domain]\label{lem:md}
Let $R:L^\infty(\Omega,\mu)\to L^\infty(\Omega,\mu)$ be positive and
unital.  If $u\in L^\infty$ satisfies $|u|=1$ and $Ru=\lambda u$ for some
$|\lambda|=1$, then, for every $h\in L^\infty$ and every continuous
$b:\Torus\to\mathbb C$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:md}
 R\bigl(b(u)h\bigr)=b(\lambda u)R(h).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A positive map from a commutative $C^*$-algebra is completely positive.
Moreover,
\[
 R(u^*u)=1=(Ru)^*Ru,
 \qquad
 R(uu^*)=1=(Ru)(Ru)^*.
\]
Thus $u$ belongs to the multiplicative domain of $R$.  Consequently $R$ is
multiplicative on $C^*(u)$ and is a $C^*(u)$-bimodule map.  Approximation of
$b$ by trigonometric polynomials gives \eqref{eq:md}.
\end{proof}

\section{Phase-slice obstruction}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Suppose for a contradiction that $Tz=\lambda z$ for some nonzero $z$ and
$\lambda\in\Torus\setminus\{1\}$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-modulus},
$e=|z|$ is fixed and is positive almost everywhere.  Put
\[
 u=\frac{z}{e}\in L^\infty;
 \qquad |u|=1\quad\text{almost everywhere}.
\]
On the principal order ideal generated by $e$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R}
 Rg=\frac{T(eg)}{e},\qquad g\in L^\infty.
\end{equation}
This is well-defined because
$|T(eg)|\le\|g\|_\infty Te=\|g\|_\infty e$.  It is a positive unital
contraction on $L^\infty$, and $Ru=\lambda u$.

Because $\lambda\ne1$, choose a nonnegative continuous function
$b:\Torus\to[0,1]$ with nonempty positivity set such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disjoint-bump}
 \{b>0\}\cap\lambda^{-1}\{b>0\}=\varnothing.
\end{equation}
We may choose $b$ so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-slice}
 A:=S\cap\{b(u)>0\}
 \quad\text{has positive measure}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, cover the circle by finitely many arcs whose closures are disjoint
from their $\lambda$-rotates, choose continuous bumps positive on smaller
arcs which still cover the circle, and use $\mu(S)>0$.

Let $q=\mathbf1_S b(u)$ and $f=eq$.  Then $0\le f\in L^p$,
$\suppop(f)\subseteq S$, and \eqref{eq:positive-slice} together with
$e>0$ almost everywhere gives $f\ne0$.  Repeated use of
Lemma~\ref{lem:md} yields, for every $n\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iterate}
 R^nq=b(\lambda^n u)R^n\mathbf1_S,
 \qquad
 T^nf=eR^nq.
\end{equation}
It follows from \eqref{eq:disjoint-bump} that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disjoint-supports}
 \suppop(T^nf)\cap\suppop(T^{n-1}f)=\varnothing
 \qquad(n\ge1).
\end{equation}
On the other hand, the strictly positive fixed functional from
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-modulus} gives
\[
 \langle\varphi,T^nf\rangle=\langle\varphi,f\rangle>0
 \qquad(n\ge0),
\]
so every $T^nf$ is nonzero.  Hence neither of the two nonzero consecutive
supports in \eqref{eq:disjoint-supports} can contain the other.  This
contradicts \eqref{eq:eventual} for the single function $f$ constructed
above.  Therefore no $\lambda\in\Torus\setminus\{1\}$ can be an eigenvalue.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proof explains why allowing $n$ to depend on $f$ causes no loss in the
irreducible theorem.  A nontrivial peripheral eigenvalue creates a measurable
phase coordinate $u$.  Once a test function is confined to a sufficiently
narrow phase arc, every iterate stays in the corresponding rotated arc, so
the obstruction recurs at every time rather than only at time one.
\end{remark}

\section*{Verification notes}

The only $L^p$-specific inputs are the KB property for $p<\infty$, the
identification of the principal order ideal generated by a strictly positive
$e$ with $L^\infty$ via $g\mapsto eg$, and the equivalence between
nonvanishing sets and supports modulo null sets.  The multiplicative-domain
calculation is performed entirely in the commutative $C^*$-algebra
$L^\infty$.  The source question page and a separate audit accompany this
packet.
