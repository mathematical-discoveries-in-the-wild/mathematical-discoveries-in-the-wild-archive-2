\begin{abstract}
Hyt\"onen asks whether the finite-type model boundary $\partial\Omega_k$
from Chang--Li--Ottazzi--Wu satisfies the complete metric
$(1,4)$-Poincar\'e assumption.  The answer is yes.  Its horizontal fields are
homogeneous H\"ormander fields, so a global theorem of
Biagi--Bonfiglioli--Bramanti gives the stronger $(1,1)$ inequality with one
constant for all control balls.  We verify the homogeneity and rank
hypotheses, pass from smooth horizontal gradients to the lower pointwise
slope of arbitrary Lipschitz functions, and prove completeness.  Hence the
source theorem applies without the previously imposed $C^2$ assumption.
\end{abstract}

\section{Geometry and precise answer}

Identify $\Om$ with $\R^3$ in coordinates $(x,y,t)$, and put
$r_0^2=x^2+y^2$.  The CR horizontal fields are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fields}
 X_1=\partial_x+y r_0^{2k-2}\partial_t,
 \qquad
 X_2=\partial_y-x r_0^{2k-2}\partial_t,
 \qquad k\ge2.
\end{equation}
Let $\dcc$ be their Carnot--Carath\'eodory distance, let $\mu=dx\,dy\,dt$,
and write $B(p,r)=B_{\dcc}(p,r)$.  For a $\dcc$-Lipschitz function define,
exactly as in the source,
\[
 \lipf(p)=\liminf_{s\downarrow0}
 \sup_{\dcc(p,q)\le s}\frac{|f(p)-f(q)|}{s}.
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every integer $k\ge2$, $(\Om,\dcc,\mu)$ is a complete doubling metric
measure space and there is a constant $C_k$ such that every
$\dcc$-Lipschitz $f$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p4}
 \fintB_{B(p,r)}|f-f_{B(p,r)}|\,d\mu
 \le C_k r
 \left(\fintB_{B(p,2r)}(\lipf)^4\,d\mu\right)^{1/4}
\end{equation}
for all $p\in\Om$ and $r>0$.  In fact the stronger $(1,1)$ inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p1}
 \fintB_{B(p,r)}|f-f_{B(p,r)}|\,d\mu
 \le C_k' r\fintB_{B(p,2r)}\lipf\,d\mu
\end{equation}
holds.
\end{theorem}

This is precisely the Poincar\'e assumption in the question, with lower
dimension $d=4$ and dilation factor $\lambda=2$.

\begin{remark}[Two distances in the cited paper]\label{rem:metric}
Chang--Li--Ottazzi--Wu also denote by $d$ the quantity
$h(p,q)^2\rho(p,q)^{2-2k}$.  Their later identity is
\[
 d(p,q)\asymp \mu\bigl(B_{\dcc}(p,\dcc(p,q))\bigr),
\]
so this $d$ is a volume gauge.  The length metric having lower dimension
$4$ and upper dimension $2k+2$ is $\dcc$.  Indeed their ball estimate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:volume}
 \mu(B((z,t),r))\asymp r^4(|z|+r)^{2k-2}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for $0<r\le R$,
\[
 (R/r)^4\lesssim\frac{\mu(B(p,R))}{\mu(B(p,r))}
 \lesssim(R/r)^{2k+2}.
\]
This also explains exactly the dimensions quoted in the open problem
\cite{CLOW}.
\end{remark}

\section{\texorpdfstring{\hspace{0.2em}}{}The global inequality}

Consider the anisotropic dilations
\[
 \delta_s(x,y,t)=(sx,sy,s^{2k}t).
\]
Both fields in \eqref{eq:fields} are homogeneous of degree one:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hom}
 X_j(g\circ\delta_s)=s(X_jg)\circ\delta_s,
 \qquad j=1,2.
\end{equation}
They are linearly independent as vector fields, since their $\partial_x$ and
$\partial_y$ components are distinct.  They also satisfy H\"ormander's rank
condition at the origin.  Directly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bracket}
 [X_1,X_2]=-2k(x^2+y^2)^{k-1}\partial_t.
\end{equation}
Since the coefficient in \eqref{eq:bracket} is independent of $t$,
bracketing another $2k-2$ times with $X_1$ simply differentiates that
coefficient in $x$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminal}
 (\ad X_1)^{2k-2}[X_1,X_2](0)
 =-2k(2k-2)!\,\partial_t.
\end{equation}
Together with $X_1(0)=\partial_x$ and $X_2(0)=\partial_y$, this spans
$T_0\R^3$.

Theorem~7.6 of Biagi--Bonfiglioli--Bramanti states that every family of
degree-one homogeneous fields satisfying this rank condition obeys the
global estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bbb}
 \fintB_{B(p,r)}|u-u_{B(p,r)}|\,d\mu
 \le C r\fintB_{B(p,2r)}|Xu|\,d\mu,
 \qquad |Xu|=(|X_1u|^2+|X_2u|^2)^{1/2},
\end{equation}
for every $p\in\R^3$, every $r>0$, and every $C^1$ function near
$B(p,2r)$ \cite{BBB}.  The point of that theorem is global uniformity: its
constant is independent of both $p$ and $r$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:hom}--\eqref{eq:terminal} verify all of its hypotheses.

\section{Lipschitz slope and completeness}

It remains to match \eqref{eq:bbb}, stated first for smooth functions, with
the lower metric slope in the question.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:slope}
If $f$ is $\dcc$-Lipschitz, then its horizontal weak derivatives exist and
\[
 |X_jf|\le\lipf\quad\text{a.e.},\qquad j=1,2.
\]
Consequently $|Xf|\le\sqrt2\,\lipf$ almost everywhere.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Phi_j^s$ be the flow of $X_j$.  It is a horizontal curve of speed one,
so
\[
 \dcc(p,\Phi_j^s(p))\le |s|.
\]
Thus $s\mapsto f(\Phi_j^s(p))$ is Lipschitz.  The one-dimensional
differentiation theorem in flow boxes, followed by Fubini, gives $X_jf$ for
almost every $p$.  At every such point,
\begin{align*}
 |X_jf(p)|
 &=\lim_{s\downarrow0}
   \frac{|f(\Phi_j^s(p))-f(p)|}{s}\\
 &\le\liminf_{s\downarrow0}
   \sup_{\dcc(p,q)\le s}\frac{|f(q)-f(p)|}{s}
 =\lipf(p).
\end{align*}
The final assertion follows by taking the Euclidean norm of the two
horizontal derivatives.
\end{proof}

Control-metric Lipschitz functions belong locally to $W_X^{1,\infty}$.
Standard smooth density for H\"ormander Sobolev spaces allows
\eqref{eq:bbb} to pass to them in $W_X^{1,1}$ on each doubled ball
\cite{BBdens}.  Lemma~\ref{lem:slope} therefore gives \eqref{eq:p1}.  Finally,
H\"older's inequality on the probability space
$(B(p,2r),\mu/\mu(B(p,2r)))$ gives \eqref{eq:p4}.

For completeness, let $\gamma$ be a horizontal curve of length $L$ starting
at $(z_0,t_0)$, parametrized with control speed at most one.  From
\eqref{eq:fields},
\[
 |z(s)-z_0|\le L,
 \qquad
 |t(s)-t_0|\le L(|z_0|+L)^{2k-1}.
\]
Thus every bounded $\dcc$-ball is Euclidean bounded.  The ball-box theorem
for bracket-generating smooth fields says that the $\dcc$ and Euclidean
topologies agree; since $\dcc$ is continuous, closed bounded control balls
are Euclidean compact.  Hence $(\R^3,\dcc)$ is proper, and therefore
complete.  This finishes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Consequence and verification boundary}

The answer is stronger than requested: no exponent-specific argument is
needed, since $(1,1)$ implies $(1,p)$ for every $p\ge1$.  Combined with the
source theorem, the positive answer removes the a priori
$b\in C^2(\Om)$ condition in the cited low-index Schatten result.

A bounded literature search on August 17, 2026 covered the exact question,
the three relevant arXiv identifiers, the model fields, and combinations of
``global Poincar\'e'', ``homogeneous H\"ormander'', and
$\partial\Omega_k$.  It found the global theorem used above but no later
paper explicitly answering the source question.  The computation of
\eqref{eq:bracket}--\eqref{eq:terminal} and the homogeneity check were also
verified symbolically for $k=2,\ldots,8$.  The main human-review point is the
notation disambiguation in Remark~\ref{rem:metric}; the dimension statement
in the question and CLOW's own volume identity both select $\dcc$.
