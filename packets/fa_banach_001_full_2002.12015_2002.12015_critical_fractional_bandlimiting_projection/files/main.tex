\section{Source question}

Monguzzi, Peloso and Salvatori introduce fractional Paley--Wiener and
Bernstein spaces and prove boundedness of the Hilbert bandlimiting projection
away from the critical indices. In Section 8 they ask whether the projection
is bounded when
\[
  s-\frac1p\in\N_0,
\]
and suggest defining the critical space through the homogeneous Sobolev class
modulo polynomials \cite{MPSPW}. The displayed question is reproduced below
from page 23 of the arXiv PDF.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.97\linewidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The source also stresses a genuine issue: at a critical index there is no
dilation-homogeneous concrete realization. There is nevertheless a canonical
homogeneous \emph{quotient}, and the BMO/Taylor conditions of
\cite{MPSReal} select a concrete representative. The theorem below treats
both descriptions and keeps their normalizations separate.

\section{Definitions and statement}

Fix $a,s>0$ and $1<p<\infty$, and suppose
\[
  m=s-\frac1p\in\N_0.
\]
Let $\dot W^{s,p}$ be the completion of $\cS(\R)$ for the norm
$\|\Delta^{s/2}f\|_p$, viewed in $\cS'/\cP_m$. Let $E^{s,p}$ be the
critical realization from \cite[Theorem 3(iii)]{MPSReal}: for $m=0$ it
is a fixed-mean representative in $\BMO$, and for $m\geq1$ it is the
corresponding Taylor/Sobolev--$\BMO$ representative. Write
\[
  U_{s,p}:E^{s,p}\longrightarrow L^p(\R),\qquad
  U_{s,p}f=\Delta^{s/2}f.
\]
The realization theorem and the definition of $\dot W^{s,p}$ make $U_{s,p}$
a surjective isometry; a short proof is included below.

Let $Q_a$ denote the interval Fourier projection
\[
  Q_a g=\cF^{-1}\big(\mathbf1_{[-a,a]}\widehat g\big).
\]
It is interpreted as the standard $L^p$ Fourier multiplier, not as an
undefined product of an arbitrary distribution by a discontinuous function.
In one dimension $Q_a$ is a difference of modulated Riesz projections, hence
is bounded for $1<p<\infty$.

Define the critical bandlimited realization
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal B}^{s,p}_{a,\crit}
 =\big\{f\in E^{s,p}:\supp\widehat f\subseteq[-a,a]\big\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Critical projection theorem]\label{thm:main}
For every $a,s>0$ and $1<p<\infty$ with $s-1/p\in\N_0$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every element of $\widetilde{\mathcal B}^{s,p}_{a,\crit}$ is the
restriction, in the sense of tempered distributions, of an entire function of
exponential type at most $a$.
\item $U_{s,p}$ restricts to a surjective isometry
\[
 U_{s,p}:\widetilde{\mathcal B}^{s,p}_{a,\crit}
 \longrightarrow \mathcal B_a^p,
\]
where $\mathcal B_a^p$ is the classical $L^p$ Bernstein space.
\item The operator
\[
 \Pi_{a,s,p}=U_{s,p}^{-1}Q_aU_{s,p}
\]
is a bounded projection of $E^{s,p}$ onto
$\widetilde{\mathcal B}^{s,p}_{a,\crit}$, and
\[
 \|\Pi_{a,s,p}\|_{E^{s,p}\to E^{s,p}}
 =\|Q_a\|_{L^p\to L^p}.
\]
\item If $p=2$, then $\Pi_{a,s,2}$ is the Hilbert-space orthogonal
projection. On the common dense domain used in \cite{MPSPW}, the operator for
general $p$ agrees with the source projection modulo $\cP_m$; it agrees
exactly after either the critical realization normalization or the
Taylor normalization is imposed.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the boundedness question in Section 8 of \cite{MPSPW} has an
affirmative answer for the canonical critical quotient and for its critical
BMO realization.
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof intuition}

The fractional Laplacian is the correct coordinate map. On the homogeneous
Sobolev quotient it is an isometric isomorphism onto $L^p$. Bandlimiting after
this change of coordinates is simply the familiar interval multiplier
$Q_a$. Conjugating $Q_a$ therefore gives a bounded idempotent immediately.

Two checks turn this operator-theoretic observation into an answer about
entire functions. First, if $\Delta^{s/2}f$ is bandlimited, then
$\widehat f$ vanishes outside the same interval: away from frequency zero one
may divide test functions by $|\xi|^s$. The possible ambiguity at zero is only
a polynomial, whose Fourier transform is supported at zero. Second, a
tempered distribution with compactly supported Fourier transform is the
restriction of an entire function of the corresponding exponential type by
Paley--Wiener--Schwartz. Thus no borderline Sobolev embedding estimate is
needed.

The critical BMO normalization and a Taylor normalization generally select
different polynomial representatives. Subtracting the Taylor polynomial only
\emph{after} bandlimiting gives an isometric identification between them and
avoids imposing two incompatible normalizations at once.

\section{Proof}

\subsection{The fractional Laplacian is onto}

Let $\cS_\infty$ be the Schwartz functions all of whose moments vanish.
It is dense in $L^p$ for $1<p<\infty$ \cite{MPSReal}. For
$g\in\cS_\infty$, the homogeneous Riesz potential
\[
 I_sg=\cF^{-1}(|\xi|^{-s}\widehat g)
\]
again lies in $\cS_\infty$, and
$\Delta^{s/2}I_sg=g$. Given $h\in L^p$, choose
$g_n\in\cS_\infty$ with $g_n\to h$ in $L^p$. Then $I_sg_n$ is Cauchy in
the defining norm of $\dot W^{s,p}$ and determines a class $[f]$ satisfying
\[
 \Delta^{s/2}[f]=h,\qquad \|[f]\|_{\dot W^{s,p}}=\|h\|_p.
\]
The realization map of \cite{MPSReal} selects the unique representative
$f\in E^{s,p}$ without changing the norm. Hence $U_{s,p}$ is a surjective
isometry.

\subsection{The support lemma}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:support}
For $f\in E^{s,p}$,
\[
 \supp\widehat{U_{s,p}f}\subseteq[-a,a]
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \supp\widehat f\subseteq[-a,a].
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The distributional fractional-Laplacian identity can be tested without any
ambiguity on functions whose Fourier support avoids zero. Suppose first that
$g=U_{s,p}f$ and $\supp\widehat g\subseteq[-a,a]$. If
$\psi\in C_c^\infty(\R\setminus[-a,a])$, then
$\theta(\xi)=|\xi|^{-s}\psi(\xi)$ is again a smooth compactly supported test
function. Therefore
\[
 \langle\widehat f,\psi\rangle
 =\langle\widehat f,|\xi|^s\theta\rangle
 =\langle\widehat g,\theta\rangle=0.
\]
This proves $\supp\widehat f\subseteq[-a,a]$.

Conversely, take a test function $\psi$ supported outside $[-a,a]$.
Multiplication or division by $|\xi|^s$ is smooth on its support, so the same
identity, in the opposite direction, gives
$\langle\widehat g,\psi\rangle=0$. Notice that changing $f$ by a polynomial
adds only derivatives of $\delta_0$ to $\widehat f$ and therefore cannot
affect either conclusion.
\end{proof}

If $f\in\widetilde{\mathcal B}^{s,p}_{a,\crit}$, Lemma
\ref{lem:support} says that $U_{s,p}f$ is an $L^p$ function with Fourier
support in $[-a,a]$. The classical Bernstein-space characterization makes it
an element of $\mathcal B_a^p$. Conversely, if $g\in\mathcal B_a^p$, let
$f=U_{s,p}^{-1}g$. Lemma \ref{lem:support} gives
$\supp\widehat f\subseteq[-a,a]$. Since $f\in\cS'$ and its Fourier
transform is compactly supported, the Paley--Wiener--Schwartz theorem gives
an entire function $F$ satisfying
\[
 |F(z)|\leq C(1+|z|)^N e^{a|\operatorname{Im}z|}
\]
for some $C,N$, whose restriction equals $f$ as a distribution. The
polynomial factor is absorbed by $e^{\varepsilon|z|}$ for every
$\varepsilon>0$, so this is exponential type at most $a$ in the convention
of \cite{MPSPW}. This proves parts 1 and 2 of Theorem \ref{thm:main}.

\subsection{Conjugating the interval multiplier}

The interval characteristic function is an $L^p$ multiplier for
$1<p<\infty$: $Q_a$ is a difference of two modulated half-line Riesz
projections. It is an idempotent whose range is precisely the $L^p$ functions
with distributional Fourier support in $[-a,a]$. Since $U_{s,p}$ is a
surjective isometry,
\[
 \Pi_{a,s,p}=U_{s,p}^{-1}Q_aU_{s,p}
\]
is an idempotent, has range
$U_{s,p}^{-1}(\mathcal B_a^p)=
\widetilde{\mathcal B}^{s,p}_{a,\crit}$, and satisfies
\[
 \|\Pi_{a,s,p}\|=\|Q_a\|_{p\to p}.
\]
This proves part 3.

When $p=2$, $U_{s,2}$ is unitary and $Q_a$ is the orthogonal projection in
$L^2$; unitary conjugation proves the first assertion of part 4. For a
Schwartz input $f$, away from zero the Fourier transform of
$\Pi_{a,s,p}f$ is $\mathbf1_{[-a,a]}\widehat f$. The source projection has
the same Fourier transform there. Their difference is supported at zero and
is therefore a polynomial; in the homogeneous space the two operators are
identical. The uniqueness of the chosen realization representative (or of a
chosen Taylor representative) then makes the equality exact. This completes
the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}.

\section{The quotient and Taylor-normalized formulations}

Define the homogeneous critical Bernstein space by
\[
 \dot{\mathcal B}^{s,p}_{a,\crit}
 =\big\{[F_0]_m\in\dot W^{s,p}:F\text{ is entire of type }\leq a\big\},
 \qquad
 \|[F_0]_m\|=\|\Delta^{s/2}F_0\|_p.
\]
The preceding proof shows directly that it is isometric to both
$\widetilde{\mathcal B}^{s,p}_{a,\crit}$ and $\mathcal B_a^p$.

If an actual entire representative is desired, take the BMO-normalized
bandlimited representative $f\in E^{s,p}$, let $F$ be its entire extension,
and set
\[
 J_mF=F-P_{F;m;0}.
\]
Then $J_mF$ has the same type, represents the same class, has the same norm,
and satisfies $P_{J_mF;m;0}=0$. Thus $J_m$ is a linear isometric bijection
from the critical BMO-normalized bandlimited realization to the
Taylor-normalized model. In this model the bounded map asked for in the
source is $J_m\Pi_{a,s,p}$; it is the same projection on the homogeneous
quotient. For $m=0$, one either subtracts the value at zero or works modulo
constants. This also resolves the minor normalization ambiguity in the
source's displayed tentative definition.

\begin{corollary}[Dual description]
With $p'=p/(p-1)$, the dual of the critical bandlimited space is canonically
\[
 \big(\widetilde{\mathcal B}^{s,p}_{a,\crit}\big)^*
 \cong L^{p'}(\R)/\ker Q_a^*,
\]
under the pairing
$\langle f,h\rangle=\int (U_{s,p}f)\overline h$. Since the multiplier is
real and $Q_a^*=Q_a$ on $L^{p'}$, this quotient is topologically identified
with the classical bandlimited subspace $\mathcal B_a^{p'}$ (with the
projection-induced quotient norm).
\end{corollary}

\section{Verification, limitations and novelty}

\begin{itemize}
\item The proof uses the critical realization theorem of
\cite{MPSReal}, the density/invertibility of the fractional Laplacian on
$\cS_\infty$, the one-dimensional interval multiplier theorem, and
Paley--Wiener--Schwartz. No unproved interpolation assertion is used.
\item The fixed-mean BMO normalization and the Taylor normalization are never
imposed simultaneously. They are related only after the output is known to be
entire.
\item The result covers exactly $1<p<\infty$. The interval projection is not
bounded on $L^1$ or $L^\infty$, so this argument correctly does not claim the
endpoints.
\item The theorem settles the bounded projection for the canonical quotient
and the source's recorded critical realization. It does not prove that a
particular concrete normalization is the complex or real interpolation space
between neighboring noncritical spaces.
\item A bounded search on 11 August 2026 covered the run's registry, solution,
attempt and proof-gap indexes; the exact title and arXiv id; the phrases
``$s-1/p$ Fractional Paley--Wiener'', ``critical fractional Bernstein
projection'', and close variants; the Springer article; and visible citing
followups. The published article still labels the question open. No later
paper answering the exact critical projection question was found. Novelty
confidence is moderate, not definitive.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Primary reviewer focus.} Check the support identity in Lemma
\ref{lem:support}, the use of the realization isomorphism at critical indices,
and the exact representative convention when comparing with the source
Hilbert projection.
