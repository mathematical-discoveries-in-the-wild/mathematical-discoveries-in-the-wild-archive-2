\paragraph{Status.}
Literature already answered: full negative answer.

\section*{Source question}

Szafraniec assumes that the densely defined operator \(S\) has invariant
domain, \(S\mathcal D(S)\subseteq\mathcal D(S)\).  For
\(f\in\mathcal D(S)\), its cyclic portion is the restriction
\[
 S_f=S\big|_{\mathcal D_f},\qquad
 \mathcal D_f=\{p(S)f:p\in\mathbb C[Z]\},
\]
viewed in the closure of \(\mathcal D_f\).  On source PDF page 21 he asks:
\[
 \boxed{\text{If every \(S_f\), \(f\in\mathcal D(S)\), is subnormal,
 must \(S\) be subnormal?}}
\]
The paper records affirmative results for bounded operators and several
additional unbounded hypotheses, but leaves the general question open.

\section*{Exact later answer}

The answer is no.  Szafraniec's own abstract for his GPOTS 2012 talk,
\emph{“Subnormality and cyclicity” in the negative}, states on abstract-book
PDF page 14 that the question whether subnormality of every cyclic portion
forces subnormality of the operator was unresolved in the unbounded case and
that the recent paper by Z.\ J.\ Jab{\l}o{\'n}ski, I.\ B.\ Jung, and
J.\ Stochel, \emph{A non-hyponormal operator generating Stieltjes moment
sequences}, JFA 262 (2012), 3946--3980, creates a counterexample.

The cited paper is arXiv:1110.0249.  Its decisive Example 4.2.1, on
supporting PDF page 21, constructs a weighted shift \(S_\lambda\) on a
directed tree with one branching vertex.  The operator generates Stieltjes
moment sequences, yet is not hyponormal.  Since every subnormal operator is
hyponormal, it is not subnormal.  The GPOTS abstract supplies the explicit
authorial identification that this construction answers the cyclic-portion
question itself, so the relation is not merely inferred from similar
terminology.

\section*{Scope and provenance}

This packet records a literature result; it does not claim a new
counterexample or reproduce the construction.  The source question is exactly
the unbounded invariant-domain local-to-global problem on cyclic portions.
The supporting paper gives the counterexample, and the later abstract by the
source author explicitly identifies it as a negative resolution of that
question.
