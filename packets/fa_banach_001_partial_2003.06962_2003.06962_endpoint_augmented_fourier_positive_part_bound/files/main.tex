\section{Source problem}

Madrid and Ramos study autocorrelation inequalities and a related Fourier
dual problem \cite{MadridRamos}. With the convention
\[
 \widehat\varphi(\xi)=\int_{\R}\varphi(x)e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx,
\]
let $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ be even and nonnegative, with
$\operatorname{supp}\varphi\subset[-1,1]$ and $\int_\R\varphi=1$.
Writing $u_+=\max(u,0)$, their Theorem~4.1 proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
 \bigl\|(\widehat\varphi)_+\bigr\|_1
 \ge \frac{1}{2(1+\theta_0)}=0.410767\ldots,
\end{equation}
where $\theta_0=-\inf_{y\in\R}\sin(y)/y=0.217\ldots$.
Immediately afterward they state that the sharp form of this lower bound is
unknown in every dimension \cite[p.~17]{MadridRamos}.

Equivalently, if
\[
 m_1=\inf\left\{\bigl\|(\widehat\varphi)_+\bigr\|_1:
 \begin{array}{l}
 \varphi\in C_c^\infty(\R)\text{ is even and nonnegative},\\
 \operatorname{supp}\varphi\subset[-1,1],\quad \int_\R\varphi=1
 \end{array}\right\},
\]
then the source proves $m_1\ge0.410767\ldots$ and asks for the sharp form.
The result below proves $m_1\ge0.479615\ldots$; it does not determine the
exact infimum.

\section{Proof intuition}

The source proof uses normalization and the center value $\varphi(0)$, but it
does not retain the boundary identity $\varphi(1)=0$. Fourier inversion turns
these two spatial identities into two moments of $\widehat\varphi$. A carefully
chosen linear combination leaves the multiplier
\[
 K(y)=\frac{2}{3}\left(2+\cos y-3\frac{\sin y}{y}\right).
\]
The Cusa--Huygens inequality makes $K$ nonnegative. Consequently only the
negative part of $\widehat\varphi$ can increase the combined moment, exactly
as needed to bound the positive mass.

The remaining issue is global: one needs a useful upper bound for $K$ on the
whole real line. Where $\sin y\ge0$ the bound is immediate. On the first
negative half-period, a tangent comparison proves monotonicity up to
$144/25$; after that point Cauchy--Schwarz controls every remaining
half-period. This yields a closed-form constant. The exact sharp problem
remains open because the argument uses only three scalar constraints and not
the full pointwise condition $\varphi\ge0$.

\section{The multiplier estimate}

Set $\sinc(0)=1$ and $\sinc(y)=\sin(y)/y$ for $y\ne0$.

\begin{lemma}[Global Cusa--Huygens inequality]\label{lem:cusa}
For every $y\in\R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cusa}
             \sinc(y)\le \frac{2+\cos y}{3}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both sides are even. For $0\le y\le\pi$, define
\[
 q(y)=y(2+\cos y)-3\sin y.
\]
Then $q(0)=0$ and
\[
 q'(y)=2-2\cos y-y\sin y
      =2\sin(y/2)\bigl(2\sin(y/2)-y\cos(y/2)\bigr)\ge0,
\]
because $\tan(y/2)\ge y/2$. Thus \eqref{eq:cusa} holds on this
interval. If $y\ge\pi$ and $\sin y\le0$, it is immediate since the
right-hand side is at least $1/3$. If $\sin y>0$, then $y\ge2\pi$ and
\[
 \sinc(y)\le\frac1y\le\frac1{2\pi}<\frac13
                    \le\frac{2+\cos y}{3}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform upper bound]\label{lem:upper}
The nonnegative even function
\[
 K(y)=\frac23\bigl(2+\cos y-3\sinc(y)\bigr)
\]
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D}
 0\le K(y)\le D:=\frac{96+\sqrt{2929}}{72}
 \qquad(y\in\R).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Nonnegativity is Lemma~\ref{lem:cusa}. By evenness, take $y\ge0$. If
$\sin y\ge0$, then $K(y)\le2<D$.

Suppose $\sin y<0$ and put
\[
 H(y)=\cos y+\frac{3|\sin y|}{y}.
\]
On $[\pi,3\pi/2]$ we have $H(y)\le3/y<1$. We next claim that $H$ is
increasing on
\[
                  \left[\frac{3\pi}{2},\frac{144}{25}\right].
\]
On this interval set $z=2\pi-y$. Since both sine and cosine are positive,
direct differentiation shows that $H'(y)>0$ is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent-comparison}
              \tan z>\frac{3y}{y^2-3}.
\end{equation}
The difference between the two sides of
\eqref{eq:tangent-comparison} has derivative
\[
 -\sec^2(2\pi-y)+\frac{3(y^2+3)}{(y^2-3)^2}<0,
\]
because $y>3$ implies $3(y^2+3)<(y^2-3)^2$. It is therefore enough to
check the right endpoint $r=144/25$.

The classical rational bound $\pi>333/106$ gives
\[
 2\pi-r>\frac{693}{1325}=:z_0.
\]
The positive Taylor coefficients of the tangent function give
\[
 \tan(2\pi-r)>z_0+\frac{z_0^3}{3}+\frac{2z_0^5}{15}
                    >\frac{3600}{6287}
                    =\frac{3r}{r^2-3}.
\]
For completeness, the middle strict inequality is the exact rational
calculation
\[
 z_0+\frac{z_0^3}{3}+\frac{2z_0^5}{15}-\frac{3600}{6287}
 =\frac{425878389221405094}{128378665258349609375}>0.
\]
This proves the claim.

For every $y\ge r$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 H(y)\le\sqrt{1+\frac9{y^2}}
       \le\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{25}{48}\right)^2}
       =\frac{\sqrt{2929}}{48}.
\]
The same bound holds on $[3\pi/2,r]$, because $H$ is increasing and its
endpoint value is bounded by the same Cauchy--Schwarz estimate. It also
dominates the bound $H<1$ on $[\pi,3\pi/2]$. Hence, whenever $\sin y<0$,
\[
 K(y)=\frac23(2+H(y))
       \le\frac23\left(2+\frac{\sqrt{2929}}{48}\right)
       =D.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Improved lower bound}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ be even and nonnegative, with
$\operatorname{supp}\varphi\subset[-1,1]$ and $\int_\R\varphi=1$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \bigl\|(\widehat\varphi)_+\bigr\|_1
 \ge \frac{72}{96+\sqrt{2929}}
 =0.4796155513\ldots .}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $F=\widehat\varphi$ and
\[
 P=\int_\R F_+(\xi)\,d\xi,
 \qquad N=\int_\R F_-(\xi)\,d\xi,
 \qquad a=\varphi(0)=P-N\ge0.
\]
The function $F$ is real, even, and rapidly decreasing. Normalization and
Fourier inversion at the endpoint give, with $y=2\pi\xi$,
\begin{align}
 1&=\int_\R F(\xi)\,2\sinc(y)\,d\xi,\label{eq:norm}\\
 0=\varphi(1)&=\int_\R F(\xi)\cos y\,d\xi.\label{eq:endpoint}
\end{align}
Subtracting $2/3$ times \eqref{eq:endpoint} from \eqref{eq:norm}, and
then subtracting $(4/3)a=\int F(4/3)$, yields
\[
 1-\frac43a
 =-\int_\R F(\xi)K(2\pi\xi)\,d\xi
 \le D N=D(P-a),
\]
where Lemma~\ref{lem:upper} was used in the last step. Since
$D>4/3$, rearrangement gives
\[
 DP\ge1+\left(D-\frac43\right)a\ge1.
\]
Therefore $P\ge1/D$, which is exactly \eqref{eq:main}.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, limitations, and search status}

The proof is analytic. The accompanying \texttt{verification.md} separately
checks the Fourier signs, all ranges in the multiplier estimate, and the final
rearrangement. The script \texttt{verify\_constant.py} reproduces the
exact rational checkpoint and performs a dense numerical contradiction scan;
the scan is not used to justify any global statement.

This is a partial answer to the source's sharpness direction. It improves the
known one-dimensional lower bound but does not determine the exact infimum,
construct an extremizer, or treat higher dimensions. A three-parameter
semi-infinite dual optimization suggests a slightly stronger certificate near
$0.47995$, but that computation is retained only in the attempt record because
it lacks a rigorous global enclosure.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
First verify the sign in the identity preceding the final estimate. Then
check Lemma~\ref{lem:upper}'s tangent comparison, especially the monotonicity
of the difference in \eqref{eq:tangent-comparison}. If those points pass, the
packet is ready to be treated as a rigorous quantitative partial result.
