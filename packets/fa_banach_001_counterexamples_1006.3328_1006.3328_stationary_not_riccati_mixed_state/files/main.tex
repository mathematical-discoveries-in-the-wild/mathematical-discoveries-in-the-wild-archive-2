\section{Source problem}

Gardas and Puchala study a qubit coupled to an environment, with total Hilbert
space $\C^2\otimes\mathcal H_E\simeq\mathcal H_E\oplus\mathcal H_E$ and block
Hamiltonian
\[
 \HH=\begin{bmatrix}H_+&V\\V^\dagger&H_-\end{bmatrix}.
\]
For an assignment map $\Phi$, the reduced evolution is
\[
 T_t(\rho)=\Tr_E\!\left(e^{-it\HH}\Phi(\rho)e^{it\HH}\right).
\]
The source explicitly permits the standard uncorrelated assignment
$\Phi(\rho)=\rho\otimes\omega$ for a fixed environment state $\omega$.

For a bounded solution $X$ of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:riccati}
 XVX+XH_+-H_-X-V^\dagger=0,
\end{equation}
Definition~3 calls the partial traces of the rank-one projectors onto vectors
of the graph
\[
 \Gamma_X=\left\{\binom{\psi}{X\psi}:\psi\in\mathcal H_E\right\}
\]
and its orthogonal complement Riccati states. Immediately after Theorem~1,
Remark~1 asks~\cite{GP}:
\begin{quote}
``The question whether all stationary states are Riccati states or if it is
possible that stationary states exist that are not Riccati states is still
open.''
\end{quote}

\section{Proof intuition}

The source's own Sylvester example ($V=0$) already makes the obstruction
visible. Separate the spectra of $H_+$ and $H_-$; then the Sylvester equation
has the unique solution $X=0$, so its graph construction yields only the two
pure computational-basis states. But stationarity is affine: under a fixed
product assignment with an environment state commuting with the conditional
environment Hamiltonians, every diagonal mixture of those two states is
stationary. A nontrivial mixture is therefore stationary but is not itself one
of the rank-one graph states in Definition~3.

The model below is not an uncoupled artifact. Its interaction is the nonzero,
non-scalar dephasing term $\sigma_z\otimes K$; only the off-diagonal block $V$
vanishes.

\section{Counterexample}

\begin{theorem}[Stationary need not mean Riccati]
There is a two-level open quantum system, with a two-dimensional environment
and a standard product assignment, having stationary states that are not
Riccati states in the sense of Definition~3 of the source. In particular,
Remark~1 has a negative answer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal H_E=\C^2$, put
\[
 K=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \HH=2\sigma_z\otimes\id+\sigma_z\otimes K.
\]
Thus $\HH$ has a genuine conditional interaction
$\HH_{\mathrm{int}}=\sigma_z\otimes K$, and in the qubit block decomposition
\[
 V=0,\qquad
 H_+=2\id+K=\operatorname{diag}(3,1),\qquad
 H_-=-2\id-K=\operatorname{diag}(-3,-1).
\]
All operators are bounded and self-adjoint, as required in the finite
dimensional special case of the source.

Equation~\eqref{eq:riccati} reduces to
\[
 XH_+-H_-X=0.
\]
Write $H_+=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)$ and
$H_-=\operatorname{diag}(\mu_1,\mu_2)$. Entrywise, this equation is
\[
 (\lambda_j-\mu_i)x_{ij}=0\qquad(1\le i,j\le2).
\]
The coefficient matrix is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}3-(-3)&1-(-3)\\3-(-1)&1-(-1)\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}6&4\\4&2\end{pmatrix},
\]
so every entry of $X$ is zero. Hence $X=0$ is the unique bounded solution of
the Riccati equation.

For this solution, graph vectors and orthogonal-graph vectors are respectively
$\binom{\psi}{0}$ and $\binom{0}{\phi}$. After normalization, their partial
traces are independent of the nonzero choices of $\psi$ and $\phi$ and equal
to
\[
 P_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 P_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Because no other Riccati solution exists, these are all the Riccati states for
this Hamiltonian in the source's sense.

Now fix the product assignment
\[
 \Phi(\rho)=\rho\otimes\omega,\qquad \omega=\tfrac12\id,
\]
which is one of the assignments explicitly admitted by the source. For any
$0<a<1$, let
\[
 \rho_a=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&1-a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both $\rho_a$ and $\omega$ commute with the two tensor factors occurring in
$\HH$, and therefore
\[
 [\HH,\rho_a\otimes\omega]=0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 e^{-it\HH}(\rho_a\otimes\omega)e^{it\HH}=\rho_a\otimes\omega
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 T_t(\rho_a)=\Tr_E(\rho_a\otimes\omega)=\rho_a
\]
for every $t$. Thus $\rho_a$ is stationary. Since $0<a<1$, it is mixed and is
neither $P_0$ nor $P_1$, so it is not a Riccati state. This proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification report}

The argument is finite-dimensional and rests on four exact checks.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The model has the source's block form, with bounded $V=0$ and
self-adjoint $H_\pm$; its interaction $\sigma_z\otimes K$ is nonzero and
non-scalar on the environment.
\item The four Sylvester coefficients are $6,4,4,2$, hence $X=0$ is the
unique solution.
\item Definition~3 applied to $X=0$ gives exactly the two normalized states
$P_0,P_1$.
\item The displayed commutator vanishes, proving stationarity under one fixed,
standard assignment map for every $t$.
\end{enumerate}
The accompanying standard-library Python script verifies the coefficient
calculation, the $4\times4$ commutator, and the partial trace exactly using
rational arithmetic. It was run once with $a=1/3$ and all assertions passed.
The computation is only a check; the entrywise and commutator arguments above
are the proof.

\section{Terminology and limitations}

This packet reads \emph{Riccati state} literally as Definition~3 does in the
context of Theorem~1: a partial trace of a rank-one projector from the graph or
orthogonal graph associated with a bounded solution of the Hamiltonian's
Riccati equation. Allowing an arbitrary operator $X$ unrelated to the
Riccati equation would trivialize the link to the Hamiltonian and is not the
question posed after Theorem~1.

The mixed state $\rho_a$ lies in the convex hull of $P_0$ and $P_1$. If one
redefines ``Riccati states'' to mean the convex hull of the states in
Definition~3, then this particular example is inside that enlarged class.
The source neither makes that definition nor states a convexified question.
Accordingly, the counterexample is a complete negative answer to the literal
open question, while the stronger convex-hull variant is not addressed here.

\section{Bounded novelty check}

The search through 11 August 2026 covered the run's registry, solution,
attempt, and proof-gap indexes; the exact sentence in Remark~1; the paper
title and arXiv identifier 1006.3328 combined with \emph{Riccati},
\emph{stationary}, \emph{counterexample}, and \emph{open quantum}; and close
stationary/Riccati phrase variants. It found the original paper and
mirrors/versions of it, but no later source explicitly answering Remark~1.
The source's own $V=0$ example lists only the two pure projections and does
not mention their stationary mixtures. Novelty confidence is moderate pending
a specialist search of non-indexed quantum-information literature; validity
of the counterexample is independent of novelty.

\section{Conclusion and review recommendation}

The literal question in Remark~1 has a negative answer: even a
finite-dimensional dephasing model with a unique Riccati solution has mixed
stationary states outside the rank-one Riccati family of Definition~3.
Promotion as a likely valid full counterexample is recommended, with human
review focused on whether the intended term was silently meant to include a
convex hull.
