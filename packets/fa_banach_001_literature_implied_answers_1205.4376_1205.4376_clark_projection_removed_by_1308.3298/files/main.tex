\section{The question}

Let $\theta$ be a scalar Schur function with $\theta(0)=0$, put
$\Dlt=(1-|\theta|^2)^{1/2}$, and let $\K_\theta$ be the two-component
Sz.-Nagy--Foia\c{s} model space.  In Theorem~6.1 of \cite{Liaw12}, Liaw
obtains, for $f\in C^1(\T)$,
\[
 \Phi_1^*f(z)=P_\theta\left[
 f(z)+\int_\T\frac{\xi(f(\xi)-f(z))}{\xi-z}\,d\mu(\xi)
 \right]
 \binom{1-\theta(z)}{-\Dlt(z)}.
\]
Remark~6.2(b), on PDF page~10, asks for an interpretation and, in particular,
``a reasonable expression for $\Phi_1^*f$ that does not involve
$P_\theta$.''  An image of the source page is preserved in the packet's
\texttt{figures} directory.

\section{The later universal formula}

Liaw and Treil treat the general Clark model in arXiv:1308.3298
\cite{LT13}.  Their Theorem~3.1, on PDF pages~11--12, applies to every
transcription of the functional model and states
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal}
 \Phi_\gamma^*f(z)=A_\gamma(z)f(z)
 +B_\gamma(z)\int_\T
 \frac{f(\xi)-f(z)}{1-\overline\xi z}\,d\mu(\xi),
\end{equation}
where $A_\gamma=c^\gamma$ and
$B_\gamma=c^\gamma-zc_1^\gamma$.  This is already a projection-free
singular-integral formula.  Equations (3.2)--(3.3) make the coefficient
vectors explicit in the Sz.-Nagy--Foia\c{s} transcription.

There is a harmless but important change of parameters.  In the survey,
$\mu=\mu_1$ is the spectral measure of the unitary Clark perturbation and
$\Phi_1^*:L^2(\mu_1)\to\K_\theta$ models the contraction
\[
 U_0=M_\xi-(\,\cdot\,,\overline\xi)\,1.
\]
In \cite{LT13}, the unperturbed unitary is named $U_1=M_\xi$ on
$L^2(\mu)$ and its contractive perturbation above is named $U_0$; its
characteristic function is $\theta_0=\theta$.  Consequently
\[
 \Phi_1^*\ \text{in \cite{Liaw12}}
 =\Phi_0^*\ \text{in \cite{LT13}}.
\]

Setting $\gamma=0$ in equations (3.1)--(3.3) of \cite{LT13} gives
\[
 A_0(z)=\binom{1}{0},\qquad
 B_0(z)=\binom{1-\theta(z)}{-\Dlt(z)}.
\]
Thus the requested formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:answer}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \Phi_1^*f(z)=\binom{f(z)}{0}
 +\binom{1-\theta(z)}{-\Dlt(z)}
 \int_\T\frac{f(\xi)-f(z)}{1-\overline\xi z}\,d\mu(\xi).}
\end{equation}
It holds for $f\in C^1(\T)$ under the assumptions of the source theorem and
contains no $P_\theta$.  Moreover, \eqref{eq:universal} answers the same
question for every $|\gamma|<1$ and every model transcription.

\section{Status and scope}

The supporting paper cites the survey and develops its unpublished
Liaw--Treil formula into the universal representation.  Because identifying
the source's parameter $1$ with the supporting paper's parameter $0$ requires
the normalization above, this is recorded as a literature-implied answer.
No claim is made about the survey's unrelated spectral and localization
questions.
