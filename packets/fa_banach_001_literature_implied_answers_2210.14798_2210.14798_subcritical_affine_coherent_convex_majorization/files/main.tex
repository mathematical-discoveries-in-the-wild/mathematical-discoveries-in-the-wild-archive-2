\begin{center}
{\Large Full affirmative answer to the subcritical affine question}\\[3pt]
{\large arXiv:2210.14798, Remark 20}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{literature\_implied\_answer (full, in every well-defined finite-value
case).}  A 2025 Dirichlet-range norm identity, combined with hyperbolic
level-set monotonicity, supplies the ingredient absent from the 2022 proof.
The supporting paper does not cite the source question or state this coherent-
state consequence, so the identification and final hinge-majorization argument
are recorded here explicitly.

\paragraph{Source question.}
For $\beta>0$ put
\[
 \psi_{a,b}(x)=2^\beta\Gamma(2\beta)^{-1/2}
 a^\beta x^{\beta-1/2}e^{-ax+ibx},
 \qquad a>0,\ b\in\R.
\]
Frank's Theorem~8 proves, when $\beta>1/2$, that normalized coherent states
maximize every convex functional of $|\langle\psi_{a,b},\psi\rangle|^2$.
Remark~20 asks whether this extends to $0<\beta<1/2$:

\paragraph{Finite-value convention.}
For $0<\beta<1/2$, the affine transform is not square-integrable, so linear
functionals diverge.  The nontrivial formulation is therefore the one in which
the displayed coherent-state integral below is a finite real number.  For a
finite-valued convex $\Phi$, this condition automatically forces
$\Phi(0)=\Phi'_+(0)=0$ and hence $\Phi\geq0$.  If instead $\Phi\geq0$ and the
coherent integral is $+\infty$, the supremum is also $+\infty$, already on a
coherent state.  Signed divergent integrals are not variational quantities and
are not assigned a value here.

\newpage
\begin{theorem}[Subcritical affine coherent-state majorization]
Let $0<\beta<1/2$ and let $\Phi:[0,1]\to\R$ be convex.  If
\[
 \frac{2\pi}{\beta}\int_0^1
 \Phi(s)s^{-1/(2\beta)-1}\dd s
\]
is finite, then
\begin{align*}
&\sup_{\substack{\psi\in L^2(\R_+)\\ \|\psi\|_2=1}}
 \iint_{\R_+\times\R}
 \Phi\!\left(|\langle\psi_{a,b},\psi\rangle|^2\right)
 \frac{\dd a\,\dd b}{a^2} \\
&\hspace{35mm}=
 \frac{2\pi}{\beta}\int_0^1
 \Phi(s)s^{-1/(2\beta)-1}\dd s .
\end{align*}
Every normalized coherent state, up to phase, attains the supremum.  If
$\Phi$ is not affine linear, these are the only maximizers.  The same equality
holds with value $+\infty$ when $\Phi\geq0$ and the right side diverges.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof intuition.}
Set $\alpha=2\beta\in(0,1)$.  The disk model is no longer an ordinary weighted
Bergman $L^2$ space; it is the Dirichlet-range RKHS with kernel
$(1-z\bar w)^{-\alpha}$.  This is exactly why the 2022 area-norm proof stops.
Brevig--Kulikov--Seip--Zlotnikov later found the correct replacement: the RKHS
norm is a renormalized weighted variation of the distribution of
$|f|^{2/\alpha}(1-|z|^2)$.  Hyperbolic isoperimetry says the associated
quantity $t(\mu(t)+1)$ decreases.  Once its total weighted variation is fixed,
a one-variable calculation shows that every hinge $(u^2-s)_+$ is largest for
the constant disk function, equivalently a coherent state.  Convex functions
are positive mixtures of hinges.

\paragraph{Step 1: the exact disk Hilbert space.}
Let
\[
 c_\alpha(n)=\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(n+1)},
 \qquad
 \|f\|_{\alpha,2}^2=\sum_{n=0}^\infty
 \frac{|\widehat f(n)|^2}{c_\alpha(n)}.
\]
This is the space $A^2_\alpha$ of \cite{BKSZ}; its reproducing kernel is
$K_w(z)=(1-z\bar w)^{-\alpha}$.  The Cayley--Laplace transform used in
\cite{Frank} is unitary from $L^2(\R_+)$ onto $A^2_\alpha$ for every
$\alpha>0$, not only for $\alpha>1$.  One direct check uses the orthonormal
Laguerre basis
\[
 e_n(x)=2^{\alpha/2}\left(\frac{n!}{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}\right)^{1/2}
 x^{(\alpha-1)/2}e^{-x}L_n^{\alpha-1}(2x),
\]
which is sent, up to harmless phases, to
$c_\alpha(n)^{1/2}z^n$, the normalized monomial basis of $A^2_\alpha$.

Write $f$ for the disk image of $\psi$.  The same algebra as on page~25 of
\cite{Frank} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disk}
 |\langle\psi_{a,b},\psi\rangle|^2
   =V_f(z):=|f(z)|^2(1-|z|^2)^\alpha,
 \qquad
 \frac{\dd a\,\dd b}{a^2}=4\pi\,dm_h(z),
\end{equation}
where $z$ is the Cayley image of $ia-b$ and
$dm_h=\pi^{-1}(1-|z|^2)^{-2}\dd A(z)$.  Normalized kernels
\[
 F_w(z)=\frac{(1-|w|^2)^{\alpha/2}}{(1-z\bar w)^\alpha}
\]
correspond exactly to affine coherent states.  The RKHS point bound gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:point}
 0\leq V_f(z)\leq\|f\|_{\alpha,2}^2.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 2: the renormalized norm constraint.}
Assume $\|f\|_{\alpha,2}=1$ and set
\[
 U_f(z)=|f(z)|^{2/\alpha}(1-|z|^2),\qquad
 \mu(t)=m_h\{U_f>t\},\qquad G(t)=t(\mu(t)+1).
\]
Notice that $V_f=U_f^\alpha$.  Lemma~3.1 of \cite{BKSZ}, originating in
Kulikov's hyperbolic level-set theorem, says that $G$ is nonincreasing on
$(0,t_0)$, where $t_0=\max U_f$.  Thus $\nu=-dG$ is a positive Stieltjes
measure and $G(t_0)=t_0$.  By \eqref{eq:point}, $t_0\leq1$.

The decisive new input is Lemma~3.2 and the endpoint passage following the
proof of Theorem~1.3 in \cite{BKSZ}.  Specializing their parameters to
$\sigma=2/\alpha$ gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normid}
 1=t_0^\alpha+\alpha\int_{(0,t_0)}t^{\alpha-1}\dd\nu(t).
\end{equation}
For functions analytic past the circle this is precisely Lemma~3.2, proved by
analytic continuation from the Bergman range.  For general $f\in A^2_\alpha$
it follows by their dilation argument; equivalently, apply the identity to
$f(\rho z)$ along radii avoiding boundary zeros and pass to the limit.  This
is the renormalized substitute for the divergent Bergman area norm.

\paragraph{Step 3: all hinge inequalities.}
Fix $0<\tau<1$ and define
\[
 H_f(\tau)=\int_\D(V_f-\tau^\alpha)_+\dd m_h.
\]
If $t_0\leq\tau$, then $H_f(\tau)=0$.  Suppose $\tau<t_0$.  Layer cake and
$\mu(t)=G(t)/t-1$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:layer}
 H_f(\tau)=\int_\tau^{t_0}\alpha t^{\alpha-2}(G(t)-t)\dd t.
\end{equation}
Since $G(t)=t_0+\nu([t,t_0])$, Tonelli rewrites this as a baseline term plus
the $\nu$-average of
\[
 K_\tau(y)=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}
 \bigl(\tau^{\alpha-1}-y^{\alpha-1}\bigr),\qquad y\geq\tau.
\]
Relative to the norm cost $\alpha y^{\alpha-1}$, this kernel has ratio
\[
 R_\tau(y)=\frac{(\tau/y)^{\alpha-1}-1}{1-\alpha},
 \qquad R_\tau'(y)=\tau^{\alpha-1}y^{-\alpha}>0.
\]
It is therefore bounded by $R_\tau(t_0)$.  Using \eqref{eq:normid}, including
the harmless mass below $\tau$ in the cost, yields
\[
 H_f(\tau)\leq Q_\tau(t_0),
\]
where elementary simplification gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q}
 Q_\tau(T)=\tau^{\alpha-1}
 \left(\frac{T^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}-T\right)
 -\frac1{1-\alpha}+\tau^\alpha.
\end{equation}
The entire extremal problem is now the derivative
\[
 Q_\tau'(T)=\tau^{\alpha-1}(T^{-\alpha}-1)\geq0
 \qquad(0<T\leq1).
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hinge}
 \int_\D(V_f-s)_+\dd m_h
 \leq \int_\D(V_{F_0}-s)_+\dd m_h,
 \qquad 0<s<1,
\end{equation}
after setting $s=\tau^\alpha$; here $F_0=1$ and
$V_{F_0}(z)=(1-|z|^2)^\alpha$.  Dilation and Fatou also give
\eqref{eq:hinge} directly if one begins with the past-the-boundary form of
\eqref{eq:normid}.

\newpage
\paragraph{Step 4: convex majorization and the constant.}
If the coherent integral of a finite-valued convex $\Phi$ is finite, its
constant and right-linear terms at zero must vanish.  Hence, for a positive
Borel measure $\lambda$ on $(0,1)$,
\[
 \Phi(v)=\int_{(0,1)}(v-s)_+\dd\lambda(s),\qquad 0\leq v\leq1.
\]
Integrating \eqref{eq:hinge} against $\lambda$ proves
\[
 \int_\D\Phi(V_f)\dd m_h
 \leq\int_\D\Phi((1-|z|^2)^\alpha)\dd m_h.
\]
With $r=|z|$, then $t=1-r^2$ and $s=t^\alpha$, the right side is
\[
 \frac1\alpha\int_0^1\Phi(s)s^{-1/\alpha-1}\dd s.
\]
Multiplying by $4\pi$ in \eqref{eq:disk}, and using $\alpha=2\beta$, gives
exactly the constant $2\pi/\beta$ in the theorem.  Every normalized kernel
has the same distribution, so every coherent state attains equality.

\paragraph{Step 5: equality.}
Corollaries~1.2 and~1.4 of \cite{BKSZ} give the sharp point estimate and
uniform boundary decay.  Thus $V_f$ attains its maximum; if that maximum is
$1$, equality in the RKHS point estimate forces $f$ to be a normalized kernel.
For every other unit vector, $t_0<1$.  Equations \eqref{eq:Q}--\eqref{eq:hinge}
are then strict at every threshold $s\in(0,1)$.  A non-affine convex function
has nonzero curvature measure in $(0,1)$, so its integrated inequality is
strict.  This proves the optimizer classification.
\hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Why this is literature-implied.}
The 2025 paper proves the Dirichlet-range norm identity and contractive power
inequalities, but it neither cites arXiv:2210.14798 nor states the affine
coherent-state convex majorization.  The answer follows only after the disk
identification and the hinge extremal calculation above.  Accordingly this
packet records a full implication of known literature, not an explicit later
answer and not a priority claim for a new theorem.

\paragraph{Verification, search, and scope.}
The algebra in \eqref{eq:Q} and its derivative are checked by
\texttt{code/verify\_extremal\_algebra.py}.  Before the proof was found,
Gamma-window formulas, an exact Hessian calculation, and polynomial searches
for real powers and hinges found no competing extremizer.  A bounded search
through 2026-08-13 covered the exact quoted question, its affine/RKHS
reformulation, local run indexes, and arXiv:1906.00223, 2112.09962,
2205.07998, 2211.03655, and 2510.14333.  No explicit later full answer was
found.  The theorem addresses the pure-state Theorem~8 question.  It does not
claim the omitted density-matrix analogue, nor assign values to signed
divergent integrals.
