\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Question 18 of Cordero-Erausquin--Eskenazis \cite{CE} has a negative answer.
Mar\'in Sola--Salerno \cite{MS} already refuted its torsional-rigidity
consequence for centrally symmetric Gaussian sets.  We give a shorter,
strictly stronger pointwise obstruction in dimension one.  This is not a
priority claim.

\section{Source question and literature status}

Let $\mu$ be an even log-concave measure on $\R^n$, let $K,L$ be symmetric
convex sets, and let $u(\lambda,\cdot)$ solve
\[
 \mathcal L_\mu u=-1\quad\hbox{on }\lambda K+(1-\lambda)L,
 \qquad u=0\quad\hbox{on the boundary}.
\]
Question 18 asks whether $(\lambda,x)\mapsto\sqrt{u(\lambda,x)}$ is concave.

\centering

\caption{Question 18 in the source PDF, page 26.}

\noindent\textbf{Faithful transcription.}
For an even log-concave $\mu$ and symmetric convex $K,L$, is
$\sqrt{u(\lambda,x)}$ jointly concave when $u(\lambda,\cdot)$ is the unique
weighted torsion solution on $\lambda K+(1-\lambda)L$?

The source explains immediately before the question that an affirmative
answer would imply the weighted Brunn--Minkowski inequality for torsional
rigidity.  In March 2026, Mar\'in Sola and Salerno explicitly addressed
\cite[Question 18]{CE} and disproved that consequence for centered Gaussian
balls in dimensions $n\ge3$.

\section{Proof intuition}

On a centered Gaussian interval the differential equation can be integrated
once.  The outward slope of the torsion function is a positive function
$B(R)$ satisfying the exceptionally simple ODE $B'=1+RB$.  Its derivative is
itself strictly convex.  This forces the square root of the torsion value at
the center to be \emph{strictly convex} as the radius grows, the opposite of
the proposed concavity.

\section{Exact pointwise counterexample}

Let $\gamma$ be the standard Gaussian measure on $\R$, so
\[
 \mathcal L_\gamma v=v''-xv'.
\]
For $R>0$, let $u_R$ be the unique solution
\[
 u_R''-xu_R'=-1\quad\hbox{on }(-R,R),\qquad u_R(-R)=u_R(R)=0.
\]

\begin{theorem}
The function
\[
 G(R)=\sqrt{u_R(0)}
\]
is strictly convex on $(0,\infty)$.  Consequently, Question 18 is false
already for $\mu=\gamma$, $K=[-1,1]$, and $L=[-3,3]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Evenness and uniqueness give $u_R'(0)=0$.  Multiplying the equation by
$e^{-x^2/2}$ and integrating from $0$ to $x$ yields
\[
 u_R'(x)=-B(x),\qquad
 B(x):=e^{x^2/2}\int_0^x e^{-s^2/2}\dd s .
\]
The boundary condition at $R$ therefore gives
\[
 F(R):=u_R(0)=\int_0^R B(t)\dd t.
\]
Direct differentiation shows
\[
 B'(R)=1+RB(R),\qquad
 B''(R)=R+(1+R^2)B(R)>0\quad(R>0).
\]
Since $F'=B$ and $F''=B'$, the sign of $G''$ is the sign of
\[
 H(R):=2F(R)B'(R)-B(R)^2.
\]
But $H(0)=0$ and the cancellation $F'=B$ gives
\[
 H'(R)=2F(R)B''(R)>0\qquad(R>0).
\]
Thus $H(R)>0$ and $G''(R)>0$ for every $R>0$.

Now $\frac12K+\frac12L=[-2,2]$.  Joint concavity in Question 18 would imply,
at $x=0$,
\[
 G(2)\ge \frac{G(1)+G(3)}2,
\]
whereas strict convexity gives the reverse strict inequality.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Scope and limitations.}
This settles only Question 18.  It does not resolve hereditary convexity
(Question 15) or the negative-exponent weighted-marginal problem (Question
19).  Eight focused upgrade routes were tried for Question 19: separable
negative moments, a quadratic perspective, max-type functions, radial
two-piece and multipiece polyhedra in dimensions up to twelve, anisotropic
quadratic perspectives, smooth log-sum-exp couplings, and anisotropic
log-concave weights.  No negative Hessian survived certified quadrature.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Novelty boundary.}
The question is already answered negatively by \cite{MS}; the value here is
the exact one-dimensional pointwise strengthening and elementary proof.
A bounded search of the run indexes and primary arXiv sources found no earlier
statement of this particular strict-convexity identity.  No priority is
claimed.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Human-review recommendation.}
High confidence.  Check only the convention
$\mathcal L_\gamma=\frac{d^2}{dx^2}-x\frac d{dx}$ and the logical
specialization to centered intervals; all remaining steps are two
differentiations and a strict sign.
