\noindent\textbf{Status.}
\emph{Literature-implied answer (complex continuous-normal subproblem).}
The argument below gives an explicit affirmative example on a complex Hilbert
space.  Its only external mathematical input is the real convex
Stone--Weierstrass theorem of Feldman and McGuire.  The pure-hyponormal
alternative in the source question is not settled here.

\section{Source question}

Berm\'udez, Bonilla, and Feldman ask in Section 7, Question 2 of
\cite{BBF}:
\begin{quote}
Are there pure hyponormal operators or continuous normal operators that are
convex-cyclic?
\end{quote}
The question occurs on page 17 of the locally rendered arXiv source.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Here a polynomial $p(z)=\sum_{n=0}^N c_nz^n$ is a
\emph{convex polynomial} when $c_n\geq0$ and $\sum c_n=1$.  An operator $T$
is convex-cyclic if some vector $x$ has
$\{p(T)x:p\in\CP\}$ dense in the ambient space.  A normal operator is called
continuous here when it has no eigenvalues (equivalently, its scalar spectral
measure has no atoms in the multiplicity-one example below).

\section{Literature input and identification}

Feldman and McGuire prove that if $\rho$ is a compactly supported finite
positive measure on $\mathbb R$, then real convex polynomials are dense in
$L^2_{\mathbb R}(\rho)$ exactly when
$\rho([-1,\infty))=0$; see \cite[Theorem 7.1]{FM}.  Their theorem is stated
for a real Hilbert space and therefore does not, by itself, make multiplication
by a negative real variable convex-cyclic on a complex $L^2$ space.  The
even--odd construction below supplies the missing complex direction.

\section{Idea of the proof}

Put the spectrum on the imaginary ray $i[a,b]$ with $a>1$.  Even powers of
$ir$ are powers of the negative real variable $-r^2$, so the real theorem
approximates the real part of an arbitrary target.  Odd powers are $ir$ times
the same negative-real powers, so they approximate the imaginary part after
division by the bounded nonvanishing function $r$.  Averaging an even convex
polynomial and an odd convex polynomial preserves nonnegative coefficients
and total coefficient mass one.

\section{The result}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $1<a<b<\infty$, let $\mu$ be a finite nonatomic positive Borel measure on
$[a,b]$, and let
\[
   H=L^2([a,b],\mu;\mathbb C),\qquad
   (Tf)(r)=irf(r).
\]
Then $T$ is a continuous normal convex-cyclic operator.  The constant function
$\mathbf 1$ is a convex-cyclic vector.  If $\operatorname{supp}\mu=[a,b]$,
then $\sigma(T)=i[a,b]$ and $\sigma_p(T)=\sigma_p(T^*)=\varnothing$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiplication by the bounded measurable function $r\mapsto ir$ is normal.
If $Tf=\lambda f$, then $(ir-\lambda)f(r)=0$ almost everywhere.  The zero set
of $ir-\lambda$ contains at most one point, and nonatomicity of $\mu$ forces
$f=0$.  The same argument applies to $T^*$, which is multiplication by
$-ir$.  Thus $T$ has no eigenvalues.  The assertion about the spectrum follows
from the usual spectrum formula for multiplication operators.

It remains to prove convex-cyclicity.  Push $\mu$ forward under
$r\mapsto x=-r^2$ and call the resulting measure $\rho$.  Its support is
contained in
\[
   [-b^2,-a^2]\subset(-\infty,-1),
\]
so \cite[Theorem 7.1]{FM} says that convex polynomials are dense in the real
space $L^2_{\mathbb R}(\rho)$.

Fix $F=U+iV\in H$, with $U,V$ real-valued, and fix $\varepsilon>0$.  Since
$r\geq a>0$, both $2U(r)$ and $2V(r)/r$ belong to
$L^2_{\mathbb R}(\mu)$.  By the cited density theorem there are convex
polynomials $q$ and $s$ such that
\begin{align*}
 \|q(-r^2)-2U(r)\|_{L^2(\mu)}&<\varepsilon,\\
 \|s(-r^2)-2V(r)/r\|_{L^2(\mu)}&<\varepsilon/b.
\end{align*}
Define
\[
   p(z)=\frac12q(z^2)+\frac12z\,s(z^2).
\]
The coefficients of $q(z^2)$ occupy the even degrees, the coefficients of
$zs(z^2)$ occupy the odd degrees, and each coefficient family is nonnegative
with sum one.  Hence $p$ is a convex polynomial.  On the spectral ray,
\[
  p(ir)=\frac12q(-r^2)+\frac{i}{2}r\,s(-r^2).
\]
Real and imaginary parts are orthogonal pointwise, so
\begin{align*}
 \|p(ir)-F(r)\|_2^2
 &=\frac14\|q(-r^2)-2U(r)\|_2^2
   +\frac14\|r\,s(-r^2)-2V(r)\|_2^2\\
 &<\frac14\varepsilon^2+\frac14b^2(\varepsilon/b)^2
 =\frac12\varepsilon^2.
\end{align*}
As $F$ and $\varepsilon$ were arbitrary, the convex polynomials in $ir$ are
dense in $H$.  Since $p(T)\mathbf1=p(ir)$, the vector $\mathbf1$ is
convex-cyclic.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
On $L^2([2,3],dr;\mathbb C)$, the operator
\[
    (Tf)(r)=irf(r)
\]
is an explicit complex continuous normal convex-cyclic operator with spectrum
$i[2,3]$ and empty point spectrum for both $T$ and $T^*$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Verification and scope}

The script \texttt{code/check\_even\_odd\_reduction.py} checks, using exact
rational arithmetic over 1,000 randomized pairs of convex polynomials, that
$\tfrac12(q(z^2)+zs(z^2))$ remains convex and that its evaluation at $z=ir$
has exactly the even/odd form used above.  This is a transcription check, not
a substitute for the density theorem.

The construction settles the continuous-normal existence alternative on a
\emph{complex} Hilbert space and gives a whole nonatomic family.  It does not
produce a pure hyponormal nonnormal operator, does not address whether every
convex-cyclic operator has densely many convex-cyclic vectors, and does not
answer how large $\sigma_p(T^*)$ can be (the example has empty adjoint point
spectrum).

\section{Search and provenance note}

A bounded search used the exact wording of Question 2 together with
``convex-cyclic'', ``continuous normal'', ``pure hyponormal'', ``point
spectrum'', and the authors' names in the local arXiv corpus and in web/arXiv
search.  It recovered \cite{BBF}, the later real theorem \cite{FM}, and
special-class convex-cyclicity papers, but no source explicitly stating this
complex imaginary-ray construction.  Because Theorem 7.1 of \cite{FM} is the
decisive input and the new step is a direct even/odd complexification, this
packet is conservatively classified as a literature-implied answer rather
than as an original full solution.  The local copies of both papers were
rendered from the archived arXiv TeX sources because network PDF retrieval was
unavailable during packaging.

\section{Human-review recommendation}

The result is likely valid.  Review should focus on the real-to-complex
identification: the pushforward measure, division by $r$, and the verification
that the average of the even and odd substitutions is still a convex
polynomial.  The literature/provenance classification should also be checked
against any later paper not returned by the bounded search.
