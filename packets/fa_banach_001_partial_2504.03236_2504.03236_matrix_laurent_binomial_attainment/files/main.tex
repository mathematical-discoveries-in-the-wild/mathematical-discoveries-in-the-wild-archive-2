\section*{Status and source question}

\noindent\textbf{Status:} candidate partial result, likely valid; human review
requested.

Let
\[
 A_{R,r}=\{z\in\C:r\le |z|\le R\},\qquad 0<r<R,
 \qquad
 \gamma(z)=\left(\frac zR,\frac rz\right).
\]
Baran, Pikul, Woerdeman, and Wojtylak prove in Corollary~5.2 that for every
rational matrix function \(F\in\M_n(\mathcal R(A_{R,r}))\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-infimum}
 \norm{F}_{\T_{A_{R,r}}}
 =\inf\left\{\norm{G}_{\infty,\overline{\D}^2}:
 G\in\M_n(\mathcal R(\overline{\D}^2)),\ G\circ\gamma=F\right\}.
\end{equation}
They then ask for which functions the infimum is attained, saying that the
general question is unanswered, and prove a scalar mixed-binomial special
case in Proposition~5.3 \cite{BPWW}.  The source passage is reproduced below.

\begin{center}
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth,height=.53\textheight,keepaspectratio]
  {figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The displayed formula in the source screenshot writes its supremum variable
as though \(G\) were one-variable.  Formula~\eqref{eq:source-infimum} uses the
bidisk supremum intended by the preceding theorem and used explicitly in the
source's Proposition~5.3.

For an invertible Hilbert-space operator \(T\), membership in the annular
class used here is simply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annular-class}
 T\in\T_{A_{R,r}}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \norm{T}\le R\quad\hbox{and}\quad \norm{T^{-1}}\le r^{-1}.
\end{equation}
For a matrix-valued function, \(F(T)\) acts on the tensor product with
\(\C^n\); thus, for example, \(F(z)=z^pA+z^qB\) gives
\(F(T)=T^p\otimes A+T^q\otimes B\).

\section*{Idea of the proof}

The upper bound comes from an explicit bidisk lift.  For mixed powers
\(p=k>0>q=-\ell\), the natural lift is
\[
 G(z_1,z_2)=R^kz_1^kA+r^{-\ell}z_2^\ell B.
\]
Its bidisk norm is the maximum of
\(\norm{R^kA+\omega r^{-\ell}B}\) over one relative phase
\(|\omega|=1\).

The key point is to recover every such phase from an annular operator.  Take a
cyclic weighted shift of size \(k+\ell\), with \(k\) successive weights \(R\)
and \(\ell\) successive weights \(r\).  It has norm \(R\) and inverse norm
\(r^{-1}\).  Starting from one carefully chosen coordinate, both \(T^k\) and
\(T^{-\ell}\) arrive at the same output coordinate, with respective weights
\(R^k\) and \(r^{-\ell}\).  Replacing \(T\) by \(\alpha T\) changes their
relative phase by \(\alpha^{-(k+\ell)}\), which sweeps the full unit circle.
This one compression therefore matches the explicit lift norm even for
arbitrary matrix coefficients.  Same-sign powers need only scalar operators
on the appropriate boundary circle.

\section*{Exact Laurent-binomial theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let \(0<r<R\), let \(p>q\) be integers, and let
\(A,B\in\M_n(\C)\).  Put
\[
 F(z)=z^pA+z^qB.
\]
Define the positive amplitudes \(a_p,a_q\) by
\[
 (a_p,a_q)=
 \begin{cases}
 (R^p,R^q),&q\ge0,\\
 (r^p,r^q),&p\le0,\\
 (R^p,r^q),&p>0>q.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-norm}
 \boxed{
 \norm{F}_{\T_{A_{R,r}}}
 =\max_{|\omega|=1}\norm{a_pA+\omega a_qB}.}
\end{equation}
In all three cases, the infimum in \eqref{eq:source-infimum} is attained.
Explicit attaining lifts are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lifts}
 G(z_1,z_2)=
 \begin{cases}
 R^pz_1^pA+R^qz_1^qB,&q\ge0,\\
 r^pz_2^{-p}A+r^qz_2^{-q}B,&p\le0,\\
 R^pz_1^pA+r^qz_2^{-q}B,&p>0>q.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

We first isolate a standard elementary observation.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:torus}
If \(G\) is a matrix polynomial on the bidisk, then
\[
 \sup_{(z_1,z_2)\in\overline\D^2}\norm{G(z_1,z_2)}
 =\sup_{|z_1|=|z_2|=1}\norm{G(z_1,z_2)}.
\]
Consequently, for nonnegative integers \(u,v\) and positive \(a,b\),
\[
 \norm{az_1^uA+bz_2^vB}_{\infty,\overline\D^2}
 =\max_{|\omega|=1}\norm{aA+\omega bB}.
\]
The same formula holds when both monomials use the same coordinate and have
distinct nonnegative powers.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For unit vectors \(x,y\in\C^n\), the scalar function
\(y^*G(z_1,z_2)x\) is a polynomial.  Applying the maximum-modulus principle
successively in the two variables, and then taking the supremum over \(x,y\),
proves the first assertion.  On the torus, multiplication by the scalar phase
of the first monomial does not change the matrix norm, while the quotient of
the two monomial phases runs through the whole unit circle.  This proves the
remaining assertions.  The possibility of a zero exponent causes no change.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Every function in \eqref{eq:lifts} is a matrix polynomial and direct
substitution of \(\gamma(z)=(z/R,r/z)\) gives \(G\circ\gamma=F\).  For example,
in the mixed case \(p=k>0>q=-\ell\),
\[
 R^k(z/R)^kA+r^{-\ell}(r/z)^\ell B
 =z^kA+z^{-\ell}B.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:torus} shows in each of the three cases that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper-lift}
 \norm{G}_{\infty,\overline\D^2}
 =\max_{|\omega|=1}\norm{a_pA+\omega a_qB}.
\end{equation}
Therefore \eqref{eq:source-infimum} gives the upper inequality in
\eqref{eq:exact-norm}.

Suppose first that \(q\ge0\).  For every \(|\xi|=1\), the scalar operator
\(T=R\xi I\) belongs to \(\T_{A_{R,r}}\), and
\[
 F(T)=\xi^p\bigl(R^pA+\xi^{q-p}R^qB\bigr).
\]
Because \(\xi^{q-p}\) runs through the unit circle, the definition of the
Agler norm gives the reverse of \eqref{eq:upper-lift}.  If \(p\le0\), use
instead \(T=r\xi I\).  It again lies in the annular class, and exactly the
same argument gives the reverse inequality with amplitudes \(r^p,r^q\).

It remains to treat \(p=k>0>q=-\ell\).  Set \(N=k+\ell\), and let
\(e_1,\ldots,e_N\) be the standard basis of \(\C^N\).  Define the cyclic
weighted backward shift \(T\) by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
 T e_{j+1}=w_j e_j\quad(1\le j<N),
 \qquad T e_1=w_Ne_N,
\end{equation}
where
\[
 w_1=\cdots=w_k=R,
 \qquad
 w_{k+1}=\cdots=w_N=r.
\]
The columns of \(T\) are orthogonal and have lengths \(w_N,w_1,\ldots,w_{N-1}\).
Hence
\[
 \norm{T}=R,\qquad \norm{T^{-1}}=r^{-1},
\]
so \(T\in\T_{A_{R,r}}\).  Following the two directed paths from
\(e_{k+1}\) in \eqref{eq:shift} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-paths}
 T^ke_{k+1}=R^ke_1,
 \qquad
 T^{-\ell}e_{k+1}=r^{-\ell}e_1.
\end{equation}
For \(|\alpha|=1\), also \(\alpha T\in\T_{A_{R,r}}\).  Thus, for every
\(x\in\C^n\), \eqref{eq:two-paths} yields
\begin{align*}
 F(\alpha T)(e_{k+1}\otimes x)
 &=e_1\otimes
 \bigl(\alpha^kR^kA+\alpha^{-\ell}r^{-\ell}B\bigr)x\\
 &=\alpha^k e_1\otimes
 \bigl(R^kA+\alpha^{-(k+\ell)}r^{-\ell}B\bigr)x.
\end{align*}
Taking the supremum over unit \(x\), then over \(\alpha\), gives
\[
 \norm{F}_{\T_{A_{R,r}}}
 \ge\max_{|\omega|=1}\norm{R^kA+\omega r^{-\ell}B},
\]
because \(\alpha^{-(k+\ell)}\) covers the full unit circle.  This matches
\eqref{eq:upper-lift}, completing both the norm identity and attainment.
\end{proof}

\section*{Two broader one-sided classes}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:one-sided}
The infimum in \eqref{eq:source-infimum} is attained in either of the
following situations.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \(H\) is a rational matrix function analytic on a neighbourhood of
 \(\{z:|z|\le R\}\), and \(F=H|_{A_{R,r}}\).  An attaining lift is
 \(G(z_1,z_2)=H(Rz_1)\).
 \item \(K\) is a rational matrix function analytic on a neighbourhood of
 \(\overline\D\), and \(F(z)=K(r/z)\).  An attaining lift is
 \(G(z_1,z_2)=K(z_2)\).
\end{enumerate}
In the first case the exact norm is
\(\max_{|z|=R}\norm{H(z)}\); in the second it is
\(\max_{|w|=1}\norm{K(w)}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proposed functions are admissible rational lifts.  Their bidisk norms are
the stated boundary maxima by the scalar maximum-modulus argument used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:torus}.  Conversely, the scalar operators \(T=R\xi I\) in the
first case and \(T=r\xi I\) in the second case belong to the annular class and
recover every point on the relevant boundary circle.  The definition of the
Agler norm supplies matching lower bounds.
\end{proof}

\section*{Scope and full-classification upgrade attempts}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} classifies every two-term Laurent support, all matrix
sizes, and all annulus radii.  It does \emph{not} classify arbitrary rational
matrix functions.  In particular, it does not decide attainment for the
source's example \(z+z^{-1}+1\) on \(A_{1,1/2}\).

Eight focused upgrade attempts were made.  Montel and weak-star compactness
can produce bounded holomorphic limiting lifts but do not preserve rationality
or neighbourhood analyticity.  Finite-dimensional realizations give no
uniform state-dimension bound along a minimizing sequence.  A quotient-ideal
counterexample scheme would require proving that a rational coset has only a
nonrational best \(H^\infty\) representative, for which no usable uniqueness
or nonproximinality criterion was found.  Polynomial-correction optimization
for the affine source example was exploratory only.  A small exact annular
operator gives a lower value near \(3.549\), but no matching rational lift was
proved.  The successful scalar-coefficient, matrix-coefficient, arbitrary-
radius, same-sign, and one-sided upgrades culminate in the results above.

The obstacle to a mechanical extension to three or more mixed Laurent terms
is concrete: the cyclic path aligns two powers and supplies one free relative
phase, while three arbitrary matrix coefficients generally require more
simultaneous phase information.  With the compactness and quotient routes also
blocked by the absence of a rational-degree bound, no further credible route
to the full classification remained in the allotted upgrade budget.
