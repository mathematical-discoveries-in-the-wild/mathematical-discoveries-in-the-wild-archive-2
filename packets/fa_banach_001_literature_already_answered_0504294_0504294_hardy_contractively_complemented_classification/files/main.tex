\paragraph{Status.}
\texttt{literature\_already\_answered}.  The later paper of Fu, Guo, and Li
classifies all contractive projections and all nontrivial 1-complemented
subspaces of $H^p(\mathbb T)$ for $1\le p<\infty$, $p\ne2$.

\section*{Source question}

Lancien, Randrianantoanina, and Ricard introduce, for an inner function
$\psi$ with $\psi(0)=0$ and a congruence class
$\Lambda=\{qk+r:k\in\mathbb N_0\}$, the 1-complemented space
\[
 H^\psi_{p,\Lambda}
 =\overline{\operatorname{span}}^{H^p}\{\psi^n:n\in\Lambda\}.
\]
At the end of Section 4, printed p.~8, they ask:
\begin{quote}
``We do not know whether every 1-complemented subspace of $H_p$ has to be
isometric to $H^\psi_{p,\Lambda}$, for some coset $\Lambda$ in $\mathbb N$
and some inner function $\psi$ vanishing at the origin.''
\end{quote}
This follows a subsection explicitly devoted to infinite-dimensional ranges.
The intended question is therefore about nontrivial (rank $>1$) ranges;
one-dimensional ranges are the elementary exception already present in every
Banach space \cite{LRR}.

\section*{Decisive later theorem}

Fu, Guo, and Li cite the 2005 paper and state that they will give explicit
expressions for all contractive projections and 1-complemented subspaces of
$H^p$ \cite{FGL}.  Their Theorem 1.2 says that every contractive projection
$P:H^p\to H^p$ of rank greater than one is
\[
 P=\varphi\,\mathbb E_\varphi(\,\cdot\,/\varphi\mid\eta)\big|_{H^p}
\]
for an admissible inner function $\eta$ and multiplier $\varphi$, and its
range is
\[
 H^p_{\eta,\varphi}
 =\{\varphi(f\circ\eta):f\in H^p\}.
\]
Definition 1.1 and Proposition 4.2 identify every such range isometrically
with $H^p$.  The paper records the consequence explicitly:

\begin{theorem}[Fu--Guo--Li, Corollary 1.4]
Let $1\le p<\infty$, $p\ne2$.  Every nontrivial 1-complemented subspace of
$H^p$ is isometric to $H^p$.
\end{theorem}

\section*{Why this answers the 2005 formulation}

The full Hardy space is one of the 2005 model spaces: take
$\psi(z)=z$ and the full congruence class $\Lambda=\mathbb N_0$.  Then
\[
 H^\psi_{p,\Lambda}=H^p.
\]
Consequently every nontrivial 1-complemented subspace is isometric to a space
of the precise form proposed in the source.  The answer is affirmative for
all parameters in the source, $1\le p<\infty$, $p\ne2$.

The later classification is actually stronger: it describes the range as an
embedded weighted-composition space $H^p_{\eta,\varphi}$ and gives the
projection itself, not merely its abstract isometry type.

\section*{Evidence and scope}

The identification used local full sources for arXiv:math/0504294 and
arXiv:2412.14808v4, exact-phrase searches, and theorem-level inspection.

$p=2$ Hilbert-space case is claimed; it was excluded in the source question.
