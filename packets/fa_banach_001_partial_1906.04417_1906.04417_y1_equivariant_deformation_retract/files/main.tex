\title{The first Lazarev--Lieb phase sublevel retracts to the circle\\
\large A partial answer to Problem 6.3 of arXiv:1906.04417}
\author{Autonomous mathematical discovery run \texttt{fa\_banach\_001}}
\date{17 August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Frick and Superdock ask for the homotopy type of the space \(Y_n\) of smooth
circle-valued paths with total variation at most \(\pi n\), equipped with the
\(L^1\) topology.  We determine the first nontrivial case.  For every variation
threshold \(0\leq L\leq\pi\), the normalized mean defines an antipodally
equivariant strong deformation retraction of the corresponding phase space
onto its circle of constant paths.  In particular,
\(Y_1\simeq_{\mathbb Z/2}S^1\) and \(\coind Y_1=1\).  The proof includes the
sharp endpoint \(L=\pi\) and verifies continuity in the source's coarse
\(L^1\) topology.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source problem and scope}

The source is F. Frick and M. Superdock, \emph{A nonlinear Lazarev--Lieb
theorem: \(L^2\)-orthogonality via motion planning}, arXiv:1906.04417;
J. Topol. Anal. 14 (2022), 569--585.  Its Theorem 6.2 defines
\[
 Y_n=\left\{h\in C^\infty([0,1];S^1):
       \|h\|_{W^{1,1}}\leq1+\pi n\right\}
\]
with the topology induced by \(L^1\), proves
\(n\leq\coind Y_n\leq2n-1\), and then asks in Problem 6.3 to determine the
homotopy type of \(Y_n\).

\includegraphics[width=0.91\textwidth]{figures/source_problem_page14.png}

Because \(|h|=1\), the condition is exactly
\(\int_0^1|h'(t)|\,dt\leq\pi n\).  We solve the problem for \(n=1\), and more
generally for every sublevel at or below this first critical threshold.  We do
not claim the all-\(n\) homotopy type.

\section{Proof intuition}

A circle-valued path of variation at most \(\pi\) fits in a closed semicircle.
Its average cannot vanish: after rotating that semicircle to the upper half
plane, cancellation would force the path to take only the two endpoints,
which continuity forbids.  More strongly, the mean direction lies strictly
inside the path's phase arc.  No path value is therefore antipodal to the
mean, so the principal argument relative to the mean is smooth.  Multiplying
that argument by \(1-s\) contracts the path to its mean direction without
increasing variation.  The mean is \(L^1\)-continuous; a bounded-convergence
argument handles the apparent branch-cut issue for the principal argument.

\section{The deformation retraction}

For \(0\leq L\leq\pi\), put
\[
 Y(L)=\left\{h\in C^\infty([0,1];S^1):
                \Var(h):=\int_0^1|h'(t)|\,dt\leq L\right\},
\]
again with the \(L^1\) topology.  The involution is \(h\mapsto-h\).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:mean}
For every \(h\in Y(\pi)\), the mean
\(m(h)=\int_0^1h(t)\,dt\) is nonzero.  If
\(u(h)=m(h)/|m(h)|\), then \(-u(h)\notin h([0,1])\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lift \(h\) to a smooth real phase \(g\), so \(h=e^{ig}\).  If
\(a=\min g\) and \(b=\max g\), then
\[
 b-a\leq\Var(g)=\Var(h)\leq\pi.
\]
If \(a=b\), the assertions are immediate.  Otherwise, continuity of \(g\)
and the intermediate value theorem give
\[
 \Im\!\left(e^{-ia}m(h)\right)
 =\int_0^1\sin(g(t)-a)\,dt>0,
\]
because \(0\leq g-a\leq\pi\) and the integrand is positive on a nonempty
open set.  Similarly,
\[
 \Im\!\left(e^{-ib}m(h)\right)
 =\int_0^1\sin(g(t)-b)\,dt<0.
\]
Thus \(m(h)\neq0\), and it has an argument \(c\) strictly between \(a\) and
\(b\).  Since \(b-a\leq\pi\), neither \(c+\pi\) nor \(c-\pi\) belongs to
\([a,b]\).  Hence no value \(e^{ig(t)}\) equals \(-e^{ic}=-u(h)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every \(0\leq L\leq\pi\), the inclusion of the constant paths
\(S^1\hookrightarrow Y(L)\) is a \(\mathbb Z/2\)-equivariant strong
deformation retract.  Consequently,
\[
 Y_1=Y(\pi)\simeq_{\mathbb Z/2}S^1,
 \qquad \coind Y_1=1.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(h\in Y(L)\), Lemma \ref{lem:mean} permits the smooth principal phase
\[
 \theta_h(t)=\Arg\!\left(\overline{u(h)}h(t)\right)\in(-\pi,\pi).
\]
Define, for \(0\leq s\leq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 H_s(h)(t)=u(h)\exp\!\big(i(1-s)\theta_h(t)\big).
\end{equation}
Then \(H_0(h)=h\), \(H_1(h)=u(h)\) is constant, and a constant path is fixed
for every \(s\).  Moreover,
\[
 \Var(H_s(h))=(1-s)\int_0^1|\theta_h'(t)|\,dt
             =(1-s)\Var(h)\leq L,
\]
so the homotopy stays in \(Y(L)\).

It remains to check the topology carefully.  The mean is a continuous linear
functional on \(L^1\), and Lemma \ref{lem:mean} makes normalization continuous
on \(Y(L)\).  Suppose \(h_k\to h\) in \(L^1\).  Then
\(u(h_k)\to u(h)\) and
\(q_k=\overline{u(h_k)}h_k\to q=\overline{u(h)}h\) in \(L^1\).
The compact set \(q([0,1])\) avoids \(-1\).  On a fixed neighborhood of this
set, the principal argument is uniformly continuous.  Outside the set where
\(q_k\) lies in that neighborhood, Markov's inequality makes the measure tend
to zero; there the two arguments are merely bounded by \(\pi\).  Splitting the
integral between these two sets proves
\(\theta_{h_k}\to\theta_h\) in \(L^1\).  Formula \eqref{eq:H} now proves joint
continuity of \(H\) in \((h,s)\).

Finally, \(u(-h)=-u(h)\), while
\(\overline{u(-h)}(-h)=\overline{u(h)}h\).  Hence
\(H_s(-h)=-H_s(h)\): the retraction is equivariant.  The antipodal circle has
coindex one (and admits no equivariant map from \(S^2\) by Borsuk--Ulam), so
the same is true of \(Y_1\).
\end{proof}

\section{Why the same formula stops at the next threshold}

The proof uses the bound \(L\leq\pi\) exactly twice: to force a semicircle and
to keep the normalized mean away from every antipodal path value.  Both fail
beyond the first threshold.  More fundamentally, at variation \(2\pi\) a
full phase slip can be compressed into an interval of vanishing measure.  Its
exponential converges in \(L^1\), while any compatible real lift retains a
\(2\pi\) jump.  Thus convexity of the space of real lifts does not descend to
an \(L^1\)-continuous contraction of \(Y_n\) for \(n\geq2\).

The accompanying attempt note records interval-moment/join, polynomial-phase,
and phase-slip-cell approaches to the general problem.  Each leaves a genuine
continuous-selection or normal-form gap, so the result above is intentionally
classified as partial.

\section{Verification and novelty}

The proof is analytic and exact.  The included numerical script stress-tests
the nonvanishing mean, antipode separation, and variation scaling on smooth
random and boundary-near phase curves; it is not used as a substitute for the
proof.  The source page and source files are included locally.

A bounded search of the run indexes, the exact problem, the notation
\(\coind Y_n\), and normalized-mean/homotopy phrases found no duplicate or
published determination of \(Y_1\).  Superdock's 2021 thesis reproduces the
general bounds and again states that exact coindex determination remains open.
Novelty is plausible, not certified; a specialist should audit especially the
\(L^1\)-continuity paragraph in Theorem \ref{thm:main}.
