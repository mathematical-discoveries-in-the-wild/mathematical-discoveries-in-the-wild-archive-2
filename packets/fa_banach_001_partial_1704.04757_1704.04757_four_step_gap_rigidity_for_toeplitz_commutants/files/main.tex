\section{Source question}

Let $A^2(\D)$ be the Bergman space of the unit disk and let $T_f$ denote the
Toeplitz operator with bounded symbol $f$.  Axler, \v{C}u\v{c}kovi\'c and Rao
asked whether, for every bounded harmonic $g$ which is neither analytic nor
anti-analytic,
\[
 [T_f,T_g]=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f=\alpha g+\beta
\]
for every bounded $f$.  Alsabi and Louhichi quote the question on PDF page 2
of \cite{AlsabiLouhichi2017}:

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\caption{The open problem as quoted in \cite[p.~2]{AlsabiLouhichi2017}.}
\end{figure}

For the fixed symbol
\[
                         h(z)=z^2+\bar z^2,
\]
their main theorem proves the desired conclusion when the polar Fourier
expansion of $f$ is right-terminating.  The result below permits infinitely
many positive and negative modes.

For $f\in L^\infty(\D)$ write
\[
 f(re^{i\theta})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}e^{ik\theta}f_k(r)
 \quad\text{in }L^2(\D),
\]
and let
\[
 S(f)=\{k\in\mathbb Z:f_k\ne0\text{ in }L^2([0,1],r\,dr)\}.
\]
In each residue class modulo $4$, regard consecutive integers at distance
$4$ as adjacent.  A \emph{four-step support component} is a connected
component of $S(f)$ in this graph.

\begin{theorem}[Four-step gap rigidity]\label{thm:main}
Let $f\in L^\infty(\D)$ and suppose
\[
                         [T_f,T_{z^2+\bar z^2}]=0.
\]
Each of the following hypotheses implies that, almost everywhere on $\D$,
\[
                         \boxed{f=\alpha(z^2+\bar z^2)+\beta}
\]
for some $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Every four-step support component of $f$ is finite.
\item The angular Fourier series is Wiener-summable,
\[
                         \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\|f_k\|_\infty<\infty,
\]
and, in every residue class $q\pmod4$, there are arbitrarily large
$k\equiv q\pmod4$ for which $f_k=0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Thus (i) covers sparse symbols with infinitely many modes in both directions,
while (ii) needs gaps only in the positive direction.  Equivalently, under
the Wiener hypothesis a non-affine commuting symbol, if one exists, must have
an eventually gapless positive ray in at least one residue class modulo $4$.

\section{Proof intuition}

Rotations split every Toeplitz operator into homogeneous pieces.  Commuting
with $T_{z^2+\bar z^2}$ couples the piece of angular degree $k$ only to the
piece of degree $k+4$.  A missing Fourier mode is therefore an exact cut in
the recurrence, not merely a small error.  Every support block between two
such cuts is itself the symbol of a Toeplitz operator commuting with
$T_{z^2+\bar z^2}$.

Finite blocks are bounded right-terminating symbols, so the source theorem
says that each block is affine in $z^2+\bar z^2$.  Under Wiener summability,
the one possible block extending to $-\infty$ also converges in
$L^\infty$ and is right-terminating, so the same theorem applies.  Finally,
the residue-class support of an affine symbol is so small that only the
constant block and the block containing both degrees $-2$ and $2$ can remain.

\section{Homogeneous commutator decomposition}

Let $U_t$ be the rotation unitary on $A^2(\D)$.  An operator $X$ is
homogeneous of degree $k$ when
\[
                         U_tXU_t^*=e^{ikt}X.
\]
The weak-operator Fourier coefficient
\[
                 \Phi_k(X)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
                 e^{-ikt}U_tXU_t^*\,dt
\]
is a bounded homogeneous operator.  Rotation covariance of Bergman Toeplitz
operators gives
\[
                  \Phi_k(T_f)=T_{e^{ik\theta}f_k(r)}=:T_k.
\]
The two summands $T_{z^2}$ and $T_{\bar z^2}$ have degrees $2$ and $-2$.
Taking the degree $k+2$ coefficient of the zero commutator yields, for every
$k\in\mathbb Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
             [T_k,T_{z^2}]+[T_{k+4},T_{\bar z^2}]=0.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Block separation]\label{lem:block}
Let $B$ be a four-step support component.  Suppose the symbol
\[
                         f_B=\sum_{k\in B}e^{ik\theta}f_k(r)
\]
defines a bounded Toeplitz operator, with the sum interpreted in
$L^\infty$ when $B$ is infinite.  Then
\[
                         [T_{f_B},T_{z^2+\bar z^2}]=0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Inside $B$, equation \eqref{eq:chain} is exactly the corresponding
homogeneous coefficient of $[T_{f_B},T_{z^2+\bar z^2}]$.  At either endpoint,
the adjacent degree outside $B$ is a missing Fourier mode, hence its operator
is zero, and \eqref{eq:chain} again gives the endpoint coefficient.  All
other homogeneous coefficients vanish trivially.  If $B$ is infinite and
the stated series converges in $L^\infty$, the operator series converges in
norm, so the same coefficient argument passes to the sum.  Therefore every
homogeneous coefficient of the block commutator is zero.  A bounded operator
whose weak rotation Fourier coefficients all vanish is zero, proving the
claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

We use the main theorem of Alsabi and Louhichi
\cite[Section~3]{AlsabiLouhichi2017}: if a bounded symbol $u$ is
right-terminating and $T_u$ commutes with $T_{z^2+\bar z^2}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
                         u=c(z^2+\bar z^2)+c_0
\end{equation}
almost everywhere.

Assume first (i).  For every support component $B$, the sum defining $f_B$
is finite, hence $f_B\in L^\infty$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:block} shows that its
Toeplitz operator commutes with $T_{z^2+\bar z^2}$.  It is right-terminating,
so \eqref{eq:source} applies.

But $B$ lies in one residue class modulo $4$, whereas the only Fourier degrees
in \eqref{eq:source} are $-2,0,2$.  A block in residue class $1$ or $3$ must
therefore be zero.  A block in residue class $0$ must be zero unless it
contains degree $0$, in which case it is constant.  A block in residue class
$2$ must be zero unless it contains both adjacent degrees $-2$ and $2$, in
which case it is a scalar multiple of $z^2+\bar z^2$.  Consequently every
Fourier coefficient of $f$ other than these three vanishes, and uniqueness of
the $L^2$ Fourier expansion proves the claimed formula.

Now assume (ii).  In a fixed residue class, the hypothesis of arbitrarily
large missing indices implies that every support component is bounded above.
All such components are finite except possibly one component extending to
$-\infty$.  Finite components are handled as above.  For a lower-infinite
component $B$, the Wiener assumption gives
\[
                  \sum_{k\in B}\|e^{ik\theta}f_k(r)\|_\infty<\infty.
\]
Hence $f_B$ converges in $L^\infty$, is bounded and right-terminating, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:block} and \eqref{eq:source} apply.  The same residue-support
argument eliminates every block except the possible constant and
$z^2+\bar z^2$ blocks.  This proves (ii). \qed

\section{Verification, limitations, and upgrade audit}

The proof is analytic and has no computational dependency.  The two points
most worth checking are the indices in \eqref{eq:chain} and the passage from a
lower-infinite block to a bounded symbol in part (ii).  Both are explicit:
commutation degrees are $(k)+2$ and $(k+4)-2$, and absolute convergence in
$L^\infty$ gives norm convergence of the corresponding Toeplitz operators.

The theorem does not settle the fixed-symbol problem for arbitrary bounded
$f$.  Eight focused upgrade attempts tested later literature, the Jacobi
spectral commutant, compact commuting remainders, smooth boundary
asymptotics, and quantitative tail decay before isolating the exact block
mechanism and its Wiener upgrade.  The remaining case contains an eventually
gapless positive four-step tail.  Replacing an exact gap by smallness would
require a uniform stability estimate for the Mellin-transform recurrence;
the source proof is qualitative and no such estimate was obtained.

\section{Literature and novelty boundary}

A bounded search on 17 August 2026 covered the run's cheap indexes, the exact
source title and arXiv id, the citation neighborhood of the source and the
original problem, Le and Tikaradze's later right-terminating theorem
\cite{LeTikaradze2017}, and the 2024 finite quasihomogeneous-sum result
\cite{AlsabiLouhichi2024}.  Those located results retain right-termination or
finite-sum restrictions.  No explicit statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
was found.  Novelty confidence is moderate: the block argument is short once
the source theorem and the four-step recurrence are placed together.

Human review is recommended, concentrating on Lemma~\ref{lem:block}, the
support-component classification, and the precise hypotheses of the source
right-terminating theorem.
