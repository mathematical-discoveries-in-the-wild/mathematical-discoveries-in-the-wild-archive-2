# Disk-algebra counterexample to relative Berkovich boundary permanence

This packet gives a full negative answer to the final open question in
arXiv:1410.5893.

Take `k=C`, `A=C(T)`, `a(z)=z`, and let `B` be the disk algebra restricted to
the circle.  Then `B` is the closed unital subalgebra generated by `a`, its
usual spectrum is the closed unit disk, and the ambient usual spectrum is the
unit circle.  In the full `A^1_Z` topology used by the source, every point of a
complex-relative Berkovich spectrum is a boundary point.  Therefore the point
represented by zero is in the boundary for `B` but absent from the spectrum
for `A`.
