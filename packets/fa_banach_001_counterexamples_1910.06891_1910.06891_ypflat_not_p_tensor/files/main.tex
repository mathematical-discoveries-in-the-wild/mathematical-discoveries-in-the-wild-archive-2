\section{Status and source question}

For \(0<p<1\), Aleksandrov and Peller put
\[
 p_\flat=\frac{2p}{2-p},\qquad
 \mathcal Y_{p_\flat}
 =\ell^{p_\flat}(\linf)
   +\bigl(\ell^{p_\flat}(\linf)\bigr)^{\mathrm t}.
\]
They ask whether this sufficient class of Schur multipliers is contained in
their \(p\)-tensor product \(\tp\) \cite{source}. The question appears in
Remark 1 on page 20:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The answer is no for every \(0<p<1\). In fact, the counterexample belongs to
the smaller one-sided class \(\ell^{p_\flat}(\linf)\).

\section{Proof intuition}

A flat \(n\)-by-\(n\) unitary has \(n\) equal singular values but entries of
size \(n^{-1/2}\). After multiplying it by \(n^{-1/p_\sharp}\), where
\(p_\sharp=p/(1-p)\), the row-envelope cost is governed by
\[
 \frac1{p_\flat}=\frac1{p_\sharp}+\frac12.                 \tag{1}
\]
Thus a block with an additional scalar weight \(c\) costs \(c^{p_\flat}\)
in the row class. On the other hand, its equal singular values force any
\(p\)-tensor representation to spend essentially \(c^p\) coefficient mass
in a window whose location is proportional to the block dimension.

Choose weights for which \(\sum c_j^{p_\flat}<\infty\) but
\(\sum c_j^p=\infty\), and separate the dimensions so rapidly that these
mandatory coefficient windows are disjoint. Sharing rank-one terms between
blocks then cannot reduce the total cost.

\section{The coefficient-window lemma}

Recall that \(A\in\tp\) means that
\[
 A=\sum_{k\ge1}x_k y_k^{\mathrm t},\qquad
 \sum_{k\ge1}\norm{x_k}_\infty^p\norm{y_k}_\infty^p<\infty. \tag{2}
\]
Since \(\ell^p\subset\ell^1\) for \(p<1\), such a series converges uniformly
entrywise.

\begin{lemma}[Scaled-unitary coefficient window]
\label{lem:window}
Let \(0<p<1\), put \(r=p_\sharp=p/(1-p)\), and let \(U\) be an
\(n\)-by-\(n\) unitary. Suppose \(c>0\) and
\[
 c n^{-1/r}U=\sum_{k\ge1}\lambda_k u_kv_k^{\mathrm t},       \tag{3}
\]
where \(\norm{u_k}_\infty,\norm{v_k}_\infty\le1\) and
\(\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots\ge0\) belongs to \(\ell^p\).
For \(0<a<1<b\),
\[
 \sum_{an<k\le bn}\lambda_k^p
 \ge c^p(1-a)-b^{p-1}\norm{\lambda}_{\ell^p}^p.              \tag{4}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(R=\sum_{k>an}\lambda_k u_kv_k^{\mathrm t}\). What was removed from
(3) has rank at most \(an\). All \(n\) singular values of the left side are
\(c n^{-1/r}\), so the singular-value approximation inequality gives
\[
 \norm R_{\Sp}^p
 \ge (1-a)n(cn^{-1/r})^p
 =c^p(1-a)n^p,                                                \tag{5}
\]
because \(p/r=1-p\).

For \(T=\sum_{k>bn}\lambda_k u_kv_k^{\mathrm t}\), the comparison of
Schatten norms in dimension \(n\), the rank-one trace norm estimate, and
monotonicity of \(\lambda\) give
\begin{align*}
 \norm T_{\Sp}
 &\le n^{1/p-1}\norm T_{\boldsymbol S_1}
 \le n^{1/p}\sum_{k>bn}\lambda_k \\
 &\le n^{1/p}(bn)^{-(1-p)/p}\norm\lambda_{\ell^p}
 =b^{-1/r}n\norm\lambda_{\ell^p}.                            \tag{6}
\end{align*}
Here the middle bound follows from
\(\sum_{k>bn}\lambda_k\le
 \lambda_{\lfloor bn\rfloor+1}^{\,1-p}\sum_{k>bn}\lambda_k^p\).

Put \(M=\sum_{an<k\le bn}\lambda_k u_kv_k^{\mathrm t}\), so \(R=M+T\).
The Schatten \(p\)-triangle inequality and (5)--(6) imply
\[
 \norm M_{\Sp}^p
 \ge c^p(1-a)n^p-b^{p-1}n^p\norm\lambda_{\ell^p}^p.         \tag{7}
\]
Each \(u_kv_k^{\mathrm t}\) has Schatten \(p\)-norm at most \(n\), hence
\(\norm M_{\Sp}^p\le n^p\sum_{an<k\le bn}\lambda_k^p\).
Dividing (7) by \(n^p\) proves (4).
\end{proof}

This is the source paper's identity-block window argument with the identity
replaced by an arbitrary unitary; the proof shows that only the constant
singular-value list matters.

\section{Counterexample}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:main}
For every \(0<p<1\), with \(q=p_\flat=2p/(2-p)\),
\[
 \ell^q(\linf)\not\subset\tp.
\]
Consequently \(\mathcal Y_{p_\flat}\not\subset\tp\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(r=p_\sharp=p/(1-p)\) and define
\[
 c_j=(j+1)^{-1/p},\qquad
 b_j=c_j^{-2p/(1-p)}=(j+1)^{2/(1-p)},\qquad j\ge1.           \tag{8}
\]
Choose positive integers \(n_j\) recursively so that
\[
 n_{j+1}\ge4b_jn_j.                                         \tag{9}
\]
Let \(U_j\) be the normalized Fourier unitary of order \(n_j\); every entry
of \(U_j\) has modulus \(n_j^{-1/2}\). Partition \(\mathbb N\) into
successive sets \(E_j\) with \(|E_j|=n_j\), and define the block diagonal
matrix
\[
 W\big|_{E_j\times E_j}=c_jn_j^{-1/r}U_j,\qquad
 W\big|_{E_j\times E_k}=0\quad(j\ne k).                    \tag{10}
\]

By (1), the supremum of the absolute values in every row belonging to
\(E_j\) is \(c_jn_j^{-1/q}\). If \(\alpha\) is the sequence of row suprema,
then
\[
 \sum_i\alpha_i^q
 =\sum_{j\ge1}n_j(c_jn_j^{-1/q})^q
 =\sum_{j\ge1}c_j^q
 =\sum_{j\ge1}(j+1)^{-q/p}<\infty,                           \tag{11}
\]
since \(q/p=2/(2-p)>1\). Thus
\(W\in\ell^q(\linf)\subset\mathcal Y_q\).

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that \(W\in\tp\). Normalize and reorder a
representation (2) to obtain
\[
 W=\sum_{k\ge1}\lambda_k u_kv_k^{\mathrm t},\qquad
 \norm{u_k}_\infty,\norm{v_k}_\infty\le1,\quad
 \lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots\ge0,\quad
 L:=\norm\lambda_{\ell^p}<\infty.                           \tag{12}
\]
Compressing (12) to \(E_j\times E_j\) does not increase either
\(\ell^\infty\)-norm. Lemma~\ref{lem:window}, with \(a=1/4\) and \(b=b_j\),
therefore gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{n_j/4<k\le b_jn_j}\lambda_k^p
 &\ge\frac34c_j^p-b_j^{p-1}L^p\\
 &=\frac34c_j^p-L^pc_j^{2p}.                                \tag{13}
\end{align*}
For all sufficiently large \(j\), the last expression is at least
\(\tfrac12c_j^p\). By (9), the intervals
\((n_j/4,b_jn_j]\) are pairwise disjoint. Hence
\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}\lambda_k^p
 \ge\frac12\sum_{j\ge j_0}c_j^p
 =\frac12\sum_{j\ge j_0}\frac1{j+1}=\infty,               \tag{14}
\]
contrary to (12). Thus \(W\notin\tp\).
\end{proof}

\section{Checks, scope, and novelty}

The construction is genuinely endpoint: one flat block has unit-scale cost
in both estimates, and the separation appears only after assembling infinitely
many blocks with \(\ell^q\setminus\ell^p\) weights. The error term in (13) is
\(L^pc_j^{2p}=o(c_j^p)\); its exponent is the reason for the particular choice
of \(b_j\). A bundled verification script checks (1), all exponent conversions,
and the flat-unitary singular values for finite Fourier matrices.

The theorem answers only the inclusion asked in Remark 1. It does not settle
automatic complete boundedness for \(1<p<\infty\), the spectral-measure
extension problem, the diagonal Lorentz conjecture, or equality between the
\(p\)-tensor product and its bounded pointwise closure.

Bounded exact-title, exact-phrase, arXiv-id, citation, and tensor-keyword
searches through August 17, 2026 found no later answer. Later related works
located concern triangular projections, Haagerup tensor products, smooth
Schur multipliers, or analytic Schur multipliers. Novelty is a candidate claim
pending specialist and database review.
