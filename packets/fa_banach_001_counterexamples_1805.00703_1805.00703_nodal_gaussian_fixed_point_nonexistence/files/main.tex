\section{The source problem}

Klebanov proposes an implicit, matrix-valued adaptation equation for
$f\in W^{2,2}(\R^d,\R)\setminus\{0\}$ and $0<\lambda<\sqrt2$.  In one
dimension, write $s(x)=\mu_f(x)^2>0$.  The equation at a fixed spatial point
$x$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed}
 s=\frac{\lambda^2s}{2}+\frac12 R_f(s;x),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R}
 R_f(s;x)=
 \frac{(f'^2-ff'')*G_{(\lambda\sqrt{s})^{-2}}^2(x)}
      {f^2*G_{(\lambda\sqrt{s})^{-2}}^2(x)}.
\end{equation}
The corresponding iteration replaces $s$ on the right by $s_n$ and defines
$s_{n+1}$.  Remark 23 assumes that the equation has a unique positive solution
and that the iteration converges to it from every positive initial value, then
states that the class of functions for which this holds is open.  The exact
source passage is reproduced in Figure~\ref{fig:source}.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\caption{Remark 23 and iteration (12), arXiv:1805.00703v1, PDF page 13.}
\label{fig:source}
\end{figure}

For the normalized one-dimensional Gaussian with covariance $C$, its square is
proportional to $e^{-y^2/C}$.  Therefore, at $x=0$, the squared window in
\eqref{eq:R} contributes precisely the factor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight}
 \exp(-\lambda^2 s y^2);
\end{equation}
its normalization cancels from the quotient.

\section{Proof intuition}

The source computes the fixed point for a nonvanishing Gaussian.  A zero of the
signal changes the calculation decisively because the denominator of
\eqref{eq:R} acquires extra powers of the integration variable.  Gaussian
moment ratios then grow linearly with $s$, feeding an additional multiple of
$s_n$ back into the iteration.

For the first nodal Gaussian $f(x)=x e^{-a x^2/2}$, the nonlinear-looking
scheme becomes the affine recurrence
\[
 s_{n+1}=\frac{3a}{2}+\frac{3\lambda^2}{2}s_n.
\]
Its slope crosses one at $\lambda^2=2/3$.  Below this value there is one
positive fixed point and geometric convergence at the node.  At the threshold
the iterates drift linearly, and above it they grow exponentially; the only
algebraic fixed point is then nonpositive.  Thus a zero of a perfectly regular
Schwartz function destroys even existence.

The same calculation works for $x^m e^{-a x^2/2}$.  The consecutive even
Gaussian moments give the exact threshold
$(4m-2)/(4m-1)$.  A further calculation away from the node shows that the first
threshold is sharp for global pointwise solvability of the implicit equation,
not merely an artifact of evaluating at one point.

\section{Exact Gaussian-monomial classification at the node}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:family}
Let $a>0$, $m\in\mathbb N$, and
\[
 f_m(y)=y^m e^{-a y^2/2}.
\]
For the source iteration at $x=0$, put $s_n=\mu_n(0)^2$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine}
 s_{n+1}=A_m+r_m s_n,
 \qquad
 A_m=\frac{a(4m-1)}{2(2m-1)},
 \qquad
 r_m=\frac{\lambda^2(4m-1)}{2(2m-1)}.
\end{equation}
Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item if $\lambda^2<(4m-2)/(4m-1)$, there is one positive fixed point,
\[
 s_* =\frac{a(4m-1)}{2(2m-1)-\lambda^2(4m-1)},
\]
and every $s_0>0$ converges to it with
$s_n-s_*=r_m^n(s_0-s_*)$;
\item at equality, no fixed point exists and $s_n=s_0+nA_m$;
\item above the threshold, no positive fixed point exists and every positive
orbit diverges exponentially.
\end{enumerate}
For $m=0$, the nonvanishing Gaussian instead gives
$s_{n+1}=a/2+(\lambda^2/2)s_n$, which converges throughout the source range
$0<\lambda<\sqrt2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $m\geq1$, direct differentiation (or differentiating
$\log|f_m|$ away from zero and extending the polynomial identity) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B}
 f_m'^2-f_mf_m''
 =\left(a y^{2m}+m y^{2m-2}\right)e^{-a y^2}.
\end{equation}
At $x=0$, combine this with \eqref{eq:weight} and put
$c=a+\lambda^2s$.  If
\[
 I_j(c)=\int_{\R}y^{2j}e^{-cy^2}\,dy,
\]
the standard integration-by-parts recurrence is
\[
 \frac{I_{m-1}(c)}{I_m(c)}=\frac{2c}{2m-1}.
\]
The quotient in \eqref{eq:R} is therefore
\begin{align*}
 R_{f_m}(s;0)
 &=a+m\frac{I_{m-1}(c)}{I_m(c)}\\
 &=\frac{a(4m-1)}{2m-1}
   +\frac{2m\lambda^2}{2m-1}s.
\end{align*}
Substitution into \eqref{eq:fixed} gives exactly \eqref{eq:affine}.  An affine
recurrence with positive intercept has a positive fixed point and convergence
from every positive start exactly when $r_m<1$.  The three cases and the
displayed formulas follow immediately.

When $m=0$, identity \eqref{eq:B} reduces to
$f_0'^2-f_0f_0''=a f_0^2$, so $R_{f_0}=a$ independently of the window and the
last assertion follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:counter}
For every $\lambda$ with
\[
 \sqrt{2/3}\leq\lambda<\sqrt2,
\]
the admissible Schwartz function $f(x)=x e^{-a x^2/2}$ has no positive
solution of the source equation at $x=0$.  Hence it has no positive adaptation
function solving the equation on $\R$, and its fixed-point iteration cannot
have the property required in Remark 23.  In particular, this failure occurs
at $\lambda=1$, inside the source's reported favorable interval $[0.6,1.2]$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $m=1$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:family}; its threshold is $2/3$.  Failure at a
single spatial point rules out a positive solution function on all of $\R$.
The source's empirical interval is stated in its conclusion.
\end{proof}

\section{Sharp global solvability for the first nodal Gaussian}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:global}
Let $f(y)=y e^{-a y^2/2}$.  The implicit equation \eqref{eq:fixed} has a unique
positive solution $s=s(x)$ at every $x\in\R$ if and only if
$\lambda^2<2/3$.  In that regime $s(x)$, and hence $\mu_f(x)=\sqrt{s(x)}$, is
continuous.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $L=\lambda^2$, $b=Ls$, and $c=a+b$.  Completing the square in the
Gaussian integrals shows that, under their normalized weight, the integration
variable has mean $bx/c$ and variance $1/(2c)$.  Since
\[
 f^2=y^2e^{-ay^2},
 \qquad f'^2-ff''=(1+ay^2)e^{-ay^2},
\]
we obtain the exact formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generalx}
 R_f(s;x)=a+\frac{2c^2}{c+2b^2x^2}.
\end{equation}
For $L<2/3$, define
$F_x(s)=R_f(s;x)-(2-L)s$.  We have $F_x(0)=3a>0$.  As $s\to\infty$,
$F_x(s)\to-\infty$: for $x\neq0$, the first term in \eqref{eq:generalx}
tends to $a+x^{-2}$, while for $x=0$ one has
$F_0(s)=3a-(2-3L)s$.

It remains to prove uniqueness.  If
$h(b)=2(a+b)^2/(a+b+2b^2x^2)$, direct differentiation gives
\[
 h'(b)=
 \frac{2(a+b)(a+b-4abx^2)}{(a+b+2b^2x^2)^2}\leq2.
\]
Thus $\partial_sR_f\leq2L$ and
\[
 \partial_sF_x\leq3L-2<0.
\]
The intermediate value theorem and strict decrease give exactly one positive
root.  The displayed strict derivative bound and the smooth formula imply
continuous dependence of the root on $x$.  The converse follows from
Corollary~\ref{cor:counter}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:global} classifies existence and uniqueness of the
implicit equation for this test function.  It does not assert convergence of
the raw iteration at every nonzero $x$.  The derivative of that map need not be
a uniform contraction over all $x$ and $s$, so a separate dynamical argument
would be required.  At the decisive node $x=0$, Theorem~\ref{thm:family} gives
the complete iteration dynamics.
\end{remark}

\section{Verification, novelty, and review}

The core calculation is exact: it uses only differentiation and the recurrence
for even Gaussian moments.  The companion script
\texttt{code/verify\_gaussian\_monomial.py} symbolically verifies the $m=1$
identity and all threshold formulas.  No numerical approximation enters the
proof.

A bounded search of the run's lightweight indexes and primary arXiv sources
found the source paper and its companion on variable kernel density estimation,
but no later treatment of this fixed-point obstruction.  The source proves the
constant fixed point for a nonvanishing Gaussian, but does not analyze the
iteration or nodal Gaussians.  Novelty confidence is moderate; exhaustive
priority is not claimed.

This packet gives a full counterexample to universal well-posedness and an exact
infinite-family threshold, but not the complete class requested by the open
problem.  A human reviewer should check the translation from the source's
Gaussian covariance notation to \eqref{eq:weight}, the Gaussian-moment quotient,
and the derivative bound in Proposition~\ref{prop:global}.
