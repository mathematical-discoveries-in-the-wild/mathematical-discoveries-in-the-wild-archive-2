\section*{Source problem and exact scope}

The source is C.~G\'omez-Cabello and F.~J.~Gonz\'alez-Do\~na,
\emph{On frequencies of parabolic Koenigs domains},
arXiv:2507.08514v1 \cite{source}.  If \(\Omega=h(\D)\) is a Koenigs
domain and \(1\leq p<\infty\), its \(H^p\)-frequencies are
\[
 \Freq_p(\Omega):=\{\lambda\in\C:e^{\lambda z}\in H^p(\Omega)\}.
\]
Equivalently, these are the eigenvalues of the infinitesimal generator of the
associated composition semigroup on \(H^p\).

Remark 5.14 on PDF page 38 of version 1 states that, for the convex domain with
defining function \(\psi_\Omega(t)=t^2\), the paper's estimates do not decide
whether the two imaginary boundary rays contain frequencies:
\[
 \lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\pi/2},\qquad
 \mu=|\mu|e^{3i\pi/2}.
\]
The current arXiv revision reorganizes this part and no longer prints this
specific sentence.  The packet therefore preserves version 1 as
\texttt{source\_paper.pdf}.

\section*{Proof intuition}

The quadratic boundary is one of the rare non-sectorial Koenigs boundaries
that can be uniformized exactly.  The strip
\(\{|\operatorname{Im}\xi|<1/2\}\), modulo the involution
\(\xi\mapsto-\xi\), is sent to the right of a parabola by
\((\xi^2+1/4)/c\).  The even map \(\cosh(\pi\xi)\) descends through the same
quotient and maps the parabolic domain conformally onto the right half-plane.
Consequently the harmonic measure of the parabola is not merely estimated:
it has the exact density \(c\,\operatorname{sech}(\pi c y)\,dy\).

On the boundary, \(|e^{\lambda z}|^p\) is a Gaussian-exponential factor
\(\exp(p(\operatorname{Re}\lambda)c y^2-p(\operatorname{Im}\lambda)y)\).
The Gaussian sign decides every non-imaginary frequency, while the exact
exponential tail of harmonic measure gives the sharp imaginary threshold.
An interior growth estimate after a Cayley transform proves the positive
Hardy-membership direction without assuming an unproved boundary-value
criterion.

\section*{Exact theorem}

For \(c>0\), put
\[
 \Omega_c:=\{x+iy\in\C:x>c y^2\}.
\]
This is a convex simply connected domain satisfying
\(\Omega_c+t\subset\Omega_c\) for \(t\geq0\), hence a parabolic Koenigs
domain.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every \(c>0\) and \(1\leq p<\infty\),
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \Freq_p(\Omega_c)
 =
 \{\lambda\in\C:\operatorname{Re}\lambda<0\}
 \ \cup\
 \{\,it:t\in\mathbb R,\ |t|<\pi c/p\,\}.
 \quad}
\]
In particular, the two critical frequencies
\(i\pi c/p\) and \(-i\pi c/p\) do not belong to
\(\Freq_p(\Omega_c)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Step 1: exact uniformization.}
Let
\[
 S:=\{\xi=u+iv:|v|<1/2\},\qquad
 P_c(\xi):=\frac{\xi^2+1/4}{c}.
\]
Writing \(P_c(\xi)=x+iy\), a direct calculation gives
\[
 x-cy^2
 =\frac{(1-4v^2)(u^2+1/4)}{c}.
\]
Thus \(P_c(S)=\Omega_c\).  Every point of \(\Omega_c\) has exactly the
two preimages \(\xi\) and \(-\xi\), except at the harmless branch point
\(\xi=0\).

The map \(\xi\mapsto\cosh(\pi\xi)\) sends \(S\) onto the right half-plane
\(\Hh:=\{q:\operatorname{Re}q>0\}\).  Indeed,
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\cosh(\pi(u+iv))
 =\cosh(\pi u)\cos(\pi v)>0,
\]
and the two preimages of a point are again \(\xi\) and \(-\xi\).
Therefore it descends to a conformal bijection
\[
 F_c:\Omega_c\longrightarrow\Hh,\qquad
 F_c(z)=\cosh\!\left(\pi\sqrt{cz-\tfrac14}\right).
\]
The displayed expression is single-valued: its power series contains only
integer powers of \(cz-1/4\).  It also makes clear that the apparent branch
at \(z=1/(4c)\) is removable.

\textbf{Step 2: exact harmonic measure.}
The boundary is parameterized by
\[
 z(y)=c y^2+iy,\qquad y\in\mathbb R.
\]
Since
\[
 cz(y)-\tfrac14=(cy+i/2)^2,
\]
we obtain
\[
 F_c(z(y))=\cosh(\pi(cy+i/2))=i\sinh(\pi c y).
\]
Choose the base point \(z_*=1/(4c)\), for which \(F_c(z_*)=1\).
Harmonic measure at \(1\) in the right half-plane has density
\[
 \frac{1}{\pi}\frac{dt}{1+t^2}
\]
on the boundary \(it\).  Substituting \(t=\sinh(\pi c y)\) gives the exact
boundary density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:harmonic-density}
 d\omega_{\Omega_c,z_*}(z(y))
 =c\,\operatorname{sech}(\pi c y)\,dy.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Step 3: the open left half-plane consists of frequencies.}
Write \(\lambda=a+ib\).  If \(a<0\), then for \(z=x+iy\in\Omega_c\),
\[
 \operatorname{Re}(\lambda z)=ax-by
 \leq acy^2-by.
\]
The right-hand side is bounded above because its quadratic coefficient is
negative.  Hence \(e^{\lambda z}\) is bounded on \(\Omega_c\), so it belongs
to \(H^\infty(\Omega_c)\subset H^p(\Omega_c)\).

\textbf{Step 4: sufficiency on the imaginary axis.}
Let \(\lambda=ib\), and let
\[
 Q(\zeta)=\frac{1+\zeta}{1-\zeta},\qquad \zeta\in\D,
\]
be the Cayley map to \(\Hh\).  Put
\(h_c=F_c^{-1}\circ Q\).  For \(q=Q(\zeta)\), choose
\(\xi=u+iv\in S\) with \(h_c(\zeta)=(\xi^2+1/4)/c\).
Then
\[
 |\operatorname{Im}h_c(\zeta)|=\frac{|2uv|}{c}\leq\frac{|u|}{c}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 |q|^2
 =|\cosh(\pi(u+iv))|^2
 =\sinh^2(\pi u)+\cos^2(\pi v),
\]
so \(\sinh(\pi|u|)\leq|q|\), and therefore
\[
 e^{\pi|u|}\leq 2|q|+1\leq\frac{C}{|1-\zeta|}
\]
for an absolute constant \(C\).  It follows that
\[
 |e^{ib h_c(\zeta)}|^p
 \leq C_{b,c,p}|1-\zeta|^{-p|b|/(\pi c)}.
\]
The standard disk estimate
\[
 \sup_{0<r<1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
 |1-re^{i\theta}|^{-\alpha}\,d\theta<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha<1
\]
now proves \(e^{ib h_c}\in H^p(\D)\) whenever
\(p|b|<\pi c\).  By conformal invariance, \(ib\in\Freq_p(\Omega_c)\).

\textbf{Step 5: necessity and endpoint exclusion.}
If \(e^{\lambda z}\in H^p(\Omega_c)\), its pullback by \(h_c\) is in
\(H^p(\D)\), so its radial boundary values are \(p\)-integrable.
Using conformal invariance of harmonic measure and
\eqref{eq:harmonic-density}, this forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-integral}
 \int_{\mathbb R}
 \exp\!\left(p(ac y^2-by)\right)
 c\,\operatorname{sech}(\pi c y)\,dy<\infty.
\end{equation}
If \(a>0\), the positive quadratic term makes
\eqref{eq:boundary-integral} diverge.  If \(a=0\), the asymptotic
\(\operatorname{sech}(\pi c y)\sim2e^{-\pi c|y|}\) shows that the integral
converges exactly when \(p|b|<\pi c\).  At \(p|b|=\pi c\), the integrand
tends to a positive constant along one tail, so both endpoints are excluded.
Together with Steps 3 and 4, this proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[all translated quadratic boundaries]
Let \(c>0\) and \(x_0,y_0\in\mathbb R\), and set
\[
 \Omega_{c,x_0,y_0}
 :=\{x+iy:x>x_0+c(y-y_0)^2\}.
\]
Then \(\Freq_p(\Omega_{c,x_0,y_0})=\Freq_p(\Omega_c)\), hence the formula
in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} remains valid.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The domain is the translate \(x_0+iy_0+\Omega_c\).  Translation multiplies
\(e^{\lambda z}\) by the nonzero constant
\(e^{\lambda(x_0+iy_0)}\), which does not affect Hardy membership.
\end{proof}

\section*{Connection to the source and limitations}

For the source's explicit example \(\psi_\Omega(y)=y^2\), take \(c=1\).
The theorem gives
\[
 \sigma_p(\Delta\mid_{H^p})
 =
 \{\operatorname{Re}\lambda<0\}
 \cup\{it:|t|<\pi/p\}.
\]
Thus each imaginary boundary ray contains precisely an open segment of
frequencies, and the critical endpoint is absent.  This completely answers
the membership question posed by the example in arXiv:2507.08514v1.

The theorem does not characterize arbitrary convex parabolic Koenigs domains,
nor defining functions that are merely asymptotic to a quadratic.  Extending
the result to that setting would require stable two-sided harmonic-measure
asymptotics; the exact strip quotient used here is then unavailable.

\section*{Verification and upgrade attempts}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item The strip-to-parabola identity and boundary formula were checked
symbolically and numerically by the included script
\texttt{code/check\_quadratic\_map.py}.  The script is only a regression
check; the algebra in the proof is exact.
\item The initial \(c=1\) observation was upgraded to every \(c>0\), and then
to all translated quadratic boundaries.  This changes the critical value
from \(\pi/p\) to \(\pi c/p\).
\item The positive endpoint direction was checked independently using an
interior disk-growth estimate, rather than relying only on boundary
integrability.
\item An upgrade to all asymptotically quadratic defining functions was
considered.  The proof does not supply the required harmonic-measure
stability, so no such claim is made.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Novelty check}

Before promotion, the run indexes were searched for arXiv:2507.08514,
parabolic Koenigs frequencies, quadratic defining functions, imaginary
boundary frequencies, and related frequency-completeness work.
Bounded web/arXiv searches through 17 August 2026 used the exact source title
and close variants involving \(\psi(t)=t^2\), \(x>y^2\), boundary
frequencies, and Hardy exponentials.  They found the source, its conference
abstract, and adjacent frequency-completeness literature, but no prior exact
uniformization or the frequency formula proved here.  Novelty confidence is
moderate: this is an elementary explicit-domain computation that may exist
outside the searched terminology.
