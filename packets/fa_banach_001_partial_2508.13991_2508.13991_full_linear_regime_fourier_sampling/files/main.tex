\noindent\textbf{Status.}
\texttt{candidate\_partial\_likely\_valid}.  The source's full open problem is
not solved.  We determine the exact asymptotic rate throughout the parameter
sector \(q\geq2\) or \(p\leq q\), under only the sharp compact-embedding
condition.

\section{Source problem and theorem}

Equip \(\T^d=(\mathbb R/2\pi\Z)^d\) with normalized measure.  Let
\[
 K_q^s=\{f:\|f\|_{B^s_\infty(L_q)}\leq1\}
\]
be the unit ball of the periodic Besov space, and define its Fourier sampling
numbers in \(L_p\) by
\[
 s_n^F(K_q^s)_{L_p}
 =\inf_{\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n\in\Z^d}
   \ \sup\left\{\|f\|_{L_p}:f\in K_q^s,\
   \widehat f(\xi_j)=0\ (1\leq j\leq n)\right\}.                 \tag{1}
\]
Siegel determines nearly sharp bounds under \(s/d>1-1/p\) and explicitly
leaves the remaining smoothness range open.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let \(d\in\N\), \(s>0\), and \(1\leq p,q\leq\infty\).  Suppose
\[
             \frac{s}{d}>\left(\frac1q-\frac1p\right)_+          \tag{2}
\]
and either \(p\leq q\) or \(q\geq2\).  Then, for \(n\geq2\),
\[
 s_n^F(K_q^s)_{L_p}
 \asymp n^{-s/d+(1/q-1/p)_+}.                                   \tag{3}
\]
The constants may depend on \(d,s,p,q\), but not on \(n\).
\end{theorem}

Thus (3) fills every omitted parameter point in the linear regime for which
the Besov ball is compact in \(L_p\).  In particular, it covers
\[
 \left(\frac1q-\frac1p\right)_+
 <\frac{s}{d}\leq1-\frac1p,
\]
whenever this strip is nonempty and \(q\geq2\) or \(p\leq q\).

\paragraph{Proof intuition.}
The source's low-frequency upper bound already needs only (2).  For the
matching lower bound, challenge an arbitrary set of sampled frequencies with
a block containing twice as many lattice points.  When \(p\leq q\), a single
unsampled exponential has the required size.  When \(q\geq2\) and \(p>q\),
sum all unsampled exponentials in the block.  The sum is coherent near the
origin, producing the \(L_p\) gain, while interpolation between \(L_2\) and
\(L_\infty\) controls its \(L_q\) norm.

\section{Two standard estimates}

Let \(\cT_m^d\) denote the trigonometric polynomials supported in
\(\{k\in\Z^d:|k|_\infty\leq m\}\).  We use the standard approximation
characterization
\[
 \|f\|_{B^s_\infty(L_q)}
 \asymp \|f\|_{L_q}
  +\sup_{m\geq0}(m+1)^s E_m(f)_q,                                \tag{4}
\]
where \(E_m(f)_q=\inf_{P\in\cT_m^d}\|f-P\|_{L_q}\).  We also use the
de la Vall\'ee Poussin operator \(V_m\): it is supported on
\([-m,m]^d\), is uniformly bounded on every \(L_r\), and satisfies
\[
                  \|f-V_mf\|_{L_r}\lesssim E_{m/2}(f)_r.         \tag{5}
\]

We record explicitly the inverse estimate needed for the witnesses.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:inverse}
If \(P\in\cT_{2N}^d\), \(N\geq1\), then
\[
                 \|P\|_{B^s_\infty(L_q)}
                 \leq C_{d,s,q}N^s\|P\|_{L_q}.                  \tag{6}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose an integer \(r>s\).  Bernstein's inequality for trigonometric
polynomials and the trivial difference estimate give
\[
 \omega_r(P,t)_q
 \lesssim \min\{1,(Nt)^r\}\|P\|_{L_q}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \sup_{t>0}t^{-s}\omega_r(P,t)_q
 \lesssim N^s\|P\|_{L_q}.
\]
Adding the \(L_q\) term proves (6).  The argument includes
\(q=1,\infty\).
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}}

\paragraph{Upper bound.}
First suppose \(p\leq q\).  Choose the largest even \(m\) for which
\((2m+1)^d\leq n\), sample every frequency in \([-m,m]^d\), and add
arbitrary frequencies if needed.  If \(f\in K_q^s\) vanishes on the sampled
coefficients, then \(V_mf=0\).  By normalized-measure H\"older, (5), and
(4),
\[
 \|f\|_{L_p}\leq\|f\|_{L_q}
 =\|f-V_mf\|_{L_q}
 \lesssim m^{-s}\lesssim n^{-s/d}.                              \tag{7}
\]

Now suppose \(q\geq2\) and \(p>q\).  Put
\[
                  t=s-d\left(\frac1q-\frac1p\right)>0.
\]
The standard Besov embedding
\[
 B^s_\infty(L_q)\hookrightarrow B^t_\infty(L_p)                 \tag{8}
\]
and the same low-frequency argument, now in \(L_p\), give
\[
 \|f\|_{L_p}\lesssim m^{-t}
 \lesssim n^{-s/d+1/q-1/p}.                                    \tag{9}
\]
This proves the upper half of (3).  Notice that (2), rather than the
source theorem's stronger assumption, is exactly what makes \(t>0\).

\paragraph{Lower bound: construction of an unsampled block.}
Fix any proposed set \(\Lambda\subset\Z^d\) of at most \(n\) sampled
frequencies.  Let \(N\) be the least positive integer with \(N^d\geq2n\)
and set
\[
 A_N=\{k\in\Z^d:N\leq k_1<2N,\quad 0\leq k_j<N\ (2\leq j\leq d)\}.
                                                                    \tag{10}
\]
Then \(|A_N|=N^d\), \(N\asymp n^{1/d}\), and
\[
 S=A_N\setminus\Lambda,\qquad M=|S|\geq\tfrac12N^d.              \tag{11}
\]

If \(p\leq q\), choose \(k\in S\).  The polynomial \(e^{ik\cdot x}\)
has every \(L_r\)-norm equal to one, and Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse} gives
Besov norm at most \(CN^s\).  Hence a constant multiple of
\(N^{-s}e^{ik\cdot x}\) belongs to \(K_q^s\), vanishes on \(\Lambda\),
and has \(L_p\)-norm comparable to
\[
                         N^{-s}\asymp n^{-s/d}.                  \tag{12}
\]

It remains to treat \(q\geq2\) and \(p>q\).  Define
\[
                         D_S(x)=\sum_{k\in S}e^{ik\cdot x}.       \tag{13}
\]
Orthogonality and the pointwise triangle inequality imply
\[
 \|D_S\|_{L_2}=M^{1/2},\qquad \|D_S\|_{L_\infty}=M.
\]
Interpolation therefore gives
\[
                         \|D_S\|_{L_q}\leq M^{1-1/q}.            \tag{14}
\]

For the reverse \(L_p\) estimate, choose
\(a=[4(d+1)]^{-1}\).  If \(|x_j|\leq a/N\) for every \(j\), then for
each \(k\in A_N\),
\[
 |k\cdot x|\leq(d+1)a=\tfrac14.
\]
Thus all summands in (13) have real part at least \(\cos(1/4)\), and
\[
 |D_S(x)|\geq \cos(1/4)M
 \quad\text{on a set of normalized measure }c_dN^{-d}.
\]
For \(p<\infty\) this yields
\[
                         \|D_S\|_{L_p}\gtrsim MN^{-d/p},         \tag{15}
\]
and for \(p=\infty\) the same formula holds with \(N^{-d/p}=1\),
since \(D_S(0)=M\).  Combining (11), (14), and (15),
\[
 \frac{\|D_S\|_{L_p}}{\|D_S\|_{L_q}}
 \gtrsim M^{1/q}N^{-d/p}
 \gtrsim N^{d(1/q-1/p)}.                                       \tag{16}
\]

Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse} shows that
\[
             f=\frac{D_S}{C N^s\|D_S\|_{L_q}}                  \tag{17}
\]
belongs to \(K_q^s\) for a fixed \(C\).  Its Fourier coefficients vanish
on \(\Lambda\), while (16) gives
\[
 \|f\|_{L_p}
 \gtrsim N^{-s+d(1/q-1/p)}
 \asymp n^{-s/d+1/q-1/p}.                                      \tag{18}
\]
Because \(\Lambda\) was arbitrary, (12) and (18) prove the lower half
of (3), completing the proof.

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

\paragraph{Internal checks.}
The witness is supported in \(\cT_{2N}^d\), its sampled coefficients
vanish identically, and the exponent in (18) is positive in the denominator
precisely under (2).  A supporting script tested randomly thinned
one-dimensional blocks at four scales.  Across forty trials per scale, the
minimum ratios after division by the predicted powers were \(0.633084\) for
\((q,p)=(2,4)\), \(0.857909\) for \((3,6)\), and \(0.707107\) for
\((2,\infty)\); all removed coefficients were exactly zero.  These numerical
checks are not used in the proof.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The nonlinear sector \(q<2\) and \(p>q\) remains open outside the source
range.  We do not settle the logarithmic factors in that sector, the
nonperiodic-domain problem, or the Radon-measurement analogue.

\paragraph{Novelty check and human review.}
Exact-title and close-variant searches through 9 August 2026 found the source
paper but no later statement of (3) under the full compactness condition.
The upper estimate is already latent in the source proof; the contribution is
the uniform lower-bound witness closing the asymptotics.  Human review should
check the endpoint inverse estimate in Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse} and search for
the dense-unsampled-block argument in older width literature.  Novelty is
therefore provisional, while the mathematical confidence is high.
