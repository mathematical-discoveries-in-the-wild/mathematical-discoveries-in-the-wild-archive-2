\section{Source theorem and question}

Mishra and Mohanta define a bounded domain $\Om\subset\R^n$ to be a
$(1,s,p)$-extension domain if there is a bounded extension
\[
  \Wdot^{1,p}(\Om)\longrightarrow \Wdot^{s,p}(\R^n).
\]
Their Theorem~1.6 claims that, when $p>1$ and $1/p<s<1$, such a domain is a
$(1,p)$-extension domain provided
\[
 \Hs^{,n-1+(sp-1)/p}(\partial\Om)=0.
\]
They then ask to what extent this Hausdorff-null hypothesis can be relaxed
\cite{MM}. The exact passage from source PDF page~3 is reproduced below.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The result below is stronger than a sharpness answer: outward power cusps
satisfy the displayed Hausdorff-null condition and still contradict the
conclusion for every admissible parameter triple.

\section{Proof intuition}

First-order extension with a loss of integrability can be converted into
fractional extension without that loss. More precisely, if
\[
 W^{1,p}(\Om)\longrightarrow W^{1,q}(\R^n),
 \qquad
 q>\frac{np}{n+(1-s)p},
\]
then the strict Sobolev embedding $W^{1,q}(\R^n)\subset W^{s,p}(\R^n)$
finishes the conversion. Mild outward cusps admit the first operator for $q$
arbitrarily close to $p$, but they fail the measure-density condition required
of a first-order $p$-extension domain.

The source's boundary hypothesis does not exclude these cusps. Their boundary
has finite $(n-1)$-dimensional measure, so it has zero measure in the strictly
larger dimension $n-1+s-1/p$. Thus the known integrability-loss extension
theory supplies counterexamples inside, rather than just beyond, the claimed
geometric regime.

\section{A strict first-order-to-fractional embedding}

We record the elementary version needed below.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:embedding}
Let $1<q<p<\infty$, $0<s<1$, $q\ne n$, and
\[
  \theta:=1-\frac nq+\frac np>s.
\]
Then
\[
 [v]_{W^{s,p}(\R^n)}\le C\|v\|_{W^{1,q}(\R^n)}
 \qquad(v\in W^{1,q}(\R^n)).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Delta_hv=v(\cdot+h)-v$. If $q<n$, put
$q^*=nq/(n-q)$. The inequality $\theta>0$ says $q<p<q^*$.
The translation estimate and Sobolev inequality give
\[
 \|\Delta_hv\|_q\le |h|\|\nabla v\|_q,
 \qquad
 \|\Delta_hv\|_{q^*}\le 2\|v\|_{q^*}
 \le C\|\nabla v\|_q.
\]
Interpolating to $L^p$ gives
\[
 \|\Delta_hv\|_p\le C|h|^\theta\|\nabla v\|_q
 \qquad (|h|\le1),
\]
because $1/p=\theta/q+(1-\theta)/q^*$. Also
$\|v\|_p\le C\|v\|_{W^{1,q}}$ by interpolation between $L^q$ and
$L^{q^*}$.

If $q>n$, Morrey's inequality and the $L^q$ translation estimate give
\[
 \|\Delta_hv\|_\infty
 \le C|h|^{1-n/q}\|\nabla v\|_q,
 \qquad
 \|\Delta_hv\|_q\le |h|\|\nabla v\|_q.
\]
Interpolation between $L^q$ and $L^\infty$ again yields the same
$|h|^\theta$ estimate, and $W^{1,q}\subset L^p$ follows similarly.

Finally use the difference representation
\[
 [v]_{W^{s,p}}^p
 =\int_{\R^n}\frac{\|\Delta_hv\|_p^p}{|h|^{n+sp}}\,dh.
\]
For $|h|\le1$, the radial integral is a constant multiple of
$\int_0^1 r^{(\theta-s)p-1}\,dr$, which is finite because $\theta>s$.
For $|h|>1$, use $\|\Delta_hv\|_p\le2\|v\|_p$.
\end{proof}

\section{Counterexamples for every admissible parameter}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $n\ge2$, $1<p<\infty$, and $1/p<s<1$. There is a bounded domain
$\Om\subset\R^n$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $\Om$ is a $(1,s,p)$-extension domain;
 \item $\Hs^{,n-1+(sp-1)/p}(\partial\Om)=0$;
 \item $\Om$ is not a $(1,p)$-extension domain.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently Theorem~1.6 of \cite{MM}, as stated in arXiv:2604.23598v1,
is false, and its following relaxation question has no positive answer without
changing some other hypothesis.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose $\tau>1$ sufficiently close to one that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau}
 1+(n-1)\tau<\min\{np,\ n+(1-s)p\}.
\end{equation}
Then choose $1<\beta<\tau$ and choose $q\ne n$ in the nonempty interval
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
 \frac{np}{n+(1-s)p}<q<\frac{np}{1+(n-1)\tau}.
\end{equation}
The left endpoint in \eqref{eq:q} is bigger than one because $sp>1$ and
$p>1$, while the right endpoint is less than $p$. Hence $1<q<p$.

Let $\psi(t)=t^\beta$ and take the capped outward cusp
\[
 \Om=\{(t,x)\in(0,1]\times\R^{n-1}:|x|<t^\beta\}
 \cup
 \{(t,x)\in[1,2)\times\R^{n-1}:|x|<1\}.
\]
Koskela and Zhu's cusp-extension theorem \cite[Theorem~1.2]{KZ}, reproduced
in part in the supporting crop below, applies with its auxiliary exponent
$\tau$: indeed $\psi(t)/t=t^{\beta-1}$ is nondecreasing and tends to zero,
and
\[
 \int_0^1\left(\frac{t^\tau}{\psi(t)}\right)^{n/(\tau-1)}\frac{dt}{t}
 =\int_0^1 t^{\,n(\tau-\beta)/(\tau-1)-1}\,dt<\infty.
\]
Moreover \eqref{eq:tau} gives
$[1+(n-1)\tau]/n<p$, and \eqref{eq:q} gives
$q<np/[1+(n-1)\tau]$. Thus there is a bounded linear extension
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pqextension}
 E:W^{1,p}(\Om)\longrightarrow W^{1,q}(\R^n).
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/cusp_extension_theorem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Every outward cusp is a $p$-Poincar\'e domain; moreover, on such a cusp the
nonhomogeneous $(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})$ extension property is equivalent to the
homogeneous $(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})$ property
\cite[Theorem~1.2]{KMZ}. Concretely, for $u\in\Wdot^{1,p}(\Om)$ one may
normalize by its average and set
\[
 Tu:=E(u-u_\Om)+u_\Om.
\]
The Poincar\'e inequality and \eqref{eq:pqextension} give
\[
 \|E(u-u_\Om)\|_{W^{1,q}(\R^n)}
 \le C\|u-u_\Om\|_{W^{1,p}(\Om)}
 \le C\|\nabla u\|_{L^p(\Om)}.
\]

For $v=E(u-u_\Om)$, condition \eqref{eq:q} is exactly
\[
 1-\frac nq+\frac np>s.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:embedding} therefore gives
\[
 [Tu]_{W^{s,p}(\R^n)}=[v]_{W^{s,p}(\R^n)}
 \le C\|\nabla u\|_{L^p(\Om)}.
\]
Since $Tu=u$ on $\Om$, this proves that $\Om$ is a $(1,s,p)$-extension
domain.

The lateral boundary has finite $(n-1)$-dimensional measure: its area is
bounded by a constant times
\[
 \int_0^1 t^{\beta(n-2)}
 \sqrt{1+\beta^2t^{2\beta-2}}\,dt<\infty,
\]
and the capped part is Lipschitz. Hence
$\Hs^{n-1}(\partial\Om)<\infty$. Since
$(sp-1)/p=s-1/p>0$, this implies
\[
 \Hs^{,n-1+(sp-1)/p}(\partial\Om)=0.
\]

It remains to exclude first-order $p$ extension. For small $r>0$, the point
$z_r=(r,0)$ belongs to $\Om$, and
\[
 |B(z_r,r)\cap\Om|
 \le C\int_0^{2r}t^{\beta(n-1)}\,dt
 \le C r^{1+\beta(n-1)}=o(r^n),
\]
because $\beta>1$. Thus $\Om$ fails the measure-density condition. Every
bounded $(1,p)$-extension domain satisfies that condition
\cite[Theorem~6]{HKT}; hence $\Om$ is not a $(1,p)$-extension domain.
\end{proof}

\section{A fully numerical planar instance}

Take
\[
 n=p=2,\qquad s=\frac34,\qquad
 \tau=\frac54,\qquad \beta=\frac65,\qquad q=\frac53.
\]
Then
\[
 \int_0^1
 \left(\frac{t^{5/4}}{t^{6/5}}\right)^{2/(5/4-1)}\frac{dt}{t}
 =\int_0^1t^{-3/5}\,dt=\frac52,
\]
and
\[
 \frac{1+\tau}{2}=\frac98<2,
 \qquad
 q=\frac53<\frac{4}{1+\tau}=\frac{16}{9}.
\]
Thus $W^{1,2}(\Om)\to W^{1,5/3}(\R^2)$. The translation exponent in
Lemma~\ref{lem:embedding} is
\[
 1-\frac{2}{5/3}+\frac22=\frac45>\frac34,
\]
so $\Om$ is a $(1,3/4,2)$-extension domain. Its boundary has finite length,
and therefore $\Hs^{5/4}(\partial\Om)=0$, but its density at the cusp tip
decays like $r^{11/5}/r^2=r^{1/5}$.

\section{Scope, novelty, and review recommendation}

This packet challenges a theorem in a very recent preprint, so the novelty
claim is necessarily provisional. The four run indexes and packet/attempt
trees were searched for arXiv:2604.23598, fixed-exponent fractional extension,
and outward cusps. Bounded arXiv/web searches through 13 August 2026 used the
exact source id and title, ``Theorem 1.6'', ``correction'', ``error'',
``counterexample'', ``$(1,s,p)$-extension'', and ``outward cusp''. They found
the source and the background papers used here, but no correction or prior
statement of this counterexample.

No numerical computation is used. Expert review should first check the exact
parameter substitution into \cite[Theorem~1.2]{KZ}, the homogeneous
normalization justified by \cite[Theorem~1.2]{KMZ}, and the translation proof
of Lemma~\ref{lem:embedding}. If these pass, the source's proof of Theorem~1.6
must contain a substantive failure; locating that failure is logically
separate from the counterexample.
