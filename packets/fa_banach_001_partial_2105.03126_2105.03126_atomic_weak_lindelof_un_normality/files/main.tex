\title[Normal un-topology from weak Lindelofness]{Normal un-topology on weakly Lindelof atomic Banach lattices}
\author{fa\_banach\_001, agent\_lane\_04}
\date{11 August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Kandi\'c and Vavpeti\v{c} ask for conditions ensuring that the unbounded norm
topology of a Banach lattice is normal, and state that the problem is open even
for atomic order-continuous Banach lattices.  We prove that every atomic
order-continuous Banach lattice which is Lindel\"of in its weak topology has a
Lindel\"of, paracompact, normal un-topology.  Thus the conclusion holds for
weakly compactly generated lattices, including nonseparable
$c_0(\Gamma)$.  A product argument also covers a complementary sum of an
atomic KB-band and a weakly Lindel\"of atomic order-continuous band; this gives
examples outside both classes previously handled in the source.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\section*{Status and source question}

This is a candidate substantial partial answer to Problem~7.3 of
Kandi\'c--Vavpeti\v{c}~\cite{KV}.  On PDF page~26 the source asks:
\begin{quote}
Under which conditions on a Banach lattice $X$ is the topological space
$(X,\tun)$ normal?
\end{quote}
It then says that the answer remains unknown even for order-continuous atomic
Banach lattices.  The source proves normality when $\tun$ is metrizable and
when $X$ is an atomic KB-space.

\section*{Definitions}

For a Banach lattice $X$, the unbounded norm topology $\tun$ is the Hausdorff
locally solid topology whose convergence is
\[
 x_\alpha\longrightarrow x\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bigl\|\,|x_\alpha-x|\wedge u\,\bigr\|\longrightarrow0
 \quad(u\in X_+).
\]
A space is \emph{Lindel\"of} when every open cover has a countable subcover.
A Banach space is \emph{weakly Lindel\"of} when it is Lindel\"of for
$\sigma(X,X^*)$.  A Banach space is weakly compactly generated (WCG) when a
weakly compact subset has norm-dense linear span.  Every WCG Banach space is
weakly Lindel\"of~\cite{Orihuela}; the wider WLD class is weakly Lindel\"of as
well~\cite{AM}.

\section*{Idea of the proof}

Atomic order continuity turns the un-topology into the topology of pointwise
convergence on the atomic coordinates.  Those coordinates are bounded linear
functionals, so the un-topology is coarser than the weak topology.  This simple
comparison transfers Lindel\"ofness from the weak topology to the
un-topology; complete regularity then gives normality.

The mixed-band extension uses a second mechanism.  An atomic KB-band is
$\sigma$-compact in un-topology, while the complementary weakly Lindel\"of
band is Lindel\"of in un-topology.  Since compact times Lindel\"of is
Lindel\"of, the product of these two factors is Lindel\"of.  This does not
settle the general atomic order-continuous case: an arbitrary atomic Banach
sequence ideal need be neither KB nor weakly Lindel\"of and need not split
into bands of these types.

\section*{The weakly Lindel\"of criterion}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:comparison}
If $X$ is an atomic order-continuous Banach lattice, then
\[
 \tun\subseteq\sigma(X,X^*).
\]
Equivalently, the identity map from $(X,\sigma(X,X^*))$ to $(X,\tun)$ is
continuous.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose one normalized representative $a$ from each atomic band and let $f_a$
be its coordinate functional, so the corresponding band projection is
$P_a=f_a\otimes a$.  Kandi\'c--Marabeh--Troitsky
\cite[Corollary~4.14]{KMT} prove that, for every net $(x_\alpha)$,
\[
 x_\alpha\xrightarrow{\mathrm{un}}x
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 f_a(x_\alpha)\longrightarrow f_a(x)\quad\hbox{for every atom }a.
\]
Hence $\tun$ is precisely the initial topology generated by the family
$(f_a)$.  Each $f_a$ belongs to $X^*$, while the weak topology is generated by
all of $X^*$.  The asserted inclusion follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:wl}
Let $X$ be an atomic order-continuous Banach lattice.  If
$(X,\sigma(X,X^*))$ is Lindel\"of, then $(X,\tun)$ is Lindel\"of,
paracompact, and normal.  In particular, this holds if $X$ is WCG (or WLD).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison}, every $\tun$-open set is weakly open.  Thus
every $\tun$-open cover is a weakly open cover and has a countable subcover.
So $(X,\tun)$ is Lindel\"of.

The un-topology is a Hausdorff locally solid vector topology, hence completely
regular; see, for example, the discussion before Theorem~7.1 in~\cite{KV}.
A regular Lindel\"of space is paracompact, and every Hausdorff paracompact
space is normal~\cite{Dugundji}.  The final assertion follows from the
classical weak-Lindel\"of theorem for WCG spaces~\cite{Orihuela} (and the
corresponding fact for WLD spaces~\cite{AM}).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:c0}
For every set $\Gamma$, $c_0(\Gamma)$ has a normal un-topology.  If $\Gamma$
is uncountable, this supplies an atomic order-continuous example whose
un-topology is nonmetrizable and which is not a KB-space.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The lattice $c_0(\Gamma)$ is atomic and order continuous.  It is WCG: if
$(e_\gamma)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}$ is its unit-vector family, then
\[
 K=\{0\}\cup\{e_\gamma:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
\]
is weakly compact.  Indeed, $K$ has its one-point-compactification topology,
because every member of $c_0(\Gamma)^*=\ell_1(\Gamma)$ is non-negligible on
only finitely many $e_\gamma$ at any fixed threshold.  The linear span of $K$
is norm dense.  Theorem~\ref{thm:wl} applies.

If $\Gamma$ is uncountable, no countable family can generate a norm-dense
ideal: the union of the supports of that family is countable.  The
metrizability criterion quoted in~\cite[Section~7]{KV} therefore shows that
$\tun$ is nonmetrizable.  Finally, for distinct $\gamma_n$, the increasing
partial sums $\sum_{j\le n}e_{\gamma_j}$ are norm bounded but not norm
convergent, so $c_0(\Gamma)$ is not a KB-space.
\end{proof}

\section*{A mixed-band upgrade}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mixed}
Let $X=Y\oplus Z$ be a decomposition into complementary projection bands.
Assume that $Y$ is an atomic KB-space and that $Z$ is atomic, order
continuous, and weakly Lindel\"of.  Then $(X,\tun)$ is Lindel\"of,
paracompact, and normal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a finite band decomposition, un-convergence in $X$ is equivalent to
un-convergence of both band coordinates; this is the finite case of
\cite[Theorem~4.12]{KMT}.  Hence
\[
 (X,\tun)\cong (Y,\tun)\times(Z,\tun).
\]
By the atomic KB theorem~\cite[Theorem~7.5]{KMT}, every closed norm ball of
$Y$ is un-compact.  Therefore $Y=\bigcup_{n\ge1}nB_Y$ is $\sigma$-compact in
$\tun$.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:wl}, $(Z,\tun)$ is Lindel\"of.

Write $Y=\bigcup_n K_n$ with each $K_n$ compact.  Each
$K_n\times Z$ is Lindel\"of because a compact space times a Lindel\"of space
is Lindel\"of.  The countable union
$Y\times Z=\bigcup_n(K_n\times Z)$ is therefore Lindel\"of.  Complete
regularity again yields paracompactness and normality.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:mixed}
If $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$ are uncountable, then
\[
 X=\ell_1(\Gamma)\oplus c_0(\Delta)
\]
(with any Banach-lattice norm realizing the displayed complementary bands)
has a normal, nonmetrizable un-topology, although $X$ is neither a KB-space
nor weakly Lindel\"of in its weak topology.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first band is atomic KB, and the second is atomic, order continuous and
WCG, so Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed} applies.  The $c_0(\Delta)$ band prevents $X$
from being KB.  Nonmetrizability follows as in Corollary~\ref{cor:c0}, since a
countable family has only countable total atomic support.

For completeness, $\ell_1(\Gamma)$ is not weakly Lindel\"of when $\Gamma$ is
uncountable.  Its unit vectors form an uncountable weakly closed discrete
subspace: coordinate functionals isolate the points, and the constant-one
functional together with finitely many coordinates separates any other
vector from the family.  A closed subspace of a regular Lindel\"of space is
Lindel\"of, while a regular Lindel\"of space cannot contain an uncountable
closed discrete subspace.  Thus the weak topology of $X$ cannot be
Lindel\"of.
\end{proof}

\section*{Scope, verification, and novelty}

The result supplies new sufficient conditions and explicit cases beyond the
two classes in the source, but it is not a characterization.  The full atomic
order-continuous problem would follow from a normality theorem for arbitrary
solid Banach sequence ideals in a Sigma-product, or from a decomposition into
the two band types above.  Neither bridge is established here; normality is
not hereditary to arbitrary subspaces of the ambient Sigma-product.
