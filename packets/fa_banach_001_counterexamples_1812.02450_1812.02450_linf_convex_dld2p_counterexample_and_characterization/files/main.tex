\section{Source problem}

The source is T.~A.~Abrahamsen, R.~Haller, V.~Lima, and K.~Pirk,
\emph{Delta- and Daugavet-points in Banach spaces}, arXiv:1812.02450
\cite{AHLP}.  Problem~1 on printed page~21 asks:
\begin{quote}
Given $X\oplus_NY$ with a $\Delta$-point/a Daugavet point/the convex
DLD2P, does $X$ have a $\Delta$-point/a Daugavet point/the convex DLD2P?
\end{quote}
This packet settles the convex-DLD2P branch negatively and, together with a
recent positive result, gives the exact characterization of the absolute norms
for which factor inheritance always holds.

\section{Definitions and input results}

All spaces are real.  For a Banach space $W$, write $B_W$ and $S_W$ for its
closed unit ball and unit sphere, and write $\Delta_W\subseteq S_W$ for its set
of $\Delta$-points.  Thus $w\in S_W$ is a $\Delta$-point precisely when, for
every slice $S$ of $B_W$ containing $w$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there is
$u\in S$ such that $\|w-u\|\geq 2-\varepsilon$.  The space $W$ has the
convex-DLD2P when
\[
 B_W=\clco(\Delta_W).
\]

For $k>1$, Haller, Pirk, and Veeorg call $w\in S_W$ a
$\Delta_k$-point if every inclusion
$w\in S(B_W,w^*,\alpha)$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ admit
\[
 u\in S(B_W,w^*,k\alpha),\qquad \|w-u\|\geq 2-\varepsilon.
\]
Every $\Delta$-point is a $\Delta_k$-point for every $k>1$.

We use the following three results from \cite[Theorem~4.1, Example~4.3,
Proposition~4.4]{HPV}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item If $(ax,by)$ is a $\Delta$-point of $X\oplus_NY$, where
      $a,b\geq0$, $N(a,b)=1$, $x\in S_X$, $y\in S_Y$, and $b\neq1$,
      then $x$ is a $\Delta$-point of $X$.
\item If $x\in S_X$ is not a $\Delta$-point and $y\in S_Y$ is a
      $\Delta$-point, then for every $k>1$
      \[
      \left((1-k^{-1})x,k^{-1}y\right)
      \quad\hbox{is a $\Delta_k$-point of }X\oplus_1Y.
      \]
\item If $p,q>1$, $p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1$, $x$ is a $\Delta_p$-point of
      $X$, and $y$ is a $\Delta_q$-point of $Y$, then $(x,y)$ is a
      $\Delta$-point of $X\oplus_\infty Y$.
\end{enumerate}

Finally, $C[0,1]$ has the Daugavet property, so every point of its unit sphere
is a Daugavet point and hence a $\Delta$-point; see, for example,
\cite[Section~2]{KSSW}.

\section{Idea of the proof}

Let $D=C[0,1]$ and first form $E=\R\oplus_1D$.  The scalar factor has no
$\Delta$-points.  Consequently the only genuine $\Delta$-points of $E$ lie on
the $D$-axis, so $E$ fails the convex-DLD2P.  Nevertheless, Example~4.3 of
\cite{HPV} supplies many weaker $\Delta_k$-points in $E$: they approach both
ends of the missing scalar axis as $k\to\infty$.  Together with the genuine
$\Delta$-points on the $D$-axis, these $\Delta_k$-points convexly generate all
of $B_E$.

Now take $Z=E\oplus_\infty D$.  Every unit vector of the second copy of $D$
is a $\Delta_q$-point for every $q>1$.  Pairing it with any one of the above
$\Delta_k$-points of $E$, with conjugate exponents, produces a genuine
$\Delta$-point of $Z$ by Proposition~4.4 of \cite{HPV}.  A product-of-convex-
hulls identity then shows that these points convexly generate the entire ball
$B_Z=B_E\times B_D$.

\section{Counterexample and characterization}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let
\[
 D=C([0,1];\R),\qquad E=\R\oplus_1D,
 \qquad Z=E\oplus_\infty D.
\]
Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Delta_E=\{0\}\times S_D$, and consequently $E$ does not have the
      convex-DLD2P;
\item $Z$ has the convex-DLD2P.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the convex-DLD2P of an $\ell_\infty$-sum need not pass to its factors.

Moreover, for an absolute normalized norm $N$ on $\R^2$, the following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] for all real Banach spaces $X,Y$, convex-DLD2P of $X\oplus_NY$
           implies convex-DLD2P of both $X$ and $Y$;
\item[(b)] $N$ is not the $\ell_\infty$-norm.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every $d\in S_D$ is a $\Delta$-point.  The standard component-to-sum slice
argument therefore shows that $(0,d)$ is a $\Delta$-point of
$E=\R\oplus_1D$.

Conversely, suppose $(r,d)\in S_E$ is a $\Delta$-point and $r\neq0$.  Put
$a=|r|$ and $b=\|d\|$, so $a+b=1$ and $b<1$.  Writing
$r=a\,\operatorname{sgn}(r)$ (and, when $d\neq0$, $d=b(d/\|d\|)$), input
result~1 implies that $\operatorname{sgn}(r)$ is a $\Delta$-point of $\R$.
This is impossible: a sufficiently small slice of $B_\R=[-1,1]$ containing
$1$ (respectively $-1$) contains no point almost at distance two from it.
The same invocation allows an arbitrary unit vector in the zero-coordinate
when $d=0$.  Hence
\[
 \Delta_E=\{0\}\times S_D.
\]
In particular, the continuous functional $(r,d)\mapsto r$ vanishes on
$\clco(\Delta_E)$ but not on $(1,0)\in B_E$, so $E$ fails the convex-DLD2P.

For $k>1$, $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$, and $d\in S_D$, set
\[
 e_{k,\sigma,d}:=\left((1-k^{-1})\sigma,k^{-1}d\right)\in S_E
\]
and define
\[
 A:=\Delta_E\cup
 \{e_{k,\sigma,d}:k>1,\ \sigma\in\{-1,1\},\ d\in S_D\}.
\]
By input result~2, $e_{k,\sigma,d}$ is a $\Delta_k$-point of $E$.  Every
member of $\Delta_E$ is a $\Delta_p$-point for every $p>1$.  Thus every
$e\in A$ is a $\Delta_{p(e)}$-point for at least one $p(e)>1$.

Fix $d_0\in S_D$.  As $k\to\infty$,
\[
 e_{k,\sigma,d_0}\longrightarrow(\sigma,0),
\]
so both ends of the scalar axis belong to $\overline A$.  The whole $D$-axis
$\{0\}\times S_D$ already lies in $A$.  Since the unit ball of an
$\ell_1$-sum is the closed convex hull of the unit balls on its two coordinate
axes, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:denseA}
 B_E=\clco(A).
\end{equation}

Let $e\in A$ and $v\in S_D$.  Choose $p=p(e)>1$ such that $e$ is a
$\Delta_p$-point, and let $q=p/(p-1)$.  Since $v$ is a genuine $\Delta$-point,
it is a $\Delta_q$-point.  Input result~3 gives
\[
 (e,v)\in\Delta_Z.
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:productsubset}
 A\times S_D\subseteq\Delta_Z.
\end{equation}

For any nonempty subsets $A_0\subseteq B_{W_0}$ and
$A_1\subseteq B_{W_1}$ one has
\[
 \clco(A_0\times A_1)=\clco(A_0)\times\clco(A_1).
\]
Indeed, one inclusion is immediate, while finite convex combinations
$x=\sum_i\alpha_i a_i$ and $y=\sum_j\beta_j b_j$ satisfy
\[
 (x,y)=\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\beta_j(a_i,b_j),
\]
and passage to closures gives the reverse inclusion.  Using
\eqref{eq:denseA}, $B_D=\clco(S_D)$, and \eqref{eq:productsubset}, we obtain
\[
 B_Z=B_E\times B_D
 =\clco(A\times S_D)
 \subseteq\clco(\Delta_Z)
 \subseteq B_Z.
\]
Thus $Z$ has the convex-DLD2P, proving the counterexample.

For the final equivalence, Guerrero-Viu and Markowicz prove in
\cite[Proposition~2]{GM} that whenever $N\neq\|\cdot\|_\infty$, convex-DLD2P
passes from $X\oplus_NY$ to both factors.  This is (b)$\Rightarrow$(a).
The example above shows that (a) fails for $N=\|\cdot\|_\infty$, proving the
converse.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification notes}

\begin{itemize}
\item The two possible hidden boundary cases in the description of
      $\Delta_E$ were checked: $d=0$ is covered by Theorem~4.1 of \cite{HPV},
      and $r=0$ gives genuine $\Delta$-points by the slice characterization.
\item Every point used in $A$ lies on $S_E$.  The limits
      $e_{k,\sigma,d_0}\to(\sigma,0)$ recover precisely the missing scalar
      endpoints.
\item The conjugate exponent attached to $e_{k,\sigma,d}$ is
      $q=k/(k-1)>1$.  A genuine $\Delta$-point is a $\Delta_q$-point for this
      (possibly $k$-dependent) value of $q$.
\item The product convex-hull equality is justified by the double weights
      $\alpha_i\beta_j$; no synchronized decompositions are assumed.
\item No numerical or computer-assisted step is used.
\end{itemize}

\section{Novelty search and limitations}

A bounded search was performed on 9 August 2026 in the run's registry,
solution, attempt, and proof-gap indexes; arXiv/source text for 1812.02450,
2001.06197, and 2607.01875; and web/arXiv queries combining
``convex-DLD2P'', ``$\ell_\infty$'', ``absolute sum'', ``factor'',
``counterexample'', and ``$\Delta_k$''.  No prior statement of this
counterexample or of the resulting if-and-only-if characterization was found.
Most significantly, the recent paper \cite{GM}, submitted 2 July 2026, proves
the positive result only for $N\neq\ell_\infty$ and explicitly identifies the
$\ell_\infty$-sum as the exceptional unresolved case.

This is nevertheless a bounded novelty check, not a claim of exhaustive
priority.  The packet settles only the convex-DLD2P branch of source
Problem~1; its questions about existence of a $\Delta$-point or a Daugavet
point are separate.  Human review should focus on the use of the three
$\Delta_k$ results from \cite{HPV} and the closure identity in the last step.
