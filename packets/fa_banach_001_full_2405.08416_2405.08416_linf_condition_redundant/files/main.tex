\section{Source question and answer}

B\'enyi, Li, Oh, and Torres prove a compact $T(1)$ theorem \`a la Stein
for a linear singular integral operator $T:\mathcal S(\R^d)\to
\mathcal S'(\R^d)$ with a standard Calder\'on--Zygmund kernel
\cite[Theorem~1]{BLOT}.  Their condition (i) says that, for some bump order
$M$ and every normalized bump $\phi$ of that order,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bump-decay}
 R^{-d/2}\bigl(\|T(\phi^{x,R})\|_2+
 \|T^*(\phi^{x,R})\|_2\bigr)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{as } |x|+R+R^{-1}\longrightarrow\infty,
\end{equation}
where $\phi^{x,R}(y)=\phi((y-x)/R)$.  Their condition (ii) is a
precompactness requirement in $\CMO$ for images of weak-star convergent
$L^\infty$ sequences.  Remark~3.6 asks whether (ii) can be removed.

\section{Proof intuition}

The source proof fixes a large cutoff $\phi_R$ and proves
$T(\phi_R)\in\CMO$, but some of its estimates are not uniform as
$R\to\infty$; condition (ii) is then used to extract a strongly convergent
subsequence.  The key is to avoid a global cutoff.

For each test cube $Q$, cut off the constant function at a scale
$A\ell(Q)$ centered on $Q$.  The local part is controlled directly by the
normalized-bump decay, while kernel H\"older regularity makes the far part
nearly constant on $Q$.  Quantitatively,
\[
 \MO_Q(T(1))\lesssim
 A^{d/2}\varepsilon_T(x_Q,A\ell(Q))+A^{-\delta}.
\]
Letting $A$ tend to infinity sufficiently slowly makes both terms vanish,
uniformly for small cubes, large cubes, and fixed cubes translated to
infinity.  Thus $T(1),T^*(1)\in\CMO$ without condition (ii).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $T$ be a linear singular integral operator with a standard
Calder\'on--Zygmund kernel, as in Theorem~1 of \cite{BLOT}.  Then $T$
extends compactly to $L^2(\R^d)$ if and only if
\eqref{eq:bump-decay} holds.  In particular, condition (ii) of that theorem
is redundant.
\end{theorem}

\section{The localized cutoff estimate}

For a locally integrable $f$, write
\[
 \MO_Q(f)=\frac1{|Q|}\int_Q|f-f_Q|,
 \qquad f_Q=\frac1{|Q|}\int_Q f.
\]
Choose once and for all $\chi\in C_c^\infty(B(0,1))$ equal to one on
$B(0,1/2)$.  Multiplying $\chi$ by a fixed constant makes it a normalized
bump of order $M$; by linearity, \eqref{eq:bump-decay} therefore applies to
$\chi$ itself.  Set
\[
 \varepsilon_T(x,R)=R^{-d/2}\|T(\chi^{x,R})\|_2,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_{T^*}(x,R)=R^{-d/2}\|T^*(\chi^{x,R})\|_2.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local}
Let $Q$ have center $x_Q$ and side length $\ell$.  If $A\geq A_0(d)$,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local}
 \MO_Q(T(1))
 \lesssim A^{d/2}\varepsilon_T(x_Q,A\ell)+A^{-\delta},
\end{equation}
where $\delta$ is the H\"older exponent of the kernel.  The analogous
estimate holds for $T^*(1)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $\eta_Q=\chi^{x_Q,A\ell}$.  For $A_0$ large enough, $\eta_Q=1$ on a
fixed dilate of $Q$.  The standard distributional definition of $T(1)$
shows that on $Q$, modulo an additive constant,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomp}
 T(1)(x)=T(\eta_Q)(x)+F_Q(x),
\end{equation}
where one may take
\[
 F_Q(x)=\int_{\R^d}
 [K(x,y)-K(x_Q,y)](1-\eta_Q(y))\,dy.
\]
This integral is absolutely convergent: its integrand is supported where
$|y-x_Q|\gtrsim A\ell$, and the kernel difference decays like
$|x-x_Q|^\delta|y-x_Q|^{-d-\delta}$.  Consequently,
\[
 \sup_{x\in Q}|F_Q(x)|\lesssim A^{-\delta}.
\]
For the local part, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \frac1{|Q|}\int_Q|T(\eta_Q)|
 \leq |Q|^{-1/2}\|T(\eta_Q)\|_2
 \lesssim A^{d/2}\varepsilon_T(x_Q,A\ell).
\]
Subtracting averages in \eqref{eq:decomp} proves \eqref{eq:local}.  Replacing
$K$ by its transposed kernel gives the assertion for $T^*$.
\end{proof}

The representation \eqref{eq:decomp} can equivalently be verified against
mean-zero test functions supported in $Q$; subtracting $K(x_Q,y)$ makes the
far-field integral absolutely convergent and removes the irrelevant
additive constant.

\section{Condition (i) forces the two CMO conditions}

Define the decreasing tail modulus
\[
 \Omega(s)=\sup_{|x|+R+R^{-1}\geq s}
 \bigl(\varepsilon_T(x,R)+\varepsilon_{T^*}(x,R)\bigr).
\]
For the fixed cutoff $\chi$, condition \eqref{eq:bump-decay} says precisely
that $\Omega(s)\to0$.  Put
$\omega(s)=\max\{\Omega(s),s^{-1}\}$ and
\[
 \alpha=1-\frac{d}{2(d+1)}>0.
\]

We verify the three vanishing-oscillation conditions in the
Bourdaud--Uchiyama characterization quoted as Lemma~2.3 in \cite{BLOT}.

\paragraph{Small cubes.}
Let $u=\ell^{-1}$ and
\[
 A(u)=\min\{u^{1/2},\omega(u^{1/2})^{-1/(d+1)}\}.
\]
Then $A(u)\to\infty$, while $A(u)\ell\leq u^{-1/2}\to0$.  Hence
$(A\ell)^{-1}\geq u^{1/2}$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:local} gives, uniformly in
the center of $Q$,
\[
 \MO_Q(T(1)) + \MO_Q(T^*(1))
 \lesssim \omega(u^{1/2})^\alpha+A(u)^{-\delta}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the small-cube quantities $\Gamma_1$ vanish.

\paragraph{Large cubes.}
For each cube put $u=\ell(Q)$ and set
\[
 A(u)=\min\{u^{1/2},\omega(u)^{-1/(d+1)}\}.
\]
The functions $\omega$ and $A$ are respectively decreasing and increasing.
Also $A(u)\to\infty$, and $A\ell\geq u$, so for every threshold $U$ the
same estimate gives
\[
 \sup_{\ell(Q)\geq U}
 \bigl(\MO_Q(T(1))+\MO_Q(T^*(1))\bigr)
 \lesssim \omega(U)^\alpha+A(U)^{-\delta}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus $\Gamma_2=0$.

\paragraph{Translations to infinity.}
Fix a cube $Q_0$.  For $Q=Q_0+x$ and $u=|x|$ sufficiently large, use the
choice
\[
 A(u)=\min\{u^{1/2},\omega(u/2)^{-1/(d+1)}\}.
\]
Its center has magnitude at least $u/2$, so
\[
 \MO_{Q_0+x}(T(1))+\MO_{Q_0+x}(T^*(1))
 \lesssim \omega(u/2)^\alpha+A(u)^{-\delta}\longrightarrow0
\]
and every far-translation quantity $\Gamma_3^{Q_0}$ vanishes.

Condition (i) implies the bounded Stein bump estimate, so the source proof
already gives $T(1),T^*(1)\in\BMO$ \cite[Section~4]{BLOT}.  The three
limits above and the quoted characterization therefore yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cmo}
 T(1),T^*(1)\in\CMO.
\end{equation}

\section{Completion of the proof}

Necessity of \eqref{eq:bump-decay} for compact $T$ is established before
Theorem~1 in \cite{BLOT}: normalized $L^2$ bumps converge weakly to zero in
the extreme position/scale regimes, and compact operators map weakly
convergent sequences to norm-convergent sequences.

Conversely, assume \eqref{eq:bump-decay}.  The source proves directly by
Cauchy--Schwarz that this condition implies the weak compactness property
\cite[Section~4]{BLOT}.  Equation \eqref{eq:cmo} supplies the other
hypothesis in the compact $T(1)$ theorem quoted there from
Mitkovski--Stockdale.  Consequently $T$ is compact on $L^2$.  This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Verification and novelty notes}

The principal review points are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the local distributional identity \eqref{eq:decomp}, including its
harmless additive constant;
\item the factor $A^{d/2}$ in \eqref{eq:local};
\item the slow-dilation choices in the three CMO regimes;
\item the exact direction in which the source compact $T(1)$ theorem is
applied.
\end{enumerate}
No numerical experiment is used: the proof is analytic.

A bounded official-arXiv search through 11 August 2026 used arXiv id
2405.08416, the exact title, and combinations of ``$L^\infty$ condition'',
``removed'', ``normalized bump'', and ``CMO''.  It found the source and
adjacent compact $T(1)$ literature, but no later paper removing condition
(ii).  This packet settles only the linear theorem under the source's
standard Calder\'on--Zygmund hypotheses; the bilinear analogue in its
Remark~3.7 is not addressed.  Expert review should focus first on
Lemma~\ref{lem:local} and the uniform tail modulus $\Omega$.
