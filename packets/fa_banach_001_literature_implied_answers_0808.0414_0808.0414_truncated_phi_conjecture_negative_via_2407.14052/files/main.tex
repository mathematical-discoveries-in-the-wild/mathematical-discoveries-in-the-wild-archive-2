\section*{Status and the exact 2008 conjecture}

This is a \texttt{literature\_implied\_answer (full negative)} packet.
Maz'ya's Theorem~1 \cite{Mazya} proves a subcritical estimate for the
Newtonian potential $u=\Gamma*f$, where $f\in L^1(\Rn)$ has mean zero.
Immediately afterward, on PDF page~2, he conjectures that at
$p=n/(n-1)$ one still has
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{R>0}\left|\int_{|x|<R}\Phi(\nabla u(x))\,dx\right|
 \leq C\|f\|_1^p                                                   \tag{M}
\end{equation}
whenever $\Phi$ is positively $p$-homogeneous, Lipschitz on the sphere,
and
\begin{equation}
 \int_{S^{n-1}}\Phi(\theta)\,d\sigma(\theta)=0.                    \tag{S}
\end{equation}
The weight in the source's formula is one at the critical exponent.

\end{center}

Stolyarov later proved the corresponding \emph{whole-space} inequality
\cite{WholeSpace}.  His subsequent bounded-domain theorem
\cite{Domains} shows that a stronger hemisphere cancellation is necessary
at a boundary.  Applying its necessity construction to a ball, and retaining
the zero-mean correction used in the same paper's half-space proof, gives an
explicit counterexample to (M).

\section*{Full negative answer}

Let $\Gamma$ be the fundamental solution of $-\Delta$ and write
\[
 K(x)=\nabla\Gamma(x)=c_n\frac{x}{|x|^n},\qquad c_n\ne0.
\]

\paragraph{Idea of the proof.}
A ball cuts a concentrated fundamental-solution profile into a hemisphere at
its boundary.  Choose an odd $\Phi$ with nonzero hemisphere mean, concentrate
unit mass there, and subtract fixed unit mass to enforce $\int f=0$.  The first
piece grows like $\log m$; weak endpoint control makes the correction $O(1)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:counterexample}
For every $n\ge2$, put $p=n/(n-1)$ and
\[
 \Phi(v)=|v|^{p-1}v_1.
\]
Then $\Phi$ satisfies every hypothesis and the spherical cancellation (S),
but there are $f_m\in C_c^\infty(\Rn)$ such that
\[
 \int f_m=0,\qquad \|f_m\|_1\le2,
 \qquad
 \left|\int_{B(0,1)}\Phi\bigl(K*f_m(x)\bigr)\,dx\right|\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Consequently (M), and hence the source conjecture, is false in every
dimension $n\ge2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The function $\Phi$ is positively $p$-homogeneous and smooth on the unit
sphere.  It is odd, so (S) holds.  Fix nonnegative
$\rho,g\in C_c^\infty(\Rn)$ of integral one, with
$\operatorname{supp}\rho\subset B(0,1/2)$ and
$\operatorname{supp}g\subset B(0,1/4)$.  At the boundary point
$z=e_1$ of $B(0,1)$ the inward normal is $\xi=-e_1$.  Set
\[
 z_m=(1-2/m)e_1,\qquad
 \rho_m(x)=m^n\rho\bigl(m(x-z_m)\bigr),\qquad f_m=\rho_m-g.
\]
For all large $m$, $\rho_m$ is supported in the unit ball; moreover
$\int f_m=0$ and $\|f_m\|_1\le2$.

First omit the compensator and write $A_m=K*\rho_m$.  Critical scaling gives
\begin{equation}
 \int_{B(0,1)}\Phi(A_m(x))\,dx
 =\int_{m(B(0,1)-z_m)}\Phi(K*\rho(y))\,dy.                           \tag{1}
\end{equation}
The asymptotic calculation in Section~2 of \cite{Domains} is
\begin{equation}
 K*\rho(y)=K(y)+O(|y|^{-n}),\qquad
 \Phi(K*\rho(y))=\Phi(K(y))+O(|y|^{-n-1}).                          \tag{2}
\end{equation}
The error in (2) is integrable at infinity.  On the sphere,
\[
 \Phi(K(\theta))=c_n|c_n|^{p-1}\theta_1,
\]
and therefore the inward-hemisphere integral
\begin{equation}
 H:=\int_{\theta\cdot\xi>0}\Phi(K(\theta))\,d\sigma(\theta)
 =c_n|c_n|^{p-1}\int_{\theta_1<0}\theta_1\,d\sigma(\theta)\ne0.     \tag{3}
\end{equation}

For completeness, the logarithmic part of the necessity argument is as
follows.  Put
\[
 M=\int_{S^{n-1}}|\Phi(K(\theta))|\,d\sigma(\theta).
\]
On radii
$4<r<m^\gamma$, the rescaled ball in (1) converges to the inward
half-space, and polar integration of the $(-n)$-homogeneous function
$\Phi\circ K$ gives
\[
 H\gamma\log m+O(1).
\]
The remaining radii contribute at most
$M(1-\gamma)\log m+O(1)$ in absolute value.  Choosing
$\gamma<1$ sufficiently close to one so that
$|H|\gamma>M(1-\gamma)$, and using (2), proves
\begin{equation}
 \left|\int_{B(0,1)}\Phi(A_m)\right|\longrightarrow\infty.          \tag{4}
\end{equation}
This is precisely the boundary-delta argument proving necessity of the
hemisphere condition in \cite[Section~2]{Domains}.

It remains to verify that imposing the source's mean-zero constraint does
not remove (4).  Put $G=K*g$, which is bounded on $B(0,1)$.  Positive
homogeneity and spherical Lipschitz continuity give the standard estimate
\[
 |\Phi(a-b)-\Phi(a)|
 \le C_\Phi\bigl(|a|^{p-1}|b|+|b|^p\bigr).                          \tag{5}
\]
The endpoint weak Hardy--Littlewood--Sobolev estimate yields
$\|A_m\|_{L^{p,\infty}}\le C\|\rho_m\|_1=C$, uniformly in $m$.
Since $p-1<p$ and the ball has finite measure,
\[
 \sup_m\int_{B(0,1)}|A_m|^{p-1}<\infty.
\]
Integrating (5), with $a=A_m$ and $b=G$, therefore shows that
\[
 \left|\int_{B(0,1)}\Phi(K*f_m)-
                 \int_{B(0,1)}\Phi(A_m)\right|=O(1).               \tag{6}
\]
The divergence (4) survives in (6).  Since the supremum in (M) includes
$R=1$, the theorem follows.
\end{proof}

\section*{What the later literature does and does not say}

The whole-space result of \cite{WholeSpace} is not contradicted.  It controls
$\int_{\Rn}\Phi(K*f)$ under full-sphere cancellation, whereas (M) asks for
uniform control of every centered-ball truncation.  Nonlinear integrals have
no domain monotonicity, a distinction emphasized explicitly in
\cite{Domains}.

Theorem~1.2 of \cite{Domains} says that on a bounded smooth domain the
correct condition is the pair of hemisphere cancellations
\[
 \int_{\theta\cdot\xi>0}\Phi(K(\theta))\,d\sigma=0,
 \qquad
 \int_{\theta\cdot\xi>0}\Phi(-K(\theta))\,d\sigma=0
 \quad(\xi\in S^{n-1}).
\]
Condition (S) only cancels opposite hemispheres together.  The explicit odd
$\Phi$ above makes that gap visible: its full-sphere mean vanishes, while
each relevant hemisphere mean does not.

The supporting paper does not state ``Maz'ya's 2008 supremum conjecture is
false.''  It restates the conjecture in the later whole-space form and then
studies domains.  The identification of its boundary necessity construction
with the original uniform-truncation statement, including the zero-mean
compensator, is the literature implication established in this packet.
