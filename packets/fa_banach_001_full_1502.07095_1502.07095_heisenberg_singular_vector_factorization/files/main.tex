\section*{Source problem and answer}

K\v{r}i\v{z}ka and Somberg study the Heisenberg parabolic of
$\mathfrak{sl}(n+2,\mathbb C)$.  In their notation the opposite nilradical
$\bar{\mathfrak u}$ has basis $f_1,\ldots,f_n,g_1,\ldots,g_n,c$, with
\[
 [f_i,g_j]=-\delta_{ij}c,\qquad c\text{ central}.
\]
On p.~35, after verifying the cases $a=1,2$, they conjecture that whenever
\[
 \lambda=\lambda_1\omega_1+\lambda_2\omega_{n+1},\qquad
 \mu=(\lambda_1-a)\omega_1+(\lambda_2-a)\omega_{n+1},
 \qquad \lambda_1+\lambda_2+n=a-1,
\]
the scalar singular vector factors as
\begin{equation}
 \varphi(v_\mu)=
 \prod_{j=0}^{a-1}\left(
   \sum_{i=1}^n f_i g_i+
   \frac{\lambda_1-\lambda_2+n-a+2j+1}{2}c
 \right)v_\lambda .                                                    \tag{F}
\end{equation}
They write that they do not know a direct proof.  The answer is affirmative.
The proof below derives (F) directly from their polynomial formula for the
singular vector and the Weyl-to-normal ordering transform.

\section*{Proof intuition}

Put $A=\sum_i f_i g_i$.  The weight relation makes the $j$th conjectured
factor simply $A+(j-\lambda_2)c$, so the right side of (F) is a rising
factorial in $A/c-\lambda_2$.

The source constructs the same singular vector on the commutative Fourier
side as $T_{a,a}^{\lambda}(q^a)$, where $q=\sum_i x_i y_i$ and the
coefficients of $T$ have an explicit generating function.  The only apparent
difficulty is converting this commutative polynomial back through
symmetrization.  Exponential generating functions remove it: the exact
symmetrization of $e^{uq}$ is
\[
 \beta(e^{uq})=(1-cu/2)^{-n}
 \left(\frac{1+cu/2}{1-cu/2}\right)^{A/c}.
\]
After inserting the source coefficient generating function, the desired
vector becomes one coefficient of a two-binomial product.  A fractional
linear change of variable turns that coefficient into
$\binom{A/c-\lambda_2+a-1}{a}$, which is precisely the conjectured product.

\section*{Statement}

\begin{theorem}[Factorization conjecture (3.140)]
Let $n>2$, $a\in\mathbb N$, and
$\lambda_1+\lambda_2+n=a-1$.  Under the conventions and the normalization of
K\v{r}i\v{z}ka--Somberg, the singular vector represented on the Fourier side
by
\[
 P_\lambda=T_{a,a}^{\lambda}(q^a)
\]
has the exact PBW factorization
\[
 \varphi(v_\mu)=
 \prod_{j=0}^{a-1}\bigl(A+(j-\lambda_2)c\bigr)v_\lambda,
 \qquad A=\sum_{i=1}^n f_i g_i.
\]
Equivalently, formula \emph{(F)} holds for every parameter allowed by the
source statement.
\end{theorem}

\section*{Proof}

We use formal power series only as coefficient-extraction devices; every
coefficient used below is a polynomial identity in $A$ and $c$.

\subsection*{1. The source polynomial and the Fourier sign}

Let $\beta:S(\bar{\mathfrak u})\to U(\bar{\mathfrak u})$ be symmetrization.
Theorem 2.4 and the algebraic Fourier transform of the source imply, for a
homogeneous commutative monomial $p$ of ordinary degree $d$,
\[
 (\tau\circ\Phi_{\lambda+\rho})(\beta(p)v_\lambda)=(-1)^d p.
\]
The source defines
\[
 T_{a,a}^{\lambda}
 =\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{\alpha_k}{2^k}(z\partial_q)^k,
 \qquad
 g(w):=\sum_{k\ge0}\alpha_k w^k
 =(1+w)^{a-\lambda_1-1}(1-w)^{a-\lambda_2-1}.
\]
Writing $\fall{a}{k}=a!/(a-k)!$, we therefore obtain the normalized preimage
of $P_\lambda$ as
\begin{equation}
 V_a=\beta\left(
   \sum_{k=0}^a\frac{(-1)^k\alpha_k}{2^k}\fall{a}{k}
      c^kq^{a-k}
 \right)v_\lambda.                                                     \tag{1}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $z^kq^{a-k}$ has ordinary degree $2a-k$, hence Fourier sign
$(-1)^k$.  The leading term in (1) is normalized to $A^a$, so no undetermined
scalar remains.

The weight relation gives
\begin{equation}
 g(w)=(1+w)^{\lambda_2+n}(1-w)^{\lambda_1+n}.                           \tag{2}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2. Exact symmetrization of the radial exponential}

Let $N$ denote normal ordering: all $f_i$ are put to the left of all $g_i$.
Set $q=\sum_i f_i g_i$ in the symmetric algebra and
$\Delta=\sum_i\partial_{f_i}\partial_{g_i}$.  The central commutator and the
Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula give the standard ordering identity
\begin{equation}
 N^{-1}\beta(p)=\exp\left(\frac c2\Delta\right)p.                       \tag{3}
\end{equation}
For completeness, (3) follows by applying both sides to
$e^{\sum_i(\xi_i f_i+\eta_i g_i)}$: the discrepancy between the Weyl and
normal exponentials is $e^{(c/2)\sum_i\xi_i\eta_i}$.

Solving the elementary heat equation produced by (3) gives
\[
 \exp\left(\frac c2\Delta\right)e^{uq}
 =(1-cu/2)^{-n}\exp\left(\frac{u}{1-cu/2}q\right).
\]
For $A_i=f_i g_i$, the relations imply
\[
 f_i^m g_i^m=\prod_{r=0}^{m-1}(A_i-rc),
 \qquad
 N(e^{vq})=\prod_i(1+cv)^{A_i/c}=(1+cv)^{A/c}.
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \beta(e^{uq})=(1-cu/2)^{-n}
 \left(\frac{1+cu/2}{1-cu/2}\right)^{A/c}.}                            \tag{4}
\end{equation}
The temporary localization at $c$ in the exponent is harmless: each
coefficient of (4), and the final identity, lies in $\mathbb C[A,c]$.

\subsection*{3. Coefficient extraction}

The expression inside $\beta$ in (1) is
\[
 a![u^a],g(-cu/2)e^{uq}.
\]
Combining (2) and (4) therefore yields
\begin{align}
 V_a/v_\lambda
 &=a![u^a]
   (1-cu/2)^{\lambda_2-A/c}
   (1+cu/2)^{\lambda_1+n+A/c}.                                        \tag{5}
\end{align}
Put
\[
 X=A/c,\qquad Y=X-\lambda_2,\qquad t=cu.
\]
Since $\lambda_1+n=a-1-\lambda_2$, equation (5) becomes
\begin{equation}
 V_a/v_\lambda
 =c^a a![t^a](1-t/2)^{-Y}(1+t/2)^{a-1+Y}.                              \tag{6}
\end{equation}

It remains to evaluate one universal coefficient.  Make the formal change
\[
 v=\frac{t}{1-t/2},\qquad t=\frac{v}{1+v/2}.
\]
Then
\begin{align*}
 (1-t/2)^{-Y}(1+t/2)^{a-1+Y}
   &=(1+v)^{a-1+Y}(1+v/2)^{-(a-1)},\
 \frac{dt}{t^{a+1}}
   &=(1+v/2)^{a-1}\frac{dv}{v^{a+1}}.
\end{align*}
Taking formal residues, the two extra factors cancel and give
\begin{equation}
 [t^a](1-t/2)^{-Y}(1+t/2)^{a-1+Y}
 =[v^a](1+v)^{a-1+Y}
 =\binom{a-1+Y}{a}=\frac{(Y)_a}{a!}.                                  \tag{7}
\end{equation}
Equations (6)--(7) now imply
\[
 V_a/v_\lambda=c^a(Y)_a
 =\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}\bigl(A+(j-\lambda_2)c\bigr).                       \tag{8}
\]
Finally, the source coefficient simplifies as
\[
 \frac{\lambda_1-\lambda_2+n-a+2j+1}{2}=j-\lambda_2
\]
under $a=\lambda_1+\lambda_2+n+1$.  Thus (8) is exactly (F), completing
the proof. \qed

\section*{Verification notes}

The script \texttt{code/verify\_factorization.py} performs two independent
exact checks.  First it verifies the universal coefficient identity (7)
symbolically for $1\le a\le10$.  Second, for several rational parameter
choices, it reconstructs the source coefficients $\alpha_k$, applies (4)
term by term to (1), and compares the result with the product in (8).  All
checks pass over exact rational polynomial arithmetic.

The most sensitive review points are the signs.  They are fixed by
$[f_i,g_i]=-c$, which gives $\beta(f_i g_i)=f_i g_i+c/2$, and by the fact
that $z^kq^{a-k}$ has ordinary degree $2a-k$ under Theorem 2.4, producing
the $(-1)^k$ in (1).  The formulas reproduce the source's displayed cases
$a=1$ and $a=2$.

\section*{Novelty boundary and human-review recommendation}

the exact title and authors, exact phrases around ``direct proof'' and
``factorization property,'' and formula/core-keyword variants.  The run's
cheap indexes were also searched.  The arXiv source and the authors' public
bibliography were checked; a nearby later paper by Pand\v{z}i\'c and Somberg
on $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-actions was inspected and treats different
commutative-nilradical examples.  No later proof of (3.140) was located.
This is a bounded novelty check, not a guarantee of priority.

Recommendation: review as a likely valid full solution.  A human verifier
should focus first on the source-to-(1) Fourier sign and then on the normal
ordering identity (3); after those two conventions are confirmed, the rest
is a formal coefficient identity.
