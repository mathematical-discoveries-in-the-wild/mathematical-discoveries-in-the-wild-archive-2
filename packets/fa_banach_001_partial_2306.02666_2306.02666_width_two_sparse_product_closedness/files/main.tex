\title{Exact closedness of a width-two sparse matrix-product image}
\author{Candidate partial result for arXiv:2306.02666}
\date{11 August 2026}

\begin{document}

\noindent\textbf{Status.} \texttt{candidate\_partial\_likely\_valid}. This
packet proves an exact hidden-width-two subcase of the efficient closedness
problem and gives an exact Bernoulli probability for the resulting
nonclosedness certificate. It does not solve the unrestricted-width problem.

\begin{abstract}
For two coordinate rectangles $S_i\times T_i$, consider the set of sums of one
rank-one matrix on each rectangle. We give a necessary and sufficient
combinatorial criterion for this set to be closed. Besides the familiar local
LU degeneration, exactly two transposed ``coalescing direction'' degenerations
occur. The criterion is linear-time after the supports are read. As an upgrade,
we derive the exact probability of nonclosedness for independent Bernoulli
supports. Through Theorem 3.1 of the source paper, every nonclosed pattern
certifies a sparse ReLU architecture for which some finite regression problem
has no optimum.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source question}

The source is Q.-T. Le, E. Riccietti, and R. Gribonval, \emph{Does a sparse
ReLU network training problem always admit an optimum?}, arXiv:2306.02666v2
\cite{source}. Section 3.3 asks for more efficient algorithms deciding the
closedness of the sparse matrix-product image $\mathcal L_{\mathbf I}$ and
leaves the general problem open. Question 3.2 asks:

\begin{quote}
If the supports of the weight matrices are randomly sampled from a
distribution, what is the probability that the corresponding training problem
potentially admits no optimal solutions?
\end{quote}

The source then gives a polynomial sufficient detector for one-hidden-layer
networks, explicitly noting that it may have false negatives. Figure

\section{Setup}

For $S\subseteq[m]$ and $T\subseteq[n]$, define the coordinate rank-one cone
\[
 \mathcal C(S,T)=\{uv^{\mathsf T}:u\in\R^m,\ v\in\R^n,
       \supp(u)\subseteq S,\ \supp(v)\subseteq T\}.
\]
For two hidden components, the source's two-factor linear image is precisely
\[
 \mathcal L=\mathcal C(S_1,T_1)+\mathcal C(S_2,T_2).
 \tag{1}\label{eq:L}
\]
Put
\[
 S_0=S_1\cap S_2,\quad T_0=T_1\cap T_2,\quad
 S_i^\circ=S_i\setminus S_{3-i},\quad
 T_i^\circ=T_i\setminus T_{3-i}.
\]

\section{Proof intuition}

Any escaping factor sequence must cancel its leading rank-one term inside the
common rectangle $S_0\times T_0$. There are only three ways for the finite
first-order residue to become impossible to express by two limiting rank-one
terms. One rectangle can carry an exclusive row and column, producing the
usual $2\times2$ LU anti-diagonal. Alternatively, the two rank-one column
directions can coalesce while two exclusive columns pin both of them to the
same limiting line, leaving a transverse common column; the transpose gives
the third case.

If none of those mechanisms is available, a short set-theoretic case split
forces the rectangles to be disjoint, cross-nested, or one-dimensional in the
only noncomparable direction. The cross-nested image is exactly a determinantal
set described by three rank inequalities, so it is closed. This also explains
why the classification yields an immediate decision algorithm. The original
general-width problem remains open because three or more cones can fill one
another's boundary points or support genuinely multi-term cancellations.

\section{Exact classification}

\begin{theorem}[Hidden width two]\label{thm:classification}
The set $\mathcal L$ in \eqref{eq:L} is nonclosed if and only if $S_0$ and
$T_0$ are nonempty and at least one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $S_1^\circ,T_1^\circ$ are nonempty, or
      $S_2^\circ,T_2^\circ$ are nonempty;
\item $|S_0|\ge2$ and both $T_1^\circ,T_2^\circ$ are nonempty;
\item $|T_0|\ge2$ and both $S_1^\circ,S_2^\circ$ are nonempty.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, closedness can be decided in $O(m+n)$ time from bit-vector
representations of the four supports.
\end{theorem}

We first record the elementary closed-case lemma used in the converse.

\begin{lemma}[Cross-nested block lemma]\label{lem:block}
Suppose $S_2\subseteq S_1$ and $T_1\subseteq T_2$. Put
$A=S_1\setminus S_2$, $C=S_2$, $B=T_2\setminus T_1$, and $D=T_1$.
After ordering rows as $A,C$ and columns as $B,D$, the image $\mathcal L$ is
exactly the set of block matrices
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}0&Y\\X&Z\end{pmatrix}
 \quad\text{such that}\quad
 \rank M\le2,\quad\rank X\le1,\quad\rank Y\le1. \tag{2}\label{eq:block}
\]
In particular, $\mathcal L$ is closed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every element of $\mathcal L$ is the sum of a rank-one matrix supported on
$(A\cup C)\times D$ and one supported on $C\times(B\cup D)$, so it satisfies
\eqref{eq:block}.

Conversely, first suppose $X$ and $Y$ are nonzero. The columns over $B$ span a
line $V$ contained in the $C$ coordinate subspace. Because $Y\ne0$, some
column over $D$ is not in $V$. Since $\rank M\le2$, this column together with
$V$ spans the entire column space. Hence every $D$-column is the sum of a
multiple of one fixed vector in $A\cup C$ and a multiple of the generator of
$V$, while every $B$-column uses only the latter. These are the required two
rank-one summands.

If $X\ne0$ and $Y=0$, the same argument applies with all column vectors in the
$C$ coordinate subspace: the $B$-columns span one line and the total column
space has dimension at most two. The case $X=0$, $Y\ne0$ follows by applying
this argument to the transpose. If $X=Y=0$, then $Z$ has rank at most two and
is the sum of two rank-one matrices supported on the common rectangle
$C\times D$. Thus every matrix in \eqref{eq:block} belongs to $\mathcal L$.
The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:block} is cut out by support equations and
minors, hence is closed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:classification}]
Assume first that (i) holds for the first rectangle. Choose
$a\in S_1^\circ$, $c\in S_0$, $b\in T_1^\circ$, and $d\in T_0$. For $t>0$,
\[
 (t e_c+e_a)(e_d+t^{-1}e_b)^{\mathsf T}
       +(-t e_c)e_d^{\mathsf T}
 \longrightarrow e_c e_b^{\mathsf T}+e_a e_d^{\mathsf T}=:M.
 \tag{3}
\]
The two summands on the left belong to the two cones. On rows $a,c$ and columns
$b,d$, the second cone can contribute only at $(c,d)$, while a rank-one first
summand cannot match the anti-diagonal entries of $M$. Thus $M\notin\mathcal L$.
The other half of (i) is symmetric.

For (ii), choose distinct $p,q\in S_0$, columns
$b\in T_1^\circ$, $e\in T_2^\circ$, and $d\in T_0$. Then
\[
 e_p(e_b-t e_d)^{\mathsf T}
 +(e_p+t^{-1}e_q)(e_e+t e_d)^{\mathsf T}
 \longrightarrow
 e_p e_b^{\mathsf T}+e_p e_e^{\mathsf T}+e_q e_d^{\mathsf T}=:N.
 \tag{4}
\]
If $N$ were in $\mathcal L$, its exclusive $b$-column would force the first
rank-one column direction to be $\R e_p$, and its exclusive $e$-column would
force the second direction to be $\R e_p$. Every common column would then lie
in $\R e_p$, contradicting the $d$-column $e_q$. Hence $N\notin\mathcal L$.
Case (iii) is the transpose of this construction. These sequences prove
nonclosedness in all listed cases.

Conversely, suppose $S_0,T_0$ are nonempty and none of (i)--(iii) holds. If
both $S_1^\circ$ and $S_2^\circ$ are nonempty, failure of (i) forces
$T_1=T_2$; failure of (iii) then forces $|T_0|=1$. In that case both cones lie
in a one-dimensional column coordinate space, and their sum is a linear
subspace. The transposed argument treats the case in which both
$T_1^\circ,T_2^\circ$ are nonempty.

In every remaining case, after possibly interchanging indices, the supports
are cross-nested: $S_2\subseteq S_1$ and $T_1\subseteq T_2$. Lemma
\ref{lem:block} shows that their sum is closed. If $S_0$ or $T_0$ is empty,
the two cones occupy disjoint coordinate sets, so coordinate projection
separates two convergent summands and their sum is closed. This exhausts all
possibilities and proves the converse.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A minimal false negative of the local-LU detector]
Let $S_1=S_2=\{1,2\}$, $T_1=\{1,3\}$, and $T_2=\{2,3\}$. The source detector
cannot find three entries exclusive to one rank-one rectangle around a shared
corner, because the row supports coincide. Nevertheless (ii) applies, and
\[
 e_1(e_1-t e_3)^{\mathsf T}
 +(e_1+t^{-1}e_2)(e_2+t e_3)^{\mathsf T}
 \longrightarrow e_1e_1^{\mathsf T}+e_1e_2^{\mathsf T}+e_2e_3^{\mathsf T}
\]
is a missing boundary point. Thus the sufficient detector's admitted false
negatives already occur with two hidden components.
\end{example}

\section{Upgrade: exact Bernoulli probability}

Let every row membership in $S_1,S_2$ be independent Bernoulli($\alpha$), and
every column membership in $T_1,T_2$ independent Bernoulli($\beta$), with the
row and column masks independent. For a row, let
$\rho=(\rho_{11},\rho_{10},\rho_{01},\rho_{00})$ count the four membership
types across the $m$ rows. Define $\tau$ analogously across the $n$ columns.
For $p\in[0,1]$ and $\kappa_\bullet$ summing to $k$, put
\[
 w_{k,p}(\kappa)=\frac{k!}{\prod_z\kappa_z!}
 (p^2)^{\kappa_{11}}[p(1-p)]^{\kappa_{10}+\kappa_{01}}
 [(1-p)^2]^{\kappa_{00}}.
\]
Let $\Phi(\rho,\tau)$ be the Boolean condition
\[
\begin{split}
 &\rho_{11}\ge1,\quad\tau_{11}\ge1,\quad\text{and}\\
 &[(\rho_{10}\ge1\wedge\tau_{10}\ge1)
 \vee(\rho_{01}\ge1\wedge\tau_{01}\ge1)
 \vee(\rho_{11}\ge2\wedge\tau_{10}\ge1\wedge\tau_{01}\ge1)\\
 &\hspace{38mm}\vee(\tau_{11}\ge2\wedge\rho_{10}\ge1\wedge\rho_{01}\ge1)].
\end{split}\tag{5}\label{eq:phi}
\]

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:probability}
Under this model, the exact probability that $\mathcal L$ is nonclosed is
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \sum_{\rho_{11}+\rho_{10}+\rho_{01}+\rho_{00}=m}
 \sum_{\tau_{11}+\tau_{10}+\tau_{01}+\tau_{00}=n}
 w_{m,\alpha}(\rho)w_{n,\beta}(\tau)\mathbf1_{\Phi(\rho,\tau)}.}
 \tag{6}\label{eq:probability}
\]
It can be evaluated in $O(m+n)$ arithmetic operations by retaining only the
threshold states $0,1,\ge2$ for common counts and $0,\ge1$ for exclusive counts.
Every mask counted in \eqref{eq:probability} is potentially ill-posed for a suitable
finite regression problem in the sense of the source paper.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The four row categories have probabilities
$\alpha^2,\alpha(1-\alpha),\alpha(1-\alpha),(1-\alpha)^2$, so $\rho$ has the
displayed multinomial mass; the column count has the analogous independent
law with parameter $\beta$. Condition \eqref{eq:phi} is exactly Theorem
\ref{thm:classification} written in terms of these counts, proving the sum.
The finite-state dynamic program follows because no larger count is
distinguished by $\Phi$. Finally, Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 3.1 of the source
turn nonclosedness of $\mathcal L$ into nonclosedness of a finite realization
set and hence a regression instance whose infimum is not attained.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, novelty, and limitations}

The accompanying script exhaustively checks that the theorem's nonclosed and
proved-closed cases partition all support patterns for $m,n\le4$. It also
checks formula \eqref{eq:probability} against direct weighted enumeration for
$m,n\le3$ and 27 dimension/parameter choices. These computations verify the
case split and bookkeeping, not the proof.

A bounded primary-source search on 11 August 2026 used the exact source title,
the phrases ``rank-one supports closedness'' and ``sum of two rank-one
cones'', and the source's cited fixed-support factorization paper
arXiv:2112.00386 \cite{fsmf}. It found the source's sufficient local-LU
criterion but no exact hidden-width-two converse or the two coalescing-direction
degenerations. Novelty confidence is moderate because this elementary result
may exist under other terminology.

The main limitation is structural: for hidden width at least three, a third
cone can fill a two-cone boundary point or join a multi-term cancellation.
Also, $\mathcal L$ closed does not imply that every ReLU realization set is
closed. Corollary \ref{cor:probability} therefore gives the exact probability
of the source's linear nonclosedness certificate at width two, but only a lower
bound for the probability of all potentially ill-posed ReLU masks.
