# Permutation-symmetric Ogata approximation fails for three Pauli observables

**Status:** candidate counterexample, likely valid, requiring human review.

**Source:** David Herrera, *A Projection Characterization and Symmetry
Bootstrap for Elements of a von Neumann Algebra that are Nearby Commuting
Elements*, arXiv:2412.20795v1 (2024), Example 2.5, PDF pages 14--15.

The source asks whether the commuting approximants in Ogata's theorem can
preserve the symmetries of the macroscopic observables when there are at least
three observables.  It specifically records that the tensor-permutation
symmetries are generated by the transposition `(1 2)` and the long cycle
`(1 2 ... N)`.

This packet gives a negative answer to that simultaneous
tensor-permutation-symmetry version already for the three Pauli observables on
qubits.  If a commuting self-adjoint triple preserves those two generating
symmetries, it is at maximum-coordinate operator-norm distance at least `1/6`
from the macroscopic Pauli triple, for every `N >= 2`.

The obstruction is elementary.  Permutation invariance makes the approximants
reduce `Sym^N(C^2)`.  On this subspace the macroscopic observables form the
normalized spin-`N/2` triple.  Its Pauli localizer has inertia `(N+2,N)`, while
the invertible localizer of a commuting triple on an `(N+1)`-dimensional space
has balanced inertia `(N+1,N+1)`.  The localizer gap is exactly `1/2`, yielding
the quantitative lower bound `1/6`.

**Scope limitation:** the result rules out simultaneous preservation of the
two noncommuting permutation generators.  It does not rule out preserving one
fixed permutation symmetry at a time, nor does it settle the transpose,
conjugation, real, symplectic, or signed symmetry patterns in the source.
