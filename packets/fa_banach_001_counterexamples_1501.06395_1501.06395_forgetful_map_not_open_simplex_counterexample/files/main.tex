\section{Source question}

Goldbring and Sinclair define $\Xc_n$ to retain the quantifier-free
multiplicative structure of an $n$-dimensional unital operator subspace, while
$\OS_n$ remembers only its operator-space structure. They prove that the
forgetful map
\[
G:\Xc_n\longrightarrow\OS_n
\]
is weak--weak and strong--strong continuous. Question 6.7 on printed page 17
asks whether $G$ is open for every $n$ \cite{GS}.

  local copy \texttt{source\_paper.pdf}.}

The source defines the $\Xc_n$ weak and strong metrics through quantities
$\gamma_m(\phi)$ that compare the norms of all noncommutative
$*$-polynomials of degree at most $m$ under a unital linear bijection $\phi$.
We denote the resulting metrics by $\gamw$ and $\gams$. The weak metric is a
weighted sum of the degree-$m$ discrepancies; the strong metric takes their
supremum.

\section{Proof intuition}

Start with the $n$-point simplex, whose affine function system is the whole
algebra $\C^n$. Add one vertex just beyond a face but let it tend to the
midpoint of an edge. Affine matrix-valued functions cannot distinguish this
new vertex in the limit, so after forgetting multiplication the resulting
spaces converge even in completely bounded distance.

Multiplication does distinguish it. The coordinate functions on $\C^n$ are
orthogonal projections. If representatives of the perturbed operator spaces
converged multiplicatively, their coordinate images would have all cross
ternary products $xx^*y$ tending to zero. The ternary envelope makes this
conclusion independent of the chosen C$^*$-cover. In the limit, Banach--Stone
rigidity forces the coordinates to be a phased permutation. At the extra
vertex, two coordinates then have modulus $1/2$, leaving a cross ternary
product of modulus $1/8$.

\section{The simplex family}

Fix $n\geq3$ and put $E_0=\C^n$ with its canonical C$^*$-algebra operator
space structure. For $0<\eps<1$, set
\[
a_\eps:=\frac{1+\eps}{2},\qquad
L_\eps(z):=a_\eps z_1+a_\eps z_2-\eps z_3,
\]
and define
\[
J_\eps:\C^n\longrightarrow\C^{n+1},\qquad
J_\eps(z)=(z_1,\ldots,z_n,L_\eps(z)),
\qquad S_\eps:=J_\eps(\C^n).
\]
Since $2a_\eps-\eps=1$, $S_\eps$ contains the unit. It is self-adjoint and
separates the $n+1$ coordinates, hence $C^*(S_\eps)=\C^{n+1}$.

Geometrically, $S_\eps$ is the space of affine functions on
\[
V_\eps=\{e_1,\ldots,e_n,w_\eps\},\qquad
w_\eps=a_\eps e_1+a_\eps e_2-\eps e_3,
\]
inside the real affine hyperplane $\sum_i t_i=1$. All $n+1$ points are exposed
vertices: $e_1,e_2$ maximize their own coordinates, $e_3$ maximizes the third
coordinate, $e_i$ for $i\geq4$ maximizes the $i$th coordinate, and $w_\eps$
uniquely minimizes the third coordinate. Thus every point is a peak point for
$S_\eps$. Consequently its Shilov boundary is all of $V_\eps$, and its
C$^*$- and ternary envelopes are $\C^{n+1}$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform matrix-level convergence]\label{lem:convergence}
For every matrix level $k$,
\[
\|J_\eps^{(k)}\|\leq1+2\eps,
\qquad \|(J_\eps^{-1})^{(k)}\|\leq1.
\]
Therefore $G(S_\eps)\to G(E_0)$ in the strong topology of $\OS_n$, and hence
also in the weak topology.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $Z=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)\in M_k(\C^n)$, put
$M=\max_i\|Z_i\|$. Then
\[
\|J_\eps^{(k)}(Z)\|
=\max\left\{M,\|a_\eps Z_1+a_\eps Z_2-\eps Z_3\|\right\}.
\]
The second entry is at most
$(2a_\eps+\eps)M=(1+2\eps)M$, while the first $n$ coordinates show that
$\|J_\eps^{(k)}(Z)\|\geq M$.
\end{proof}

\section{The cover-independent obstruction}

We use the following standard consequence of Hamana's ternary-envelope
universal property \cite{Hamana}. It is stated explicitly to isolate the only
operator-space structure theorem used in the proof.

\begin{lemma}[Ternary-envelope contraction]\label{lem:ternary}
Let $F$ be a unital operator subspace of a C$^*$-algebra and let
$u:F\to S_\eps$ be a surjective complete isometry; $u$ need not be unital.
Then for all $x,y,z\in F$,
\[
\|u(x)u(y)^*u(z)\|_{\C^{n+1}}
\leq \|xy^*z\|_{C^*(F)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because $F$ contains the unit, the TRO generated by $F$ inside $C^*(F)$ is
all of $C^*(F)$. The universal property gives a surjective ternary morphism
from this TRO cover onto the ternary envelope $T(F)$. The complete isometry
$u$ induces a ternary isomorphism $T(F)\cong T(S_\eps)=\C^{n+1}$ carrying the
canonical image of $x$ to $u(x)$. Ternary morphisms preserve $xy^*z$ and are
contractive, which gives the inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[No multiplicative lifts]\label{lem:obstruction}
Suppose $\eps_j\downarrow0$ and $F_j\in\Xc_n$ satisfy
$G(F_j)=G(S_{\eps_j})$. Then neither
$\gamw(F_j,E_0)\to0$ nor $\gams(F_j,E_0)\to0$ is possible.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Either convergence would provide unital linear bijections
$\phi_j:E_0\to F_j$ for which
\[
\gamma_1(\phi_j)\longrightarrow0,
\qquad \gamma_3(\phi_j)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Choose surjective complete isometries $u_j:F_j\to S_{\eps_j}$, which need not
be unital, and define
\[
A_j:=J_{\eps_j}^{-1}u_j\phi_j:\C^n\to\C^n.
\]
Testing $\gamma_1$ on the polynomial $p(X)=X$ gives, uniformly in $z$,
\[
(1-o(1))\|z\|_\infty
\leq\|u_j\phi_j(z)\|
\leq(1+o(1))\|z\|_\infty.
\]
Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:convergence}, this yields
\[
\frac{1-o(1)}{1+2\eps_j}\|z\|_\infty
\leq\|A_jz\|_\infty
\leq(1+o(1))\|z\|_\infty.
\]
After taking a subsequence, the finite-dimensional maps $A_j$ converge to a
surjective linear isometry $A:\ell_\infty^n\to\ell_\infty^n$. By the
finite-dimensional Banach--Stone theorem, there are a permutation $\sigma$
and unimodular scalars $\lambda_r$ such that
\[
Ae_r=\lambda_r e_{\sigma(r)}\qquad(1\leq r\leq n).
\]

Choose distinct $r,s$ with $\sigma(r)=1$ and $\sigma(s)=2$, and put
$f_{j,t}=u_j\phi_j(e_t)=J_{\eps_j}(A_je_t)$. At the extra coordinate,
\[
f_{j,t}(n+1)=a_{\eps_j}(A_je_t)_1
            +a_{\eps_j}(A_je_t)_2
            -\eps_j(A_je_t)_3.
\]
It follows that
\[
f_{j,r}(n+1)\longrightarrow\frac{\lambda_r}{2},
\qquad
f_{j,s}(n+1)\longrightarrow\frac{\lambda_s}{2},
\]
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eighth}
\liminf_j\|f_{j,r}f_{j,r}^*f_{j,s}\|_{\C^{n+1}}
\geq\frac18.
\end{equation}

On the other hand, $e_re_r^*e_s=0$ in $E_0=\C^n$. Testing
$\gamma_3(\phi_j)$ on the degree-three polynomial
$p(X,Y)=XX^*Y$ gives
\[
\|\phi_j(e_r)\phi_j(e_r)^*\phi_j(e_s)\|_{C^*(F_j)}
\leq\gamma_3(\phi_j)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:ternary} contradicts \eqref{eq:eighth}.
\end{proof}

\section{Negative answer to Question 6.7}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $n\geq3$, the forgetful map
$G:\Xc_n\to\OS_n$ is not weak-open and is not strong-open. In particular,
Question 6.7 has a negative answer under either topology introduced in the
source paper.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We give the weak argument; the strong argument is identical. Suppose $G$ were
weak-open at $E_0$. For each $k$, let
\[
U_k=\{F\in\Xc_n:\gamw(F,E_0)<1/k\}.
\]
Then $G(U_k)$ is a neighborhood of $G(E_0)$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:convergence}, $G(S_{1/m})\to G(E_0)$, so one may choose
$m_k\geq k$ and $F_k\in U_k$ such that
\[
G(F_k)=G(S_{1/m_k}).
\]
Thus $1/m_k\to0$ and $\gamw(F_k,E_0)\to0$, contradicting
Lemma~\ref{lem:obstruction}. Replacing $\gamw$ by $\gams$ proves failure of
strong openness.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty bounds}

The argument is non-computational. The following points were checked
independently in the proof:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the coefficient sum in $L_\eps$ is $1$, so the construction is unital;
\item the matrix-level distortion is bounded by $1+2\eps$ uniformly in $k$;
\item all $n+1$ evaluation points are exposed, so no coordinate disappears
      in the Shilov/ternary envelope;
\item arbitrary, possibly nonunital complete isometries are handled by the
      ternary envelope rather than by an unjustified multiplicative extension;
\item the extra-coordinate obstruction is exactly $(1/2)^2(1/2)=1/8$;
\item nested open balls convert openness into the forbidden sequence of
      multiplicative lifts.
\end{enumerate}

The construction covers every $n\geq3$. It does not determine openness for
$n=1,2$, which is not needed to refute the statement ``open for each $n$.''
