\section{Original conjecture}

M. Rudelson and R. Vershynin,
\emph{The Littlewood--Offord Problem and invertibility of random matrices},
arXiv:math/0703503~\cite{source}, discuss random $n\times n$ matrices with
independent symmetric $\{-1,1\}$ entries in Section~1.1.  On arXiv PDF
page~3 they call estimation of the singularity probability a longstanding
open problem and record the conjectured optimal base $1/2+o(1)$.

Equivalently, if $M_n$ has independent random-sign entries, the conjecture is
\begin{question}
\[
 \mathbb P\{M_n\text{ is singular}\}
   =\bigl(\tfrac12+o_n(1)\bigr)^n.
\]
\end{question}
Their paper improves and develops least-singular-value estimates, but it does
not prove this sharp singularity asymptotic.

\section{Separate later answer}

K. Tikhomirov,
\emph{Singularity of random Bernoulli matrices}, arXiv:1812.09016,
published in \emph{Annals of Mathematics} \textbf{191} (2020),
593--634~\cite{answerpaper}, states in its abstract and equation~(1) on
arXiv PDF page~1:

\begin{answer}
For an $n\times n$ matrix $M_n$ with independent $\{-1,1\}$ entries,
\[
 \mathbb P\{M_n\text{ is singular}\}
   =\bigl(\tfrac12+o_n(1)\bigr)^n.
\]
\end{answer}

The paper explicitly says that this confirms the conjecture.  Its Theorem~A
is formulated more generally for Bernoulli($p$) zero--one matrices and a
rank-one shift.  Taking $p=1/2$ and shift $s=-1/2$ produces a matrix with
independent entries $\pm 1/2$; multiplying by $2$ preserves singularity and
gives the random-sign model above.

Thus the precise open conjecture recorded in arXiv:math/0703503 is completely
answered by a separate later paper.  No new theorem is claimed here.
