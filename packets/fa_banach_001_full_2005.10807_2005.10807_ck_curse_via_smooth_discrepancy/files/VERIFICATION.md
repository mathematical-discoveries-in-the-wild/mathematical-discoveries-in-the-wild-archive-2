# Verification report

## Exact target

- arXiv:2005.10807, Introduction: conjecture that the Lipschitz target in the
  second main statement can be replaced by a \(C^k\) target for fixed \(k\).
- Open Problems, item 2: asks for the empirical decay rate of the dual metric
  generated by 1-Lipschitz functions with bounded \(k\)-th derivative.
- The source's \(L^2\) proof uses radius-\(n^{-1/d}\) ball averages to replace
  point evaluations by uniformly bounded functionals on \(L^2\).

## Proof audit

1. **Smooth bump normalization.**  A bump of spatial radius \(h\) and
   amplitude \(h^k\) has every derivative of order at most \(k\) uniformly
   bounded.  Disjoint supports keep the norm of a sum uniformly bounded.
2. **Counting.**  A grid with \(m^d\asymp n\) centers has a positive fraction
   whose support is disjoint from all \(n\) averaging balls when the ball
   radius is a sufficiently small multiple of \(n^{-1/d}\).
3. **Discrepancy size.**  Each surviving bump has integral
   \(\asymp h^{d+k}\); summing \(\asymp h^{-d}\) bumps gives
   \(\asymp h^k\asymp n^{-k/d}\).  Its ball averages are exactly zero.
4. **Hypothesis-class estimate.**  Translation invariance of uniform measure
   transfers the assumed point-sampling Monte-Carlo estimate to ball
   averages without requiring translation invariance of the space \(X\).
5. **\(L^2\) estimate.**  The centered ball-average functional has density
   \(g_S=n^{-1}\sum_i K_\varepsilon(\cdot-X_i)-1\), and
   \(\mathbb E\|g_S\|_2^2=n^{-1}(\|K_\varepsilon\|_2^2-1)=O(1)\) when
   \(\varepsilon\asymp n^{-1/d}\).
6. **Width exponent.**  Substitution \(\alpha=1/2\), \(\beta=k/d\) into the
   separation lemma gives
   \(\beta/(\alpha-\beta)=2k/(d-2k)\).
7. **Fixed-target upgrade.**  The Baire argument is checked with the exact
   scaling identity
   \(E_{2t}(\delta u)=\delta E_{2t/\delta}(u)\).  It needs only a uniform
   width lower bound and \(d>2k\), not the source lemma's \(d>4k\).

## Literature screen

Cheap run indexes were searched for arXiv:2005.10807, smooth Wasserstein,
smooth empirical measures, \(C^k\) Barron approximation, and Kolmogorov-width
decay.  A focused primary-source web search found B. Kloeckner,
*Empirical measures: regularity is a counter-curse to dimensionality*,
arXiv:1802.04038.  Its Theorem C gives the prior upper rates
\(n^{-k/d}\) for \(d>2k\), \((\log n)n^{-1/2}\) at \(d=2k\), and
\(n^{-1/2}\) for \(d<2k\).  No located source performed the mollified
discrepancy bridge to the arXiv:2005.10807 \(L^2\) approximation theorem or
the Baire fixed-target upgrade.
