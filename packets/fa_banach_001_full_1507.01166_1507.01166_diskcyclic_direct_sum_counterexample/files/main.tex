\fbox{\parbox{0.91\linewidth}{\textbf{Candidate full negative answer.}
There is a diskcyclic operator $T$ on a separable complex Banach space
such that $T\oplus T$ is not $2$-bitransitive.  In fact the $k$-fold
direct sum of this same $T$ is not $k$-bitransitive for any $k\ge2$.}}
\end{center}

\section{The question and definitions}

For $S\in\mathcal B(X)$, a vector $x\in X$ is \emph{diskcyclic} when
\[
 \{\alpha S^n x:n\ge0,\ |\alpha|\le1\}
\]
is dense in $X$.  The direct sum $S_1\oplus\cdots\oplus S_k$ is
\emph{$k$-bitransitive} if, for all nonempty open $U_i,V_i\subset X$,
there are a common $n\ge0$ and nonzero $\alpha_i\in\D$ such that
\[
 S_i^n(\alpha_i U_i)\cap V_i\ne\varnothing
 \qquad(1\le i\le k).
\]
Bamerni, K\i l\i\c cman and Noorani prove positive results under common
diskcyclic-criterion or compoundness assumptions, and end with the
question shown below.

\section{Statement of the answer}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $\lambda>1$ there are a separable complex Banach space $X$ and
$T\in\mathcal B(X)$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $T$ is diskcyclic;
 \item $T^{\oplus k}$ is not $k$-bitransitive for any $k\ge2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The only existence input is the theorem of de la Rosa and Read
\cite{deLaRosaRead}: there are a separable complex Banach space $Y$ and a
hypercyclic $R\in\mathcal B(Y)$ for which $R\oplus R$ is not hypercyclic.
By the Birkhoff transitivity theorem, $R\oplus R$ is therefore not
topologically transitive.

\section{Idea of the proof}

Put a fixed one-dimensional coordinate beside $R$, then amplify both
coordinates at the same exponential rate:
\[
 X=Y\oplus\C,\qquad T=\lambda(R\oplus I_{\C}).
\]
The factor $\lambda^n$ makes $(x,1)$ diskcyclic whenever $x$ is
hypercyclic for $R$: at time $n$ the disk scalar can implement every
effective scalar of modulus at most $\lambda^n$.

The fixed coordinate also detects those effective scalars.  If an input
and its image both have scalar coordinate close to $1$, their quotient is
close to $1$.  Thus alleged $2$-bitransitivity of $T\oplus T$, tested on
such scalar-coordinate neighborhoods, would remove the two independent
disk scalars and force ordinary transitivity of $R\oplus R$.  This
contradicts the choice of $R$.

\section{Proof}

Choose a hypercyclic vector $x$ for $R$.  We first show that $(x,1)$ is
diskcyclic for $T$.  Since
\[
 \alpha T^n(x,1)=\beta(R^n x,1),\qquad
 \beta=\alpha\lambda^n,
\]
the available effective scalars at time $n$ are exactly
$|\beta|\le\lambda^n$.

Let $(y,c)\in Y\oplus\C$ and $\varepsilon>0$.  If $c\ne0$, tail-density
of the hypercyclic orbit lets us choose arbitrarily large $n$ with
\[
 \|R^n x-y/c\|<\varepsilon/|c|
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad \lambda^n\ge |c|.
\]
Taking $\beta=c$ gives the required approximation.  If $c=0$, choose
$0<|\beta|<\varepsilon$, then choose arbitrarily large $n$ such that
\[
 \|R^n x-y/\beta\|<\varepsilon/|\beta|
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad \lambda^n\ge |\beta|.
\]
Now $\beta(R^n x,1)$ is within $2\varepsilon$ of $(y,0)$ in the sum norm.
In both cases $\alpha=\beta/\lambda^n\in\D$, so $T$ is diskcyclic.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $T\oplus T$ is $2$-bitransitive.
Let $U_i,V_i\subset Y$ be arbitrary nonempty open sets, $i=1,2$.  Choose
$v_i\in V_i$.  Continuity of $(\gamma,w)\mapsto\gamma^{-1}w$ at
$(1,v_i)$ gives nonempty open $W_i\subset V_i$ and a common $\eta>0$
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:buffer}
 |\gamma-1|<\eta, w\in W_i
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \gamma^{-1}w\in V_i.
\end{equation}
Choose $0<\delta<1$ so small that
\[
 \frac{2\delta}{1-\delta}<\eta.
\]
Apply $2$-bitransitivity to the two input/output pairs
\[
 A_i=U_i\times D(1,\delta),\qquad
 B_i=W_i\times D(1,\delta).
\]
There are a common $n$, nonzero $\alpha_i\in\D$, and
$(u_i,z_i)\in A_i$ such that
\[
 \alpha_iT^n(u_i,z_i)
   =(\beta_iR^nu_i,\beta_i z_i)\in B_i,
 \qquad \beta_i=\alpha_i\lambda^n.
\]
Both $z_i$ and $\beta_i z_i$ belong to $D(1,\delta)$, whence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pin}
 |\beta_i-1|
 =\frac{|\beta_i z_i-z_i|}{|z_i|}
 <\frac{2\delta}{1-\delta}<\eta.
\end{equation}
Moreover $\beta_iR^nu_i\in W_i$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:buffer}--\eqref{eq:pin} therefore give
$R^nu_i\in V_i$, while $u_i\in U_i$, for both $i$ at the same $n$.
Thus
\[
 (R\oplus R)^n(U_1\times U_2)\cap(V_1\times V_2)\ne\varnothing.
\]
Since the four open sets were arbitrary, $R\oplus R$ is topologically
transitive, the required contradiction.

Finally, if $T^{\oplus k}$ were $k$-bitransitive for some $k>2$, take
the remaining $k-2$ input and output open sets to be all of $X$.  The
first two coordinates would make $T\oplus T$ $2$-bitransitive.  Hence no
$k\ge2$ works, proving Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The proof uses no computation.  The two limiting issues in the
construction are explicit: $\lambda>1$ supplies arbitrarily large
effective disk scalars, while $\delta<1$ keeps the pinning denominator
$|z_i|$ away from zero.  Independent scalars are allowed throughout; the
added fixed coordinate is precisely what pins each one separately.

The run registry, parsed corpus, exact question phrase, exact title, and
bounded web searches through 11 August 2026 were checked for
``diskcyclic $k$-fold direct sum'', ``diskcyclic direct sum
$k$-bitransitive'', and citations/close variants of arXiv:1507.01166.  We
found the source paper and its sufficient criteria, but no later direct
answer to its final question.  The de la Rosa--Read paper supplies the
hypercyclic operator used here but does not state this diskcyclic
construction.  Novelty confidence is moderate.

Human review should verify only the interpretation of the source's final
question as asking whether the identical direct sum is $k$-bitransitive,
and the standard hypercyclicity--transitivity equivalence.  Conditional on
that intended interpretation, the theorem is a complete negative answer.
