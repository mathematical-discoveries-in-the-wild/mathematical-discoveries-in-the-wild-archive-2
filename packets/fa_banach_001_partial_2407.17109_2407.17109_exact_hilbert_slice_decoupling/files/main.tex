\begin{abstract}
For every finite frequency partition (indeed, every finite family of
frequency sets) and every $1\le q\le\infty$, the classical and quantum
decoupling constants at output exponent $p=2$ coincide exactly:
\[
 \Dq_{2,q}(\mathcal P_\Omega)=\Dc_{2,q}(\mathcal P_\Omega).
\]
The source paper records only the case $p=q=2$.  The proof is an isometric
identification: $U=\Fc\Fw$ is a unitary map from Hilbert--Schmidt operators
onto $L^2$, and it preserves the localization constraint term by term.  If
$\mathcal P_\Omega$ has $N$ pairwise disjoint positive-measure pieces, the
common optimal constant is explicitly
\[
 1\quad(1\le q\le2),\qquad
 N^{1/2-1/q}\quad(2\le q\le\infty).
\]
This is a candidate new exact refinement, not a solution of the queue's
literal ``natural question,'' which the paper itself immediately answers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definitions and the isometric correspondence}

Let $\mathcal P=\{\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_N\}$ be a finite family of measurable
subsets of $\mathbb R^{2d}$.  The classical constant $\Dc_{2,q}(\mathcal P)$
is the least $D$ such that
\[
 \left\|\sum_{j=1}^N f_j\right\|_2
 \le D\,\bigl\|(\|f_j\|_2)_{j=1}^N\bigr\|_{\ell^q}
\]
whenever $f_j\in L^2(\mathbb R^{2d})$ and
$\operatorname{supp}(\Fc f_j)\subseteq\theta_j$.  The quantum constant
$\Dq_{2,q}(\mathcal P)$ is defined by the analogous inequality for
Hilbert--Schmidt operators $T_j$, with $\|\cdot\|_2$ replaced by the
Hilbert--Schmidt norm and localization
$\operatorname{supp}(\Fw T_j)\subseteq\theta_j$.  These are precisely the
finite-norm objects in the source definitions when $p=2$.

The source normalizations give two unitary maps:
\[
 \Fw:\Sp^2\longrightarrow L^2(\mathbb R^{2d}),
 \qquad
 \Fc:L^2(\mathbb R^{2d})\longrightarrow L^2(\mathbb R^{2d}),
 \qquad \Fc^2=I.
\]
Consequently
\[
 U:=\Fc\Fw:\Sp^2\longrightarrow L^2(\mathbb R^{2d})
\]
is unitary, and for every $T\in\Sp^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localization}
 \Fc(UT)=\Fc^2(\Fw T)=\Fw T.
\end{equation}
Thus $U$ preserves the localization set exactly, rather than merely
enlarging it.

\section{Equality for every inner exponent}

\begin{theorem}[exact Hilbert-slice transference]\label{thm:transfer}
For every finite family $\mathcal P$ of measurable frequency sets and every
$1\le q\le\infty$,
\[
 \boxed{\Dq_{2,q}(\mathcal P)=\Dc_{2,q}(\mathcal P).}
\]
The statement remains true when the sets overlap.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take any admissible quantum collection $(T_j)_{j=1}^N$ and set $f_j=UT_j$.
Equation \eqref{eq:localization} makes $(f_j)$ classically admissible, while
unitarity and linearity give
\[
 \left\|\sum_j f_j\right\|_2
 =\left\|\sum_jT_j\right\|_{\Sp^2},
 \qquad
 \|f_j\|_2=\|T_j\|_{\Sp^2}\quad(1\le j\le N).
\]
Applying the classical inequality therefore proves
$\Dq_{2,q}\le\Dc_{2,q}$, including the usual maximum convention for
$q=\infty$.

Conversely, $U$ is onto and
\[
 U^{-1}f=\Fw^{-1}(\Fc f).
\]
For every classically admissible $(f_j)$, set $T_j=U^{-1}f_j$.  Then
$\Fw T_j=\Fc f_j$, so the same identities and the quantum inequality yield
$\Dc_{2,q}\le\Dq_{2,q}$.  The two optimal constants are equal.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact value for a disjoint partition}

\begin{theorem}[sharp finite-partition formula]\label{thm:formula}
Suppose $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_N$ are pairwise disjoint up to null sets and
each has positive Lebesgue measure.  Then
\[
 \Dc_{2,q}(\mathcal P)=\Dq_{2,q}(\mathcal P)
 =\begin{cases}
 1,&1\le q\le2,\\[2mm]
 N^{\frac12-\frac1q},&2\le q\le\infty,
 \end{cases}
\]
where $1/\infty=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $(f_j)$ is admissible, then the functions $\Fc f_j$ have pairwise
disjoint supports.  Plancherel therefore makes the $f_j$ pairwise
orthogonal, and with $a_j=\|f_j\|_2$,
\[
 \left\|\sum_j f_j\right\|_2
 =\left(\sum_j a_j^2\right)^{1/2}=\|a\|_{\ell^2}.
\]
The optimal upper bound is exactly the norm of the identity map
$\ell^q_N\to\ell^2_N$.  Monotonicity of finite-dimensional $\ell^r$ norms
gives norm $1$ for $q\le2$; H\"older gives
$N^{1/2-1/q}$ for $q\ge2$.

These constants are sharp within the localized class.  For $q\le2$, choose
one nonzero component.  For $q\ge2$, positive measure lets us choose
$0\ne g_j\in L^2$ supported in $\theta_j$, normalized by $\|g_j\|_2=1$,
and put $f_j=\Fc g_j$.  Then every $a_j=1$, so equality holds in the stated
finite-dimensional norm comparison.  The quantum sharpness follows either
from Theorem \ref{thm:transfer} or by applying $U^{-1}$ term by term.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[infinitely many pieces]
For a countable pairwise-disjoint family with infinitely many
positive-measure pieces, uniform decoupling over its finite subfamilies has
constant $1$ for $1\le q\le2$ and no finite constant for $q>2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem \ref{thm:formula} applied to arbitrary $N$-element subfamilies gives
the claim, since $N^{1/2-1/q}\to\infty$ exactly when $q>2$.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, novelty boundary, and limitations}

The proof uses only three identities stated in the latest source version:
$\Fw:\Sp^2\to L^2$ is unitary, $\Fc$ is unitary, and $\Fc^2=I$.  Direct
substitution gives \eqref{eq:localization}; hence there is no convolution
loss, support enlargement, density passage, or dependence on $\Omega$.
The endpoint checks are also explicit: at $q=1$ the constant is $1$, at
$q=2$ it is $1$, and at $q=\infty$ it is $\sqrt N$.

A search of the run's lightweight result indexes and targeted searches for
the exact all-$q$ identity found the source's $p=q=2$ remark but no statement
of Theorems \ref{thm:transfer}--\ref{thm:formula}.  This is evidence, not a
literature guarantee.  Human review should verify novelty against the
published version and standard quantum-harmonic-analysis references.

This packet does not improve the comparison for $p\ne2$.  It does not answer
a genuinely open question quoted from the paper: the queue's ``natural
question'' is answered by Theorem 1.3 of the same paper.  The promoted result
is instead an exact, fully proved strengthening of the adjacent optimality
remark.
