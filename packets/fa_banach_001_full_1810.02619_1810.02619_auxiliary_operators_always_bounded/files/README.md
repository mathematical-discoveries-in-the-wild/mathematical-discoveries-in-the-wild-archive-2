# The generalized Krein--von Neumann auxiliary operators are always bounded

Status: candidate full solution — likely valid; human review requested.

Source: Zsigmond Tarcsay and Tamás Titkos, *Operators on anti-dual pairs:
Generalized Krein--von Neumann extension*, arXiv:1810.02619, Theorem 3.3 and
the paragraph after its proof on PDF pages 9–10.

## Result

Under exactly the general anti-dual-pair hypotheses of Theorem 3.3, both
auxiliary operators have bounded everywhere-defined closures. More precisely,
if

\[
 \rho=\sup\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in
 \sigma(BC|_{\operatorname{dom}A})\},
\]

with \(\rho=0\) for empty spectrum, then

\[
 \|\widehat B^{**}\|
 =\|\widehat C^{**}\|
 \le \sqrt{\rho}.
\]

This fully answers the source question positively and extends the bound that
the source records only for Banach spaces to every weak-star sequentially
complete anti-dual pair covered by its theorem.

## Main mechanism

The source already proves that the two closures are mutual adjoints. Put
\(T=\widehat B^{**}\); then \(Q=TT^*\) is positive self-adjoint and extends
\(\widehat B\widehat C\), which represents \(BC\) on the dense subspace
\(\operatorname{ran}A\).

For every real \(\lambda>\rho\), algebraic resolvent surjectivity of
\(\lambda-BC\) implies

\[
 \operatorname{ran}A\subseteq\operatorname{ran}(\lambda-Q).
\]

Consequently, each dense-core vector has a \(Q\)-spectral measure whose
inverse-square potential is finite at every \(\lambda>\rho\). An elementary
covering lemma shows that a finite measure with finite inverse-square potential
at every point of an interval must vanish on that interval. Thus the spectral
projection of \(Q\) above \(\rho\) vanishes. Therefore \(Q\) is bounded by
\(\rho\), which bounds both \(T\) and \(T^*\) by \(\sqrt\rho\).

## Verification

The proof is self-contained in main.tex and solution_packet.pdf. It is
analytic and uses no numerical evidence. The two source crops are reproducibly
generated by code/crop_open_problem.py from source_paper.pdf rendered at
180 dpi.

The decisive checks are:

- resolvent surjectivity is used only after applying \(A\), so no injectivity
  of \(A\) or quotient inverse is assumed;
- \(TT^*\), rather than \(T^*T\), is the product aligned with \(BC\);
- the spectral-measure identity automatically removes any atom at the
  resolvent parameter;
- the \(5r\)-covering proof handles singular measures as well as atoms and
  absolutely continuous measures;
- once \(TT^*\) is bounded as a self-adjoint operator, its domain is the whole
  Hilbert space, which forces \(T^*\) and \(T\) bounded.
