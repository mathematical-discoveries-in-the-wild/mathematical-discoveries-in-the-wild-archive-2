\section*{Source problem}

Turco and Villafa\~ne, \emph{Galois connection between Lipschitz and linear
operator ideals and minimal Lipschitz operator ideals}, arXiv:1807.10786,
ask in Problem 4.15:
\begin{quote}
If a Banach Lipschitz operator ideal $\I$ has $\I^{\min}$ or $\I^{\max}$ of
composition type, must $\I$ itself be of composition type?
\end{quote}

\section*{Exact later answer}

Albarrac\'{\i}n and Turco, \emph{On the Lipschitz operator ideal
$\Lip\circ\A\circ\Lip$}, arXiv:2211.14240v2, explicitly cite Problem 4.15
and give a negative answer.  Let $\OF$ denote the Banach ideal of
approximable linear operators and set
\[
 \boxed{\I=\Lip\circ\OF\circ\Lip.}
\]
Their Propositions 2.13 and 2.16 prove, respectively,
\[
 \I\text{ is not of composition type},
 \qquad
 \I^{\min}=\OF\circ\Lip\text{ is of composition type}.
\]
Thus $\I$ satisfies the premise of Problem 4.15 through its minimal kernel,
but violates the proposed conclusion.  A counterexample for the minimal
alternative is enough to make the disjunctive implication false; no statement
about $\I^{\max}$ is needed.

\end{center}

\section*{How the two supporting propositions work}

Proposition 2.13 is more general: for every Banach linear operator ideal
$\A$, the strong Lipschitz ideal
$\Lip\circ\A^{\min}\circ\Lip$ is not of composition type.  For a nonzero
finite-dimensional Banach space $E$, this ideal contains the Dirac map
$\delta_E:E\to\Ffree(E)$.  Were the ideal of composition type, its standard
linearization criterion would put
$I_{\Ffree(E)}$ in $(\A^{\min})^{\mathrm{reg}}$.  That identity would be
compact, although the Lipschitz-free space $\Ffree(E)$ is infinite
dimensional, a contradiction.  Taking $\A=\OF=\OF^{\min}$ gives the first
displayed assertion about $\I$.

Proposition 2.16 treats every closed Banach operator ideal $\A$.  Minimality
gives
\[
 (\Lip\circ\A\circ\Lip)^{\min}\subseteq\OF\circ\Lip,
\]
while $\A\circ\Lip\subseteq\Lip\circ\A\circ\Lip$ gives
\[
 \A^{\min}\circ\Lip
 \subseteq(\Lip\circ\A\circ\Lip)^{\min}.
\]
For closed $\A$, one has $\A^{\min}=\OF$, so the two inclusions force
equality with $\OF\circ\Lip$, a composition ideal.  The later paper then
specializes to $\A=\OF$ and explicitly labels the result a negative answer
to Problem 4.15.

\section*{Proof intuition}

The original implication fails because taking the minimal kernel can erase
the nonlinear ``two-sided Lipschitz'' obstruction.  The original ideal is
large enough to contain Dirac maps whose linearizations are identities on
infinite-dimensional free spaces, preventing composition type.  Its minimal
kernel, however, collapses exactly to the ordinary approximable composition
ideal $\OF\circ\Lip$.

\section*{Exactness, scope, and provenance}

This is a direct answer, not an inference: the supporting authors cite the
source as reference [30], name Problem 4.15, and state that their example
gives its negative answer.  It settles the yes/no problem completely, while
their following sentence leaves open only the classification of further
examples besides $\OF$.
