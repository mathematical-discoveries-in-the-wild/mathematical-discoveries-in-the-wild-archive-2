\large The several-variable half of the possible-rank problem}
\author{Autonomous solve-first investigation, lane 12}

\begin{abstract}
Le and Tikaradze ask whether the Ding--Qin--Zheng possible-rank theorem for a
single product of Toeplitz operators on the disc extends to several
variables.  We prove the rank-one half.  On every weighted Bergman space
$A^2_\gamma(\B_N)$ with $N\ge2$ and nonnegative integer $\gamma$, under the
source theorem's boundedness and smoothness assumptions, a difference
$T_\phi T_\psi-T_h$ of rank at most one must be zero.  The proof converts the
source's Brown--Halmos identity into a doubly centered holomorphic kernel.
Its alleged separation rank is at most one, whereas a coefficient-tensor
estimate gives the lower bound
\[
 \binom{2N+1+\gamma}{N}-2\ge8.
\]
The construction and classification of nonzero ranks at least two remain
open in this packet.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and result}

Let $\B_N$ be the unit ball in $\C^N$.  For a bounded symbol $a$, let $T_a$
denote the Toeplitz operator on $A^2_\gamma(\B_N)$.  Throughout,
$\gamma\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$.  The main theorem of Le--Tikaradze \cite{LT}
states, in the case of one product, that if $\phi=f+\bar g$ and
$\psi=u+\bar v$ are bounded pluriharmonic functions, $h$ is bounded and
$C^{2N+2+2\gamma}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operator}
 T_\phi T_\psi=T_h+\sum_{\ell=1}^r x_\ell\otimes y_\ell,
\end{equation}
then, writing $s=N+1+\gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BH}
 h-\bar g u\ \text{is pluriharmonic},\qquad
 \phi\psi=h+(1-|z|^2)^s\sum_{\ell=1}^r x_\ell\bar y_\ell.
\end{equation}
The converse also holds.  Here $x\otimes y$ maps $q$ to
$\langle q,y\rangle x$.

In their Remark 4.3, the authors recall that Ding--Qin--Zheng \cite{DQZ}
proved in one variable that rank one in \eqref{eq:operator} is impossible,
while every rank $r\ge2$ occurs.  They ask for a several-variable
generalization.

\begin{theorem}[rank-one vanishing]\label{thm:main}
Let $N\ge2$ and let $\gamma$ be a nonnegative integer.  Suppose $\phi,\psi$
are bounded pluriharmonic functions on $\B_N$ and $h$ is bounded and
$C^{2N+2+2\gamma}$.  If
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(T_\phi T_\psi-T_h)\le1,
\]
then $T_\phi T_\psi=T_h$.
\end{theorem}

Thus the first half of the Ding--Qin--Zheng possible-rank theorem survives in
every complex dimension at least two and for every integer weight covered by
the source.  The proof actually gives an eight-dimensional obstruction in
the smallest case $N=2$, $\gamma=0$.

\section{A centered tensor-rank lemma}

For a holomorphic function $q$ on $\B_N$, put $Qq=q-q(0)$.  If
\[
 K(z,w)=\sum_{j=1}^m a_j(z)\overline{b_j(w)},
\]
its \emph{separation rank} $\srank K$ is the least length of such a
representation.  Equivalently, it is the rank of the associated finite
coefficient tensor.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rank}
Let $x,y$ be nonzero holomorphic functions on $\B_N$, let $s\ge1$, and put
\[
 R(z,w)=Q_zQ_w\bigl[
 (1-\langle z,w\rangle)^s x(z)\overline{y(w)}\bigr].
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 \srank R\ge \binom{N+s}{N}-2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $M=\binom{N+s}{N}$, the number of multi-indices $\alpha$ with
$|\alpha|\le s$.  The multinomial expansion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expansion}
 (1-\langle z,w\rangle)^s
 =\sum_{|\alpha|\le s}c_\alpha z^\alpha\overline{w^\alpha},
 \qquad
 c_\alpha=(-1)^{|\alpha|}\binom{s}{|\alpha|}
             \frac{|\alpha|!}{\alpha!},
\end{equation}
and every $c_\alpha$ is nonzero.

Let $E$ be the $M$-dimensional coefficient space with basis $e_\alpha$ and
define
\[
 A e_\alpha=z^\alpha x,\qquad B e_\alpha=z^\alpha y.
\]
Both $A$ and $B$ are injective.  For example,
$\sum d_\alpha z^\alpha x=0$ forces the polynomial
$\sum d_\alpha z^\alpha$ to vanish on the nonempty open set where $x\ne0$,
and hence identically.

Put $U=QA$ and $V=QB$.  Since $A$ is injective and the space of constant
functions is one-dimensional,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernels}
 \dim\ker U\le1,\qquad \dim\ker V\le1.
\end{equation}
Let $C:E\to E$ be diagonal with diagonal entries $c_\alpha$.  Expansion
\eqref{eq:expansion} says that the coefficient tensor of $R$ is $UCV^{\mathsf
T}$.  The map $C$ is invertible.  Applying Sylvester's rank inequality in
the finite-dimensional images of $U$ and $V$ gives
\[
 \srank R=\operatorname{rank}(UCV^{\mathsf T})
 \ge \operatorname{rank}U+\operatorname{rank}V-M
 \ge (M-1)+(M-1)-M=M-2.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:lower}.
\end{proof}

The two lost dimensions in the lemma are genuine possibilities: centering
may remove a constant lying in either monomial-multiple span.  No
independence between $Qx$ and the positive-degree monomial multiples is
silently assumed.

\section{Proof of rank-one vanishing}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Assume for contradiction that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rankone}
 T_\phi T_\psi-T_h=x\otimes y
\end{equation}
for nonzero $x,y\in A^2_\gamma(\B_N)$.  In the decompositions
$\phi=f+\bar g$ and $\psi=u+\bar v$, constants may be transferred between
the holomorphic and antiholomorphic summands, so we normalize
$f(0)=v(0)=0$.

\pagebreak
Apply \eqref{eq:BH} to \eqref{eq:rankone}.  Since
\begin{align*}
 h-\bar g u
 &=\phi\psi-\bar g u-(1-|z|^2)^s x\bar y\\
 &=fu+f\bar v+\bar g\bar v-(1-|z|^2)^s x\bar y
\end{align*}
is pluriharmonic, and the first and third terms on the last line are
holomorphic and antiholomorphic respectively, the function
\[
 f\bar v-(1-|z|^2)^s x\bar y
\]
is pluriharmonic.

Complexify the real-analytic identity by replacing $\bar z$ with an
independent $\bar w$.  The complexification of a pluriharmonic function has
no terms of positive holomorphic and antiholomorphic degree; applying
$Q_zQ_w$ therefore kills it.  The normalization of $f$ and $v$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered}
 f(z)\overline{v(w)}
 =Q_zQ_w\bigl[(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^s
                 x(z)\overline{y(w)}\bigr].
\end{equation}
The left side has separation rank at most one.  Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} says
that the right side has separation rank at least
\[
 \binom{N+s}{N}-2
 =\binom{2N+1+\gamma}{N}-2.
\]
For $N\ge2$ and $\gamma\ge0$, the minimum is
$\binom{5}{2}-2=8$.  This contradicts \eqref{eq:centered}.  Hence no
nonzero rank-one difference exists.
\end{proof}

The same coefficient argument also rules out rank one when $N=1$ and
$\gamma\ge1$.  In the sole borderline case $N=1$, $\gamma=0$, its lower
bound is only one; that is precisely the case requiring the sharper
one-variable argument of \cite{DQZ}.

\section{Boundary of the result}

The theorem settles the rank-one half of the question, not the entire
possible-rank program.  For rank $r>1$, equation \eqref{eq:centered} is
replaced by a sum over $x_\ell,y_\ell$.  Although minimal rank
representations make each family $\{x_\ell\}$ and $\{y_\ell\}$ linearly
independent over $\C$, their monomial multiples can have polynomial-module
relations such as $x_2=z_1x_1$.  Those relations can cancel the diagonal
blocks used in Lemma~\ref{lem:rank}.

Eight upgrade routes were tested.  In particular, a prospective
finite-rank divisibility lemma for
$(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^sK(z,w)$ would finish the classification, but the
obvious zero-multiplicity proof is invalid because the hypersurface
$\langle z,w\rangle=1$ lies outside the product of unit balls and the
holomorphic factors need not continue there.  Lifting the one-variable
examples through one coordinate likewise creates mixed monomials in all
remaining coordinates.  No controlled construction of every rank
$r\ge2$, nor a proof that all nonzero finite ranks are impossible, is
claimed.

A bounded search on 13 August 2026 covered the exact quoted question,
arXiv id and title, later citations, and rank-one/semicommutator terminology.
It found the source and the one-variable theorem, but no later
several-variable resolution.  Novelty is plausible, not certified.
