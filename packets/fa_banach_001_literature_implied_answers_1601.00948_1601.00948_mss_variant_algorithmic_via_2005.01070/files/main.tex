\section*{Source question}

Naor--Youssef ask in the introduction of
\emph{Restricted invertibility revisited} (arXiv:1601.00948, source PDF
page 8) whether their Theorems 6, 7, 9, and 11 can be made algorithmic.
For an $n\times m$ matrix $A$ of rank $R>k$, their Theorem 11 gives a
set $S$ of $k$ columns such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
 \|(A_S)^{-1}\|
 \le \frac{\sqrt m}{\sqrt R-\sqrt k}
 \left(\frac1R\sum_{i=1}^R\frac1{\sigma_i(A)^2}\right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The source proof is existential and explicitly includes this theorem in
its algorithmic question.

\section*{Later algorithmic theorem}

Xie's \emph{A note on restricted invertibility with weighted columns}
(arXiv:2005.01070) proves in Theorem 1.1 that for any
$k\le r\le R$ and any invertible diagonal column-weight matrix $W$, a
set $S$ of size $k$ satisfies
\[
 \smin(A_SW_S)^2\ge
 \frac{(\sqrt r-\sqrt{k-1})^2}{\|W^{-1}\|_F^2}
 \frac{r}{\sum_{i=1}^r\sigma_i(A)^{-2}}.
\]
The paragraph following that theorem states that its proof provides a
deterministic algorithm with running time
$O(k(m-k/2)n^{\theta+1})$, where $\theta$ is the matrix-multiplication
exponent.

Set $W=I_m$ and $r=R$.  Since $\|I_m\|_F^2=m$, Corollary 1.2 of the
later paper becomes
\[
 \smin(A_S)^2\ge
 \frac{(\sqrt R-\sqrt{k-1})^2}{m}
 \frac{R}{\sum_{i=1}^R\sigma_i(A)^{-2}}.
\]
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:answer}
 \|(A_S)^{-1}\|
 \le \frac{\sqrt m}{\sqrt R-\sqrt{k-1}}
 \left(\frac1R\sum_{i=1}^R\frac1{\sigma_i(A)^2}\right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Because $\sqrt{k-1}<\sqrt k$, estimate~\eqref{eq:answer} is slightly
stronger than~\eqref{eq:source}.  The later deterministic algorithm
therefore gives a polynomial-time construction with all guarantees of
the source's Theorem 11.

\section*{Scope}

This is a full affirmative algorithmic answer for one of the four listed
theorems, not for the entire compound question.  Xie's displayed estimate
does not reproduce the tail-singular-value guarantee of source Theorem 6
or the max dual-column-distance guarantee of source Theorem 9.  The
separate question of combining an RMS dual-column parameter with
$1/\sqrt\varepsilon$ dependence also remains outside this identification.
