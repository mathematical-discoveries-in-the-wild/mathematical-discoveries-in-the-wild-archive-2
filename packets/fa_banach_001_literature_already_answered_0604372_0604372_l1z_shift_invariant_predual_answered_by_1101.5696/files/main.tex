\section{Original question}

Daws asks in Section~4, PDF page~13, of \emph{Dual Banach algebras:
representations and injectivity}~\cite{source} whether $\ell^1(\mathbb Z)$
has a unique predual compatible with convolution.  His equivalent concrete
formulation is:

\begin{quote}
Let $X\subseteq\ell^\infty(\mathbb Z)$ be a shift-invariant subspace such
that $X'$ is naturally identified with $\ell^1(\mathbb Z)$.  Is
$X=c_0(\mathbb Z)$?
\end{quote}

The compatibility condition means that convolution on $\ell^1(\mathbb Z)$
is separately weak-star continuous for the duality induced by $X$.

\section{Separate later answer}

Daws, Haydon, Schlumprecht, and White explicitly cite the source paper and
state on PDF page~2 of \emph{Shift invariant preduals of
$\ell_1(\mathbb Z)$} that their results answer the question
negatively~\cite{answerpaper}.  Proposition~2.3 identifies compatible
preduals with concrete shift-invariant subspaces of
$\ell^\infty(\mathbb Z)$.  Theorem~3.4 and Corollary~3.5, PDF pages~9--10,
construct preduals $F^{(\lambda)}$ for every $|\lambda|>1$ and prove that
they induce continuum many distinct weak-star topologies on
$\ell^1(\mathbb Z)$, each different from the topology induced by
$c_0(\mathbb Z)$.

Therefore $X=c_0(\mathbb Z)$ is false in general, and the compatible predual
is not unique.  The same paper later gives examples not even isomorphic to
$c_0$ as Banach spaces.

\section{Scope and status audit}

This completely answers the source's exact convolution-compatible unique
predual question.  It does not classify all shift-invariant preduals.  Nor
does it refute the stronger result, discussed in the answer paper, that the
canonical predual is unique if both convolution and the natural coproduct
are required to be weak-star continuous.

The supporting authors explicitly knew they were answering Daws's question:
their introduction names the first author's question, cites the source as
reference~[12], and says that the results answer it negatively.  A bounded
search through 11 August 2026 checked the run indexes, both arXiv ids, the
exact question wording, title/author searches, the source citation in the
answer paper, and the journal metadata.  This is an exact literature answer,
not an original mathematical result.
