\begin{abstract}
For the Dirichlet \(p\)-Laplacian on
\(\Ra=(0,a)\times(0,1)\), Hor\'ak conjectured a single transition from the
second Ljusternik--Schnirelman eigenvalue to a lower nonsymmetric branch.
For every fixed \(1<a<2\), we prove the entire post-last-contact half: the
second eigenvalue is strictly below the vertical antisymmetric branch for all
sufficiently large \(p\). We give an explicit sufficient inequality. The
proof combines an opposite-corner two-disk packing, a genus-two family of
distance cones, and sharp one-dimensional Poincar\'e inequality on the
half-rectangle. The pre-contact equality interval and the regime \(a\ge2\)
remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source conjecture and notation}

For a bounded domain \(\Omega\subset\R^2\), write
\[
 R_p[u;\Omega]=
 \frac{\int_\Omega |\nabla u|^p}{\int_\Omega |u|^p}
\]
and let \(\lambda_2(p;\Omega)\) be the second variational eigenvalue defined
by the Krasnosel'ski\u\i{}-genus minimax on the unit \(L^p\)-sphere.
Following \cite{source}, let \(\lbar(p;\Ra)\) denote the branch whose
eigenfunction is antisymmetric about \(x=a/2\). By definition,
\[
 \lbar(p;\Ra)=\lambda_1(p;\Da),
 \qquad \Da=(0,a/2)\times(0,1).
\]

Remark~3.4 of \cite{source}, reproduced in
Figure~\ref{fig:conjecture}, records Hor\'ak's conjecture: for every \(a>1\)
there should be \(p^*(a)>2\) such that \(\lambda_2=\lbar\) on
\([2,p^*]\) and \(\lambda_2<\lbar\) for \(p>p^*\). The source anticipates
\(p^*(a)=+\infty\) for \(a\ge2\).

\section{Proof intuition}

Two tangent equal disks can be put into opposite corners of \(\Ra\). Their
distance-to-boundary cones span a two-dimensional, genus-two test family for
\(\lambda_2\), and disjoint congruent supports make the Rayleigh quotient
constant on that family. For \(a<2\), this tangent-packing radius is strictly
larger than the inradius \(a/4\) of the half-rectangle \(\Da\).
That strict geometric gap becomes an exponential gap after taking \(p\)-th
powers. A one-dimensional Poincar\'e bound on each horizontal slice of
\(\Da\) then beats the two-cone upper bound for every sufficiently large
\(p\).

\section{Explicit eventual inequality}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let \(1<a<2\), and define
\[
 r_a=\frac{a+1-\sqrt{2a}}2,
 \qquad \rho_a=\frac{a}{4r_a}.
\]
Then \(0<\rho_a<1\), and for every \(p>1\) satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:criterion}
 \frac{(p+1)(p+2)}{2(p-1)}\rho_a^p<1
\end{equation}
one has
\[
 \lambda_2(p;\Ra)<\lbar(p;\Ra).
\]
Consequently, the strict inequality holds for all sufficiently large \(p\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Place open disks \(B_1,B_2\) of radius \(r_a\) at the opposite-corner
centers
\[
 (r_a,r_a),\qquad (a-r_a,1-r_a).
\]
Since \(1<a<2\), direct calculation gives
\[
 r_a<\frac12<\frac a2,
 \qquad
 r_a-\frac a4=\frac{(\sqrt a-\sqrt2)^2}{4}>0,
\]
so both disks fit in \(\Ra\). They are tangent because
\[
 (a-2r_a)^2+(1-2r_a)^2=4r_a^2.
\]

For \(i=1,2\), let
\(\delta_i(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial B_i)\) on \(B_i\), extended
by zero to \(\Ra\). The interiors of the supports are disjoint. The unit
\(L^p\)-sphere in \(\operatorname{span}\{\delta_1,\delta_2\}\) is
odd-homeomorphic to \(S^1\), hence is an admissible compact symmetric set of
genus two. Because the profiles are congruent and their supports are
disjoint, every nonzero \(c_1\delta_1+c_2\delta_2\) has the same Rayleigh
quotient. Polar integration gives
\[
 \int_{B_i}|\nabla\delta_i|^p=\pi r_a^2,
 \qquad
 \int_{B_i}|\delta_i|^p
 =2\pi\int_0^{r_a}(r_a-s)^p s\,ds
 =\frac{2\pi r_a^{p+2}}{(p+1)(p+2)}.
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper}
 \lambda_2(p;\Ra)
 \le \frac{(p+1)(p+2)}{2r_a^p}.
\end{equation}

For \(u\in W^{1,p}_0(\Da)\), apply sharp one-dimensional Dirichlet
Poincar\'e inequality to almost every horizontal slice, then integrate in
\(y\). Since \(|\nabla u|^p\ge |u_x|^p\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 \lbar(p;\Ra)=\lambda_1(p;\Da)
 \ge (p-1)\left(\frac{2\pi_p}{a}\right)^p
 \ge (p-1)\left(\frac4a\right)^p,
\end{equation}
where
\[
 \pi_p=2\int_0^1(1-s^p)^{-1/p}\,ds\ge2.
\]
Condition~\eqref{eq:criterion} says exactly that the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:upper} is strictly smaller than the last expression in
\eqref{eq:lower}. This proves the comparison. Finally,
\(\rho_a<1\) follows from \(r_a>a/4\); its exponential decay dominates the
polynomial prefactor in \eqref{eq:criterion}, so the condition holds
eventually.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite last contact]\label{cor:last}
For every \(1<a<2\), the set
\[
 E_a=\{p\ge2:\lambda_2(p;\Ra)=\lbar(p;\Ra)\}
\]
is nonempty, closed, and bounded. Hence
\(p_{\rm last}(a)=\max E_a<\infty\), and
\[
 \lambda_2(p;\Ra)<\lbar(p;\Ra)
 \qquad (p>p_{\rm last}(a)).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The vertical two-half-rectangle test gives
\(\lambda_2(p;\Ra)\le\lbar(p;\Ra)\) for all \(p>1\). At \(p=2\), since
\(1<a<2\), the \((2,1)\) rectangle mode is the second Laplace mode, and its
eigenvalue is \(\lbar(2;\Ra)\). Thus \(2\in E_a\). Both branches are
continuous in \(p\), so \(E_a\) is closed. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} makes it
bounded. Its supremum is therefore a maximum, and after that maximum the
only possible relation is strict inequality.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, verification, and novelty}

The theorem proves eventual strictness, and Corollary~\ref{cor:last} proves
the whole post-last-contact assertion, for every fixed \(1<a<2\). It does
not prove the conjectured equality throughout \([2,p_{\rm last}(a)]\): that
appears to require nonlinear branch uniqueness or a global optimal
two-partition classification. At \(a=2\), the two-disk radius becomes
\(1/2=a/4\), so the exponential gap vanishes. The anticipated
\(p^*(a)=+\infty\) for \(a\ge2\) therefore remains untouched.

The reproducible checker performs 29,997 dense-grid geometry checks and 14
finite-threshold checks. For \(a=1.01,1.10,1.25,1.50,1.75,1.90,1.99\), the
first integer \(p\) certified by \eqref{eq:criterion} is respectively
\(15,23,49,195,1375,13016,2209774\). These computations audit the constants
but do not replace the proof.

Four cheap run indexes and bounded exact-title, exact-conjecture,
rectangle-symmetry, and \(p\)-to-infinity searches found no proof or later
resolution through 11 August 2026. The elementary nature of the argument
leaves an unindexed-folklore risk, so novelty confidence is moderate. Expert
review should focus on the genus-two test family, the quotient calculation
at the tangent supports, the one-dimensional eigenvalue normalization, and
the continuity/last-contact inference.
