\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\textwidth}
\textbf{Status: candidate full theorem, likely valid.}
The nonlocal functionals in Example 6.3 of Lahti--Pinamonti--Zhou
\(\Gamma(L^1)\)-converge, despite their failure to converge pointwise to
one multiple of variation. For \(1<q<\infty\), their exact limit is
\[
 u\longmapsto 2^{1/q}\|Du\|_\mu .
\]
More generally, if \(d\mu=w\,dx\), \(m\le w\le M\), and \(w=m\) on an
open dense set, then the limit is
\(2^{1/q}m^{1+1/q}|Du|=2^{1/q}m^{1/q}\|Du\|_\mu\).
The proof combines the Euclidean \(\Gamma\)-liminf with recovery
sequences whose gradients are moved into the dense minimum-weight region.
\end{minipage}}

\section{The source problem and concrete target}

Lahti, Pinamonti, and Zhou study nonlocal \(BV\) functionals on PI metric
measure spaces. Their published Remark 1.9 proposes investigating
\(\Gamma\)-convergence in that setting \cite{LPZ}. Their Example 6.3 is
especially sharp: on \(I=[0,1]\), let \(A\) be a fat Cantor set of
Lebesgue measure \(1/2\), put
\[
 w=2\mathbf1_A+\mathbf1_{I\setminus A},
 \qquad d\mu=w\,dx,
 \qquad \rho_i(x,y)=i\mathbf1_{\{|x-y|\le1/i\}},
 \tag{1}
\]
and, for \(1<q<\infty\), define
\begin{equation}
 \F_i^w(u)=
 \int_I\left[
 \int_I\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^q}{|x-y|^q}\rho_i(x,y)w(x)\,dx
 \right]^{1/q}w(y)\,dy.
 \tag{2}
\end{equation}
The paper exhibits Lipschitz functions with incompatible pointwise
asymptotic constants. Its source also contains a commented concrete
question asking whether these very functionals nevertheless
\(\Gamma\)-converge to \(a\|D\cdot\|_\mu\).

\section{A stronger weighted-interval theorem}

The mechanism is clearer in the following general form. For a weight
\(w\), formula (2) retains its meaning with \(w\) in place of the
fat-Cantor weight.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let \(I=[0,1]\), \(1<q<\infty\), and let \(w:I\to(0,\infty)\) be
measurable with
\[
 0<m\le w\le M<\infty\quad\text{a.e.}
 \tag{3}
\]
Assume there is an open dense set \(G\subset I\) on which \(w=m\)
almost everywhere. Set \(d\mu=w\,dx\). Then, with respect to
\(L^1(I,\mu)\),
\[
 \F_i^w\ \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\Gamma}\
 \F^w,\qquad
 \F^w(u)=
 \begin{cases}
 2^{1/q}m^{1+1/q}|Du|(I),&u\in BV(I),\\
 +\infty,&u\notin BV(I).
 \end{cases}
 \tag{4}
\]
Moreover, the metric-measure variation obtained by relaxation against
\(\mu\) satisfies
\[
 \|Du\|_\mu(I)=m|Du|(I).
 \tag{5}
\]
Consequently \(\F^w(u)=2^{1/q}m^{1/q}\|Du\|_\mu(I)\).
\end{theorem}

For the source's weight, \(m=1\) and \(G=I\setminus A\) is open dense.
Thus (4)--(5) answer the concrete question with
\[
 a=2^{1/q}.
 \tag{6}
\]

\section{Moving variation into the minimum-weight set}

We first record the recovery device responsible for both (5) and the
\(\Gamma\)-limsup.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:recovery}
For every \(u\in BV(I)\), there are \(g_k\in\Lip(I)\) and compact sets
\(K_k\subset G\) such that
\[
 g_k\to u\quad\text{in }L^1(I),\qquad
 g_k'=0\quad\text{a.e. on }I\setminus K_k,\qquad
 \int_I|g_k'|\,dx\longrightarrow |Du|(I).
 \tag{7}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Approximate \(u\) strictly in \(BV(I)\) by step functions \(s_k\) with
finitely many jumps:
\[
 s_k\to u\text{ in }L^1,\qquad
 \TV(s_k)\to|Du|(I).
\]
For a fixed step function, move each jump by an arbitrarily small
distance to a point of the dense open set \(G\), preserving the order of
the jumps. Around those new points choose pairwise disjoint closed
intervals compactly contained in \(G\). Replace every jump by the
corresponding affine transition on its interval and leave the function
constant elsewhere. The resulting Lipschitz function \(g\) has
derivative supported in the union \(K\) of those intervals,
\[
 \int_I|g'|\,dx=\sum_{\text{jumps}}|\text{jump height}|=\TV(s),
\]
and its \(L^1\)-distance from \(s\) can be made arbitrarily small.
A diagonal choice proves (7).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of (5)]
The lower bound \(w\ge m\) and the relaxation definition of
metric-measure variation give
\[
 \|Du\|_\mu(I)\ge m|Du|(I).
\]
For the sequence from Lemma \ref{lem:recovery}, \(w=m\) almost
everywhere on the support of \(g_k'\), so
\[
 \int_I \operatorname{lip}g_k\,d\mu
 =m\int_I|g_k'|\,dx\longrightarrow m|Du|(I).
\]
Taking the relaxation infimum gives the reverse inequality.
\end{proof}

\section{The \texorpdfstring{\(\Gamma\)}{Gamma}-liminf}

Let \(\F_i^1\) denote (2) with Lebesgue measure, that is, with \(w=1\).
From (3), pointwise in every measurable \(u\),
\begin{align}
 \F_i^w(u)
 &=\int_I w(y)\left[
 i\int_{I\cap[y-1/i,y+1/i]}
 \frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^q}{|x-y|^q}w(x)\,dx
 \right]^{1/q}dy \notag\\
 &\ge m^{1+1/q}\F_i^1(u).
 \tag{8}
\end{align}
The bounds in (3) make the \(L^1(dx)\) and \(L^1(\mu)\) topologies
equivalent. Brezis--Nguyen's Euclidean theorem states that
\[
 \F_i^1\ \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\Gamma(L^1)}\
 \left[u\mapsto2^{1/q}|Du|(I)\right]
 \tag{9}
\]
for this radial mollifier \cite[Proposition 15]{BN}. Therefore, whenever
\(u_i\to u\) in \(L^1(\mu)\), (8)--(9) give
\[
 \liminf_{i\to\infty}\F_i^w(u_i)
 \ge2^{1/q}m^{1+1/q}|Du|(I).
 \tag{10}
\]
This also yields \(+\infty\) when \(u\notin BV(I)\).

\section{The \texorpdfstring{\(\Gamma\)}{Gamma}-limsup}

Fix \(u\in BV(I)\) and take \(g_k,K_k\) from Lemma
\ref{lem:recovery}. Let
\[
 d_k=\operatorname{dist}(K_k,I\setminus G)>0.
\]
If \(i>2/d_k\) and the integrand in (2) is nonzero for a pair
\((x,y)\) with \(|x-y|\le1/i\), then the segment between \(x\) and \(y\)
meets \(K_k\). Hence both points lie in \(G\). Thus, for every fixed
\(k\) and all sufficiently large \(i\),
\begin{equation}
 \F_i^w(g_k)=m^{1+1/q}\F_i^1(g_k).
 \tag{11}
\end{equation}
The classical pointwise formula for Lipschitz functions gives
\[
 \lim_{i\to\infty}\F_i^1(g_k)
 =2^{1/q}\int_I|g_k'|\,dx.
 \tag{12}
\]
Choose increasing integers \(N_k\) so large that (11) holds and the
difference between its left side and the limit in (12), after the
factor \(m^{1+1/q}\), is at most \(1/k\) whenever \(i\ge N_k\).
Set \(u_i=g_k\) for \(N_k\le i<N_{k+1}\). By (7),
\[
 u_i\to u\quad\text{in }L^1(\mu)
\]
and
\[
 \limsup_{i\to\infty}\F_i^w(u_i)
 \le2^{1/q}m^{1+1/q}|Du|(I).
 \tag{13}
\]
Equations (10) and (13) prove Theorem \ref{thm:main}.

\section{Why pointwise failure and
\texorpdfstring{\(\Gamma\)}{Gamma}-convergence coexist}

At density points of the high-weight Cantor set, a fixed smooth slope
is sampled with both an inner and an outer copy of the large weight.
This produces the source's anomalously large pointwise values.
\(\Gamma\)-convergence instead permits recovery sequences. Lemma
\ref{lem:recovery} moves each finite collection of transitions into
nearby complementary intervals where the weight is minimal, and the
diagonal choice makes the interaction scale much smaller than those
intervals. This is precisely why the relaxed constant is the minimum
weight rather than the pointwise high-weight density.

Thus the paper's counterexample to pointwise convergence is not a
counterexample to \(\Gamma\)-convergence. It is an explicit positive
instance of the metric-space program in Remark 1.9, and the dense-minimum
theorem identifies the exact structural reason.
