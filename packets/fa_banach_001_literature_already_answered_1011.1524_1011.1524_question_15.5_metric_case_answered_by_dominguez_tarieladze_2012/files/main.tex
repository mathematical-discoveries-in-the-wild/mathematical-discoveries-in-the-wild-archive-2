Literature already answered (metrizable case).

\section{The source question}

Dikranjan, Shakhmatov, and Sp\v{e}v\'ak ask the following on PDF page 31,
Question 15.5, of arXiv:1011.1524v1 \cite{DSS}:
\begin{quote}
Does there exist a (separable metric) group which contains an
$f_\omega$-productive sequence but does not contain any unconditionally
$f_\omega$-Cauchy productive sequence?
\end{quote}
Here $f_\omega$ is the constant weight taking value $\omega$; thus all integer
multipliers are allowed.  In the terminology of the supporting paper,
$f_\omega$-productive means hyper-multipliable, and unconditionally
$f_\omega$-Cauchy productive means unconditionally hyper-premultipliable.

\section{The explicit literature answer}

Dom\'inguez and Tarieladze's later paper
\emph{Metrizable TAP, HTAP and STAP groups} \cite{DT} identifies the source
question by paper and number.  On PDF page 11, Remark 2.16, it reproduces
Question 15.5 and states that Corollary 2.15(b) gives a negative answer in the
metrizable case.

The mechanism appears immediately above the remark.  Proposition 2.14 proves
that every hyper-convergent sequence in a metrizable topological group has an
unconditionally hyper-premultipliable subsequence.  Since every
hyper-multipliable sequence is hyper-convergent, Corollary 2.15(b) concludes:
\begin{quote}
If a metrizable group contains an injective hyper-multipliable sequence, then
it contains an injective unconditionally hyper-premultipliable sequence.
\end{quote}
Translating the terminology back gives
\[
 \boxed{\text{No metrizable---hence no separable metrizable---group satisfies
 Question 15.5.}}
\]
The identification is explicit rather than inferred: Remark 2.16 names
Question 15.5, gives both terminology correspondences, and states the negative
answer.

\section{Scope and search status}

The parenthetical wording in the source also permits a broader reading asking
about arbitrary Hausdorff topological groups.  Dom\'inguez and Tarieladze say
in Remark 2.16 that they do not know whether Corollary 2.15(b) remains true for
nonmetrizable groups.  Therefore the precise disposition is
\[
 \boxed{\text{metrizable case: answered negatively; nonmetrizable case:
 not settled by the cited theorem.}}
\]

A bounded search through 13 August 2026 checked the local arXiv corpus, exact
question phrases, the source's citation trail, and later papers on productive,
hyper-multipliable, TAP, HTAP, and STAP sequences.  It located the direct 2012
answer above but no primary-source claim resolving the nonmetrizable variant.
