\section{Source question}

An operator algebra is \emph{residually finite dimensional} (RFD) when its
matrix norms are separated by finite-dimensional completely contractive
representations.  Thompson asks whether this property must pass to the
maximal $C^*$-cover \cite[Question 1]{Thompson2021}:
\[
 \A\text{ RFD}
 \quad\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}\quad
 C^*_{\max}(\A)\text{ RFD}.
\]

\section{Answer and counterexample}

\paragraph{Answer.} No.

Let $A(\D)$ denote the disc algebra, regarded as a subalgebra of
$C(\T)$, and define
\begin{equation}
 \Balg=
 \left\{
 \begin{bmatrix}f&0\\h&\overline g\end{bmatrix}:
 f,g\in A(\D),\ h\in C(\T)
 \right\}
 \subset M_2(C(\T)).
 \tag{1}\label{eq:B}
\end{equation}
Hartz proves that $\Balg$ is a unital RFD operator algebra and, more
strongly, is completely isometrically $2$-subhomogeneous
\cite[Theorem 1.3]{Hartz2022}.

\section{Idea / proof intuition}

The RFD half is transparent from the concrete realization: evaluation at
points of $\T$ gives two-dimensional representations, and the norm in
$M_2(C(\T))$ is the supremum of the resulting matrix norms.

The obstruction appears only after taking the maximal $C^*$-cover.  Hartz
constructs the Toeplitz representation
\[
 \pi:\Balg\longrightarrow B(H^2\oplus H^2),
 \qquad
 \begin{bmatrix}f&0\\h&\overline g\end{bmatrix}
 \longmapsto
 \begin{bmatrix}T_f&0\\T_h&T_{\overline g}\end{bmatrix},
\]
and proves that $\pi$ is not a point-strong-operator limit of
finite-dimensional representations of $\Balg$.

If $C^*_{\max}(\Balg)$ were RFD, its universal property would extend $\pi$
to a $*$-representation of $C^*_{\max}(\Balg)$.  The Exel--Loring theorem
would approximate that $*$-representation point-strongly by
finite-dimensional $*$-representations.  Restriction to $\Balg$ would then
approximate $\pi$ point-strongly by finite-dimensional representations,
contradicting Hartz's theorem.

Thus
\[
 \boxed{\Balg\text{ is RFD, but }C^*_{\max}(\Balg)\text{ is not RFD}.}
\]
Hartz states this exact consequence as Corollary 1.4.

\section{Provenance and scope}

Hartz's arXiv submission followed Thompson's by sixteen months and explicitly
states that the example answers the existing maximal-$C^*$ question.  The
counterexample is especially sharp: the underlying algebra is unital and
$2$-subhomogeneous, while its $C^*$-envelope is the RFD algebra
$M_2(C(\T))$; it is specifically the \emph{maximal} $C^*$-cover that fails
to be RFD.

This is therefore classified as a full answer already present in later
literature, not as a new counterexample produced by this run.
