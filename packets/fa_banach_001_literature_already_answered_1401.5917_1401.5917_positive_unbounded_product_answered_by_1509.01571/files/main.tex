\paragraph{Status.}
The conjecture is answered affirmatively by Gustafson and Mortad,
Theorem~2.5 of arXiv:1509.01571.  Their conclusion is stronger than the
requested self-adjointness: it also identifies the closure of the reversed
product.

\begin{question}[Chellali--Mortad, arXiv:1401.5917, Section 3, printed p.~8]
Let $A$ and $B$ be self-adjoint operators on a complex Hilbert space.  Assume
that $B$ is bounded, $A\geq0$, and $AB$ is normal.  Must $AB$ be
self-adjoint?
\end{question}

The source explicitly says that neither a proof nor a counterexample was then
known.  Notice the domain asymmetry: $D(AB)=\{x:Bx\in D(A)\}$ need not equal
$D(A)=D(BA)$, and $BA$ need not be closed even when $AB$ is normal.

\begin{answer}[Gustafson--Mortad, arXiv:1509.01571, Theorem 2.5,
printed pp.~5--8]
Let $A$ and $B$ be self-adjoint, let $B$ be bounded, and suppose that
$A\geq0$ and $AB$ is normal.  Then $AB$ and $\overline{BA}$ are
self-adjoint, and
\[
   AB=\overline{BA}.
\]
\end{answer}

This theorem has exactly the source conjecture's hypotheses, so it gives a
direct affirmative answer without an additional invertibility, spectral-gap,
or closedness assumption on $BA$.

\paragraph{Idea of the answering proof.}
Because $B$ is bounded and self-adjoint,
\[
  (BA)^*=AB,\qquad \overline{BA}=(AB)^*.
\]
Normality of $AB$ therefore makes both $AB$ and $\overline{BA}$ normal.
Fuglede--Putnam applied to a suitable intertwining identity first yields
$B^2A\subset AB^2$, and a square-root commutativity theorem gives
$|B|A\subset A|B|$.  Writing the polar decomposition
$B=U|B|$ with $U=U^*=U^{-1}$, the authors establish the remaining
intertwining relations and deduce
\[
  BA\subset AB.
\]
Taking adjoints gives $(AB)^*\subset AB$.  Since a normal operator and its
adjoint have the same domain, this forces $AB=(AB)^*$; the identity
$AB=\overline{BA}$ then follows from the first display.

\paragraph{Identification check.}
The later paper's introduction restates two questions with $A\geq0$ and
$B$ bounded self-adjoint: the $AB$-normal and $BA$-normal cases.  It cites
Chellali--Mortad directly, labels these as its main questions, and proves the
$AB$ case in Theorem~2.5.  Its Proposition~2.14 also uses that theorem to
answer the source paper's related $\lambda$-commutativity problem.
