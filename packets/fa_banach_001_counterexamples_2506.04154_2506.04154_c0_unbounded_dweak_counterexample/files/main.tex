\fbox{\parbox{0.92\textwidth}{\textbf{Status: candidate full counterexample, likely valid, subject to human review.}
In the Banach space $c_0$ with its supremum norm, the unbounded sequence
$x_n=n e_n$ converges $d$-weakly to $0$ in the sense introduced by
Guti\'errez and Nevanlinna.  More strongly, the amplitudes may be arbitrary,
and the same conclusion holds in every sup-norm coordinate space containing
the coordinate vectors.  This disproves Conjecture~1.5 of the source.}}
\end{center}

\section{Source conjecture}

For a pointed metric space $(X,d,0)$ and $w\in X$, the source defines the
internal metric functional
\[
 h_w(x)=d(x,w)-d(0,w).
\]
The metric functionals $X^\diamond$ are the pointwise closure of the internal
ones.  Thus every $h\in X^\diamond$ is the pointwise limit of a net
$(h_{w_\alpha})$.  A sequence $(x_n)$ converges \emph{$d$-weakly} to $z$ when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dweak}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty} h(x_n)\ge h(z)
 \qquad\text{for every }h\in X^\diamond.
\end{equation}
After proving that unbounded sequences cannot converge $d$-weakly in
$\ell_1$, in $C[0,1]$, or when the dual norm is strictly convex, Guti\'errez
and Nevanlinna state \cite[Conjecture~1.5]{source}:
\begin{quote}
``There is no normed linear space where unbounded sequences converge
$d$-weakly.''
\end{quote}
The source pages containing the definition and conjecture are reproduced in
Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}.

\section*{Idea of the proof}

An internal functional on a sup-norm space cannot be negative by a fixed
amount at two vectors supported on different coordinates.  Indeed, negativity
at $a e_i$ forces every coordinate of its centre except possibly $i$ to lie
strictly below the centre's supremum norm; negativity at $b e_j$, $j\ne i$,
also controls coordinate $i$.  Together they say every coordinate is below
the supremum by a uniform amount, a contradiction.  Pointwise convergence of
a net is simultaneous on any fixed finite set, so the same two-point exclusion
holds for every metric functional.  Consequently, along a sequence of
distinct coordinate spikes, any metric functional can dip below
$-\varepsilon$ at most once.  This is exactly the liminf condition at zero.

\section{The two-spike exclusion}

Let $I$ be an infinite set and let $Y$ be a real linear subspace of
$\ell_\infty(I)$, equipped with the inherited supremum norm, such that
$e_i\in Y$ for every $i\in I$.  This includes $c_0(I)$ and
$\ell_\infty(I)$.  We use basepoint $0$.

\begin{lemma}[internal two-spike exclusion]\label{lem:internal}
Let $w\in Y$, let $i\ne j$, let $a,b\in\mathbb R$, and let
$\varepsilon>0$.  It is impossible that both
\[
 h_w(ae_i)<-\varepsilon
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 h_w(be_j)<-\varepsilon.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $r=\lVert w\rVert_\infty$.  The first inequality says
\[
 \lVert ae_i-w\rVert_\infty<r-\varepsilon.
\]
In particular, $|w_k|<r-\varepsilon$ for every $k\ne i$.  The second
inequality similarly gives $|w_k|<r-\varepsilon$ for every $k\ne j$.
Because $i\ne j$, the two collections cover all $k\in I$.  Hence
\[
 \lVert w\rVert_\infty=\sup_{k\in I}|w_k|\le r-\varepsilon<r,
\]
a contradiction.  (If $r-\varepsilon\le0$, either original inequality is
already impossible.)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[closure preserves exclusion]\label{lem:closure}
For every metric functional $h\in Y^\diamond$, every $i\ne j$, every
$a,b\in\mathbb R$, and every $\varepsilon>0$, it is impossible that both
\[
 h(ae_i)<-\varepsilon
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 h(be_j)<-\varepsilon.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose both inequalities hold.  Choose a net of internal functionals
$h_{w_\alpha}$ converging pointwise to $h$.  Pointwise convergence on the
two-element set $\{ae_i,be_j\}$ supplies a common tail on which
\[
 h_{w_\alpha}(ae_i)<-\varepsilon/2,
 \qquad
 h_{w_\alpha}(be_j)<-\varepsilon/2.
\]
This contradicts Lemma~\ref{lem:internal}.
\end{proof}

\section{The counterexample}

\begin{theorem}[arbitrary coordinate amplitudes]\label{thm:main}
Let $(i_n)$ be pairwise distinct elements of $I$ and let $(a_n)$ be any real
sequence.  In the whole normed linear space $Y$,
\[
 a_ne_{i_n}\dweak 0.
\]
In particular, $x_n=n e_n$ is an unbounded $d$-weakly convergent sequence in
$c_0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every internal functional vanishes at the basepoint, so every metric
functional does as well: $h(0)=0$ for all $h\in Y^\diamond$.  Fix such an
$h$ and $\varepsilon>0$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:closure} shows that at most one index
$n$ can satisfy
\[
 h(a_ne_{i_n})<-\varepsilon.
\]
Therefore, for every $\varepsilon>0$, the sequence is eventually bounded below
by $-\varepsilon$.  It follows that
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}h(a_ne_{i_n})\ge0=h(0).
\]
Since this holds for every metric functional, definition~\eqref{eq:dweak}
gives $d$-weak convergence to zero.  Taking $Y=c_0$, $i_n=n$, and $a_n=n$
gives $\lVert x_n\rVert_\infty=n$, so the sequence is unbounded.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
Conjecture~1.5 of arXiv:2506.04154 is false.  It already fails in the classical
separable Banach space $c_0$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Checks, scope, and novelty}

The proof uses only the definitions and the elementary supremum-norm argument
in Lemma~\ref{lem:internal}.  Its delicate point is the passage from internals
to their pointwise closure: a convergent net is eventually close at both fixed
test points simultaneously, which is precisely what
Lemma~\ref{lem:closure} records.  No sequential compactness or explicit
classification of the metric compactification of $c_0$ is assumed.

The source itself observes that the non-linear metric subspace
$\{0,e_1,2e_2,3e_3,\ldots\}\subset\ell_1$ admits unbounded $d$-weak
convergence, while its Theorem~1.4 rules this out in the \emph{whole} space
$\ell_1$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} supplies exactly the missing ambient-linear
example.  It does not classify all unbounded $d$-weakly convergent sequences,
all Banach spaces admitting them, or their possible limits.

The bounded novelty check on 11 August 2026 searched the run registry and the
solution, attempt, and proof-gap indexes for the arXiv id and the central
$c_0$/coordinate-spike phrases.  Exact-title, exact-conjecture, and close-phrase
primary-source web searches found the source preprint and unrelated general
horofunction literature, but no later answer or matching counterexample.  This
is not an exhaustive priority claim.

\textbf{Human-review recommendation.} Check the quantifiers in
Lemma~\ref{lem:closure}, especially simultaneous approximation at two points by
one tail of the net.  Once that standard finite-intersection step is accepted,
the counterexample is decisive.
