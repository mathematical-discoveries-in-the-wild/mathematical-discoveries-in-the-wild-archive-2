\begin{abstract}
Arhancet asks whether a weak* continuous semigroup of hyper-factorizable
Markov maps always has a dilation on a hyperfinite von Neumann algebra.  We
prove three structural results.  First, the hypothesis itself forces the base
algebra to be semidiscrete; with separable predual it is therefore
hyperfinite.  This remains true if hyper-factorizability is assumed only for
positive times.  Second, every finite set of nonnegative rational times has
an exact simultaneous hyperfinite dilation, so no finite rational fragment
can witness failure.  Third, we give a sufficient condition that completes
the program: compatible hyperfinite discrete dilations along the factorial
meshes $1/n!$ have a hyperfinite inductive limit, whose rational action
extends to the required continuous real action.  The unsolved step is exactly
the construction of one infinite compatible tower.  Arbitrarily long finite
towers exist, but the ultraproduct compactness used in the source does not
preserve hyperfiniteness.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question and conventions}

Let $M$ be a von Neumann algebra with normal faithful state $\phi$.  A
$\phi$-Markov map $T:M\to M$ is \emph{hyper-factorizable} if there are a
hyperfinite von Neumann algebra $P$ with normal faithful state $\chi$ and
$(\phi,\chi)$-Markov embeddings $J_0,J_1:M\to P$ such that
\[
 T=J_0^*J_1.
\]
Here $J_0^*$ is the adjoint Markov map, equivalently the state-preserving
conditional expectation onto $J_0(M)$ followed by $J_0^{-1}$.

A weak* continuous semigroup $(T_t)_{t\geq0}$ is \emph{hyper-dilatable} if
there are a hyperfinite $(N,\psi)$, a $(\phi,\psi)$-Markov embedding
$J:M\to N$, and a weak* continuous state-preserving group
$(U_t)_{t\in\R}\subset\Aut(N)$ such that
\[
 T_t=J^*U_tJ,\qquad t\geq0.                         \tag{1}
\]
Remark 3.10 of Arhancet~\cite{Arhancet} asks whether pointwise
hyper-factorizability implies (1).

We use two standard preservation facts.  Hyperfinite von Neumann algebras
are semidiscrete.  Also, the discrete dilation construction for a
hyper-factorizable Markov map can be carried out through the hyperfinite
factorization algebra and countable state-preserving inductive limits; hence
it yields a hyperfinite discrete dilation.  This is the hyperfinite version
of the factorization--dilation construction in~\cite[Theorem 4.4]{HM} and is
also the finite-mesh input behind Remark 3.9 of~\cite{Arhancet}.

\section{The base algebra is already semidiscrete}

\begin{proposition}[Hyperfinite-base reduction]\label{prop:base}
Let $(T_t)_{t\geq0}$ be weak* continuous at $0$.  If $T_t$ is
hyper-factorizable for every $t>0$, then $M$ is semidiscrete.  In particular,
if $M$ has separable predual, then $M$ is hyperfinite.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We prove the finite-set form of semidiscreteness.  Fix a finite set
$F\subset M$, a finite set $\Omega\subset M_*$, and $\varepsilon>0$.
Weak* continuity gives $t>0$ such that
\[
 |\omega(T_t(x)-x)|<\varepsilon/2
 \quad(x\in F,\ \omega\in\Omega).                 \tag{2}
\]
Choose a hyperfinite factorization
\[
 M\xrightarrow{J_1}P\xrightarrow{J_0^*}M,
 \qquad T_t=J_0^*J_1.                              \tag{3}
\]
Since $P$ is semidiscrete, there are a matrix algebra $A$ and normal ucp
maps $a:P\to A$ and $b:A\to P$ such that
\[
 |\omega(J_0^*baJ_1(x)-J_0^*J_1(x))|<\varepsilon/2
 \quad(x\in F,\ \omega\in\Omega).                \tag{4}
\]
The ucp map $J_0^*baJ_1:M\to M$ factors through the matrix algebra $A$.
Combining (2)--(4) shows that it approximates $\id_M$ on $F$ in every
functional in $\Omega$ within $\varepsilon$.  Directing these choices by
$(F,\Omega,\varepsilon)$ gives a point-ultraweak approximation of $\id_M$
by normal ucp maps through matrix algebras.  Thus $M$ is semidiscrete.
For separable predual, semidiscreteness is equivalent to injectivity and to
hyperfiniteness.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
If the phrase ``every $T_t$'' includes $T_0=\id_M$, there is a shorter
argument: a hyperfinite factorization of $\id_M$ makes $M$ a ucp retract of a
hyperfinite algebra.  Proposition~\ref{prop:base} is stronger because it uses
only positive times and continuity at zero.
\end{remark}

\section{Every finite rational fragment is hyper-dilatable}

\begin{proposition}[Exact local compatibility]\label{prop:finite}
Assume every $T_t$, $t>0$, is hyper-factorizable.  Given a finite set
$F\subset\Q_+$, there are a hyperfinite $(N_F,\psi_F)$, a Markov embedding
$J_F:M\to N_F$, and a state-preserving automorphism $S_F$ such that
\[
 T_q=J_F^*S_F^{dq}J_F\qquad(q\in F)                \tag{5}
\]
for some positive integer $d$ with $dq\in\N$ for all $q\in F$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose a common denominator $d$.  The map $T_{1/d}$ is
hyper-factorizable, so its discrete factorization--dilation gives a
hyperfinite $(N_F,\psi_F,J_F,S_F)$ satisfying
\[
 J_F^*S_F^kJ_F=(T_{1/d})^k=T_{k/d}\qquad(k\geq0).
\]
Taking $k=dq$ proves (5).
\end{proof}

Thus all relations involving finitely many rational times can be realized in
one hyperfinite algebra.  The source's ultraproduct turns these locally
compatible models into one global rational action.  The obstruction is that
hyperfiniteness, unlike QWEP, is not preserved by that ultraproduct step.

\section{A compatible-root criterion for the full conclusion}

Put $h_n=1/n!$.  A \emph{factorial dilation tower} consists of hyperfinite
von Neumann algebras $(N_n,\psi_n)$ with separable preduals, Markov embeddings
$J_n:M\to N_n$, state-preserving automorphisms $S_n$, and Markov embeddings
$\iota_n:N_n\to N_{n+1}$ such that
\begin{align}
 J_n^*S_n^kJ_n&=T_{kh_n} &&(k\geq0),               \tag{6}\\
 \iota_nJ_n&=J_{n+1},                              \tag{7}\\
 \iota_nS_n&=S_{n+1}^{n+1}\iota_n.               \tag{8}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Compatible-root completion]\label{thm:tower}
Suppose $M$ has separable predual and $(T_t)_{t\geq0}$ admits a factorial
dilation tower.  Then the semigroup is hyper-dilatable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take the state-preserving von Neumann inductive limit
$(N_\infty,\psi)$ of the system $(N_n,\iota_n)$.  We identify each $N_n$
with its image.  The modular compatibility of Markov embeddings gives
state-preserving conditional expectations between the stages.  Since the
tower is countable, has separable preduals, and each stage is hyperfinite,
$N_\infty$ is hyperfinite.

Relations (8) define a state-preserving action $U:\Q\to\Aut(N_\infty)$.
Indeed, for $q\in\Q$ and $x\in N_m$, choose
$\ell\geq m$ with $q/h_\ell\in\mathbb Z$ and set
\[
 U_q(x)=S_\ell^{q/h_\ell}(x),                     \tag{9}
\]
where $x$ is viewed in $N_\ell$.  Since
$h_{\ell+1}=h_\ell/(\ell+1)$, relation (8) makes (9) independent of
$\ell$.  It also gives $U_{q+r}=U_qU_r$.

The embeddings $J_n$ induce a Markov embedding $J:M\to N_\infty$.
For $q\in\Q_+$, choose $n$ with $q/h_n\in\N$.  Equations (6) and (9) give
\[
 J^*U_qJ=T_q.                                      \tag{10}
\]
Moreover, for $x\in M$ and $q\downarrow0$ through $\Q_+$,
\begin{align*}
 \|U_qJ(x)-J(x)\|_{2,\psi}^2
 &=2\phi(x^*x)-2\operatorname{Re}\phi(x^*T_qx)
 \longrightarrow0.                                \tag{11}
\end{align*}
Applying (11) to $x^*$ gives strong* continuity at zero on $J(M)$; negative
$q$ follow by invariance.  Hence the same is true on every rational translate
$U_rJ(M)$.

Let
\[
 N_0=\big(\,U_qJ(M):q\in\Q\,\big)''\subset N_\infty.
\]
On bounded sets, the strong* topology is compatible with multiplication,
and the continuous vectors of an isometric group representation form a
closed subspace.  It follows first on the algebra generated by the translates
and then by strong* closure that $q\mapsto U_q(x)$ is strong* continuous for
every $x\in N_0$.  Thus $U|_{N_0}$ extends uniquely to a weak* continuous
group $(\widetilde U_t)_{t\in\R}$ of state-preserving automorphisms.

The algebra $N_0$ has separable predual and is invariant under the modular
group of $\psi$, because $J$ is Markov and every $U_q$ preserves $\psi$.
Takesaki's theorem therefore supplies a normal conditional expectation
$N_\infty\to N_0$.  Hence $N_0$ is injective; separability makes it
hyperfinite.  Finally, weak* continuity of both sides of (10), together with
density of $\Q_+$ in $\R_+$, yields
\[
 T_t=J^*\widetilde U_tJ\qquad(t\geq0).
\]
This is the required hyperfinite dilation.
\end{proof}

\section{How close the criterion is to being automatic}

For every $m$, choose a hyperfinite discrete dilation of
$T_{1/m!}$.  Restricting its shift to the powers
\[
 1,\quad m,\quad m(m-1),\quad\ldots,\quad m!
\]
produces a compatible tower of every prescribed finite length.  Therefore
all finite subsystems of (6)--(8) are satisfiable.  What is missing is one
infinite branch.  Ordinary compactness supplies such a branch only in an
ultraproduct, and a tracial ultraproduct of hyperfinite factors can be
noninjective.  Likewise, weak* limits of finite-dimensional moment models
can have noninjective GNS closures.  These facts explain both why the source's
QWEP argument works and why it does not settle the hyperfinite question.

Theorem~\ref{thm:tower} reduces the open problem to a concrete lifting issue:
choose the discrete dilations of the successive roots so that the coarse
shift is exactly the appropriate power of the fine shift under a Markov
embedding.  No finite-time obstruction remains.

\section{Scope and novelty guardrail}

The packet does not claim a full answer to Remark 3.10.  Its unconditional
content is Proposition~\ref{prop:base} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite}; Theorem~\ref{thm:tower} is a rigorous
completion criterion whose compatibility hypothesis is the remaining gap.

Eight focused upgrade attempts were made.  They tested direct preservation
of injectivity in the source ultraproduct, nested dyadic/factorial dilations,
compact path-state models, product-system ideas, and counterexamples based on
noninjective minimal path algebras.  The detailed attempt log records why
each unresolved route returns to the same coherence obstruction.

On 11 August 2026, bounded novelty searches covered the run's cheap indexes,
the exact question and terminology, and all 17 citing works reported by
OpenAlex for the published paper.  They found special positive results for
Schur and Fourier multipliers and work on individual absolute dilations, but
no later full answer to Remark 3.10 and no occurrence of the
semidiscrete-base reduction above.  This is a novelty guardrail, not a
priority claim.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Review as a substantial partial result.  The main checks are: (i) the
matrix-factorization net in Proposition~\ref{prop:base}; (ii) preservation of
hyperfiniteness in the standard discrete dilation of one hyper-factorizable
map; and (iii) the strong*-continuous extension and expected-subalgebra step
in Theorem~\ref{thm:tower}.
