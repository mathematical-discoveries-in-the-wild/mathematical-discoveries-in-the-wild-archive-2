\section{Source question and notation}

Martin, Menet, and Puig asked in Question 5.1 of
\cite{MMP} whether two reiteratively hypercyclic operators can be disjoint
hypercyclic but not disjoint reiteratively hypercyclic, and whether unilateral
weighted shifts on $c_0(\N)$ can provide such an example.  Ri, Ju, and Kim
quote the question as Question 1.1 and rule out the phenomenon on reflexive
Banach spaces \cite{RJK}.  Since $c_0$ is nonreflexive, their result does not
settle the weighted-shift part.

Let $B_{w_s}$ be a unilateral weighted backward shift on $c_0(\N)$,
with bounded nonzero weights, and put
\[
 W^s_{j,n}=\prod_{\nu=1}^{n}w_{s,j+\nu}\qquad(j,n\geq1),
 \quad W^s_{j,0}=1.
\]
Thus $(B_{w_s}^n x)_j=W^s_{j,n}x_{j+n}$.  Recall that $B_{w_s}$ is
mixing precisely when
\[
 |W^s_{j,n}|\longrightarrow\infty\quad(n\to\infty)
 \qquad\text{for every fixed }j.                         \tag{M}
\]
For $A\subset\N$, $\Bd(A)$ denotes its upper Banach density.

\section{Idea of the proof}

Disjoint hypercyclicity allows one to prescribe, at one arbitrarily late
time $n$, all weight-product ratios on any finite set of coordinates.
Prescribe the data not only at $j\leq l$, but at the shifted coordinates
$j+rM$.  The cocycle identity then transports those prescriptions to every
time $n+rM$.  Repeating this with block lengths tending to infinity produces
a common time set with upper Banach density at least $1/M$.

The remaining condition in the $c_0$ characterization is a cross-tail
estimate.  If two selected times differ by $d$, its ratio factors as a
bounded same-time ratio divided by $W^s_{j,d}$.  The selected set is
$M$-separated, and mixing makes the denominator uniformly large for every
$d\geq M$.  This is the step unavailable under individual reiterative
hypercyclicity alone.

\section{Finite ratio interpolation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:interp}
Suppose $B_{w_1},\ldots,B_{w_N}$ are disjoint hypercyclic on $c_0(\N)$.
For every $L,R\geq1$, every nonzero scalars
$c_{s,j}$ ($s\in[N]$, $j\in[L]$), and every $\eta>0$, there is $n\geq R$
such that
\[
 \left|\frac{W^s_{j,n}}{W^t_{j,n}}-
             \frac{c_{s,j}}{c_{t,j}}\right|<\eta
 \quad(s,t\in[N],\ j\in[L]).                              \tag{1}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $x$ be a disjoint hypercyclic vector.  Every tail of the diagonal orbit
$((B_{w_1}^n x,\ldots,B_{w_N}^n x))_{n\geq0}$ is dense.  Hence, for an
arbitrarily small $\delta>0$, some $n\geq R$ satisfies
\[
 |W^s_{j,n}x_{j+n}-c_{s,j}|<\delta
 \quad(s\in[N],\ j\in[L]).
\]
All $c_{s,j}$ are nonzero.  Taking $\delta$ sufficiently small and dividing
the $s$ and $t$ coordinate inequalities gives (1), simultaneously for the
finitely many ordered pairs.
\end{proof}

\section{The mixing $c_0$ theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $N\geq2$.  If $B_{w_1},\ldots,B_{w_N}$ are mixing unilateral weighted
backward shifts on $c_0(\N)$ and the tuple is disjoint hypercyclic, then the
tuple is disjoint reiteratively hypercyclic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We verify Theorem 3.6 of \cite{MMP} for the Furstenberg family of sets of
positive upper Banach density.

Fix $l\geq1$, nonzero target coefficients
$a_{s,j}$ ($s\in[N]$, $j\in[l]$), and $\varepsilon>0$.  Replacing
$\varepsilon$ by $\min\{\varepsilon,1/2\}$ causes no loss.  Choose $K\geq1$
so large that
\[
 \left|\frac{a_{s,j}}{a_{t,j}}\right|+1\leq K
 \quad(s,t\in[N],\ j\in[l]).                               \tag{2}
\]
By (M), there is an integer $M>l$ such that
\[
 |W^s_{j,d}|>K/\varepsilon
 \quad(s\in[N],\ j\in[l],\ d\geq M).                     \tag{3}
\]

For every $q\geq1$, set $L_q=l+qM$.  The sets of coordinates
$\{j+rM:j\in[l]\}$, $0\leq r\leq q$, are pairwise disjoint.  Define
nonzero scalars on $[L_q]$ by
\[
 b^{(q)}_{s,j+rM}=\frac{a_{s,j}}{W^s_{j,rM}}
 \quad(j\in[l],\ 0\leq r\leq q),                         \tag{4}
\]
and put $b^{(q)}_{s,i}=1$ on unused coordinates.  Lemma
\ref{lem:interp}, with a tolerance small enough to absorb the finitely many
factors $W^s_{j,rM}/W^t_{j,rM}$, gives a time $n_q$ for which
\[
 \left|\frac{W^s_{j,n_q+rM}}{W^t_{j,n_q+rM}}-
             \frac{a_{s,j}}{a_{t,j}}\right|<\varepsilon
 \quad\substack{s,t\in[N],\ j\in[l],\ 0\leq r\leq q}.   \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, this follows from (4) and the exact cocycle identity
\[
 \frac{W^s_{j,n_q+rM}}{W^t_{j,n_q+rM}}
 =\frac{W^s_{j,rM}}{W^t_{j,rM}}
  \frac{W^s_{j+rM,n_q}}{W^t_{j+rM,n_q}}.                   \tag{6}
\]
Choose the $n_q$ successively so large that the blocks
\[
 A_q=\{n_q+rM:0\leq r\leq q\}
\]
are mutually separated by at least $M$, and let $A=\bigcup_{q\geq1}A_q$.
Every two distinct points of $A$ are at distance at least $M$.  Moreover,
for every window length $H$, every sufficiently long block $A_q$ contains
an interval of length $H$ with at least
$\lfloor(H-1)/M\rfloor+1$ selected points.  There are such blocks arbitrarily
far out.  Therefore
\[
 \Bd(A)\geq\lim_{H\to\infty}
 \frac{\lfloor(H-1)/M\rfloor+1}{H}=\frac1M>0.               \tag{7}
\]

We now check the three conditions in Theorem 3.6 of \cite{MMP}.  First,
(M) and $n\to\infty$ along $A$ give
$|W^s_{j,n}|\to\infty$ for every $s$ and $j\leq l$.  Third, (5) is exactly
the required same-time ratio approximation.

For the cross-tail condition, take $m,n\in A$, $s,t\in[N]$, and $j\leq l$
such that
\[
 j+m\in n+\{l+1,l+2,\ldots\}.
\]
Then $m>n$; because $A$ is $M$-separated, $d=m-n\geq M$.  From (2) and
(5), after initially choosing the approximation tolerance below $1$, all
same-time ratios appearing below are bounded by $K$.  The cocycle identity
gives
\begin{align*}
 \frac{|W^s_{j+d,n}|}{|W^t_{j,m}|}
 &=\frac{|W^s_{j,m}|}{|W^t_{j,m}|\,|W^s_{j,d}|}\\
 &<\frac{K}{K/\varepsilon}=\varepsilon,                    \tag{8}
\end{align*}
where (3) was used in the last step.  Thus all three conditions hold on the
same $A$ of positive upper Banach density.  The cited characterization
proves disjoint reiterative hypercyclicity.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, upgrade audit, and verification}

For two shifts Theorem \ref{thm:main} directly rules out the requested gap
whenever both shifts are mixing.  It does not settle the source question:
individual reiterative hypercyclicity on $c_0$ need not provide (3).  Attempts
to replace mixing by chaos, frequent hypercyclicity, or reiterative
hypercyclicity leave the same obstruction: the difference set of the common
positive-upper-Banach-density set need not lie inside the times where every
relevant product is large.

Eight focused upgrade routes were recorded in the accompanying attempt log.
The final route tried to force entire arithmetic-block difference sets into
the individual good-product sets; no available density principle justifies
that step.  The honest status is therefore a complete theorem for the mixing
subclass, not a full answer.

The script \texttt{code/verify\_block\_identities.py} checks (6), the
cross-tail factorization in (8), and the finite arithmetic-block densities
for deterministic random complex weights.  This is a sanity check, not part
of the proof.  The proof itself is symbolic and rests on the published
$c_0$ characterization.

A bounded novelty search on August 11, 2026 used arXiv/web exact phrases for
``mixing unilateral weighted shifts,'' ``disjoint reiteratively
hypercyclic,'' ``$c_0$,'' and ``upper Banach density.''  It found
\cite{MMP}, \cite{RJK}, and general background on disjoint hypercyclic
weighted shifts, but no matching statement.  Novelty confidence is moderate,
not exhaustive.
