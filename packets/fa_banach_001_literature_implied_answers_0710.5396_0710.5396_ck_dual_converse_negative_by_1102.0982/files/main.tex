\section*{Status}

\texttt{literature\_implied\_answer}; full negative answer.  Both questions
in Problem 2.13 of Richard J. Smith, \emph{Gruenhage compacta and strictly
convex dual norms}, arXiv:0710.5396, are false.

\section*{Source question}

On PDF page 9, at the end of Section 2, Problem 2.13 asks:
\begin{quote}
If $C(K)^*$ admits a strictly convex dual norm then is $K$ Gruenhage?
More ambitiously, if $X^*$ is a dual Banach space with strictly convex dual
norm, is $B_{X^*}$ Gruenhage?
\end{quote}
The topology on a dual ball here is the weak-star topology.

\section*{Decisive later result}

Richard J. Smith, \emph{Tree Duplicates, $G_\delta$-diagonals and Gruenhage
spaces}, arXiv:1102.0982, gives the needed ZFC counterexample.  Its Theorem
1.4, quoted from Theorem 3.1 of Orihuela--Smith--Troyanski,
arXiv:1012.5595, says that for scattered compact $K$, property $(*)$ is
equivalent to the existence on $C(K)$ of an equivalent norm with strictly
convex dual norm.  Corollary 2.5 then states that the one-point compactification
$K$ of the constructed space $D$ is scattered, compact, non-Gruenhage, and has
$(*)$; consequently $C(K)$ admits an equivalent lattice norm with strictly
convex dual norm.

Thus $C(K)^*$ has a strictly convex dual norm although $K$ is not Gruenhage,
which answers the first question negatively.

\section*{Why the same example answers the ambitious question}

Let $X=C(K)$ with the equivalent predual norm from Corollary 2.5, and write
$\vert\!\vert\!\vert\cdot\vert\!\vert\!\vert$ for its strictly convex dual
norm.  Equivalence with the canonical dual norm gives
\[
 M:=\sup_{x\in K}\vert\!\vert\!\vert\delta_x\vert\!\vert\!\vert<\infty.
\]
Choose a fixed $a>M$.  The map
\[
 K\longrightarrow B_{X^*},\qquad x\longmapsto a^{-1}\delta_x
\]
is a weak-star homeomorphic embedding: each coordinate against $f\in C(K)$
is $a^{-1}f(x)$, and compactness of $K$ makes the continuous injection a
homeomorphism onto its image.  Gruenhage spaces are hereditary, since one may
restrict every witnessing open family to a subspace.  If $(B_{X^*},w^*)$
were Gruenhage, its embedded copy of $K$ would be Gruenhage, contradicting
Corollary 2.5.  Hence the ambitious dual-ball assertion is false as well.

\section*{Provenance and scope}

The later paper cites Smith's 2009 article and states the exact $C(K)$
counterexample, but does not label Corollary 2.5 as an answer to Problem 2.13.
The first implication is immediate from that corollary; the second uses the
fixed-scalar Dirac embedding above.  This is therefore a literature-implied
answer rather than a newly discovered counterexample.  Both parts of the
source problem are settled; no stated subcase remains open.
