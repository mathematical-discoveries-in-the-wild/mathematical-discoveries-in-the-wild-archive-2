## Exact match

- The source question asks whether, for \(M=\mathbb R^d\) and translation
  invariant \(I\), only translation-invariant linear operators are needed in
  the min–max formula.
- The later paper’s informal Theorem 2 says precisely that, when \(I\)
  commutes with translations, all Lévy operators in the formula commute with
  translations.
- Its formal Theorem 1.10 gives constant \(f_{ab}\) and linear
  translation-invariant \(L_{ab}:C_b^2(\mathbb R^d)\to C_b^0(\mathbb R^d)\)
  with constant coefficients.
- The proof of Theorem 1.10 explicitly starts from \(F(u)=I(u,0)\) and uses
  \(I(u,x)=F(\tau_xu)\), so there is no merely adjacent implication.
