\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{New partial result; pointwise differentiability remains open.}
For order isomorphisms between differentiable function spaces on real
intervals, the associated homeomorphism cannot have a cusp, a flat point, or
an infinite metric slope.  It and its inverse are locally absolutely
continuous, and its derivative is finite and nonzero almost everywhere.

\section*{Source question}
Leung and Tang \cite{source} ask whether the homeomorphism associated to an
order isomorphism \(C^p(X)\to C^p(Y)\) must be differentiable, or even
\(C^p\).  They emphasize that this is unknown already for \(p=1\) and
\(X=Y=\R\), and separately ask whether different smoothness orders can be
order-isomorphic.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}
\noindent\emph{Source evidence: arXiv:1408.4930, p.~5.}

Let \(I,J\subset\R\) be nonempty open intervals and let
\(p,q\in\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}\), \(p,q\ge1\).  The representation theorem
of \cite{source} associates to every order isomorphism
\(T:C^p(I)\to C^q(J)\) a homeomorphism \(\varphi:I\to J\) and increasing
homeomorphisms \(\Phi(y,\cdot):\R\to\R\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:representation}
 Tf(y)=\Phi\bigl(y,f(\varphi^{-1}(y))\bigr).
\end{equation}
For fixed \(t\), the function \(y\mapsto\Phi(y,t)=T\bar t(y)\) lies in
\(C^q(J)\), where \(\bar t\) denotes the constant function.

\section*{Idea of the proof}
Suppose \(\varphi\) flattened a sequence: its output increments
\(\delta_n\) would be negligible compared with input increments
\(\varepsilon_n\).  Transforming the affine pencil
\(c\mapsto c\pm(x-x_0)\) says that each vertical homeomorphism
\(H_n(t)=\Phi(\varphi(x_n),t)\) maps every scalar interval of length
\(\varepsilon_n\) to length \(O_c(\delta_n)\).  Baire category makes the
constant uniform on a whole scalar interval.  Partitioning that interval
into steps of length \(\varepsilon_n\) would collapse a fixed positive
vertical distance to zero, contradicting monotonicity and convergence of the
constant images.  Applying the same argument to \(T^{-1}\) gives the upper
metric bound.  A countable Lipschitz-piece argument and Banach--Zarecki then
give absolute continuity in both directions.

\begin{lemma}[Baire partition obstruction]\label{lem:baire}
For every \(x_0\in I\),
\[
 \liminf_{x\to x_0}
 \frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(x_0)|}{|x-x_0|}>0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Assume otherwise.  There are \(x_n\to x_0\), \(x_n\ne x_0\), such that,
with
\[
 \varepsilon_n=|x_n-x_0|,
 \qquad
 \delta_n=|\varphi(x_n)-\varphi(x_0)|,
\]
one has \(\delta_n/\varepsilon_n\to0\).  Pass to a subsequence on one side
of \(x_0\), and choose \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\) so that
\(\sigma(x_n-x_0)=\varepsilon_n\).  Put
\[
 y_n=\varphi(x_n),\quad y_0=\varphi(x_0),\quad
 H_n(t)=\Phi(y_n,t).
\]
For every \(c\in\R\), take the affine function
\(f_c(x)=c+\sigma(x-x_0)\).  Formula \eqref{eq:representation} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:increment}
 H_n(c+\varepsilon_n)-H_n(c)
 =Tf_c(y_n)-T\bar c(y_n).
\end{equation}
The function \(Tf_c-T\bar c\) belongs to \(C^1(J)\) and vanishes at
\(y_0\).  Therefore, for each fixed \(c\), there are integers \(M,N\) such
that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pointwise}
 0\le H_n(c+\varepsilon_n)-H_n(c)\le M\delta_n
 \qquad(n\ge N).
\end{equation}

Fix a compact scalar interval \(K\) with nonempty interior.  For positive
integers \(M,N\), let \(E_{M,N}\subset K\) be the set of all \(c\) for which
\eqref{eq:pointwise} holds for every \(n\ge N\).  Each \(E_{M,N}\) is closed,
because every \(H_n\) is continuous, and their countable union is \(K\).
Baire's theorem supplies \(M,N\) and a nondegenerate interval
\([a,b]\subset E_{M,N}\).

Let \(r_n=\lfloor(b-a)/\varepsilon_n\rfloor\).  Since \(H_n\) is increasing,
partitioning from \(a\) into \(\varepsilon_n\)-steps and allowing one final
overshoot gives, for all large \(n\),
\begin{align*}
 0<H_n(b)-H_n(a)
 &\le \sum_{j=0}^{r_n}
 \bigl[H_n(a+(j+1)\varepsilon_n)-H_n(a+j\varepsilon_n)\bigr]\\
 &\le M(r_n+1)\delta_n\\
 &\le M\left(\frac{b-a}{\varepsilon_n}+1\right)\delta_n
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{align*}
On the other hand, for fixed \(t\),
\(H_n(t)=T\bar t(y_n)\to T\bar t(y_0)=H_0(t)\).  Hence the left side
converges to \(H_0(b)-H_0(a)>0\), because \(H_0\) is an increasing
homeomorphism.  This contradiction proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[pointwise metric regularity]\label{thm:metric}
For every \(x_0\in I\),
\[
 0<\liminf_{x\to x_0}
 \frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(x_0)|}{|x-x_0|}
 \le
 \limsup_{x\to x_0}
 \frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(x_0)|}{|x-x_0|}<\infty.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:baire} is the first inequality.  Apply the same lemma to the
order isomorphism \(T^{-1}:C^q(J)\to C^p(I)\), whose associated homeomorphism
is \(\varphi^{-1}\).  Thus, near \(y_0=\varphi(x_0)\),
\[
 |\varphi^{-1}(y)-x_0|\ge c|y-y_0|
\]
for some \(c>0\).  Substituting \(y=\varphi(x)\) gives the finite upper
bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[bi-absolute continuity]\label{cor:ac}
The restrictions of \(\varphi\) and \(\varphi^{-1}\) to compact
subintervals are absolutely continuous.  Moreover,
\[
 0<|\varphi'(x)|<\infty
 \quad\text{for almost every }x\in I.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix a compact interval \(L\Subset I\).  The finite upper metric slope in
Theorem~\ref{thm:metric} covers \(L\) by countably many sets \(E_{m,n}\) on
which
\[
 |\varphi(z)-\varphi(x)|\le m|z-x|
 \quad\text{whenever }x,z\in L, |z-x|<1/n.
\]
After subdividing \(L\) into finitely many intervals of length less than
\(1/n\), \(\varphi\) is \(m\)-Lipschitz on each corresponding piece of
\(E_{m,n}\).  Hence \(\varphi\) maps every null subset of \(L\) to a null
set.  A real homeomorphism is monotone; the Banach--Zarecki theorem
\cite{royden} now implies that \(\varphi|_L\) is absolutely continuous.
The same argument applies to \(\varphi^{-1}\).

Absolute continuity gives a finite derivative almost everywhere.  If
\(Z=\{x:\varphi'(x)=0\}\) had positive measure, absolute continuity would
give measure zero to \(\varphi(Z)\).  The null-set property of
\(\varphi^{-1}\) would then give measure zero to
\(Z=\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(Z))\), a contradiction.  Thus the derivative is
nonzero almost everywhere.
\end{proof}

\section*{Scope and full-upgrade audit}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.32\textwidth}X@{}}
\toprule
Route & Outcome\\
\midrule
Cusp or singular coordinate counterexample & Excluded by the Baire partition
obstruction and its inverse.\\
Pointwise metric slope & Positive lower and finite upper slope at every point.\\
Measure-theoretic upgrade & Both coordinate directions are locally absolutely
continuous; the derivative is nonzero and finite almost everywhere.\\
Affine-pencil tangent limits & Blocked by the possible singularity of the
vertical homeomorphism \(t\mapsto\Phi(y_0,t)\).\\
Higher-order nonnegative contact & Gives additional small-o estimates but no
unique first-order tangent for \(\varphi\).\\
Different smoothness orders & The theorem works for arbitrary \(p,q\ge1\),
but does not rule out \(p\ne q\).\\
Jointly \(C^1\) vertical factor & Would imply local \(C^1\) regularity by the
implicit function theorem, but is not forced by the source hypotheses.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Thus the source's pointwise differentiability, \(C^p\)-regularity, and
distinct-order questions remain open.  The result nevertheless rules out the
entire singular-homeomorphism and cusp classes as possible associated maps.

\paragraph{Novelty and verification.}
Exact-title, exact-question, \(C^1(\R)\), metric-slope,
bi-absolute-continuity, and later order-isomorphism searches through
11 August 2026 found no statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:metric} or
Corollary~\ref{cor:ac}.  Human review should focus on the Baire uniformization,
the final overshooting interval in the telescoping sum, and the countable
Lipschitz-piece proof of the null-set property.
