\section{Source question and answer}

Let $\M_{n,m}$ be the real $n\times m$ matrices, $n\ge m>1$, with
$|y|_m=\det(y'y)^{1/2}$.  For the orders considered in the source,
the matrix Riesz potential is
\[
 I^\alpha f(x)=\int_{\M_{n,m}}f(x-y)\,d\mu_\alpha(y).
\]
If $a=\operatorname{Re}\alpha>m-1$, absolute convergence is governed,
up to a harmless constant, by
$d\nu_a(y)=|y|_m^{a-n}\,dy$.  If $\alpha=k$ is one of the positive
integral Wallach orders, $1\le k\le\min(m-1,n-m)$, $\mu_k$ is the
positive rank-$k$ measure displayed in formula (2.15) of the source.

Theorem 2.5 of \cite{OOR} proves a.e. absolute convergence for every
$f\in L^p$ when
\[
 1\le p<\frac{n}{a+m-1},
\]
and asks whether this restriction is necessary for $m>1$:

\begin{center}
 \includegraphics[width=0.94\linewidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

It is not necessary.  Write
\[
 D=n+m-1,\qquad A=a+m-1,
 \qquad p_c=\frac{D}{A}.
\]
The meaningful range of the question is $0<a<n-m+1$, so $1<p_c<\infty$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp threshold]\label{thm:main}
Let $n\ge m>1$ and let $\alpha\ne0$ be either
\begin{enumerate}
 \item a positive integral Wallach order $k\le\min(m-1,n-m)$, with
       $a=k$, or
 \item an order with $m-1<a=\operatorname{Re}\alpha<n-m+1$ for which
       the density representation above is defined.
\end{enumerate}
Then the following are sharp.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item If $1\le p<p_c=(n+m-1)/(a+m-1)$, then the defining integral for
       $I^\alpha f$ converges absolutely for almost every $x$ for every
       $f\in L^p(\M_{n,m})$.
 \item If $p_c\le p\le\infty$, there is a nonnegative
       $f\in L^p(\M_{n,m})$ for which the absolute potential is infinite
       at every point of a fixed ball.
\end{enumerate}
For $\alpha=0$, of course, $I^0f=f$, so there is no restriction on $p$.
\end{theorem}

Since $m>1$,
\[
 \frac{n+m-1}{a+m-1}>\frac{n}{a+m-1}.
\]
Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:main} both answers the source question negatively and
identifies the exact replacement.

\section{The smoothed-kernel lemma}

Let $s_1(x)\ge\cdots\ge s_m(x)$ be the singular values of $x$ and put
$d=n-a$.  Let $g(x)=e^{-\tr(x'x)}$.  The analytic input is the following
standard matrix-beta estimate, included with its reduction for auditability.

\begin{lemma}[Gaussian smoothing]\label{lem:smooth}
For every order in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, with total variation used in the
nonreal case,
\[
 (|\mu_\alpha|*g)(x)
 \le C\prod_{j=1}^m(1+s_j(x)^2)^{-d/2}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Consequently $|\mu_\alpha|*g\in L^q(\M_{n,m})$ whenever
\[
 dq>n+m-1. \tag{2}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First suppose $m-1<a<n-m+1$.  The heat-kernel representation of the
matrix Riesz measure (equivalently, formula (5.8) in \cite{Rubin}) and the
change of variables $r=t^{-1}$ give, with $S=x'x/4$,
\[
 (\nu_a*g)(x)=C\!\int_{0<r<I_m}
 e^{-\tr(Sr)}|r|^{d/2-(m+1)/2}
 |I_m-r|^{a/2-(m+1)/2}\,dr. \tag{3}
\]
Both beta parameters exceed $(m-1)/2$.  Diagonalize $S$ and successively
use Schur-complement coordinates in the matrix beta integral.  At each
stage the only unbounded eigenvalue-dependent factor is the scalar beta
Laplace integral
\[
 \int_0^1e^{-\lambda u}u^{d/2-1}(1-u)^b\,du
 \le C_b(1+\lambda)^{-d/2},\qquad b>-1.
\]
The remaining Schur-complement beta integral has the same form in one lower
dimension.  Induction on $m$ therefore yields
\[
 \int_{0<r<I_m}e^{-\tr(Sr)}\,d\beta_{d/2,a/2}(r)
 \le C\prod_{j=1}^m(1+\lambda_j(S))^{-d/2},
\]
which is (1).

For an integral order $a=k$, insert the rank-$k$ representation (2.15) of
\cite{OOR} and first perform the Gaussian integral over
$\M_{k,m}$.  This gives
\[
 (\mu_k*g)(x)=C\int_{V_{n,k}}
 \exp\{-\tr[x'(I-vv')x]\}\,dv. \tag{4}
\]
The same Schur-complement induction, now on the Stiefel chart, gives (1)
with $d=n-k$.  Concretely, when one singular direction of $x$ has size
$s$, the set of $k$-frames within angle $O(s^{-1})$ of that direction has
codimension $n-k$ and contributes $O(s^{-(n-k)})$; conditioning on it
leaves the identical estimate for the orthogonal complement.  Iteration
over the singular directions gives the product in (1).  This also covers
the boundary (singular-beta) cases of (3).

It remains to check integrability.  Polar decomposition on $\M_{n,m}$
reduces the $q$th power of the right side of (1) to
\[
 C\int_{r>0}|r|^{n/2-(m+1)/2}
 |I_m+r|^{-dq/2}\,dr. \tag{5}
\]
The matrix beta integral (5) is finite exactly when
$dq>n+m-1$.  The necessity can already be seen by letting only its largest
eigenvalue tend to infinity.  This proves (2).
\end{proof}

\section{Sufficiency up to the sharp exponent}

It is enough to consider $f\ge0$.  Tonelli's theorem and translation
invariance give
\[
 \int_{\M_{n,m}}g(x)(|\mu_\alpha|*f)(x)\,dx
 =\int_{\M_{n,m}}f(z)(|\mu_\alpha|*g)(z)\,dz. \tag{6}
\]
For $p=1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth} makes the right side at most
$C\|f\|_1$.  For $1<p<p_c$, the inequality $p<p_c$ is equivalent to
\[
 d p'>n+m-1.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth} and H\"older's inequality again make (6) finite.
Since $g$ is strictly positive everywhere, the absolute potential is finite
for almost every $x$.  This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(1).

\section{Sharp counterexamples}

Set
\[
 E_R=\{y\in\M_{n,m}:R<s_1(y)<2R,\ s_2(y)<2\},\qquad R\ge10.
\]
A tubular parametrization of the rank-one stratum gives
\[
 cR^D\le |E_R|\le CR^D,\qquad D=n+m-1. \tag{7}
\]
Indeed a nonzero rank-one matrix has $D$ parameters (including radius),
and the remaining normal directions are confined to a fixed-size tube.

Take $R_j=4^j$, so the $E_{R_j}$ are disjoint, and put
$f=\sum_ja_j\mathbf1_{E_{R_j}}$.  Fix a sufficiently small ball $B$.
If $x\in B$ and $y\in E_R$, Weyl's singular-value inequality gives
$s_1(x-y)\le3R$ and $s_i(x-y)\le3$ for $i\ge2$.  In the density case,
therefore,
\[
 |x-y|_m^{a-n}\ge cR^{-d}.
\]
Together with (7), the $j$th block contributes at least
\[
 c a_jR_j^{D-d}=c a_jR_j^{a+m-1}=c a_jR_j^A \tag{8}
\]
to the absolute potential at every $x\in B$.

For an integral order $a=k$, use formula (2.15) of \cite{OOR}.  Restrict
the inner $\M_{k,m}$ integral to matrices with largest singular value in
$[6R/5,9R/5]$ and second singular value below $1$.  Their measure is
comparable to $R^{k+m-1}=R^A$, uniformly in the orthogonal parameter, and
adding $x\in B$ places them in $E_R$.  Hence (8) holds in the integral-order
case as well.

If $p=p_c$, choose $a_j=R_j^{-A}/j$.  Then
\[
 \|f\|_p^p\asymp\sum_j a_j^pR_j^D=\sum_jj^{-p}<\infty,
 \qquad
 (|\mu_\alpha|*f)(x)\ge c\sum_jj^{-1}=\infty
\]
for $x\in B$.  If $p>p_c$, take
$a_j=R_j^{-D/p}j^{-2/p}$; the $L^p$ sum is finite while the terms in
(8) grow exponentially.  For $p=\infty$, take $a_j=1$.
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(2).

\section{Scope and novelty check}

The proof concerns absolute convergence of the defining convolution, exactly
the meaning used around Theorem 2.5 of the source.  It handles both the
ordinary density range relevant to the displayed question and every positive
integral Wallach order.  The zero order is the explicit identity exception.

A bounded search on 12 August 2026 covered the exact title and arXiv id,
the displayed exponent, ``Riesz potentials on matrix spaces'', and the two
candidate thresholds.  It found the source, Rubin's expanded 2006 treatment,
and later book material still repeating the old sufficient condition, but no
paper proving the sharp criterion above.  Novelty is plausible, not certified.
Human review should focus on the Schur-complement induction in
Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth}, particularly its singular-beta/Stiefel boundary case.
