\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{New partial result; the original deterministic conjecture remains
open.}
The 2025 counterexample to the Gaussian hyperplane-tessellation conjecture is
not a counterexample to Plan--Vershynin's original, not-necessarily-Gaussian
conjecture.  In fact, the same lifted thin-cylinder set which needs order
\(\eta^{-3}\) iid Gaussian hyperplanes has an explicit deterministic
\(\eta\)-uniform tessellation using only order \(\eta^{-1}\) hyperplanes, and
this deterministic order is sharp.

\section*{The two conjectures}
For \(K\subset\Sph^{N-1}\), let \(m(K,\eta)\) be the least number of
homogeneous hyperplanes for which the fraction separating any \(x,y\in K\)
approximates their normalized geodesic distance
\[
 d_{\Sph}(x,y)=\pi^{-1}\arccos\langle x,y\rangle
\]
within additive error \(\eta\).  Plan and Vershynin \cite{pv} conjectured that
\[
 \sup_{\eta>0}\eta^2m(K,\eta)\asymp w_*(K)^2,
 \qquad
 w_*(K)=\mathbb E\sup_{x\in K}|\langle g,x\rangle|.
\]
Their upper conjecture permits arbitrary hyperplanes.

Dirksen and Strachan \cite{ds} isolate the later, stronger claim that
\(\eta^{-2}w_*(K)^2\) \emph{iid Gaussian} hyperplanes always suffice.  They
disprove that claim by constructing sets \(S_\eta\) for which the Gaussian
threshold is \(\Theta(\eta^{-3}w_*(S_\eta)^2)\).  Their introduction
explicitly distinguishes this from the original not-necessarily-Gaussian
question.

\section*{An explicit thin-cylinder theorem}
For \(\lambda>0\), define the spherical lift
\[
 Q_\lambda(u)=\frac{(u,\lambda)}
 {\sqrt{\|u\|_2^2+\lambda^2}}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[axis-plane grid]\label{thm:grid}
Let \(a,r,\lambda>0\), \(n\ge1\), and
\[
 S=Q_\lambda\bigl([-a,a]\oplus rB_2^n\bigr)
 \subset\Sph^{n+1}.
\]
For every integer \(m\ge1\), there is an explicit arrangement \(A_m\) of
\(m\) homogeneous hyperplanes such that
\[
 \sup_{x,y\in S}|d_{A_m}(x,y)-d_{\Sph}(x,y)|
 \le \frac{2}{m}+\frac{2r}{\lambda}.
\]
Consequently, if \(2r/\lambda\le\eta/2\), then
\(m(S,\eta)\le\lceil4/\eta\rceil\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(P\) be the two-dimensional plane spanned by the cylinder axis and the
lifting coordinate.  On the projective circle of homogeneous hyperplanes in
\(P\), choose \(m\) equally spaced normals.  For any two points \(u,v\) on
the great circle \(\Sph^{n+1}\cap P\), a hyperplane separates them exactly
when its projective zero lies in the interval between them.  Counting an
equally spaced grid in an arbitrary projective interval gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grid}
 |d_{A_m}(u,v)-d_{\Sph}(u,v)|\le 2/m,
\end{equation}
where the harmless constant \(2\) covers both endpoint conventions.

Write
\[
 x=Q_\lambda(t,z),\qquad x_0=Q_\lambda(t,0).
\]
Every selected normal lies in \(P\), so
\[
 \operatorname{sign}\langle h,x\rangle
 =\operatorname{sign}\langle h,x_0\rangle.
\]
Thus the sign map ignores the orthogonal fiber \emph{exactly}.  Moreover,
normalization on vectors of norm at least \(\lambda\) gives
\[
 \|x-x_0\|_2\le \frac{2\|z\|_2}{\lambda}.
\]
For unit vectors, normalized geodesic distance is at most half their chord
distance; hence \(d_{\Sph}(x,x_0)\le r/\lambda\).

For \(y=Q_\lambda(s,w)\) and \(y_0=Q_\lambda(s,0)\), the triangle inequality
yields
\[
 |d_{\Sph}(x,y)-d_{\Sph}(x_0,y_0)|
 \le d_{\Sph}(x,x_0)+d_{\Sph}(y,y_0)
 \le 2r/\lambda.
\]
Since \(d_{A_m}(x,y)=d_{A_m}(x_0,y_0)\), combining this with
\eqref{eq:grid} proves the result.
\end{proof}

The proof gives a more general transfer principle: whenever a retraction
\(\rho:S\to\Gamma\) moves points by at most \(r_0\) and the selected
hyperplanes assign the same sign to \(x\) and \(\rho(x)\), an
\(\varepsilon\)-uniform tessellation of \(\Gamma\) becomes an
\((\varepsilon+2r_0)\)-uniform tessellation of \(S\).

\section*{The 2025 Gaussian bad set is deterministically easy}
We now unwind the parameters in the proof of Theorem 1.4 of \cite{ds}.  Write
\(\eta\) for its final spherical accuracy and set
\[
 q=\sqrt{4\pi C_0\eta},\qquad
 \lambda=\frac{2C_0}{q}.
\]
The constructed set has the form
\[
 S_\eta
 =Q_\lambda\bigl([-3q,3q]\oplus r_\eta B_2^{n(\eta)}\bigr),
 \qquad
 r_\eta=O\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\log(1/\eta)}\right).
\]
Here the necessarily positive logarithmic denominator is the intended
reading of the radius displayed on p.~10 of \cite{ds}.  Therefore
\[
 \frac{2r_\eta}{\lambda}
 =O\!\left(\frac{\eta^{3/2}}{\log(1/\eta)}\right)=o(\eta).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:grid} gives \(m(S_\eta,\eta)=O(\eta^{-1})\).

This order is also necessary.  The two axial endpoints have normalized
geodesic distance
\[
 L_\eta=\frac{2}{\pi}\arctan\!\left(\frac{3q}{\lambda}\right)
 =\frac{2}{\pi}\arctan(6\pi\eta).
\]
For all sufficiently small \(\eta\),
\[
 6\eta\le L_\eta\le12\eta.
\]
In an \(\eta\)-uniform tessellation, their Hamming distance is \(k/m\) for
some integer \(k\ge1\) and satisfies
\[
 \frac1m\le\frac{k}{m}\le L_\eta+\eta\le13\eta.
\]
We have proved:

\begin{corollary}[deterministic/Gaussian separation]\label{cor:separation}
For the family \(S_\eta\) in \cite{ds},
\[
 m(S_\eta,\eta)=\Theta(\eta^{-1}).
\]
At the same time \(w_*(S_\eta)\asymp1\), while iid Gaussian hyperplanes need
and suffice in number
\(\Theta(\eta^{-3}w_*(S_\eta)^2)=\Theta(\eta^{-3})\).
\end{corollary}

\section*{Scope and full-upgrade audit}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.34\textwidth}X@{}}
\toprule
Route & Outcome\\
\midrule
Latest Gaussian counterexample & Defeated deterministically by
the axis-plane grid.\\
Thin tubes over low-dimensional bases & The sign-preserving retraction
transfers any base tessellation with only twice the tube-radius loss.\\
Optimized direction measure & Promising reformulation as wedge quadrature,
but no width-controlled equal-weight construction was found.\\
Deterministic lower bound from the Gaussian proof & Impossible by that
mechanism: its random small-shift quotient is absent for axis-plane normals.\\
Entropy/communication lower bounds & Controlled at the conjectural scale by
Sudakov; they do not produce a counterexample.\\
Multiscale chaining & Known net arguments retain a logarithm or an extra
power of \(\eta\); sign discontinuity blocks the full upgrade.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Thus Corollary~\ref{cor:separation} resolves the strongest current candidate
family in favor of deterministic hyperplanes, but it neither proves nor
disproves the original conjecture for arbitrary \(K\).

\paragraph{Novelty and verification.}
Exact-id, phrase, core-keyword, and later-citation searches through
11 August 2026 found the Gaussian counterexample but no statement of its
deterministic \(\Theta(\eta^{-1})\) tessellation.  A numerical checker audits
the projective interval count, the fiber-distance inequality, and the exact
arc-length scaling.  Human review should check the parameter unwinding from
the final substitution in \cite{ds} and the source's sign typo in the
logarithmic radius.
