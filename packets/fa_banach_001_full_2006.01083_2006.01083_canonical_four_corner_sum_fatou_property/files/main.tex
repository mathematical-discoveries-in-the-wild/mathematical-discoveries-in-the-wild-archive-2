\begin{abstract}
For arbitrary sigma-finite measure spaces $X$ and $Y$, consider
\[
 \Sspace
 =L^1(X\times Y)+L^\infty(X\times Y)
  +\Lp{1}{\infty}(X\times Y)+\Lp{\infty}{1}(X\times Y)
\]
with its canonical infimal-sum norm.  Holighaus and Voigtlaender proved that
this norm is equivalent to a Fatou norm, but left open whether the canonical
norm itself has the Fatou property.  We prove that it does.  The proof also
shows that the infimum defining the canonical norm is attained for every
element of $\Sspace$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source question}

The source is N.~Holighaus and F.~Voigtlaender, \emph{Schur-type Banach
modules of integral kernels acting on mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces},
arXiv:2006.01083v2 (2020), later published in the \emph{Journal of Functional
Analysis} \cite{HV}.  On page 30, in the unnumbered remark following
Proposition A.7, the authors state that the canonical norm has the weak Fatou
property, but that they ``could not confirm whether it actually satisfies the
Fatou property as well.''

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{The open statement on page 30 of arXiv:2006.01083v2.}
\end{figure}

Let $(X,\mathcal F,\mu)$ and $(Y,\mathcal G,\nu)$ be sigma-finite measure
spaces.  Following the source, for a measurable $F:X\times Y\to\mathbb C$ put
\[
 \|F\|_{p,q}
 =\left\|y\longmapsto \|F(\mathord\cdot,y)\|_{L^p(\mu)}
   \right\|_{L^q(\nu)},
 \qquad p,q\in\{1,\infty\}.
\]
Thus $L^{1,1}=L^1$ and $L^{\infty,\infty}=L^\infty$.  The canonical norm in
question is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sum-norm}
 \|F\|_{\Sspace}
 =\inf_{F=F_{11}+F_{\infty\infty}+F_{1\infty}+F_{\infty1}}
 \bigl(
   \|F_{11}\|_{1,1}+\|F_{\infty\infty}\|_{\infty,\infty}
   +\|F_{1\infty}\|_{1,\infty}+\|F_{\infty1}\|_{\infty,1}
 \bigr).
\end{equation}

\section{Proof intuition}

For $0\leq F_n\uparrow F$, choose almost optimal four-way decompositions of
each $F_n$.  Solidity lets us make all four pieces positive, so every piece is
bounded pointwise by $F$.  After division by $1+F$, the resulting vector of
four pieces is bounded in a single Hilbert space $L^2$ built from an equivalent
finite measure.  Hilbert-space Cesaro compactness then produces almost
everywhere limits for all four pieces simultaneously.  The Fatou lemma in
each of the four mixed-norm spaces preserves the four norm budgets, while the
sums of the Cesaro means converge to $F$.  Consequently the limiting pieces
decompose $F$ at cost no larger than $\lim\|F_n\|_{\Sspace}$.

The only preliminary issue is that $F$ might initially take the value infinity.
Sigma-finiteness supplies bounded, strictly positive integrable functions on
$X$ and $Y$.  Their tensor product belongs to all four associate spaces, so a
duality estimate and monotone convergence force $F$ to be finite almost
everywhere.

\section{The result}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $(X,\mathcal F,\mu)$ and $(Y,\mathcal G,\nu)$ be sigma-finite measure
spaces.  The canonical norm \eqref{eq:sum-norm} has the Fatou property.  More
precisely, if $0\leq F_n\uparrow F$ almost everywhere, then
\[
 \|F_n\|_{\Sspace}\uparrow\|F\|_{\Sspace},
\]
with the convention that both sides may be infinite.

Moreover, for every $F\in\Sspace$ there are
\[
 F_{11}\in L^1,\quad F_{\infty\infty}\in L^\infty,\quad
 F_{1\infty}\in L^{1,\infty},\quad
 F_{\infty1}\in L^{\infty,1}
\]
whose sum is $F$ and for which the infimum in \eqref{eq:sum-norm} is attained.
\end{theorem}

We isolate two elementary compactness facts.

\begin{lemma}[positive decompositions]\label{lem:positive}
If $G\geq0$ belongs to $\Sspace$, then the infimum in
\eqref{eq:sum-norm} can be computed using only decompositions into four
nonnegative functions.  The sum norm is solid, and
$\|F\|_{\Sspace}=\||F|\|_{\Sspace}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $G=\sum_{i=1}^4 U_i$ is any complex-valued decomposition and put
$R=\sum_i|U_i|$.  On $\{R>0\}$ define
\[
 V_i=G\frac{|U_i|}{R},
\]
and put $V_i=0$ on $\{R=0\}$.  Since $G=|\sum_iU_i|\leq R$, we have
$0\leq V_i\leq|U_i|$ and $\sum_iV_i=G$.  Solidity of each of the four
component spaces shows that this replacement cannot increase any component
norm.  Taking infima proves the first assertion.  Multiplying a positive
decomposition of $|F|$ by the measurable phase of $F$ proves the reverse
comparison and hence $\|F\|_{\Sspace}=\||F|\|_{\Sspace}$.  The same argument
with $0\leq G\leq H$ proves solidity of the sum norm.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[simultaneous Cesaro extraction]\label{lem:cesaro}
Let $(\Omega,\mathcal A,\mathbb P)$ be a probability space and let
$(U_n)$ be bounded in $L^2(\mathbb P;\mathbb R^m)$, where $m<\infty$.  There
is a subsequence $(U_{n_k})$ such that the Cesaro means
\[
 A_N=\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^N U_{n_k}
\]
have a subsequence converging almost everywhere.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The closed linear span of $(U_n)$ is a separable Hilbert space.  A diagonal
argument against an orthonormal basis gives a weakly convergent subsequence.
After subtracting its weak limit, choose a further subsequence $(W_k)$ with
$|\langle W_j,W_k\rangle|\leq2^{-k}$ for $j<k$.  Expanding the square shows
that the norms of $N^{-1}\sum_{k\leq N}W_k$ tend to zero.  Thus the Cesaro
means converge in $L^2$.  Choosing a subsequence for which the squared
$L^2$ errors are summable and applying Chebyshev's inequality followed by
Borel--Cantelli gives almost everywhere convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
The assertion is immediate when
$L:=\sup_n\|F_n\|_{\Sspace}=\infty$, so assume $L<\infty$.
If $\mu\otimes\nu$ is the zero measure, there is nothing to prove, so assume
that it is nonzero.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Step 1: a strictly positive common dual test.}
Sigma-finiteness gives bounded functions
$a:X\to(0,\infty)$ and $b:Y\to(0,\infty)$ with
$a\in L^1(\mu)$ and $b\in L^1(\nu)$.  (For example, use disjoint
finite-measure exhaustions and summable positive coefficients.)  Set
$H(x,y)=a(x)b(y)$.  Then $H>0$ almost everywhere, $H\in L^1$, and
\begin{align*}
 \|H\|_{1,1}&=\|a\|_1\|b\|_1,&
 \|H\|_{\infty,\infty}&=\|a\|_\infty\|b\|_\infty,\\
 \|H\|_{1,\infty}&=\|a\|_1\|b\|_\infty,&
 \|H\|_{\infty,1}&=\|a\|_\infty\|b\|_1.
\end{align*}
Mixed-norm Holder inequalities therefore give a constant $C_H<\infty$ such
that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-test}
 \int_{X\times Y}|G|H\,d(\mu\otimes\nu)
 \leq C_H\|G\|_{\Sspace}
 \qquad(G\in\Sspace).
\end{equation}
Indeed, pair the four summands respectively with
$L^\infty,L^1,L^{\infty,1},L^{1,\infty}$ and take the infimum over all
decompositions.  By monotone convergence,
\[
 \int FH=\lim_n\int F_nH\leq C_HL.
\]
Since $H>0$ almost everywhere, $F<\infty$ almost everywhere.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Step 2: almost optimal positive decompositions.}
For each $n$, Lemma~\ref{lem:positive} yields nonnegative functions
$G_{i,n}$ in the four component spaces $E_i$, in the order
\[
 (E_1,E_2,E_3,E_4)
 =(L^1,L^\infty,L^{1,\infty},L^{\infty,1}),
\]
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:near-opt}
 F_n=\sum_{i=1}^4G_{i,n},
 \qquad
 \|F_n\|_{\Sspace}
 \leq\sum_{i=1}^4\|G_{i,n}\|_{E_i}
 \leq\|F_n\|_{\Sspace}+\frac1n.
\end{equation}
In particular $0\leq G_{i,n}\leq F_n\leq F$.  Passing to a subsequence, we
may assume simultaneously that
\[
 \|G_{i,n}\|_{E_i}\longrightarrow\alpha_i
 \quad(1\leq i\leq4).
\]
Since $\|F_n\|_{\Sspace}\uparrow L$, \eqref{eq:near-opt} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
 \sum_{i=1}^4\alpha_i=L.
\end{equation}

Normalize $H$ so that $H\,d(\mu\otimes\nu)$ is a probability measure
$\mathbb P$, and define
\[
 U_n=\frac{(G_{1,n},G_{2,n},G_{3,n},G_{4,n})}{1+F}.
\]
Every coordinate of $U_n$ lies between zero and one, so $(U_n)$ is bounded
in $L^2(\mathbb P;\mathbb R^4)$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:cesaro}, followed if
necessary by a further subsequence, supplies integers $N_j\to\infty$ for
which the Cesaro means
\[
 A_{i,N_j}:=\frac1{N_j}\sum_{k=1}^{N_j}G_{i,n_k}
\]
converge almost everywhere to nonnegative functions $G_i$.

Because $F_{n_k}\to F$ pointwise,
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^4G_i
 =\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac1{N_j}\sum_{k=1}^{N_j}F_{n_k}
 =F
 \qquad\text{almost everywhere}.
\]
Each $E_i$ has the Fatou property: this is the usual Fatou lemma, applied
first in the inner variable and then in the outer variable for the two mixed
spaces.  Hence
\begin{align*}
 \|G_i\|_{E_i}
 &\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|A_{i,N_j}\|_{E_i}\\
 &\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}
       \frac1{N_j}\sum_{k=1}^{N_j}\|G_{i,n_k}\|_{E_i}
 =\alpha_i.
\end{align*}
Together with \eqref{eq:budget}, this yields
\[
 \|F\|_{\Sspace}\leq\sum_{i=1}^4\|G_i\|_{E_i}
 \leq\sum_{i=1}^4\alpha_i=L.
\]
Solidity gives $\|F_n\|_{\Sspace}\leq\|F\|_{\Sspace}$, so the reverse
inequality $L\leq\|F\|_{\Sspace}$ follows.  This proves the Fatou property.

Finally, fix $F\in\Sspace$ and apply exactly the same extraction to a sequence
of positive decompositions of $|F|$ whose total costs decrease to
$\|F\|_{\Sspace}$.  Here the dominating function is simply $|F|$.  The
limiting four pieces have total norm at most $\|F\|_{\Sspace}$, hence exactly
that value by the definition of the infimum.  Multiplication by the phase of
$F$ gives an attaining decomposition of $F$.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and scope}

The proof is computation-free.  The following points were checked separately.
\begin{itemize}
\item The passage to positive decompositions loses no norm and gives the
      crucial domination $G_{i,n}\leq F$.
\item The tensor $H=a\otimes b$ belongs to all four associate endpoint spaces,
      so \eqref{eq:dual-test} rules out an infinite-valued limit before the
      normalization by $1+F$.
\item Only four component norm budgets are present; a subsequence can make all
      of them converge, and Cesaro averaging preserves their limiting total.
\item The simultaneous extraction occurs in one $\mathbb R^4$-valued Hilbert
      space, so no incompatible componentwise subsequences are used.
\item The argument does not address arbitrary infinite lattice sums.  Its
      finite-component compactness is essential.
\end{itemize}
