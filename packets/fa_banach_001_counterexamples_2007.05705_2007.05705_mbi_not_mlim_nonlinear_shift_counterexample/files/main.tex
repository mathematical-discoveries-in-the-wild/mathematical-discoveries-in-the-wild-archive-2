\section{Source question}

Mironchenko, Kawan, and Gl\"uck~\cite{MKG} study, on an ordered Banach space
$(X,X^+)$, the discrete-time inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:system}
 x(k+1)\leq A(x(k))+u(k).
\end{equation}
They say that \eqref{eq:system} has the \emph{monotone limit property (MLIM)}
if some $\xi\in\Kinf$ has the following property: for every $\varepsilon>0$,
every constant input $u(k)\equiv w\in X^+$, and every decreasing solution,
there is an $N$ such that
\[
 \|x(N)\|\leq \varepsilon+\xi(\|w\|).
\]
They say that $\id-A$ has the \emph{monotone bounded invertibility (MBI)}
property if some $\xi\in\Kinf$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mbi}
 (\id-A)(v)\leq w\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \|v\|\leq\xi(\|w\|) \qquad(v,w\in X^+).
\end{equation}
After proving MLIM $\Rightarrow$ MBI, they ask whether MBI is strictly weaker
than MLIM or whether the two properties are equivalent.  Figure~\ref{fig:source}
records the exact passage.

\section{Proof intuition}

The missing phenomenon is escape without norm convergence.  In
$\ell_\infty^+$, successive tail indicators form a decreasing norm-one
sequence.  A linear shift propagates these tails, but fails MBI because shallow
ramps are approximate fixed points.  We replace its scalar edge map by a
bistable nonlinear gain: values near zero contract quadratically, the value one
propagates exactly, and large values contract affinely.  Small forcing is then
trapped near zero, which gives MBI, while an unforced tail of ones still travels
to infinity and violates MLIM.

\section{Counterexample}

Let $X=\ell_\infty(\N)$ with its usual norm and positive cone.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g}
 g(t):=
 \begin{cases}
 t^2,&0\leq t\leq1,\\
 (t+1)/2,&t\geq1,
 \end{cases}
\qquad
 (Ax)_1:=0,\quad (Ax)_{n+1}:=g(x_n).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The map $g$ belongs to $\Kinf$, and
$A:\ell_\infty^+\to\ell_\infty^+$ is a globally $2$-Lipschitz monotone
operator.  Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\id-A$ has the MBI property;
\item the system \eqref{eq:system} does not have the MLIM property.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, MBI is strictly weaker than MLIM, even for globally Lipschitz
monotone max-type gain operators on $\ell_\infty^+$ whose nonzero edge gains
belong to $\Kinf$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two formulas in \eqref{eq:g} agree at one.  Each is strictly increasing,
and their union is strictly increasing; also $g(0)=0$ and $g(t)\to\infty$.
Thus $g\in\Kinf$.  Since the slopes are bounded in absolute value by two,
$g$ is $2$-Lipschitz.  It follows coordinatewise that $A$ is well-defined,
monotone, and $2$-Lipschitz on $\ell_\infty^+$.

We prove MBI directly.  Suppose $v,w\in\ell_\infty^+$ and
$(\id-A)(v)\leq w$, and put $\delta=\|w\|_\infty$.  Coordinatewise,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
 v_1\leq\delta,\qquad v_{n+1}\leq g(v_n)+\delta\quad(n\geq1).
\end{equation}
First assume $0\leq\delta\leq1/4$ and set
\[
 \alpha_\delta:=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\delta}}2
 =\frac{2\delta}{1+\sqrt{1-4\delta}}.
\]
Then $\delta\leq\alpha_\delta\leq1/2$,
$\alpha_\delta^2+\delta=\alpha_\delta$, and
$\alpha_\delta\leq2\delta$.  Induction in \eqref{eq:recurrence} gives
$v_n\leq\alpha_\delta$ for every $n$: the base case follows from
$v_1\leq\delta$, and if $v_n\leq\alpha_\delta\leq1$, then
\[
 v_{n+1}\leq v_n^2+\delta
 \leq\alpha_\delta^2+\delta=\alpha_\delta.
\]
Hence $\|v\|_\infty\leq2\delta$.

Now assume $\delta\geq1/4$ and put $B_\delta=1+2\delta$.  Since
$B_\delta\geq1$,
\[
 g(B_\delta)+\delta
 =\frac{B_\delta+1}{2}+\delta=B_\delta.
\]
As $v_1\leq\delta\leq B_\delta$, monotonicity of $g$ and induction in
\eqref{eq:recurrence} yield $v_n\leq B_\delta$ for all $n$.  Therefore
$\|v\|_\infty\leq1+2\delta$.

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi}
 \xi(r):=
 \begin{cases}
 6r,&0\leq r\leq1/4,\\
 1+2r,&r\geq1/4.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The formulas agree at $r=1/4$, where both equal $3/2$.  Thus $\xi$ is
continuous, strictly increasing, unbounded, and $\xi(0)=0$, so
$\xi\in\Kinf$.  The preceding two bounds imply
$\|v\|_\infty\leq\xi(\delta)$, proving \eqref{eq:mbi}.

It remains to disprove MLIM.  For $k\geq0$, let
\[
 x(k)=(\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{k\text{ entries}},1,1,1,\ldots).
\]
Since $g(0)=0$ and $g(1)=1$, we have $x(k+1)=A(x(k))$.  Hence $x$ is a
zero-input solution of \eqref{eq:system}.  It is decreasing in the cone order,
but $\|x(k)\|_\infty=1$ for every $k$.  If MLIM held with any
$\widetilde\xi\in\Kinf$, then applying it with $w=0$ and
$\varepsilon=1/2$ would give some $N$ with
\[
 1=\|x(N)\|_\infty\leq\frac12+\widetilde\xi(0)=\frac12,
\]
a contradiction.

Finally, $A$ has the max-type gain-operator form: the only nonzero scalar gain
entering coordinate $n+1$ is $\gamma_{n+1,n}=g$.  This proves the strengthened
scope assertion.\qedhere
\end{proof}

\section{Why the positive results in the source do not apply}

The standard positive cone of $\ell_\infty$ is normal and generating, but it
does not have the Levi property: the decreasing sequence $x(k)$ above is not
norm convergent.  The operator $A$ is not compact, because the sequence
$A(x(k))=x(k+1)$ has no norm-convergent subsequence.  Thus the example lies
exactly beyond the two additional hypotheses in Proposition 5.4 of~\cite{MKG}.
It is nonlinear, so it also does not conflict with the source's equivalence
theorem for positive linear operators.
