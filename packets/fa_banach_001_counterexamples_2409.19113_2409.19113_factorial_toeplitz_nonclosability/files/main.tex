\section*{Source and exact question}

G. J. Groenewald, S. ter Horst, J. Jaftha, and A. C. M. Ran,
``A Toeplitz-like operator with rational matrix symbol having poles on the
unit circle: Matrix representation and spectral analysis,''
arXiv:2409.19113, Remark 2.2.  For $1<p<\infty$, an operator
$T:H_m^p\to H_m^p$ is in the Sarason class when it is closed and densely
defined, its domain is shift invariant, and it satisfies the two additional
shift conditions stated in the source.  The authors ask:

\begin{quote}
Start with a Toeplitz matrix and assume the ``analytic part'' is in $H_m^p$.
Can we then define an unbounded Toeplitz operator from that?  It is clear how
$T$ acts on the vector polynomials of size $m$.  Is there maybe a unique way
to extend the operator on $\mathcal P^m$ to one that satisfies (i)--(iv)?
Even without uniqueness this would be a relevant result.
\end{quote}

\section*{Counterexample theorem}

For matrices $a_j\in\C^{m\times m}$, the semi-infinite Toeplitz matrix is
$[a_{i-j}]_{i,j\ge0}$.  Its forced action on a vector polynomial
$f(z)=\sum_{j=0}^Nx_jz^j$ is
\[
 T_0f(z)=\sum_{i\ge0}\left(\sum_{j=0}^Na_{i-j}x_j\right)z^i,
\]
whenever the displayed output belongs to $H_m^p$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Fix $1<p<\infty$, $m\ge1$, and any analytic matrix function
\[
                 G(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}A_jz^j\in H^p_{m\times m}.
\]
Define a Toeplitz matrix by
\[
                 a_j=A_j\quad(j\ge0),\qquad
                 a_{-k}=k!I_m\quad(k\ge1).                \tag{1}
\]
Its analytic part is $G\in H^p_{m\times m}$, and its matrix action maps
$\cP^m$ into $H_m^p$.  Nevertheless, that polynomial operator is not
closable.  Consequently there is no closed operator on $H_m^p$ extending
the matrix action on $\cP^m$, and in particular no extension in the Sarason
class.
\end{theorem}

\section*{Proof intuition}

The hypothesis controls only the nonnegative diagonals.  Put factorial
growth on the uncontrolled negative diagonals and test the matrix on a
far-out monomial scaled by the same factorial.  The inputs then vanish, but
the top matrix entry survives as a nonzero constant; all other coanalytic
entries and the prescribed analytic part disappear.  This produces a
nonvertical graph limit $(0,e)$ and destroys closability before any of the
shift axioms can help.

\section*{Proof}

Fix a nonzero $e\in\C^m$.  Since $a_{i-j}=0$ is not assumed for
$i-j\ge0$, we first separate the two parts.  Applied to the monomial
$z^ne$, the negative diagonals in (1) contribute only when $0\le i<n$,
and the nonnegative diagonals contribute when $i\ge n$.  Hence
\[
 T_0(z^ne)
 =\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(n-i)!z^ie+z^nG(z)e.                   \tag{2}
\]
This belongs to $H_m^p$; by linearity the matrix therefore defines $T_0$
on every vector polynomial.

Set
\[
                         f_n(z)=\frac{z^n}{n!}e.
\]
Since $\lvert z^n\rvert=1$ almost everywhere on the unit circle,
\[
                       \|f_n\|_{H_m^p}=\frac{\|e\|}{n!}\longrightarrow0.
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
After division of (2) by $n!$,
\[
 T_0f_n
 =e+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{(n-i)!}{n!}z^ie
   +\frac{z^n}{n!}G(z)e.                                  \tag{4}
\]
For $n\ge3$, the middle term is uniformly bounded in norm by
\[
 \|e\|\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{(n-i)!}{n!}
 \le \|e\|\left(\frac1n+\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\right)
 \longrightarrow0.                                      \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, after the first summand, each successive denominator gains a
factor at least two.  The last term in (4) has $H_m^p$ norm
\[
                 \frac{\|G(\,\cdot\,)e\|_{H_m^p}}{n!}
                 \longrightarrow0.                       \tag{6}
\]
Equations (4)--(6) give
\[
                         T_0f_n\longrightarrow e
                         \quad\text{in }H_m^p,             \tag{7}
\]
where $e$ denotes the nonzero constant function.

Thus $f_n\to0$ while $T_0f_n\to e\ne0$.  This is the standard sequential
criterion showing that $T_0$ is not closable.  More directly, if a closed
operator $T$ extended $T_0$, then closedness and (3), (7) would put
$(0,e)$ in the graph of $T$, contradicting $T(0)=0$.  Since closedness is
condition (i) of the Sarason class, no Sarason-class extension exists.
\qed

\section*{Verification and scope}

The construction satisfies more than the minimal counterexample requires:
it works for every $1<p<\infty$, every block size $m$, and every prescribed
admissible analytic part $G$.  Thus adding or changing the controlled
analytic diagonals cannot repair the obstruction.  The theorem answers the
unrestricted existence question negatively; it does not classify the
additional growth or distribution conditions on the negative diagonals that
would make the polynomial core closable or Sarason-extendable.

\section*{Human-review recommendation}

Check the matrix indexing in (2) against the source convention
$[a_{i-j}]_{i,j\ge0}$.  Once that is fixed, the uniform estimate and the
closed-graph contradiction are elementary.
