\section{Source question}

For a closed convex subset $C$ of a Banach space $X$, Paul uses the following
definition: $C$ is \emph{uniformly proximinal} if, for every
$\varepsilon,R>0$, there is $\delta(\varepsilon,R)>0$ such that whenever
\[
 \dist(x,C)\le R,\qquad y\in C,\qquad \|x-y\|<R+\delta,
\]
there is $y'\in C$ satisfying
\[
 \|y-y'\|<\varepsilon,\qquad \|x-y'\|\le R.
\]
Problem~4.7 of \cite{Paul} asks:
\begin{quote}
Let $Y$ be a subspace of $X$ which is uniformly proximinal. Is it necessary
that $B_Y$ is also uniformly proximinal in $X$?
\end{quote}
The source passage (PDF page~14) is reproduced below.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The immediately following Remark~4.8(a) recalls that some Banach-space unit
balls are not uniformly proximinal. Thus, under the convention that a subspace
may equal its ambient space, $Y=X$ already gives a literal negative answer.
The result below removes that wording issue completely: $Y$ is proper and has
codimension one, and the bad unit ball is given by an explicit elementary norm.

\section{Proof intuition}

There are two independent pieces. First, construct a normed space $E$ whose
unit ball has an extreme point $u$ that is not strongly extreme. Unit vectors
$a_n$ can then remain far from $u$ while both endpoints of the approximate
chord through $u$ stay asymptotically in the unit ball. Seen from $2u$, these
$a_n$ are approximate nearest points, whereas extremality makes $u$ the unique
exact nearest point. This is precisely the obstruction to uniform
proximinality.

Second, place $E$ as the coordinate hyperplane in $E\oplus_\infty\R$.
Coordinate hyperplanes in this geometry have the strongest elementary
proximinality modulus: move radially in the $E$ coordinate until the distance
is exactly $R$. At the same time, restricting to the zero scalar coordinate
preserves every distance involved in the bad unit-ball configuration.

\section{An explicit unit ball which is not uniformly proximinal}

Let $H=\ell_2(\{2,3,\ldots\})$ with canonical vectors $(e_n)_{n\ge2}$, and
put $E=\R\times H$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm}
 \N(t,x)=\max\left\{|t|+\sup_{n\ge2}\frac{|x_n|}{n},\ \|x\|_2\right\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:badball}
Equation~\eqref{eq:norm} defines a Banach norm on $E$, and its closed unit
ball $B_E$ is not uniformly proximinal in $E$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both entries in the maximum are subadditive and absolutely homogeneous, and
their common kernel is zero. Moreover
\[
 \max\{|t|,\|x\|_2\}\le \N(t,x)\le |t|+\|x\|_2.
\]
Thus $\N$ is an equivalent Banach norm on $\R\times\ell_2$.

Write $u=(1,0)$. We first show that $u$ is an extreme point of $B_E$.
Suppose
\[
 u=\frac{(s,x)+(2-s,-x)}2,
 \qquad (s,x),(2-s,-x)\in B_E,
\]
and set $\alpha=\sup_{n\ge2}|x_n|/n$. The first entry of
\eqref{eq:norm} gives
\[
 |s|+\alpha\le1,
 \qquad |2-s|+\alpha\le1.
\]
Since $|s|+|2-s|\ge2$, adding the inequalities forces $\alpha=0$.
Hence $x=0$, and $|s|\le1$, $|2-s|\le1$ force $s=1$. Therefore both
endpoints equal $u$, proving extremality.

For $n\ge2$, define
\[
 a_n=\left(1-\frac1n,e_n\right).
\]
Direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:values}
 \N(a_n)=1,
 \qquad \N(a_n-u)=1,
 \qquad \N(2u-a_n)=1+\frac2n.
\end{equation}
For the unit ball of any normed space,
$\dist(z,B)=\max\{\|z\|-1,0\}$; hence
$\dist(2u,B_E)=1$. Furthermore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection}
 P_{B_E}(2u)=\{u\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $a\in B_E$ and $\N(2u-a)\le1$, then both $a$ and $2u-a$
belong to $B_E$ and have midpoint $u$. Extremality gives $a=u$.

Now fix $R=1$ and $\varepsilon=1/2$. For every $\delta>0$, choose $n$
so large that $2/n<\delta$. By \eqref{eq:values}, $a_n\in B_E$ and
\[
 \N(2u-a_n)<R+\delta.
\]
Any $a'\in B_E$ satisfying $\N(2u-a')\le R$ must equal $u$ by
\eqref{eq:projection}, but then
$\N(a_n-a')=1>\varepsilon$. This is the negation of uniform
proximinality for $B_E$.
\end{proof}

\section{The proper codimension-one counterexample}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There are a real Banach space $X$ and a proper closed codimension-one subspace
$Y\subset X$ such that $Y$ is uniformly proximinal in $X$, with the modulus
$\delta(\varepsilon,R)=\varepsilon$, but $B_Y$ is not uniformly proximinal
in $X$. Consequently Problem~4.7 of \cite{Paul} has a negative answer even
under the additional requirement that $Y$ be proper and codimension one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $E$ be the Banach space of Lemma~\ref{lem:badball}, and set
\[
 X=E\oplus_\infty\R,
 \qquad Y=E\times\{0\}.
\]
Plainly $Y$ is closed, proper, and of codimension one. We prove uniform
proximinality with $\delta(\varepsilon,R)=\varepsilon$.

Take $R,\varepsilon>0$, $z=(a,t)\in X$ with
\[
 \dist(z,Y)=|t|\le R,
\]
and $y=(b,0)\in Y$ with
\[
 \|z-y\|_X=\max\{\N(a-b),|t|\}<R+\varepsilon.
\]
If $\N(a-b)\le R$, take $y'=y$. Otherwise define
\[
 b'=a+\frac{R}{\N(b-a)}(b-a),
 \qquad y'=(b',0).
\]
Then
\[
 \|z-y'\|_X=\max\{R,|t|\}=R
\]
and, by homogeneity along the segment,
\[
 \|y-y'\|_X=\N(b-b')=\N(b-a)-R<\varepsilon.
\]
Thus $Y$ is uniformly proximinal.

It remains to show that $B_Y=B_E\times\{0\}$ is not. Embed the obstruction
from Lemma~\ref{lem:badball} by putting
\[
 z_0=(2u,0),\qquad y_n=(a_n,0).
\]
The $\ell_\infty$ norm gives
\[
 \dist_X(z_0,B_Y)=\dist_E(2u,B_E)=1,
 \quad \|z_0-y_n\|_X=1+\frac2n,
 \quad P_{B_Y}(z_0)=\{(u,0)\}.
\]
Also $\|y_n-(u,0)\|_X=1$. With $R=1$ and
$\varepsilon=1/2$, choosing $n$ so that $2/n<\delta$ defeats every proposed
$\delta>0$, exactly as in Lemma~\ref{lem:badball}. Hence $B_Y$ is not
uniformly proximinal.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, novelty, and review recommendation}

The construction is explicit and uses no computational or external
mathematical dependency. The four lightweight run indexes and the packet and
attempt indexes were searched for arXiv:1610.03209, ``uniformly proximinal'',
``unit ball'', and ``codimension one''. Bounded arXiv/web searches through
13 August 2026 used the exact wording of Problem~4.7 and combinations with
``counterexample'', ``proper subspace'', and ``codimension-one''. They found
the source paper and background literature on unit balls that fail strong or
uniform proximinality, but no answer to Problem~4.7 and no matching
codimension-one construction.

There is an important scope distinction. The literal question allows $Y=X$,
and the source's own Remark~4.8(a) already makes that special case immediate.
The potentially intended proper-subspace version is what
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} settles. Human review should verify the elementary
extremality calculation, the exact norm values in \eqref{eq:values}, and the
distance-preserving transfer to $E\oplus_\infty\R$. If these checks pass, the
result is a complete negative answer to the strongest natural reading of the
source question.
