\noindent\textbf{Status: literature-implied answer, complete for Problem 1(d).}
This is not claimed as a new result.  The supporting authors cite the source
but do not explicitly identify the implication below.

\section*{Source question}

Ehler and Okoudjou ask in Problem~1(d), PDF page~9 of \cite{source}, whether the
$W_2$-minimizing coupling of two tight probabilistic frames on $\mathbb R^N$,
at least one having zero mean, is itself a tight probabilistic frame on
$\mathbb R^{2N}$.  They also ask whether tight couplings other than the product
measure exist.

\section*{Decisive later identity}

For a coupling $\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)$, Chen and Schmoll write its frame
operator in Section~4.6, equation~(4.7), PDF page~13 of \cite{support}, as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
 S_\gamma=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 S_\mu&\Psi_\gamma\\
 \Psi_\gamma^{\mathsf T}&S_\nu
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Psi_\gamma=\int_{\mathbb R^N\times\mathbb R^N}
 xy^{\mathsf T}\,d\gamma(x,y).
\end{equation}
They also express the transport objective through $\operatorname{tr}
\Psi_\gamma$ and observe that graph couplings are never probabilistic frames.

Suppose $\mu$ and $\nu$ are tight with frame bounds $A$ and $B$.  Then
$S_\mu=A I_N$ and $S_\nu=B I_N$, so \eqref{eq:block} immediately gives the
complete criterion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:criterion}
 \gamma\text{ is tight on }\mathbb R^{2N}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A=B\ \text{ and }\ \Psi_\gamma=0.
\end{equation}
In particular, the equal-frame-bound condition omitted from the wording of
Problem~1(d) is necessary.  When $A=B$ and one marginal has zero mean, the
product coupling has
$\Psi_{\mu\otimes\nu}=(\int x\,d\mu)(\int y\,d\nu)^{\mathsf T}=0$, recovering
the intended product example.

\section*{Answers to both questions}

\paragraph{The optimal plan need not be tight.}
Let $\mu=\nu$ be any nonzero zero-mean tight probabilistic frame with bound
$A>0$.  The unique $W_2$-optimal plan has cost zero and is the diagonal coupling
$\gamma_\Delta=(\mathrm{Id},\mathrm{Id})_\#\mu$.  Its cross block is
\[
 \Psi_{\gamma_\Delta}=\int xx^{\mathsf T}\,d\mu(x)=A I_N\neq0.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:criterion} shows that $\gamma_\Delta$ is not tight.  Indeed, its
support lies in the proper diagonal subspace, so it is not even a probabilistic
frame on $\mathbb R^{2N}$.  This answers the first question negatively under
the intended equal-bound and zero-mean hypotheses.

\paragraph{Nonproduct tight couplings do exist.}
Fix $0<\varepsilon<1$ and let
\[
 \mu=\nu=\varepsilon\delta_0+
 \frac{1-\varepsilon}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^N
 (\delta_{e_i}+\delta_{-e_i}).
\]
This is zero mean and tight with bound $(1-\varepsilon)/N$.  Define
\[
 \gamma=\tfrac12(\mathrm{Id},\mathrm{Id})_\#\mu
       +\tfrac12(\mathrm{Id},-\mathrm{Id})_\#\mu.
\]
Both marginals equal $\mu$, while the two cross blocks cancel, so
$\Psi_\gamma=0$ and \eqref{eq:criterion} makes $\gamma$ tight.  It is not the
product coupling: $\gamma(\{(0,0)\})=\varepsilon$, whereas
$(\mu\otimes\mu)(\{(0,0)\})=\varepsilon^2$.  This works in every dimension.
