\begin{abstract}
We answer Szegedy's question under the paper's standing separability
assumption.  A sub-sigma-algebra $\cB$ of the product space is weakly random
if and only if the conditional rank-one tensors
\[
 \E\bigl(f(x)\overline{g(y)}\mid\cB\bigr),
 \qquad \|f\|_\infty,\|g\|_\infty\leq1,
\]
form a relatively compact subset of $L^1(\cB)$.  Equivalently, it is enough
to test conditional expectations of rectangle indicators.  Thus weak
randomness is exactly compactness of a natural bilinear conditional-
expectation map.  One direction is a finite-net argument; the other follows
from Arzel\`a--Ascoli on the weakly compact $L^\infty$ unit ball in
$L^2(\cB)$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question and the characterization}

Throughout, $(V,\cA,\mu)$ is the separable probability space fixed in the
source paper, $X=V\times V$, and $P_{\cB}=\E(\,\cdot\mid\cB)$ for a
sub-sigma-algebra $\cB\subseteq\cA\otimes\cA$.  The source definition and
Question 1 appear on PDF page 16 \cite{Szegedy}:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The Hilbert inner product is
$\langle a,b\rangle=\int_X\overline a b\,d(\mu\otimes\mu)$, as in the
source.  Define
\begin{align*}
 \cK_{\cB}
  &=\left\{P_{\cB}\bigl(f\otimes\overline g\bigr):
        f,g\in L^\infty(V),\ \|f\|_\infty,\|g\|_\infty\leq1\right\},\\
 \cR_{\cB}
  &=\left\{P_{\cB}(\one_S\otimes\one_T):S,T\in\cA\right\}.
\end{align*}
Both families lie in the unit ball of $L^\infty(\cB)$.

\begin{theorem}[complete characterization]\label{thm:main}
The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\cB$ is weakly random in the sense of Szegedy: on
  $U_{\cB}=\{M\in L^\infty(\cB):\|M\|_\infty\leq1\}$, weak $L^2$
  convergence implies cut-norm convergence.
\item $\cK_{\cB}$ is relatively compact in $L^1(\cB)$.
\item $\cR_{\cB}$ is relatively compact in $L^1(\cB)$.
\end{enumerate}
Equivalently, the bounded bilinear map
\[
 \beta_{\cB}:L^\infty(V)\times L^\infty(V)\longrightarrow L^1(\cB),
 \qquad \beta_{\cB}(f,g)=P_{\cB}(f\otimes\overline g),
\]
is compact.  In finite-net form, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there are finitely
many $h_1,\dots,h_m\in L^1(\cB)$ such that every conditional rectangle
$P_{\cB}(\one_S\otimes\one_T)$ is within $\varepsilon$ of some $h_j$ in
$L^1$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof intuition}

For a $\cB$-measurable kernel, conditional expectation moves every cut-norm
test onto $\cB$.  Hence the cut norm is uniform evaluation against
$\cK_{\cB}$.  Uniform evaluation on a norm-precompact family is controlled by
a finite net, which immediately converts weak convergence into cut
convergence.

The converse uses the same identity in reverse.  The $L^\infty$ unit ball is
a compact metrizable space in its weak $L^2$ topology.  Weak randomness says
that its cut-norm metric is continuous on this compact space.  Therefore the
linear functions indexed by $\cK_{\cB}$ are equicontinuous.  Arzel\`a--Ascoli
makes them compact in uniform norm, and $L^1$--$L^\infty$ duality identifies
that uniform norm exactly with the $L^1$ distance between their indices.

\section{The proof}

For $M\in L^2(\cB)$, the definition of the source cut norm gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutrepresentation}
 \|M\|_\square
 =\sup_{\|f\|_\infty,\|g\|_\infty\leq1}
   \left|\int_{V^2}\overline{f(x)}M(x,y)g(y)\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y)\right|
 =\sup_{k\in\cK_{\cB}}|\langle k,M\rangle|.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the unprojected tensor in the middle pairing is
$f\otimes\overline g$, and orthogonal projection onto $L^2(\cB)$ does not
change its pairing with the $\cB$-measurable $M$.

\begin{proof}[Proof that \textup{(ii)} implies \textup{(i)}]
Suppose $M_n,M\in U_{\cB}$ and $M_n\rightharpoonup M$ in $L^2(\cB)$.  Given
$\varepsilon>0$, choose $k_1,\dots,k_m\in\cK_{\cB}$ so every
$k\in\cK_{\cB}$ satisfies $\|k-k_j\|_1<\varepsilon$ for some $j$.
For such a pair,
\[
 |\langle k,M_n-M\rangle|
 \leq |\langle k_j,M_n-M\rangle|
      +\|k-k_j\|_1\|M_n-M\|_\infty
 < |\langle k_j,M_n-M\rangle|+2\varepsilon.
\]
The maximum of the finitely many first terms tends to zero by weak
convergence.  Taking the supremum in \eqref{eq:cutrepresentation}, then
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$, proves $\|M_n-M\|_\square\to0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof that \textup{(i)} implies \textup{(ii)}]
Regard $U_{\cB}$ as a subset of the Hilbert space $H=L^2(\cB)$.  It is
convex, norm closed, and bounded, hence weakly compact.  The standing
separability assumption makes $H$ separable, so the weak topology on
$U_{\cB}$ is metrizable.

Let $d_\square(M,N)=\|M-N\|_\square$.  Assumption (i) says precisely that
the identity
\[
 \iota:(U_{\cB},\text{weak }L^2)\longrightarrow(U_{\cB},d_\square)
\]
is sequentially continuous.  Its domain is metrizable, so $\iota$ is
continuous.  For $k\in\cK_{\cB}$ define
$\phi_k(M)=\langle k,M\rangle$.  Each $\phi_k$ is weakly continuous, and
\[
 |\phi_k(M)-\phi_k(N)|\leq d_\square(M,N).
\]
Continuity of $\iota$ therefore makes the family
$\{\phi_k:k\in\cK_{\cB}\}$ equicontinuous on the compact space
$(U_{\cB},\text{weak }L^2)$.  It is also pointwise bounded.  Arzel\`a--Ascoli
shows that this family is relatively compact in $C(U_{\cB})$ with the
uniform norm.

For $k,\ell\in\cK_{\cB}$, $k-\ell$ is $\cB$-measurable, and the measurable
phase of $k-\ell$ belongs to $U_{\cB}$.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:isometry}
 \|\phi_k-\phi_\ell\|_{C(U_{\cB})}
 =\sup_{N\in U_{\cB}}|\langle k-\ell,N\rangle|
 =\|k-\ell\|_1.
\end{equation}
Thus $k\mapsto\phi_k$ is an isometry from $\cK_{\cB}$ with its $L^1$
metric into $C(U_{\cB})$.  Relative compactness of the image proves (ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Equivalence of \textup{(ii)} and \textup{(iii)}]
The implication (ii)$\Rightarrow$(iii) is immediate because
$\cR_{\cB}\subseteq\cK_{\cB}$.  Conversely, suppose $\cR_{\cB}$ is
$L^1$-precompact.  If $0\leq u,v\leq1$, the layer-cake formula gives
\[
 P_{\cB}(u\otimes v)
 =\int_0^1\!\int_0^1
 P_{\cB}(\one_{\{u>s\}}\otimes\one_{\{v>t\}})\,ds\,dt,
\]
where the integral is in $L^1$.  Hence this function belongs to the closed
convex hull of $\cR_{\cB}$.  The closed convex hull of a norm-precompact set
in a Banach space is compact: replace the set by a finite $\varepsilon$-net
and observe that the convex hull of that finite net is compact.

Decomposing the real and imaginary parts of each unit-bounded $f,g$ into
positive and negative parts expresses $P_{\cB}(f\otimes\overline g)$ as a
sum of at most sixteen fixed scalar multiples of members of that compact
convex hull.  A finite sum of compact sets is compact, so
$\cK_{\cB}$ is precompact.  This proves (iii)$\Rightarrow$(ii) and completes
the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

\section{Sanity checks and scope}

If $\cB$ is finite, then $L^1(\cB)$ is finite-dimensional and bounded
subsets are precompact, so every finite sub-sigma-algebra is weakly random.
This agrees with the sequential definition.

At the opposite extreme, let $V$ be an atomless standard probability space
and $\cB=\cA\otimes\cA$.  Choose Rademacher functions $r_n$ on $V$ and put
$M_n(x,y)=r_n(x)$.  The sequence is orthonormal, hence weakly null in
$L^2(V^2)$, but taking $f=r_n$ and $g=1$ gives
$\|M_n\|_\square=1$.  Thus the full product sigma-algebra is not weakly
random.  Equivalently, the associated conditional rank-one tensors are not
$L^1$-precompact.

The separability hypothesis is not cosmetic: it converts the sequential
condition in the source into ordinary continuity on $U_{\cB}$.  For a
nonseparable probability space, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} remains true if weak
randomness is formulated for weakly convergent nets; the source question,
however, lives under its initial separability assumption and uses sequences.

The result is a functional-analytic characterization, not a classification
up to measure-space isomorphism.  It gives a concrete finite-$L^1$-net test
and exactly identifies the compact operator property behind the definition.
Exact-phrase and run-index searches found no explicit later answer; this is
only a novelty check, not part of the proof.

\section{Verification}

The proof was separately audited at the following pressure points:
conditional-expectation conjugations in \eqref{eq:cutrepresentation}, weak
compactness and metrizability of $U_{\cB}$, the passage from sequential to
ordinary continuity, complex $L^1$ duality in \eqref{eq:isometry}, and the
rectangle layer-cake reduction.  The finite verifier checks
\eqref{eq:cutrepresentation} and \eqref{eq:isometry} over randomized finite
product partitions; those computations are sanity checks, not substitutes
for the proof.
