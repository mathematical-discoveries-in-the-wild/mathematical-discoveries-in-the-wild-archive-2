\section{Source question and answer}

Y. Lin, \emph{The Petty projection inequality for sets of finite perimeter},
arXiv:2102.07103 \cite{source}, writes $\mathcal C^n$ for the compact sets of
finite perimeter in $\R^n$ with nonempty interior. For $E\in\mathcal C^n$ and
directions $u_i\in S^{n-1}$, let
\[
 E_i=S_{u_i}\cdots S_{u_1}E,
\]
and let $E^\star=\overline B_r$ be the origin-centered ball with
$|E^\star|=|E|$. Problem 1 asks whether convergence of one subsequence
$E_{i_j}\to E^\star$ in Hausdorff distance forces the entire sequence to
converge.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/problem_1_crop.png}
  \caption{Problem 1 in the source PDF.}
\end{figure}

The answer is yes. The proof uses only volume preservation and a Lyapunov
quantity that is especially transparent here: the radius of the smallest
\emph{origin-centered} ball containing the set.

\section{Proof intuition}
Steiner symmetrization preserves volume and cannot increase the centered
circumradius.  The ball-convergent subsequence forces that monotone radius
to have the ball radius as its limit.  Once every late set lies in an
arbitrarily thin outer shell around the equal-volume ball, any macroscopic
hole inside the ball would have more volume than that shell can hold.
This converts radius convergence into Hausdorff convergence for the whole
sequence.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:answer}
Let $E\in\mathcal C^n$, let $(u_i)$ be any sequence of directions, and put
$E_i=S_{u_i}\cdots S_{u_1}E$. If a subsequence converges in Hausdorff distance
to $E^\star=\overline B_r$, then
\[
 E_i\longrightarrow E^\star
 \qquad\text{in Hausdorff distance.}
\]
Thus Problem 1 in the source has an affirmative answer.
\end{theorem}

\section{Equal volume upgrades outer-radius convergence}

For a nonempty compact set $A$, write
\[
 R(A)=\max_{x\in A}|x|.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:shell}
Let $A_k\subset\R^n$ be compact measurable sets with
$|A_k|=|\overline B_r|>0$. If $A_k\subset\overline B_{R_k}$ and
$R_k\to r$, then $A_k\to\overline B_r$ in Hausdorff distance.
More quantitatively, for all sufficiently large $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quant}
 d_H(A_k,\overline B_r)
 \leq \max\left\{R_k-r,\ 2(R_k^n-r^n)^{1/n}\right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equal volume and $A_k\subset\overline B_{R_k}$ imply $R_k\geq r$. The outer
half of the Hausdorff distance is at most $R_k-r$.

Set
\[
 \eta_k=\sup_{x\in\overline B_r}\dist(x,A_k).
\]
Fix $x\in\overline B_r$ and $0<\varepsilon<\min\{\dist(x,A_k),2r\}$. Put
\[
 y=\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2r}\right)x.
\]
Then
\[
 B(y,\varepsilon/2)\subset B(x,\varepsilon)\cap\overline B_r
 \subset \overline B_r\setminus A_k.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \omega_n(\varepsilon/2)^n
 \leq |\overline B_r\setminus A_k|
 =|A_k\setminus\overline B_r|
 \leq |\overline B_{R_k}\setminus\overline B_r|
 =\omega_n(R_k^n-r^n).
\]
Since the last term tends to zero, $\eta_k<2r$ for all sufficiently large
$k$. Letting $\varepsilon\uparrow\dist(x,A_k)$ and then taking the supremum
over $x$ gives $\eta_k\leq2(R_k^n-r^n)^{1/n}$. This proves
\eqref{eq:quant} and the lemma.
\end{proof}

\section{The centered circumradius is monotone}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:radius}
For every compact measurable $A\subset\R^n$ and every direction
$u\in S^{n-1}$,
\[
 R(S_uA)\leq R(A).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $R=R(A)$, so $A\subset\overline B_R$. Write a point as $x+tu$ with
$x\in u^\perp$. The one-dimensional section of $A$ above $x$ is contained in
\[
 \left[-\sqrt{R^2-|x|^2},\sqrt{R^2-|x|^2}\right].
\]
Its length is therefore at most $2\sqrt{R^2-|x|^2}$. Steiner symmetrization
replaces that section by the centered interval of the same length, which is
still contained in the displayed interval. Hence $S_uA\subset\overline B_R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:answer}]
Steiner symmetrization preserves Lebesgue measure, so
$|E_i|=|E|=|\overline B_r|$ for all $i$. Lemma~\ref{lem:radius} shows that
\[
 R(E_{i+1})\leq R(E_i).
\]
Moreover $R(E_i)\geq r$, since a set of volume $|\overline B_r|$ cannot fit
inside a smaller centered ball.

The map $A\mapsto R(A)$ is $1$-Lipschitz for the Hausdorff metric. Thus the
assumed subsequential convergence gives $R(E_{i_j})\to r$. The decreasing
sequence $R(E_i)$ must consequently converge to $r$. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:shell} with $A_k=E_k$ and $R_k=R(E_k)$ to obtain
$E_i\to\overline B_r$ in Hausdorff distance.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, verification, and Problem 2}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.35em]
 \item The proof applies to arbitrary compact measurable starting sets of
 positive finite volume for which successive Steiner symmetrals are compact;
 finite perimeter is not used for Problem 1.
 \item Null-set representatives cause no gap: compactness defines $R(A)$ and
 Hausdorff distance, while the volume comparison in Lemma~\ref{lem:shell}
 works for the chosen measurable representatives.
 \item The case where $x$ lies on the boundary of the ball is covered by the
 explicit inner ball $B(y,\varepsilon/2)$.
 \item Formula \eqref{eq:quant} was checked independently on model sets
 consisting of a ball-volume compact set contained in a shrinking outer ball.
\end{enumerate}

The source's Problem 2 asks in addition whether $P(E_i)\to P(E^\star)$ when
the full sequence converges in Hausdorff distance. The argument above does not
settle that question. Hausdorff convergence and equal volume alone do not
control perimeter: arbitrarily fine boundary corrugations can retain excess
perimeter. The monotonicity $P(E_{i+1})\leq P(E_i)$ gives existence of a limit
and lower semicontinuity gives $P(E^\star)\leq\lim_iP(E_i)$, but a new upper
bound exploiting the iterative Steiner structure would still be required.

The local run indexes were searched for the arXiv identifier, both exact
problem statements, and the core Steiner/Hausdorff keywords. A bounded
external search on 11 August 2026 checked the exact wording and later work on
convergence of symmetrization processes. It found broad convergence theorems
and pathological direction sequences, but no explicit answer to this precise
subsequence-to-full-sequence question for the source's compact finite-perimeter
setting. This is not an exhaustive citation-graph review, so originality
remains a candidate claim pending expert checking.
