\title{A fixed-point-free condition-\((L)\) map on a weakly compact convex subset of \(c_0\)}
\author{Solution packet for arXiv:1209.5368, Remark 4.8}
\date{13 August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\vspace{-1.5em}

\resultbox{There is a weakly compact convex set $K\subset c_0$ and a Borel
self-map $T:K\to K$ with no fixed point which satisfies condition $(L)$,
hence also property $(*)$.  Moreover $c_0$ is NUNC and
$M(c_0)=MW(c_0)=2$.  Thus the proposed extension in Remark 4.8 is false
under each of these three hypotheses.  The uniformly nonsquare subcase is
not decided here.}

\section*{1. Source question and answer}

Betiuk-Pilarska and Wiśnicki prove fixed-point theorems for maps satisfying
Suzuki's condition $(C)$ (or continuous maps satisfying $(C_\lambda)$) on
weakly compact convex sets under the NUNC, $M(X)>1$, and $MW(X)>1$
hypotheses.  Their final Remark 4.8 asks whether the results remain true for
maps satisfying $(L)$ or $(*)$ \cite{source}.  The following is a crop of
page 14 of the official arXiv PDF; the geometric lead-in and the exact
question are retained.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{source_question_crop.png}
\end{center}
\vspace{-0.6em}
\begin{center}\small\emph{Official source evidence: arXiv:1209.5368, p.~14.}\end{center}

Recall only the two clauses of $(L)$ that will be used.  A map $T:C\to C$
satisfies $(L)$ when (i) every nonempty closed convex invariant subset
contains an approximate fixed-point sequence (\afps), and (ii), for every
\afps $(x_j)$ and $x\in C$,
\[
 \limsup_j\|x_j-Tx\|\leq \limsup_j\|x_j-x\|.
\]
The source observes that $(L)$ implies $(*)$.

\section*{2. Proof intuition}

The construction needs an orbit with two apparently conflicting features.
It must escape weakly so that its closed convex hull is weakly compact, yet
successive orbit points must become arbitrarily close so that every
invariant subset acquires an \afps.  Wide triangular ``tents'' moving to the
right in $c_0$ do both: their supports escape every coordinate, while their
centers move much less than their widths.

For an arbitrary point of the convex hull, send it to the successor of its
nearest tent.  Any \afps must then be close to tents whose indices tend to
infinity.  Such a sequence has asymptotic distance exactly one from
\emph{every} point of the convex hull.  Condition $(L)$ becomes the equality
$1\leq1$, whereas a fixed point is impossible because the map always moves
one step forward.

\section*{3. The moving tents}

Put, for $n\geq1$ and $k\in\N$,
\[
 c_n=n^4,\qquad w_n=n^{7/2},\qquad
 u_n(k)=\max\left\{0,1-\frac{|k-c_n|}{w_n}\right\}.
\]
Thus $u_n\in c_0$, $0\leq u_n(k)\leq1$, and $\norm{u_n}=1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:orbit}
The sequence $(u_n)$ is weakly null.  If
$d_n=\norm{u_{n+1}-u_n}$, then $d_n>0$ for every $n$ and $d_n\to0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The left endpoint of the support is
$c_n-w_n=n^{7/2}(\sqrt n-1)\to\infty$.  For
$a=(a_k)\in(c_0)^*=\ell_1$,
\[
 |\langle a,u_n\rangle|
 \leq\sum_{k\geq c_n-w_n}|a_k|\longrightarrow0,
\]
so $u_n\rightharpoonup0$.

For the step estimate, let
$\phi_{c,w}(t)=\max\{0,1-|t-c|/w\}$ on $\R$.  If $w'\geq w$, widening a
tent and then translating its center gives
\[
 \|\phi_{c',w'}-\phi_{c,w}\|_\infty
 \leq \frac{|c'-c|}{w'}+1-\frac{w}{w'}.
\]
(The first term is the Lipschitz translation bound; the second is the
maximum change caused by widening.)  Restriction to integer coordinates and
$c_n=n^4,w_n=n^{7/2}$ give
\[
 d_n\leq
 \frac{(n+1)^4-n^4}{(n+1)^{7/2}}
 +1-\left(\frac n{n+1}\right)^{7/2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Finally $u_n$ and $u_{n+1}$ have different unique peak coordinates, so
$d_n>0$.
\end{proof}

Let
\[
 K=\overline{\co}^{\|\cdot\|}\{0,u_1,u_2,\ldots\}\subset c_0.
\]
The set $\{0,u_1,u_2,\ldots\}$ is weakly compact by Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit};
the closed-convex-hull theorem therefore makes $K$ weakly compact.  It is
nonempty and convex, and all coordinates of every $z\in K$ lie in $[0,1]$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:distance}
For every $z\in K$, $\norm{u_n-z}\to1$.  Consequently every $x\in K$ has a
least-index nearest orbit point.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $c_n\to\infty$ and $z\in c_0$, $z(c_n)\to0$.  Positivity gives
\[
 1-z(c_n)\leq\norm{u_n-z}\leq1,
\]
which proves the limit.  Put $r_n=\norm{x-u_n}\leq1$.  If
$\inf_n r_n<1$, the tail $r_n\to1$ leaves only a finite minimization.  If
$\inf_n r_n=1$, then $r_n=1$ for every $n$.  In both cases a minimum is
attained, and the least minimizing index exists.
\end{proof}

Define
\[
 \nu(x)=\min\operatorname*{argmin}_{n\geq1}\norm{x-u_n},
 \qquad T(x)=u_{\nu(x)+1}.
\]

\section*{4. Classification of all approximate fixed points}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:afps}
If $(x_j)$ is an \afps for $T$ in $K$, then for every $z\in K$,
\[
 \norm{x_j-z}\longrightarrow1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $n_j=\nu(x_j)$ and
$\delta_j=\norm{Tx_j-x_j}=\norm{u_{n_j+1}-x_j}\to0$.  Minimality of
$u_{n_j}$ yields
\[
 \norm{x_j-u_{n_j}}\leq\delta_j,
 \qquad d_{n_j}\leq\norm{u_{n_j}-x_j}
       +\norm{x_j-u_{n_j+1}}\leq2\delta_j.
\]
Because every $d_n$ is strictly positive, the last convergence forces
$n_j\to\infty$: otherwise some fixed index would recur along a subsequence.
Lemma~\ref{lem:distance} and $\norm{x_j-u_{n_j}}\to0$ now give
\[
 \big|\norm{x_j-z}-\norm{u_{n_j}-z}\big|
 \leq\norm{x_j-u_{n_j}}\longrightarrow0,
\]
and the claimed limit follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:counterexample}
The self-map $T:K\to K$ has no fixed point and satisfies condition $(L)$.
It also satisfies property $(*)$ directly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First, $\nu(u_m)=m$.  If $Tx=x$, then $x=u_m$ with
$m=\nu(x)+1$, while $\nu(x)=\nu(u_m)=m$, a contradiction.

Let $D\subset K$ be nonempty, closed, convex, and $T$-invariant.  Choose
$x\in D$.  Then $Tx=u_m\in D$ for some $m$, and
$T(u_r)=u_{r+1}$ puts the whole tail $(u_r)_{r\geq m}$ in $D$.  This is an
\afps by Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit}, proving clause (i) of $(L)$.

For any \afps $(x_j)$ and any $x\in K$, Lemma~\ref{lem:afps}, applied first
to $Tx\in K$ and then to $x\in K$, gives
\[
 \limsup_j\norm{x_j-Tx}=1=\limsup_j\norm{x_j-x}.
\]
Thus clause (ii) holds with equality.

For completeness, take invariant closed convex $D,E\subset K$ and an \afps
in $D$.  Every point of $E$ has asymptotic distance one by
Lemma~\ref{lem:afps}; hence its asymptotic center relative to $E$ is all of
$E$, which is invariant.  Together with the preceding tail argument this
verifies $(*)$ without invoking $(L)\Rightarrow(*)$.
\end{proof}

The map is Borel.  Indeed, for continuous $r_n(x)=\norm{x-u_n}$,
\[
 \{\nu=n\}=\bigcap_{m\geq1}\{r_n\leq r_m\}
 \cap\bigcap_{m<n}\{r_n<r_m\}.
\]
Thus no nonmeasurable selection is hidden in the construction.

\section*{5. Why $c_0$ meets the paper's hypotheses}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:geometry}
For every $a\geq0$,
\[
 R(a,c_0)=RW(a,c_0)=\max\{1,a\}.
\]
Consequently $M(c_0)=MW(c_0)=2$, and $c_0$ is NUNC.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $(y_n)$ be weakly null in $B_{c_0}$ and $x\in c_0$.  Given
$\eta>0$, choose a finite set $F$ such that $|x(k)|<\eta$ off $F$.
Coordinatewise convergence makes $\max_{k\in F}|y_n(k)|<\eta$ eventually,
while off $F$ we have $|y_n(k)\pm x(k)|\leq1+\eta$.  Hence
\[
 \limsup_n\norm{y_n\pm x}\leq\max\{1,\norm{x}\}.
\]
For $x=ae_1$ and $y_n=e_{n+1}$, both signs attain
$\max\{1,a\}$, and $D[(y_n)]=1$.  This proves both modulus formulas,
including the asymptotic-diameter restriction in $R$.  Therefore
\[
 M(c_0)=MW(c_0)
 =\sup_{a\geq0}\frac{1+a}{\max\{1,a\}}=2.
\]

For NUNC, fix $x\in S_{c_0}$, a weakly null $(y_n)\subset S_{c_0}$, and
$0<t\leq1$.  The norm of $x$ is attained at some coordinate $k_0$, so
$\liminf_n\norm{x+t y_n}\geq1$ because $y_n(k_0)\to0$.  Repeating the
finite/tail estimate above gives the reverse limsup bound; hence
$\norm{x+t y_n}\to1$.  In the source notation this says
$b(t,x)=0$ for every $x\in S_{c_0}$ and $0<t\leq1$.  For each
$\varepsilon>0$, choose (say) $t=1/2$; then
$b(t,x)=0\leq\varepsilon t$ uniformly in $x$, which is precisely the second
NUNC alternative.
\end{proof}

Combining Theorem~\ref{thm:counterexample} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:geometry} answers Remark 4.8 negatively for NUNC,
$M(X)>1$, and $MW(X)>1$.  Since $c_0$ is not uniformly nonsquare
(take $x=e_1+e_2$, $y=e_1-e_2$), the uniformly nonsquare specialization is
not resolved by this packet.

\section*{6. Reproducibility and literature scope}

The included script \texttt{code/verify\_tents.py} exhaustively checks the
first sampled tent steps, the analytic step bound, large-index decay using
the piecewise-linear knots, nearest-point behavior on orbit points and
random finite convex combinations, and the inequalities used in the \afps
classification.  Run it with:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 code/verify_tents.py
\end{verbatim}
The expected first line is \texttt{moving-tent checks: PASS}.  These finite
checks are diagnostic; all infinite-dimensional claims are proved above.

Exact-ID and core-phrase searches of the run indexes found no duplicate.
A bounded literature search through 2026-08-13 found general
fixed-point-free $(L)$ examples, but no later answer with the simultaneous
weak compactness and $c_0$/NUNC/$M$ geometry used here.  The claimed result
is the explicit counterexample above, not a claim about the uniformly
nonsquare subcase or every conclusion in the source paper.
