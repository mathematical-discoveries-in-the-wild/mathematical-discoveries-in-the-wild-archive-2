\section{Source question}

Andrzej Wi\'snicki, \emph{Around the nonlinear Ryll--Nardzewski theorem},
arXiv:1903.12123v2, asks on page 10:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Question 1.} Is it true that Corollary 3.2 remains true for
Namioka--Phelps spaces?
\end{quote}

Here Corollary 3.2 says that if $X$ is Asplund, $Q\subset X^*$ is weak-star
compact and convex, and a dynamical system on $Q$ is nonexpansive and
norm-distal, then it has a fixed point \cite{Wisnicki2020}.  The locally convex
conventions come from Glasner--Megrelishvili \cite{GM2012}: the standard
uniformity $\xi^*$ on $V^*$ is uniform convergence on bounded subsets of $V$,
and a Namioka--Phelps space is one for which every equicontinuous subset of
$V^*$ is $(\wstar,\xi^*)$-fragmented.

\section{Definitions and statement}

Let $V$ be a Hausdorff locally convex space.  For every bounded $B\subset V$,
put
\[
 p_B(x^*)=\sup_{v\in B}|x^*(v)| \qquad (x^*\in V^*).
\]
These seminorms generate $\xi^*$.  A semigroup action $(S,Q)$ is
$\xi^*$-nonexpansive if
\[
 p_B(sx-sy)\le p_B(x-y)
 \quad (s\in S,\ x,y\in Q)
\]
for every bounded $B\subset V$.  It is $\xi^*$-distal if for every $x\ne y$
there are a bounded $B\subset V$ and $\varepsilon>0$ such that
$p_B(sx-sy)>\varepsilon$ for all $s\in S$.

\begin{theorem}[Affirmative answer to Question 1]\label{thm:main}
Let $V$ be a Namioka--Phelps locally convex space and let
$Q\subset V^*$ be a nonempty equicontinuous weak-star compact convex set.
Suppose $S$ acts on $Q$ by weak-star continuous maps and the action is
$\xi^*$-nonexpansive and $\xi^*$-distal.  Then $S$ has a common fixed point
in $Q$.
\end{theorem}

If $V$ is barrelled, every weak-star compact subset of $V^*$ is
equicontinuous, so the explicit equicontinuity clause may then be omitted.

\section{Proof intuition}

The Banach-space proof in \cite{Wisnicki2020} uses the Radon--Nikod\'ym
property first to fragment a minimal subsystem and later to make that subsystem
norm compact.  Namioka--Phelps fragmentation supplies the first step directly.
For the second step, one does not need a countable dense set: fix one bounded
$B\subset V$ and one scale.  Fragmentation produces a positive-measure set of
small $p_B$-diameter.  Nonexpansivity moves such sets to neighborhoods of all
orbit points without decreasing their invariant measure, so only finitely many
orbit points can be separated at that scale.  Repeating this independently for
all $B$ proves $\xi^*$-total boundedness.  A short net argument supplies the
completeness that the source identified as problematic.

\section{Proof}

We isolate the completeness observation.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:complete}
Every equicontinuous weak-star compact subset $K\subset V^*$ is complete for
the uniformity induced by $\xi^*$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $(x_i)$ be a $\xi^*$-Cauchy net in $K$.  Passing to a subnet, weak-star
compactness gives $x_{i_\alpha}\to x\in K$ weak-star.  Fix a bounded
$B\subset V$ and $\varepsilon>0$.  Eventually
$p_B(x_i-x_j)\le\varepsilon$ for all sufficiently large $i,j$.  Fix such an
$i$ and let $j=i_\alpha$ tend weak-star to $x$.  For every $v\in B$,
\[
 |(x_i-x)(v)|=\lim_\alpha |(x_i-x_{i_\alpha})(v)|\le\varepsilon.
\]
Taking the supremum over $v\in B$ gives $p_B(x_i-x)\le\varepsilon$.
Thus $x_i\to x$ in $\xi^*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Zorn's lemma, replace $Q$ by a minimal nonempty weak-star compact convex
$S$-invariant subset.  Inside it choose a minimal nonempty weak-star compact
$S$-invariant subset $K$, not assumed convex.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{1. Lifting distality.}
We claim that $(S,K)$ is weak-star distal.  Otherwise there are distinct
$x,y\in K$, a net $(s_\alpha)\subset S$, and $u\in K$ such that
$s_\alpha x$ and $s_\alpha y$ both converge weak-star to $u$.  By
$\xi^*$-distality, choose an entourage $E$ of $\xi^*$ such that
$(sx,sy)\notin E$ for all $s\in S$.  Since $V$ is Namioka--Phelps and $K$ is
equicontinuous, fragmentation gives a weak-star open $U$ meeting $K$ whose
intersection with $K$ is $E$-small (after replacing $E$ by a smaller
symmetric entourage if necessary).  Minimality gives
$K=\overline{Su}^{\wstar}$, so some $s_0u$ lies in $U\cap K$.  Both
$s_0s_\alpha x$ and $s_0s_\alpha y$ eventually lie in $U\cap K$, contrary to
the choice of $E$.

The standard Furstenberg--Namioka invariant-measure theorem for compact distal
systems now gives an $S$-invariant Radon probability measure $\mu$ on $K$
\cite{Wisnicki2020,GM2012}.  Replacing $K$ by $\supp\mu$ and using minimality,
we have
\[
 \supp\mu=K,\qquad s(K)=K \quad(s\in S).
\]
Indeed invariance shows first $s(K)\subset K$ and then that the weak-star
compact set $s(K)$ has full measure, forcing the reverse inclusion.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{2. Total boundedness from packing.}
Fix a bounded $B\subset V$ and $\varepsilon>0$.  Fragmentation supplies a
weak-star open $U$ meeting $K$ such that
\[
 \diam_{p_B}(U\cap K)<\varepsilon/2.
\]
Choose $x\in U\cap K$.  Because $x\in\supp\mu$, the set
\[
 A=\{y\in K:p_B(x-y)<\varepsilon/2\}
\]
has measure $\delta>0$.  Nonexpansivity and invariance imply, for every
$s\in S$,
\[
 \mu\{y\in K:p_B(sx-y)<\varepsilon/2\}\ge\delta.
\]
The displayed neighborhoods belonging to orbit points that are pairwise
$p_B$-separated by at least $\varepsilon$ are disjoint.  Hence there can be at
most $1/\delta$ such orbit points.  A greedy maximal separated family is
therefore finite and is an $\varepsilon$-net for $Sx$.  As $B$ and
$\varepsilon$ were arbitrary, $Sx$ is $\xi^*$-totally bounded.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:complete}, the $\xi^*$-closure $C$ of $Sx$ in $K$ is
$\xi^*$-compact.  The identity from $\xi^*$ to weak-star is continuous, so
$C$ is weak-star compact and therefore weak-star closed.  Minimality gives
$K=\overline{Sx}^{\wstar}\subset C$, while $C\subset K$ is immediate.  Thus
$K$ is $\xi^*$-compact.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{3. Normal-structure contradiction.}
Suppose $K$ has more than one point.  Some bounded $B\subset V$ satisfies
\[
 r=\sup_{x,y\in K}p_B(x-y)>0.
\]
The locally convex DeMarr lemma (the seminorm version of the usual compact-set
normal-structure lemma) gives $u\in\overline{\co K}^{\xi^*}$ and $r_0<r$ with
\[
 \sup_{y\in K}p_B(u-y)\le r_0.
\]
Since $Q$ is convex and weak-star closed, $u\in Q$.  Define
\[
 Q_0=\{z\in Q:p_B(z-y)\le r_0\text{ for every }y\in K\}.
\]
The seminorm $p_B$ is weak-star lower semicontinuous (it is a supremum of
weak-star continuous functions), so $Q_0$ is weak-star compact and convex.
It is nonempty, contains $u$, and is proper because $r_0<\diam_{p_B}K$.
Finally, $s(K)=K$ and nonexpansivity give $s(Q_0)\subset Q_0$ for every
$s\in S$.  This contradicts the minimality of $Q$.

Therefore $K$ is a singleton, and its unique point is fixed by every element
of $S$.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The proof avoids the source's separability bottleneck: total boundedness is
proved one strong-dual seminorm at a time, and completeness is supplied by
Lemma~\ref{lem:complete}.  No metrizability, countable bounded-set base, or
completeness of the whole strong dual is assumed.

Question 2 of the source is broader and is not claimed here.  The result also
retains equicontinuity of $Q$, as required in the standard locally convex dual
fixed-point formulation of \cite{GM2012}.

A bounded novelty check searched the run indexes, arXiv, and the web using the
exact question, source title and id, and combinations of ``Namioka--Phelps'',
``nonlinear'', ``nonexpansive'', ``distal'', and ``fixed point''.  Later local
arXiv sources citing the paper were also checked.  No later exact answer was
found.  This is not exhaustive bibliographic certification.

\section*{Human review recommendation}

Check especially (i) Lemma~\ref{lem:complete}, including its use of nets, and
