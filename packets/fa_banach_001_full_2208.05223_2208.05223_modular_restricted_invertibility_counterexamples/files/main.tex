\fbox{\parbox{0.92\linewidth}{\textbf{Candidate full negative answers.}
The commutative and noncommutative modular Bourgain--Tzafriri conjectures
in arXiv:2208.05223 are false, even with determinant zero as explicitly
allowed.  A single fixed separable commutative $C^*$-algebra works in all
dimensions.  The construction also works over a commutative von Neumann
algebra, defeating the suggested W*-algebra fallback.}}
\end{center}

\section{The conjectures}

For a unital $C^*$-algebra $\A$, the source equips the left module
$\A^d$ with
\[
 \langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{r=1}^d x_ry_r^*,\qquad
 \|x\|=\|\langle x,x\rangle\|^{1/2}.
\]
Its Conjecture 1.2 asserts that for every unital commutative $\A$ there
are constants $a_0,c_0>0$, depending only on $\A$, such that every
$M\in M_d(\A)$ with unit columns and
$\det M\in\A^\times\cup\{0\}$ has a coordinate subset $\sigma$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:size}
 |\sigma|\ge \frac{c_0d}{\|M\|^2}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 \left\langle\sum_{j\in\sigma}a_jMe_j,
                    \sum_{j\in\sigma}a_jMe_j\right\rangle
 \ge a_0\sum_{j\in\sigma}a_ja_j^*
 \quad(a_j\in\A).
\end{equation}
Conjecture 1.3 makes the analogous assertion for Manin matrices over an
arbitrary unital $C^*$-algebra, using the column determinant.

\section{The simultaneous counterexample}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Both Conjecture 1.2 and Conjecture 1.3 are false.  More precisely, there is
a fixed separable unital commutative $C^*$-algebra $\A$ such that, for
every $d\ge2$, there is $M_d\in M_d(\A)$ satisfying
\[
 \langle M_de_j,M_de_j\rangle=\one,\qquad
 \det M_d=0,\qquad \|M_d\|=\sqrt2,
\]
but no coordinate subset of cardinality at least two satisfies
\eqref{eq:lower} for any $a_0>0$.

The same assertion holds with $\A=\ell_\infty$, which is a commutative
von Neumann algebra.  All the matrices are Manin matrices, and their Manin
column determinants are zero.
\end{theorem}

\section{Idea of the proof}

For $\A=C(K)$ a module matrix is a continuous family of ordinary scalar
matrices.  The conjecture demands one coordinate subset that works at
every fiber.  We arrange that every pair $\{j,k\}$ of columns coincides at
one designated fiber.  Therefore every fixed subset of two or more
columns is singular somewhere.

The decisive quantitative point is that duplicating exactly one pair of
otherwise orthonormal unit columns costs only a factor $\sqrt2$ in
operator norm.  Hence the conjectured size lower bound remains linear in
$d$, while the largest uniformly invertible coordinate subset has size
one.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

Let
\[
 \Omega=\{(d,j,k):d\ge2,\ 1\le j<k\le d\}
\]
with the discrete topology, let $K=\Omega\cup\{\infty\}$ be its one-point
compactification, and set $\A=C(K)$.  Thus $\A$ is the algebra of
convergent scalar sequences; in particular it is unital, separable, and
commutative.

For $1\le j<k\le d$, define a scalar matrix $E_{jk}^{(d)}$ as follows.
Choose a bijection
\[
 g:\{1,\ldots,d\}\setminus\{k\}\longrightarrow\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
\]
and let its $r$th column be
\[
 E_{jk}^{(d)}e_r=
 \begin{cases}
  e_{g(r)},&r\ne k,\\
  e_{g(j)},&r=k.
 \end{cases}
\]
Here the vectors on the right are the first $d-1$ standard vectors of
$\C^d$.  Thus columns $j$ and $k$ coincide, while the other columns are
distinct members of an orthonormal family.  It follows immediately that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fiberfacts}
 \|E_{jk}^{(d)}e_r\|=1,\qquad
 \det E_{jk}^{(d)}=0,\qquad
 \|E_{jk}^{(d)}\|=\sqrt2.
\end{equation}
Indeed its nonzero singular values are $\sqrt2$ and $1$ with multiplicity
$d-2$.

For fixed $d$, define the $C(K)$-valued matrix $M_d$ fiberwise by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family}
 M_d(\omega)=
 \begin{cases}
  E_{jk}^{(d)},&\omega=(d,j,k),\\
  E_{12}^{(d)},&\text{otherwise, including }\omega=\infty.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Only finitely many fibers differ from the value at infinity, so every
entry of $M_d$ is continuous on $K$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:fiberfacts} and pointwise evaluation give
\[
 \langle M_de_r,M_de_r\rangle=\one,
 \qquad \det M_d=0.
\]
For matrices over $C(K)$ the adjointable module-operator norm is the
supremum of the scalar fiber norms.  Hence $\|M_d\|=\sqrt2$.

Now let $\sigma\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}$ have at least two elements and
choose $j<k$ in $\sigma$.  In \eqref{eq:lower}, take the constant
$C(K)$-coefficients
\[
 a_j=\one,\qquad a_k=-\one,\qquad a_r=0\quad(r\notin\{j,k\}).
\]
At the fiber $\omega=(d,j,k)$, the left side of \eqref{eq:lower} is zero
because the two selected columns coincide, whereas the right side is
$2a_0>0$.  Positivity in $C(K)$ is pointwise, so \eqref{eq:lower} fails.
Consequently every admissible $\sigma$ has cardinality at most one.

If Conjecture 1.2 supplied constants $a_0,c_0>0$ for this fixed $\A$,
choose $d>2/c_0$.  Since $\|M_d\|^2=2$, equation \eqref{eq:size} would
force $|\sigma|>1$, a contradiction.  This disproves Conjecture 1.2.

Every entry of $M_d$ lies in a commutative algebra.  Therefore all Manin
commutator identities hold automatically, and the Manin column
determinant equals the ordinary determinant, namely zero.  The same
matrices consequently satisfy every hypothesis of Conjecture 1.3 and
disprove it as well.

Finally, use the same bounded coordinate functions on $\Omega$ as entries
in $\ell_\infty(\Omega)$.  The preceding norm and point-evaluation proof is
unchanged.  Since $\ell_\infty(\Omega)$ is a commutative W*-algebra, the
counterexample persists in that proposed fallback class.

\section{The Johnson--Lindenstrauss statement as printed}

There is a separate, elementary issue in Conjecture 2.6: both its lower
and upper bounds contain $1-\varepsilon$, rather than an upper factor
$1+\varepsilon$.  Taken literally, this conjecture is also false already
for $\A=\C$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:jl}
Conjecture 2.6 as printed is false over the scalar algebra $\C$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose its constant is $C_0>0$ and fix $0<\varepsilon<1$.  In $\C^N$
take the $2N+1$ points
\[
 0,e_1,\ldots,e_N,ie_1,\ldots,ie_N.
\]
The two printed inequalities force equality
\[
 \|L(x-y)\|^2=(1-\varepsilon)\|x-y\|^2
\]
for every pair of these points.  Setting $u_j=Le_j$, the pairs
$(0,e_j)$ give $\|u_j\|^2=1-\varepsilon$; the pairs $(e_j,e_k)$ and
$(e_j,ie_k)$ give respectively the vanishing of the real and imaginary
parts of $\langle u_j,u_k\rangle$ for $j\ne k$.  Thus $u_1,\ldots,u_N$
are nonzero mutually orthogonal vectors in $\C^m$, so $m\ge N$.

For all sufficiently large $N$ there is an integer
\[
 C_0\varepsilon^{-2}\log(2N+1)<m<N,
\]
contradicting the asserted existence of $L\in M_{m\times N}(\C)$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:jl} addresses only the literal statement.  It makes
no claim about the evidently intended variant with upper factor
$1+\varepsilon$.

\section{Verification, novelty, and review}

All parts of the principal construction are exact: the spectrum is fixed
for every $d$; only finitely many values differ from the limit fiber; the
fiber norms are exactly $\sqrt2$; and the determinant is the zero element,
not merely noninvertible.  The use of constant coefficients $1,-1$ makes
failure of the module order inequality visible at a single character.

The run registry, parsed corpus, exact conjecture/title searches,
author/citation searches, and close searches for modular or continuous
matrix-function restricted invertibility were checked through 11 August
2026.  No later direct solution or counterexample was found.  The closest
located work, Fan et al.\ \cite{FanEtAl}, allows the selected subspace to
vary with the fiber; it does not produce the one fixed coordinate subset
demanded by the source conjectures.  Novelty confidence is moderate.

Human review should prioritize the quantifier that $a_0,c_0$ may depend on
$\A$ but not on $d$; this is why one fixed infinite-dimensional algebra is
essential.  The restricted-invertibility counterexamples are independent
of the likely typographical error in Conjecture 2.6 and give complete
negative answers to Conjectures 1.2 and 1.3 as written.
