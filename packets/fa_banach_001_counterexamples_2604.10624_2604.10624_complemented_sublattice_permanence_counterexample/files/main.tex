\section{Source problem and scope}

Kania and Niwi\'nski introduce Banach-lattice semiprojectivity in
arXiv:2604.10624v1~\cite{source}.  Their Question~5.14 on arXiv PDF page~16
asks:

\begin{sourcequestion}
Which permanence properties---including passage to complemented sublattices,
quotients by closed ideals, and Fremlin tensor products---does
semiprojectivity enjoy?
\end{sourcequestion}

The full question also mentions finite $\ell_1$- and $\ell_\infty$-sums.  The
present result answers only the complemented-sublattice branch.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Here a \emph{complemented sublattice} means a closed vector sublattice which
is the range of a bounded linear projection.  This is the standard
Banach-space complementation meaning; no positivity or lattice-homomorphism
property is imposed on the projection.

\section{Idea of the proof}

Use disjoint triangular bumps supported on intervals that shrink toward the
origin.  Their closed span is exactly a lattice-isometric copy of $c_0$.
Ordinary point evaluations at the bump peaks would produce a sequence
converging to $f(0)$, not to zero.  Subtracting the value at a nearby endpoint
fixes this: uniform continuity makes the difference coefficients tend to
zero, while each difference still extracts the corresponding bump
coefficient.  This gives an explicit norm-two projection.

The source paper supplies both semiprojectivity verdicts needed to finish:
Theorem~A says $C(X)$ is semiprojective exactly when a compact metric $X$ is
an absolute neighbourhood retract, and Corollary~5.8 says that $c_0$ is not
semiprojective.

\section{Counterexample}

\begin{theorem}
There exist a semiprojective Banach lattice $E$ and a closed sublattice
$Y\subseteq E$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $Y$ is lattice-isometric to $c_0$;
\item there is a bounded linear projection $P:E\to Y$ with $\|P\|=2$; and
\item $Y$ is not semiprojective.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, semiprojectivity does not pass to complemented sublattices in
the bounded-linear-complementation sense.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $E=C[0,1]$.  For $n\ge1$, let
\[
 a_n=2^{-2n},\qquad b_n=2^{-2n+1},\qquad
 t_n=\frac{a_n+b_n}{2}.
\]
The intervals $[a_n,b_n]$ are pairwise disjoint and converge to $0$.  Let
$u_n\in C[0,1]$ be the triangular function supported on $[a_n,b_n]$, affine
on $[a_n,t_n]$ and $[t_n,b_n]$, and satisfying
\[
 0\le u_n\le1,\qquad u_n(t_n)=1,
 \qquad u_n(a_n)=u_n(b_n)=0.
\]

For $\alpha=(\alpha_n)\in c_0$, define
\[
 J\alpha=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_nu_n.
\]
Because the supports are disjoint and $\alpha_n\to0$, this is a continuous
function (also at the only accumulation point $0$), and
\[
  \|J\alpha\|_\infty=\sup_n|\alpha_n|.
\]
Since every $u_n$ is positive and the supports are disjoint, $J$ preserves
absolute values and hence all lattice operations.  Thus $J$ is a lattice
isometry onto the closed sublattice
\[
  Y:=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{u_n:n\ge1\}\subseteq C[0,1].
\]

Define $T:C[0,1]\to c_0$ by
\[
   (Tf)_n=f(t_n)-f(a_n).
\]
Both $t_n$ and $a_n$ tend to $0$, so continuity of $f$ gives $(Tf)_n\to0$.
Moreover,
\[
  \|Tf\|_\infty\le2\|f\|_\infty,
\]
so $T$ is bounded.  For every $\alpha\in c_0$, disjointness of the supports
and the endpoint conditions give
\[
  (TJ\alpha)_n=(J\alpha)(t_n)-(J\alpha)(a_n)=\alpha_n.
\]
Therefore $TJ=\operatorname{id}_{c_0}$ and
\[
  P:=JT:C[0,1]\longrightarrow Y
\]
is a bounded projection with $\|P\|\le2$.  The norm is exactly $2$: fix one
$n$ and choose $f\in C[0,1]$ with $\|f\|_\infty=1$, $f(t_n)=1$, and
$f(a_n)=-1$.  Then $|(Pf)(t_n)|=2$.

The interval $[0,1]$ is a compact metric absolute neighbourhood retract, so
Theorem~A of~\cite{source} makes $E=C[0,1]$ semiprojective.  Corollary~5.8 of
the same paper (whose statement is on arXiv PDF page~12 and proof on page~13)
says that $c_0$ is not semiprojective.  Since $Y$ is lattice-isometric to
$c_0$, $Y$ is not semiprojective.  Hence $Y$ is the required complemented
sublattice of the semiprojective lattice $E$.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and limitations}

The only convergence point requiring attention is $0$.  It is harmless for
$J\alpha$ because $\alpha_n\to0$, and for $Tf$ because
$f(t_n)-f(a_n)\to f(0)-f(0)=0$.  The identities $TJ=\mathrm{id}_{c_0}$ and
$P^2=P$ are exact, not approximate.  The proof is otherwise elementary once
Theorem~A and Corollary~5.8 of the source are accepted.

The projection $P$ is not claimed positive or lattice-homomorphic.  Thus the
theorem does not contradict Proposition~2.6 of~\cite{source}, which proves
inheritance under \emph{contractive lattice-homomorphic retracts}.  If the
authors intended ``complemented sublattice'' to mean that narrower categorical
notion, this packet is a scoped answer to the literal standard interpretation,
not a solution of the narrower variant.  It also does not settle the finite
direct-sum, Fremlin-tensor-product, or extension questions.

\section{Novelty check and review recommendation}

Before promotion, the four lightweight run indexes were searched by exact
arXiv id and semiprojectivity/permanence keywords.  A bounded web/arXiv search
through 11 August 2026 used the exact title, arXiv id, and combinations of
``semiprojective Banach lattice'', ``complemented sublattice'', and ``$c_0$''.
It found the source preprint and summaries of it, but no later paper or note
recording this counterexample.  The elementary fact that $C[0,1]$ contains a
complemented sublattice isometric to $c_0$ is classical in spirit; the claimed
new point is its combination with the source's new semiprojectivity results.
Novelty confidence is therefore bounded rather than exhaustive.

Human review should focus on the intended meaning of ``complemented
sublattice'' in Question~5.14.  Under bounded linear complementation, the
proof is complete.  Under lattice-homomorphic complementation, retain the
construction only as a separation from that stronger notion.
