\section{Source question}

Let $G$ be a stratified homogeneous group with horizontal basis
$X_1,\ldots,X_m$ and homogeneous dimension $Q$.  Van Schaftingen and Yung
consider homogeneous operators
\[
 A(D)=\sum_{\gamma\in\{1,\ldots,m\}^k}A^\gamma X_{\gamma_1}\cdots X_{\gamma_k}
 :C^\infty(G;V)\longrightarrow C^\infty(G;E)
\]
and the endpoint estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint}
 \norm{D^{k-1}u}_{L^{Q/(Q-1)}(G;V)}
 \le C\norm{A(D)u}_{L^1(G;E)},\qquad u\in C_c^\infty(G;V).
\end{equation}
They ask whether \eqref{eq:endpoint} forces (i) maximal hypoellipticity and
(ii) a compatible operator with cocanceling symmetrization \cite{VSY}.

\section{Proof intuition}

There are two tests.  First, if the horizontal commutative symbol has a kernel
at a direction $c$, keep a test function compact along $c$ while dilating all
transverse coordinates.  The only term with all $k$ derivatives on the
one-dimensional factor is killed by the symbol kernel.  Every other term
costs $R^{-1}$, leaving the same strict $R^{1/Q}$ contradiction as in the
scalar first-order case.

Second, on a Heisenberg group, a failure of the Rockland condition occurs
either at a character (already detected by the first test) or in a
Schr\"odinger representation.  A smooth representation-kernel vector gives an
exact $A$-harmonic matrix coefficient, Schwartz horizontally and oscillatory
in the center.  Cut it off only at central scale $R^2$.  Its left norm grows
as $R^{2(Q-1)/Q}$, whereas every term on the right differentiates the cutoff,
costs $R^{-2}$, and is integrated over an interval of length $O(R^2)$.

\section{A necessary horizontal-symbol condition in all steps}

Define the commutative horizontal symbol
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symbol}
 A_{\mathrm h}(\xi)
 =\sum_{\gamma\in\{1,\ldots,m\}^k}
   \xi_{\gamma_1}\cdots\xi_{\gamma_k}A^\gamma,
 \qquad \xi\in\R^m.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Horizontal injective ellipticity]\label{thm:symbol}
If \eqref{eq:endpoint} holds on a stratified homogeneous group with $Q\ge2$,
then $A_{\mathrm h}(\xi):V\to E$ is injective for every
$\xi\in\R^m\setminus\{0\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $A_{\mathrm h}(c)v=0$ for nonzero $c\in\R^m$ and $0\ne v\in V$.
Choose the first horizontal basis vector in the direction $c$.  In the new
coordinates, the coefficient of $X_1^k$ kills $v$.

Complete the horizontal basis to a homogeneous basis $Y_1,\ldots,Y_N$ of
$\mathfrak g$, with weights $w_1=\cdots=w_m=1$ and
$\sum_{\nu=1}^Nw_\nu=Q$.  In exponential coordinates of the first kind,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fields}
 X_j=\partial_{x_j}+\sum_{\nu>m}p_{j\nu}(x)\partial_{x_\nu},
\end{equation}
where $p_{j\nu}$ is weighted-homogeneous of degree $w_\nu-1$.
Choose $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ with $\phi^{(k-1)}\ne0$ and nonzero
$\eta\in C_c^\infty(\R^{N-1})$, and set
\[
 \eta_R=\eta(R^{-w_2}x_2,\ldots,R^{-w_N}x_N),
 \qquad u_R=\phi(x_1)\eta_Rv.
\]

The monomial calculation from \eqref{eq:fields} shows that every fixed word
of horizontal derivatives in which at least one derivative falls on
$\eta_R$ is $O(R^{-1})$ on the support.  Indeed, a monomial $x^\alpha$ in
$p_{j\nu}$ contributes at most
\[
 R^{\sum_{\mu\ge2}w_\mu\alpha_\mu}R^{-w_\nu}
 =R^{-1-\alpha_1}\le R^{-1},
\]
and differentiating such polynomial coefficients a fixed number of times
does not worsen this bound.

Consequently the $X_1^{k-1}$ component of $D^{k-1}u_R$ is
\[
 \phi^{(k-1)}(x_1)\eta_Rv+O(R^{-1}),
\]
so, with $q=Q/(Q-1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:left}
 \norm{D^{k-1}u_R}_{L^q}\ge cR^{(Q-1)/q}
 =cR^{Q-2+1/Q}
\end{equation}
for large $R$.  In $A(D)u_R$, the only term with every derivative on $\phi$
is $A_{\mathrm h}(c)v\,\phi^{(k)}\eta_R=0$; every other term is
$O(R^{-1})$.  Since the transverse support has measure $O(R^{Q-1})$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:right}
 \norm{A(D)u_R}_{L^1}\le CR^{Q-2}.
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:left}--\eqref{eq:right} contradict
\eqref{eq:endpoint} as $R\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

\section{The full Rockland condition on Heisenberg groups}

Let $\mathbb H^n$ have coordinates $(z,t)\in\R^{2n}\times\R$, horizontal
fields $X_1,\ldots,X_{2n}$, central weight $2$, and homogeneous dimension
$Q=2n+2$.

\begin{theorem}[Maximal hypoellipticity on $\mathbb H^n$]\label{thm:heis}
For an arbitrary homogeneous $A(D):C^\infty(\mathbb H^n;V)\to
C^\infty(\mathbb H^n;E)$ of order $k$, validity of
\eqref{eq:endpoint} implies that $A(D)$ satisfies the Rockland condition.
Equivalently, $A(D)$ is maximally hypoelliptic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose the Rockland condition fails.  Then some nontrivial irreducible
unitary representation $\pi$ has a nonzero smooth vector
$w\in\mathcal H_\pi^\infty\otimes V$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rep-kernel}
 d\pi(A)w=0.
\end{equation}
For a one-dimensional representation, $d\pi(A)$ is a nonzero scalar multiple
of $A_{\mathrm h}(\xi)$ for a nonzero horizontal covector $\xi$, contradicting
Theorem~\ref{thm:symbol}.  We may therefore take $\pi=\pi_\lambda$ to be a
Schr\"odinger representation, $\lambda\ne0$.

Its smooth vectors are $\Sch(\R^n)$-vectors.  Choose
$\zeta\in\Sch(\R^n)$ and form the $V$-valued matrix coefficient
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient}
 F(g)=\langle\pi_\lambda(g)w,\zeta\rangle_{\mathcal H_\pi}.
\end{equation}
Here $\zeta$ is chosen so that some horizontal derivative of order $k-1$ of
$F$ is nonzero.  Such a choice exists: if all words of length $k-1$ killed
$w$, then in particular $d\pi(X_j)^{k-1}w=0$ for every $j$.  For $k>1$ the
skew-adjointness of $d\pi(X_j)$ gives $d\pi(X_j)w=0$ for every $j$; for $k=1$
the claim is immediate from $w\ne0$.  Since the horizontal fields generate
the Lie algebra, the former alternative would make $w$ invariant in a
nontrivial irreducible representation, a contradiction.

With the source convention $Xf(g)=\frac d{ds}|_{s=0}f(g\exp(sX))$,
\eqref{eq:rep-kernel} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:AFzero}
 A(D)F=0.
\end{equation}
The explicit Schr\"odinger formula shows
\[
 F(z,t)=e^{i\lambda t}f(z),
\]
where $f$ and all horizontal derivatives of the coefficient are Schwartz in
$z$.

Choose nonzero $\chi\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ and put
\[
 U_R(z,t)=F(z,t)\chi(t/R^2).
\]
These functions are not yet compactly supported in $z$, but multiplication
by $\rho(z/S)$ and passage $S\to\infty$ makes them admissible in
\eqref{eq:endpoint}; all required convergence follows from Schwartz decay.

Let $\gamma$ be a word of length $k-1$ with $X_\gamma F\ne0$.  Product
differentiation gives
\[
 X_\gamma U_R=\chi(t/R^2)X_\gamma F+\mathcal E_R.
\]
Every term in $\mathcal E_R$ differentiates the central cutoff at least once.
Since a horizontal Heisenberg field has the form $\partial_z+$ a linear
function of $z$ times $\partial_t$, such a term is $R^{-2}$ times a Schwartz
function of $z$ and a bounded derivative of $\chi(t/R^2)$ (terms with more
cutoff derivatives are smaller).  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heis-left}
 \norm{D^{k-1}U_R}_{L^q}
 \ge cR^{2/q}-O(R^{2/q-2})\ge c'R^{2/q}.
\end{equation}

By \eqref{eq:AFzero}, every product-rule term in $A(D)U_R$ also has at least
one derivative on the cutoff.  Its $z$ factor is Schwartz and its central
support has length $O(R^2)$, so the factor $R^{-2}$ cancels that length:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heis-right}
 \norm{A(D)U_R}_{L^1}\le C
\end{equation}
uniformly in $R$.  Equations \eqref{eq:heis-left}--\eqref{eq:heis-right}
contradict \eqref{eq:endpoint}.

Thus $A$ is Rockland.  The equivalence with maximal hypoellipticity follows
from the Rockland theorem applied to the formally positive operator
$A^t(D)A(D)$, as in Theorem 3.3 of \cite{VSY}.
\end{proof}

\section{What remains open}

Theorem~\ref{thm:symbol} supplies a necessary shadow of condition (i) on
every stratified group, and Theorem~\ref{thm:heis} proves condition (i) itself
on every Heisenberg group.  Neither theorem constructs the compatible
cocanceling operator required in condition (ii).  Moreover, extending the
matrix-coefficient cutoff from Heisenberg groups to arbitrary higher-step
groups requires uniform control in general induced-representation models.
The original question therefore remains open beyond the stated conclusions.

\section{Verification and novelty status}

The proof uses no computation.  Its main checks are: the strict exponent gap
$1/Q$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:symbol}; the left/right infinitesimal-representation
convention in \eqref{eq:coefficient}; and the $R^{-2}$ central-cutoff cost in
Theorem~\ref{thm:heis}.

A bounded search through the run indexes, local arXiv corpus, exact-title
queries, and the source's three citing works found no explicit statement of
these strengthened necessity results.  Novelty confidence is moderate rather
than definitive, and human expert review is recommended.
