\section*{Status and source problem}

Bakonyi--Constantinescu \cite{BC} define the \emph{completion number}
$\cn(G)$ as the least integer $k$ such that every partial positive Hermitian
matrix with graph $G$ has a Hermitian completion with at most $k$ negative
eigenvalues.  They ask for a graph-theoretic way to determine $\cn(G)$ and,
more specifically, for characterizations of the graphs with prescribed
completion number.

This packet gives graph-theoretic lower and upper bounds that coincide on a
large exact class.  It is a partial answer, not a full classification.

\section*{Definitions}

A partial Hermitian matrix with graph $G$ has every diagonal entry specified
and has the off-diagonal entry $a_{uv}$ specified exactly when $uv\in E(G)$.
It is \emph{partial positive} if its principal matrix on every clique of $G$
is positive semidefinite.

Let $\cvd(G)$ be the \emph{chordal vertex-deletion number}:
\[
 \cvd(G)=\min\{|S|:S\subseteq V(G),\ G-S\text{ is chordal}\}.
\]
Define the \emph{joined-obstruction packing number} $\jp(G)$ to be the largest
integer $r$ for which there are pairwise disjoint vertex sets
$V_1,\ldots,V_r$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item every induced graph $G[V_i]$ is nonchordal; and
\item for $i\ne j$, every vertex of $V_i$ is adjacent to every vertex of
      $V_j$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the induced graph on $V_1\cup\cdots\cup V_r$ is the complete join of
the nonchordal induced graphs $G[V_i]$.

\section*{Idea of the proof}

For the upper bound, delete a smallest set $S$ making the graph chordal and
positively complete the principal block outside $S$.  Restoring one deleted
row and column can increase the negative index by at most one, by Cauchy
interlacing.

For the lower bound, place on each nonchordal joined block a partial positive
matrix having no positive semidefinite completion.  Specify all cross-block
entries to be zero.  Those entries exist because the blocks are completely
joined.  Hence every completion contains a block-diagonal principal matrix
with at least one negative eigenvalue in each diagonal block.

\section*{The graph-theoretic sandwich}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:sandwich}
For every finite undirected graph $G$,
\[
 \boxed{\qquad \jp(G)\leq \cn(G)\leq \cvd(G).\qquad}
\]
Consequently, if $\jp(G)=\cvd(G)$, then this common graph-theoretic value is
the completion number of $G$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $k=\cvd(G)$ and choose $S\subseteq V(G)$ with $|S|=k$ such that
$H=G-S$ is chordal.  Let $A$ be any partial positive matrix with graph $G$.
Its restriction $A[H]$ is partial positive.  By the positive semidefinite
completion theorem for chordal graphs \cite{GJSW}, it has a positive
semidefinite completion $B$.

Use $B$ for the principal block indexed by $V(G)\setminus S$, retain every
specified entry of $A$ involving a vertex of $S$, and assign arbitrary values
(say zero) to all remaining unspecified entries.  This gives a Hermitian
completion $M$ of $A$ containing the positive semidefinite matrix $B$ as a
principal submatrix of codimension $k$.  Repeated Cauchy interlacing yields
\[
 i_-(M)\leq i_-(B)+k=k.
\]
Since $A$ was arbitrary, $\cn(G)\leq\cvd(G)$.

For the other inequality, let $V_1,\ldots,V_r$ witness $r=\jp(G)$ and write
$H_i=G[V_i]$.  Because $H_i$ is nonchordal, the converse part of the chordal
completion theorem gives a partial positive matrix $A_i$ with graph $H_i$
that has no positive semidefinite completion \cite{GJSW,BC}.

Construct a partial matrix $A$ with graph $G$ as follows.  Its restriction to
$V_i$ is $A_i$.  Put $1$ on the diagonal at every vertex outside the union of
the $V_i$, and put $0$ in every other specified off-diagonal position.  This
includes every position joining $V_i$ to $V_j$, because the two blocks are
completely adjacent.

The matrix $A$ is partial positive.  Indeed, on a clique $K$ its specified
principal matrix is the direct sum of the positive semidefinite clique
restrictions $A_i[K\cap V_i]$ and scalar $1$ blocks for the remaining
vertices.

Let $M$ be any Hermitian completion of $A$.  Its principal submatrix on
$V_1\cup\cdots\cup V_r$ is
\[
 M[V_1\cup\cdots\cup V_r]
   =M_1\oplus\cdots\oplus M_r,
\]
because all cross-block entries were specified as zero.  Here each $M_i$ is a
completion of $A_i$, so $i_-(M_i)\geq1$.  Therefore
\[
 i_-(M)\geq i_-(M[V_1\cup\cdots\cup V_r])
 =\sum_{i=1}^r i_-(M_i)\geq r.
\]
Thus $\cn(G)\geq\jp(G)$.
\end{proof}

\section*{Exact classes}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:one}
If $G$ is nonchordal and $G-v$ is chordal for some vertex $v$, then
$\cn(G)=1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Nonchordality gives $\jp(G)\geq1$, while the hypothesis gives
$\cvd(G)\leq1$. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:sandwich}.
\end{proof}

Every chordless cycle is covered by Corollary~\ref{cor:one}.  The source's
graph on $4r$ vertices whose complement is a matching of size $2r$ is the
join of $r$ chordless four-cycles.  Those factors witness $\jp(G)\geq r$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:sandwich} recovers the sharp lower bound in the source's
exact calculation $\cn(G)=r$.

\section*{Limitations and upgrade barrier}

The bounds need not be equal.  A tempting strengthening would claim that a
join of chordal graphs is chordal, but this is false: the join of two
independent two-vertex graphs is $K_{2,2}$.  The checker caught the analogous
failure for joins of cycles before promotion.

For example, if $m,n\geq2$, then
$K_{m,n}$ is nonchordal and
\[
 1\leq\cn(K_{m,n})\leq\min(m-1,n-1).
\]
The upper value is its chordal vertex-deletion number, while the joined-
obstruction construction gives only one block.  Ordinary vertex-disjoint
induced cycles do not repair the lower bound: entries between such cycles may
be unspecified, allowing a completion to couple their negative directions.
This coupling phenomenon is the remaining obstruction to a full graph-
theoretic characterization.

\section*{Verification and novelty notes}

The proof was audited separately at the three sensitive steps: extension of a
partial positive matrix after completing the chordal block, preservation of
partial positivity in the joined-obstruction construction, and additivity of
negative inertia on the fully specified zero cross blocks.  A small brute-
force graph checker verifies chordal-deletion number one for cycles and
confirms that the join of two four-cycles has deletion number three, thereby
falsifying the discarded join shortcut.  The proof does not depend on it.

The local run indexes and arXiv source corpus had no duplicate.  On
2026-08-11 an exact OpenAlex record lookup reported no citations to the source,
and bounded exact-phrase searches found no later solution; searches were noisy
because unrelated graph invariants share the phrase ``completion number.''
Novelty confidence is moderate.
