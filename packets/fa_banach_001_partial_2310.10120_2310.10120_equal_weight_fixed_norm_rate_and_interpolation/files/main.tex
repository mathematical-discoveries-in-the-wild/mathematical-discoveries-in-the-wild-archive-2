For equal weights and a probability density of fixed $L^p$ norm, the optimal
$N$-power is $N^{-1-1/d}$ for every $1<p<2$, not the power
$N^{-1-q/(2d)}$ in the source lower bound.  More generally, a continuum of
rigorous lower bounds interpolates between these two endpoints.  This does
not settle the optimal joint dependence on $N$ and $\|f\|_p$.  If the
source theorem is read literally with arbitrary nonnegative weights, however,
its full displayed dependence is sharp by a one-active-weight construction.}}
\end{center}

\section{The source question}

Let $1<p<2$, let $q=p/(p-1)$, and let
\[
 \mu_N=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_{z_j},\qquad
 \nu=\mu_N-f(x)\,dx,
\]
where $f\ge0$ and $\int_{\T^d}f=1$.  For fixed $0<a<b<1/2$, put
\[
 \Ecal_{a,b}(\nu)=
 \int_a^b\int_{\T^d}|\Dcal_N(x,r)|^2\,dx\,dr.
\]
The Fourier computation in Brandolini--Colzani--Travaglini~\cite{source}
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sobolev-equivalence}
 \Ecal_{a,b}(\nu)\asymp_{a,b,d}
 \sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^d}(1+|m|)^{-d-1}|\widehat\nu(m)|^2
 =\|\nu\|_{H^{-(d+1)/2}(\T^d)}^2.
\end{equation}
Their Theorem~6 proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-bound}
 \Ecal_{a,b}(\nu)\gtrsim
 N^{-1-q/(2d)}\|f\|_p^{-q/d}
\end{equation}
in the equal-weight normalization, and asks whether this is sharp for
$1<p<2$.  The exact statement is reproduced in Figure~\ref{fig:source}.

\section{The interpolation theorem}

For $u\in[p,2]$ define
\[
 \theta_u=1-\frac1u,
 \qquad
 \beta_u=d+1-2d\theta_u
           =1+d\left(\frac2u-1\right).
\]
Notice that $\beta_u\ge1$.

\begin{theorem}[Equal-weight interpolation family]\label{thm:interpolation}
Let $f\ge0$, $\int_{\T^d}f=1$, and $F=\|f\|_p$.  For every
$u\in[p,2]$ and every equal-weight empirical measure $\mu_N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family}
 \Ecal_{a,b}(\mu_N-fdx)
 \ge c_{a,b,d,p,u}\,
 N^{-2\theta_u-q\theta_u\beta_u/d}
 F^{-q\beta_u/d}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

At $u=2$, one has $\theta_u=1/2$ and $\beta_u=1$, so
\eqref{eq:family} is exactly \eqref{eq:source-bound}.  At the other endpoint,
$u=p$, it becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p-endpoint}
 \Ecal_{a,b}(\mu_N-fdx)
 \ge c_{a,b,d,p}
 N^{-1-1/d}F^{-(q-2)-q/d}.
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Proof intuition}
Smooth the empirical measure at a scale $h$.  Positivity and equal weights
force the $L^u$ norm of the resulting sum of $N$ bumps to be at least
$N^{-1/u'}h^{-d/u'}$, regardless of collisions among the points.  Young's
inequality bounds the smoothed density by
$Fh^{-d(1/p-1/u)}$.  The difference of the two exponents of $h$ is exactly
$1/q$.  Choosing the scale at which the atomic term is twice the density
term leaves a definite $L^u$, hence $L^2$, discrepancy.  The critical
Sobolev multiplier costs $h^{-(d+1)}$.  Varying $u$ gives the entire family.

\begin{proof}
Choose $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^d)$ nonnegative, with
$\int\phi=1$, and periodize
\[
 \phi_h(x)=h^{-d}\phi(x/h),\qquad 0<h<h_0.
\]
Put $g_h=\mu_N*\phi_h$.  Since $(\sum_j a_j)^u\ge\sum_j a_j^u$ for
nonnegative $a_j$,
\begin{align}
 \|g_h\|_u^u
 &=\int_{\T^d}\left|\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N
          \phi_h(x-z_j)\right|^u dx \\
 &\ge N^{-u}\sum_{j=1}^N\|\phi_h\|_u^u
 =c_{\phi,u}N^{1-u}h^{-d(u-1)}.
\end{align}
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:atomic-lu}
 \|g_h\|_u\ge c_0N^{-\theta_u}h^{-d\theta_u}.
\end{equation}

Let $v$ be determined by
$1+1/u=1/p+1/v$.  Young's inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-lu}
 \|f*\phi_h\|_u
 \le \|f\|_p\|\phi_h\|_v
 \le C_0Fh^{-d(1/p-1/u)}.
\end{equation}
Because
\[
 \theta_u-\left(\frac1p-\frac1u\right)=1-\frac1p=\frac1q,
\]
we may choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:h-choice}
 h=c_1N^{-q\theta_u/d}F^{-q/d},
\end{equation}
with $c_1=c_1(d,p,u,\phi)>0$ small enough that the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:atomic-lu} is at least twice that of
\eqref{eq:density-lu}.  Since $F\ge1$, the scale lies below a fixed $h_0$
after decreasing $c_1$ if necessary.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:difference-lu}
 \|(\mu_N-fdx)*\phi_h\|_u
 \ge c_2N^{-\theta_u}h^{-d\theta_u}.
\end{equation}
As $u\le2$ and $|\T^d|=1$, the same lower bound holds for the $L^2$ norm.

The rapid decay of $\widehat\phi$ implies, uniformly for $m\in\mathbb Z^d$,
\[
 |\widehat\phi(hm)|^2
 \le C h^{-d-1}(1+|m|)^{-d-1}.
\]
Parseval consequently gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smoothing-sobolev}
 \|(\mu_N-fdx)*\phi_h\|_2^2
 \le Ch^{-d-1}\|\mu_N-fdx\|_{H^{-(d+1)/2}}^2.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:difference-lu}--\eqref{eq:smoothing-sobolev}, then
substituting \eqref{eq:h-choice}, yields
\[
 \|\mu_N-fdx\|_{H^{-(d+1)/2}}^2
 \ge cN^{-2\theta_u}h^{d+1-2d\theta_u}
 =cN^{-2\theta_u-q\theta_u\beta_u/d}F^{-q\beta_u/d}.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:sobolev-equivalence} finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{A sharp fixed-norm consequence}

\begin{corollary}[Optimal $N$-power when $F$ is fixed]\label{cor:fixed}
Fix $F\ge1$.  Among probability densities with $\|f\|_p=F$ and equal-weight
$N$-point measures, the best possible $N$-power in the squared averaged-ball
discrepancy is $N^{-1-1/d}$ (with constants allowed to depend on $F$).  The
upper bound holds for $N=H^d$ and hence already proves sharpness of the power.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The lower bound follows from \eqref{eq:p-endpoint}.  For the upper bound,
let $Q\subset\T^d$ be a cube of volume $F^{-q}$ and set
$f=F^q\mathbf1_Q$, so $\int f=1$ and $\|f\|_p=F$.  If $N=H^d$, partition
$Q$ into $N$ congruent subcubes $Q_j$ of diameter
\[
 \ell\asymp F^{-q/d}N^{-1/d}.
\]
Choose independent $X_j$ uniformly in $Q_j$.  In the Hilbert space
$H^{-(d+1)/2}(\T^d)$ the centered random measures
\[
 Y_j=\delta_{X_j}-Nf\mathbf1_{Q_j}\,dx
\]
have vanishing cross terms.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dirac-distance}
 \|\delta_x-\delta_y\|_{H^{-(d+1)/2}}^2\le C_d|x-y|.
\end{equation}
Indeed, split the defining Fourier sum at $|m|=|x-y|^{-1}$, use
$|e^{2\pi imx}-e^{2\pi imy}|\le C|m||x-y|$ below the split, and the trivial
bound above it.  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E\left\|\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^NY_j\right\|_{H^{-(d+1)/2}}^2
 =\frac1{N^2}\sum_{j=1}^N\mathbb E\|Y_j\|_{H^{-(d+1)/2}}^2
 \le C_d\frac{\ell}{N}.
\]
Some realization therefore satisfies
\[
 \Ecal_{a,b}(\mu_N-fdx)
 \lesssim N^{-1-1/d}F^{-q/d},
\]
by \eqref{eq:sobolev-equivalence}.
\end{proof}

This fixed-norm rate is consistent with the independent May 2026 theorem of
Colasanto--Focardi--Fornasier--Mattesini~\cite{mmd}: for the power kernel
$-|x-y|$ and a $d$-Ahlfors regular target, their energy distance squared has
sharp rate $N^{-1-1/d}$.  Their theorem does not cover arbitrary $L^p$
densities or determine the dependence on $F$.

\section{The literal arbitrary-weight statement is sharp}

The wording surrounding the source question points to equal weights, as does
the construction in its Theorem~7.  The displayed lower bound itself permits
arbitrary nonnegative weights.  Under that literal reading there is a short
complete answer.

\begin{proposition}[One-active-weight sharpness]\label{prop:oneweight}
For every $1<p\le2$, the joint powers of $N$, $\|\alpha\|$, and
$\|f\|_p$ in the source inequality are simultaneously sharp when arbitrary
nonnegative weights are allowed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $\alpha_1=\sqrt N$, $\alpha_2=\cdots=\alpha_N=0$ and
$z_1=0$.  Then the normalized weight norm in the source is
$\|\alpha\|=1$, while the atomic measure is
$N^{-1/2}\delta_0$.  With the same approximate identity as above, set
\[
 f_h=N^{-1/2}\phi_h.
\]
Then $\|f_h\|_p\asymp N^{-1/2}h^{-d/q}$ and, as $h\downarrow0$,
\[
 \Ecal_{a,b}(N^{-1/2}(\delta_0-\phi_hdx))\asymp N^{-1}h.
\]
The last equivalence follows directly by splitting the Fourier series in
\eqref{eq:sobolev-equivalence} at $|m|=h^{-1}$; the tail already has size
$h$.  On the other hand, the right-hand side of the source bound equals
\[
 N^{-1-q/(2d)}\|\alpha\|^{2+q/d}\|f_h\|_p^{-q/d}
 \asymp N^{-1-q/(2d)}N^{q/(2d)}h=N^{-1}h.
\]
Thus all displayed exponents are attained.
\end{proof}

\section{What remains open}

The intended equal-weight problem asks for the optimal joint dependence on
$N$ and $F=\|f\|_p$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:interpolation} supplies a continuous
tradeoff, but its $u=p$ endpoint has the extra factor $F^{-(q-2)}$, while the
source $u=2$ endpoint has the weaker $N$-power.  A natural attempt to remove
this gap uses empty-ball or Whitney bump tests at scales adapted to the local
$L^p$ mass.  At the critical Sobolev order each bump has exactly the right
scale, but an arbitrary density may distribute its mass across infinitely
many spatial scales; the resulting stopping-time sum is not controlled by
$\|f\|_p$ alone.  No valid argument found here removes that multiscale
obstruction, and no claim about the optimal joint exponents is made.
