\section{Source question}

Park and Slep\v{c}ev~\cite{PS} define the length metric $\ellSW$ induced by
the quadratic sliced-Wasserstein distance $\SW$ on $\Pp(\R^d)$. Their
Remark~4.7, on printed and PDF page~26 of arXiv:2311.05134, asks whether an
$\ellSW$-geodesic between measures supported in the closed unit ball must
remain supported there.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Write $R_\theta\mu=(x\mapsto\theta\cdot x)_\#\mu$ and use normalized surface
measure in every spherical average. Thus
\[
 \SW^2(\mu,\nu)=\sphereavg
 W_2^2(R_\theta\mu,R_\theta\nu)\,d\theta,
 \qquad \SW\le \ellSW\le d^{-1/2}W_2.
\]

\section{Statement and proof intuition}

For $\rho\in\Pp(\R^d)$ define its unit-ball leakage by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leak}
 \mathcal E_B(\rho)^2
 :=\sphereavg\int_{\R} (|r|-1)_+^2\,d(R_\theta\rho)(r)\,d\theta.
\end{equation}
This quantity vanishes exactly when $\rho$ is supported in the unit ball.

The one-dimensional $W_2$ quantile formula realizes $\SW$ as a chordal
distance in a Hilbert space. Unit-ball support becomes a closed convex
pointwise box in that space, and $\mathcal E_B$ is precisely distance to the
box. A simple projection inequality says that a path from one point of a
convex set to another cannot pass a distance $r$ from the set with total
chord length less than $\sqrt{D^2+4r^2}$, where $D$ is the endpoint chord.
An intrinsic geodesic has available total chord length at most its intrinsic
length. The difference between these two quantities therefore pays for every
possible support excursion.

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic-gap leakage bound]\label{thm:main}
Let $\mu_0,\mu_1\in\Pp(\R^d)$ be supported in the closed unit ball, and let
$(\mu_t)_{0\le t\le1}$ be any constant-speed $\ellSW$-geodesic between them.
Set
\[
 L=\ellSW(\mu_0,\mu_1),\qquad D=\SW(\mu_0,\mu_1).
\]
Then for every $t\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 \mathcal E_B(\mu_t)
 \le \min\left\{tL,(1-t)L,\frac12\sqrt{L^2-D^2}\right\}.
\end{equation}
In particular, if $\ellSW(\mu_0,\mu_1)=\SW(\mu_0,\mu_1)$, then every
$\ellSW$-geodesic between the endpoints stays supported in the unit ball.
\end{theorem}

\section{The Hilbert-space argument}

Let $Q_\mu(\theta,s)$ be the generalized quantile at $s\in(0,1)$ of
$R_\theta\mu$. The one-dimensional quantile identity gives the isometric
embedding
\begin{equation}\label{eq:embed}
 \|Q_\mu-Q_\nu\|_{L^2(\mathbb S^{d-1}\times(0,1))}
 =\SW(\mu,\nu).
\end{equation}
Consider the closed convex subset of this Hilbert space
\[
 \mathcal C=\{q: |q(\theta,s)|\le1\text{ for a.e. }(\theta,s)\}.
\]
Pointwise clipping to $[-1,1]$ is the metric projection onto $\mathcal C$.
Consequently, by the quantile change of variables,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distC}
 \operatorname{dist}(Q_\rho,\mathcal C)^2
 =\sphereavg\int_0^1(|Q_\rho(\theta,s)|-1)_+^2\,ds\,d\theta
 =\mathcal E_B(\rho)^2.
\end{equation}

We isolate the elementary geometry used next.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hilbert}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a closed convex subset of a Hilbert space, let
$x,y\in\mathcal C$, and let $z$ be arbitrary. If
$D=\|x-y\|$ and $r=\operatorname{dist}(z,\mathcal C)$, then
\[
 \|z-x\|+\|z-y\|\ge\sqrt{D^2+4r^2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $p$ be the metric projection of $z$ onto $\mathcal C$. The projection
inequality $\langle z-p,q-p\rangle\le0$ for $q\in\mathcal C$ gives
\[
 \|z-x\|^2\ge r^2+\|p-x\|^2,
 \qquad
 \|z-y\|^2\ge r^2+\|p-y\|^2.
\]
Put $u=\|p-x\|$ and $v=\|p-y\|$. Since $u+v\ge D$ and
$s\mapsto\sqrt{r^2+s^2}$ is convex,
\begin{align*}
 \|z-x\|+\|z-y\|
 &\ge \sqrt{r^2+u^2}+\sqrt{r^2+v^2}\\
 &\ge2\sqrt{r^2+((u+v)/2)^2}
 \ge\sqrt{4r^2+D^2}.
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Put $x=Q_{\mu_0}$, $y=Q_{\mu_1}$, and $z=Q_{\mu_t}$. The endpoint support
assumption gives $x,y\in\mathcal C$. Since $(\mu_t)$ is a constant-speed
$\ellSW$-geodesic and $\SW\le\ellSW$,
\[
 \|z-x\|=\SW(\mu_0,\mu_t)\le tL,
 \qquad
 \|z-y\|=\SW(\mu_t,\mu_1)\le(1-t)L.
\]
Thus each of the first two bounds in \eqref{eq:main} follows from
\eqref{eq:distC}, since $x,y\in\mathcal C$. Their sum is at most $L$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:hilbert}, \eqref{eq:embed}, and \eqref{eq:distC} therefore give
\[
 L\ge\sqrt{D^2+4\mathcal E_B(\mu_t)^2},
\]
which is the final bound in \eqref{eq:main}.

It remains to justify that zero leakage is genuine support containment.
Tonelli's theorem rewrites \eqref{eq:leak} as
\[
 \mathcal E_B(\rho)^2
 =\int_{\R^d}\sphereavg (|\theta\cdot x|-1)_+^2\,d\theta\,d\rho(x).
\]
For every $x$ with $|x|>1$, the inner integral is strictly positive because
an open set of directions near $x/|x|$ has $|\theta\cdot x|>1$. Hence
$\mathcal E_B(\rho)=0$ forces $\rho(\overline B(0,1))=1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Complete subclasses}

The preceding result immediately converts every equality
$\ellSW=\SW$ into an affirmative support theorem.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:classes}
Under the endpoint support hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, every
$\ellSW$-geodesic remains in the unit ball in each of the following cases:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item one endpoint is a Dirac mass;
 \item both endpoints are supported on one affine line;
 \item $\mu_1=T_\#\mu_0$ for $T(x)=\lambda x+b$ with $\lambda\ge0$;
 \item more generally, the pair admits an extrinsic $\SW$-geodesic.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the first case,
\[
 \SW^2(\delta_a,\mu)=\sphereavg\int|\theta\cdot(x-a)|^2\,d\mu(x)d\theta
 =d^{-1}W_2^2(\delta_a,\mu).
\]
If both measures live on an affine line with unit direction $u$, every
projected $W_2$ distance is $|\theta\cdot u|$ times the one-dimensional
distance along that line; spherical averaging again gives
$\SW=d^{-1/2}W_2$.

For $T(x)=\lambda x+b$, the map induced on every projection is the
nondecreasing affine map $r\mapsto\lambda r+\theta\cdot b$, hence is optimal
in one dimension. The same spherical identity gives
\[
 \SW^2(\mu_0,T_\#\mu_0)
 =d^{-1}\int|T(x)-x|^2\,d\mu_0(x)
 =d^{-1}W_2^2(\mu_0,T_\#\mu_0).
\]
In all three cases $\SW\le\ellSW\le d^{-1/2}W_2$ forces $L=D$. In the fourth
case the very existence of a path whose $\SW$-length equals the endpoint
$\SW$ distance likewise forces $L=D$. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

Translations and dilations were already known extrinsic geodesics; Hopper
\cite{Hopper} gives further extrinsic examples. Han~\cite{Han} proves, under
an absolute-continuity hypothesis, rigidity and stability for the different
deficit $d^{-1}W_2^2-\SW^2$ and identifies affine homotheties as its equality
case. Those known facts are not claimed as novel here.

\section{Upgrade attempts and remaining obstruction}

\paragraph{Metric projection.}
The most direct proof would project a minimizing curve radially onto the
ball. Remark~4.7 of the source supplies an explicit counterexample showing
that radial projection can increase $\SW$, so endpoint contraction is false.

\paragraph{Quantile clipping.}
Pointwise clipping is contractive in the Hilbert quantile model and is exactly
what produces Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. But a clipped family of one-dimensional
distributions need not be the Radon transform of a probability measure. The
failure of inverse Radon transforms to preserve positivity is also the source
of the strict gap $\ellSW>\SW$ exhibited in the source paper. Thus the clipped
Hilbert path cannot simply replace the original geodesic.

\paragraph{Particle approximation.}
For a curve of finitely many separated atoms, the infinitesimal sliced action
is the Euclidean particle action divided by $d$; Euclidean projection then
does reduce length. This does not pass to arbitrary $\SW$-absolutely
continuous curves: at diffuse measures the optimal sliced flux lives in a
much weaker negative-Sobolev geometry and need not be of the form $v\mu$.
Approximating it by particle curves can raise the action to the Wasserstein
scale.

\paragraph{First-exit and gap iteration.}
Applying the convex-set estimate at a hypothetical first exit yields the
quantitative bound \eqref{eq:main}, but no contradiction when $L>D$. The
strict intrinsic gap provides exactly the budget for an excursion. Neither
subsegment iteration nor the endpoint moment identities eliminate that
budget without an additional Radon-consistent retraction or a new local
convexity theorem for the image of probability measures in quantile space.

These attempts isolate a precise obstruction. The unrestricted question is
therefore not promoted to a full solution or counterexample.

\section{Novelty audit and review recommendation}

A bounded search on 11 August 2026 covered the run indexes; the exact source
question; combinations of ``intrinsic sliced Wasserstein'', ``geodesic'',
``support'', ``ball'', ``quantile'', and ``length gap''; Hopper's 2024
extrinsic-geodesic paper; and Han's May 2026 deficit-rigidity paper. No located
source states \eqref{eq:main} or resolves the general support question. This
supports, but cannot certify, novelty.

The qualitative zero-gap conclusion is close to implicit in source
Remark~4.6, which characterizes a geodesic attaining $\SW$ slice by slice.
Accordingly, the primary candidate-new contribution is the quantitative
intrinsic-gap leakage estimate, not the known translation/dilation examples.
Expert review should focus on Lemma~\ref{lem:hilbert} and the distance identity
\eqref{eq:distC}.
