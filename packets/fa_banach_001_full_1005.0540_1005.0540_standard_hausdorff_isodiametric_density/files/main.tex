\noindent\textbf{Status.} Full affirmative solution, high confidence.  The
conjecture in arXiv:1005.0540 follows from a uniform blow-up lemma in which
each competing set is rescaled by its \emph{own} diameter.  The same lemma
also proves continuity of the density and answers all three ordinary
Hausdorff-measure questions later isolated in arXiv:1702.00241.

\section{Source conjecture}

Let $(M,\mathcal D,g)$ be an equiregular sub-Riemannian manifold of
Hausdorff dimension $Q$, let $\mu$ be a smooth volume, and let $\Hh^Q$ be
ordinary (not spherical) Hausdorff measure, with covering cost
$(\diam A)^Q$.  Agrachev, Barilari, and Boscain conjecture that the density in
$d\mu=f_{\mu\Hh}\,d\Hh^Q$ is the largest induced tangent volume of a set of
diameter one~\cite{ABB}:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=.96\textwidth]{figures/source_conjecture_crop.png}
\end{center}

At $p\in M$, write $(G_p,\widehat d_p)$ for the nilpotent approximation and
$\widehat\mu_p$ for the induced Haar volume, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C}
 C(p):=\sup\{\widehat\mu_p(A):A\subset G_p,\ \diam_{\widehat d_p}A=1\}.
\end{equation}
The supremum can equivalently be taken over compact sets and is attained by
the existence theorem for isodiametric sets~\cite{Rigot}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every regular point $p$,
\[
 \frac{d\mu}{d\Hh^Q}(p)=C(p).
\]
Here the left side is its canonical Federer-density representative; in
particular $d\mu=C(\cdot)\,d\Hh^Q$.  Moreover $C$ is continuous on every
equiregular component.
\end{theorem}

Consequently the tangent formula conjectured in the source is true.  In the
notation of Ghezzi--Jean~\cite{GJ}, the local isodiametric constants satisfy
$\mathscr I(p,\epsilon)\to\mathscr I_p$.  The function
$p\mapsto\mathscr I_p$ and the density $d\Hh^Q/d\mu=1/C(p)$ are continuous,
and the ordinary Hausdorff metric-measure blow-ups converge to
$(G_p,\widehat d_p,\Hh^Q_{\widehat d_p},e)$.

\section{Proof intuition}

Federer differentiation expresses $d\mu/d\Hh^Q(p)$ as a supremum over all
sufficiently small closed sets containing $p$.  Arbitrary shapes are not the
real difficulty; the scale is.  If all competitors are enlarged by the same
outer cutoff $\epsilon$, a set much smaller than $\epsilon$ sees a useless
additive metric error.  Instead, rescale each set by its own diameter $t$.
Uniform pairwise convergence of the rescaled sub-Riemannian distance then
makes its tangent diameter $1+o(1)$, uniformly over every possible shape.
Smoothness of the volume form gives an equally uniform volume comparison.
The tangent isodiametric inequality does the rest.

\section{The uniform-supremum lemma}

Choose privileged coordinates $x$ centered at $p$, write $\delta_t$ for
their anisotropic dilations, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
 J(p,\epsilon):=\sup\left\{
 \frac{\mu(S)}{(\diam_d S)^Q}: S\subset M\text{ closed},\ p\in S,
 0<\diam_d S<\epsilon\right\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:blowup}
$\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}J(p,\epsilon)=C(p)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The precise metric blow-up used below is the standard uniform pairwise
convergence, recorded explicitly in the proof of Ghezzi--Jean,
Proposition~3.3~\cite{GJ}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metric}
 a(t):=\sup_{u,v\in \overline B_d(p,t)}
 \left|\frac{d(u,v)}t-
 \widehat d_p\bigl(\delta_{1/t}x(u),\delta_{1/t}x(v)\bigr)\right|
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}

Take a competitor $S$ in \eqref{eq:J} and put $t=\diam_d S$.  Since $p\in
S$, it is contained in $\overline B_d(p,t)$.  The rescaled set
$A_t=\delta_{1/t}x(S)$ lies in one fixed compact set $K$ by the ball--box
theorem.  Equation \eqref{eq:metric} gives, uniformly over all such $S$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diam}
 1-a(t)\leq \diam_{\widehat d_p}A_t\leq1+a(t).
\end{equation}

Write $d\mu=g(z)\,dz$ in these coordinates.  The anisotropic Jacobian of
$\delta_t$ is $t^Q$, while $d\widehat\mu_p=g(0)\,dz$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:volume}
 t^{-Q}\mu(S)=\int_{A_t}g(\delta_tz)\,dz.
\end{equation}
If $b(t)=\sup_{z\in K}|g(\delta_tz)-g(0)|$, then $b(t)\to0$ and
\[
 \left|t^{-Q}\mu(S)-\widehat\mu_p(A_t)\right|
 \leq b(t)|K|.
\]
By homogeneity of \eqref{eq:C},
$\widehat\mu_p(A)\leq C(p)(\diam_{\widehat d_p}A)^Q$.  Together with
\eqref{eq:diam}, this proves
\[
 \frac{\mu(S)}{t^Q}\leq C(p)(1+a(t))^Q+b(t)|K|,
\]
uniformly for all competitors, and therefore
$\limsup_{\epsilon\downarrow0}J(p,\epsilon)\leq C(p)$.

Conversely, fix $\eta>0$ and choose a compact $A\subset G_p$ of diameter
one with $\widehat\mu_p(A)>C(p)-\eta$.  A left translation preserves both
quantities, so assume $e\in A$.  For small $t$, let
$S_t=x^{-1}(\delta_tA)$.  Uniform metric convergence and
\eqref{eq:volume} give
\[
 \frac{\diam_d S_t}{t}\to1,
 \qquad \frac{\mu(S_t)}{t^Q}\to\widehat\mu_p(A).
\]
Thus $\liminf J(p,\epsilon)\geq C(p)-\eta$.  Letting $\eta\downarrow0$
finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{Density and continuity}

Ghezzi--Jean record the Federer formula~\cite{GJ}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Federer}
 \frac{d\mu}{d\Hh^Q}(p)=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}J(p,\epsilon).
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}'s density identity follows immediately from
Lemma~\ref{lem:blowup}.  This direct route also fixes the normalization: in
Euclidean space it reads $d\mathcal L^n/d\Hh^n=\omega_n/2^n$, the volume of
a diameter-one Euclidean ball.

It remains to prove the asserted continuity.  Around $p$, choose privileged
coordinates smoothly in $q$ and identify all $G_q$ with $\R^n$.  The tangent
radial distances vary uniformly on compact sets~\cite{ABB}; since their group
laws also vary smoothly, the pairwise distances $\widehat d_q$ do likewise.
The induced Haar densities vary uniformly on compact sets as well.

Let $q_j\to p$.  For each $j$, translate a compact diameter-one
near-maximizer $A_j$ for $C(q_j)$ so that it contains the identity.  Uniform
ball--box estimates place every $A_j$ in one compact $K$.  Uniform convergence
of distance and density gives
\[
 \diam_{\widehat d_p}A_j=1+o(1),\qquad
 \widehat\mu_p(A_j)=\widehat\mu_{q_j}(A_j)+o(1),
\]
and hence $\limsup_jC(q_j)\leq C(p)$.  For the reverse inequality, use one
compact near-maximizer for $C(p)$ as a competitor in every tangent structure
at $q_j$.  Its diameter and volume converge, so
\[
 \liminf_j C(q_j)\geq C(p).
\]
This proves continuity.

Finally, the spherical density formula of~\cite{ABB} and Rigot's identity
$\Ss^Q=\mathscr I_p\Hh^Q$ on $G_p$ give the independent check
\[
 \mathscr I_p=\frac{2^Q C(p)}{\widehat\mu_p(\widehat B_p)},
 \qquad
 \frac{d\Hh^Q}{d\mu}(p)=
 \frac{2^Q}{\mathscr I_p\widehat\mu_p(\widehat B_p)}=\frac1{C(p)}.
\]
Continuity of $C(p)$ and of tangent ball volume proves continuity of
$\mathscr I_p$.  Proposition~3.10 of~\cite{GJ} then gives the stated measured
Gromov--Hausdorff convergence.

\section{Scope and verification}

The theorem concerns regular/equiregular points.  It does not claim an
ordinary-Hausdorff tangent formula at singular points, where the homogeneous
dimension and volume behavior can change.  No classification or explicit
shape of tangent isodiametric sets is needed.

The proof was audited at four levels: the cutoff-versus-own-diameter scale
issue; the uniform Jacobian bound for arbitrary varying sets; the direct
Federer normalization; and continuity under varying tangent structures.  The
source, the later equivalent formulation, and Rigot's tangent normalization
were all checked in primary sources.  Bounded searches through 13 August 2026
found no later proof of these exact questions.
