\textbf{Status.} \texttt{literature\_already\_answered}. The later paper
removes exactly the hypothesis queried by the source; this packet records the
identification and does not claim a new proof.

\section*{The source question}
Bonicatto--Pasqualetto--Rajala work on a PI space
$(X,d,\mathfrak m)$, meaning a complete metric measure space with doubling
measure and a weak $(1,1)$-Poincar\'e inequality. A finite-perimeter set $E$
is indecomposable if it has no disjoint positive-measure partition
$E=F\cup G$ satisfying
\[
 P(E,X)=P(F,X)+P(G,X).
\]
Their Theorem~2.14 proves, under an additional \emph{isotropicity}
assumption, a unique finite or countable perimeter-additive partition of every
$E$ into maximal indecomposable pieces. Immediately before stating their
results, the introduction asks whether isotropicity is in fact needed for the
decomposition to hold.

\section*{Exact later answer}
Lahti's arXiv:2103.14459 is titled \emph{A note on indecomposable sets of
finite perimeter}. Its abstract says that the isotropicity condition can be
removed. Theorem~1.1 states:

\begin{quote}
Let $(X,d,\mathfrak m)$ be a PI space and let $E\subset X$ have finite
perimeter. Then there is a unique finite or countable partition
$\{E_i\}_{i\in I}$ of $E$ into indecomposable subsets such that
$\mathfrak m(E_i)>0$ and
\[
 P(E,X)=\sum_{i\in I}P(E_i,X).
\]
Uniqueness is modulo $\mathfrak m$-null sets. Moreover, the $E_i$ are maximal:
every finite-perimeter indecomposable $F\subset E$ is contained modulo null
sets in a unique $E_i$.
\end{quote}

This reproduces every clause of the source's Theorem~2.14 while dropping
isotropicity. Hence the source question has a complete affirmative answer:
\[
 \boxed{\text{isotropicity is not needed for the decomposition theorem.}}
\]

\section*{Why the later proof applies}
The obstacle in the source proof was a hereditary additivity lemma obtained
from the Hausdorff-density representation of perimeter and isotropicity.
Lahti replaces it by two results proved directly from BV relaxation and weak
upper gradients:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $F\cap G$ is null and perimeter is additive for $F\cup G$ on a set
$D$, then it is additive for every finite-perimeter pair
$F'\subset F$, $G'\subset G$ on $D$.
\item If perimeter is additive for disjoint $F,G$ on an open set $W$, it
remains additive on every smaller open set $W'\subset W$.
\end{enumerate}
The first recovers the partition-refinement lemma and stability of increasing
unions of indecomposable sets. The second recovers local-to-global
indecomposability. Lahti then follows the source's decomposition proof almost
verbatim. No isotropicity is reintroduced elsewhere.

\section*{Scope boundary}
The answer concerns the decomposition theorem, exactly as asked. The later
paper notes that some other assertions in the 2020 paper, particularly about
simple sets, can fail without isotropicity or an even stronger two-sided
property. Those separate assertions are not promoted here.
