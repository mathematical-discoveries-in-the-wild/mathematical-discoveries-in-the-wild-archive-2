\section*{Status and source question}

\textbf{Status:} candidate full counterexample, likely valid pending expert
review.

Gennaioli--Stefani \cite{GS} consider nonnegative locally integrable kernels
$\rho_t$ and the BBM functionals
\[
 \mathscr F^{\rho}_{t,p}(u)
 =\int_{\R^N}\frac{\|u(\,\cdot+z)-u\|_{L^p}^p}{|z|^p}
   \rho_t(z)\,dz,
 \qquad 1\le p<\infty.
\]
Their Theorem~1.2 says that their kernel hypotheses plus maximal rank imply
coercivity: if $u_k\to u$ in $L^p$ and
$\liminf_k\mathscr F_{t_k,p}(u_k)<\infty$, then
$u\in W^{1,p}$ for $p>1$, and $u\in BV$ for $p=1$.  Immediately afterward,
on source PDF page~3, they ask whether maximal rank is necessary for this
conclusion.

\section*{Proof intuition}

The functional cannot detect how mass is split between $z$ and $-z$: its
translation-difference factor is even.  Begin with the normalized radial disk
kernel, whose energies are coercive.  At every scale, move all mass from one
half-disk to the opposite half-disk and double what remains.  This changes the
raw directional mass, but not a single value of the functional.  If the chosen
half-disk rotates through a dense set of orientations as the scale shrinks,
every fixed narrow cone is entirely missed infinitely often.  Thus coercivity
survives exactly while maximal rank fails as strongly as possible.

\section*{Counterexample theorem}

Let $N=2$, let $\omega_2=|B_1|$, and for $0<t<1$ put
\[
 \kappa_t(z)=\frac{\mathbf 1_{B_t}(z)}{\omega_2t^2},
 \qquad
 v_t=\bigl(\cos(\log(1/t)),\sin(\log(1/t))\bigr),
\]
\[
 \rho_t(z)=2\kappa_t(z)\mathbf 1_{\{z\cdot v_t>0\}},
 \qquad t_k=e^{-k}.
\]

\begin{theorem}
For every $p\in[1,\infty)$, the family $(\rho_{t_k})$ satisfies condition
\emph{(A)} of Theorem~1.1 in \cite{GS}, and the functionals
$(\mathscr F^{\rho}_{t_k,p})$ are coercive on
\[
 \mathcal S^p(\R^2)=
 \begin{cases}
 W^{1,p}(\R^2),&p>1,\\
 BV(\R^2),&p=1.
 \end{cases}
\]
Nevertheless $(\rho_{t_k})$ does not have maximal rank.  Consequently,
maximal rank is not necessary for the conclusion of source Theorem~1.2.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The half-disk has half the area of $B_t$, so $\rho_t\ge0$,
$\rho_t\in L^1(\R^2)$, and $\int\rho_t=1$.  Its support lies in $B_t$;
hence $\rho_{t_k}\,dz\rightharpoonup^*\delta_0$.  Moreover, for every $R>0$,
\[
 R^p\int_{\R^2}\frac{\rho_{t_k}(z)}{R^p+|z|^p}\,dz
 \le \int_{\R^2}\rho_{t_k}(z)\,dz=1.
\]
This is condition~(A), with $\alpha=1$.

For $u\in L^p(\R^2)$ define
\[
 A_u(z)=\frac{\|u(\,\cdot+z)-u\|_{L^p}^p}{|z|^p},\qquad z\ne0.
\]
A change of variables shows $A_u(-z)=A_u(z)$.  Since $\kappa_t$ is even and
the hyperplane $z\cdot v_t=0$ is null,
\begin{align*}
 \mathscr F^{\rho}_{t,p}(u)
 &=2\int_{z\cdot v_t>0}A_u(z)\kappa_t(z)\,dz\\
 &=\int_{z\cdot v_t>0}A_u(z)\kappa_t(z)\,dz
   +\int_{z\cdot v_t<0}A_u(z)\kappa_t(z)\,dz\\
 &=\mathscr F^{\kappa}_{t,p}(u).
\end{align*}
The equality holds for every $t$, every $p$, and every $u$, including when
the value is infinite.

The radial family $(\kappa_t)$ has maximal rank and satisfies condition~(A).
Therefore Theorem~1.2 of \cite{GS} gives: whenever $u_k\to u$ in $L^p$ and
$\liminf_k\mathscr F^{\kappa}_{t_k,p}(u_k)<\infty$, one has
$u\in\mathcal S^p(\R^2)$.  The exact functional identity transfers this
coercivity statement verbatim to $(\rho_{t_k})$.

It remains to disprove maximal rank.  Fix any $w\in\Sph^1$ and
$\tau\in(0,1)$.  The cone
\[
 \mathcal C_\tau(w)
 =\{z:z\cdot w\ge(1-\tau)|z|\}
\]
has angular half-width $a_\tau=\arccos(1-\tau)<\pi/2$.  The sequence
$v_{t_k}=(\cos k,\sin k)$ has dense tails on $\Sph^1$.  Hence there are
infinitely many $k$ for which the angle between $v_{t_k}$ and $-w$ is less
than $\pi/2-a_\tau$.  For each such $k$,
\[
 z\cdot v_{t_k}<0
 \quad\text{for every }z\in\mathcal C_\tau(w)\setminus\{0\},
\]
and consequently, for every $\delta>0$,
\[
 \int_{B_\delta\cap\mathcal C_\tau(w)}\rho_{t_k}(z)\,dz=0.
\]
Thus the liminf cone mass is zero for every fixed $w$ and every admissible
$\tau$.  No basis can satisfy Definition~2.8 of \cite{GS}; maximal rank
fails.
\end{proof}

\section*{Verification, limitations, and novelty}

The proof uses no numerical approximation.  The normalization, condition~(A),
exact even-part identity, coercivity transfer, and cone exclusion were checked
separately; details are recorded in \texttt{verification.md}.  Notice that no
symmetry of $\rho_t$ is among the source hypotheses.  The construction answers
the literal question, but it does not decide whether maximal rank (or a
symmetrized variant) is necessary when every kernel is required to be even.

The run indexes and exact-phrase/arXiv searches through 11 August 2026 found
the current source, whose November 2025 revision still asks the question, but
no later resolution or matching rotating-half-space construction.  Novelty
confidence is moderate to high, subject to specialist citation review.

\section*{Human-review recommendation}

Verify that Definition~2.8 is indeed applied to the raw kernel rather than its
even part.  If so, the counterexample is complete.  A useful follow-up is to
reformulate maximal rank invariantly in terms of
$(\rho_t(z)+\rho_t(-z))/2$ and revisit necessity under that added symmetry.
