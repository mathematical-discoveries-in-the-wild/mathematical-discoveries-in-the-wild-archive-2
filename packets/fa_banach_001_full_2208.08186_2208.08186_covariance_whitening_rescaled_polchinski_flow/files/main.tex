\textbf{Status.} Likely valid full answer to the rescaling question in
Section 3 of arXiv:2208.08186.  The answer is matrix-valued, works for every
finite-dimensional positive-definite covariance decomposition, and reduces
to the known Langevin/Ornstein--Uhlenbeck construction for the heat-kernel
decomposition.

\section*{Source question}

Serres considers
\[
 V_t=-\log(\gamma_{C_t}*e^{-V_0}),\qquad
 \nu_t=e^{V_\infty(0)-V_t(x)}\gamma_{C_\infty-C_t},
\]
where $0=C_0\nearrow C_\infty$, and observes that $\nu_t\to\delta_0$.
The paper asks for a rescaling of the Polchinski semigroup having a
non-Dirac ergodic law, so that a transport to $\nu_0$ can be constructed.
It further asks that the rescaling remove the correction generated by the
Gaussian Hessian $(C_\infty-C_t)^{-1}$ in its quantity $\alpha_t'$.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=.92\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Write $C=C_\infty$ and $\Sigma_t=C-C_t$.  We assume $C>0$ and
$\Sigma_t>0$ at every finite time; singular intermediate covariances follow
on their common supports or by approximation.  Put
\[
 Z=\int e^{-V_0}\,d\gamma_C,
 \qquad \rho_0=Z^{-1}e^{-V_0}.
\]
Thus $e^{V_\infty(0)}=Z^{-1}$ in the source normalization.

\section*{Proof intuition}

The part of $\nu_t$ that collapses is completely explicit: its Gaussian
covariance is $\Sigma_t$.  Choose a linear map $M_t$ carrying the fixed
Gaussian $\gamma_C$ to $\gamma_{\Sigma_t}$, and observe the flow in the
blown-up coordinate $y=M_t^{-1}x$.  The convolution increments then become
Gaussian transitions
\[
 y\longmapsto M_s^{-1}M_t y+\text{noise}
\]
which preserve the same $\gamma_C$ at every step.  A single covariance
identity proves both invariance and the two-parameter semigroup law.  In
these coordinates the varying quadratic Gaussian potential is absorbed into
the fixed reference measure; only the interaction density remains in the
transport velocity.  This is why the Gaussian part of $\alpha_t'$ vanishes.

\section*{The exact rescaling}

Choose any differentiable $M_t\in GL(d)$ such that
\begin{equation*}
                 M_t C M_t^{\mathsf T}=\Sigma_t,\qquad M_0=I.       \tag{1}
\end{equation*}
For example,
$M_t=\Sigma_t^{1/2}C^{-1/2}$ works, with no commutativity assumption.
Define
\begin{equation*}
 \mu_t=(M_t^{-1})_\#\nu_t,\qquad
 \rho_t(y)=\frac{d\mu_t}{d\gamma_C}(y)
   =Z^{-1}e^{-V_t(M_ty)}.                                          \tag{2}
\end{equation*}

For $0\le s\le t$, set
\begin{equation*}
 K_{s,t}=M_s^{-1}M_t,\qquad
 N_{s,t}=M_s^{-1}(C_t-C_s)M_s^{-\mathsf T}.                         \tag{3}
\end{equation*}
The key identity is
\begin{equation*}
 N_{s,t}=C-K_{s,t}CK_{s,t}^{\mathsf T}\succeq0.                    \tag{4}
\end{equation*}
Indeed, conjugating
$\Sigma_s-\Sigma_t=C_t-C_s$ by $M_s^{-1}$ and using (1) gives (4).
Let
\begin{equation*}
 Q_{s,t}f(y)=\int f(K_{s,t}y+z)\,d\gamma_{N_{s,t}}(z).              \tag{5}
\end{equation*}

\begin{theorem}[Noncollapsing conjugacy]\label{thm:main}
The operators $Q_{s,t}$ are Gaussian Markov kernels preserving
$\gamma_C$, satisfy the two-parameter evolution law, and obey
\begin{equation*}
                         \rho_t=Q_{s,t}\rho_s.                     \tag{6}
\end{equation*}
Consequently, $(\mu_t)$ is an inhomogeneous Ornstein--Uhlenbeck evolution
with the fixed invariant law $\gamma_C$.  Moreover,
\begin{equation*}
                     \|\mu_t-\gamma_C\|_{\TV}\longrightarrow0.    \tag{7}
\end{equation*}
Thus (2) is a rescaling of the Polchinski flow whose ergodic distribution is
the nondegenerate Gaussian $\gamma_C$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $Y\sim\gamma_C$ and $Z\sim\gamma_{N_{s,t}}$ are independent, then
(4) gives
$K_{s,t}Y+Z\sim\gamma_C$; hence $Q_{s,t}$ preserves $\gamma_C$.
The convolution relation
\[
 e^{-V_t(x)}=\int e^{-V_s(x+\xi)}\,d\gamma_{C_t-C_s}(\xi)
\]
evaluated at $x=M_ty$ and changed by $\xi=M_sz$ gives
\[
 \rho_t(y)
 =\int\rho_s(M_s^{-1}M_ty+z)\,d\gamma_{N_{s,t}}(z),
\]
which is (6).

For $r\le s\le t$,
$K_{r,s}K_{s,t}=K_{r,t}$.  The covariance of the two successive noises is
\[
 N_{r,s}+K_{r,s}N_{s,t}K_{r,s}^{\mathsf T}
 =C-K_{r,t}CK_{r,t}^{\mathsf T}=N_{r,t}.
\]
This proves the evolution law (with the corresponding composition order).

At $s=0$, $K_{0,t}=M_t\to0$ and $N_{0,t}=C_t\to C$, while
\[
 \rho_t(y)=\int\rho_0(M_ty+z)\,d\gamma_{C_t}(z).                   \tag{8}
\]
If $\rho_0$ is bounded and continuous, (8) converges pointwise to
$\int\rho_0\,d\gamma_C=1$ and hence in $L^1(\gamma_C)$ by dominated
convergence.  For general $\rho_0\in L^1(\gamma_C)$, approximate it in
$L^1$ by bounded continuous functions.  Markov invariance implies
\[
 \|Q_{0,t}f\|_{L^1(\gamma_C)}\le\|f\|_{L^1(\gamma_C)},
\]
so the approximation is uniform in $t$.  Therefore
$\rho_t\to1$ in $L^1(\gamma_C)$, which is (7).
\end{proof}

The literal conjugate of the source's Polchinski semigroup is also explicit.
With
\[
 P_{s,t}f(x)=e^{V_t(x)}
   \int f(x+\xi)e^{-V_s(x+\xi)}\,d\gamma_{C_t-C_s}(\xi),
\]
define
\[
 \widetilde P_{s,t}g(y)
 =P_{s,t}(g\circ M_s^{-1})(M_ty).
\]
The same change of variables gives the Doob formula
\begin{equation*}
             \widetilde P_{s,t}g
             =\frac{Q_{s,t}(g\rho_s)}{\rho_t}.                     \tag{9}
\end{equation*}
Thus the result is a rescaling of the full semigroup, not only of its
one-time marginal measures.

\section*{The transport velocity and disappearance of $\alpha$}

Set
\begin{equation*}
 G_t=M_t^{-1}\dot M_t,\qquad
 2D_t=M_t^{-1}\dot C_tM_t^{-\mathsf T},\qquad
 H_t=G_t+D_tC^{-1}.                                                 \tag{10}
\end{equation*}
Differentiating (1) gives
\begin{equation*}
 G_tC+CG_t^{\mathsf T}=-2D_t,\qquad
 H_tC+CH_t^{\mathsf T}=0.                                         \tag{11}
\end{equation*}
The infinitesimal density generator in (5) is
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal A_tf=D_t:\Hess f+\langle G_ty,\nabla f\rangle
 =D_t:\Hess f-\langle D_tC^{-1}y,\nabla f\rangle
   +\langle H_ty,\nabla f\rangle.                                 \tag{12}
\end{equation*}
The first two terms are reversible for $\gamma_C$; the last is a
$\gamma_C$-preserving rotation.  If $p_t$ denotes the Lebesgue density of
$\mu_t$, then (12) is exactly
\begin{equation*}
 \partial_tp_t+\nabla\!\cdot(v_tp_t)=0,
 \qquad
 v_t(y)=-D_t\nabla\log\rho_t(y)-H_ty.                              \tag{13}
\end{equation*}
Indeed, $H_t^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}+C^{-1}H_t=0$ and $\Tr H_t=0$, so
$\nabla\cdot(H_typ_t)=\gamma_C\langle H_ty,\nabla\rho_t\rangle$.

Equation (13) supplies the source's requested Lagrangian construction.
Under the standard local-Lipschitz and nonexplosion assumptions, its integral
curves transport $\mu_s$ to $\mu_t$.  The inverse maps transport $\mu_t$ to
$\mu_0=\nu_0$; whenever these inverses converge under the usual integrable
Hessian bounds, (7) shows that their limit transports $\gamma_C$ to $\nu_0$.

The cancellation mechanism is now visible.  In the original coordinates the
total potential is
\[
 V_t(x)+\tfrac12\langle x,\Sigma_t^{-1}x\rangle,
\]
and $\Sigma_t^{-1}$ is precisely the additional Gaussian Hessian in
$\alpha_t'$.  In (2), however,
\[
 \mu_t=\rho_t\gamma_C,\qquad
 \log\rho_t=\text{constant}-V_t(M_ty).
\]
The quadratic part is fixed in the invariant reference law and is absent
from the score in (13).  Also, the covariance-only term $H_t$ has zero
logarithmic expansion in the $C^{-1}$ metric by (11).  Hence transport
expansion is controlled by the transformed interaction Hessian, with no
additive $\Sigma_t^{-1}$ term.  This removes the Gaussian contribution to
$\alpha_t$ in the precise sense relevant to Lipschitz transport.

\section*{Established heat-kernel specialization}

Let $C=A^{-1}$ and
\[
 C_t=A^{-1}(I-e^{-tA}),\qquad \Sigma_t=A^{-1}e^{-tA}.
\]
Then $M_t=e^{-tA/2}$, $D_t=I/2$, and $H_t=0$.  Formula (2) becomes
\begin{equation*}
 \rho_t(y)=Z^{-1}e^{-V_t(e^{-tA/2}y)}
           =U_{t/2}\rho_0(y),                                    \tag{14}
\end{equation*}
where $U$ is the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck semigroup with invariant law
$\gamma_{A^{-1}}$, and (13) becomes
$v_t=-\frac12\nabla\log\rho_t$.  This is exactly the Langevin transport
flow analyzed by Shenfeld; under the multiscale Bakry--Emery bound his
Theorem 2.2 gives a Lipschitz limiting transport.  Thus the arbitrary-path
formula above agrees with the known construction in the standard schedule.

\section*{Why a scalar rescaling cannot be the general answer}

Take $V_0=0$ and
$C=\operatorname{diag}(c_1,\ldots,c_d)$ in the heat-kernel schedule.  The
covariance eigenvalues of $\nu_t$ are
\[
                         c_i e^{-t/c_i}.
\]
After any scalar blow-up $x\mapsto r_tx$, the ratio of the $i$th and $j$th
eigenvalues remains
\[
 \frac{c_i}{c_j}\exp\{-t(1/c_i-1/c_j)\}.
\]
If $c_i\ne c_j$, this tends to $0$ or $+\infty$.  No scalar rescaling---in
particular no scalar chosen only from the largest eigenvalue $\alpha_t'$---
can yield a finite, full-dimensional terminal law.  The matrix whitening
(1)--(2) is essential.

\section*{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The identities (1), (4), the covariance telescoping law, (10)--(12), and the
heat-kernel specialization were independently checked on random noncommuting
positive-definite matrices by the included script.  The proof itself is
algebraic and does not rely on the computation.

Bounded searches used the exact source title, ``rescaling of the Polchinski
semigroup,'' ``blow up the Dirac mass,'' arbitrary covariance decomposition,
and Ornstein--Uhlenbeck/transport variants.  Shenfeld's
arXiv:2205.01642v4 explicitly contains the heat-kernel/Langevin special case.
Faris gives the classical link between Gaussian convolution plus scaling and
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck semigroups.  No source found stated the arbitrary
two-parameter covariance-whitening conjugacy (3)--(9).  Novelty confidence is
moderate: the core covariance identity is elementary and may be folklore.

The result solves the rescaling and supplies its transport velocity.
Quantitative Lipschitz constants for a completely arbitrary noncommuting
covariance schedule still require bounds on the transformed interaction
Hessian.  This is an estimate for the resulting transport, not a missing
piece of the rescaling.
