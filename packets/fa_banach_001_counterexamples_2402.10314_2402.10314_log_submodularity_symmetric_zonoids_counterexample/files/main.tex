\section{Source conjecture}

The current version of \cite{FLMZ} asks for which measures $\mu$ and classes
of convex sets one has
\begin{equation}
 \mu(A)\mu(A+B+C)\leq \mu(A+B)\mu(A+C),
 \label{eq:source}
\end{equation}
and conjectures that \eqref{eq:source} holds whenever $A,B,C$ are symmetric
zonoids and $\mu$ is any log-concave measure.  This appears on page 27,
immediately after equation (44).

\section{Proof intuition}

The conjecture allows the measure to be highly anisotropic.  Concentrate a
log-concave measure in a thin parallelogram around the diagonal $x=y$.
Horizontal enlargement alone and vertical enlargement alone barely increase
the amount of diagonal mass captured by a centered square.  Applying both
enlargements captures a much longer diagonal interval.  Centered rectangles
are already zonotopes, and a width-$1/10$ parallelogram makes this mechanism
an exact full-dimensional area computation rather than a limiting argument.

\section{Counterexample}

\begin{theorem}
The conjecture following equation \emph{(44)} of \cite{FLMZ} is false as
stated.  The failure occurs in dimension two with an absolutely continuous
log-concave probability measure and full-dimensional symmetric zonotopes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $\delta=1/10$ and consider the invertible linear map
\[
 T(u,v)=(u+v,u-v).
\]
Let
\[
 P=T\bigl([-4,4]\times[-\delta,\delta]\bigr)
\]
and let $\mu$ be normalized two-dimensional Lebesgue measure on $P$:
\[
 \mu(E)=\frac{\area(E\cap P)}{\area(P)}.
\]
The density of $\mu$ is a positive constant times the indicator of the convex
body $P$, hence $\mu$ is a log-concave probability measure.  Equivalently,
this follows directly from the Brunn--Minkowski inequality applied to
$P\cap E$ and $P\cap F$.

Define the centered rectangles
\[
 \begin{split}
 A&=[-1,1]\times[-1,1],\\
 B&=[-1,1]\times[-\delta,\delta],\\
 C&=[-\delta,\delta]\times[-1,1].
 \end{split}
\]
They have nonempty interior and are origin-symmetric zonotopes, hence
symmetric zonoids.

We compute their measures and those of their Minkowski sums.  Write
$R(s,t)=[-s,s]\times[-t,t]$.  In $(u,v)$ coordinates, membership of $T(u,v)$
in $R(s,t)$ is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 |u+v|\leq s,\qquad |u-v|\leq t.
 \label{eq:constraints}
\end{equation}
The Jacobian magnitude of $T$ is $2$, and it cancels from all normalized-area
ratios.  The support rectangle in $(u,v)$ coordinates has area $16\delta$.

For $R(q,q)$, the two inequalities in \eqref{eq:constraints} are equivalent
to $|u|+|v|\leq q$.  For the values of $q$ used below, its preimage inside the
support has area
\[
 \int_{-\delta}^{\delta}2(q-|v|)\,dv
   =4q\delta-2\delta^2.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \mu(R(q,q))=\frac{4q\delta-2\delta^2}{16\delta}
 =\frac q4-\frac\delta8.
 \label{eq:square-prob}
\end{equation}

Next, for $R(2,11/10)$ the constraint $|u+v|\leq2$ is redundant: indeed,
$|u-v|\leq11/10$ and $|v|\leq1/10$ imply
$|u+v|\leq13/10$.  Thus its preimage has area
$2\delta\cdot 2(11/10)$, giving
\begin{equation}
 \mu(R(2,11/10))=\frac{11}{40}.
 \label{eq:rectangle-prob}
\end{equation}
The same value holds for $R(11/10,2)$ by symmetry.

The relevant Minkowski sums are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 D & D\text{ as a centered rectangle} & \mu(D)\\ \hline
 A & R(1,1) & 19/80\\
 A+B & R(2,11/10) & 11/40\\
 A+C & R(11/10,2) & 11/40\\
 A+B+C & R(21/10,21/10) & 41/80.
 \end{array}
\]
Here the square entries follow from \eqref{eq:square-prob} and the other two
from \eqref{eq:rectangle-prob}.  Therefore
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\mu(A)\mu(A+B+C)-\mu(A+B)\mu(A+C)\\
 &\qquad=\frac{19}{80}\frac{41}{80}
          -\left(\frac{11}{40}\right)^2
 =\frac{779-484}{6400}
 =\frac{59}{1280}>0.
 \end{split}
\]
This is the strict reverse of \eqref{eq:source}, and proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Robustness and limitations}

The measure is already full-dimensional and absolutely continuous.  If a
smooth, everywhere-positive density is desired, convolve $\mu$ with a
centered nondegenerate Gaussian of variance $\varepsilon^2$.  Convolution
preserves log-concavity \cite{Gardner}.  As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the four probabilities
in the proof converge to their displayed values because rectangle boundaries
have $\mu$-measure zero.  The defect is strictly positive, so it remains
positive for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.

The argument refutes the quantifier ``any log-concave measure.''  It makes no
claim about variants restricted to radial measures, the standard Gaussian,
or Lebesgue measure.  Those narrower questions may remain open.
