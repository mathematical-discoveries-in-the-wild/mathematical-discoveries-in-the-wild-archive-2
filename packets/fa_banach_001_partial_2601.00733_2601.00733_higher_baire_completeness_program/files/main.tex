\section{Source problem}

Gabriyelyan, Osipov, and Reznichenko characterize completeness and
reflexivity-type properties of the pointwise space $B_1(X)$ and then ask for
the corresponding program for every countable $\alpha>1$ and for
$B(X)=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_1}B_\alpha(X)$
\cite[Problem~3.10, PDF p.~11]{GOR2026}.  The exact problem is reproduced
below.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{Problem 3.10 in arXiv:2601.00733v2, PDF page 11.}
\end{figure}

All function spaces below carry the topology of pointwise convergence,
inherited from $\R^X$.  We use the source's definitions of complete,
quasi-complete, (semi-)reflexive, (semi-)Montel, sequentially complete, and
locally complete.  In particular, local completeness means that the closed
absolutely convex hull of every null sequence is compact.

For a nonzero countable ordinal $\alpha$, $B_\alpha(X)$ is the usual Baire
class: $B_0(X)=C(X)$ and $B_\alpha(X)$ consists of pointwise limits of
sequences from $\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}B_\xi(X)$.  For a metrizable $X$, write
$\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_\xi(X)$ and $\boldsymbol\Pi^0_\xi(X)$ for the additive
and multiplicative Borel classes.

\section{Proof intuition}

There are two different mechanisms.

First, quasi-completeness is so strong in a pointwise function space that it
has nothing specifically to do with Baire rank.  Continuous functions can
interpolate arbitrary zero--one data on each finite subset of a Tychonoff
space.  The resulting net is pointwise Cauchy and stays in the bounded unit
box.  If the ambient pointwise subspace is quasi-complete, the net must
converge inside it, so every characteristic function belongs to the space.
Bounded finite-range approximations then put every real function in the
space.  Thus any quasi-complete pointwise vector space containing $C(X)$ is
already the whole product $\R^X$.

Second, on a metrizable domain the Lebesgue--Hausdorff theorem identifies
Baire rank $\alpha$ with Borel rank $\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_{\alpha+1}$.
If $A$ has additive rank $\alpha+1$, it can be partitioned into disjoint
ambiguous-rank-$\alpha+1$ pieces $A_n$.  The functions
$2^{n+1}\mathbf 1_{A_n}$ form a null sequence.  Local completeness makes
its closed disk compact, while the disk contains partial sums converging to
$\mathbf 1_A$.  Hence $\mathbf 1_A$ already has Baire rank $\alpha$, forcing
the additive and multiplicative Borel classes to coincide.  That equality
collapses the next Baire class and gives sequential completeness.

\section{The abstract quasi-completion theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Pointwise quasi-completion]\label{thm:abstract}
Let $X$ be a Tychonoff space and let $E$ be a vector subspace of $\R^X$,
endowed with the pointwise topology, such that $C(X)\subseteq E$.  Then
\[
                 E\text{ is quasi-complete}
                 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E=\R^X.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Only the forward implication requires proof.  Fix an arbitrary set
$A\subseteq X$.  Direct the finite subsets $F\subseteq X$ by inclusion.
For $a\in F\cap A$, complete regularity gives a function
$v_{a,F}\in C(X,[0,1])$ such that
\[
 v_{a,F}(a)=1,
 \qquad v_{a,F}(F\setminus\{a\})=\{0\}.
\]
Set $u_F=0$ if $F\cap A=\varnothing$, and otherwise set
$u_F=\max_{a\in F\cap A}v_{a,F}$.  Then
\[
 u_F\in C(X,[0,1]),
 \qquad u_F|_F=\mathbf 1_A|_F.
\]

The box
\[
 K=E\cap[0,1]^X
\]
is closed and bounded in $E$, hence complete by quasi-completeness.  The net
$(u_F)$ lies in $K$ and is pointwise Cauchy: once $F$ contains a fixed
$x\in X$, every later $u_G(x)$ equals $\mathbf 1_A(x)$.  Therefore $(u_F)$
converges in $K$.  Its pointwise limit is uniquely $\mathbf 1_A$, so
$\mathbf 1_A\in E$.  Since $A$ was arbitrary, $E$ contains every finite-range
real function on $X$.

Now fix $f\in\R^X$.  Define the finite-range function
\[
 s_n(x)=2^{-n}\max\bigl\{-n2^n,
           \min\{n2^n,\lfloor 2^n f(x)\rfloor\}\bigr\}.
\]
Then $s_n\in E$, $s_n(x)\to f(x)$ for every $x$, and
$|s_n(x)|\leq |f(x)|+1$.  Thus $(s_n)$ is a pointwise Cauchy sequence whose
range is bounded in $E$.  Its closure in $E$ is a closed bounded set and is
therefore complete.  Hence $(s_n)$ converges in $E$, and uniqueness of the
coordinatewise limit gives $f\in E$.  Thus $E=\R^X$.

The product $\R^X$ is complete, so the converse follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[All strong properties]\label{cor:strong}
Let $X$ be Tychonoff and let $E$ be either $B_\alpha(X)$, for a nonzero
countable ordinal $\alpha$, or $B(X)$.  The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $E$ is Montel;
\item $E$ is semi-Montel;
\item $E$ is reflexive;
\item $E$ is semi-reflexive;
\item $E$ is complete;
\item $E$ is quasi-complete;
\item $E=\R^X$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The source proves that $B_1(X)$ is barrelled
\cite[Theorem~1.1(i)]{GOR2026}.  A locally convex space containing a dense
barrelled subspace, with the inherited topology on that subspace, is
barrelled: if $D$ is a closed barrel, $D$ intersects the dense subspace in a
neighborhood; shrinking that neighborhood in the ambient space and using
density and closedness puts an ambient neighborhood inside $D$.  Hence every
$E$ in the statement is barrelled.

Finite interpolation shows that $C(X)$, and therefore $E$, is pointwise dense
in $\R^X$.  Every continuous linear functional on $E$ depends on finitely many
coordinates, so the pointwise topology is precisely the weak topology of
$E$.  The standard theorem for barrelled locally convex spaces carrying their
weak topology makes semi-Montel, reflexive, semi-reflexive, and
quasi-complete equivalent; this is the same result used in the source
\cite[proof of Theorem~3.3]{GOR2026} and comes from
\cite[Proposition~11.4.2]{Jarchow}.  Completeness implies
quasi-completeness, and Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract} says that
quasi-completeness forces $E=\R^X$.  Finally, $\R^X$ has every listed
property.
\end{proof}

\section{Sequential completeness at an arbitrary rank}

\begin{theorem}[Successor-rank criterion]\label{thm:sequential}
For every Tychonoff space $X$ and every nonzero countable ordinal $\alpha$,
\[
 B_\alpha(X)\text{ is sequentially complete}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 B_\alpha(X)=B_{\alpha+1}(X).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because the Baire hierarchy is increasing, $B_{\alpha+1}(X)$ is exactly the
set of pointwise limits of sequences from $B_\alpha(X)$.  Every pointwise
Cauchy sequence in $B_\alpha(X)$ has a coordinatewise limit in $\R^X$, and
that limit belongs to $B_{\alpha+1}(X)$.  Thus equality of the two classes is
equivalent to retaining every Cauchy-sequence limit in $B_\alpha(X)$.
\end{proof}

\section{The complete metrizable program}

We use the classical Lebesgue--Hausdorff theorem: if $X$ is metrizable and
$0<\alpha<\omega_1$, then a real function $f$ is of Baire class $\alpha$ if
and only if $f^{-1}(U)\in\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_{\alpha+1}(X)$ for every open
$U\subseteq\R$; in particular,
\[
 \mathbf 1_A\in B_\alpha(X)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A\in\boldsymbol\Delta^0_{\alpha+1}(X)
 :=\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_{\alpha+1}(X)
   \cap\boldsymbol\Pi^0_{\alpha+1}(X).
\]
See, for example, the classical treatments in
\cite{Kuratowski,Kechris}.

\begin{theorem}[Metrizable characterization]\label{thm:metric}
Let $X$ be metrizable and let $0<\alpha<\omega_1$.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{(A) Strong properties.}
Each of the seven equivalent conditions in Corollary~\ref{cor:strong} for
$E=B_\alpha(X)$ is equivalent to
\[
        \boldsymbol\Sigma^0_{\alpha+1}(X)=\cP(X),
\]
that is, every subset of $X$ has additive Borel rank at most $\alpha+1$.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{(B) Sequential and local completeness.}
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B_\alpha(X)$ is sequentially complete;
\item $B_\alpha(X)$ is locally complete;
\item $B_\alpha(X)=B_{\alpha+1}(X)$;
\item
$\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_{\alpha+1}(X)
 =\boldsymbol\Pi^0_{\alpha+1}(X)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For (A), Corollary~\ref{cor:strong} reduces everything to
$B_\alpha(X)=\R^X$.  If that equality holds, every characteristic function
has Baire class $\alpha$, so every subset lies in
$\boldsymbol\Delta^0_{\alpha+1}$.  Conversely, if every subset lies in
$\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_{\alpha+1}$, then the inverse image of every open set
under every $f:X\to\R$ lies in that class.  The Lebesgue--Hausdorff theorem
gives $f\in B_\alpha(X)$.

For (B), Theorem~\ref{thm:sequential} gives (1)$\Leftrightarrow$(3), and
sequential completeness implies local completeness in every locally convex
space.

Assume (2), and fix
$A\in\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_{\alpha+1}(X)$.  Write
$A=\bigcup_n C_n$ with $C_n\in\boldsymbol\Pi^0_\alpha(X)$, and put
$D_n=\bigcup_{k\leq n}C_k$.  Finite unions preserve
$\boldsymbol\Pi^0_\alpha$.  The sets
\[
 A_0=D_0,
 \qquad A_n=D_n\setminus D_{n-1}\quad(n\geq1)
\]
are pairwise disjoint, cover $A$, and belong to
$\boldsymbol\Delta^0_{\alpha+1}(X)$.  Therefore
$\mathbf 1_{A_n}\in B_\alpha(X)$.

Set $g_n=2^{n+1}\mathbf 1_{A_n}$.  Disjointness makes $(g_n)$ a null sequence
in the pointwise topology.  By local completeness, its closed absolutely
convex hull $K$ is compact.  For every $N$,
\[
 F_N=\sum_{n\leq N}2^{-n-1}g_n
     =\mathbf 1_{\bigcup_{n\leq N}A_n}
     \in K,
\]
because the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients is at most one.
The sequence $(F_N)$ converges pointwise to $\mathbf 1_A$.  Compactness gives
a cluster point in $K$, and its coordinates force that cluster point to be
$\mathbf 1_A$.  Hence $\mathbf 1_A\in B_\alpha(X)$, so
$A\in\boldsymbol\Pi^0_{\alpha+1}(X)$.  Taking complements yields
\[
 \boldsymbol\Sigma^0_{\alpha+1}(X)
 =\boldsymbol\Pi^0_{\alpha+1}(X),
\]
which proves (2)$\Rightarrow$(4).

Finally, (4) collapses the next additive class:
\[
 \boldsymbol\Sigma^0_{\alpha+2}(X)
 =\Bigl(\boldsymbol\Pi^0_{\alpha+1}(X)\Bigr)_\sigma
 =\Bigl(\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_{\alpha+1}(X)\Bigr)_\sigma
 =\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_{\alpha+1}(X).
\]
The Lebesgue--Hausdorff theorem therefore gives
$B_{\alpha+1}(X)\subseteq B_\alpha(X)$; the reverse inclusion is automatic.
Thus (4)$\Rightarrow$(3), completing the cycle.
\end{proof}

For $\alpha=1$, part (B) recovers the source's $\sigma$-space criterion in
the metrizable setting.  For higher $\alpha$, it identifies the exact Borel
level whose collapse is measured by local and sequential completeness.

\section{The full Baire union}

\begin{theorem}[The $B(X)$ program]\label{thm:union}
Let $X$ be Tychonoff.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B(X)$ is always sequentially complete and locally complete.
\item Each strong property in Corollary~\ref{cor:strong} is equivalent to
      $B(X)=\R^X$.
\item The equality $B(X)=\R^X$ holds if and only if
      $\mathbf 1_A\in B(X)$ for every $A\subseteq X$.
\end{enumerate}
If $X$ is metrizable, the last condition is equivalent to every subset of
$X$ being Borel.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The source proves sequential completeness of $B(X)$
\cite[Proposition~3.1]{GOR2026}; the general implication from sequential to
local completeness gives the rest of (1).  Part (2) is
Corollary~\ref{cor:strong}.

For the nontrivial direction of (3), suppose every characteristic function
belongs to $B(X)$.  Every finite-range real function then belongs to $B(X)$.
The truncation--quantization sequence $(s_n)$ from the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract} consists of such functions and converges pointwise
to an arbitrary $f:X\to\R$.  Sequential completeness puts $f$ in $B(X)$.
The converse is immediate.  On a metrizable space, the
Lebesgue--Hausdorff theorem identifies characteristic Baire functions with
Borel sets of some countable rank.
\end{proof}

\section{Upgrade audit, limitations, and novelty check}

Eight focused routes were audited.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Finite interpolation in the bounded unit box produced the abstract
      quasi-completion theorem.
\item Successor-rank closure produced the exact sequential criterion.
\item A direct rescaling of telescoping differences failed: pointwise
      convergence supplies no common rate over an arbitrary $X$.
\item Disjoint Borel-rank decomposition repaired that failure for metrizable
      spaces and proved local $\Rightarrow$ sequential completeness.
\item Lebesgue--Hausdorff converted both functional conclusions into exact
      Borel-hierarchy criteria.
\item Sequential completeness of $B(X)$ completed the full-union program.
\item The same disjointification works on broader domains whenever the
      appropriate generalized Lebesgue--Hausdorff identification is known;
      this was not promoted as an unconditional arbitrary-space theorem.
\item A universal nonmetrizable upgrade was tested through functional
      additive/multiplicative classes.  The missing converse from functional
      measurability to Baire class on arbitrary domains is a genuine
      obstruction, so the packet stops at the stated scope.
\end{enumerate}

Thus the remaining part of Problem~3.10 is an intrinsic characterization of
local completeness of a fixed $B_\alpha(X)$ for arbitrary nonmetrizable
Tychonoff $X$.  The packet also does not address the source's other open
problems about $Q_f$-spaces or special sets of reals.

A bounded novelty search was performed on 11 August 2026.  It checked the
source arXiv page and published-title result; exact searches for combinations
of ``$B_\alpha(X)$'', ``quasi-complete'', ``locally complete'',
``$B_\alpha(X)=B_{\alpha+1}(X)$'', and higher Baire-class pointwise spaces;
and arXiv searches for the abstract pointwise quasi-completion statement.
The searches returned the source paper, its $B_1$ results, and general papers
on Baire classification, but no statement of Theorems~\ref{thm:abstract} or
\ref{thm:metric}.  This supports only bounded novelty confidence.

The most important human checks are: (i) completeness of the bounded box in
Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract}; (ii) the barrelled weak-space transfer in
Corollary~\ref{cor:strong}; and (iii) the transfinite Borel indexing in
Theorem~\ref{thm:metric}.
