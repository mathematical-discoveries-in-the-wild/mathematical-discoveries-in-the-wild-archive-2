\section{Source question}

The source is M.~Arsenovi\'c, M.~Krsti\'c, M.~Milovi\'c, and
S.~Milo\v sevi\'c, \emph{Strongly Integrable Operator-Valued Functions,
Generated Vector Measures and Compactness of Integrals}, arXiv:2605.12454v1
(2026) \cite{source}.  Its Theorem~4.1 assumes that $X^*$ contains no copy of
$c_0$ and that $A\in L_s^1(\Omega,\mu,\B(X))$ is a norm-strongly measurable
family of mutually commuting operators, and proves
\[
 r\!\left(\int_\Omega A_t\dd(t)\right)
 \leq \int_\Omega r(A_t)\dd(t).
\]
On PDF page 36, the authors ask whether norm-strong measurability can be
replaced by a weaker assumption.

Here $A\in L_s^1$ already says that every orbit $t\mapsto A_tx$ is strongly
measurable and Bochner integrable.  The extra assumption in question is
strong measurability of $t\mapsto A_t$ in the norm of $\B(X)$.

\section{Proof intuition}

Pettis integration is precisely strong enough to commute with every bounded
linear scalarization.  Put all values $A_t$ into the closed unital Banach
algebra they generate.  Commutativity lets characters calculate spectral
radius, while Pettis integration lets each character pass through the
integral.  The pointwise character value is bounded by the pointwise spectral
radius.  A possible concern---that spectra can grow in a subalgebra---vanishes
for radii: the ambient and subalgebra radii use the same norms of the same
powers.

The paper's original hypotheses imply Pettis integrability.  Indeed, its key
theorem makes the strong integral a norm-countably-additive operator measure.
On countably many bounded finite-measure pieces the density is Bochner
integrable; the finite variation of every scalarization then glues these
pieces into a global Pettis integral.

\section{The general theorem}

We use the standard definition: $f:\Omega\to\A$ is Pettis integrable if it is
weakly measurable, every $\varphi\circ f$ with $\varphi\in\A^*$ is integrable,
and for every measurable $E$ there is an element $I_E\in\A$ satisfying
$\varphi(I_E)=\int_E\varphi(f(t))\dd(t)$ for all $\varphi\in\A^*$.

\begin{theorem}[Pettis commuting spectral-radius inequality]\label{thm:main}
Let $\A$ be a complex unital Banach algebra and let
$f:\Omega\to\A$ be Pettis integrable.  Suppose that, after deletion of one
null set, the elements $f(t)$ commute pairwise.  If $t\mapsto r_\A(f(t))$ is
measurable, then
\[
 r_\A\!\left(\mathop{\mathrm{P}}\!\int_\Omega f(t)\dd(t)\right)
 \leq \int_\Omega r_\A(f(t))\dd(t),
\]
with the assertion understood trivially when the right side is infinite.
Without measurability, the same inequality holds with the upper integral.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Delete the common null set and let $D$ be the closed unital subalgebra of
$\A$ generated by $\{f(t):t\in\Omega\}$.  It is commutative.  If
$I=\mathrm{P}\!\int_\Omega f\dd$, then $I\in D$: for the quotient map
$q:\A\to\A/D$ and every $\psi\in(\A/D)^*$,
\[
 \psi(qI)=\int_\Omega \psi(qf(t))\dd(t)=0,
\]
so $qI=0$.

Let $\chi$ be a character of $D$.  Extend it by Hahn--Banach to
$\widetilde\chi\in\A^*$.  Pettis integrability gives
\[
 \chi(I)=\widetilde\chi(I)
 =\int_\Omega \widetilde\chi(f(t))\dd(t)
 =\int_\Omega \chi(f(t))\dd(t).
\]
For every $a\in D$, the spectral-radius formula and the inherited norm give
\[
 r_D(a)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|a^n\|_D^{1/n}
       =\lim_{n\to\infty}\|a^n\|_\A^{1/n}=r_\A(a).
\]
Consequently
$|\chi(f(t))|\leq r_D(f(t))=r_\A(f(t))$.  Hence
\[
 |\chi(I)|\leq\int_\Omega r_\A(f(t))\dd(t).
\]
Taking the supremum over the character space of the commutative unital
algebra $D$ and using the Gelfand spectral-radius formula yields
\[
 r_\A(I)=r_D(I)=\sup_{\chi}|\chi(I)|
 \leq\int_\Omega r_\A(f(t))\dd(t),
\]
as required.
\end{proof}

\section{Application to the source question}

\begin{lemma}[the original assumption implies Pettis integrability]
Let $X$ be a complex Banach space such that $X^*$ contains no isomorphic copy
of $c_0$.  Suppose $A\in L_s^1(\Omega,\mu,\B(X))$ and
$A:\Omega\to\B(X)$ is norm-strongly measurable.  Then $A$ is Pettis
integrable as a $\B(X)$-valued function, and its Pettis integral over every
measurable $E$ is its strong integral $\nu_A(E)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~3.3 of the source paper \cite{source},
\[
 \nu_A(E)x=\int_E A_tx\dd(t),\qquad E\in\Sigma,
\]
defines a norm-countably-additive $\B(X)$-valued measure.  Norm-strong
measurability gives a sigma-finite support.  Partition that support into
countably many measurable sets $(D_j)$, each of finite measure and with
$\ess\sup_{D_j}\|A_t\|<\infty$.  On each $D_j$, $A$ is Bochner integrable;
therefore, for $E\subseteq D_j$,
\[
 \nu_A(E)=\mathop{\mathrm{B}}\!\int_E A_t\dd(t).
\]

Fix $\Phi\in\B(X)^*$ and let $\lambda=\Phi\circ\nu_A$.  This is a finite
countably additive complex measure, hence its total variation satisfies
$|\lambda|(\Omega)<\infty$.  On each $D_j$ its density is $\Phi(A_t)$, so
\[
 \int_\Omega |\Phi(A_t)|\dd(t)
 =\sum_j\int_{D_j}|\Phi(A_t)|\dd(t)
 =\sum_j|\lambda|(D_j)=|\lambda|(\Omega)<\infty.
\]
For arbitrary $E$, absolute convergence and countable additivity now give
\[
 \int_E\Phi(A_t)\dd(t)
 =\sum_j\lambda(E\cap D_j)=\lambda(E)=\Phi(\nu_A(E)).
\]
Thus all scalarizations are integrable and $\nu_A(E)$ represents them, which
is exactly Pettis integrability.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[affirmative answer to the literal question]\label{cor:source}
In Theorem~4.1 of the source paper, norm-strong measurability may be replaced
by the weaker assumption that $A$ is Pettis integrable as a $\B(X)$-valued
function, provided $t\mapsto r(A_t)$ is measurable.  Under this replacement
the hypothesis $X^*\not\supset c_0$ is unnecessary.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main} to $\A=\B(X)$.  The preceding lemma shows that,
within the original setting, the old measurability hypothesis implies the new
one.
\end{proof}

\section{Strictness of the replacement}

\begin{proposition}
There is a Pettis-integrable, commuting family in
$L_s^1([0,1],\B(H))$ that is not norm-strongly measurable, where $H$ is a
Hilbert space and $H^*$ contains no copy of $c_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $H=\ell_2([0,1])$, let $e_t$ be its canonical unit vectors, and let $P_t$
be projection onto $\C e_t$.  Put $A_t=I+P_t$.  Every $x\in H$ has countable
support, hence $P_tx=0$ for almost every $t$.  Thus $A\in L_s^1$ and its
strong integral is $I$.

For $\Phi\in\B(H)^*$, set $a_t=\Phi(P_t)$.  Given finite $F\subset[0,1]$,
choose unimodular $c_t$ so that $c_ta_t=|a_t|$.  Orthogonality gives
\[
 \sum_{t\in F}|a_t|
 =\Phi\!\left(\sum_{t\in F}c_tP_t\right)\leq\|\Phi\|,
 \qquad
 \left\|\sum_{t\in F}c_tP_t\right\|=1.
\]
Therefore $(a_t)_{t\in[0,1]}\in\ell_1([0,1])$ and has countable support.
It follows that $t\mapsto\Phi(A_t)=\Phi(I)+a_t$ is measurable, integrable,
and has integral $\Phi(I)$.  Hence $A$ is Pettis integrable with integral $I$.

For $s\neq t$, however, $\|A_s-A_t\|=\|P_s-P_t\|=1$.  On every conull set
the range is therefore an uncountable $1$-separated set.  It is not essentially
separably valued, so the Pettis measurability theorem rules out norm-strong
measurability.  Finally, the values commute and $r(A_t)=2$, so the example is
also inside the spectral-radius setting.  (As an $L_s^1$ equivalence class it
is pointwise equivalent to the constant family $I$; the strictness here is at
the representative level at which the source states the extra hypothesis.)
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, limitations, and novelty}

The proof uses only Pettis scalarization, Hahn--Banach extension, the Gelfand
formula in a commutative Banach algebra, and Theorem~3.3 of the source paper.
The key ambient/subalgebra issue was checked explicitly through the norm power
formula.  No numerical computation is involved.

This packet does \emph{not} prove the inequality for every bare pointwise
$L_s^1$ family.  Without Pettis compatibility, a character of the generated
operator algebra need not be strong-operator continuous, and neither
measurability of $\chi(A_t)$ nor the identity
$\chi(\int A)=\int\chi(A_t)$ follows.  That sharper endpoint remains open.

The local run indexes and a bounded web/arXiv search on 12 August 2026 used
the exact title and combinations of \emph{Pettis integral}, \emph{commuting
family}, and \emph{spectral radius}.  No later answer or exact formulation was
found.  Since the source is recent and the character argument is elementary,
the novelty confidence is moderate; the correctness confidence is high,
subject to human review of the intended scope of the word ``weaker.''
