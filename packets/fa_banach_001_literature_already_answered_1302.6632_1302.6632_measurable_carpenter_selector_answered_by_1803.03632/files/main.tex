\section{The two source questions}

Let $X$ be a finite or $\sigma$-finite measure space, let $I$ be countable,
and let $\sigma:X\times I\to[0,1]$ be measurable.  For each $\xi$, the source
forms the Kadison sums
\[
 a(\xi)=\sum_{\sigma(\xi,i)<1/2}\sigma(\xi,i),\qquad
 b(\xi)=\sum_{\sigma(\xi,i)\ge1/2}(1-\sigma(\xi,i)).
\]
Assume pointwise almost everywhere that either one sum is infinite, or both
are finite and $a-b\in\mathbb Z$.

Problem 2 asks whether there is a measurable range function $J(\xi)$ whose
orthogonal projection has diagonal $(\sigma(\xi,i))_{i\in I}$.  Problem 1 is
the shift-invariant specialization: do these pointwise conditions exactly
characterize spectral functions of shift-invariant subspaces of
$L^2(\mathbb R^d)$?  The source explicitly says the measurable-selector issue
is the missing step.  Its complete question page is reproduced next.

\section{The exact later theorem}

The abstract of Bownik--Szyszkowski's arXiv:1803.03632 says explicitly that
the paper solves the problem posed by Jasper and Bownik.  Its title is
\emph{Measurable selector in Kadison's carpenter's theorem}.  Theorem 1.2
states:

\begin{quote}
For any measurable space $X$ and measurable functions
$f_i:X\to[0,1]$, if Kadison's divergence-or-integer alternative holds at
every point, there is a weak-operator measurable projection
$P:X\to\mathcal B(H)$ with diagonal $(f_i)$.
\end{quote}

This is exactly the requested selector after identifying the countable set
$I$ with $\mathbb N$ and discarding a null exceptional set (or assigning
arbitrary admissible data there).  The later result is slightly stronger:
it imposes no finite or $\sigma$-finite measure hypothesis on $X$.

The two papers use opposite conventions for entries equal to $1/2$.  This is
harmless.  Moving one such entry from the upper sum to the lower sum changes
$a-b$ by $1$, preserving integrality; infinitely many such entries force the
divergent alternative.

The complete theorem page follows.

\section{The shift-invariant consequence}

Theorem 4.1 of arXiv:1803.03632 applies the measurable selector to the fibers
$(\sigma(\xi+k))_{k\in\mathbb Z^d}$.  It proves the exact equivalence:

\begin{enumerate}
\item $\sigma$ is almost everywhere the spectral function of a
shift-invariant subspace of $L^2(\mathbb R^d)$;
\item Kadison's divergence-or-integer condition holds almost everywhere.
\end{enumerate}

The proof explains the precise bridge: Theorem 1.2 produces the measurable
field of projections, hence a measurable range function, and Helson's theorem
then produces the shift-invariant space.  Thus both source problems are
answered affirmatively, not merely a special case.

\section{Conclusion and audit}

The match is exact in hypotheses, object constructed, and application:
Problem 2 is Theorem 1.2, and Problem 1 is Theorem 4.1 of the later paper.
No independent proof or counterexample should be promoted for this target.

\paragraph{Source audit.}
The official arXiv PDFs of 1302.6632 and 1803.03632 were inspected.  Bounded
exact-title and exact-phrase searches recovered the later paper as the direct
resolution.  The target was absent from the run's cheap solution indexes
before this packet.
