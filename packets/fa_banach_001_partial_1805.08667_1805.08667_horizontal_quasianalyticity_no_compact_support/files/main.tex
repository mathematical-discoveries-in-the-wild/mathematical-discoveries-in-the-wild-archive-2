\section{Source question and scope}

Fischer--Ruzhansky--Taranto introduce three subelliptic Gevrey classes from a
globally defined H\"ormander family \(\X=\{X_1,\ldots,X_r\}\): bounds on all
horizontal words in \(L^\infty\), the analogous bounds in \(L^2\), and bounds
on iterates of the sub-Laplacian
\(\mathcal L=-\sum_jX_j^2\) \cite{source}.  Their Question 3 asks how these
spaces relate to elliptic Gevrey spaces and, at order \(s=1\), whether they too
can contain no nonzero compactly supported functions.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{Question 3 in the source paper (PDF p.~4).}
\end{figure}

The theorem below completely answers the compact-support clause for the two
horizontal definitions.  The source's own equivalence theorem then answers it
for the sub-Laplacian definition on \(\mathbb H_n\).  We isolate the still-open
general spectral scope in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\section{Horizontal quasianalyticity}

For a word \(I=(i_1,\ldots,i_m)\), write
\(X_I=X_{i_1}\cdots X_{i_m}\).  Following the source, if the index \(j\)
occurs \(\alpha_j\) times, put \(I!=\prod_j\alpha_j!\).  Thus the repeated word
\((j,\ldots,j)\) has \(I!=m!\).

\subsection*{Proof intuition}

The full noncommutative word structure is not needed for uniqueness.  Repeating
just one generator shows that the restriction of the function to each
\(X_j\)-integral curve has ordinary analytic derivative bounds.  An open zero
set therefore travels intact along every local flow.  Finite compositions of
those flows generate the H\"ormander orbit, and bracket generation makes that
orbit all of a connected manifold.  The argument uses neither analytic
coefficients nor estimates for higher-order Riesz transforms.

\begin{theorem}[Open-set uniqueness]\label{thm:unique}
Let \(M\) be a connected smooth manifold and
\(\X=\{X_1,\ldots,X_r\}\) a finite family of smooth real vector fields whose
Lie brackets span \(T_pM\) at every \(p\in M\).  Suppose
\(\phi\in C^\infty(M)\) and, for some \(C,A>0\) and \(0<s\le1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gevrey}
 \|X_I\phi\|_{L^\infty(M)}
 \le C A^{|I|}(I!)^s
 \qquad\text{for every word }I.
\end{equation}
If \(\phi\) vanishes on a nonempty open subset of \(M\), then
\(\phi\equiv0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(j\), a point \(p\), and a maximal integral curve
\(\gamma(t)=\operatorname{Fl}_{X_j}^{t}(p)\), defined on an interval \(J\).
For \(u(t)=\phi(\gamma(t))\), the chain rule gives
\[
 u^{(m)}(t)=X_j^m\phi(\gamma(t)).
\]
The repeated word has factorial \(m!\), so \eqref{eq:gevrey} and \(s\le1\)
give
\[
 |u^{(m)}(t)|\le CA^m(m!)^s\le CA^m m!.
\]
Taylor's formula therefore bounds the remainder after order \(N\), whenever
the segment from \(t\) to \(t+h\) lies in \(J\), by
\[
 |R_N(t,h)|
 \le \frac{CA^{N+1}(N+1)!}{(N+1)!}|h|^{N+1}
 =C(A|h|)^{N+1}.
\]
Thus \(u\) is real analytic on \(J\).  In particular, if it vanishes on any
nonempty subinterval, it vanishes on all of \(J\).

Let \(U\) be a nonempty open set on which \(\phi=0\).  The preceding paragraph
shows that, for every local flow time for which it is defined,
\(\operatorname{Fl}_{X_j}^{t}(U)\) is again an open zero set.  The same is true
after any finite composition of local flows of the \(X_j\)'s.  Hence the flow
saturation of \(U\) is contained in \(\{\phi=0\}\).

By the orbit theorem, the orbit of the family \(\X\) is an immersed submanifold
whose tangent space contains the evaluations of all iterated Lie brackets
\cite{sussmann}.  The H\"ormander hypothesis makes every orbit open.  These
orbits partition \(M\); connectedness therefore forces a single orbit.  The
saturation of \(U\) is all of \(M\), so \(\phi\equiv0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Proper compact support]\label{cor:compact}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:unique}, no nonzero function obeying
\eqref{eq:gevrey} can have proper compact support.  In particular, if \(M\) is
connected and noncompact, then
\[
 \gamma^s_{\X,L^\infty}(M)=\{0\},\qquad 0<s\le1,
\]
with the source's convention that this class consists of compactly supported
smooth functions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The complement of a proper support is a nonempty open zero set.  On a
noncompact manifold every compact subset is proper.
\end{proof}

\section{Transfers to the other source definitions}

\begin{corollary}[Horizontal \(L^2\) class]\label{cor:l2}
Assume in addition that the horizontal Sobolev embedding of Definition 2.5 in
the source holds.  Then a compactly supported member of
\(\gamma^s_{\X,L^2}(M)\), \(0<s\le1\), which vanishes on a nonempty open set is
zero.  Consequently, on a connected noncompact \(M\), that class has no
nonzero compactly supported member.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Proposition 2.6 of the source shows that a compactly supported member of the
horizontal \(L^2\) class belongs to the horizontal \(L^\infty\) class, with
the same Gevrey order \cite{source}.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:unique}.
\end{proof}

The paper verifies the required Sobolev embedding for Lie groups of polynomial
volume growth, so Corollary~\ref{cor:l2} applies throughout that principal
noncompact setting.

\begin{corollary}[All three definitions on \(\mathbb H_n\)]\label{cor:heis}
Let \(\mathcal L\) be the standard sub-Laplacian on the Heisenberg group
\(\mathbb H_n\).  For every \(0<s\le1\),
\[
 C_c^\infty(\mathbb H_n)\cap\gamma^s_{\mathcal L}(\mathbb H_n)
 =\{0\}.
\]
The same conclusion holds for both horizontal classes in the source.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem 4.1 of the source proves
\(\gamma^s_{\mathcal L}(\mathbb H_n)=
\gamma^s_{\X,L^2}(\mathbb H_n)\) for every \(s>0\), and it identifies the two
horizontal definitions on compactly supported functions \cite{source}.
Corollaries~\ref{cor:compact} and~\ref{cor:l2} finish the proof.
\end{proof}

Thus the answer to the compact-support clause of Question 3 is affirmative for
the horizontal definitions on every connected noncompact H\"ormander manifold
in the stated setting, and for all three definitions on the Heisenberg groups.

\section{Verification, limitations, and novelty}\label{sec:limits}

The proof uses only four checkable ingredients: the repeated-word identity
\(I!=m!\), differentiation along a flow, Taylor's remainder, and the orbit
theorem.  It works for smooth vector fields; analytic coefficients and
completeness of the flows are unnecessary.  It also strengthens the questioned
endpoint from \(s=1\) to the whole range \(0<s\le1\).

The remaining limitation is substantive.  For a general sub-Laplacian, the
source only proves
\(\gamma^s_{\X,L^2}\subseteq\gamma^s_{\mathcal L}\).  Reversing this inclusion
requires word-uniform higher-order Riesz-transform estimates and is essentially
Question 2 of the same paper.  A bound on \(\mathcal L^k\phi\) alone therefore
cannot be inserted into the flow proof without assuming precisely the missing
upgrade.  The packet makes no claim for the general
\(\gamma^s_{\mathcal L}\) class beyond settings, such as \(\mathbb H_n\), where
the equivalence is known.

On a compact manifold the phrase ``compactly supported'' is vacuous: constants
are compactly supported and belong to these classes.  Open-set uniqueness is
the invariant statement, and proper compact support is the correct corollary.
