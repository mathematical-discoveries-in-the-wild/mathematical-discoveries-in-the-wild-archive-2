\section{Source question}

Friedland and Youssef prove that for every $A\in\R^{n\times m}$ and
$\varepsilon>0$ there is a nonnegative diagonal matrix $D$ with at most
$O(\operatorname{srank}(A)/\varepsilon^2)$ nonzero entries such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
 \|ADA^*-AA^*\|\le\varepsilon\|A\|^2,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{srank}(A)=\frac{\|A\|_F^2}{\|A\|^2}.
\end{equation}
Immediately after their Theorem 1.1 they explicitly identify this as an
equivalent diagonal formulation of their reweighted column extraction.  In
the introduction they note that their proof is existential and say they do
not know how to produce an algorithm attaining it \cite{FY}.

\section{Answering theorem}

Cohen, Nelson, and Woodruff prove the following result in their Section 4
\cite[Theorem 5]{CNW}.

\begin{theorem}[Cohen--Nelson--Woodruff]
Let $B\in\R^{N\times d}$ satisfy $\|B\|^2\le1$ and
$\|B\|_F^2\le k$, and let $0<\varepsilon<1$.  There exists a diagonal
matrix $S$ with $O(k/\varepsilon^2)$ nonzero entries such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cnw}
 \|(SB)^T(SB)-B^TB\|\le\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
Moreover, $S$ can be computed by a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm.
\end{theorem}

Their proof is a stable-rank extension of the Batson--Spielman--Srivastava
barrier method.  It is constructive: the final paragraph verifies that all
barrier invariants and step sizes can be evaluated in polynomial time.

\section{One-line reduction}

Assume $A\ne0$ and apply the answering theorem to
\[
 B=\frac{A^*}{\|A\|}\in\R^{m\times n},
 \qquad
 k=\left\lceil\operatorname{srank}(A)\right\rceil.
\]
Then $\|B\|=1$ and
$\|B\|_F^2=\operatorname{srank}(A)\le k$.  Put $D=S^2$.
Since $S$ is diagonal, $D$ is nonnegative diagonal with the same support,
and \eqref{eq:cnw} becomes
\[
 \left\|
 \frac{AS^2A^*}{\|A\|^2}-
 \frac{AA^*}{\|A\|^2}
 \right\|\le\varepsilon.
\]
Multiplication by $\|A\|^2$ is exactly \eqref{eq:source}.  A nonzero matrix
has stable rank at least one, so replacing it by its ceiling changes only the
absolute constant in the support bound.  The zero matrix is trivial.

Thus the source's diagonal extraction can be produced deterministically in
polynomial time with the same optimal-order
$O(\operatorname{srank}(A)/\varepsilon^2)$ support.

\section{Chronology and scope}

The chronology is unusual.  Version 3 of the answering preprint was posted
on 2 March 2016.  Version 1 of the source was posted on 12 May 2016.  The
answer therefore predates the question by about ten weeks; its ICALP
publication appeared in August 2016.

The reduction resolves exactly the arbitrary nonnegative diagonal-weight
formulation that Friedland--Youssef state as equivalent to their extraction
conclusion.  Cohen--Nelson--Woodruff do not require the selected weights to
be equal.  Accordingly, this packet does not claim a stronger algorithm in
which every weight is equal or an integer multiple of a common quantum.
