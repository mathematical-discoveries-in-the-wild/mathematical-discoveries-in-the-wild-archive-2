\title{A bp-continuous holomorphic functional that is not compact-open continuous\\
\large An explicit example requested in arXiv:2403.11712}
\author{Autonomous solve-first investigation --- lane 12}
\date{13 August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Haak--Haase ask for an example, on an open subset of an arbitrary Banach
space, of a bp-continuous functional on $\Hin$ which is not continuous on
bounded sets for the compact-open topology $\tc$.  Let
$X=C([0,\omega_1])$ and $O=\B_X$.  For $f\in\Hin(O)$, the derivative
$Df(0)$ is a Radon measure on $[0,\omega_1]$; set
\[
 \Phi(f)=Df(0)(\{\omega_1\}).
\]
Every sequence of such measures has jointly countable support, which makes
$\Phi$ bp-continuous.  In contrast, the net of linear functions
$g_\beta(x)=x(\beta)$ converges to $g_{\omega_1}$ uniformly on compact
subsets as $\beta\uparrow\omega_1$, while
$\Phi(g_\beta)=0$ and $\Phi(g_{\omega_1})=1$.  Thus $\Phi$ fails
$\tc$-continuity even on the unit ball.
\end{abstract}

\section{The source question}

For an open set $O$ in a Banach space, let $\Hin(O)$ be the Banach space
of bounded scalar holomorphic functions.  A sequence converges
\emph{bounded pointwise} (bp) if it is uniformly bounded in supremum norm
and converges pointwise.  The topology $\tc$ is uniform convergence on
compact subsets of $O$.

Remark~3.2(2) of Haak--Haase \cite{source} extends their Linearization
Theorem to an arbitrary Banach-space domain when the vector-valued
holomorphic map is separably valued.  They observe that a bp-continuous
functional need not be $\tc$-continuous on bounded sets, but explicitly
state that they know no example.  The construction below supplies one.

\section{The sequentially continuous endpoint mass}

Let $K=[0,\omega_1]$ carry the order topology, and let
$X=C(K)$ over $\C$.  Since $K$ is scattered, every finite Radon measure on
$K$ is atomic with countable support.  Thus the Riesz representation gives
\[
 X'=M(K)=\ell^1(K).
\]
Define the norm-one functional $T:X'\to\C$ by
\[
 T\mu=\mu(\{\omega_1\}).
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:T}
$T$ is sequentially $\wstar$-continuous on $X'$, but its restriction to
the dual unit ball is not $\wstar$-continuous.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $\mu_n\to\mu$ in $\sigma(X',X)$.  The union of the supports of
$\mu,\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots$, after deleting $\omega_1$, is countable.  Its
supremum is therefore some countable ordinal $\alpha<\omega_1$.  The tail
$(\alpha,\omega_1]$ is clopen in $K$, so
$u=\mathbf 1_{(\alpha,\omega_1]}$ belongs to $C(K)$.  By the choice of
$\alpha$,
\[
 T\mu_n=\langle u,\mu_n\rangle\longrightarrow
 \langle u,\mu\rangle=T\mu.
\]

For noncontinuity, let $\delta_\beta$ denote point mass at $\beta$.  Every
$x\in C(K)$ is constant on a sufficiently late ordinal interval.  Hence
$\delta_\beta\to\delta_{\omega_1}$ in $\sigma(X',X)$ as the net of
countable ordinals $\beta$ increases to $\omega_1$.  Yet
$T\delta_\beta=0$ for every $\beta<\omega_1$, whereas
$T\delta_{\omega_1}=1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Transfer to bounded holomorphic functions}

Take the open unit ball $O=\B_X$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi}
 \Phi:\Hin(O)\longrightarrow\C,
 \qquad \Phi(f)=T(Df(0))=Df(0)(\{\omega_1\}).
\end{equation}
Cauchy's estimate on complex lines gives
$\|Df(0)\|\leq\|f\|_\infty$, so $\Phi$ is in fact norm-continuous.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:derivative}
If $f_n\to f$ bounded pointwise in $\Hin(O)$, then
$Df_n(0)\to Df(0)$ in $\sigma(X',X)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The derivatives are norm-bounded by Cauchy's estimate.  Fix $x\in X$ and
choose $r>0$ with $r\|x\|<1$.  The scalar holomorphic functions
\[
 h_n(z)=f_n(zx),\qquad |z|<r,
\]
are uniformly bounded and converge pointwise to $h(z)=f(zx)$.  The
one-variable normal-family theorem makes this convergence locally uniform;
hence $h_n'(0)\to h'(0)$.  Since
$h_n'(0)=Df_n(0)x$, this is precisely weak-star convergence of the
derivatives.
\end{proof}

Combining Lemmas~\ref{lem:T} and \ref{lem:derivative} proves that $\Phi$
is bp-continuous.  It remains to show that the stronger-looking norm
continuity does not rescue compact-open continuity on bounded sets.

For $\beta\leq\omega_1$, define
\[
 g_\beta(x)=x(\beta),\qquad x\in\B_X.
\]
Each $g_\beta$ is a linear holomorphic function of supremum norm one.  The
net $g_\beta$ converges to $g_{\omega_1}$ in $\tc$: it converges pointwise
because every $x\in C(K)$ is eventually constant, and the functionals
$\delta_\beta$ have a common norm bound, so a finite $\varepsilon$-net
upgrades pointwise convergence to uniform convergence on every norm-compact
subset of $\B_X$.  On the other hand,
\[
 \Phi(g_\beta)=T\delta_\beta=0\quad(\beta<\omega_1),
 \qquad
 \Phi(g_{\omega_1})=T\delta_{\omega_1}=1.
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The functional \eqref{eq:Phi} is bp-continuous on
$\Hin(\B_{C([0,\omega_1])})$ but is not $\tc$-continuous on its unit ball.
Consequently it is the example requested in Remark~3.2(2) of
arXiv:2403.11712.
\end{theorem}

\section{Checks, mechanism, and scope}

The construction separates sequences from nets exactly where required.
Joint countability of the supports proves sequential continuity; the
uncountable ordinal net witnesses genuine topological discontinuity.  No
separability is assumed of the domain space.  The result is even slightly
stronger than requested because $\Phi$ is norm-continuous and fails
$\tc$-continuity already on the unit ball.

More generally, the same derivative-at-zero construction works whenever a
Banach space $Y$ admits a bounded functional on $Y'$ which is weak-star
sequentially continuous but not weak-star continuous on a bounded set---in
particular, whenever $Y$ fails the Mazur property in this form.

Exact source-phrase, arXiv-id, bp/compact-open, and holomorphic/Mazur-property
searches on 13 August 2026 found the source question but no answer to it.
The endpoint-mass map on $M([0,\omega_1])$ is a classical weak-star
sequential counterexample; the transfer through $Df(0)$ is the solve-first
step.  This bounded search is not a priority claim.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Accept as a candidate full counterexample.  The two substantive checks are
the jointly countable-support argument in Lemma~\ref{lem:T} and the
compact-uniform convergence of the evaluation net; both are explicit.
