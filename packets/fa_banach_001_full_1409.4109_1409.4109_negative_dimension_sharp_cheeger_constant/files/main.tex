\section*{Source and question}

Milman \cite{milman} proves that a weighted manifold satisfying
\(\mathrm{CDD}(0,N,D)\), with
\(N\in(-\infty,0]\cup[n,\infty]\) and \(D<\infty\), has
\[
 \Che(M,g,\mu)
 :=\inf_{0<v<1}\frac{\Ical(M,g,\mu)(v)}{\min(v,1-v)}
 \ge \frac1D.
\]
Remark 5.20 observes that the optimal \(2/D\) bound is known when
\(N\in[n,\infty]\), and asks whether it also holds for \(N\le0\).  The paper
reduces the missing case to an explicit calculation for the one-dimensional
densities \(t^{N-1}\) on \([\xi,\xi+D]\).

\section*{Answer and proof intuition}

The answer is yes.  For a decreasing density on an interval, the half-line
Cheeger constant is the smallest reverse hazard to the right of the median.
Writing \(\beta=-N\), scaling the upper endpoint to one, and taking the
reciprocal turns the entire calculation into maximizing
\[
 \phi_\beta(x)=\frac{x(1-x^\beta)}{\beta}
\]
from the median to \(1\); at \(\beta=0\), its continuous limit is
\(\phi_0(x)=x\log(1/x)\).  The median is explicit.  Comparing it with the
unique stationary point of \(\phi_\beta\), a single tangent-line inequality
shows that the maximum is at most half the interval length.  Inverting gives
the desired \(2/D\).

\begin{theorem}[Sharp negative-dimension Cheeger bound]\label{thm:main}
Let \((M^n,g,\mu)\) satisfy \(\mathrm{CDD}(0,N,D)\), where \(N\le0\) and
\(0<D<\infty\).  Then
\[
 \Che(M,g,\mu)\ge\frac2D.
\]
The constant is optimal for every such \(N\).
\end{theorem}

\section*{The one-dimensional calculation}

We first record the exact calculation needed by the source's model theorem.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:model}
For \(N\le0\), \(\xi>0\), and \(D>0\), let \(\nu\) be the probability
measure on \([\xi,\xi+D]\) with density proportional to \(t^{N-1}\).  If
\(\Jcal_\nu\) denotes its half-line isoperimetric profile, then
\[
 \inf_{0<v<1}\frac{\Jcal_\nu(v)}{\min(v,1-v)}\ge\frac2D.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(w(t)=t^{N-1}\), \(b=\xi+D\), and
\(Z=\int_\xi^b w(t)\dd t\).  Since \(w\) is decreasing, if \(0<v\le1/2\)
and \(x\) is determined by
\[
 \int_x^b w(t)\dd t=vZ,
\]
then the right half-line has the smaller of the two possible half-line
boundary densities.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hazard}
 \frac{\Jcal_\nu(v)}v
 =\frac{w(x)}{\int_x^b w(t)\dd t}.
\end{equation}
As \(v\) ranges over \((0,1/2]\), \(x\) ranges from the median \(m\) to
\(b\).  Symmetry of \(\Jcal_\nu\) handles \(v\ge1/2\).  It follows that the
reciprocal of the left-hand side in the lemma is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
 \max_{m\le x<b}\frac{\int_x^b w(t)\dd t}{w(x)}.
\end{equation}

Dilate by \(b\), so the interval becomes \([a,1]\), where
\(a=\xi/b\), and its length becomes \(d=1-a=D/b\).  The quantity in
\eqref{eq:residual} scales by \(b\).  Set \(\beta=-N\ge0\), so the scaled
density is \(x^{-\beta-1}\).  We will prove that the scaled residual quantity
is at most \(d/2\).

Assume first that \(\beta>0\).  Direct integration gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi}
 \phi_\beta(x)
 :=\frac{\int_x^1t^{-\beta-1}\dd t}{x^{-\beta-1}}
 =\frac{x(1-x^\beta)}{\beta}.
\end{equation}
The median relation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:median}
 m^\beta=\frac{2a^\beta}{1+a^\beta},
 \qquad
 a=A_\beta(m),\quad
 A_\beta(x):=x(2-x^\beta)^{-1/\beta}.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
 \phi_\beta'(x)=\frac{1-(\beta+1)x^\beta}{\beta},
 \qquad
 x_*=(\beta+1)^{-1/\beta}
\]
is its unique maximizer on \((0,1)\).

We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar}
 2\phi_\beta(x)\le1-A_\beta(x)
 \qquad(x_*\le x\le1).
\end{equation}
Indeed, let
\[
 F_\beta(x)=1-A_\beta(x)-2\phi_\beta(x),\qquad u=x^\beta.
\]
Since
\[
 A_\beta'(x)=2(2-x^\beta)^{-1-1/\beta},
\]
we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fprime}
 \frac12F_\beta'(x)
 =\frac{(\beta+1)u-1}{\beta}
  -(2-u)^{-1-1/\beta}.
\end{equation}
The function \(g(u)=(2-u)^{-1-1/\beta}\) is convex.  Its tangent at
\(u=1\) is
\[
 g(1)+g'(1)(u-1)
 =\frac{(\beta+1)u-1}{\beta}.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:Fprime} is nonpositive for
\(u\in[1/(\beta+1),1]\), equivalently for \(x\in[x_*,1]\).  Since
\(F_\beta(1)=0\), this proves \eqref{eq:scalar}.

If \(m\ge x_*\), then \(\phi_\beta\) decreases on \([m,1]\), and
\eqref{eq:scalar} together with \eqref{eq:median} yields
\[
 2\max_{[m,1]}\phi_\beta
 =2\phi_\beta(m)\le1-A_\beta(m)=1-a=d.
\]
If \(m<x_*\), then \(A_\beta(m)<A_\beta(x_*)\), because \(A_\beta'>0\).
Using \eqref{eq:scalar} at \(x_*\) gives
\[
 2\max_{[m,1]}\phi_\beta
 =2\phi_\beta(x_*)\le1-A_\beta(x_*)<1-a=d.
\]
So the desired residual bound holds for every \(\beta>0\).

When \(\beta=0\), the density is \(x^{-1}\),
\[
 \phi_0(x)=x\log(1/x),\qquad m=\sqrt a,\qquad x_*=e^{-1}.
\]
Set \(A_0(x)=x^2\) and
\[
 F_0(x)=1-A_0(x)-2\phi_0(x)=1-x^2+2x\log x.
\]
For \(x\in[x_*,1]\),
\[
 F_0'(x)=2(1-x+\log x)\le0,
\]
and \(F_0(1)=0\), so \(F_0\ge0\) there.  The same two cases
\(m\ge x_*\) and \(m<x_*\), using \(a=A_0(m)\), prove
\(\max_{[m,1]}\phi_0\le(1-a)/2\).

Undoing the dilation, \eqref{eq:residual} is at most \(D/2\).
Equation \eqref{eq:hazard} now proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\section*{Passage to the manifold and sharpness}

For \(N\le0\), Case 3 of the source's model-space corollary gives, pointwise
in \(v\),
\[
 \Ical(M,g,\mu)(v)
 \ge\inf_{\xi>0}
 \Jcal\bigl(t^{N-1},[\xi,\xi+D]\bigr)(v).
\]
Dividing by \(\min(v,1-v)\) and applying Lemma \ref{lem:model} to every
\(\xi\) yields
\[
 \frac{\Ical(M,g,\mu)(v)}{\min(v,1-v)}\ge\frac2D
\]
for every \(v\in(0,1)\).  Taking the infimum proves Theorem
\ref{thm:main}.

The uniform probability measure on an interval of length \(D\) satisfies
\(\mathrm{CDD}(0,N,D)\) for every \(N\le0\): its density is constant and its
generalized Ricci tensor is zero.  Its isoperimetric profile equals \(1/D\)
on \((0,1)\), so its linear Cheeger constant is \(2/D\), attained at
\(v=1/2\).  The bound is therefore sharp.

\section*{Verification, novelty, and scope}

The scalar formula was checked independently at 80 decimal digits on 10,000
random parameter pairs spanning twelve decades of \(\beta\); the smallest
sampled value of \(D\) times the model Cheeger constant was
\(2.00000000000000000000000008357\ldots\).  This computation is only a
sanity check; the proof above is exact.

On 2026-08-11, exact-phrase, exact-title, negative-dimension
Cheeger/diameter, and citation searches found no later claimed resolution.
The run's lightweight indexes and the local arXiv papers citing the source
also contained no matching result.  This is a bounded novelty check, not an
exhaustive survey of all non-arXiv literature.

The theorem answers precisely the missing \(N\le0\) branch of Remark 5.20.
Together with the already-known \(N\in[n,\infty]\) branch, it improves
Theorem 5.19 from \(1/D\) to the sharp \(2/D\) throughout that theorem's
entire stated dimension range.
