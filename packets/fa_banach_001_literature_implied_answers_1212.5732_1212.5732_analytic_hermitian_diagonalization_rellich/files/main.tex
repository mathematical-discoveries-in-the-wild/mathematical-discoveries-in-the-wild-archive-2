\textbf{Status.} \texttt{literature\_implied\_answer} (complete answer to
Question 2.9 of arXiv:1212.5732).

\textbf{Source question.} Cyr--Ekstrand--Meyers--Peoples--Peters,
arXiv:1212.5732v1, Question 2.9 on page 8, ask whether a Hermitian
matrix family $A\in M_n(C[a,b])$ is diagonalizable over $[a,b]$ when its
entries extend analytically to a complex domain containing $[a,b]$.
The question refers to the three-entry notation for the $2\times2$ matrix in
equation (1), although it writes $M_n$.  The answer below covers both readings.

\textbf{Answer.} Yes, for every finite $n$; moreover, the diagonalizing
unitary may be chosen real analytic.

\textbf{Identification.} Rainer's Theorem 1.1(A) in
arXiv:1111.4475v2, page 1, gives global analytic parameterizations of the
eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a one-real-parameter analytic normal family.
The real-analytic class is quasianalytic, so the finite-order-of-contact
condition in that theorem is automatic.  In the finite-dimensional argument,
page 18 shows that normality makes the transport unitary and hence the
eigenvectors can be chosen orthonormal.

For completeness, choose an open real interval $I$ containing $[a,b]$ and
contained in the complex domain of the entrywise extensions.  Hermiticity on
$[a,b]$ extends to $I$: for example, on the real axis each function
\[
  x\longmapsto A_{kj}(x)-\overline{A_{jk}(x)}
\]
is real analytic and vanishes on $(a,b)$, hence vanishes on the connected
interval $I$; the diagonal identities are identical.  Thus $A|_I$ is a
real-analytic Hermitian, hence normal, matrix family.  Rellich--Rainer supplies
real-analytic functions $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$ and an orthonormal
real-analytic eigenframe $v_1,\ldots,v_n$.  With
\[
  U(t)=\bigl[v_1(t)\ \cdots\ v_n(t)\bigr]
\]
we have, for every $t\in[a,b]$,
\[
  U(t)^*U(t)=I,
  \qquad
  U(t)^*A(t)U(t)=\operatorname{diag}
     (\lambda_1(t),\ldots,\lambda_n(t)).
\]
This is precisely continuous diagonalizability, with stronger analytic
regularity.

\textbf{Provenance and scope.} This is an implication identified by the run,
not an explicit later claim to answer Question 2.9.  Rainer's preprint was
submitted in November 2011 and revised in April 2012, before the source paper's
December 2012 submission, and identifies the self-adjoint analytic case with
Rellich's classical theorem.  The result fully answers Question 2.9, but does
not settle the source's separate arbitrary-$n$, $C^1$ extension problem for
Theorem 2.6.
