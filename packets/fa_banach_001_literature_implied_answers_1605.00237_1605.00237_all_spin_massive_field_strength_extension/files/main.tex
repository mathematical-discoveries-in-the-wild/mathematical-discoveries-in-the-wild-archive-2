\begin{center}
{\Large Literature-implied answer for arXiv:1605.00237}\\[4pt]
{\large All massive integer-spin field strengths have the conjectured fine
ultraviolet extension}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{literature\_implied\_answer (full conjectured scope).}
This is not a new theorem produced by the run.  It is an agent-identified
implication from a later all-spin lemma, combined with the extension criterion
proved and used in the source paper.

\paragraph{Source conjecture.}
Joseph C. V\'arilly and Jos\'e M. Gracia-Bond\'ia,
\emph{Stora's fine notion of divergent amplitudes}, arXiv:1605.00237,
Section~6, PDF page~9, prove the massive spin-one and spin-two cases and then
state:
\begin{quote}
``We conjecture that our conclusions extend to all the massive
$F_{\mu_1\nu_1,\ldots,\mu_h\nu_h}$-fields.''
\end{quote}
Here the conclusion is that the off-diagonal time-ordered two-point function
has the optimal fine ultraviolet behavior and extends to the diagonal without
further renormalization.

\paragraph{Decisive later theorem.}
Jens Mund and Erichardson T. de Oliveira,
\emph{String-localized free vector and tensor potentials for massive
particles with any spin: I. Bosons}, arXiv:1609.01667, Lemma~14,
PDF page~28, construct the massive spin-$s$ point-local field strength and
prove that its on-shell two-point function is a homogeneous polynomial
$P_s(p)$ of degree $2s$.  Their proof writes the all-spin intertwiner as the
symmetric trace-free projection of $s$ spin-one field-strength intertwiners;
each scalar-product term is consequently a product of $s$ homogeneous
quadratic spin-one factors.

\paragraph{Why this answers the conjecture.}
Off the diagonal the corresponding Feynman two-point function is represented
by $P_s(\partial)D_m^F$.  Its leading massless singularity has the form
\[
 P_s(\partial)\frac{1}{x^2-i0}
   =\frac{H_{2s}(x)}{(x^2-i0)^{2s+1}},
\]
where $H_{2s}$ is homogeneous of degree $2s$.  It is harmonic: away from the
origin the left side is killed by $\Box$, and the radial identity in
Section~3 of the source paper, with $\alpha=-(2s+1)$ and $k=2s$, then forces
$\Box H_{2s}=0$.

The source paper's NST criterion compares the harmonicity degree $k$ with the
degree of divergence $2r-k-4$.  Here $k=2s$ and $r=2s+1$, so
\[
 2s>2(2s+1)-2s-4=2s-2.
\]
Hence the leading term has a unique Lorentz-covariant homogeneous extension.
The massive Riesz/associate-homogeneity construction in Section~6 of the
source paper canonically extends the lower mass-dependent terms.  Thus
Mund--de Oliveira's Lemma~14 provides precisely the all-spin algebraic input
missing from the two cases checked in the source.

An on-shell polynomial determines the off-diagonal Feynman numerator up to a
multiple of $p^2-m^2$; after division by the Klein--Gordon denominator, that
freedom is a contact term supported on the diagonal.  It therefore does not
create an obstruction to the asserted extension.

\begin{claim}
For every positive integer $s$, the massive point-local field strength
$F_{\mu_1\nu_1,\ldots,\mu_s\nu_s}$ has the fine ultraviolet extension to the
diagonal conjectured in arXiv:1605.00237.
\end{claim}

\paragraph{Provenance and scope.}
The supporting paper cites arXiv:1605.00237 when discussing massive
associate-homogeneity, but it does not announce Lemma~14 as a resolution of
the displayed conjecture.  The relation is therefore an agent-identified
literature implication, not an explicit literature answer.  It covers every
positive integer spin in the conjectured family; it makes no claim about
interacting composite fields or higher time-ordered products.

\paragraph{Search evidence.}
The run's cheap indexes had no prior result for arXiv:1605.00237.  A bounded
source and citation search used the arXiv id, the displayed field-strength
notation, ``all massive'', ``homogeneous polynomial of degree $2s$'', and
``associate homogeneity''.  It located arXiv:1609.01667 and the cross-citation
above, but no paper explicitly claiming to resolve the exact conjecture.
