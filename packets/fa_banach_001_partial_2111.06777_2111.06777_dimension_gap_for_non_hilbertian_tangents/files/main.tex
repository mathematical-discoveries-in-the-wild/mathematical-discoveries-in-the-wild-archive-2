\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Candidate substantial partial result, likely valid. It proves that a
non-Hilbertian tangent at a generic \(n\)-dimensional chart point must have
Hausdorff dimension at least \(n+1\). The unrestricted question remains open.

\section*{Source question and exact scope}

The source is D.~Lu\v{c}i\'c, E.~Pasqualetto, and T.~Rajala,
\emph{Non-Hilbertian tangents to Hilbertian spaces},
arXiv:2111.06777v1 \cite{source}. Question 1.5 on PDF page 4 asks:

\begin{quote}
Is it true that if \((X,d,m)\) is an infinitesimally Hilbertian PI space,
then \(\Tan_x(X,d,m)\) contains only infinitesimally Hilbertian elements for
\(m\)-a.e. \(x\in X\)?
\end{quote}

The same page identifies the difficulty. At a generic point of an
\(n\)-dimensional differentiability chart, each tangent \(Y\) has a blow-up
map \(F:Y\to T_xX\) that is a metric submersion; although \(T_xX\) is Hilbert,
\(Y\) might have additional independent directions.

\section*{Proof intuition}

The exact, constant-one nature of the metric submersion retains more
information than mere surjectivity. If \(\ell\) is a linear functional on
\(T_xX\), then \(\ell\circ F\) has pointwise Lipschitz constant exactly
\(\|\ell\|\): every target direction can be lifted without increasing its
length. PI calculus therefore turns pullback by \(F\) into an isometric
embedding of the \(n\)-dimensional Hilbert cotangent \(T_x^*X\) into the
cotangent of \(Y\).

An \(r\)-dimensional differentiability chart cannot live in a space of
Hausdorff dimension below \(r\). Hence, if \(\dimH Y<n+1\), the cotangent of
\(Y\) has rank at most \(n\). The \(n\) directions already supplied by \(F\)
must span it. The entire cotangent norm is then Hilbert, so the Cheeger energy
of \(Y\) is quadratic. What remains open is exactly the case in which a
tangent has room for at least one additional analytic direction.

\section*{Dimension-gap theorem}

We use the usual Cheeger measurable cotangent bundle of a PI space. The norm
of \(du\) agrees almost everywhere with the pointwise Lipschitz constant of a
Lipschitz function \(u\) \cite{cks}. An infinitesimally Hilbertian PI space
has Hilbertian tangent and cotangent fibers almost everywhere; see the source
and the references there.

\begin{lemma}[metric-submersion pullback]\label{lem:pullback}
Let \(F:(Y,d_Y)\to(V,\|\cdot\|_V)\) be a metric submersion onto a
finite-dimensional normed space, and suppose \(Y\) is PI. Then, almost
everywhere on \(Y\),
\[
 F^*:V^*\longrightarrow T^*Y,\qquad
 \ell\longmapsto d(\ell\circ F),
\]
is a linear isometric embedding.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A metric submersion is \(1\)-Lipschitz and lifts every target point with no
increase in distance \cite[Definition 1.13]{cks}. Therefore, for every
\(y\in Y\) and \(\ell\in V^*\),
\[
 \lip(\ell\circ F)(y)\leq\|\ell\|_{V^*}.
\]
Choose a unit vector \(v\in V\) with
\(\ell(v)=\|\ell\|_{V^*}\). For every \(t>0\), the submersion property gives
\(y_t\in Y\) such that
\[
 F(y_t)=F(y)+tv,\qquad d_Y(y,y_t)=t.
\]
The corresponding difference quotient equals \(\|\ell\|_{V^*}\), proving
the reverse inequality and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-lip}
 \lip(\ell\circ F)(y)=\|\ell\|_{V^*}
 \quad\text{for every }y\in Y.
\end{equation}
On a PI space, the cotangent norm of a Lipschitz differential agrees almost
everywhere with its pointwise Lipschitz constant \cite{cks}. Apply this first
to a countable dense subset of \(V^*\), intersect the full-measure sets, and
then use continuity and linearity. Equation \eqref{eq:exact-lip} gives
\(\|F^*\ell\|=\|\ell\|_{V^*}\) for every \(\ell\in V^*\) almost everywhere.
Thus \(F^*\) is a linear isometry, in particular injective.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[analytic rank is bounded by Hausdorff dimension]
If a Lipschitz differentiability space \(Y\) has an \(r\)-dimensional chart of
positive measure, then \(r\leq\dimH Y\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a chart \((U,\varphi)\), De Philippis--Marchese--Rindler prove
\(\varphi_\#(m|_U)\ll\mathcal L^r\) \cite[Theorem 1.1]{dpmr}.
After restricting to a finite positive-measure subset if necessary, this is a
nonzero measure, so \(\varphi(U)\) has positive \(r\)-dimensional Lebesgue
measure. Hence \(\dimH\varphi(U)\geq r\). Since \(\varphi\) is Lipschitz,
\(\dimH U\geq\dimH\varphi(U)\), and the claim follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[one full dimension is necessary]\label{thm:gap}
Let \((X,d,m)\) be an infinitesimally Hilbertian PI space. For \(m\)-almost
every point \(x\) in an \(n\)-dimensional differentiability chart, the
following holds for every pmGH tangent
\((Y,d_Y,m_Y,q)\in\Tan_x(X,d,m)\):
\[
 \boxed{\quad \dimH Y<n+1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 Y\text{ is infinitesimally Hilbertian}.\quad}
\]
Equivalently, any infinitesimally non-Hilbertian tangent at such a point has
\(\dimH Y\geq n+1\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(x\) in the common full-measure set on which the analytic tangent
\(V=T_xX\) is an \(n\)-dimensional Hilbert space and the blow-up theorem of
Cheeger--Kleiner--Schioppa applies to every tangent. For the given \(Y\), it
provides a blow-up chart
\[
 F:Y\longrightarrow V
\]
which is a metric submersion \cite[Theorem 1.12]{cks}; this is precisely the
map recalled by the source immediately after Question 1.5.

The space \(Y\) is again PI \cite{source}, hence it has a measurable
cotangent bundle. Lemma \ref{lem:pullback} supplies \(n\) independent
cotangent directions at almost every point of \(Y\), so the local analytic
rank is at least \(n\). On the other hand, every positive-measure chart has
integer dimension at most \(\dimH Y<n+1\), hence at most \(n\). Thus the
cotangent rank is exactly \(n\) almost everywhere.

Consequently the isometric embedding
\(F^*:V^*\to T_y^*Y\) from Lemma \ref{lem:pullback} is onto at almost every
\(y\). Since \(V^*\) is Hilbert, the full pointwise cotangent norm of \(Y\) is
Hilbert almost everywhere. The Cheeger energy is the integral of the square
of this norm, so it satisfies the parallelogram identity. Therefore \(Y\) is
infinitesimally Hilbertian.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Ahlfors dimension gap]\label{cor:ahlfors}
Suppose in addition that \(X\) is Ahlfors \(Q\)-regular. At almost every
point \(x\) of an \(n\)-dimensional chart, if \(Q<n+1\), every tangent at \(x\)
is infinitesimally Hilbertian.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every pmGH tangent is again Ahlfors \(Q\)-regular and PI, and therefore has
Hausdorff dimension \(Q\). Apply Theorem \ref{thm:gap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[the sub-two-dimensional regime]
Every Ahlfors \(Q\)-regular PI space with \(1\leq Q<2\) is infinitesimally
Hilbertian, and every one of its pmGH tangents is infinitesimally Hilbertian.
Thus Question 1.5 has an affirmative answer in this nonrectifiable regime,
even without assuming the original space to be infinitesimally Hilbertian.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every positive-measure differentiability chart has positive integer dimension
and, by the preceding rank bound, dimension at most \(Q<2\); hence its
dimension is one. Every norm on a one-dimensional vector space is Hilbertian,
so the cotangent norm and Cheeger energy are Hilbertian. Tangents preserve
the Ahlfors \(Q\)-regular PI hypotheses, so the same argument applies to each
tangent.
\end{proof}

\section*{Limitations, attempts, and novelty check}

The theorem does not resolve Question 1.5 when a tangent has Hausdorff
dimension at least \(n+1\). In that range, \(F^*V^*\) can be a proper
Hilbertian subspace of the tangent cotangent, and the metric submersion gives
no control on the norm of the additional vertical directions.

Eight focused upgrade attempts were recorded in the accompanying attempt
note. They covered direct rank preservation, rank-one inverse limits,
products, positive-measure oscillating coefficients, the successful
submersion pullback argument, the Hausdorff-dimension closure, the
Ahlfors-regular specialization, and a final attempt to remove the dimension
gap. The last step runs into exactly the unresolved extra-direction
obstruction stated by the source.

Four run indexes and the local parsed corpus were searched for the arXiv id,
exact title, Question 1.5, metric submersions, analytic-rank growth, and
Ahlfors regular PI spaces. Bounded web/arXiv searches through 17 August 2026
used the exact question/title and combinations of analytic dimension,
Hausdorff dimension, metric submersion, \(Q<n+1\), and non-Hilbertian tangent.
They found the structural results cited below and adjacent work, but no
explicit statement of Theorem \ref{thm:gap} and no resolution of the
unrestricted question. Novelty confidence is moderate: the argument is a
short synthesis of known theorems and may be folklore.

\section*{Verification and review recommendation}

The proof was audited at the three nonformal interfaces: exact lifting in the
metric-submersion lemma, simultaneous almost-everywhere passage from slopes to
cotangent norms, and the chart-dimension bound from absolute continuity. No
numerical computation is relevant. Recommended human review should focus on
whether the chosen PI cotangent formalism identifies the norm of every
Lipschitz differential with the pointwise slope exactly (rather than only up
to equivalence), and on the standard equivalence between pointwise Hilbertian
cotangent norms and quadratic Cheeger energy.
