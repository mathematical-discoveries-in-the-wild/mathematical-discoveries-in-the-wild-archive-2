\section{Source question and result}

Krej\v{c}i\v{r}'ik, Nguyen Duc, and Raymond construct WKB pseudomodes for a
two-dimensional Laplacian with a smooth complex magnetic potential.  If
$x^0$ belongs to their noncritical set $\Gamma$ and the magnetic field $\B$ is
real analytic at $x^0$, their Theorem~1.5 gives
\[
 \norm{(\Lop_{h,\A}-h\B(x^0))u_h}
 \leq \exp(-C h^{-1/7})\norm{u_h}.
\]
They explicitly ask whether the power $1/7$ is optimal
\cite[p.~69 and Theorem~1.5]{Source}.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_question_crop.png}
  \caption{Source PDF page~69 (arXiv PDF page~4): the exact optimality
  question and its connection to the transport estimate.}
\end{figure}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Under the hypotheses of source Theorem~1.5, there are $C,h_0>0$ and
$u_h\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2)$, $0<h\leq h_0$, such that
\[
 \norm{(\Lop_{h,\A}-h\B(x^0))u_h}
 \leq \exp(-C h^{-1/4})\norm{u_h}.
\]
Consequently the source power $1/7$ is not optimal.
\end{theorem}

The WKB construction itself is unchanged.  The improvement comes from a local
Cauchy estimate centered at each evaluation point of the transport recursion.

\section{The source recursion and the lost powers}

Translate $x^0$ to zero.  Source Theorem~3.2 constructs a phase $P$ and
real-analytic amplitudes $(a_j)_{j\geq0}$ on one neighborhood, with
$a_0(0)=1$ and $a_j(0)=0$ for $j\geq1$.  The uniform radii fixed in the proof
of source Lemma~3.9, especially (3.19), put all their holomorphic
complexifications on one common polydisc.  In the complex variables $(z,w)$,
write $\widetilde a_j$ for those complexifications and relabel the source
characteristic curve as $w=\omega(z)$.

The exact source recursion (3.13) has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recursion}
\begin{split}
 \widetilde a_{j+1}(z,w)
 &=J(z,w)\int_{\omega(z)}^w
   \frac{T_j(z,u)}{2J(z,u)V(z,u)}\,du\\
 &\quad+\widetilde a_0(z,w)\int_0^z
   \frac{G_j(u)}{\mathcal A_0(u)}\,du,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where
\[
 T_j(z,w)=\int_0^1
 \partial_w^2\partial_z\widetilde a_j
   (z,\omega(z)+t(w-\omega(z)))\,dt,
\]
and $G_j$ is a bounded analytic linear combination, evaluated at
$(u,\omega(u))$, of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiindices}
 \partial_z\partial_w^2\widetilde a_j,
 \qquad
 \partial_z^2\partial_w^2\widetilde a_j,
 \qquad
 \partial_z\partial_w^3\widetilde a_j.
\end{equation}
All the other factors in \eqref{eq:recursion} are fixed holomorphic functions;
the source has already shrunk the neighborhood so that the required
denominators do not vanish.

The derivative multi-orders in \eqref{eq:multiindices} have total orders
$3,4,4$.  The source instead uses Cauchy contours centered at the origin.
Their denominators carry one extra power in each variable, and the proof then
takes the largest separate $z$- and $w$-losses simultaneously, producing
$(R_1-|z|)^{-3}(R_2-|w|)^{-4}$ and hence $j^7$ per step.  The following local
form of Cauchy's estimate retains the actual total derivative order.

\begin{lemma}[Centered Cauchy estimate]\label{lem:cauchy}
Let $f$ be holomorphic on the polydisc
$\mathcal P(r_1,r_2)=\{|z|<r_1,|w|<r_2\}$.  Suppose
$(z,w)$ has coordinate margins at least $\delta_1,\delta_2>0$.  Then, for
$\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb N_0$,
\[
 |\partial_z^\alpha\partial_w^\beta f(z,w)|
 \leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\,
 \delta_1^{-\alpha}\delta_2^{-\beta}
 \norm{f}_{r_1,r_2},
\]
where $\norm{f}_{r_1,r_2}=\sup_{\mathcal P(r_1,r_2)}|f|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply the two-variable Cauchy formula on circles centered at $(z,w)$ with
radii arbitrarily close to $(\delta_1,\delta_2)$.  Differentiating the Cauchy
kernels gives the stated powers (and the harmless factorial constant).
\end{proof}

\section{Improved transport growth}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:transport}
There are $M,\delta_0>0$ such that for every $j\geq1$ and
$x\in D(0,\delta_0)\subset\mathbb R^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport-bound}
 |a_j(x)|+|\nabla a_j(x)|+|\Delta a_j(x)|
 \leq M^{j+1}j^{4j}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $R_1,R_2>0$ so that the closed polydisc with radii
$(2R_1,2R_2)$ lies in the common holomorphic neighborhood of all data in
\eqref{eq:recursion}.  As in the source proof, after shrinking we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve}
 |\omega(z)|\leq C_0|z|,
 \qquad C_0R_1<R_2,
\end{equation}
and every fixed coefficient in \eqref{eq:recursion} is uniformly bounded.

Fix $n\geq1$.  For $k=0,\ldots,n$, set
\[
 r_{1,k}=\left(2-\frac{k}{n}\right)R_1,
 \qquad
 r_{2,k}=\left(2-\frac{k}{n}\right)R_2.
\]
The coordinate gaps between consecutive polydiscs are exactly
$R_1/n$ and $R_2/n$.

Take $(z,w)\in\mathcal P(r_{1,k+1},r_{2,k+1})$.  Both endpoints
$\omega(z)$ and $w$ lie in the $w$-disc of radius $r_{2,k+1}$, by
\eqref{eq:curve}; hence their entire joining segment does too.  If $u$ lies
on the segment from $0$ to $z$, then
\[
 |u|\leq |z|<r_{1,k+1},
 \qquad |\omega(u)|\leq C_0|u|<r_{2,k+1}.
\]
Thus every point where a derivative of $\widetilde a_k$ is evaluated in
\eqref{eq:recursion} lies in the $(k+1)$-st polydisc and has margins at least
$R_1/n,R_2/n$ inside the $k$-th polydisc.

Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy} and the exhaustive derivative list
\eqref{eq:multiindices} now give
\[
 \norm{T_k}_{r_{1,k+1},r_{2,k+1}}
 \leq C n^3\norm{\widetilde a_k}_{r_{1,k},r_{2,k}},
 \qquad
 \sup|G_k|\leq C n^4\norm{\widetilde a_k}_{r_{1,k},r_{2,k}}.
\]
The two path lengths in \eqref{eq:recursion} and all fixed coefficients are
bounded independently of $k,n$.  Therefore
\[
 \norm{\widetilde a_{k+1}}_{r_{1,k+1},r_{2,k+1}}
 \leq C n^4\norm{\widetilde a_k}_{r_{1,k},r_{2,k}}.
\]
Multiplication for $k=0,\ldots,n-1$ yields
\[
 \norm{\widetilde a_n}_{R_1,R_2}
 \leq (Cn^4)^n\norm{\widetilde a_0}_{2R_1,2R_2}.
\]
A final application of Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy} across the fixed margins from
$(R_1,R_2)$ to $(R_1/2,R_2/2)$ gives the same growth for the first derivatives
and for $4\partial_z\partial_w\widetilde a_n=\widetilde{\Delta a_n}$.
Enlarging one constant and restricting to a fixed real disk proves
\eqref{eq:transport-bound}.
\end{proof}

\section{Optimal truncation and proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

Let $M$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:transport}, and for small $h>0$ put
\[
 N(h)=\left\lfloor(eMh)^{-1/4}\right\rfloor.
\]
For $1\leq j\leq N(h)$,
\[
 Mh j^4\leq e^{-1},
 \qquad
 h^jM^{j+1}j^{4j}\leq M e^{-j}.
\]
Consequently the truncated amplitude and its first derivatives are uniformly
bounded.  Since $a_j(0)=0$ for $j\geq1$, integration of the gradient along
$[0,x]$ also gives
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{N(h)}h^j|a_j(x)|\leq C|x|.
\]
Together with $a_0(0)=1$, this supplies a uniform lower bound on the amplitude
near zero.

Use the source phase $P$ and a fixed cutoff $\chi$ supported in the disk where
\eqref{eq:transport-bound} holds:
\[
 u_h=\chi e^{-P/h}\sum_{j=0}^{N(h)}h^ja_j.
\]
The positive-definite quadratic expansion of $\operatorname{Re}P$ implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm-lower}
 \norm{u_h}\geq c h^{1/2};
\end{equation}
indeed, integrate on a real ball of radius comparable to $\sqrt h$.

The source transport identities cancel every interior power through
$h^{N(h)+1}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:transport} bounds the remaining coefficient by
\begin{align*}
 h^{N+2}|\Delta a_N|
 &\leq Mh^2(MhN^4)^N\\
 &\leq Mh^2e^{-N}
 \leq \exp(-c h^{-1/4})
\end{align*}
after decreasing $c$ and $h_0$.  Its Gaussian $L^2$ norm is $O(h^{1/2})$,
which matches \eqref{eq:norm-lower}.  On the support of $\nabla\chi$,
$\operatorname{Re}P$ has a fixed positive lower bound; the unchanged
commutator term is therefore $O(e^{-c_0/h})$ up to polynomial factors.
After division by \eqref{eq:norm-lower}, this is smaller than
$e^{-c h^{-1/4}}$.  We have used the source's gauge-restored identity (3.14),
whose phase is already $P=S+i\theta$, so no additional gauge factor has to be
estimated here.  Combining the two remainders proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Verification, novelty, and scope}

The proof was checked in nine independent pieces: common analyticity,
characteristic-curve geometry, centered Cauchy margins, exhaustive derivative
orders, the one-step recursion, iteration, fixed-margin derivative recovery,
optimal truncation, and relative $L^2$ normalization.  A deterministic script
independently verifies the multi-index and truncation bookkeeping.

A bounded search on 11 August 2026 covered the local run and full-source
indexes, the exact title/id and $h^{1/7}$ wording, close official arXiv searches
for a $1/4$ improvement and local-Cauchy WKB estimates, the September 2025
arXiv revision, and the January 2026 publication record.  No prior exponent
improvement was found.  The argument proves only that $1/7$ is not optimal;
whether $1/4$ can be improved, possibly all the way to $1$, remains open here.
