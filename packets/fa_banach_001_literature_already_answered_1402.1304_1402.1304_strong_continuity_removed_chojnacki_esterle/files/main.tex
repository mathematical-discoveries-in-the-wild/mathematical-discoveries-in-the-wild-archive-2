\section*{Source question}

Schwenninger and Zwart prove that a strongly continuous cosine family
$C:\mathbb R\to\mathcal L(X)$ satisfies
\[
 \limsup_{t\to0}\|C(t)-I\|<2
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \lim_{t\to0}\|C(t)-I\|=0.
\]
Immediately after Theorem~1.1 on PDF page~2 of arXiv:1402.1304 \cite{SZ},
they write that it remains open whether strong continuity can be removed.

\section*{Decisive answer}

Chojnacki's paper explicitly announces that it extends the two
Schwenninger--Zwart results to cosine families with no continuity assumption.
Theorem~3 on PDF page~8 states \cite{Chojnacki}:
\begin{quote}
Let $A$ be a normed algebra with unity $e$, and let
$c=\{c(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb R}$ be a cosine family in $A$ such that
$\limsup_{t\to0}\|c(t)-e\|<2$.  Then $\lim_{t\to0}c(t)=e$.
\end{quote}
Taking $A=\mathcal L(X)$ gives exactly the source conclusion, without strong
continuity, measurability, or any other regularity hypothesis.

Esterle's separate arXiv paper \cite{Esterle} explicitly identifies the same
Schwenninger--Zwart question, notes Chojnacki's affirmative answer, and gives
an independent short direct proof.  Its Theorem~3.2 on PDF page~6 proves the
same implication for a cosine function in a unital Banach algebra.

\section*{Status, scope, and search evidence}

This is a complete affirmative literature answer, not a new result of the
run.  Chojnacki proves a stronger normed-algebra theorem, while Esterle gives
an independent Banach-algebra proof.  Exact arXiv-id, title, quoted-question,
strong-continuity-removal, and general-cosine-function searches found these
explicit matches.  The local run indexes contained no prior packet for this
question; the existing scaled/global cosine-family packet addresses a
different problem.
