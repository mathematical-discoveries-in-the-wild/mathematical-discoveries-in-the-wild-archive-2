\begin{abstract}
Rabier asks whether every real-valued, densely continuous, quasiconvex
function on a separable Banach space is continuous and classically
G\^ateaux differentiable at the points of a dense subset. We prove that the
answer is yes. In the only unresolved region, the interior of the essential
infimum sublevel, we obtain the stronger conclusion that Fr\'echet
differentiability points with derivative zero are dense. The new ingredient
is a uniform relative-hole lemma for closed convex sets with empty interior.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source question and notation}

Let \(X\) be a separable Banach space and let \(f:X\to\R\) be real-valued,
quasiconvex, and densely continuous. Following \cite{source}, put
\[
 F_\alpha=\{x:f(x)<\alpha\},\qquad
 F'_\alpha=\{x:f(x)\le\alpha\},
\]
and let
\[
 m=\mathcal T\operatorname{-ess\,inf}_X f
 =\sup\{\alpha:F_\alpha\text{ is of first category}\}.
\]
The Introduction of \cite{source} asks whether every such \(f\) is continuous
and classically G\^ateaux differentiable at the points of a dense subset;
see Figure~\ref{fig:question}. Its Theorem~9.2 answers only after weakening
G\^ateaux differentiability to testing a residual set of directions.

The source settles all but the following case. Assume \(m>-\infty\) and
\(F'_m\) is of second category. Source Theorem~3.2(ii) says that
\[
 V:=\operatorname{int}F'_m\ne\varnothing.
\]
Since \(F'_m\) is convex, \(V\) is dense in \(F'_m\). Source Theorem~3.1 says
that \(F_\alpha\) is nowhere dense whenever \(\alpha<m\).

\section{Proof intuition}

Inside an arbitrary ball in \(V\), avoid the closure of one lower sublevel,
then a higher lower sublevel, and so on. Closed convex sets with empty
interior leave holes of a fixed relative size: an \(R\)-ball always contains
an \(R/8\)-ball at distance more than \(R/4\) from the set. We may therefore
nest holes with geometrically shrinking radii. If the selected levels
approach \(m\) quadratically faster than those radii shrink, their unique
common point \(x\) satisfies \(f(x)=m\), while every nearby \(y\) is trapped
between \(f(x)\) and the appropriate lower level. Dividing this vertical gap
by \(\|y-x\|\) gives a quantity tending to zero. Thus the construction yields
a Fr\'echet point, not merely directional differentiability.

\section{Uniform convex holes}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform convex-hole lemma]\label{lem:hole}
Let \(C\subset X\) be closed and convex with empty interior. Every closed
ball \(\overline B(a,R)\), \(R>0\), contains a closed ball
\(\overline B(b,R/8)\) such that
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(w,C)>R/4
 \quad\text{for every }w\in\overline B(b,R/8).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because \(C\) has empty interior, choose
\(z\in B(a,R/4)\setminus C\). Strong separation of the point \(z\) from the
closed convex set \(C\) gives \(\ell\in X^*\) with \(\|\ell\|=1\) and
\[
 \ell(z)>\sup_{c\in C}\ell(c).
\]
Choose \(v\in X\), \(\|v\|=1\), with \(\ell(v)>3/4\), and put
\(b=z+(R/2)v\). If \(\|w-b\|\le R/8\), then
\[
 \ell(w)>\ell(z)+\frac{3R}{8}-\frac R8
 >\sup_{c\in C}\ell(c)+\frac R4.
\]
Since \(\|\ell\|=1\), this implies \(\operatorname{dist}(w,C)>R/4\).
Moreover,
\[
 \|w-a\|<\frac R4+\frac R2+\frac R8=\frac{7R}{8}<R,
\]
so the new closed ball lies in the original one.
\end{proof}

\section{Dense Fr\'echet differentiability at the essential level}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hard}
Suppose \(m>-\infty\) and \(F'_m\) is of second category. Then the points
\(x\in V=\operatorname{int}F'_m\) at which \(f\) is Fr\'echet differentiable
with derivative zero are dense in \(V\), and hence dense in \(F'_m\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix any closed ball
\(B_0=\overline B(a_0,R_0)\subset V\). Set \(q=1/8\),
\(R_k=R_0q^k\), and, for \(k\ge1\),
\[
 \alpha_k=m-R_0q^{2k},\qquad \delta_k=m-\alpha_k=R_0q^{2k}.
\]
Then \(\alpha_k\uparrow m\). By source Theorem~3.1,
\(F_{\alpha_k}\) is nowhere dense. Hence
\[
 C_k:=\overline{F_{\alpha_k}}
\]
is closed, convex, and has empty interior.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:hole} inside \(B_{k-1}\) to obtain recursively a closed
ball \(B_k\subset B_{k-1}\), of radius \(R_k\), such that
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(B_k,C_k)>\frac{R_{k-1}}4=2R_k.
\]
The nested closed balls have radii tending to zero. Completeness gives a
unique point \(x\in\bigcap_{k\ge0}B_k\). Since \(x\notin C_k\),
\(f(x)\ge\alpha_k\) for every \(k\), and therefore \(f(x)\ge m\). On the
other hand, \(x\in B_0\subset V\subset F'_m\), so \(f(x)\le m\). Thus
\(f(x)=m\).

Let \(y\to x\), \(y\ne x\). Eventually \(y\in V\), hence \(f(y)\le m\).
Choose the unique \(k\ge0\) such that
\[
 R_{k+1}\le\|y-x\|<R_k.
\]
As \(y\to x\), this \(k\to\infty\), so we may assume \(k\ge1\). Because
\(x\in B_k\) and \(\operatorname{dist}(B_k,C_k)>2R_k\), the inequality
\(\|y-x\|<R_k\) gives \(y\notin C_k\). Consequently
\(f(y)\ge\alpha_k\), and
\[
 0\le
 \frac{f(x)-f(y)}{\|x-y\|}
 \le \frac{m-\alpha_k}{R_{k+1}}
 =\frac{R_0q^{2k}}{R_0q^{k+1}}
 =q^{k-1}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since \(f(y)\le f(x)\) near \(x\), this is precisely
\(f(y)-f(x)=o(\|y-x\|)\). Thus \(f\) is Fr\'echet differentiable at \(x\)
with derivative zero.

The initial ball \(B_0\subset V\) was arbitrary, so these points are dense
in \(V\). Finally, any convex set with nonempty interior is contained in the
closure of its interior; hence \(V\) is dense in \(F'_m\).
\end{proof}

\section{Full answer}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:full}
Every real-valued, densely continuous, quasiconvex function on a separable
Banach space is continuous and classically G\^ateaux differentiable at the
points of a dense subset.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \(m=-\infty\), source Corollary~4.3 gives Hadamard differentiability
outside an Aronszajn-null set. Suppose \(m>-\infty\). If \(F'_m\) is of
first category, source Theorem~5.2 gives Hadamard differentiability at the
points of a dense subset of \(X\).

It remains to consider the second-category case. Theorem~\ref{thm:hard}
gives a set \(D_0\) of Fr\'echet points dense in \(F'_m\). Source
Theorem~4.2 gives Hadamard differentiability on \(X\setminus F'_m\) except
at the points of an Aronszajn-null set. Source Theorem~3.2(iii) says that
\(X\setminus F'_m\) is semi-open; as observed immediately after source
Theorem~4.2, the resulting Hadamard points form a set \(D_1\) dense in
\(X\setminus F'_m\). Thus \(D_0\cup D_1\) is dense in \(X\).

Fr\'echet and Hadamard differentiability each imply continuity and classical
G\^ateaux differentiability, proving the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, novelty, and limitations}

The exact-arithmetic checker performs 30,003 audits of the hole constants,
distance margin, and rate identity
\(R_0q^{2k}/R_{k+1}=q^{k-1}\). These checks corroborate the bookkeeping but
do not replace the proof.

Four cheap run indexes and bounded exact-title, exact-question,
author-plus-question, and core-keyword searches found no duplicate or later
resolution through 11 August 2026. Because the decisive geometric lemma is
elementary, the possibility of unindexed folklore remains; novelty confidence
is moderate. Human review should focus on the strong-separation form used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:hole}, the distance margin in the nested construction, and the
assembly of the relative-density statements from \cite{source}.

The theorem answers the stated dense classical G\^ateaux question. It does
not assert a residual, Aronszajn-conull, or Haar-conull set of classical
G\^ateaux points in the hard case, nor dense Hadamard or Fr\'echet
differentiability on all of \(X\).
