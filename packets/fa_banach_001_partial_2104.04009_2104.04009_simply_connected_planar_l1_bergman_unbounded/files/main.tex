\section*{Source question}

The source is G.~M.~Dall'Ara, \emph{Around $L^1$ (un)boundedness of Bergman
and Szeg\H{o} projections}, arXiv:2104.04009, published in the
\emph{Journal of Functional Analysis} 283 (2022), Paper No.~109550.
It proves that the Bergman projection of every bounded smoothly bounded
domain in $\C^n$ is unbounded on $L^1$, and then asks whether the same is true
for every bounded domain in $\C^n$.

Here area measure is unnormalized Lebesgue measure $dA$, and
$\Berg_\Omega$ denotes the $L^2(\Omega)$ Bergman projection.  Boundedness on
$L^1(\Omega)$ means that the restriction to
$L^1(\Omega)\cap L^2(\Omega)$ admits a bounded extension to $L^1(\Omega)$.

\section*{Main result}

\begin{theorem}[finite-area simply connected domains]
\label{thm:main}
Let $\Omega\subset\C$ be a simply connected domain with
$0<A(\Omega)<\infty$.  Then $\Berg_\Omega$ is unbounded on
$L^1(\Omega)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}
Every bounded simply connected domain in $\C$, with completely arbitrary
boundary, has an $L^1$-unbounded Bergman projection.
\end{corollary}

The finite-area formulation is slightly stronger than the bounded-domain
subcase of the source question.  It includes non-Jordan domains, domains with
fractal boundary, and domains with no rectifiable boundary arc.

\section*{Proof intuition}

Let $\psi:\D\to\Omega$ be a Riemann map.  Conformal change of variables
turns $\Berg_\Omega$ into the ordinary disk Bergman projection acting on
$L^1(\D,w\,dA)$, where $w=|\psi'|$.  Endpoint boundedness would force every
weighted kernel column to satisfy
\[
  \int_{\D}\frac{w(z)}{\pi|1-z\overline a|^2}\,dA(z)
  \le Cw(a).                                             \tag{1}
\]

There is a two-step feedback contradiction.  First, the disk kernel is
bounded below everywhere, so (1) forces $w$ to have a positive global lower
bound.  Feeding that lower bound back into (1) and integrating the unweighted
kernel exactly forces
\[
  w(a)\gtrsim \frac{-\log(1-|a|^2)}{|a|^2},
\]
uniformly in the argument of $a$.  Thus $|\psi'|$ tends uniformly to infinity
on concentric circles.  But $\psi'$ never vanishes, so $1/\psi'$ is analytic;
the maximum principle would then force $1/\psi'$ to vanish identically.

\section*{Conformal transfer}

The disk Bergman kernel is
\[
  K_{\D}(z,u)=\frac{1}{\pi(1-z\overline u)^2}.
\]
Put $w=|\psi'|$ and define
\[
  Uf=(f\circ\psi)\psi'.
\]
The change-of-variables formula gives
\[
  \|Uf\|_{L^2(\D)}=\|f\|_{L^2(\Omega)},
  \qquad
  \|Uf\|_{L^1(\D,w\,dA)}=\|f\|_{L^1(\Omega)}.          \tag{2}
\]
The transformation rule for Bergman kernels gives, on $L^2$,
\[
  U\Berg_\Omega U^{-1}=\Berg_{\D}.                     \tag{3}
\]
Hence $L^1$-boundedness of $\Berg_\Omega$ would imply boundedness of
$\Berg_{\D}$ on $L^1(\D,w\,dA)$.

Since $\psi$ is injective, the area formula and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
  \int_{\D}|\psi'(z)|^2\,dA(z)=A(\Omega)<\infty,
  \qquad
  0<\int_{\D}w\,dA<\infty.                             \tag{4}
\]

\section*{The weighted endpoint obstruction}

\begin{lemma}[necessary column estimate]
\label{lem:column}
Let $w$ be positive and continuous on $\D$.  If $\Berg_{\D}$ is bounded on
$L^1(\D,w\,dA)$ with norm at most $C$, then for every $a\in\D$,
\[
  I_w(a):=\int_{\D}|K_{\D}(z,a)|w(z)\,dA(z)\le Cw(a).  \tag{5}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, let
\[
  h_\varepsilon(u)=\frac{\mathbf 1_{B(a,\varepsilon)}(u)}
  {A(B(a,\varepsilon))}.
\]
Then $h_\varepsilon\in L^2(\D)\cap L^1(\D,w\,dA)$,
\[
  \|h_\varepsilon\|_{L^1(w)}
  =\frac{1}{A(B(a,\varepsilon))}
    \int_{B(a,\varepsilon)}w(u)\,dA(u)\longrightarrow w(a),
\]
and $\Berg_{\D}h_\varepsilon(z)\to K_{\D}(z,a)$ pointwise.  The operator
bound and Fatou's lemma yield (5).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[zero-free analytic weights never work]
\label{thm:weighted}
Let $g$ be a zero-free analytic function on $\D$ such that
\[
  0<\int_{\D}|g|\,dA<\infty.
\]
Then $\Berg_{\D}$ is unbounded on $L^1(\D,|g|\,dA)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose the contrary, write $w=|g|$, and let $C$ be an operator bound.
Set
\[
  M=\int_{\D}w\,dA>0.
\]
Because $|1-z\overline a|\le2$ for $z,a\in\D$, we have
\[
  I_w(a)\ge \frac{M}{4\pi}.
\]
Combining this with (5) gives the uniform positive lower bound
\[
  w(a)\ge m:=\frac{M}{4\pi C}>0
  \qquad(a\in\D).                                      \tag{6}
\]

Use (6) once more inside the column integral.  For $a\ne0$, orthogonality of
the monomials (or direct angular integration) gives
\begin{align*}
 \frac1\pi\int_{\D}\frac{dA(z)}{|1-z\overline a|^2}
 &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a|^{2n}}{n+1}\\
 &=\frac{-\log(1-|a|^2)}{|a|^2}.                       \tag{7}
\end{align*}
Therefore (5)--(7) imply
\[
  w(a)\ge \frac{m}{C},
  \frac{-\log(1-|a|^2)}{|a|^2}.                        \tag{8}
\]
The right side tends to infinity with $|a|$, independently of the argument
of $a$.

Since $g$ has no zeros, $1/g$ is analytic on $\D$.  From (8), for
$0<r<1$,
\[
  \max_{|z|=r}\left|\frac1{g(z)}\right|
  \le \frac{C}{m},
       \frac{r^2}{-\log(1-r^2)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The maximum principle now gives
\[
  \left|\frac1{g(0)}\right|
  \le \max_{|z|=r}\left|\frac1{g(z)}\right|
  \longrightarrow0,
\]
which is impossible.  This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\section*{Proof of the main theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}]
Let $\psi:\D\to\Omega$ be a Riemann map.  Its derivative is zero-free,
and (4) shows that $|\psi'|$ is integrable.  If $\Berg_\Omega$ were bounded
on $L^1(\Omega)$, (2)--(3) would make $\Berg_{\D}$ bounded on
$L^1(\D,|\psi'|dA)$, contradicting Theorem \ref{thm:weighted}.
\end{proof}

\section*{Scope, search status, and novelty caution}

This completely removes boundary assumptions in the simply connected planar
finite-area case, but it does not answer the source question for multiply
connected planar domains or domains in $\C^n$ with $n\ge2$.  The earlier
rectifiable-boundary-arc version of this packet is strictly subsumed by
Theorem \ref{thm:main}.

Bounded searches through August 9, 2026 included the exact source question,
the source article and its citations, exact phrases combining simply
connected planar domains with strong $L^1$ Bergman boundedness, and recent
weighted and weak-type work.  No prior statement of Theorem \ref{thm:main}
or Theorem \ref{thm:weighted} was located.  This is a bounded novelty check,
not a claim of priority.  The recent paper \cite{GSW} treats weak-type
endpoint estimates on simply connected planar domains but does not state the
strong $L^1$ theorem proved here.
