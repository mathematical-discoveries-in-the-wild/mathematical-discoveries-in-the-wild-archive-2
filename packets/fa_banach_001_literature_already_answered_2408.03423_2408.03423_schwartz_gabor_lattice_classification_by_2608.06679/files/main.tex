\noindent\textbf{Status.} \texttt{literature\_already\_answered}.  This note
identifies an exact later resolution of the question in arXiv:2408.03423; it
does not claim a new theorem.  The answer is negative in general, and the
later paper gives a sharp if-and-only-if replacement.

\section*{The source question and its partial answer}
For a full-rank phase-space lattice $\Gamma\subset\mathbb R^{2d}$, Enstad,
Thiel and Vilalta ask whether
\begin{equation*}
 \covol(\Gamma)<1                                      \tag{1}
\end{equation*}
always implies the existence of $g\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^d)$ such that the
Gabor system $\{\pi(\gamma)g:\gamma\in\Gamma\}$ is a frame
\cite[Problem A]{ETV}.  The unresolved part is $d\ge2$ with $\Gamma$
rational for the symplectic form.

Write
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{int}}
 =\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\Omega(\gamma,\gamma')\in\mathbb Z
       \text{ for every }\gamma'\in\Gamma\},
 \qquad
 n_\Gamma=[\Gamma:\Gamma_{\mathrm{int}}]^{1/2}.
\]
The source proves existence under the stronger condition
\begin{equation*}
 \covol(\Gamma)<1-\frac{d-1}{n_\Gamma}.                \tag{2}
\end{equation*}
It explicitly reports that no Schwartz frame was then known for a lattice
violating (2).

\section*{Exact later classification}
For the symplectic adjoint lattice $\Gamma^\circ$, put
$\nu(\Gamma)=[\Gamma:\Gamma\cap\Gamma^\circ]^{1/2}$ whenever this index is
finite.  Thus, on the rational lattices of the source,
$\nu(\Gamma)=n_\Gamma$.  Caragea, Kolountzakis and Pfander prove the
following in a preprint posted August 7, 2026 \cite[Theorems 1.2 and
1.3]{CKP}.

\begin{theorem}[Caragea--Kolountzakis--Pfander]
A full-rank lattice $\Gamma\subset\mathbb R^{2d}$ admits a Schwartz function
$g$ generating a Parseval Gabor frame if and only if
\[
 \covol(\Gamma)<1
 \quad\text{and, when $\Gamma$ is symplectically rational,}\quad
 \nu(\Gamma)\ge \nu(\Gamma^\circ)+d.                 \tag{3}
\]
If either inequality fails, no window in the Feichtinger algebra
$\Szero(\mathbb R^d)$ can generate a Gabor frame.
\end{theorem}

This is stronger on the nonexistence side because
$\mathcal S(\mathbb R^d)\subset\Szero(\mathbb R^d)$.  It also gives Parseval
windows on the existence side, which of course are frames.

\section*{Why the classification exactly sharpens the source theorem}
The later paper proves, for a rational lattice,
\begin{equation*}
 \covol(\Gamma)=\frac{\nu(\Gamma^\circ)}{\nu(\Gamma)}. \tag{4}
\end{equation*}
Set $n=n_\Gamma=\nu(\Gamma)$ and $m=\nu(\Gamma^\circ)$.  Since $m,n$ are
integers, the source inequality (2) is equivalent to
\[
 \frac mn<1-\frac{d-1}{n}
 \iff m<n-d+1
 \iff m\le n-d
 \iff n\ge m+d.
\]
This is exactly (3).  Thus the source's sufficient condition was already the
sharp one; the later theorem supplies the missing necessity.

\section*{Concrete counterexample to the broad question}
Take $d=2$, use coordinates $(x_1,x_2,\omega_1,\omega_2)$, and let
\[
 \Gamma=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,\tfrac12)\mathbb Z^4.
\]
Then $\covol(\Gamma)=1/2<1$, while the later paper computes
\[
 \nu(\Gamma)=2,
 \qquad \nu(\Gamma^\circ)=1.
\]
Condition (3) would require $2\ge1+2$, which is false.  Therefore no
$g\in\Szero(\mathbb R^2)$, and in particular no Schwartz function, generates
a Gabor frame over this lattice.  This single lattice gives a full negative
answer to Problem A.  More generally, (3) classifies every rational and
non-rational lattice.

\section*{Verification scope}
The included PDFs and TeX sources record both sides of the implication.  In
the source, Problem A, Definition B, Theorem C, and the concluding remarks
give the question and (2).  In the later preprint, Theorems 1.2, 1.3 and 1.4,
Examples 1.5(ii), and equation (1.8) give the classification, the stronger
$\Szero$ obstruction, the displayed counterexample, and identity (4).
Because \cite{CKP} is a very recent arXiv preprint, this packet records an
exact literature resolution rather than an independently refereed
verification of its long proof.
