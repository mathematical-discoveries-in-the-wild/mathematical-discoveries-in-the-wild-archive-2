\section*{Source Question and Status}

Gargiulo and Ok define the order-preserving extension constants
$e_{k,\uparrow}(X,Y)$ and $e_{\uparrow}(X,Y)$ in their concluding section.
For $\R^n$ with Euclidean metric and coordinatewise order, they obtain the
coordinatewise upper bound $\sqrt n$ and ask whether it is best; they also
ask for finite-$k$ estimates \cite[p.~12]{GO}.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{figures/open_question_crop.png}
\end{center}

This packet proves that $\sqrt n$ is exactly sharp even for finite radial
metric posets, and that the constant has already saturated when the datum
has $2n$ points.  It also records a necessary scope correction: the scalar
identity used to deduce the upper bound requires the order of $X$ to be
radial.  Without radiality there is no finite dimension-only bound.  Status:
\emph{candidate full solution, likely valid}, subject to human review.

\section*{Definitions and the Coordinatewise Upper Bound}

For nonempty $S\subset X$, let $e_\uparrow(X,S,Y)$ be the least $K$ such
that every order-preserving 1-Lipschitz $f:S\to Y$ has an order-preserving
$K$-Lipschitz extension to $X$, and put
\[
 e_{k,\uparrow}(X,Y)=\sup_{0<|S|\leq k}e_\uparrow(X,S,Y),
 \qquad e_\uparrow(X,Y)=e_{\infty,\uparrow}(X,Y).
\]
The scalar monotone extension theorem says that
$e_\uparrow(X,\R)=1$ exactly when the order on $X$ is radial
\cite[Sec.~2.2]{GO}; see also \cite{Ok}.  Hence, if $X$ is radial and
$f:S\to\R^n$ is order-preserving and 1-Lipschitz, each coordinate of $f$
has an order-preserving 1-Lipschitz extension $F_j:X\to\R$.  Then
\[
 F=(F_1,\ldots,F_n),\qquad
 \|F(x)-F(y)\|_2\leq\sqrt n\,d_X(x,y),
 \tag{1}\label{eq:upper}
\]
so $e_\uparrow(X,\R^n)\leq\sqrt n$ for every radial $X$.

\section*{Sharpness and Finite-Set Saturation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:sharp}
For every $n\geq1$ there is a finite radial metric poset $X_n$ such that
\[
 e_{2n,\uparrow}(X_n,\R^n)
 =e_\uparrow(X_n,\R^n)=\sqrt n.
\]
More generally, for $1\leq m\leq n$,
\[
 e_{2m,\uparrow}(X_n,\R^n)\geq\sqrt m.
\]
Consequently, the $\sqrt n$ upper bound in \eqref{eq:upper} is best possible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 X_n=\{\top,\bot\}\cup
     \{p_j^+,p_j^-:1\leq j\leq n\}.
\]
Give $X_n$ the discrete metric: distinct points have distance one.  Order it
as a height-three poset, with
\[
 \top\succ p_j^\pm\succ\bot\quad(1\leq j\leq n),
\]
and no comparabilities between distinct middle points.  Every partial order
on a discrete metric space is radial: whenever either radial inequality has
a nontrivial premise, the two distances being compared are both one.

Let $S_n=\{p_j^\pm:1\leq j\leq n\}$ and, with $(e_j)$ the standard basis of
$\R^n$, define
\[
 f(p_j^+)=\tfrac12e_j,
 \qquad f(p_j^-)=-\tfrac12e_j.
 \tag{2}\label{eq:data}
\]
The set $S_n$ is an antichain, so $f$ is order-preserving.  It is
1-Lipschitz: opposite points on the same axis have image distance one, and
all other distinct image pairs have distance $1/\sqrt2$ or less.

Let $F:X_n\to\R^n$ be any order-preserving extension.  For each $j$,
monotonicity and \eqref{eq:data} give
\[
 F(\top)_j\geq\tfrac12,
 \qquad F(\bot)_j\leq-\tfrac12.
\]
Thus every coordinate of $F(\top)-F(\bot)$ is at least one, and
\[
 \|F(\top)-F(\bot)\|_2\geq\sqrt n.
\]
Since $d_{X_n}(\top,\bot)=1$, every extension has Lipschitz constant at
least $\sqrt n$.  Hence $e_{2n,\uparrow}(X_n,\R^n)\geq\sqrt n$.  The reverse
inequality follows from radiality of $X_n$ and \eqref{eq:upper}, proving both
equalities.

For $m\leq n$, use only the $2m$ data points $p_j^\pm$, $1\leq j\leq m$,
with the same formula.  The first $m$ coordinates of the top-bottom
difference are forced to be at least one, giving the lower bound $\sqrt m$.
\end{proof}

In particular, for every $k\geq2n$,
$e_{k,\uparrow}(X_n,\R^n)=\sqrt n$.  The construction supplies the explicit
finite-$k$ lower estimate
\[
 e_{k,\uparrow}(X_n,\R^n)
 \geq \sqrt{\min\{n,\lfloor k/2\rfloor\}}
 \qquad(k\geq2).
\]

\section*{Why Radiality Cannot Be Dropped}

The source paragraph says parenthetically that $X$ is ``any metric poset,''
but its premise $e_\uparrow(X,\R)=1$ is equivalent to radiality.  The
following shows that this is not merely a technical distinction.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:unbounded}
For every $n\geq1$ there is a metric poset $X$ with
\[
 e_{2,\uparrow}(X,\R^n)=\infty.
\]
Thus no bound depending only on $n$ holds for arbitrary metric posets.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $0<\varepsilon<1$, let $X_\varepsilon=\{a,b,c\}$, with
\[
 d(a,b)=d(a,c)=1,\qquad d(b,c)=\varepsilon,
\]
and with $c\succ a$ as its only strict order relation.  On $S=\{a,b\}$ put
$f(a)=e_1$ and $f(b)=0$.  The two points of $S$ are incomparable and their
distance is one, so $f$ is order-preserving and 1-Lipschitz.  Every
order-preserving extension satisfies $F(c)_1\geq1$, hence
\[
 \Lip(F)\geq
 \frac{\|F(c)-F(b)\|_2}{d(c,b)}\geq\frac1\varepsilon.
 \tag{3}\label{eq:blowup}
\]

Now take the disjoint union of $X_{1/r}$, $r=2,3,\ldots$, retain the order
inside each component, and put distance two between distinct components.
This is a metric poset (indeed, its topology is discrete).  Applying
\eqref{eq:blowup} in its $r$th component shows that its two-point extension
constant is infinite.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:unbounded} corrects only the scope of the sentence; it
does not weaken Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp}, whose extremal domains are radial.

\section*{Human Review Recommendation}

Verify that the intended upper bound assumes radiality, and check the two
order inequalities forcing the images of $\top$ and $\bot$.  The latter
gives a particularly short audit of sharpness.
