\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Literature-implied answer: a full negative answer to automatic extension in
the standard, holomorphic-functional-calculus sense.  This is not claimed as
a new theorem.

\section*{Source question}
Blower and Doust interpret their hypergroup representation as a functional
calculus
\[
 \Phi_A:\Aalg\longrightarrow\Lop(E),\qquad \Phi_A(\psi)=\psi(A),
\]
where \(\Aalg\) is the range of the hypergroup Fourier transform.  Remark
4.2 on printed page 11 asks whether \(\Phi_A\) extends to a bounded algebra
homomorphism
\[
 H^\infty(\Sigma_{\omega_0})\longrightarrow\Lop(E),
 \qquad \Sigma_\omega=\{z\in\mathbb C:|\operatorname{Im}z|<\omega\}.
\]

\section*{Counterexample}
\begin{theorem}
For every \(q\in(1,\infty)\setminus\{2\}\), there is a contractive strongly
continuous cosine family on \(E=L^q(\R)\) to which the Mehler--Fock
operational calculus of Blower--Doust applies with \(\omega_0=1/2\), but whose
map \(\Phi_A\) has no bounded extension to
\(H^\infty(\Sigma_{1/2})\) compatible with the holomorphic functional
calculus.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(A\) be the closed Fourier-multiplier operator with symbol \(\xi\):
\((Au)^\wedge(\xi)=\xi\widehat u(\xi)\), on its natural domain in
\(L^q(\R)\).  If \(S_tu(x)=u(x-t)\), then
\[
 \cos(tA)=\frac12(S_t+S_{-t}),
 \qquad \|\cos(tA)\|\leq1.
\]
Consequently the hypothesis
\(\|\cos(tA)\|\leq\kappa\cosh(t/2)\) of
Blower--Doust Proposition 6.2 holds with \(\kappa=1\).  Their proposition
therefore produces the Mehler--Fock hypergroup operational calculus, whose
Fourier-transform range is contained in \(H^\infty(\Sigma_{1/2})\).

Now let \(T=\exp A\), the sectorial Fourier multiplier with symbol
\(e^\xi\).  Cowling--Doust--McIntosh--Yagi, Example 5.2 and Lemma 5.3,
prove that, when \(q\neq2\), this \(T\) has no bounded
\(H^\infty(S_\theta)\) functional calculus for any \(\theta>0\).  In their
example \(T^{iu}\) is translation by \(u\), so this is exactly the logarithmic
pair \(A=\log T\) used above.

Suppose that the extension asked for in Remark 4.2 existed as a bounded strip
functional calculus for \(A\).  Given \(m\in H^\infty(S_{1/2})\), put
\(b(z)=m(e^z)\).  The exponential maps \(\Sigma_{1/2}\) into
\(S_{1/2}\), hence
\[
 b\in H^\infty(\Sigma_{1/2}),\qquad
 \|b\|_\infty\leq\|m\|_\infty.
\]
Compatibility of the holomorphic calculi gives
\[
 b(A)=m(e^A)=m(T).
\]
The assumed bounded strip calculus would thus imply
\(\|m(T)\|\leq C\|m\|_\infty\) for every such \(m\), giving \(T\) a
bounded \(H^\infty(S_{1/2})\) calculus.  This contradicts the cited Lemma
5.3.
\end{proof}

\section*{Why the obstruction is sharp enough}
The supporting proof constructs bounded holomorphic strip functions by
Gaussian interpolation of arbitrary bounded sequences on \(\mathbb Z\).  If
all those functions acted boundedly on \(L^q(\R)\), de Leeuw restriction would
make every bounded sequence a Fourier multiplier on \(L^q(\mathbb T)\), which
is possible only for \(q=2\).  Thus the failure is a genuine Banach-space
multiplier obstruction, despite the contractive cosine bound.

\section*{Scope and interpretation}
The theorem disproves an automatic extension statement for the hypotheses of
Remark 4.2.  It does not preclude positive extensions on Hilbert spaces or for
operators satisfying additional square-function or multiplier estimates.

The phrase ``functional calculus map'' is used in its standard
resolvent-compatible sense, as signaled by the source formula
\(\Phi_A(\psi)=\psi(A)\).  A purely abstract algebra homomorphism with no
compatibility or convergence axiom would not be an operator functional
calculus and is outside this identification.

\section*{Literature and novelty check}
The exact arXiv id and title, the quoted sentence from Remark 4.2, and searches
combining ``finite propagation speed'', ``bounded \(H^\infty\) calculus'', and
``cosine family'' were checked.  No later paper explicitly advertising a
resolution of the remark was found.  Nevertheless this result is classified
as literature-implied, not new: the decisive 1996 lemma predates the source,
and the source itself cites the same paper in its earlier multiplier warning.
