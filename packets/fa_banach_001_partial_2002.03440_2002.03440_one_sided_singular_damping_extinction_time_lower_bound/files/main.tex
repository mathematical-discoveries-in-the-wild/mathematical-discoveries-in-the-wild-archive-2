\textbf{Status: candidate partial result, likely valid.}
For the Dirichlet damped wave equation on the unit interval, no damping that
is regular away from at most one endpoint can make every energy solution
extinct before time $2$.  Thus the extinction time $2$ of the critical
damping $a(x)=1/x$ is optimal throughout this one-sided-singularity class.
The unrestricted problem remains open when critical nonintegrable damping is
allowed at both endpoints or in the interior.}}
\end{center}

\section{Source problem}

Freitas, Hefti, and Siegl study
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wave}
 u_{tt}+2a(x)u_t=u_{xx},\qquad 0<x<1,\qquad
 u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0,
\end{equation}
on the energy space
$\Hh=H^1_0(0,1)\times L^2(0,1)$~\cite{FHS}.
For $a(x)=1/x$, every solution vanishes after time $2$.  At the end of
Section~4 (arXiv PDF page 10), they ask:

\begin{quote}
``Are there damping terms which will cause finite time extinction to occur
for all solutions for all time larger than some $T$ which is now smaller
than $2$ (on an interval with unit length)?''
\end{quote}

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{The second open problem in Section~4 of arXiv:2002.03440,
  PDF page 10.}
\end{figure}

For a strongly continuous solution semigroup $S_a(t)$, saying that every
solution is zero for all $t>T$ is equivalent to $S_a(T)=0$: strong
continuity supplies the endpoint once $S_a(t)=0$ for $t>T$.

\section{Result and scope}

Call $a$ \emph{one-sided admissible} if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:class}
 a\in C^1((0,1]),\qquad a\geq0,\qquad
 \sup_{0<x\leq1}x a(x)<\infty,
\end{equation}
and use the standard maximal-dissipative (form) realization of
\eqref{eq:wave} on $\Hh$.  The Hardy estimate gives the same energy
realization used for $\alpha/x$ in~\cite{FHS}; the only fact about this
realization needed below is ordinary local finite propagation on every
strip $[\delta,1]\times[0,T]$, where $a$ is bounded and smooth.  The theorem
therefore also applies to any broader energy realization that agrees locally
with the classical equation away from $x=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Two-traversal lower bound]\label{thm:main}
If $a$ is one-sided admissible, then
\[
 S_a(T)\neq0\qquad(0<T<2).
\]
Consequently, a universal finite extinction time in this class is at least
$2$.
\end{theorem}

The reflected statement holds when $a$ may be singular only at $x=1$.
The growth bound in \eqref{eq:class} is a convenient sufficient condition
for the energy realization, not the mechanism behind the lower bound.  The
proof only uses that $a$ is integrable along the broken characteristic before
it reaches the singular endpoint.

\begin{corollary}[Sharpness of the known example]\label{cor:sharp}
For $a(x)=1/x$, the universal extinction time is exactly $2$.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives the lower bound, while the extinction
result recalled and reproved in~\cite{FHS} gives the upper bound.

\section{Proof intuition}

Write the wave as two first-order travelling components.  Put
\[
 p=u_t+u_x,\qquad q=u_t-u_x.
\]
Then $p$ travels left, $q$ travels right, and damping couples them without
changing their characteristic speeds.  Start with energy data for which
$q$ has one jump at a point $x_0>0$ and $p$ is continuous.  Along the
right-going ray, the jump is multiplied by an exponential of a finite
damping integral, so it cannot disappear.  At the regular endpoint $x=1$,
the Dirichlet condition reflects it into a nonzero jump of $p$.  That jump
then travels left and remains nonzero until it reaches the only possible
singularity at $x=0$, at time $2-x_0$.

Given any $T<2$, choose $x_0<2-T$.  The reflected jump is still in the
interior at time $T$, so the state cannot be zero.  The key point is not an
energy estimate: it is the impossibility of annihilating a wavefront by a
finite damping integral.

\section{Jump propagation}

The following elementary lemma records the local mechanism.  All jumps are
differences of the two one-sided traces.

\begin{lemma}[Characteristic jump law]\label{lem:jump}
On a region where $a$ is locally integrable and a solution of
\eqref{eq:wave} is piecewise $C^1$, the Riemann invariants satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:riemann}
 p_t-p_x=-a(p+q),\qquad q_t+q_x=-a(p+q).
\end{equation}
If initially $q$ has a jump $J$ at $x_0$ and $p$ is continuous there, then,
until the ray $x=x_0+t$ meets the boundary,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rightamp}
 [p]=0,\qquad
 [q](t)=J\exp\!\left(-\int_0^t a(x_0+s)\,ds\right).
\end{equation}
At $x=1$ the jump reflects into $p$ with the opposite sign.  On the outgoing
ray $x=2-x_0-t$, its amplitude is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leftamp}
 [p](t)=-J\exp\!\left(
 -\int_{x_0}^{1}a(s)\,ds
 -\int_{2-x_0-t}^{1}a(s)\,ds\right),
 \qquad 1-x_0<t<2-x_0.
\end{equation}
In particular, every displayed amplitude is nonzero when the integrals are
finite.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:riemann} follows directly from
$u_t=(p+q)/2$ and $u_x=(p-q)/2$.  Across the right-going characteristic
$x-x_0-t=0$, a jump of $p$ would contribute a Dirac mass with coefficient
$-2[p]$ to $p_t-p_x$.  The right side of \eqref{eq:riemann} has no Dirac
mass, so $[p]=0$.  Taking ordinary trace differences in the equation for
$q$ now gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}[q](t)=-a(x_0+t)[q](t),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:rightamp}.

For a piecewise classical Dirichlet solution, $u(1,t)=0$ implies
$u_t(1,t)=0$ and hence $p(1,t)+q(1,t)=0$.  The time jump arriving in $q$
therefore creates the opposite time jump in the boundary value of $p$.
Repeating the preceding calculation on the left-going characteristic gives
the second exponential in \eqref{eq:leftamp}.  This jump calculus is local;
standard characteristic approximation extends it to the corresponding
piecewise-smooth energy solution.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

Fix $0<T<2$.  Choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xchoice}
 0<x_0<\min\{1,2-T\},\qquad x_0\neq1-T.
\end{equation}
The excluded value merely prevents the observed time from being the instant
when the jump is at the boundary.

We first construct globally legitimate energy data with exactly the desired
local jump.  Choose a smooth function $\chi$ on $[x_0,1]$ which equals $1$
near $x_0$ and vanishes near $1$.  Next choose a unit-integral function
\[
 \psi\in C_c^\infty((x_0/4,x_0/2)),\qquad \int_0^1\psi=1,
\]
and put
\[
 c=\int_{x_0}^1\chi(s)\,ds,\qquad
 q_0(x)=\mathbf 1_{(x_0,1)}(x)\chi(x)-c\psi(x),\qquad p_0(x)=0.
\]
Then $q_0$ has a single jump of size $1$ at $x_0$, is smooth elsewhere,
and has integral zero.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:data}
 u_0(x)=-\frac12\int_0^xq_0(s)\,ds,
 \qquad u_1(x)=\frac12q_0(x).
\end{equation}
Thus $u_0\in H^1_0(0,1)$, $u_1\in L^2(0,1)$, and the corresponding initial
invariants are precisely $p_0=0$ and $q_0$.

The broken ray issued from the jump is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ray}
 \gamma(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 x_0+t,&0\leq t\leq1-x_0,\\
 2-x_0-t,&1-x_0\leq t<2-x_0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:xchoice}, $T<2-x_0$ and $\gamma(T)>0$; moreover
$\gamma(T)\neq1$.  The entire ray segment up to time $T$ lies in
$[\delta,1]$ for some $\delta>0$.  Hence $a$ is bounded there, both damping
integrals in Lemma~\ref{lem:jump} are finite, and the appropriate invariant
has a nonzero jump at the interior point $\gamma(T)$.  Therefore
\[
 S_a(T)(u_0,u_1)\neq(0,0).
\]
Since $T<2$ was arbitrary, the theorem follows.  \qed

\begin{remark}[The critical example]
For $a(x)=1/x$, the incoming amplitude in \eqref{eq:rightamp} is
$Jx_0/(x_0+t)$.  After reflection, \eqref{eq:leftamp} becomes
\[
 [p](t)=-Jx_0(2-x_0-t),
 \]
which is nonzero until the ray reaches $x=0$ at time $2-x_0$.  Letting
$x_0\downarrow0$ displays geometrically why the sharp universal threshold is
$2$ even though each fixed tracked jump dies slightly earlier.
\end{remark}

\section{Focused upgrade attempts}

Because the characteristic theorem is a substantial partial answer, eight
focused attempts were made to upgrade it to the unrestricted question.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.06\textwidth}p{0.31\textwidth}p{0.55\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
No. & Route & Outcome \\
\midrule
1 & Isolated wavefront & Gave the nonvanishing exponential jump law. \\
2 & Energy-compatible data & The correction in \eqref{eq:data} produces a genuine $H^1_0\times L^2$ state without adding another jump. \\
3 & Boundary reflection & Extended survival from one crossing to the full broken-ray time $2-x_0$. \\
4 & Rough coefficients & The proof only needs a finite line integral along the ray; the packet states a conservative smooth-away-from-zero class to keep the realization unambiguous. \\
5 & Two endpoint poles & The candidate $a=1/x+1/(1-x)$ defeats this ray: the reflection point itself has infinite damping.  Its spectral ODE did not yield a nilpotent semigroup or a $T<2$ formula. \\
6 & Spectral determinant & Nilpotence forces empty spectrum, but classifying zero-free entire Dirichlet determinants for arbitrary critical singularities is an unresolved new problem. \\
7 & Darboux/gauge factorization & A single exponential-affine factorization forces a one-pole logarithmic derivative $a=\pm h'/h$; it does not cover two poles or interior barriers. \\
8 & Interior absorbers and literature threshold & A critical interior singularity requires a nontrivial choice of energy trace/interface realization.  Existing backward-uniqueness theorems exclude broad subcritical classes but do not settle this critical regime. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The remaining full problem is therefore concentrated in damping with a
nonintegrable critical barrier at both endpoints or in the interior.  Neither
the characteristic proof nor the located literature classifies the resulting
singular scattering realization.  Continuing without such a new theorem had
low probability of producing a rigorous full answer.

\section{Verification and novelty status}

The proof was audited at the following points: the factor of $a$ in
\eqref{eq:riemann}; the distributional coefficient forcing $[p]=0$; the
reflection sign; both exponential integrals; the zero-integral correction in
the initial data; the strict inequalities in \eqref{eq:xchoice}; and the
exact $a=1/x$ amplitude.  The accompanying script checks the broken-ray
geometry and the explicit critical amplitude on a grid of $399$ test times.
Those computations are sanity checks, not part of the proof.

A bounded novelty search on 13 August 2026 covered the local run indexes, the
exact source title and quoted question, ``singular damping extinction time
smaller than 2,'' ``minimal extinction time,'' the arXiv record through 2026,
and later work citing the critical example.  Kleinhenz and Wang prove optimal
abstract backward uniqueness under a subcritical unboundedness threshold in
arXiv:2310.19911~\cite{KW23}; their later Theorem~4 rules out finite-time
extinction for normally $L^p$ damping, $p>1$~\cite{KW26}.  Both explicitly
contrast their result with the critical $2/x$ example.  Neither gives a
minimal-time theorem in the critical one-sided class or answers the
two-sided/interior-singular case.  No later answer to the exact $T<2$ question
was found.  Novelty is plausible but not certified, and priority is not
claimed; the argument may be implicit in standard one-dimensional
propagation theory.

\section{Limitations and review recommendation}

This is not a full answer to either open problem in~\cite{FHS}.  It does not
address the norm-constrained optimization question.  For the second problem,
it does not rule out a damping with critical nonintegrable singularities at
both endpoints or at an interior interface, and it does not construct such a
damping.  It also does not claim a general semigroup construction beyond the
standard one-sided form class \eqref{eq:class}.

Human review should focus first on the local-to-global use of the
piecewise-smooth energy solution in Lemma~\ref{lem:jump}, then on the boundary
reflection step.  Once those standard hyperbolic facts are accepted, every
remaining step is explicit.  The best route to a full result would be a
classification of maximal-dissipative interface conditions for critical
interior/two-endpoint singularities, followed by a zero-free spectral-
determinant or characteristic-scattering analysis.
