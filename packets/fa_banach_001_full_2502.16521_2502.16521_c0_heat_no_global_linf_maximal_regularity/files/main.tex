\section{The question}

The source is Sebastian Kr\'ol, Mieczys\l aw Masty\l o, and Jaros\l aw
Sarnowski, \emph{Maximal regularity estimates for the abstract Cauchy
problems}, arXiv:2502.16521v1 \cite{KMS2025}.  On PDF page 40, Remark 3.4(c)
asks:
\begin{quote}
\emph{We do not know whether for the negative Laplacian
$\mathcal A:=-\Delta$ on $\mathcal X=C_0(\mathbb R^n)$ its part in
$X:=(\mathcal X,D_{\mathcal A})_{\theta,\infty}$,
$\theta\in(0,1)$, has $L^\infty$-m.r. on the whole $\mathbb R_+$.}
\end{quote}
Here $\cA=-\Delta$ has domain
\[
 \D(\cA)=\{f\in C_0(\R^n):-\Delta f\in C_0(\R^n)
                 \text{ in the distributional sense}\},
\]
and $H_t=e^{-t\cA}=e^{t\Delta}$ denotes the heat semigroup.  If
\[
 X_\theta=(C_0(\R^n),\D(\cA))_{\theta,\infty},
\]
the part $A$ of $\cA$ in $X_\theta$ is given by
\[
 \D(A)=\{f\in \D(\cA)\cap X_\theta:\cA f\in X_\theta\},
 \qquad Af=\cA f.
\]

\section{Proof intuition}

A single dilated bump cannot disprove maximal regularity: both sides of the
relevant semigroup characterization scale in the same way.  The obstruction
appears after stacking many \emph{nested} low-frequency bumps.  At the common
center their values add, so the norm of the stack grows like the number of
scales.  At any fixed heat time, however, only the bumps whose spatial scale
matches that time contribute substantially to $tAH_t$; the other scales form
two convergent geometric tails.  Thus $\sup_t\|tAH_t f\|$ remains uniformly
bounded.

The interpolation space with exponent $\infty$ has a non-dense generator,
so one cannot immediately invoke the standard Kalton--Portal
characterization.  This is resolved by passing to the closure of the domain.
Global $L^\infty$-maximal regularity would pass to this closed subspace, where
the restricted heat semigroup is strongly continuous.  Kalton--Portal then
turns the multiscale construction into a contradiction.

\section{Preliminaries}

The semigroup representation of the real interpolation norm used in the
source gives the equivalent norm
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thermic}
 \|f\|_{\theta,*}
 :=\|f\|_\infty+
   \sup_{s>0}s^{1-\theta}\|\cA H_s f\|_\infty
 \qquad (f\in X_\theta).
\end{equation}
Maximal regularity and the Kalton--Portal inequality are unchanged by an
equivalent renorming, so we use \eqref{eq:thermic} throughout.

We first isolate the non-dense-domain step.

\begin{lemma}[passage to the closure of the domain]\label{lem:closure}
Let $A$ be a closed operator on a Banach space $X$, and put
$Y=\closure{\D(A)}^X$.  Let $A_0$ be the part of $A$ in $Y$.  Suppose that
$-A_0$ generates the restriction to $Y$ of the semigroup generated by $-A$.
If $A$ has $L^\infty$-maximal regularity on $\R_+$, then so does $A_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take $f\in L^\infty(\R_+;Y)$ and let $u$ be the strong solution in $X$ of
$u'+Au=f$, $u(0)=0$.  Since $u(t)\in\D(A)\subset Y$ for almost every $t$
and $u$ is continuous as an $X$-valued function, closedness of $Y$ gives
$u(t)\in Y$ for every $t$.  The derivative of an absolutely continuous
$X$-valued function whose values lie in the closed subspace $Y$ belongs to
$Y$ almost everywhere: this follows directly from the difference quotients.
Hence $u'(t)\in Y$ and
\[
 Au(t)=f(t)-u'(t)\in Y
\]
almost everywhere.  Thus $u(t)\in\D(A_0)$ almost everywhere and it is the
strong solution for $A_0$.  The $L^\infty$ estimate is the same because $Y$
has the inherited norm, and uniqueness in $Y$ follows from uniqueness in
$X$.
\end{proof}

We will also use a standard geometric-sum observation.

\begin{lemma}[dyadic profiles]\label{lem:dyadic}
If $a,b>0$ and $q>1$, then
\[
 \sup_{v>0}\sum_{j=1}^\infty
 \min\bigl\{(v/q^j)^a,(v/q^j)^{-b}\bigr\}<\infty.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Split the indices at the last $j$ for which $v/q^j\geq1$.  On either side
the summands are bounded by a geometric progression, with ratios $q^{-b}$
and $q^{-a}$, respectively.  The same proof covers the cases in which one of
the two index sets is empty.
\end{proof}

\section{The multiscale obstruction}

Choose $0\leq\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\R^n)$ with $\varphi(0)=1$.  For
$m=0,1,2$ put
\[
 F_m(u)=\|\cA^mH_u\varphi\|_\infty,\qquad u>0.
\]
There are constants $C_m$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heatprofile}
 F_m(u)\leq C_m\min\{1,u^{-m-n/2}\},\qquad u>0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, for $0<u\leq1$, commutation with $\cA^m$ and heat contractivity give
$F_m(u)\leq\|\cA^m\varphi\|_\infty$.  For $u\geq1$, convolution with the
$m$-th Laplacian of the Gaussian heat kernel gives
\[
 F_m(u)\leq\|\cA^m k_u\|_\infty\|\varphi\|_1
 \leq C_m u^{-m-n/2}.
\]

Set
\[
 R_j=2^j,\qquad r_j=R_j^2=4^j,\qquad
 \varphi_j(x)=\varphi(x/R_j),\qquad
 x_N=\sum_{j=1}^N\varphi_j.
\]
Every $\varphi_j$ belongs to $C_c^\infty(\R^n)$, and both it and all its
Laplacians belong to $X_\theta$.  Hence $x_N\in\D(A)$.  On the other hand,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 \|x_N\|_{\theta,*}\geq\|x_N\|_\infty
 \geq x_N(0)=N.
\end{equation}

Heat scaling gives, for $m=0,1,2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaling}
 \|\cA^mH_t\varphi_j\|_\infty
 =r_j^{-m}F_m(t/r_j).
\end{equation}
The base part of the thermic norm of $tAH_tx_N$ is therefore bounded by
\begin{align}
 \|tAH_tx_N\|_\infty
 &\leq\sum_{j=1}^N (t/r_j)F_1(t/r_j)\notag\\
 &\leq C\sum_{j=1}^N
 \min\{t/r_j,(t/r_j)^{-n/2}\}\leq C,             \label{eq:base}
\end{align}
uniformly in $t>0$ and $N$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:dyadic}.

For the homogeneous part, fix $s,t>0$.  Semigroup commutation and
\eqref{eq:scaling} yield
\begin{align*}
 s^{1-\theta}\|\cA H_s[tAH_t\varphi_j]\|_\infty
 &=s^{1-\theta}t r_j^{-2}
   F_2((s+t)/r_j).
\end{align*}
Put $u=(s+t)/r_j$.  Since
$s^{1-\theta}t\leq(s+t)^{2-\theta}$ and $r_j\geq1$, the last expression is
at most
\begin{align*}
 r_j^{-\theta}u^{2-\theta}F_2(u)
 &\leq C\min\{u^{2-\theta},u^{-n/2-\theta}\}.
\end{align*}
Both exponents in Lemma~\ref{lem:dyadic} are positive.  Summing over $j$ and
then taking the supremum over $s$ proves, together with \eqref{eq:base}, that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform}
 \sup_{t>0}\|tAH_tx_N\|_{\theta,*}\leq C_{n,\theta,\varphi}
 \qquad\text{for every }N.
\end{equation}

The large-time term in the Kalton--Portal condition vanishes.  For each
fixed $j$,
\[
 \|H_t\varphi_j\|_\infty
 \leq C R_j^n t^{-n/2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Moreover, once $t\geq r_j$, \eqref{eq:heatprofile} gives
\begin{align*}
 \sup_{s>0}s^{1-\theta}\|\cA H_{s+t}\varphi_j\|_\infty
 &\leq C\sup_{s>0}s^{1-\theta}r_j^{-1}
       ((s+t)/r_j)^{-1-n/2}\\
 &\leq C_{n,\theta}R_j^n t^{-\theta-n/2}
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{align*}
Since $N$ is fixed, summing proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vanish}
 \lim_{t\to\infty}\|H_tx_N\|_{\theta,*}=0.
\end{equation}

\section{Full negative answer}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $n\geq1$ and every $\theta\in(0,1)$, the part $A$ of
$\cA=-\Delta$ in
\[
 X_\theta=(C_0(\R^n),\D(\cA))_{\theta,\infty}
\]
does not have $L^\infty$-maximal regularity on $\R_+$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose to the contrary that $A$ has global $L^\infty$-maximal regularity.
Let
\[
 Y=\closure{\D(A)}^{X_\theta}
\]
with the inherited thermic norm, and let $A_0$ be the part of $A$ in $Y$.
The source proves that $A$ generates the bounded analytic heat semigroup on
$X_\theta$ and that its restriction to $Y$ is a bounded analytic
$C_0$-semigroup with generator $-A_0$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:closure}, $A_0$
has $L^\infty$-maximal regularity on $\R_+$.

The Kalton--Portal characterization \cite[Theorem 3.7]{KaltonPortal2008},
also recorded as Lemma 2.13 of the source, now supplies a constant $C$ such
that every $y\in Y$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KP}
 \|y\|_{\theta,*}
 \leq C\sup_{t>0}\|tA_0H_ty\|_{\theta,*}
       +\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|H_ty\|_{\theta,*}.
\end{equation}
Each $x_N$ lies in $\D(A)\subset Y$.  Applying \eqref{eq:KP} to $x_N$ and
using \eqref{eq:lower}, \eqref{eq:uniform}, and \eqref{eq:vanish} gives
\[
 N\leq C C_{n,\theta,\varphi}
\]
for every $N$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[answer to Remark 3.4(c)]
The answer to the question in arXiv:2502.16521 is no for every dimension and
every interpolation parameter covered by the question.
\end{corollary}

\section{Scope, novelty, and verification}

The proof concerns the \emph{big} real interpolation space with fine index
$\infty$ and global time $\R_+$.  It does not contradict the finite-interval
Da Prato--Grisvard theorem, nor the source's homogeneous-seminorm estimates.
The obstruction is specifically the accumulation of arbitrarily many low
frequencies in the inhomogeneous $C_0$ component of the norm.

The run's cheap indexes and parsed corpus were searched for the arXiv id, the
exact question, the interpolation space over $C_0(\R^n)$, and global
$L^\infty$ maximal regularity.  Exact-title and exact-question searches
through 11 August 2026 recovered the source but no later primary-source
resolution.  Because an unindexed or differently phrased antecedent remains
possible, novelty confidence is moderate; the mathematical claim is
submitted as a candidate full solution.

The argument is analytic and uses no computational evidence.  Review should
focus on Lemma~\ref{lem:closure}, the powers of $r_j$ in
\eqref{eq:scaling}, and the use of the thermic norm in the large-time
estimate.  All three points have been expanded explicitly above.
