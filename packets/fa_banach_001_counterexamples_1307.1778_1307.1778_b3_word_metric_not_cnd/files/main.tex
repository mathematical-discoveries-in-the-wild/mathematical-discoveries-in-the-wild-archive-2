\section*{Result}

J\'oziak asks whether the word metric of a general Artin group is
conditionally negative definite (CND) \cite[p.~2]{Joziak}.  The following
eight-point witness gives a negative answer to the universal reading of that
question.

\begin{theorem}
Let
\[
 \B=\langle a,b\mid aba=bab\rangle
\]
carry the word metric associated to
\(\{a^{\pm1},b^{\pm1}\}\).  This metric is not conditionally negative
definite.
\end{theorem}

Here and below, capital letters denote inverses: \(A=a^{-1}\) and
\(B=b^{-1}\).

\section*{Proof Intuition}

The braid relation creates shortcuts among the second sphere of the Cayley
graph.  Split eight length-two braids into two four-point classes.  Every pair
inside either class is distance four, whereas sufficiently many cross-class
pairs are only distance two.  Giving the two classes opposite signs makes the
within-class contribution dominate, producing a positive value of the
Schoenberg quadratic form.  The only bookkeeping issue is certifying the
small word distances; an injective \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\)-plus-exponent
coordinate makes that finite check exact.

\section*{Exact word-distance certificate}

Define
\[
 \rho(a)=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \rho(b)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
and let \(e:\B\to\mathbb Z\) be exponent sum, so \(e(a)=e(b)=1\).

\begin{lemma}
The homomorphism \(\Phi=(\rho,e):\B\to
\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\times\mathbb Z\) is injective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The center of \(\B\) is generated by
\(z=(ab)^3=(aba)^2\), and \(\B/\langle z\rangle\cong
\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)\).  If \(\Phi(g)=(I,0)\), the projective image
of \(g\) is trivial, hence \(g=z^k\).  Since \(\rho(z)=-I\), the equality
\(\rho(g)=I\) makes \(k\) even; since \(e(z)=6\), the equality \(e(g)=0\)
then forces \(k=0\).
\end{proof}

Order the points as
\[
 (g_1,\ldots,g_8)=(ab,BA,Ab,bA,ba,AB,Ba,aB).
\]
Direct multiplication of their \(\Phi\)-coordinates gives the following
word-distance matrix:
\[
D=(d(g_i,g_j))_{i,j=1}^8=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&4&4&4&2&4&2&2\\
4&0&4&4&4&2&2&2\\
4&4&0&4&2&2&4&4\\
4&4&4&0&2&2&4&4\\
2&4&2&2&0&4&4&4\\
4&2&2&2&4&0&4&4\\
2&2&4&4&4&4&0&4\\
2&2&4&4&4&4&4&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For completeness, this is a particularly small finite verification.  Each
point has word length two, so every displayed distance is at most four.
Exponent parity makes every distance between these points even.  Injectivity
of \(\Phi\) distinguishes the eight points; comparing
\(\Phi(g_i^{-1}g_j)\) with the twelve reduced words of length two decides
whether the distance is two, and otherwise it is four.  The included verifier
performs exactly this enumeration using integer arithmetic.

\section*{Violation of conditional negative definiteness}

Let
\[
 \lambda=(1,1,1,1,-1,-1,-1,-1)^T.
\]
Then \(\sum_i\lambda_i=0\), but the displayed matrix gives
\[
 \sum_{i,j=1}^8 \lambda_i\lambda_j d(g_i,g_j)
 =\lambda^TD\lambda=8>0.
\]
This contradicts the defining inequality for a CND kernel, completing the
proof.

\section*{Scope and checks}

The result refutes the assertion that standard word metrics of all Artin
groups are CND; it does not classify which Artin groups have CND word metric.
The source paper's nearby ``natural question'' about quadratic embeddings is
answered inside that paper by affine independence and is not the target here.

Cheap run indexes contained no prior packet for arXiv:1307.1778 or this
Artin-word-metric question.  Bounded exact-phrase searches through 11 August
2026 found the source question and literature on braid normal forms, but no
later paper stating this eight-point obstruction or an answer for the standard
\(B_3\) word metric.  Novelty therefore remains a candidate assessment, while
the finite calculation itself is exact.
