\section{Source question}

Hassanlou, Laitila, and Vaezi ask for boundedness and compactness criteria for
operator-valued weighted compositions on $H^p(X)$ in
\emph{Weighted composition operators between weak spaces of vector-valued
analytic functions}, arXiv:1401.1697v2 \cite{HLV}.  Figure~\ref{fig:source}
reproduces the statement on page 6.  Laitila and Tylli formulate the broader
version as Question 21 of their survey, arXiv:1505.01945v1 \cite{LTsurvey}:
characterize boundedness and (weak) compactness on both $H^p(X)$ and $A^p(X)$
for $1\leq p<\infty$; see Figure~\ref{fig:formal}.

\section{Setting and a vector-lifting lemma}

Let $X,Y$ be complex Banach spaces, let $1\leq p<\infty$, let
$\varphi:\D\to\D$ be analytic, and let
$\psi:\D\to\cL(X,Y)$ be analytic.  On either
\[
 \F_p(X)=H^p(X)\qquad\text{or}\qquad \F_p(X)=A^p(X),
\]
put
\[
 (\wc f)(z)=\psi(z)f(\varphi(z)).
 \tag{2.1}
\]
For scalar analytic $a$, write $\wa a$ for the scalar weighted composition
and $\widetilde W_{a,\varphi}^{,X}$ for its pointwise lift to $\F_p(X)$.
If $U:X\to Y$ is bounded, write $\widetilde U f(z)=Uf(z)$.

We use the following standard scalar-to-vector consequence of the Carleson
embedding proof.

\begin{lemma}[scalar lifting and compact postcomposition]\label{lem:lift}
For $\F_p=H^p$ or $A^p$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item If $\wa a$ is bounded on scalar $\F_p$, then
$\widetilde W_{a,\varphi}^{,X}$ is bounded on $\F_p(X)$ for every Banach
space $X$.
\item If in addition $\wa a$ is compact and $U:X\to Y$ is compact, then
\[
 \widetilde U\,\widetilde W_{a,\varphi}^{,X}:
 \F_p(X)\longrightarrow\F_p(Y)
 \tag{2.2}
\]
is compact.  No approximation property of $X$ or $Y$ is required.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For (a), scalar boundedness is equivalent to the appropriate Carleson
embedding estimate for the pullback measure defined by $a$ and $\varphi$.
The proof of that estimate uses only the submean inequality.  Apply the same
proof to the nonnegative subharmonic function
$z\mapsto\|f(z)\|_X^p$.  This gives the vector-valued estimate, for both the
boundary Carleson measure in the Hardy case and the area Carleson measure in
the Bergman case.

For (b), let $K=\overline{U^*(B_{Y^*})}$, which is norm compact.  Given
$\delta>0$, choose $x_1^*,\ldots,x_m^*\in K$ forming a $\delta$-net.  For
$h\in\F_p(X)$, pointwise duality gives
\begin{align*}
 \|U(a(z)h(\varphi(z)))\|_Y
 &\leq \max_{1\leq j\leq m}
 |a(z)x_j^*(h(\varphi(z)))|\\
 &\hspace{18mm}+\delta |a(z)|\|h(\varphi(z))\|_X.
 \tag{2.3}
\end{align*}
Each map
$h\mapsto \wa a(x_j^*\circ h)$ from $\F_p(X)$ to scalar $\F_p$ is compact.
The finite product of these maps sends the unit ball to a totally bounded
set.  Equation (2.3), the lattice inequality
$\|\max_j|g_j|\|_p\leq(\sum_j\|g_j\|_p^p)^{1/p}$, and the uniform bound from
(a) show that sufficiently close points in that finite product have images
under (2.2) within $O(\delta)$.  Hence (2.2) maps the unit ball to a totally
bounded set and is compact.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof intuition}

Finite-dimensional operator range eliminates the genuinely operator-valued
Carleson obstruction.  Choose a basis $U_1,\ldots,U_N$ of the linear span of
$\psi(\D)$ and write
\[
 \psi(z)=\sum_{k=1}^N a_k(z)U_k.
 \tag{3.1}
\]
The forward reduction is immediate: the operator is the finite sum of the
vector lifts of the scalar weighted compositions, followed by $U_k$.

The reverse reduction is the key point.  On the finite-dimensional operator
space $E=\operatorname{span}\psi(\D)$, the elementary functionals
$U\mapsto y^*(Ux)$ span $E^*$.  Consequently each coefficient $a_k$ is a
finite linear combination of scalarizations $y^*\psi(\cdot)x$, and the
corresponding scalar weighted composition factors through the original
operator.  Compactness also forces each value $\psi(z)$ to be compact by
testing constant functions and evaluating at $z$.  Minimality of (3.1)
provides finitely many values $\psi(z_j)$ spanning $E$, so this forces every
$U_k$ to be compact.  Weak compactness is handled identically and its converse
uses factorization of weakly compact operators through reflexive spaces.

\section{The finite-span classification}

\begin{theorem}[boundedness, compactness, and weak compactness]\label{thm:main}
Assume $\psi\not\equiv0$ and
\[
 E=\operatorname{span}\{\psi(z):z\in\D\}
\]
has finite dimension $N$.  Choose a basis $U_1,\ldots,U_N$ of $E$ and the
unique analytic coefficient functions $a_1,\ldots,a_N$ in (3.1).  Then, for
$\F_p=H^p$ or $A^p$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\wc:\F_p(X)\to\F_p(Y)$ is bounded if and only if every scalar
$\wa{a_k}:\F_p\to\F_p$ is bounded.
\item $\wc$ is compact if and only if every $U_k:X\to Y$ and every scalar
$\wa{a_k}$ is compact.
\item If $1<p<\infty$ and $\wc$ is bounded, then $\wc$ is weakly compact if
and only if every $U_k:X\to Y$ is weakly compact.
\end{enumerate}
The conditions do not depend on the chosen basis of $E$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every linear functional on $E$ is a finite linear combination of elementary
functionals $U\mapsto y^*(Ux)$.  Indeed, those elementary functionals
separate points of $E$, so their restrictions span $E^*$ by finite
dimensionality.  In particular, for each coordinate functional $\lambda_k$
of the basis $(U_k)$, there are finitely many $x_{k,l}\in X$,
$y_{k,l}^*\in Y^*$, and $c_{k,l}\in\mathbb C$ such that
\[
 a_k(z)=\lambda_k(\psi(z))
 =\sum_l c_{k,l}\,y_{k,l}^*(\psi(z)x_{k,l}).
 \tag{4.1}
\]
Let $J_x:g\mapsto gx$ and $Q_{y^*}:f\mapsto y^*\circ f$.  Then
\[
 \wa{a_k}=\sum_l c_{k,l}Q_{y_{k,l}^*}\,\wc\,J_{x_{k,l}}.
 \tag{4.2}
\]
Thus boundedness, compactness, or weak compactness of $\wc$ passes to every
scalar coefficient operator.

Conversely, (3.1) gives the operator identity
\[
 \wc=\sum_{k=1}^N
 \widetilde U_k\,\widetilde W_{a_k,\varphi}^{,X}.
 \tag{4.3}
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}(a) proves the converse in (i).  If all $U_k$ and all
$\wa{a_k}$ are compact, Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}(b) makes every summand in (4.3)
compact, proving one direction of (ii).

For the reverse direction in (ii), let $j:X\to\F_p(X)$ send $x$ to the
constant function $x$, and let $\delta_z:\F_p(Y)\to Y$ be evaluation at $z$.
Then
\[
 \psi(z)=\delta_z\,\wc\,j.
 \tag{4.4}
\]
Hence compactness of $\wc$ makes every $\psi(z)$ compact.  Since (3.1) is
minimal, the vectors $(a_1(z),\ldots,a_N(z))$ span $\mathbb C^N$ as $z$
varies.  Choose $z_1,\ldots,z_N$ for which the coefficient matrix is
invertible.  Each $U_k$ is then a linear combination of
$\psi(z_1),\ldots,\psi(z_N)$ and is compact.  Equation (4.2) already showed
that every scalar $\wa{a_k}$ is compact.

The same evaluation argument shows that weak compactness of $\wc$ forces
every $U_k$ to be weakly compact.  Conversely, let $1<p<\infty$ and factor a
weakly compact $U_k$ as
\[
 X\xrightarrow{A_k} Z_k\xrightarrow{B_k}Y
 \tag{4.5}
\]
through a reflexive Banach space $Z_k$, using the Davis--Figiel--Johnson--
Pe\l czy\'nski factorization theorem \cite{DFJP}.  The space
$\F_p(Z_k)$ is reflexive, and
\[
 \widetilde U_k\widetilde W_{a_k,\varphi}^{,X}
 =\widetilde B_k\widetilde W_{a_k,\varphi}^{,Z_k}\widetilde A_k
 \tag{4.6}
\]
factors through it.  Each summand in (4.3) is therefore weakly compact, which
proves (iii).  Finally, a change of basis makes finite linear combinations on
both the operator and scalar sides, so the stated conditions are intrinsic.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[constant operator weights, including $p=1$]\label{cor:constant}
Let $0\ne U\in\cL(X,Y)$ and put $\psi(z)=U$.  On either $H^p$ or $A^p$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item $W_{U,\varphi}$ is compact if and only if $U$ and scalar $C_\varphi$
are compact.
\item If $1<p<\infty$, $W_{U,\varphi}$ is weakly compact if and only if $U$
is weakly compact.
\item If $p=1$, $W_{U,\varphi}$ is weakly compact if and only if $U$ is
weakly compact and scalar $C_\varphi$ is compact.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Only (c) is not contained in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  Necessity follows by
factoring $U$ through constant functions and $C_\varphi$ through
$W_{U,\varphi}$ using one $x\in X$ and $y^*\in Y^*$ with $y^*(Ux)=1$.
Weak compactness of scalar $C_\varphi$ on $H^1$ implies compactness by
Sarason's theorem; on $A^1$, it does so because $A^1$ has the Schur property.
Conversely, factor weakly compact $U$ through a reflexive $Z$.  For reflexive
$Z$, compactness of scalar $C_\varphi$ implies weak compactness of the vector
composition on $H^1(Z)$ and on $A^1(Z)$ by the vector-valued composition
theorems summarized in \cite[Section 3]{LTsurvey}.  The factorization (4.6)
with $a=1$ finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, verification, and novelty}

The theorem gives a complete answer whenever $\psi(\D)$ has finite linear
span, not merely when $X$ or $Y$ is finite dimensional.  It includes
nonconstant operator weights and does not assume approximation properties.
It does not settle the infinite-span case.  There, the natural approximation
route would require uniform finite-span approximation of $\psi$ in a tensor
norm that controls the vector Carleson embedding; pointwise norm approximation
alone is insufficient.  At $p=1$, the weak-compactness statement for general
nonconstant finite-span weights is also left open because the required
weighted reflexive-target lifting is not part of the cited unweighted theorem.

The proof was audited at four possible cancellation points: elementary
functionals really span $E^*$; minimality supplies an invertible evaluation
matrix; (2.3) uses compactness of $U^*(B_{Y^*})$ and therefore does not assume
finite-rank approximation; and (4.6) has the correct order of pointwise maps.
Eight focused upgrade routes are recorded in the accompanying attempt note.

Repository indexes and bounded exact-phrase, arXiv/title/author, constant
operator-weight, finite-operator-span, and later-work searches through
13 August 2026 found the scalar theory and the source survey but no explicit
statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  Novelty is therefore provisional, not a
claim of priority.  Human review should focus first on the scalar-to-vector
Carleson lifting in Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}(a), and then on the compactness
finite-net estimate (2.3).
