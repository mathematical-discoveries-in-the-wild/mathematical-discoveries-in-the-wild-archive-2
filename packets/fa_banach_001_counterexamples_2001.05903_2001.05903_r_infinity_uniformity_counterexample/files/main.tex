\section*{Source question}

Fraccaroli~\cite{Fraccaroli} studies outer $L^p_\mu(\ell^r)$ spaces on a
finite set $X$ equipped with an outer measure $\mu$ and a strictly positive
weight $\omega$. After Theorem~1.1, the paper states:

\begin{quote}
For $1<p<\infty$, $r=\infty$, the question about uniformity remains open.
\end{quote}

Here uniformity concerns both the reverse dual estimate
\[
 \|f\|_{L^p_\mu(\ell^\infty)}
 \le C_p\sup_{\|g\|_{L^{p'}_\mu(\ell^1)}=1}
       \sum_{x\in X}\omega(x)|f(x)g(x)|                         \tag{D}
\]
and the countable quasi-triangle estimate
\[
 \left\|\sum_j f_j\right\|_{L^p_\mu(\ell^\infty)}
 \le C_p\sum_j\|f_j\|_{L^p_\mu(\ell^\infty)},                 \tag{T}
\]
with $C_p$ independent of $(X,\mu,\omega)$. The opposite direction in
\emph{(D)} follows from the established outer H\"older and $\ell^1$-domination
inequalities~\cite[Propositions~8.4 and~8.6]{Fraccaroli}; see also
Do--Thiele~\cite{DoThiele}.

\section*{Definitions}

For $r=\infty$, the size is
\[
 \ell^\infty(f)(A)=\sup_{x\in A}|f(x)|,
\]
and the superlevel outer measure is simply $\mu(\{|f|>\lambda\})$.
Consequently
\[
 \|f\|_{\Lpinf}^p
 =\int_0^\infty p\lambda^{p-1}\mu(\{|f|>\lambda\})\,d\lambda.
 \tag{1}
\]
In particular, for $a\ge0$ and $A\subseteq X$,
\[
             \|a\1_A\|_{\Lpinf}=a\,\mu(A)^{1/p}.               \tag{2}
\]

\section*{Proof intuition}

The outer measure will be a set-cover cost. We choose $N$ unit-cost covering
sets so that every point belongs to exactly $k$ of them, but covering the
whole ground set requires $N-k+1$ of them. Each covering-set indicator then
has norm one. Their sum, however, is the constant $k$ on a set of outer
measure $N-k+1$, so its norm has an extra factor $(N-k+1)^{1/p}$. Taking
$k=N/2$ makes this factor diverge. The proposed dual expression cannot hide
the obstruction: it is subadditive, and the already-known H\"older half bounds
it uniformly on every individual indicator.

\section*{Counterexample theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Fix $1<p<\infty$. There is no constant depending only on $p$ for which
\emph{(T)} holds in every finite outer measure setting. There is likewise no
constant depending only on $p$ for which the reverse dual estimate
\emph{(D)} holds in every such setting.

More quantitatively, for each $m\ge1$ there is a finite setting and $2m$
nonnegative functions $f_1,\dots,f_{2m}$ such that
\[
 \frac{\|\sum_{i=1}^{2m}f_i\|_{L^p_\mu(\ell^\infty)}}
      {\sum_{i=1}^{2m}\|f_i\|_{L^p_\mu(\ell^\infty)}}
       =\frac12(m+1)^{1/p}.                                    \tag{3}
\]
If $D_p$ denotes the supremum on the right of \emph{(D)}, then for the same
sum $F_m$,
\[
 \frac{\|F_m\|_{L^p_\mu(\ell^\infty)}}{D_p(F_m)}
 \ge \frac{(m+1)^{1/p}}{2H_p},                                \tag{4}
\]
Here $H_p<\infty$ is independent of the finite setting and satisfies
$D_p(h)\le H_p\|h\|_{L^p_\mu(\ell^\infty)}$ by the already-known estimates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first give the construction with integers $N>k\ge1$. Write
$[N]=\{1,\dots,N\}$ and let
\[
 X=\binom{[N]}k=\{S\subseteq[N]:|S|=k\}.
\]
For each $i\in[N]$, define the incidence set
\[
 E_i=\{S\in X:i\in S\}.
\]
Give the covering family $\cD=\{E_1,\dots,E_N\}$ the pre-measure
$\sigma(E_i)=1$, and let $\mu$ be its induced covering outer measure:
\[
 \mu(A)=\min\bigl\{|I|:I\subseteq[N],\ 
                   A\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}E_i\bigr\}.
 \tag{5}
\]
This is an outer measure of exactly the type allowed in the source paper.
Every subset is covered by the full family. Moreover, every singleton
$\{S\}$ has outer measure one: it is nonempty and is covered by $E_i$ for
any $i\in S$. Thus $\mu$ is finite and strictly positive on singletons. Take
the strictly positive counting weight $\omega(S)=1$.

Each $E_i$ is nonempty and covers itself, hence
\[
                         \mu(E_i)=1.                            \tag{6}
\]
We also have
\[
                         \mu(X)=N-k+1.                          \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, $\{E_i:i\in I\}$ covers $X$ exactly when every $k$-subset of
$[N]$ meets $I$. This fails exactly when the complement $[N]\setminus I$
contains a $k$-subset. Hence it is a cover exactly when
$|[N]\setminus I|\le k-1$, or equivalently $|I|\ge N-k+1$.

Set $f_i=\1_{E_i}$. By \emph{(2)} and \emph{(6)},
\[
                         \|f_i\|_{\Lpinf}=1.                   \tag{8}
\]
Every point $S\in X$ belongs to exactly $k$ of the incidence sets, so
\[
                 F:=\sum_{i=1}^N f_i=k\1_X.                   \tag{9}
\]
Equations \emph{(2)}, \emph{(7)}, and \emph{(9)} give
\[
 \|F\|_{\Lpinf}=k(N-k+1)^{1/p},\qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^N\|f_i\|_{\Lpinf}=N.                             \tag{10}
\]
Now set $N=2m$ and $k=m$. Dividing the two quantities in \emph{(10)} gives
\emph{(3)}, which tends to infinity with $m$. This proves the failure of a
uniform constant in \emph{(T)}.

For completeness, define
\[
 D_p(h)=\sup_{\|g\|_{L^{p'}_\mu(\ell^1)}=1}
               \sum_{S\in X}\omega(S)|h(S)g(S)|.
\]
This functional is subadditive. The uniform outer H\"older direction proved
in the source gives
\[
                    D_p(h)\le H_p\|h\|_{\Lpinf}                \tag{11}
\]
for a constant $H_p$ independent of the finite setting. Applying
\emph{(11)} to \emph{(8)} and using subadditivity yields
\[
                       D_p(F)\le\sum_{i=1}^N D_p(f_i)\le H_pN.
\]
Together with \emph{(10)}, and again $N=2m$, this is \emph{(4)}. The ratio
diverges, so no setting-independent reverse constant in \emph{(D)} exists.
\end{proof}

\section*{Why this resolves the endpoint}

The source asks about uniformity for the entire missing range
$1<p<\infty$, $r=\infty$. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} treats every exponent in
that range, under the source's full finite-setting hypotheses, and disproves
both requested uniform statements. No unproved condition is left. The
example does not concern the positive upper-half-space theorem, whose
additional geometric structure is absent here.

The construction also gives a cardinality lower bound. Since
$|X|=\binom{2m}{m}\le 2^{2m}$, the necessary quasi-triangle constants are at
least a fixed $p$-dependent multiple of $(\log |X|)^{1/p}$. No optimality is
claimed.

\section*{Verification and novelty check}

The proof uses only the exact set-cover computation in \emph{(7)}, the
incidence identity in \emph{(9)}, the source definition \emph{(1)}, and the
already-established H\"older half \emph{(11)}. The accompanying script
exhaustively enumerates the covering subfamilies for $2\le N\le10$ and checks
\emph{(6)}--\emph{(10)}. These finite checks are corroboration, not proof.

A bounded search on August 9, 2026 included the run's four lightweight
indexes; the exact arXiv id, title, and open-question phrase; combinations of
``outer $L^p$'', ``$\ell^\infty$'', ``Choquet'', ``finite outer measure'',
``uniformity'', and ``triangle inequality''; the author's later
outer-$L^p$ papers and current publication list; and the author's 2022 thesis.
The thesis repeats that this exact endpoint remains open. No later solution or
this set-cover example was found. Novelty confidence is therefore moderate to
high, subject to specialist review.

\section*{Human-review recommendation}

The direct quasi-triangle obstruction is elementary and should be checked
first. A reviewer should then confirm that the normalization and direction of
the source's outer H\"older estimate give \emph{(11)}. If both checks pass,
this is a full negative resolution of the stated uniformity question.
