\section{Source problem and scope}

B. Bukh and R. Karasev, \emph{Suborbits in Knaster's problem},
arXiv:1212.5351 \cite{source}, prove that a unit sphere of a
three-dimensional subspace cannot lie in a fixed small neighborhood of an
orbit of an abelian group.  Their Problem~2.6 (PDF page~4) asks whether the
symmetric-group construction that approximates spheres by suborbits can be
modified to use a finite $p$-group.  They point in particular to a
$2$-group generated by sign changes and a Sylow-$2$ permutation group.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/problem_2_6_crop.png}
  \caption{The comparison and Problem~2.6 in the source PDF, page~4.}
\end{figure}

The result below does not settle high-dimensional irreducible $p$-group
actions.  It proves that those blocks are necessary, quantitatively: a close
covering orbit must place almost all of the target plane inside constituents
of real degree at least the plane dimension.

\section{Proof intuition}

Decompose the representation into irreducible orthogonal blocks.  Along one
group orbit, the norm in each block is fixed.  If projection from the target
$k$-plane into a block has a kernel, move a random unit vector of the plane
along a kernel direction.  Its projected norm acquires the nonconstant factor
$\sqrt{1-t^2}$.  No fixed orbit radius can track this factor: even its best
constant approximation leaves a definite relative variance $\delta_k$.

Sum this unavoidable error over all noninjective blocks.  When every
irreducible block has dimension less than $k$, every projection has a kernel
and the block energies sum to one.  With larger constituents present, the same
calculation says exactly how much of the target plane must concentrate there
if the approximation error is small.

\section{The scalar variance}

For $k\ge2$, let $T_k$ be the first coordinate of a uniform point on
$S^{k-1}$.  Its density on $[-1,1]$ is proportional to
$(1-t^2)^{(k-3)/2}$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta}
 m_k=\E\sqrt{1-T_k^2}
 =\frac{\Gamma(k/2)^2}
 {\Gamma((k-1)/2)\Gamma((k+1)/2)},
 \qquad
 \delta_k=1-\frac{k}{k-1}m_k^2.
\end{equation}
Since $\sqrt{1-T_k^2}$ is nonconstant, $\delta_k>0$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:scalar}
For every $R,a\ge0$,
\[
 \E\bigl(R\sqrt{1-T_k^2}-a\bigr)^2
 \ge \delta_k\,\E\bigl(R^2(1-T_k^2)\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $Q=\sqrt{1-T_k^2}$.  Minimizing over all real $a$ only makes the
left-hand side smaller, and the minimum is $R^2\operatorname{Var}(Q)$.  Also
$\E Q^2=(k-1)/k$.  Thus
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Var}(Q)}{\E Q^2}
 =1-\frac{(\E Q)^2}{\E Q^2}=\delta_k.
\]
The beta-integral ratio for $\E Q$ is exactly the gamma quotient in
\eqref{eq:delta}.
\end{proof}

\section{The block obstruction}

Let a finite group $G$ act orthogonally on a finite-dimensional real inner
product space $V$. Fix an orthogonal decomposition into irreducible invariant
subspaces
\[
 V=V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_m,
\]
and write $P_j$ for the projection onto $V_j$. Let $L\subset V$ have
dimension $k\ge2$, let $S(L)$ be its unit sphere, and set
\[
 J_0=\{j:\ P_j|_L\text{ is not injective}\},\qquad
 \alpha(L)=\int_{S(L)}\sum_{j\in J_0}\|P_jv\|^2\,d\sigma_L(v).
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:block}
For every $c\in V$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mainbound}
 \sup_{v\in S(L)}\dist(v,Gc)
 \ge \sqrt{\delta_k\,\alpha(L)}.
\end{equation}
In particular, if every real irreducible constituent has dimension less than
$k$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal}
 \sup_{v\in S(L)}\dist(v,Gc)\ge\eta_k:=\sqrt{\delta_k}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each $j$, put $a_j=\|P_jc\|$. Since $V_j$ and its orthogonal complement
are invariant, $P_j$ commutes with the action and
$\|P_j(gc)\|=a_j$ for every $g\in G$.  Therefore, for $v\in S(L)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial}
 \dist(v,Gc)^2
 \ge \sum_{j=1}^m(\|P_jv\|-a_j)^2.
\end{equation}

Fix $j\in J_0$ and choose a unit $e_j\in L\cap\ker P_j$.  A uniform vector
$v\in S(L)$ can be sampled as
\[
 v=T_ke_j+\sqrt{1-T_k^2}\,u,
\]
where $u$ is uniform on the unit sphere of $L\cap e_j^\perp$ and is
independent of $T_k$. Hence
\[
 \|P_jv\|=\sqrt{1-T_k^2}\,\|P_ju\|.
\]
Condition on $u$ and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:scalar} with
$R=\|P_ju\|$ and $a=a_j$.  Averaging in $u$ gives
\[
 \int_{S(L)}(\|P_jv\|-a_j)^2\,d\sigma_L(v)
 \ge\delta_k\int_{S(L)}\|P_jv\|^2\,d\sigma_L(v).
\]
Integrate \eqref{eq:radial}, retain only $j\in J_0$, and sum.  The resulting
mean lower bound is $\delta_k\alpha(L)$, so some $v$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:mainbound}.

If $\dim V_j<k$, the map $P_j|_L$ cannot be injective. Thus if all
constituents have dimension less than $k$, then $J_0=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and
$\alpha(L)=1$ by orthogonality. This proves \eqref{eq:universal}.
\end{proof}

\section{Necessary concentration in large irreducibles}

Let $V_{<k}$ be the sum of all real irreducible blocks of dimension less than
$k$, let $Q_{<k}$ be its orthogonal projection, and let $P_L$ denote
orthogonal projection onto $L$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:energy}
If $S(L)\subset(Gc)_\varepsilon$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace}
 \frac1k\Tr(P_LQ_{<k})\le\frac{\varepsilon^2}{\delta_k}.
\end{equation}
Thus for $\varepsilon\ll\eta_k$, almost all mean-square energy of $L$ lies in
real irreducible constituents of dimension at least $k$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every block in $V_{<k}$ belongs to $J_0$, and spherical averaging gives
\[
 \int_{S(L)}\|Q_{<k}v\|^2\,d\sigma_L(v)
 =\frac1k\Tr(P_LQ_{<k}).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:block} therefore bounds the square of the covering radius
below by $\delta_k\Tr(P_LQ_{<k})/k$. Rearrangement proves
\eqref{eq:trace}.
\end{proof}

For $k=3$, $m_3=\pi/4$ and
\[
 \eta_3=\sqrt{1-\frac{3\pi^2}{32}}\approx0.273358>\frac14.
\]
Hence the result recovers and slightly sharpens the source's obstruction for
abelian actions, while also covering every finite-group representation with
real irreducible degree at most two (for example, dihedral $2$-groups).

\section{Limitations, verification, and novelty}

High-dimensional irreducible $p$-group constituents are the exact unresolved
case for this method: $P_j|_L$ can be injective and can even have constant norm
on $S(L)$, leaving no radial fluctuation.  Complex irreducibles of finite
$p$-groups admit monomial models, suggesting a structured permutation-phase
problem, but replacing the full symmetric matching in the known construction
by a Sylow-wreath action was not achieved.  The packet makes no claim that the
full p-group problem has a negative answer.

The gamma constants were checked numerically for $2\le k\le10$, including the
closed form for $k=3$; code is included.  The proof itself is exact and does
not depend on computation.

The local run indexes and a bounded external search on 11 August 2026 checked
arXiv id 1212.5351, the exact wording of Problem~2.6, weak-Knaster and
p-group/suborbit variants, and the source's citation graph.  No later
resolution or statement of this representation-degree obstruction was found.
This was not an exhaustive review, so novelty remains pending specialist
checking.
