\section{Source question and answer}

Khasnis and Sholapurkar study when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-polynomial}
 \beta(m,n)=\frac{1}{b(m)+a(m)n},\qquad m,n\in\Nzero,
\end{equation}
is jointly completely monotone \cite{KhasnisSholapurkar}.  In their
Question~4.6 they take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-family}
 a(x)=a_0(x+a_1),\qquad
 b(x)=b_0(x+b_1)(x+b_2)(x+b_3)
\end{equation}
with positive parameters satisfying
$0<b_1<a_1<b_2<b_3$, and ask whether the answer is yes.  The current arXiv
version still poses the question on source PDF page~10:

\begin{center}
 \includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/question_4_6_crop.png}
\end{center}

The answer is \emph{no for every choice} in \eqref{eq:source-family}.  More
generally, a polynomial degree gap at least two always rules out joint
complete monotonicity.  The proof comes from an exact reduction to
one-variable Hausdorff moment sequences.

\section{Definitions and the explicit vertical measure}

For a sequence $u=(u_m)_{m\ge0}$, write
$\D u_m=u_{m+1}-u_m$.  It is a Hausdorff moment sequence precisely when
\[
 (-1)^k\D^ku_m\ge0\qquad(m,k\in\Nzero),
\]
equivalently when $u_m=\int_0^1s^m\,d\eta(s)$ for a finite positive measure
$\eta$ on $[0,1]$.  The two-variable analogue says that a net is jointly
completely monotone precisely when it is the moment net of a finite positive
measure on $[0,1]^2$ \cite[Proposition~4.6.11]{BergChristensenRessel}; this is
also recalled at the start of the source paper.

The following observation is the key.  It is stated for arbitrary positive
sequences, so no polynomial structure is used.  Put
\[
 a_m>0,\qquad b_m>0,\qquad r_m=\frac{b_m}{a_m},
 \qquad
 h_m(t)=\frac1{a_m}t^{r_m-1}\quad(0<t<1).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertical-integral}
 \int_0^1t^nh_m(t)\,dt
 =\frac1{a_m(n+r_m)}
 =\frac1{b_m+a_mn}.
\end{equation}

\section{An exact slice characterization}

\begin{theorem}[Vertical-slice criterion]\label{thm:slice}
For positive sequences $(a_m)$ and $(b_m)$, the net
\[
 \beta(m,n)=\frac1{b_m+a_mn},\qquad m,n\in\Nzero,
\]
is jointly completely monotone if and only if, for every $t\in(0,1)$, the
sequence $(h_m(t))_{m\ge0}$ is a Hausdorff moment sequence.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $\beta$ is jointly completely monotone, and let $\mu$ be
its representing measure on $[0,1]^2$.  For every $m$, define a measure on
$[0,1]$ by
\[
 \nu_m(E)=\int_{[0,1]\times E}s^m\,d\mu(s,t).
\]
Its $n$th moment is $\beta(m,n)$.  By \eqref{eq:vertical-integral}, the
measure $h_m(t)\,dt$ has exactly the same moments.  Hausdorff moment measures
are unique: equality on monomials gives equality on polynomials, and
Weierstrass approximation gives equality on every continuous function.
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-identification}
 d\nu_m(t)=h_m(t)\,dt.
\end{equation}

For $m,k\ge0$, the measure
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^j\binom{k}{j}\nu_{m+j}(E)
 =\int_{[0,1]\times E}s^m(1-s)^k\,d\mu(s,t)
\]
is positive.  By \eqref{eq:density-identification}, its Lebesgue density is
$(-1)^k\D^kh_m(t)$, so that density is nonnegative almost everywhere.  It is
continuous on $(0,1)$; therefore it is nonnegative everywhere.  This holds
for all $m,k$, proving that $(h_m(t))_m$ is a Hausdorff moment sequence for
every $t\in(0,1)$.

Conversely, assume the stated one-variable condition.  Let $\eta_t$ be the
unique positive measure on $[0,1]$ whose moments are $h_m(t)$.  We briefly
justify that these measures can be integrated without invoking a measurable
selection theorem.  For every polynomial $P$, the map
$t\mapsto\int P\,d\eta_t$ is a finite linear combination of the continuous
functions $h_m(t)$.  Uniform polynomial approximation then makes
$t\mapsto\int f\,d\eta_t$ measurable for every $f\in C[0,1]$.  Indeed, the
approximation error is bounded by
$\lVert P-f\rVert_\infty\eta_t([0,1])
=\lVert P-f\rVert_\infty h_0(t)$.

For $F\in C([0,1]^2)$ define
\[
 L(F)=\int_0^1\left(\int_0^1F(s,t)\,d\eta_t(s)\right)dt.
\]
Finite sums of products $f(s)g(t)$ approximate $F$ uniformly, which proves
measurability of the inner integral.  Moreover,
\[
 \int_0^1h_0(t)\,dt=\frac1{b_0}<\infty,
\]
so $L$ is a bounded positive linear functional.  The Riesz representation
theorem gives a finite positive measure $\mu$ on $[0,1]^2$ representing $L$.
Finally,
\[
 \int_{[0,1]^2}s^mt^n\,d\mu(s,t)
 =\int_0^1t^nh_m(t)\,dt
 =\beta(m,n)
\]
by \eqref{eq:vertical-integral}.  Thus $\beta$ is jointly completely
monotone.
\end{proof}

\section{The degree-gap theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Two necessary concavity conditions]\label{lem:concavity}
If the net in Theorem~\ref{thm:slice} is jointly completely monotone, then,
for every $m\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:necessary}
 \D^2r_m\le0,
 \qquad
 a_{m+1}^2\ge a_ma_{m+2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A Hausdorff moment sequence is log-convex by Cauchy--Schwarz.  Applying this
to $(h_m(t))_m$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:slice} gives
\[
 h_{m+1}(t)^2\le h_m(t)h_{m+2}(t),
\]
or equivalently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
 \frac{a_ma_{m+2}}{a_{m+1}^2}
 t^{-\D^2r_m}\le1\qquad(0<t<1).
\end{equation}
If $\D^2r_m>0$, the left side tends to infinity as $t\downarrow0$, a
contradiction.  Thus $\D^2r_m\le0$.  Letting $t\uparrow1$ in
\eqref{eq:ratio} gives the second inequality in \eqref{eq:necessary}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial degree-gap obstruction]\label{thm:gap}
Let $a,b$ be real polynomials which are positive on $\Nzero$ and have
positive leading coefficients.  If
\[
 \deg b-\deg a\ge2,
\]
then the net in \eqref{eq:beta-polynomial} is not jointly completely
monotone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $d=\deg b-\deg a\ge2$ and let $c>0$ be the ratio of the leading
coefficients.  The rational function $r(x)=b(x)/a(x)$ satisfies
\[
 r''(x)=c\,d(d-1)x^{d-2}+O(x^{d-3})>0
\]
for all sufficiently large $x$.  (This follows directly by differentiating
the asymptotic expansion $r(x)=cx^d(1+O(x^{-1}))$.)  For every sufficiently
large integer $m$,
\[
 \D^2r_m
 =r(m+2)-2r(m+1)+r(m)
 =\int_0^1\!\int_0^1r''(m+u+v)\,du\,dv>0.
\]
This contradicts Lemma~\ref{lem:concavity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Full answer to Question 4.6]
For every positive $a_0,a_1,b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3$---in particular, for every
choice satisfying $0<b_1<a_1<b_2<b_3$---the net obtained from
\eqref{eq:source-family} is not jointly completely monotone.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Here $\deg b=3$ and $\deg a=1$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} applies.  The root
ordering is immaterial.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, checks, and novelty}

The result completely answers the concrete ordered-root question and proves
a stronger coefficient-free theorem for every polynomial degree gap at least
two.  The exact slice criterion also identifies what remains in degree gaps
zero and one, but it is not presented as a root-by-root solution of the
paper's much broader Problem~1.1.

The sign mechanism is robust: convexity of $r=b/a$ produces
$t^{-\D^2r_m}$ in \eqref{eq:ratio}, which diverges at zero and cannot be
cancelled by the fixed positive coefficient involving $a$.  Thus the proof
does not depend on a low-order numerical counterexample or on the ordering of
the roots.

A bounded literature check through 12 August 2026 examined the current arXiv
v2 record and full text, exact title/id/question/order searches, related
rational-function work, and Bhattacharjee--Nailwal's characterization of
completely alternating functions \cite{BhattacharjeeNailwal}.  The latter
handles the unprefactored net $1/(\psi(m)+n)$, whereas the present problem has
the variable factor $1/a(m)$.  No later answer to Question~4.6 or statement of
Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} was found.  This is a bounded novelty screen, not an
exhaustive bibliographic guarantee.
