\section{The source question}

For fixed templates $\mathbb X^1,\ldots,\mathbb X^S$, the source writes
$\mathbb Y_\lambda$ for a GW barycenter and, for an arbitrary target
$\mathbb Y$, considers
\[
  \mathbf J(\lambda)=\GW^2(\mathbb Y_\lambda,\mathbb Y).
\]
Supplement~E asks whether, when $\mathbb Y$ is outside the GW barycenter
span, the weights produced by its fixed-point and blow-up analysis algorithms
coincide with, or at least lie near, a minimizer of $\mathbf J$.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The construction below gives a negative answer to the exact coincidence
question, even for genuine finite metric-measure spaces and exact global GW
couplings. It also exhibits a GW Karcher mean which is not a true barycenter.

\section{Idea of the counterexample}

The four-point metrics have a single nontrivial symmetry, the transposition
of the first two points. Near a symmetric anchor, this symmetry acts on two
perturbation coordinates by $(u,v)\mapsto(-u,-v)$. The true geodesic between
the templates uses the identity alignment. The target is instead the
midpoint obtained by aligning the second template with the transposition.
Consequently, each source algorithm sees an exact zero-residual midpoint and
returns equal weights. But this midpoint lies on the wrong alignment branch.
Projecting it onto the true barycenter geodesic gives a different weight.

Conditional negative definiteness of all metrics makes the GW coupling
objective concave on the coupling polytope. Thus the full continuum of
couplings reduces rigorously to the $24$ permutations; no integrality
assumption or numerical GW solver is used.

\section{A four-point metric family}

Give $\{1,2,3,4\}$ the uniform probability vector
$q=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)$ and, for $|u|,|v|\leq1/5$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D}
D(u,v)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&10&11+u&12+v\\
10&0&11-u&12-v\\
11+u&11-u&0&13\\
12+v&12-v&13&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
All off-diagonal entries lie between $49/5$ and $66/5$, so every triangle
inequality is strict.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:permutations}
For any two matrices in \eqref{eq:D}, their squared GW distance is the
minimum, over permutations $P$ of four letters, of
\[
  \frac1{16}\|D(u,v)-P D(u',v')P^T\|_F^2.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $D(u,v)=10(J-I)+E(u,v)$. The maximum absolute row sum of the symmetric
matrix $E(u,v)$ is at most $7$. Hence, for $x\perp\one$,
\[
 x^TD(u,v)x\leq -10\|x\|^2+7\|x\|^2=-3\|x\|^2.
\]
Thus every $D(u,v)$ is strictly conditionally negative definite.

For a coupling $\pi$ with uniform marginals, the terms in the GW objective
other than
$-2\operatorname{tr}(D(u,v)\pi D(u',v')\pi^T)$ are constant. If $H$ has
zero row and column sums, conditional negative definiteness gives
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(D(u,v)H D(u',v')H^T)\geq0.
\]
The GW objective is therefore concave on the coupling polytope. A concave
function on this scaled Birkhoff polytope has a minimum at an extreme point,
and those points are $P/4$. Substitution gives the formula.
\end{proof}

Let $S$ transpose points $1$ and $2$. The anchor $D(0,0)$ has automorphism
group exactly $\{I,S\}$: its unique edges of lengths $10$ and $13$ force the
pairs $\{1,2\}$ and $\{3,4\}$, and the lengths $11$ and $12$ distinguish
points $3$ and $4$. Hence a permutation outside $\{I,S\}$ changes some
anchor edge by at least $1$. The perturbations change an edge by at most
$1/5$ on either side, so for such a permutation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outside-gap}
 \|D(u,v)-P D(u',v')P^T\|_F^2\geq2(1-2/5)^2=18/25.
\end{equation}
For the remaining two permutations,
\begin{align}
 \|D(u,v)-D(u',v')\|_F^2
   &=4\big((u-u')^2+(v-v')^2\big),\label{eq:id-cost}\\
 \|D(u,v)-S D(u',v')S^T\|_F^2
   &=4\big((u+u')^2+(v+v')^2\big).\label{eq:swap-cost}
\end{align}

\section{The templates and the false zero residual}

Set
\[
 a_1=(1/5,0),\qquad a_2=(1/10,1/5),\qquad
 y=\frac{a_1-a_2}{2}=(1/20,-1/10),
\]
and let $\mathbb X_1,\mathbb X_2,\mathbb Y$ be the uniform metric spaces with
matrices $D(a_1),D(a_2),D(y)$, respectively. Equations
\eqref{eq:outside-gap}--\eqref{eq:swap-cost} give the following exact data.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{pair}&\text{unique optimal permutation}&\text{squared Frobenius cost}
 &\GW^2\\ \hline
(\mathbb X_1,\mathbb X_2)&I&1/5&1/80\\
(\mathbb X_1,\mathbb Y)&I&13/100&13/1600\\
(\mathbb X_2,\mathbb Y)&S&13/100&13/1600
\end{array}
\]

For a deterministic uniform coupling, the source's matrix
$F(\mathbb Y,s)$ is just the optimally relabeled template. The blow-up
representatives in its second method are the same matrices. Therefore both
analysis quadratic programs use
\[
 D(a_1),\qquad S D(a_2)S^T=D(-a_2),\qquad D(y),
\]
and
\[
 D(y)=\frac12D(a_1)+\frac12D(-a_2).
\]
Both objectives have the unique zero at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alg-weight}
 \lambda_{\rm alg}=(1/2,1/2).
\end{equation}
Thus both methods certify exact representability in their surrogate
Karcher/fixed-alignment span.

The target is nevertheless outside the true GW barycenter span. Indeed, for
weights $(1-t,t)$ its synthesis cost is always
\[
 (1-t)\GW^2(\mathbb X_1,\mathbb Y)
 +t\GW^2(\mathbb X_2,\mathbb Y)=13/1600.
\]
The unique optimal endpoint alignment is the identity. Its geodesic point
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B}
 \mathbb B_t:=D(a(t)),\qquad
 a(t)=(1-t)a_1+ta_2=(1/5-t/10,t/5),
\end{equation}
has synthesis cost $t(1-t)/80\leq1/320=5/1600$. Hence $\mathbb Y$ is not a
true barycenter for any weight.

For completeness, $\mathbb B_t$ is the unique barycenter class. Glue optimal
couplings from any candidate $\mathbb Z$ to the two templates. The elementary
identity
\[
 (1-t)|r-z|^2+t|s-z|^2
 =t(1-t)|r-s|^2+|z-((1-t)r+ts)|^2
\]
shows that equality in the two-point barycenter lower bound requires the
induced template coupling to be endpoint-optimal and the candidate edge
weights to equal the displayed convex combination almost everywhere. Since
the endpoint coupling is uniquely the identity, this makes $\mathbb Z$
weakly isomorphic to \eqref{eq:B}.

\section{The true projection weight}

It remains to minimize
$\mathbf J(t)=\GW^2(\mathbb B_t,\mathbb Y)$. The two permutations $I,S$
have squared parameter costs
\begin{align*}
 f_I(t)&=\|y-a(t)\|^2
       =\frac{13}{400}+\frac{t}{100}+\frac{t^2}{20},\\
 f_S(t)&=\|y+a(t)\|^2
       =\frac{29}{400}-\frac{9t}{100}+\frac{t^2}{20}.
\end{align*}
Both corresponding squared Frobenius costs are below the non-symmetry bound
\eqref{eq:outside-gap}; Lemma~\ref{lem:permutations} therefore gives
\[
 \mathbf J(t)=\frac14\min\{f_I(t),f_S(t)\}.
\]
The branches meet at $t=2/5$. The first is increasing on $[0,1]$, while the
second has its unique minimum at $t=9/10$. Direct evaluation yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
 \min_{0\leq t\leq1}\mathbf J(t)=\mathbf J(9/10)=1/125,
 \qquad
 \mathbf J(1/2)=1/100.
\end{equation}
Consequently the true projection weight and the output of both analysis
methods are
\[
 \lambda_{\rm proj}=(1/10,9/10)\neq(1/2,1/2)=\lambda_{\rm alg}.
\]
The weight error is $2/5$ in either coordinate, and the algorithmic squared
projection cost is $5/4$ times optimal. Multiplying every metric by a common
constant preserves both weights and makes the additive gap in
\eqref{eq:gap} arbitrarily large.

\section{Scope}

This completely answers the source supplement's exact coincidence question
in the negative and shows that an unconditional Karcher-mean/GW-barycenter
equivalence is false. It does not preclude guarantees under additional
alignment-consistency, curvature, separation, or uniqueness hypotheses, and
it does not supply a full characterization of such hypotheses.
