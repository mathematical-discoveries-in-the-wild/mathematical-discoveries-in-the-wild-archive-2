\begin{abstract}
Cao, Li, Shen, Wick, and Yan ask which entire two-variable kernels induce
bounded Gaussian integral operators on $\F^2(\C^n)$.  We give a complete
answer to the question as written.  In the normalized monomial expansion of
the kernel, the coefficient array is exactly the operator matrix; boundedness
is therefore equivalent to boundedness of that matrix on $\ellTwo$, with
equality of norms.  We also give an equivalent finite coherent-state
inequality involving only kernel values.  An explicit block example shows
that separate Fock-slice and pointwise-growth conditions are not sufficient.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source problem}

Let
\[
 d\lambda(w)=\pi^{-n}e^{-|w|^2}\,dw
\]
and let $\F^2(\C^n)$ be the corresponding Gaussian Fock space.  The source
asks for a characterization of the entire functions $K(z,w)$ on $\C^{2n}$
for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operator}
 T_KF(z)=\int_{\C^n}K(z,\bar w)F(w)\,d\lambda(w)
\end{equation}
is bounded on $\F^2(\C^n)$~\cite{CaoEtAl2020}.  Figure~\ref{fig:source}
reproduces the exact question and its $T(1)$ motivation.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/source_question_crop.png}
  \caption{The open problem on page 13 of arXiv:1907.00574.}
  \label{fig:source}
\end{figure}

For a multi-index $\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^n$, put
\[
 e_\alpha(z)=\frac{z^\alpha}{\sqrt{\alpha!}}.
\]
The $e_\alpha$ form an orthonormal basis of $\F^2(\C^n)$, and every entire
$K$ has a unique normalized expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expansion}
 K(z,w)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb N_0^n}
 a_{\alpha\beta}e_\alpha(z)e_\beta(w).
\end{equation}

\section{Full characterization}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For an entire function $K$ with expansion \eqref{eq:expansion}, the following
are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The Gaussian integral \eqref{eq:operator}, in its ordinary Lebesgue
interpretation, defines a bounded operator on $\F^2(\C^n)$.
\item The matrix $A=(a_{\alpha\beta})$ defines a bounded operator on
$\ellTwo$.
\item There is a constant $C$ such that, for all finite point families
$z_1,\ldots,z_r,w_1,\ldots,w_s\in\C^n$ and scalar families
$\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_r,\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_s$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:coherent}
&\left|\sum_{i=1}^r\sum_{j=1}^s
 \xi_i\overline{\eta_j}K(z_i,\overline{w_j})\right|\\
&\quad\leq C
 \left(\sum_{i,k=1}^r\xi_i\overline{\xi_k}
 e^{z_i\cdot\overline{z_k}}\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_{j,l=1}^s\eta_j\overline{\eta_l}
 e^{w_j\cdot\overline{w_l}}\right)^{1/2}.\nonumber
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}
Moreover,
\[
 \|T_K\|=\|A\|=\inf\{C:\eqref{eq:coherent}\text{ holds}\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

Thus the answer is intrinsic in either Taylor coefficients or kernel values.
No asymptotic or additional regularity hypothesis is needed.

\section{Proof}

The Gaussian moments satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
 \int_{\C^n}\overline{e_\beta(w)}e_\gamma(w)\,d\lambda(w)
 =\delta_{\beta\gamma}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, whenever \eqref{eq:operator} is bounded, angular integration
against the monomial $e_\beta$ extracts the $\beta$th coefficient in the
second variable and gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix-action}
 T_Ke_\beta=\sum_\alpha a_{\alpha\beta}e_\alpha.
\end{equation}
The Lebesgue interpretation in the theorem justifies the Gaussian moments;
equivalently one may first test on polynomials.  Hence, under the unitary
coefficient map
\[
 U:\F^2(\C^n)\longrightarrow\ellTwo,
 \qquad U\!\left(\sum_\alpha f_\alpha e_\alpha\right)=(f_\alpha)_\alpha,
\]
we have $UT_KU^{-1}=A$.  This proves $(1)\Rightarrow(2)$ and
$\|A\|=\|T_K\|$.

Conversely, assume $A$ is bounded.  Define $T=U^{-1}AU$.  The coherent
vector
\[
 v(z)=(e_\alpha(z))_\alpha
\]
belongs to $\ellTwo$ and satisfies $\|v(z)\|=e^{|z|^2/2}$.  Boundedness of
$A$ and Cauchy--Schwarz imply locally uniform convergence of
\[
 v(z)^T A\overline{v(w)}
 =\sum_{\alpha,\beta}a_{\alpha\beta}
 e_\alpha(z)\overline{e_\beta(w)}
 =K(z,\bar w).
\]
For fixed $z$, the anti-holomorphic slice belongs to $L^2(d\lambda)$ because
$A^T$ is bounded.  Therefore the integral in \eqref{eq:operator} converges
absolutely by Cauchy--Schwarz, and \eqref{eq:moments}, first for polynomials
and then by density, gives $T_K=T$.  This proves $(2)\Rightarrow(1)$.

If $A$ is bounded, then
\[
 \sum_{i,j}\xi_i\overline{\eta_j}K(z_i,\overline{w_j})
 =X^T A\overline{Y},
 \quad
 X=\sum_i\xi_i v(z_i),\quad Y=\sum_j\eta_jv(w_j).
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives \eqref{eq:coherent}, since
\[
 \|X\|^2=\sum_{i,k}\xi_i\overline{\xi_k}
 e^{z_i\cdot\overline{z_k}},
\]
and similarly for $Y$.  This proves $(2)\Rightarrow(3)$ with $C=\|A\|$.

Finally, coherent vectors have dense linear span: a vector orthogonal to all
of them would define an entire Fock function vanishing everywhere, hence
would be zero.  Therefore \eqref{eq:coherent} defines a bounded bilinear form
on dense subspaces of two copies of $\ellTwo$, and it extends with norm at
most $C$.  Its matrix on the standard basis is forced by the Taylor
coefficients to be $A$.  Thus $(3)\Rightarrow(2)$ and the least constant is
$\|A\|$, completing the proof.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ideals}
The operator $T_K$ is compact exactly when $A$ is compact.  It is
Hilbert--Schmidt exactly when
\[
 \sum_{\alpha,\beta}|a_{\alpha\beta}|^2<\infty,
\]
and in that case this sum is $\|T_K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$.
For every bounded kernel,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growth}
 |K(z,\bar w)|\leq\|T_K\|
 e^{(|z|^2+|w|^2)/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The ideal statements follow from unitary equivalence.  The growth estimate is
the coherent-vector bound with one point in each family.
\end{proof}

\section{Why separate kernel tests are insufficient}

The source's Proposition 2.1 records necessary Fock-slice and exponential
growth conditions.  Even together, they do not imply boundedness.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:block}
There is an entire kernel $K$ on $\C^2$ such that every slice belongs to
$\F^2(\C)$ and
\[
 |K(z,w)|\leq C e^{|z|^2+|w|^2},
\]
but $T_K$ is unbounded.  Its normalized coefficient matrix has every row and
every column of $\ell^2$-norm one.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Partition $\mathbb N_0$ into consecutive blocks $I_k$ with $|I_k|=k$ and set
\[
 a_{ij}=\begin{cases}
 k^{-1/2},&i,j\in I_k,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Since $|a_{ij}|\leq1$, the normalized double power series converges locally
uniformly and defines an entire $K$.  Every row and column has squared norm
$k\cdot k^{-1}=1$.  For fixed $z$, the squared Fock norm of the second slice
is
\[
 \sum_k\left|\sum_{i\in I_k}e_i(z)\right|^2
 \leq\sum_k k\sum_{i\in I_k}|e_i(z)|^2
 \leq\sum_{i\geq0}(i+1)\frac{|z|^{2i}}{i!}<\infty.
\]
The other slices are identical.  Also, Cauchy--Schwarz with weights
$(i+1)^{-1}$ gives
\[
 \sum_{i\geq0}\frac{r^i}{\sqrt{i!}}
 \leq C\bigl((r^4+3r^2+1)e^{r^2}\bigr)^{1/2}
 \leq C'e^{r^2},
\]
which proves the stated coarse growth bound for the double series.

On $\ell^2(I_k)$, however, the matrix is $k^{-1/2}J_k$, whose norm is
$\sqrt{k}$.  The full matrix is unbounded, so Theorem~\ref{thm:main} shows
that $T_K$ is unbounded.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof intuition and scope}

\paragraph{Proof intuition.}
The Gaussian weight makes the normalized monomials exactly orthonormal.
Integrating the kernel against one monomial therefore selects one column of
its Taylor array.  Nothing is lost: an arbitrary entire kernel represents an
arbitrary infinite operator matrix.  The coherent-state condition is the
same fact expressed without coordinates; it asks that all finite matrix
coefficients be controlled in the natural reproducing-kernel norm.

The theorem fully answers the displayed all-entire-kernels question.  The
preceding $T(1)$ discussion also suggests a more ambitious goal: local size,
smoothness, cancellation, and testing conditions for a specified Fock
Calderón--Zygmund class.  No such class or hypotheses are specified in the
source, and the class of all entire kernels realizes all of
$B(\ellTwo)$.  A local $T(1)$ theory is therefore a distinct future program,
not an omitted step here.

Six focused routes were audited, including Bargmann conjugation, coherent
states, Schur-type slice tests, and a local $T(1)$ upgrade.  Bounded
primary-source searches through 11 August 2026 found no later general answer
to the displayed problem.  The coefficient argument is elementary RKHS
theory and may be folklore, so novelty confidence is low; the claim is a
literal full characterization, not a claim of a new David--Journé theorem.
