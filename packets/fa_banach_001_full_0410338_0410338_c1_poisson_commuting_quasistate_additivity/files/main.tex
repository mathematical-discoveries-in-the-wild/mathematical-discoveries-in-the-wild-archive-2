\section{Question and answer}

Entov and Polterovich prove strong quasi-additivity on a closed symplectic
surface for smooth Poisson-commuting functions.  They then ask whether the
identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:add}
  \zeta(F+G)=\zeta(F)+\zeta(G)
\end{equation}
extends to Poisson-commuting functions of finite smoothness
\cite[Problem 8.2]{EP}.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Here a quasi-state means an Aarnes quasi-state on $C(M)$: a normalized
positive functional whose restriction to the closed unital algebra generated
by any one function is linear.  No symplectic invariance is needed in the
argument below.

\begin{theorem}[Lipschitz additivity]\label{thm:main}
Let $M$ be a closed surface and let $\zeta$ be any quasi-state on $C(M)$.
If $F,G\in\Lip(M)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wedge}
  dF\wedge dG=0\qquad\text{almost everywhere on }M,
\end{equation}
then \eqref{eq:add} holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Full finite-smoothness answer]\label{cor:c1}
Let $(M,\omega)$ be a closed symplectic surface.  If $F,G\in C^1(M)$ and
$\{F,G\}=0$, then every quasi-state on $C(M)$ satisfies \eqref{eq:add}.
In particular this holds for the quasi-state associated to every symplectic
quasi-measure in the source paper.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, on a symplectic surface
$dF\wedge dG=\{F,G\}\omega$ (up to the harmless sign convention), and every
$C^1$ function on compact $M$ is Lipschitz.  Thus Corollary~\ref{cor:c1}
answers Problem 8.2 at the minimal classical differentiability at which the
Poisson bracket is pointwise defined.

\section{Proof}

The source proof studies the joint image
\[
 \Phi:M\longrightarrow\R^2,\qquad \Phi(x)=(F(x),G(x)),
 \qquad \Delta:=\Phi(M),
\]
and obtains $\dim_{\rm cov}\Delta\leq1$ from a smooth Sard-type theorem.
At Lipschitz regularity, the area formula and elementary dimension theory
give the same topological conclusion more directly.

\begin{lemma}[The joint image is at most one-dimensional]
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main},
$\dim_{\rm cov}\Delta\leq1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose finitely many coordinate patches and compact subsets of them covering
$M$.  In each chart, $\Phi$ is a Lipschitz map between planar sets.  Its
approximate Jacobian is
\[
 J_2\Phi=\bigl|\det D(F,G)\bigr|,
\]
which vanishes almost everywhere by \eqref{eq:wedge}.  The Lipschitz area
formula therefore gives, on every such chart domain $E$,
\[
 \int_{\R^2}N(\Phi,E,y)\,dy
   =\int_E J_2\Phi(x)\,dx=0.
\]
Since $N(\Phi,E,y)\geq1$ on $\Phi(E)$, every chart image has planar
Lebesgue measure zero.  Hence $\cL^2(\Delta)=0$.

The set $\Delta$ is compact, because $M$ is compact and $\Phi$ is continuous.
It has empty interior because it is planar-null.  The classical Euclidean
dimension theorem says that a subset of $\R^2$ has covering dimension $2$
if and only if it contains an interior point; equivalently, every subset
with empty interior has covering dimension at most $1$
\cite[Theorem 1.8.10]{Engelking}.  Thus $\dim_{\rm cov}\Delta\leq1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Define
\[
 \eta:C(\Delta)\longrightarrow\R,
 \qquad \eta(h)=\zeta(h\circ\Phi).
\]
This is a quasi-state.  Positivity and normalization are immediate.  If
$h\in C(\Delta)$, pullback sends the singly generated algebra
$\{\varphi\circ h:\varphi\in C(h(\Delta))\}$ into the singly generated
algebra of $h\circ\Phi$, on which $\zeta$ is linear.

By the Wheeler--Shakhmatov theorem used in the proof of
\cite[Theorem 8.1]{EP}, every quasi-state on a compact metric space of
covering dimension at most one is linear.  Hence $\eta$ is linear.  Apply
linearity to the two coordinate functions $p_1,p_2$ on $\Delta$:
\[
 \zeta(F+G)
 =\eta(p_1+p_2)
 =\eta(p_1)+\eta(p_2)
 =\zeta(F)+\zeta(G).
\]
This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{Checks, scope, and novelty status}

There is no hidden injectivity assumption in the measure step: the area
formula counts multiplicity, and zero Jacobian forces the multiplicity
integral---hence the entire image measure---to vanish.  Nor is Hausdorff
dimension being inferred from measure zero; the proof uses the separate
topological theorem that a planar subset of full covering dimension must
have interior.  This distinction is essential because planar-null sets can
have Hausdorff dimension two.

The Lipschitz statement is stronger than requested and avoids the source's
suggested simultaneous smooth approximation problem.  For $C^1$ functions,
Poisson commutation gives \eqref{eq:wedge} everywhere.  At the Lipschitz
level the natural invariant formulation is exactly \eqref{eq:wedge} almost
everywhere, using Rademacher differentials.  Purely continuous functions
have no intrinsic Poisson bracket, so no still-lower regularity is claimed.

Exact-title, exact-problem-phrase, ``finite smoothness'',
Wheeler--Shakhmatov, area-formula, and Poisson-commuting quasi-state searches
through 11 August 2026 found the source problem and later neighboring work
on $C^0$ Poisson-bracket rigidity, but no paper stating this resolution.
The proof should therefore be treated as a candidate new observation until
an expert completes a citation and novelty audit.
