\section*{Status and source question}

This is a \texttt{candidate\_partial\_likely\_valid} result for Sasmita
Patnaik and Rahul Sethi, \emph{On Simultaneous Triangularization of Matrices
and Quasinilpotency of Commutator of Compact Operators}, arXiv:2401.16916
\cite{PS}.  The unnumbered question on PDF page~2 asks:

\begin{quote}
Is every compact quasinilpotent operator a commutator of compact operators?
\end{quote}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The theorem below answers this for a concrete sparse subfamily, and in fact
does not need quasinilpotence.  The general question remains open.

Fix an orthonormal basis $(e_i)_{i\in\N}$ of a separable Hilbert space.  If
$T=(t_{ij})$, where $t_{ij}=\langle Te_j,e_i\rangle$, its \emph{undirected
support graph} has vertex set $\N$ and an edge $\{i,j\}$, $i\ne j$, exactly
when $t_{ij}\ne0$ or $t_{ji}\ne0$.

\section*{Proof intuition}

A commutator with a diagonal operator is entrywise division by differences
of diagonal entries.  Small differences are the usual obstruction.  Bounded
support degree gives a finite proper coloring.  Put the colors on finitely
many distinct complex rays and give vertex $i$ a radial length equal to the
square root of the largest matrix entry touching it.  Compactness makes these
lengths tend to zero, so the diagonal factor is compact.  Distinct rays keep
the denominator on every support edge comparable to the larger radial
length; after division, the new entry is bounded by the smaller radial
length.  The bounded degree then turns entrywise tail decay into operator-norm
tail decay by the Schur test, proving that the second factor is compact.

This mechanism fails for the currently known universal block-tridiagonal
forms because their scalar support degrees grow with the block sizes.

\section*{Main theorem}

\begin{theorem}[bounded-degree support]\label{thm:main}
Let $T\in\Kc(\Hc)$ have zero diagonal in an orthonormal basis $(e_i)$.
Suppose its undirected support graph has degree at most $\Delta<\infty$.
Then there are compact operators $A,B\in\Kc(\Hc)$ such that
\[
 T=[A,B]=AB-BA.
\]
Moreover, $A$ may be chosen diagonal and $B$ may be chosen with matrix
support contained in that of $T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $G$ for the support graph.  A greedy coloring of a countable graph of
maximum degree $\Delta$ gives a proper coloring
$c:\N\to\{0,\ldots,\Delta\}$.  Choose distinct unit complex numbers
$\zeta_0,\ldots,\zeta_\Delta$.  Since there are only finitely many distinct
rays, the number
\[
 \delta=\min_{p\ne q}\ \min_{0\le s\le1}
       |\zeta_p-s\zeta_q|
\]
is strictly positive.  Consequently, for $r,u\ge0$ and $p\ne q$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:raysep}
 |r\zeta_p-u\zeta_q|\ge\delta\max\{r,u\}.
\end{equation}

For each vertex put
\[
 \rho_i=\sup_{j\ne i}\max\{|t_{ij}|,|t_{ji}|\},
 \qquad a_i=\sqrt{\rho_i},
 \qquad \lambda_i=a_i\zeta_{c(i)}.
\]
(The supremum is zero at an isolated vertex.)  Compactness of $T$ and $T^*$
gives
\[
 \rho_i\le\max\{\|Te_i\|,\|T^*e_i\|\}\longrightarrow0,
\]
because $(e_i)$ is weakly null.  Hence the diagonal operator
$Ae_i=\lambda_i e_i$ is compact.

For $i\ne j$, define
\[
 b_{ij}=\begin{cases}
 \dfrac{t_{ij}}{\lambda_i-\lambda_j},&t_{ij}\ne0,\\[6pt]
 0,&t_{ij}=0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad b_{ii}=0.
\]
Whenever $t_{ij}\ne0$, the vertices $i,j$ are adjacent and hence differently
colored.  Both $\rho_i$ and $\rho_j$ dominate $|t_{ij}|$.  By
\eqref{eq:raysep},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bij}
 |b_{ij}|
 \le \delta^{-1}\frac{|t_{ij}|}{\max\{a_i,a_j\}}
 \le \delta^{-1}\min\{a_i,a_j\}.
\end{equation}
There are at most $\Delta$ nonzero entries in each row and column of
$B=(b_{ij})$.  Thus the Schur test first shows that this matrix defines a
bounded operator.

It also shows compactness.  Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $M$ so that
$a_i<\varepsilon\delta/\Delta$ for $i>M$ (with the evident harmless change
when $\Delta=0$).  The first $M$ vertices have only finitely many neighbors;
choose $N\ge M$ larger than all their indices.  Every support edge with at
least one endpoint greater than $N$ then has both endpoints greater than
$M$.  By \eqref{eq:bij}, all corresponding entries have modulus less than
$\varepsilon/\Delta$.  The row and column sum bounds in the Schur test give
\[
 \|B-P_NBP_N\|<\varepsilon,
\]
where $P_N$ projects onto $\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\ldots,e_N\}$.  Hence
$B$ is a norm limit of finite-rank operators and is compact.

Finally, the diagonal entries of $[A,B]$ vanish, as do those of $T$, and for
$i\ne j$,
\[
 [A,B]_{ij}=(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)b_{ij}=t_{ij}.
\]
Therefore $T=[A,B]$.
\end{proof}

\section*{Consequences and an upgrade}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:band}
Every compact zero-diagonal finite-band matrix is a commutator of two compact
operators.  In particular, every compact unilateral or bilateral weighted
shift is such a commutator.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A matrix of bandwidth $m$ has support degree at most $2m$.  A weighted shift
has support degree at most two.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

Every compact unilateral weighted shift is quasinilpotent: if its weights
tend to zero, its spectral radius is bounded by the supremum of every tail of
the weight sequence.  Taking all weights nonzero gives a nonnilpotent compact
quasinilpotent covered by Corollary~\ref{cor:band}.  Thus the corollary is a
literal nonnilpotent subcase of the source question, not merely a reproof of
the known nilpotent case.

The same argument has a useful block form.

\begin{corollary}[uniform finite blocks]\label{cor:block}
Let $\Hc=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}\Hc_n$, with $1\le\dim\Hc_n\le r<\infty$, and let
$T=(T_{mn})$ be compact.  Suppose the graph joining block vertices $m,n$ when
$T_{mn}\ne0$ or $T_{nm}\ne0$ has uniformly bounded degree, and
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(T_{nn})=0\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
Then $T$ is a commutator of two compact operators.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every trace-zero matrix has an orthonormal basis with zero diagonal.  Indeed,
zero is in the convex hull of its eigenvalues and hence in its numerical
range, so choose a unit vector $x$ with $\langle Sx,x\rangle=0$; the
compression to $x^\perp$ again has trace zero, and induction completes the
basis.  Apply this inside each $\Hc_n$ and take the direct sum of the resulting
unitaries.  The transformed $T$ has zero scalar diagonal.  A scalar vertex is
adjacent to at most $r-1$ vertices in its own block and to at most $r$ times
the block-degree outside it, so the scalar support degree is uniformly
bounded.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} applies, and unitary conjugation transfers
the commutator representation back to $T$.
\end{proof}

\section*{Verification, limitations, and novelty status}

The included script tests the exact factorization, proper coloring, ray
separation, and estimate \eqref{eq:bij} on 2,900 random finite sparse
matrices.  All cases passed with operator-norm residual below $2\cdot10^{-15}$.
This is a regression check only; compactness is proved by the Schur-tail
argument above.

Eight focused routes were examined.  Finite-rank nilpotent approximation
only produces a limit of commutators; a commutator range need not be closed.
The diagonal-derivation route succeeded for bounded-degree zero-diagonal
support and was upgraded to Corollary~\ref{cor:block}.  It does not extend
directly to unbounded degrees.  Universal block-tridiagonalization has
exponentially growing block sizes, while changing to a zero-diagonal basis
does not preserve sparsity.  Hardy-type factorization and stabilization of a
sum of commutators also fail to give the original operator rather than an
amplification.  Finally, the usual eigenvalue/trace constraints yield no
counterexample for quasinilpotents.

The run indexes and bounded web/arXiv searches used the exact source question
and the phrases \emph{zero diagonal commutator of compact operators},
\emph{bounded degree sparse matrix compact commutator}, and \emph{compact
weighted shift commutator compact}.  They located the general
Pearcy--Topping literature, including the block-tridiagonal analysis of
Loreaux--Patnaik--Petrovic--Weiss \cite{LPPW}, but no exact theorem with the
bounded support-degree hypothesis and two compact factors.  Novelty confidence
is moderate pending expert review.  The source's question for arbitrary
compact quasinilpotents remains open.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Check especially the passage from \eqref{eq:bij} to norm compactness of $B$
and search older sparse-matrix commutator literature for an equivalent
formulation.  The proof itself is elementary and otherwise self-contained.
