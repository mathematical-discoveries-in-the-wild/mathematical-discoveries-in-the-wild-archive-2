\section{Source question}

Bobkov and Madiman~\cite{BM} prove that if a probability density on $\R^n$
has the form
\[
 f=\varphi^{-\beta},
\]
where $\varphi$ is positive and convex and $\beta\geq n+1$, then fixing
$\|f\|_\infty$ makes Shannon entropy maximal for the $n$-dimensional Pareto
density. Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sourcebound}
 h(f)+\log\|f\|_\infty
 \leq \beta\sum_{j=1}^n\frac1{\beta-j}.
\end{equation}
In their final discussion they ask whether the theorem can be improved to the
full normalizable range $\beta>n$. The statement is on source PDF page 13:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The result below proves the complete extension when $n=1$. In particular, it
settles the entire range $1<\beta<2$ missing from the source theorem.

\section{Proof intuition}

The source proof is already valid below $n+1$ except at one inverse-Hölder
step: it uses log-concavity of a normalized negative moment only on
$p\geq n+1$. In one dimension the missing extension has an elementary
geometric proof. The length $L(t)$ of the sublevel interval
$\{\varphi<t\}$ is a concave function of $t$. Every nonnegative increasing
concave function on a half-line is an affine term plus a positive superposition
of hinges $\min(t-1,s-1)$. Layer cake turns these hinges into
$1-s^{1-p}$. Consequently
\[
 (p-1)\int\varphi^{-p}
\]
is itself a positive concave function of $p-1$, hence is log-concave for every
$p>1$. This is exactly the strengthened input needed by the source entropy
argument.

\section{A full-range one-dimensional negative-moment lemma}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:moment}
Let $I\subset\R$ be an open interval and let
$\varphi:I\to(0,\infty)$ be convex. Assume that
$Z(p)=\int_I\varphi(x)^{-p}\,dx$ is finite in the range under discussion.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 p\longmapsto (p-1)Z(p)
\end{equation}
is log-concave on $p>1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Multiplying $\varphi$ by a positive constant only multiplies \eqref{eq:H} by
a log-affine function of $p$, so normalize $\inf_I\varphi=1$. Put
\[
 L(t)=\bigl|\{x\in I:\varphi(x)<t\}\bigr|,\qquad t\geq1.
\]
The sublevel sets are intervals. If $s,t\geq1$ and $0<\lambda<1$, convexity
gives
\[
 (1-\lambda)\{\varphi<s\}+\lambda\{\varphi<t\}
 \subseteq
 \{\varphi<(1-\lambda)s+\lambda t\}.
\]
Length is affine under Minkowski addition of intervals, so $L$ is
nonnegative, nondecreasing, and concave.

Let $c=L(1+)$ and let $a\geq0$ be the limiting slope of $L$. Integrating the
decreasing right derivative of $L$ gives a positive measure $\nu$ on
$(1,\infty)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hinge}
 L(t)=c+a(t-1)+\int_{(1,\infty)}\min\{t-1,s-1\}\,\nu(ds).
\end{equation}
The layer-cake formula and Tonelli's theorem yield
\[
 Z(p)=p\int_1^\infty L(t)t^{-p-1}\,dt.
\]
The three terms in \eqref{eq:hinge} can be integrated explicitly. For $p>1$,
\begin{align*}
 (p-1)Z(p)
 &=c(p-1)+a
   +\int_{(1,\infty)}\bigl(1-s^{1-p}\bigr)\,\nu(ds).
\end{align*}
Writing $x=p-1>0$, the right side is
\[
 cx+a+\int_{(1,\infty)}\bigl(1-e^{-x\log s}\bigr)\,\nu(ds).
\]
It is positive and concave in $x$: every integrand has nonpositive second
derivative. Every positive concave function is log-concave, proving the
lemma.
\end{proof}

\section{Sharp entropy bound for every \texorpdfstring{$\beta>1$}{beta > 1}}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $\beta>1$, let $I\subset\R$ be an open interval, and let
$f=\varphi^{-\beta}$ be a probability density on $I$, where
$\varphi:I\to(0,\infty)$ is convex. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 h(f)+\log\|f\|_\infty\leq\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}.
\end{equation}
The constant is sharp. Equality holds for the one-sided Pareto family, up to
affine changes of variable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The quantity on the left of \eqref{eq:main} is invariant under invertible
affine changes of the variable. Normalize $\|f\|_\infty=1$. Then
$\inf_I\varphi=1$ and, with
\[
 Z(p)=\int_I\varphi^{-p},
\]
we have $Z(\beta)=1$.

We first record the elementary estimate used in the source proof:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
 Z(\beta+1)\geq1-\frac1\beta.
\end{equation}
Indeed, fix $y\in I$. Convexity of $\varphi$ and the change of variable
$z=tx+(1-t)y$ give, for $0<t\leq1$,
\[
 t^{-1}
 \geq\int_I\bigl[t\varphi(x)+(1-t)\varphi(y)\bigr]^{-\beta}\,dx,
\]
with equality at $t=1$. Comparing left derivatives at $t=1$ yields
\[
 \beta-1\leq\beta\varphi(y)Z(\beta+1).
\]
Choose $y$ with $\varphi(y)$ arbitrarily close to its infimum $1$ to obtain
\eqref{eq:shift}.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:moment},
\[
 R(p)=\log\bigl((p-1)Z(p)\bigr)
\]
is concave on $p>1$. Therefore
\begin{align*}
 R'(\beta)
 &\geq R(\beta+1)-R(\beta)\\
 &=\log\frac{\beta Z(\beta+1)}{(\beta-1)Z(\beta)}\geq0,
\end{align*}
where the last inequality uses \eqref{eq:shift} and $Z(\beta)=1$. Thus
\[
 \frac1{\beta-1}+Z'(\beta)\geq0.
\]
Since
\[
 h(f)=-\int_I f\log f
     =\beta\int_I\varphi^{-\beta}\log\varphi
     =-\beta Z'(\beta),
\]
we conclude that $h(f)\leq\beta/(\beta-1)$ under the normalization
$\|f\|_\infty=1$. Undoing the affine change proves \eqref{eq:main}.

Finally,
\[
 f_\beta(x)=\left(1+\frac{x}{\beta-1}\right)^{-\beta},
 \qquad x>0,
\]
has mass $1$, supremum $1$, and entropy $\beta/(\beta-1)$. Hence the bound
is sharp.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, higher-dimensional obstruction, and novelty}

All interchanges in Lemma~\ref{lem:moment} involve nonnegative integrands, so
Tonelli applies. For $\beta>1$, convexity plus finite mass ensures the
logarithmic moment is finite; alternatively the conclusion follows first by
truncation and then by monotone convergence. The equality density is checked
by the substitution $u=1+x/(\beta-1)$.

For $n\geq2$, the same sublevel argument only shows that
$L(t)^{1/n}$ is concave. Raising the hinge representation to the $n$th power
does not preserve the direct concavity argument. The full source question
would follow from log-concavity of
\[
 (p-1)\cdots(p-n)\int\varphi^{-p}
 \quad\text{for every }p>n,
\]
but this packet does not prove that assertion. The included script tests exact
piecewise-affine sublevel-radius models in dimensions $2$--$4$ and finds no
counterexample; this is non-proof evidence only.

A bounded literature search used the exact title and arXiv id, the phrase
``improved to only require $\beta>n$'', arXiv's API, Crossref, OpenAlex, and
author publication pages. No explicit later answer was found. However, a 2024
paper~\cite{FM} cites an unpublished/preprint work by Bobkov, Fradelizi,
Langharst, Li, and Madiman titled \emph{When can one invert Hölder's
inequality? (and why one may want to)}. Its text was not located, and it may
overlap with Lemma~\ref{lem:moment} or its higher-dimensional analogue.
Novelty confidence is therefore moderate rather than high.
