\begin{abstract}
Adamowicz and Gonz\'alez ask for quasiregular maps satisfying their
M\"obius-invariant multiplicity condition (M) and Miniowitz estimate (Min),
but not the radial omitted-set hypothesis, preferably maps omitting only the
origin.  The elementary holomorphic map
\[
 F(z)=\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^3
\]
does exactly this.  It is $1$-quasiregular, maps the disk onto
$\C\setminus\{0\}$, has valence at most two, satisfies (M) with exponent
$a=0$, and satisfies (Min) after every conformal recentering with $C=1$ and
$\alpha=3$.  More generally every integer power at least three works.  We
also record the exact Hardy threshold: $F\in\Hp$ precisely for $0<p<1/3$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The source question}

The source is T.~Adamowicz and M.~J.~Gonz\'alez, \emph{Hardy spaces and
quasiregular mappings} \cite{AG}.  Its arXiv identifier is 2309.12947.  For a quasiregular map
$f:B^n\to\mathbb R^n$, condition (M) requires constants $A>0$ and
$0\le a<n-1$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M}
 \sup_{x\in B^n}N(f\circ T_x^{-1},B(0,r))
 \le \frac{A}{(1-r)^a}\qquad(0<r<1),
\end{equation}
where $T_x$ is a conformal automorphism taking $x$ to zero.  Estimate (Min)
requires, uniformly after every such recentering $g=f\circ T_x^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Min}
 \frac1C\left(\frac{1-r}{1+r}\right)^\alpha
 \le \frac{|g(y)|}{|g(0)|}
 \le C\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^\alpha,
 \qquad r=|y|.
\end{equation}

The sufficient hypothesis preceding the question assumes that $f$ omits a
set meeting every sphere centered at the origin.  The authors ask for maps
satisfying \eqref{eq:M} and \eqref{eq:Min} for which that hypothesis is
unnecessary, ``omit[ting] e.g. only the origin.''  The construction below
meets that strongest requested range condition.

\section{The construction}

\begin{theorem}[Cayley-power family]\label{thm:main}
For every integer $m\ge3$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fm}
 H(z)=\frac{1+z}{1-z},\qquad F_m(z)=H(z)^m\qquad(z\in\D).
\end{equation}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $F_m$ is $1$-quasiregular and $F_m(\D)=\C\setminus\{0\}$;
 \item its valence is at most $\lceil m/2\rceil$, so it satisfies (M) with
       $a=0$ and $A=\lceil m/2\rceil$;
 \item after every conformal disk recentering, (Min) holds with
       $C=1$ and $\alpha=m$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, $F_3$ is an at-most-two-valent explicit solution of the source
problem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Cayley map $H$ is a conformal bijection from $\D$ onto the right
half-plane $\mathbb H=\{u:\operatorname{Re}u>0\}$.  The argument interval of
$u^m$ as $u$ ranges over $\mathbb H$ is
$(-m\pi/2,m\pi/2)$.  Its length is $m\pi>2\pi$, so it contains a
representative of every argument modulo $2\pi$.  Every positive modulus is
also attained.  Hence $u\mapsto u^m$ maps $\mathbb H$ onto
$\C\setminus\{0\}$, proving the exact range assertion.  Holomorphicity makes
$F_m$ $1$-quasiregular.

For fixed $w\ne0$, the arguments of its $m$th roots differ by $2\pi/m$.
The open right-half-plane argument interval has length $\pi$, and therefore
contains at most $\lceil m/2\rceil$ of these roots.  Since $H$ is injective,
the same is a global valence bound for $F_m$.  Precomposition by $T_x^{-1}$
does not change it; every restriction to $B(0,r)$ has no larger
multiplicity.  Thus \eqref{eq:M} holds with $a=0$.

It remains to prove the uniform estimate, for which no distortion theorem is
needed.  Fix a disk automorphism $T$, put $g=F_m\circ T^{-1}$,
$z=T^{-1}(y)$, $z_0=T^{-1}(0)$, $u=H(z)$, and $v=H(z_0)$.  Conformal
invariance of the pseudohyperbolic metric gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pseudo}
 r=|y|=\left|\frac{u-v}{u+\overline v}\right|.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\[
 \bigl||u|-|v|\bigr|\le |u-v|
 =r|u+\overline v|\le r(|u|+|v|).
\]
Rearranging this inequality in both directions yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
 \frac{1-r}{1+r}\le\frac{|u|}{|v|}
 \le\frac{1+r}{1-r}.
\end{equation}
Since $|g(y)|/|g(0)|=(|u|/|v|)^m$, raising \eqref{eq:ratio} to the
$m$th power is exactly \eqref{eq:Min} with $C=1$ and $\alpha=m$.
\end{proof}

Because the range of $F_m$ is all of $\C\setminus\{0\}$, every set omitted by
$F_m$ is contained in $\{0\}$.  It therefore cannot meet any centered circle
of positive radius.  Thus the construction does not merely use a smaller
omitted set; it fails the source's radial omitted-set condition maximally.

\section{Exact Hardy membership}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:hardy}
For $m\ge3$ and $p>0$,
\[
 F_m\in\mathcal H^p(\D)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad mp<1.
\]
In particular, $F_3\in\mathcal H^p$ exactly when $0<p<1/3$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The only boundary singularity is at $z=1$.  Uniformly for $r$ close to one
and $|t|$ small,
\[
 |F_m(re^{it})|^p
 =\frac{|1+re^{it}|^{mp}}{|1-re^{it}|^{mp}}
 \asymp \bigl((1-r)^2+t^2\bigr)^{-mp/2}.
\]
The integrals of the right-hand side over a fixed small interval are
uniformly bounded as $r\uparrow1$ exactly when $mp<1$.  At $mp=1$ they grow
logarithmically, and for $mp>1$ they grow as a negative power of $1-r$.
Away from $t=0$ the integrand is uniformly bounded.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and literature scope}

The proof checks the source's stronger M\"obius-invariant form of (M), not
only multiplicity in centered Euclidean balls.  Equation \eqref{eq:pseudo}
also establishes (Min) separately for every recentering, with constants
independent of the center.  No boundary-value theorem is used.

A bounded search on 13 August 2026 used the exact source sentence, the paper
title, Miniowitz-class terminology, bounded multiplicity, and the displayed
Cayley power.  It found the source and later work noting abstractly that the
Miniowitz class includes bounded-multiplicity maps, but no explicit
Cayley-power construction.  The source itself cites the general
bounded-multiplicity estimate.  Accordingly, this packet claims an explicit
full answer and elementary sharp verification, not priority for that general
structural fact.
