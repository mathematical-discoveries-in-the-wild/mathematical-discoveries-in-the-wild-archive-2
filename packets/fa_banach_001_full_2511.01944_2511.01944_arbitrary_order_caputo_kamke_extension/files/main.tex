\textbf{Status.} Candidate full positive answer to one of the explicit closing
questions in arXiv:2511.01944.  The source proves its existence theorem only
for $0<\alpha<1$ and asks for an extension to arbitrary $\alpha>0$.  The
theorem below supplies that extension, including all initial data and an
explicit full-interval criterion.

\section{Statement}

Let $E$ be a real Banach space, $\alpha>0$, and $m=\lceil\alpha\rceil$ (with
$m=\alpha$ when $\alpha$ is an integer).  Given $u_0,\ldots,u_{m-1}\in E$, put
\[
 P(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{(t-a)^j}{j!}u_j.                 \tag{1}
\]
For this packet the arbitrary-order operators are defined by
\[
 D_{a+}^{\alpha}h=D^mJ^{m-\alpha}h,
 \qquad {}^CD_{a+}^{\alpha}u=D_{a+}^{\alpha}(u-P),           \tag{1a}
\]
where $J^\rho$ is the Riemann--Liouville integral (and $J^0$ is the identity),
whenever the right sides exist.  At integer order this is the ordinary
$m$th derivative; below order one it is exactly the source's definition.
Fix $\widetilde\delta,\beta>0$, set $\widetilde I=[a,a+\widetilde\delta]$, and
consider the tube
\[
 R=\{(t,x):t\in\widetilde I,\ \|x-P(t)\|\leq\beta\}.
\]
A sublinear measure of noncompactness $\mu$ is understood in exactly the
axiomatic sense of the source, and it has the \emph{singleton property} if
$\mu(\{x\})=0$ for every $x\in E$.

We call a continuous
$w_\alpha:\widetilde I\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ a Kamke function of
order $\alpha$ when $w_\alpha(t,0)=0$ and zero is the unique nonnegative
continuous function $z$ satisfying
\[
 z(t)\leq \frac1{\Ga(\alpha)}\int_a^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}
 w_\alpha(s,z(s))\,ds,\qquad
 \frac{z(t)}{(t-a)^\alpha}\longrightarrow0.                \tag{2}
\]
This is the source definition, now made for every positive order.

\begin{theorem}[arbitrary-order extension]\label{thm:main}
Suppose $f:R\to E$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $f$ is uniformly continuous on $R$;
\item $\|f(t,x)\|\leq M$ on $R$ for some $M>0$;
\item for every $t\in\widetilde I$ and every nonempty bounded
      $X\subseteq\B(P(t),\beta)$,
\[
 \mu(f(t,X))\leq w_\alpha(t,\mu(X)).                         \tag{3}
\]
\end{enumerate}
Then the Caputo problem
\[
 {}^CD_{a+}^{\alpha}u(t)=f(t,u(t)),\qquad
 u^{(j)}(a)=u_j\quad(0\leq j<m)                              \tag{4}
\]
has a solution on $I=[a,a+\delta]$, where
\[
 \boxed{\ \delta=\min\left\{\widetilde\delta,
 \left(\frac{\beta\Ga(\alpha+1)}{M}\right)^{1/\alpha}
 \right\}.\ }                                               \tag{5}
\]
The constructed $u$ is continuous, the derivative in (1a) exists and is
continuous, and all identities in (4) hold.  It is obtained as a fixed point
of the corresponding Volterra equation (12).
\end{theorem}

For $0<\alpha<1$, $m=1$ and $P\equiv u_0$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is
exactly the source theorem.  The moving tube is necessary for higher order:
the prescribed initial derivatives generate $P$ before the nonlinear term is
integrated.

\begin{corollary}[whole prescribed interval]\label{cor:global}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, if
\[
 M\widetilde\delta^\alpha\leq\beta\Ga(\alpha+1),             \tag{6}
\]
then the solution exists on all of $\widetilde I$.  In particular, this gives
the full-interval extension asked about separately by the source whenever its
boundedness and noncompactness hypotheses hold on an invariant tube satisfying
(6).
\end{corollary}

\section{Two kernel facts for every positive order}

Write
\[
 (J^\alpha g)(t)=\frac1{\Ga(\alpha)}
 \int_a^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}g(s)\,ds.                           \tag{7}
\]

\begin{lemma}[uniform modulus]\label{lem:mod}
If $\|g(t)\|\leq M$ on $I$, then
\[
 \|J^\alpha g(t)-J^\alpha g(s)\|\leq L_\alpha|t-s|^q,
 \qquad q=\min\{\alpha,1\},                                \tag{8}
\]
where one can take
\[
 L_\alpha=\begin{cases}
 2M/\Ga(\alpha+1),&0<\alpha<1,\\
 M,&\alpha=1,\\
 M\delta^{\alpha-1}/\Ga(\alpha),&\alpha>1.
 \end{cases}                                                  \tag{9}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $0<\alpha<1$, split the two Volterra integrals at the smaller time and
integrate the difference of the decreasing kernels; this gives the first
line.  At $\alpha=1$ the assertion is immediate.  For $\alpha>1$,
$J^\alpha g$ is differentiable with derivative $J^{\alpha-1}g$, whose norm is
at most $M\delta^{\alpha-1}/\Ga(\alpha)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[noncompactness through the kernel]\label{lem:mnc}
Let $H\subset\C(I,E)$ be bounded and uniformly equicontinuous.  For every
$t\in I$,
\[
 \mu\left(\left\{\int_a^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}h(s)\,ds:h\in H\right\}\right)
 \leq\int_a^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\mu(H(s))\,ds.                 \tag{10}
\]
This holds for every $\alpha>0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The source proves (10) by approximating the Bochner integrals with common
Riemann sums and using homogeneity and subadditivity of $\mu$.  The proof uses
only that the scalar kernel is nonnegative and integrable.  Both properties
hold for $(t-s)^{\alpha-1}$ for every $\alpha>0$; its being singular when
$\alpha<1$ is not otherwise used.  Passing to the limit is justified by the
common equicontinuity modulus, exactly as in the source.
\end{proof}

For continuous $g$, the semigroup identity gives directly
\[
 D_{a+}^{\alpha}J^\alpha g
 =D^mJ^{m-\alpha}J^\alpha g=D^mJ^m g=g.                     \tag{11}
\]
Moreover, the derivatives of $J^\alpha g$ through order $m-1$ vanish at
$a$.  Consequently every fixed point of
\[
 u(t)=P(t)+J^\alpha(f(\cdot,u(\cdot)))(t).                  \tag{12}
\]
satisfies both the differential equation and the full initial jet in (4).

\section{Proof of the theorem}

The numerical choice (5) gives
\[
 \frac{M\delta^\alpha}{\Ga(\alpha+1)}\leq\beta.            \tag{13}
\]
Let $\omega_P$ be the modulus of continuity of $P$ on $I$, let $q,L_\alpha$
be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:mod}, and define $X_0\subset\C(I,E)$ by
\[
 \begin{split}
 X_0=\{u:\ &\|u(t)-P(t)\|\leq\beta,\quad u(a)=u_0,\\
 &\|u(t)-u(s)\|\leq\omega_P(|t-s|)+L_\alpha|t-s|^q\}.
 \end{split}                                                  \tag{14}
\]
It is nonempty, bounded, closed, convex, and uniformly equicontinuous.  Define
on $X_0$
\[
 (Fu)(t)=P(t)+J^\alpha(f(\cdot,u(\cdot)))(t).                \tag{15}
\]
Equations (8) and (13) show $F(X_0)\subseteq X_0$.  Uniform continuity of
$f$ makes $F$ continuous.

Set
\[
 X_{k+1}=\cl\co F(X_k),\qquad v_k(t)=\mu(X_k(t)).             \tag{16}
\]
Then $X_{k+1}\subseteq X_k$.  The common modulus in (14) and the standard
evaluation estimate for a sublinear measure of noncompactness make
$\{v_k\}$ uniformly equicontinuous.  It is uniformly bounded and decreases
pointwise, hence converges uniformly to a continuous $v_\infty\geq0$.

Uniform continuity of $f$ and the common modulus (14) ensure that
$f(\cdot,X_k(\cdot))$ is uniformly equicontinuous.  The singleton property
removes the deterministic polynomial $P(t)$ from the measure estimate.
Lemma~\ref{lem:mnc}, (3), and (16) give
\[
 v_{k+1}(t)\leq\frac1{\Ga(\alpha)}\int_a^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}
 w_\alpha(s,v_k(s))\,ds.                                    \tag{17}
\]
Uniform convergence of $v_k$ and uniform continuity of $w_\alpha$ on the
compact range involved allow passage to the limit, so $v_\infty$ satisfies
the first part of (2).

It remains to verify the small-time condition in (2); this is the only step
where the higher-order initial data might conceivably cause trouble.  Put
\[
 y(t)=P(t)+\frac{(t-a)^\alpha}{\Ga(\alpha+1)}f(a,u_0).        \tag{18}
\]
For every $u\in X_0$,
\[
 \|Fu(t)-y(t)\|
 \leq\frac{(t-a)^\alpha}{\Ga(\alpha+1)}\gamma(t),            \tag{19}
\]
where
\[
 \gamma(t)=\sup_{u\in X_0}\sup_{a\leq s\leq t}
 \|f(s,u(s))-f(a,u_0)\|\longrightarrow0.                    \tag{20}
\]
Indeed, (14) gives $u(s)\to u_0$ uniformly over $u\in X_0$ as $s\downarrow
a$, and then uniform continuity of $f$ gives (20).  Inequality (19) persists
under closed convex hulls.  Homogeneity, subadditivity, and the singleton
property therefore yield
\[
 v_1(t)\leq\frac{(t-a)^\alpha\gamma(t)}{\Ga(\alpha+1)}
 \mu(\B(0,1)).                                                \tag{21}
\]
Since $0\leq v_\infty\leq v_1$, the second part of (2) follows.  Hence
$v_\infty\equiv0$.

The usual measure of noncompactness on $\C(I,E)$ combines the common
equicontinuity modulus with $\sup_t\mu(X_k(t))$.  Its value on $X_k$ tends to
zero.  The intersection axiom consequently gives a nonempty relatively
compact closed convex set
\[
 X_\infty=\bigcap_{k\geq1}X_k,\qquad F(X_\infty)\subseteq X_\infty. \tag{22}
\]
Schauder's theorem supplies a fixed point of $F$ in $X_\infty$.  By (11)--(12)
it solves (4).  This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; Corollary~\ref{cor:global}
is immediate from (5).

\section{Checks, scope, and literature screen}

The proof was checked separately in the three kernel regimes
$0<\alpha<1$, $\alpha=1$, and $\alpha>1$.  The only changes after order one
are the polynomial initial jet and the Lipschitz, rather than fractional
Hölder, modulus in Lemma~\ref{lem:mod}.  The decisive estimate (21) remains
of exact order $(t-a)^\alpha$ because the whole initial polynomial is placed
in the deterministic center $y(t)$.

On 12 August 2026 the run indexes and current arXiv/web records were searched
for the source identifier, its title, arbitrary-order Caputo equations in
Banach spaces, Kamke functions, and measure-of-noncompactness existence
theorems.  Related arbitrary-order, semigroup, Hilfer, weak-solution, and
special-measure results were found, but no result matching this source's
general sublinear-measure/Kamke theorem with its singleton hypothesis and
arbitrary initial jet was located.  This bounded search supports, but cannot
guarantee, novelty.
