\begin{abstract}
Bez, Nakamura, and Tsuji ask whether their sharp Talagrand deficit estimate
for $0<\beta<1$ remains true under the sole hypothesis
$\Cov(\mu)\leq\beta\Id$.  We prove this and a sharper anisotropic theorem.  If
$0<\Sigma=\Cov(\mu)\leq\Id$, then
\[
 \frac12W_2(\gamma,\mu)^2-H(\mu\mid\gamma)
 \leq \frac12\log\det\Sigma-\tr\sqrt\Sigma+n.
\]
Equality holds exactly for translates of $N(0,\Sigma)$.  The proof is a short
combination of the Brenier change-of-variables identity, Gaussian integration
by parts, concavity of $\log\det$, and a block-covariance inequality.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source question}

Let $\gamma=N(0,\Id)$ on $\R^n$, and write
\[
 H(\mu\mid\gamma)=\int_{\R^n}\log\!\left(\frac{d\mu}{d\gamma}\right)d\mu.
\]
The source paper \cite{BezNakamuraTsuji} proves, under a
$\beta$-semi-log-concavity hypothesis, that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
 \frac12W_2(\gamma,\mu)^2-H(\mu\mid\gamma)
 \leq n\left(1+\frac12\log\beta-\sqrt\beta\right)
 \qquad(0<\beta<1).
\end{equation}
In Section 1.4 it asks whether \eqref{eq:source} remains true under only
$\Cov(\mu)\leq\beta\Id$.  Figure~\ref{fig:question} reproduces Theorem 1.5,
the explicit sharp value, and the complete two-page question statement.

Here covariance is centered, so translations are permitted.  In fact, every
translate of $N(0,\beta\Id)$ attains equality in \eqref{eq:source}.

\section{Proof intuition}

Let $T$ be the Brenier map carrying the standard Gaussian to $\mu$.  The
Talagrand deficit can be written using only the Jacobian $A=\nabla T$:
\[
 \frac12W_2^2-H=\E[\log\det A]-\tr(\E A)+n.
\]
Concavity of $\log\det$ says that replacing $A$ by its mean can only increase
this expression.  Gaussian integration by parts identifies $M=\E A$ with the
cross-covariance of $T(X)$ and $X$.  Positivity of their joint covariance then
gives $M^2\leq\Cov(\mu)$.  The remaining scalar-looking matrix functional
$\log\det M-\tr M+n$ is increasing in Loewner order below the identity.  This
both proves the conjecture and retains the full covariance spectrum.

\section{Sharp anisotropic theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Covariance-sharp Talagrand deficit]\label{thm:main}
Let $\mu$ be an absolutely continuous probability measure on $\R^n$ with
finite second moment, and let $\Sigma=\Cov(\mu)$.  If
$0<\Sigma\leq\Id$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix}
 \boxed{
 \frac12W_2(\gamma,\mu)^2-H(\mu\mid\gamma)
 \leq \frac12\log\det\Sigma-\tr\sqrt\Sigma+n.}
\end{equation}
If the relative entropy is finite, equality holds if and only if $\mu$ is a
translate of $N(0,\Sigma)$.

In particular, if $0<\beta<1$ and $\Sigma\leq\beta\Id$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar}
 \frac12W_2(\gamma,\mu)^2-H(\mu\mid\gamma)
 \leq n\left(1+\frac12\log\beta-\sqrt\beta\right).
\end{equation}
Equality in \eqref{eq:scalar} holds if and only if $\mu$ is a translate of
$N(0,\beta\Id)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $H(\mu\mid\gamma)=+\infty$, the assertion is immediate, so assume it is
finite.  We first give the argument in the smooth nondegenerate setting; the
standard approximation step is recorded at the end.

Let $X\sim\gamma$ and let $T=\nabla\varphi$ be the Brenier map transporting
$\gamma$ to $\mu$; see, for example, \cite[Chapter 10]{Villani}.  Put
$A(x)=\nabla T(x)=\nabla^2\varphi(x)>0$.  The change-of-variables formula
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-map}
 H(\mu\mid\gamma)
 =\frac12\E\bigl[|T(X)|^2-|X|^2\bigr]-\E\log\det A(X).
\end{equation}
Since $T$ is the optimal quadratic-cost map,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:w2-map}
 W_2(\gamma,\mu)^2=\E|T(X)-X|^2.
\end{equation}
Subtracting \eqref{eq:entropy-map} from half of \eqref{eq:w2-map}, and using
$\E|X|^2=n$, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deficit-map}
 \frac12W_2^2-H=n-\E[X\cdot T(X)]+\E\log\det A(X).
\end{equation}

Let $m=\E T(X)$ and $M=\E A(X)$.  Gaussian integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stein}
 M=\E\bigl[(T(X)-m)X^{\mathsf T}\bigr],
 \qquad
 \E[X\cdot T(X)]=\tr M.
\end{equation}
In particular, $M$ is symmetric positive definite.  Concavity of the matrix
function $\log\det$ now turns \eqref{eq:deficit-map} into
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jensen}
 \frac12W_2^2-H
 \leq \log\det M-\tr M+n=:F(M).
\end{equation}

The joint covariance matrix of $(T(X),X)$ is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \Sigma&M\\ M&\Id
 \end{pmatrix}\geq0.
\]
Its Schur complement gives $M^2\leq\Sigma$.  By operator monotonicity of the
positive square root,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix-order}
 0<M\leq\sqrt\Sigma\leq\Id.
\end{equation}

It remains only to observe that $F$ is increasing in Loewner order on the
positive matrices below $\Id$.  Indeed, if $0<B\leq C\leq\Id$ and
$C_t=B+t(C-B)$, then
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}F(C_t)
 =\tr\bigl[(C_t^{-1}-\Id)(C-B)\bigr]\geq0.
\]
Applying this with \eqref{eq:matrix-order} gives
\[
 \frac12W_2^2-H
 \leq F(\sqrt\Sigma)
 =\frac12\log\det\Sigma-\tr\sqrt\Sigma+n,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:matrix}.  If $\Sigma\leq\beta\Id<\Id$, the same
monotonicity gives
\[
 F(\sqrt\Sigma)\leq F(\sqrt\beta\Id)
 =n\left(1+\frac12\log\beta-\sqrt\beta\right),
\]
proving the source conjecture.

For equality, strict concavity of $\log\det$ in \eqref{eq:jensen} forces
$A(x)$ to be constant almost everywhere.  Hence $T(x)=Mx+a$ and $\mu$ is a
Gaussian translate.  The covariance and the equality in
\eqref{eq:matrix-order} force $M=\sqrt\Sigma$.  Equality in the scalar step
further forces $\Sigma=\beta\Id$.  Conversely, direct substitution shows
that precisely these Gaussian translates attain equality.

For completeness, the same chain works directly without smoothness.  Let
$A$ be the density of the absolutely continuous part of the distributional
Hessian of $\varphi$, and define the cross-covariance
$M=\E[(T(X)-m)X^{\mathsf T}]$.  The generalized Monge--Amp\`ere formula gives
\eqref{eq:entropy-map} with this Alexandrov derivative.  Distributional
Gaussian integration by parts gives
\[
 \E A\leq M,
\]
because the singular part of the Hessian is a positive-semidefinite
matrix-valued measure.  Therefore Jensen and monotonicity of $\log\det$ give
\[
 \E\log\det A\leq\log\det(\E A)\leq\log\det M.
\]
The block-covariance argument for $M$ is unchanged, proving the stated
generality.  Equivalently, this passage can be obtained by the standard smooth
approximation in the Brenier change-of-variables method
\cite[Chapter 10]{Villani}.
\end{proof}

\section{Checks, scope, and novelty status}

For $\mu=N(a,\Sigma)$, the standard Gaussian formulas give
\[
 H(\mu\mid\gamma)
 =\frac12\bigl(\tr\Sigma+|a|^2-n-\log\det\Sigma\bigr),
\]
\[
 W_2(\gamma,\mu)^2
 =|a|^2+\tr(\Id+\Sigma-2\sqrt\Sigma).
\]
Their deficit is exactly the right side of \eqref{eq:matrix}; the mean cancels,
as required.  The accompanying script checks this identity and the scalar
specialization on 10,000 random covariance spectra and translations.

The theorem resolves only the first problem in Section 1.4.  It does not
address the source's separate semi-log-superharmonic hypercontractivity/LSI
question or the appended Mahler discussion.

For novelty, the run indexes were searched by arXiv id, title, authors, and
core phrases.  A bounded primary-source web search through 9 August 2026
checked arXiv:2201.12478, Mikulincer's small-covariance result
arXiv:1906.05904, the dimensional Talagrand refinements in arXiv:1507.01086,
and later searches combining ``Talagrand deficit'', ``small covariance'',
``Brenier'', and ``log det''.  No explicit resolution of the question or
statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} was found.  This is bounded evidence for
candidate novelty, not an exhaustive claim.

\section{Human-review recommendation}

The core proof is the four-step chain
\[
 \text{transport identity}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{Jensen}\
 \Longrightarrow\ M^2\leq\Sigma\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{Loewner monotonicity}.
\]
The result appears likely valid and resolves the source conjecture sharply.
A human reviewer should focus on the approximation paragraph and perform an
independent literature search for the same argument under a covariance-matrix
or Gaussian optimal-transport formulation.
