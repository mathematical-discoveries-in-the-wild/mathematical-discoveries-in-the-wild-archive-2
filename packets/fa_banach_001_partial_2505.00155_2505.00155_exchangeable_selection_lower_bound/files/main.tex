\begin{abstract}
Burstein proves an upper bound with loss
$(\log\log n)^{\alpha/2}$ when selecting
$n/(e\log^{\alpha+1}n)$ functions from a bounded orthogonal system, and
shows that independent Bernoulli selection cannot improve this loss.  We
extend the obstruction from Bernoulli thinning to every exchangeable
(permutation-invariant) random selector that returns at least the target
cardinality.  Along an explicit sequence of dimensions, the trigonometric
system incurs the full loss with probability tending to one.  The proof uses
sampling without replacement, negative correlation of disjoint full-block
events, a second-moment inequality, and a Dirichlet-kernel lower bound in the
Luxemburg norm.  This is a substantial randomized-selection subcase; it does
not resolve the unrestricted deterministic existence question.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source question}

The source is Will Burstein, \emph{$\Lambda_p$ Style Bounds in Orlicz Spaces
Close to $L^2$}, arXiv:2505.00155v2 \cite{Burstein}.  On page 13, after
Theorem 2.10 and in Remark 2.11, the paper states that the optimality of its
equation (2.4) remains open.  The relevant source passage is reproduced below.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Fix $\alpha>0$.  Let $\Phi$ be the Young function used in the source:
for fixed constants $u_0\ge e$ and $c(\alpha)>0$,
\[
 \Phi(u)=u^2\log^\alpha u\quad (u\ge u_0),
 \qquad
 \Phi(u)=c(\alpha)u^2\quad (u<u_0).
\]
For $J\subseteq[n]$, define the Fourier synthesis norm
\[
 \mathcal N_\Phi(J)
 =\sup_{\|a\|_2\le1}
 \left\|\sum_{j\in J}a_j e^{2\pi i jx}\right\|_{L^\Phi(\T)},
\]
where the Orlicz space has its Luxemburg norm.  The source proves a matching
lower bound for an independent Bernoulli set.  The open question asks whether
the logarithmic loss is necessary for the existence of a subset, without
restricting how that subset is chosen.

\section{Proof intuition}

The Bernoulli lower bound in the source partitions the frequency interval into
short consecutive blocks.  If a random set contains one complete block, then
the normalized Dirichlet kernel on that block has Orlicz norm of order
$(\log\log n)^{\alpha/2}$.  Independence makes a full block likely.

Independence is not essential.  If one samples a fixed number of indices
without replacement, disjoint full-block events are negatively correlated.
Their expected number tends to infinity on a suitable dimension subsequence,
and the elementary second-moment inequality then makes a full block occur
with probability tending to one.  An exchangeable selector is uniform after
conditioning on its cardinality.  Monotonicity therefore transfers the same
obstruction to every exchangeable selector with at least the target size.

What remains open is precisely the non-exchangeable, label-dependent choice:
a specially constructed deterministic subset may avoid every full consecutive
block, so this proof does not establish deterministic optimality.

\section{The partial theorem}

Recall that a random subset $J\subseteq[n]$ is \emph{exchangeable} if its law
is invariant under every permutation of $[n]$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Fix $\alpha>0$ and put $\rho=\alpha+1$ and
$C=2(\rho+1)$.  For integers $m\ge2$, set
\[
 q_m=\lceil Cm\rceil,\qquad
 B_m=m^{q_m},\qquad
 n_m=mB_m,
\]
and
\[
 k_m=\left\lceil
 \frac{n_m}{e(\log n_m)^\rho}
 \right\rceil.
\]
For each $m$, let $J_m\subseteq[n_m]$ have an arbitrary exchangeable law
satisfying $|J_m|\ge k_m$ almost surely.  Then there is a constant
$c_\alpha>0$ such that
\[
 \Pp\left\{
 \mathcal N_\Phi(J_m)
 \ge c_\alpha(\log\log n_m)^{\alpha/2}
 \right\}\longrightarrow1
 \qquad(m\to\infty).
\]
In particular, no exchangeable random selection rule at the source
cardinality can replace the factor $(\log\log n)^{\alpha/2}$ by
$o((\log\log n)^{\alpha/2})$, even along all sufficiently large members of
this explicit dimension subsequence.
\end{theorem}

We separate the analytic and probabilistic ingredients.

\begin{lemma}[A complete consecutive block is bad]\label{lem:dirichlet}
There are $c_\alpha>0$ and $m_0(\alpha)$ such that, whenever
$m\ge m_0(\alpha)$ and $J$ contains $m$ consecutive integers,
\[
 \mathcal N_\Phi(J)\ge c_\alpha(\log m)^{\alpha/2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let the block be $\{r+1,\ldots,r+m\}$ and take coefficients $m^{-1/2}$
on the block and zero elsewhere.  Multiplication by $e^{2\pi irx}$ does not
change the absolute value.  For $0\le x\le1/(8m)$, every real part in
$\sum_{j=1}^m e^{2\pi ijx}$ is at least $1/\sqrt2$.  Hence the normalized
sum $f$ satisfies
\[
 |f(x)|\ge\sqrt{m/2}
 \quad\text{on a set of measure }\frac1{8m}.
\]

Set $w=c(\log m)^{\alpha/2}$, where $c>0$ will be fixed sufficiently small.
For large $m$, $\sqrt{m/2}/w\ge u_0$ and
\[
 \log\left(\frac{\sqrt{m/2}}w\right)\ge\frac13\log m.
\]
Therefore the Luxemburg modular at scale $w$ is bounded below by
\begin{align*}
 \int_\T \Phi(|f|/w),dx
 &\ge \frac1{8m}\frac{m}{2w^2}
 \log^\alpha\left(\frac{\sqrt{m/2}}w\right)\\
 &\ge \frac{1}{16\,3^\alpha c^2}.
\end{align*}
Choose $c=c_\alpha$ so that the last expression is greater than $1$.
The definition of the Luxemburg norm gives $\|f\|_\Phi\ge w$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A uniform target-size set contains a full block]\label{lem:block}
Partition $[n_m]$ into the $B_m$ consecutive blocks of size $m$.  If
$U_m$ is uniform among the $k_m$-element subsets of $[n_m]$, then
\[
 \Pp\{U_m\text{ contains at least one complete block}\}\longrightarrow1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $n=n_m$, $k=k_m$, and $B=B_m$.  If $X_t$ is the indicator that the
$t$-th block is contained in $U_m$, then
\[
 p_m:=\E X_t
 =\frac{(k)_m}{(n)_m},
 \qquad
 \lambda_m:=\E\sum_{t=1}^B X_t=Bp_m,
\]
where $(r)_m=r(r-1)\cdots(r-m+1)$.

Put $\delta_m=1/[e(\log n_m)^\rho]$.  Since $k_m/n_m=\delta_m(1+o(1))$
and $m^2/k_m\to0$, expansion of the product gives
\[
 \log p_m=m\log\delta_m+o(1).
\]
Also
\[
 \log n_m=(q_m+1)\log m,
 \quad
 \log\log n_m=\log m+\log\log m+O(1).
\]
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
 \log\lambda_m
 &=q_m\log m-m-m\rho\log\log n_m+o(m)\\
 &=(C-\rho)m\log m-\rho m\log\log m+O(m)
 \longrightarrow+\infty,
\end{align*}
because $C>\rho$.  Thus $\lambda_m\to\infty$.

For two distinct blocks, sampling without replacement gives
\[
 \E(X_sX_t)=\frac{(k)_{2m}}{(n)_{2m}}\le p_m^2.
\]
Indeed, conditional on containing the first block, the probability of
containing the second is $(k-m)_m/(n-m)_m$, and each factor satisfies
\[
 \frac{k-m-j}{n-m-j}\le\frac{k-j}{n-j}
 \qquad(0\le j<m).
\]
For $X=\sum_tX_t$, it follows that
\[
 \E X^2\le\lambda_m+\lambda_m^2.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to $X\mathbf1_{\{X>0\}}$ yields
\[
 \Pp(X>0)\ge\frac{(\E X)^2}{\E X^2}
 \ge\frac{\lambda_m}{1+\lambda_m}\longrightarrow1.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Condition on $|J_m|=s$.  Exchangeability makes $J_m$ uniform among the
$s$-element subsets of $[n_m]$.  For $s\ge k_m$, a uniform $s$-element set
can be coupled to contain a uniform $k_m$-element set: first sample the
$s$-element set and then choose $k_m$ of its elements uniformly.  Hence the
probability of containing a complete block is at least the probability in
Lemma~\ref{lem:block}, uniformly for every $s\ge k_m$.

On that event, Lemma~\ref{lem:dirichlet} gives
\[
 \mathcal N_\Phi(J_m)\ge c_\alpha(\log m)^{\alpha/2}.
\]
Finally, $\log m\sim\log\log n_m$.  Absorb the asymptotic comparison into
$c_\alpha$ and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:block}.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and novelty notes}

The proof uses no computation and no unproved external theorem.  The
second-moment inequality is proved in line, as are the negative-correlation
comparison and the Luxemburg lower bound.  The main quantifier to check is
that the exchangeable law may vary arbitrarily with $m$; conditioning on its
cardinality removes that dependence.

A bounded novelty check on August 11, 2026 searched the run indexes for
arXiv:2505.00155 and the core endpoint-Orlicz terms, then searched arXiv for
the exact title and for combinations of ``exchangeable selection'', ``uniform
fixed-cardinality'', ``sampling without replacement'', Orlicz, and bounded
orthogonal systems.  The search found the source paper and background papers
on $\Lambda(\Phi)$ sets and Orlicz sampling, but no later resolution of the
source question and no matching exchangeable-selector theorem.

\section{Limitations and human-review recommendation}

This packet does not prove that every deterministic subset of size $k_m$ is
bad.  The full-block event is avoidable by a label-dependent choice, so the
source's unrestricted optimality question remains open.  The theorem instead
shows that replacing independent Bernoulli sampling by any other
permutation-invariant random cardinality rule cannot remove the loss.

The result is recommended for expert review as a likely valid substantial
partial theorem.  The most useful checks are: (i) the asymptotic lower bound
for $Bp_m$, (ii) the negative-correlation inequality for two disjoint blocks,
and (iii) the distinction between exchangeable randomized selection and an
unrestricted deterministic subset.
