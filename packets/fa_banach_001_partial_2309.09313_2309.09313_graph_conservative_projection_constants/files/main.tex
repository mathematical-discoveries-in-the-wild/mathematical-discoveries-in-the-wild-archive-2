\section{Source question and normalization}

Schlumprecht asks for the smallest norm of an extension of the integral
operator from conservative vector fields on a finite weighted graph to all
vector fields \cite{Schlumprecht}.  The exact source passage is reproduced
below.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.99\linewidth]{figures/source_question.png}
  \caption{The question on page 33 of arXiv:2309.09313v1.}
\end{figure}

Fix one orientation of each edge.  Write
\(\VF(G)=\ell_\infty(E(G))\), and identify \(\CVF(G)\) with the
subspace of fields whose weighted integral around every cycle is zero.
Define the relative projection constant
\[
 \lambda(G)=\inf\{\|P\|_\infty:P^2=P,\ \ran P=\CVF(G)\}.
\]
This is exactly the constant in the source question.  Indeed, if
\(\widetilde I:\VF(G)\to\Lip_0(V(G))\) extends integration, then
\(P=\nabla\widetilde I\) is a projection onto \(\CVF(G)\) with the same
norm.  Conversely, \(I P\) is an extension whenever \(P\) is such a
projection.  Both norm equalities follow because
\(\nabla:\Lip_0(V(G))\to\CVF(G)\) and its inverse \(I\) are isometries.

\section{Results}

For a weighted cycle \(C\), let its positive edge lengths be
\(a_1,\dots,a_r\), and put \(A=\sum_i a_i\).  Since the edge lengths come
from a geodesic graph metric, \(a_i\leq A/2\).

\begin{theorem}[Weighted cycle]\label{thm:cycle}
If some \(a_i=A/2\), then \(\lambda(C)=1\).  Otherwise
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycle}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \lambda(C)=1+
 \left(\sum_{i=1}^r\frac{a_i}{A-2a_i}\right)^{-1}.}
\end{equation}
In particular, for the unweighted cycle \(C_r\),
\[
 \lambda(C_r)=2-\frac2r.
\]
\end{theorem}

A cactus is a connected graph in which every edge lies in at most one
simple cycle.

\begin{corollary}[Weighted cactus]\label{cor:cactus}
For a weighted cactus \(G\),
\[
 \lambda(G)=\max\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{\lambda(C):C\text{ is a simple cycle of }G\}\bigr),
\]
where each cycle constant is given by Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle}.  Thus trees
have constant one.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Two overlapping-cycle families]\label{thm:dense}
For the unweighted complete graph and complete bipartite graph,
\begin{align}
 \lambda(K_n)&=2-\frac2n,\label{eq:kn}\\
 \lambda(K_{p,q})&=3-\frac2p-\frac2q+\frac{2}{pq}.
 \label{eq:kpq}
\end{align}
Formula \eqref{eq:kpq} also gives one when \(p=1\) or \(q=1\), as it
should for a tree.
\end{theorem}

Recall that the blocks of a connected graph are its maximal
2-connected subgraphs together with its bridges.  Each edge belongs to one
block, and each cycle is contained in one block.

\begin{theorem}[Cut-vertex gluing]\label{thm:blocks}
For every connected weighted graph,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockmax}
 \lambda(G)=\max_{B\text{ a block of }G}\lambda(B).
\end{equation}
Consequently, the preceding formulas give the exact answer for any graph
whose non-bridge blocks are weighted cycles, unweighted complete graphs, or
unweighted complete bipartite graphs.
\end{theorem}

Thus the result includes weighted cacti, clique trees, biclique trees, and
arbitrary cut-vertex mixtures of these classes.  The complete bipartite
case is important because its cycle constraints overlap heavily; the
answer is not merely a consequence of separating edge-disjoint cycles.

\section{Proof intuition}

There are three mechanisms.  A weighted cycle has only one constraint, so
its conservative fields form a hyperplane in \(\ell_\infty^r\).  Every
projection onto a hyperplane has a rank-one defect, reducing the full
operator optimization to distributing one unit of mass among the edges.
The cheapest distribution gives \eqref{eq:cycle}.

For a highly symmetric graph, average an arbitrary projection over its
automorphism group.  On \(K_n\), symmetry forces the averaged map to be the
divergence projection.  On \(K_{p,q}\), it forces the familiar two-way
additive projection: row mean plus column mean minus grand mean.  Since
averaging cannot increase the norm, explicit row sums give both upper and
lower bounds.

Finally, blocks have disjoint edge coordinates and every cycle stays
inside a single block.  Conservativity therefore becomes an
\(\ell_\infty\)-direct sum condition, for which the optimal constant is the
largest block constant.

\section{The weighted-cycle computation}

Orient the cycle consistently.  Its conservative fields are
\[
 H=\ker\varphi,\qquad \varphi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^r a_i x_i.
\]
Every projection from \(\ell_\infty^r\) onto \(H\) has the form
\[
 P=I-u\otimes\varphi,\qquad \varphi(u)=1.
\]
Indeed, \(I-P\) vanishes on the codimension-one space \(\ker\varphi\), so
it factors through \(\varphi\); idempotence fixes the normalization.

Put \(t_i=a_i u_i\) and \(c_i=(A-a_i)/a_i\).  The \(i\)-th row sum of
\(P\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rowsum}
 R_i=|1-t_i|+c_i|t_i|,
 \qquad \sum_i t_i=1.
\end{equation}
This argument works over both the real and complex scalar fields.  If
\(a_i<A/2\), then \(c_i>1\), and the triangle inequality gives
\[
 R_i\geq 1+(c_i-1)|t_i|.
\]
Consequently, if all \(R_i\leq L\), then
\[
 1=\left|\sum_i t_i\right|
 \leq\sum_i|t_i|
 \leq(L-1)\sum_i\frac1{c_i-1}
 =(L-1)\sum_i\frac{a_i}{A-2a_i}.
\]
This proves the lower bound in \eqref{eq:cycle}.  Equality is attained by
the positive choice
\[
 t_i=\frac{a_i/(A-2a_i)}{\sum_j a_j/(A-2a_j)},
 \qquad u_i=\frac{t_i}{a_i},
\]
because then every row sum in \eqref{eq:rowsum} has the claimed value.
If \(a_k=A/2\), take \(t_k=1\) and all other \(t_i=0\).  Every row sum is
one, proving the endpoint.  The unweighted specialization follows by
substituting \(a_i=1\) and \(A=r\).

\section{Symmetry computations}

\subsection{Complete graphs}

Represent an edge field on \(K_n\) antisymmetrically:
\(f_{ij}=-f_{ji}\).  Put
\[
 d_i=\sum_k f_{ik}.
\]
The canonical projection onto gradients is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completeP}
 (Pf)_{ij}=\frac{d_i-d_j}{n}.
\end{equation}
It is a projection because for a gradient \(f_{ij}=x_j-x_i\), one has
\(d_i=n(\bar x-x_i)\), and \eqref{eq:completeP} returns \(f\).

For a fixed output edge \((i,j)\), the input edge \((i,j)\) has coefficient
\(2/n\), and the \(2(n-2)\) other edges incident with \(i\) or \(j\) have
coefficient of absolute value \(1/n\).  Hence
\[
 \|P\|_\infty=\frac{2+2(n-2)}n=2-\frac2n.
\]

For optimality, average any projection over the vertex-permutation group
\(S_n\).  The average has no larger norm.  Compose it with the isometric
inverse gradient to obtain an equivariant map \(T\) from edge fields to
mean-zero vertex potentials.  The stabilizer of a vertex \(i\) forces
\(T_i(f)\) to have one common coefficient on all outgoing incident edges.
Edges not incident with \(i\) have coefficient zero: swapping their two
endpoints fixes \(i\) and reverses their sign.  Thus
\(T_i(f)=c\sum_k f_{ik}=c d_i\).  The identity on gradients forces
\(c=-1/n\), so the averaged projection is uniquely \eqref{eq:completeP}.
Its norm is therefore a lower bound for every projection, proving
\eqref{eq:kn}.

\subsection{Complete bipartite graphs}

Write a field on \(K_{p,q}\) as a \(p\)-by-\(q\) matrix \(x=(x_{ij})\),
orienting every edge from the first part to the second.  Gradients are
exactly the additive matrices
\[
 \mathcal A=\{x:x_{ij}=a_i+b_j\}.
\]
The two-way additive projection is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anova}
 (Qx)_{ij}=\frac1q\sum_{\ell}x_{i\ell}
 +\frac1p\sum_kx_{kj}
 -\frac1{pq}\sum_{k,\ell}x_{k\ell}.
\end{equation}
It fixes \(\mathcal A\).  The absolute row sum of \eqref{eq:anova} is
\begin{align*}
 &\frac{p+q-1}{pq}
 +(q-1)\frac{p-1}{pq}
 +(p-1)\frac{q-1}{pq}
 +(p-1)(q-1)\frac1{pq}\\
 &\hspace{35mm}=3-\frac2p-\frac2q+\frac{2}{pq}.
\end{align*}

It remains to prove minimality.  Average any projection over
\(S_p\times S_q\).  Let
\(\mathcal Z\) be the matrices with every row sum and every column sum
zero.  Then the whole matrix space is \(\mathcal A\oplus\mathcal Z\), and
\eqref{eq:anova} is the associated projection.  If \(R\) is any invariant
projection onto \(\mathcal A\) and \(z\in\mathcal Z\), invariance under
the stabilizer of an entry \((i,j)\) shows that \((Rz)_{ij}\) is a linear
combination of four orbit sums: the entry itself, the rest of its row, the
rest of its column, and all remaining entries.  On \(\mathcal Z\), these
four sums are respectively \(z_{ij},-z_{ij},-z_{ij},z_{ij}\).  Transitivity
therefore gives \(Rz=c z\) for a scalar \(c\).  But \(Rz\in\mathcal A\),
while \(z\in\mathcal Z\) and \(\mathcal A\cap\mathcal Z=\{0\}\); hence
\(c=0\).  Thus the invariant projection is unique and equals \(Q\).
Averaging proves its minimality and establishes \eqref{eq:kpq}.

\section{Block gluing}

The edge sets of the blocks partition \(E(G)\), so
\[
 \VF(G)=\bigoplus_{B}^{\ell_\infty}\VF(B).
\]
Every cycle of a graph is contained in one block.  Therefore a field is
conservative on \(G\) exactly when its restriction to every block is
conservative, and
\[
 \CVF(G)=\bigoplus_B^{\ell_\infty}\CVF(B).
\]
Choosing optimal block projections gives
\(\lambda(G)\leq\max_B\lambda(B)\).  Conversely, if \(P\) is any global
projection, inject fields supported on one block \(B\), apply \(P\), and
restrict the output back to \(B\).  The resulting map is a projection onto
\(\CVF(B)\) of norm at most \(\|P\|\).  Taking the infimum over \(P\) and
then the maximum over blocks proves \eqref{eq:blockmax}.

\section{Verification, novelty, and remaining obstruction}

The companion script \texttt{code/verify\_projection\_constants.py}
performed exact rational checks of idempotence, range constraints, and row
norms on 36 weighted cycles, \(K_n\) for \(2\leq n\leq8\), seven
complete-bipartite cases, and mixed block sums.  Independently formulated
linear programs recovered the claimed optima in all 36 cycle cases, for
\(K_n\) with \(2\leq n\leq5\), and for six small bicliques.  The script
reported 190 exact assertions and 46 independent LP optimum assertions.

The general link between invariant projections, graph cycle/cut spaces,
and transportation cost spaces is established in
Dilworth--Kutzarova--Ostrovskii \cite{DKO2023,DKO2018}.  Their work also
warns that invariant projections need not be unique in less rigid graph
families.  Bohnenblust's classical codimension-one bound and its equality
theory are discussed by Kobos \cite{Kobos}; the weighted-cycle formula here
is therefore best viewed as an explicit hyperplane calculation, not as a
claim of a new general projection theorem.  Rieffel gives a broader
extension/projection correspondence \cite{Rieffel}.

Bounded exact-phrase, title/id, cut-space, weighted-hyperplane, complete,
and complete-bipartite searches through 11 August 2026 did not locate the
full collection of formulas and block synthesis stated here.  Some network
literature does record the norm \(2(n-1)/n\) of the standard cut projection
for uniform rings and complete graphs.  Accordingly, this packet claims a
verified exact answer for the listed families, but makes no priority claim.

For a general 2-connected graph, several independent cycle equations
overlap, and the conservative subspace is no longer a single hyperplane or
a disjoint coordinate sum.  Symmetry averaging still produces a minimal
invariant projection, but that projection can lie in a nontrivial family;
uniqueness arguments used for \(K_n\) and \(K_{p,q}\) then fail.  Resolving
that optimization uniformly is the remaining obstruction to the source's
full question.
