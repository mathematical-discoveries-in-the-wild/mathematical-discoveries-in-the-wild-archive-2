\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Literature answer to the property-[H] characterization}\par
\medskip
{\small arXiv:0801.0085, Section 2 $\longrightarrow$ arXiv:0812.0889, Theorem 2.5}
\end{center}

\textbf{Status: literature\_already\_answered.}

\textbf{Original source.} Jacek Chmieli\'nski, Dijana Ili\v sevi\'c,
Mohammad Sal Moslehian, and Ghadir Sadeghi,
\emph{Perturbation of the Wigner equation in inner product $C^*$-modules},
arXiv:0801.0085; J. Math. Phys. \textbf{49} (2008), 033519.
On PDF page 4, immediately after defining condition {\sf [H]}, the authors write:
\begin{quote}
It is an interesting question to characterize the class of all inner product
$C^*$-modules in which {\sf [H]} is satisfied.
\end{quote}
Here {\sf [H]} says that every norm-bounded sequence $(x_n)$ has a subsequence
$(x_{n_k})$ and $x_0$ such that
$\|\langle x_{n_k},y\rangle-\langle x_0,y\rangle\|\to0$ for every $y$.

\textbf{Decisive later answer.} Ljiljana Aramba\v si\'c, Damir Baki\'c, and
Mohammad Sal Moslehian,
\emph{A characterization of Hilbert $C^*$-modules over finite dimensional
$C^*$-algebras}, arXiv:0812.0889; Operators and Matrices \textbf{3} (2009),
357--364.  Its abstract explicitly says that it answers the question posed in
the original paper.  Theorem 2.5 on PDF page 5 states:
\begin{quote}
A full Hilbert $C^*$-module over a $C^*$-algebra $A$ satisfies {\sf [H]} if
and only if $A$ is finite dimensional.
\end{quote}

\textbf{Identification and full scope.} For an arbitrary Hilbert $A$-module
$X$, let
\[
J=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\langle x,y\rangle:x,y\in X\}.
\]
Then $J$ is the coefficient ideal seen by $X$, and the same inner product and
norm make $X$ a full Hilbert $J$-module.  Condition {\sf [H]} is unchanged.
Thus the characterization requested by the source is equivalently
\[
\boxed{\quad X\text{ satisfies {\sf [H]}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
J\text{ is finite dimensional}.\quad}
\]
The reverse implication is the original paper's Proposition 2.1; the later
paper proves the converse for full modules and explicitly presents it as the
answer to the source question.

\textbf{Limitations.} This status closes only the property-{\sf [H]}
characterization.  It does not claim to settle the source paper's separate
critical-control problem $\varphi(x,y)=\varepsilon\|x\|\|y\|$ or its closing
uniqueness question for exact Wigner approximants.  Several direct
counterexample attempts for the latter are recorded in the linked attempt
note but were not promoted.

\textbf{Evidence and verification.} Searches by the exact title, question
phrase, property {\sf [H]}, and Hilbert $C^*$-module located arXiv:0812.0889.
The source question and supporting theorem were checked in the official PDFs
saved with this note.  The supporting authors explicitly cite the original
paper and say that their theorem answers its question.
