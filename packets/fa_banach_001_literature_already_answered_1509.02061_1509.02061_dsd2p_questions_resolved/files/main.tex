\section*{Status and source questions}

\textbf{Status: literature already answered (three full positive answers).}

In Section~4, Questions~1, 3, and 4 on PDF page~24 of
\cite{BLR}, the authors ask:
\begin{enumerate}
\item whether the converse of
  $\mathrm{DP}\Rightarrow\DSD$ holds;
\item whether a Banach space $X$ has the $\DSD$ if and only if
  $X^*$ has the weak-star $\DSD$;
\item whether $X\oplus_1Y$ has the $\DSD$ whenever both $X$ and $Y$ do.
\end{enumerate}

All three answers are yes.  Kadets explicitly identifies the source
questions and proves the decisive equality $\DSD=\mathrm{DP}$ in
\cite[Theorem~2.3, PDF pp.~3--4]{Kadets}; his introduction states that this
also gives positive answers to source Questions~4.3 and~4.4.  Haller--Pirk--
P\~oldvere had already answered the direct-sum question directly in
\cite[Theorem, PDF p.~1]{HPP}.

\section*{Identification}

Kadets proves that every Banach space with the diametral strong diameter two
property has the Daugavet property.  The reverse implication was already
known and recorded in the source.  Hence
\[
                 X\text{ has }\DSD
                 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                 X\text{ has the Daugavet property}.                 \tag{1}
\]
This settles the converse of implication~(1) in the source diagram.

For the dual question, use the standard dual characterization of the
Daugavet property: $X$ has the Daugavet property if and only if $X^*$ has
the weak-star Daugavet property.  The weak-star analogue of Kadets's
argument (or the corresponding weak-star geometric characterization) then
identifies weak-star $\DSD$ with weak-star Daugavet.  Combining this with
(1) gives
\[
 X\text{ has }\DSD
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 X^*\text{ has weak-star }\DSD.
\]
Kadets explicitly records this as the positive answer to source
Question~4.3.

Finally, the Daugavet property is stable under $\ell_1$-sums, so (1) implies
that $X\oplus_1Y$ has $\DSD$ whenever $X$ and $Y$ do.  Independently,
Haller--Pirk--P\~oldvere prove exactly this assertion and explicitly say that
it answers the open problem from arXiv:1509.02061.

\section*{Scope and remaining frontier}

This packet resolves only the DSD2P cluster.  It does \emph{not} resolve the
source questions whether DLD2P implies DD2P, whether weak-star DLD2P implies
weak-star DD2P, or whether
$X$ has DD2P exactly when $X^*$ has weak-star DD2P.  The July 2026 preprint
\cite{WeakDD2P} still states explicitly that DLD2P $\Rightarrow$ DD2P (even
DLD2P $\Rightarrow$ D2P) remains open.

The bounded status check used the run indexes, the full local sources of all
four cited arXiv papers, and exact-title/keyword web searches.  Because
Kadets explicitly cites the original paper and says he answers its Questions
4.1, 4.3, and 4.4, this belongs in \texttt{literature\_already\_answered},
not among new mathematical discoveries.

\textbf{Human-review recommendation.} Verify the question numbering on PDF
page~24 of the source, Kadets's Theorem~2.3 and the paragraph immediately
before it, and the direct theorem on PDF page~1 of Haller--Pirk--P\~oldvere.
Keep the DLD2P/DD2P and DD2P duality questions open.
