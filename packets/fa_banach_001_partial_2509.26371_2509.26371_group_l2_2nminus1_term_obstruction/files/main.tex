\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{New partial result; the source's structural characterization remains
open.}  For every \(N\ge2\), we construct a finite integral vector-valued
RKBS with output space \(\R^2\) and a regularized least-squares problem on
\(N\) data sites whose unique minimizer requires exactly \(2N-1\) kernel
sites.  Its RKBS norm is the standard group-\(\ell_2\) penalty.  Thus this
basic form of groupwise sparsity alone cannot improve the source's \(Nd\)
bound to \(N\); when \(d=2\), the example misses the \(2N\) bound by only one.

\section*{Source question}
Dummer, Heeringa, and Iglesias [1] prove representer theorems for
integral and neural vv-RKBSs which, for \(N\) measurements in \(\R^d\), give
at most \(Nd\) terms.  In their future-work section they ask what additional
structure ensures at most \(N\) terms, and suggest that groupwise sparsity
could play a central role.

\section*{Proof intuition}
In a finite discrete integral vv-RKBS, the total-variation norm of a vector
measure is exactly a sum of Euclidean coefficient norms.  This is ordinary
group lasso, one output vector per kernel site.  We expose a large flat face
of that norm with a dual matrix \(G\).  Each of \(2N-1\) atoms lies on the
face because \(G^Ta_j\) is its unit output direction.  The atoms' prediction
matrices \(a_js_j^T\) are linearly independent.  Consequently equality in
the dual bound first pins every coefficient to its prescribed nonnegative
ray and then pins every ray scale to one.  A squared loss is centered so that
this exposed point satisfies the exact optimality equations.

Finally, we complete the training feature matrix to an invertible square
matrix on the whole finite domain.  This removes a possible loophole: the
same minimizer cannot secretly have a shorter kernel expansion away from the
training data.

\begin{theorem}[a \(2N-1\)-site group-\(\ell_2\) obstruction]\label{thm:main}
For every integer \(N\ge2\), there exist finite sets \(X,\Omega\), a scalar
feature map \(\phi:X\times\Omega\to\R\), an integral vv-RKBS
\(\B\subseteq\{f:X\to\R^2\}\) with kernel sections
\[
 K_{\U}(x,w)u=\phi(x,w)u,
\]
\(N\) distinct training sites \(x_1,\ldots,x_N\), and targets
\(t_i\in\R^2\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:objective}
 \min_{f\in\B}\left\{
 \frac1{2N}\sum_{i=1}^N\|f(x_i)-t_i\|_2^2
 +\frac1N\|f\|_{\B}\right\}
\end{equation}
has a unique minimizer.  Every kernel expansion of that minimizer uses
exactly \(2N-1\) distinct sites of \(\Omega\), and
\(\|f\|_{\B}\) is the group-\(\ell_2\) coefficient norm.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(m=2N-1\).  Let \(e_1,\ldots,e_N\) be the standard basis of \(\R^N\),
and let \(G\in\R^{N\times2}\) have first row \((1,0)\), second row
\((0,1)\), and all other rows zero.  We specify \(m\) feature columns
\(a_j\in\R^N\) and unit directions \(s_j\in\R^2\).  The first three are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
 j&1&2&3\\ \hline
 a_j&e_1&e_2&(e_1+e_2)/\sqrt2\\
 s_j&(1,0)&(0,1)&(1,1)/\sqrt2 .
 \end{array}
\]
For each \(k=3,\ldots,N\), add two more pairs
\[
 (a_{k,0},s_{k,0})=(e_1+e_k,(1,0)),\qquad
 (a_{k,1},s_{k,1})=(e_2+e_k,(0,1)).
\]
There are \(3+2(N-2)=m\) pairs, and every one satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expose}
 G^Ta_j=s_j,\qquad \|s_j\|_2=1.
\end{equation}

The matrices \(b_j=a_js_j^T\in\R^{N\times2}\) are linearly independent.
Indeed, for each \(k\ge3\), row \(k\) of a linear relation separately forces
the coefficients of \(b_{k,0}\) and \(b_{k,1}\) to vanish.  Among the first
three matrices, the off-diagonal entries force the coefficient of \(b_3\)
to vanish, and the two diagonal entries then dispose of \(b_1,b_2\).
Also the matrix \(A=[a_1\ \cdots\ a_m]\in\R^{N\times m}\) has rank \(N\):
besides \(e_1,e_2\), its column span contains
\(a_{k,0}-a_1=e_k\).

Choose an \((m-N)\times m\) matrix \(C\) so that
\(F=\begin{bmatrix}A\\C\end{bmatrix}\) is invertible.  Take
\(X=\{x_1,\ldots,x_m\}\),
\(\Omega=\{w_1,\ldots,w_m\}\), and
\(\phi(x_i,w_j)=F_{ij}\).  These finite sets are discrete and compact, so
all features are continuous.  Define
\[
 f_c(x)=\sum_{j=1}^m\phi(x,w_j)c_j,
 \qquad
 \|f_c\|_{\B}=\sum_{j=1}^m\|c_j\|_2 .
\]
This is the finite discrete integral vv-RKBS obtained from vector measures on
\(\Omega\).  Invertibility of \(F\) makes the coefficient tuple
\(c=(c_1,\ldots,c_m)\) unique, so the displayed formula really is its RKBS
norm.

Let the candidate coefficients be \(c_j^0=s_j\).  Its training prediction
matrix, with one output row per training point, is
\[
 Y_0=Ac^0=\sum_{j=1}^m a_js_j^T=\sum_{j=1}^m b_j.
\]
Set the target matrix to \(T=Y_0+G\).  In coefficient form,
\eqref{eq:objective} is the convex objective
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-objective}
 J(c)=\frac1{2N}\|Ac-T\|_F^2
      +\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^m\|c_j\|_2.
\end{equation}
At \(c^0\), the residual is \(-G\).  Since no \(s_j\) is zero, the rowwise
optimality equation is, by \eqref{eq:expose},
\[
 \frac1N a_j^T(-G)+\frac1N s_j=0.
\]
Hence \(c^0\) minimizes \(J\).

It remains to prove uniqueness and the support claim.  Strict convexity of
the squared loss in the prediction \(Ac\), together with convexity of the
norm, implies that every minimizer has prediction \(Y_0\).  For any
\(c\) satisfying \(Ac=Y_0\), the same matrix \(G\) is a dual certificate:
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{j=1}^m\|c_j\|_2
 &\ge \sum_{j=1}^m\ip{s_j}{c_j}
  =\ip{G}{Ac}_F
  =\ip{G}{Y_0}_F\\
 &=\sum_{j=1}^m\ip{G^Ta_j}{s_j}
  =m.
\end{align*}
The candidate attains equality.  Equality term by term in the first
inequality forces \(c_j=r_js_j\) with \(r_j\ge0\).  The prediction identity
then becomes
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m r_jb_j=\sum_{j=1}^m b_j.
\]
Linear independence of the \(b_j\)'s forces every \(r_j=1\).  Thus \(c^0\)
is the unique minimizer and all its \(m=2N-1\) coefficients are nonzero.
Since \(F\) is invertible, every expansion of the resulting function has
this same coefficient tuple (after combining repeated sites).  No expansion
on fewer distinct kernel sites exists.
\end{proof}

\section*{What this answers---and what it does not}
Taking the measurement operator to be the identity on \(\U=\R^2\) gives
\(d=2\) in the source's setup.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} therefore supplies,
for every sample size, an example requiring \(2N-1>N\) terms.  It disproves
the sufficiency of the most direct interpretation of the suggested route:
the standard Euclidean group-\(\ell_2\) norm by itself.  It also nearly
saturates the general \(Nd=2N\) guarantee.

The theorem does \emph{not} say that stronger structured group penalties are
ineffective, and it does not characterize the additional geometry needed for
an \(N\)-term theorem.  Those parts of the source question remain open.
Three focused upgrade attempts progressed from a three-site \(N=2\) example,
to an \(N+1\)-site polygon family, and finally to the \(2N-1\)-site exposed
face above.  The last construction reaches the dimension of the natural
rank-two exposed-face span; no credible route to \(2N\) under the same smooth
Euclidean certificate remained.

The bundled script independently checks ranks, the exposing identities, the
optimality equations, the invertible completion, the equality-case linear
system, and random perturbations for \(2\le N\le12\).  These are sanity checks
only; the general result is proved symbolically above.  Exact-title,
exact-question, vv-RKBS representer, group-lasso active-set, and
\(2N-1\)-support searches through 13 August 2026 found no later source with
this construction or an answer to the stated structural question.  This was
a bounded search, not a systematic bibliographic review.
