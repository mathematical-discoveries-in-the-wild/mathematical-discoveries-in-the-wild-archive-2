\section*{Idea of the proof}

The source's scalar problem \cite{source} allows the approximating functional
to attain either $1$ or $-1$.  Encode those two choices by the two boundary
components of the lens $B_X\cap(x+B_X)$.  A nearby functional supporting the
lens splits into a nonnegative combination of the two ball normals.  If the
first coefficient dominates, its positive norming pair is close; if the
second dominates, the translated-ball normal yields a close pair attaining
$-1$.  This gives the universal cap one.  For a property-$\beta$ range with
parameter zero, a rank-one correction changes only one coordinate of the
norming family and lifts the scalar construction to operators.

\section{The geometric lemma}

For a closed convex set $D\subset X$ and $u\in D$, write
\[
 N_D(u)=\{q\in X^*:q(v-u)\le0\text{ for every }v\in D\}
\]
for its normal cone.  We use two standard convex-analytic facts.  First, the
Bishop--Phelps theorem says that the functionals attaining their supremum on
a nonempty closed bounded convex set are dense in $X^*$ \cite{bp}.  Second,
if closed convex sets $A,D$ have intersecting interiors, then the
interior-point normal-cone rule gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sumrule}
 N_{A\cap D}(u)=N_A(u)+N_D(u)\qquad(u\in A\cap D).
\end{equation}
This is the normal-cone form of the Moreau--Rockafellar sum rule
\cite{phelps}.

\begin{lemma}[absolute-norming lens lemma]\label{lem:lens}
Let $X$ be a real Banach space.  For every $x\in B_X$ and $f\in B_{X^*}$,
\[
 \inf_{\substack{y\in S_X,\ g\in S_{X^*}\\ |g(y)|=1}}
 \max\{\|x-y\|,\|f-g\|\}\le1.
\]
Equivalently, for every $\delta>1$ there are $y\in S_X$ and
$g\in S_{X^*}$ with $|g(y)|=1$ and both distances smaller than $\delta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 C=B_X\cap(x+B_X).
\]
The point $x/2$ is an interior point of both balls, so the qualification in
\eqref{eq:sumrule} holds.  Fix $\eta>0$.  By Bishop--Phelps choose a nonzero
$p\in X^*$ with $\|p-f\|<\eta$ which attains its supremum on $C$, say at
$u\in C$.  When $f=0$, nonzeroness can be ensured by first perturbing zero
to a functional of norm smaller than $\eta/2$ and then applying the density
theorem closely enough.  Set
\[
 \varphi=\frac{p}{\max\{1,\|p\|\}}.
\]
Then $\varphi\ne0$, $\|\varphi\|\le1$, $\varphi\in N_C(u)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closephi}
 \|\varphi-f\|<2\eta.
\end{equation}
Indeed, when $\|p\|>1$ the extra normalization error is
$\|p\|-1\le\|p-f\|$.

The sum rule and the elementary normal cones of the two balls give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomp}
 \varphi=\alpha g+\beta h,
\end{equation}
where $\alpha,\beta\ge0$; if $\alpha>0$, then
$u\in S_X$, $g\in S_{X^*}$, and $g(u)=1$; if $\beta>0$, then
$u-x\in S_X$, $h\in S_{X^*}$, and $h(u-x)=1$.  At least one coefficient is
positive.

If $\beta=0$, then $\alpha=\|\varphi\|\le1$, and $(u,g)$ is an
absolute-norming pair satisfying
\[
 \|x-u\|\le1,\qquad \|\varphi-g\|=1-\alpha\le1.
\]
If $\alpha=0$, put $v=x-u$.  Then $v\in S_X$, $h(v)=-1$, and
\[
 \|x-v\|=\|u\|\le1,\qquad \|\varphi-h\|=1-\beta\le1.
\]

It remains to suppose that $\alpha,\beta>0$.  Put again $v=x-u$.
Now $u,v\in S_X$, $x=u+v$, $g(u)=1$, and $h(v)=-1$.  Hence
\[
 g(v)=g(x)-1\le\|x\|-1\le0,
 \qquad
 h(u)=h(x)+1\ge1-\|x\|\ge0.
\]
Evaluating \eqref{eq:decomp} at $u$ and $v$, using
$\|\varphi\|\le1$, yields
\[
 \alpha+\beta h(u)=\varphi(u)\le1,
 \qquad
 \alpha g(v)-\beta=\varphi(v)\ge-1.
\]
In particular, $\alpha\le1$ and $\beta\le1+\alpha g(v)\le1$.  If
$\alpha\ge\beta$, select $(u,g)$; then
\[
 \|x-u\|\le1,
 \qquad
 \|\varphi-g\|
 \le (1-\alpha)+\beta\le1.
\]
If $\beta\ge\alpha$, select $(v,h)$; then
\[
 \|x-v\|\le1,
 \qquad
 \|\varphi-h\|
 \le \alpha+(1-\beta)\le1.
\]
Thus one of the two pairs is within one of $(x,\varphi)$.  By
\eqref{eq:closephi}, it is within $1+2\eta$ of $(x,f)$.  Letting
$\eta\downarrow0$ proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\section{The scalar problem is solved}

We use the operator moduli exactly as in the source: the input pair is in
$B_X\times B_{\Lin(X,Y)}$ for $\Phi$, in
$S_X\times S_{\Lin(X,Y)}$ for $\PhiS$, and the approximating operator has
norm one and attains its norm at a unit vector.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:scalar}
For every real Banach space $X$ and every $0<\varepsilon<1$,
\[
 \PhiS(X,\R,\varepsilon)\le \Phi(X,\R,\varepsilon)
 \le \min\{\sqrt{2\varepsilon},1\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Identify a scalar operator with $f\in B_{X^*}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:lens}
says that every $(x,f)\in B_X\times B_{X^*}$ can be approximated, to every
distance strictly larger than one, by $y\in S_X$ and $g\in S_{X^*}$ with
$|g(y)|=1$.  Thus $\Phi(X,\R,\varepsilon)\le1$; the near-attainment
hypothesis is not even needed for this half.

The classical Bishop--Phelps--Bollob\'as estimate, or equivalently the
source's property-$\beta$ theorem at parameter zero, gives
$\Phi(X,\R,\varepsilon)\le\sqrt{2\varepsilon}$.  Taking the smaller bound
and using $\PhiS\le\Phi$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

This is stronger than the printed question in two ways: it bounds the
nonspherical modulus, and the cap-one part holds for every input pair rather
than only for pairs nearly attaining a norm.  Together with the source's
sharp lower bound, it also recovers
\[
 \PhiS(\ell_1^{(2)},\R,\varepsilon)
 =\Phi(\ell_1^{(2)},\R,\varepsilon)
 =\min\{\sqrt{2\varepsilon},1\}.
\]

\section{Upgrade: all property-$\beta$ ranges with parameter zero}

Recall that $\beta(Y)=0$ means that there are
$(y_\gamma,y_\gamma^*)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\subset S_Y\times S_{Y^*}$
such that
\[
 y_\gamma^*(y_\gamma)=1,
 \qquad y_\gamma^*(y_\nu)=0\ (\gamma\ne\nu),
 \qquad \|w\|=\sup_\gamma|y_\gamma^*(w)|\ (w\in Y).
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:beta0}
If $X,Y$ are real Banach spaces and $\beta(Y)=0$, then for every
$0<\varepsilon<1$,
\[
 \PhiS(X,Y,\varepsilon)\le \Phi(X,Y,\varepsilon)
 \le \min\{\sqrt{2\varepsilon},1\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Only the cap one is new.  Let $x\in B_X$, $T\in B_{\Lin(X,Y)}$, and
$\|Tx\|>1-\varepsilon$.  Choose $\gamma_0$ with
$|y_{\gamma_0}^*(Tx)|>1-\varepsilon$ and put
$f=T^*y_{\gamma_0}^*\in B_{X^*}$.  For any $\delta>1$, the lens lemma
gives $z\in S_X$ and $g\in S_{X^*}$ such that
\[
 |g(z)|=1,\qquad \|x-z\|<\delta,\qquad \|f-g\|<\delta.
\]
Define
\[
 F(t)=T(t)+(g(t)-f(t))y_{\gamma_0}\qquad(t\in X).
\]
Orthogonality of the property-$\beta$ family gives
\[
 F^*y_{\gamma_0}^*=g,
 \qquad
 F^*y_\gamma^*=T^*y_\gamma^*\quad(\gamma\ne\gamma_0).
\]
Since the family $(y_\gamma^*)$ is norming,
\[
 \|F\|=\sup_\gamma\|F^*y_\gamma^*\|=1.
\]
Moreover $\|Fz\|\ge|y_{\gamma_0}^*(Fz)|=|g(z)|=1$, so $F$ attains its
norm at $z$, and
\[
 \|F-T\|=\|g-f\|<\delta.
\]
Thus $\Phi(X,Y,\varepsilon)\le1$.  The
$\sqrt{2\varepsilon}$ bound is Theorem 2.1 of the source with $\rho=0$.
\end{proof}

This settles the source's second, more general cap-one question only in the
endpoint case $\rho=0$.  The argument uses the exact vanishing of all
off-diagonal coefficients and does not prove the cap for $0<\rho<1$.

\section{Novelty and verification boundary}

the locally parsed arXiv corpus, and bounded web searches found the source but
no later answer to its scalar question.  A later-title and citation scan also
found no theorem matching the cap-one claim.  This is a bounded novelty
check, not an exhaustive priority claim.

The substantive external inputs are the Bishop--Phelps density theorem, the
interior-point normal-cone sum rule, and the source's already-proved
$\sqrt{2\varepsilon}$ estimate.  The cap-one argument itself is independent
of the near-attainment condition and is given in full above.
