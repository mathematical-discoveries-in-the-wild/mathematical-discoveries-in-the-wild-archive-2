\section*{1. Exact question isolated in the source}

Li--Pastor--Piskarev ask the following inverse generator question.  Suppose that
$A$ generates a bounded $C_0$-semigroup on a Banach space $X$ and has dense
range.  Must $A^{-1}$ generate a bounded $C_0$-semigroup, or at least a
$C_0$-semigroup?  They note that counterexamples were already known on general
Banach spaces, but explicitly state that the question was still open when $X$
is a Hilbert space.

\section*{2. Resolving theorem}

Lorist--Meyries--Veraar subsequently proved the following theorem.  There are a
separable Hilbert space $H$ and a bounded operator $A\in\mathcal L(H)$ with
dense range such that
\[
 \sup_{t\geq 0}\lVert e^{tA}\rVert<\infty,
 \qquad e^{tA}x\longrightarrow0\quad(x\in H),
\]
but the closed, densely defined operator
\[
 A^{-1}:\operatorname{Ran}(A)\longrightarrow H
\]
does not generate a $C_0$-semigroup.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.97\textwidth,height=0.67\textheight,keepaspectratio]{figures/resolving-theorem.png}
\end{center}

\small
\textbf{Evidence.} E. Lorist, M. Meyries, and M. Veraar, \emph{A
counterexample to the inverse generator problem and related questions},
arXiv:2608.06272v1 (2026), Theorem~1.2(i), p.~2.  The full downloaded source is
preserved as \texttt{resolving\_paper.pdf}.
\normalsize

\section*{3. Exact implication and scope}

Theorem~1.2(i) satisfies every hypothesis in the Hilbert-space version of the
question and violates its weakest proposed conclusion:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Requirement in the question & Property of the counterexample \\
\midrule
$X$ is a Hilbert space & $H$ is separable Hilbert \\
$A$ has dense range & stated explicitly \\
$A$ generates a bounded $C_0$-semigroup & indeed strongly stable \\
$A^{-1}$ is the natural inverse on $\operatorname{Ran}(A)$ & closed and densely defined \\
Weak conclusion: $A^{-1}$ generates some $C_0$-semigroup & false \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Consequently the answer is \textbf{no}, both to bounded generation and to the
weaker question of generation at all.  The counterexample is stronger than the
source formulation in two useful respects: $A$ itself is bounded, and its
semigroup is not merely uniformly bounded but strongly stable.

The resolving paper also proves a complementary statement in
Theorem~1.2(ii): even when $A$ generates an exponentially stable semigroup and
$A^{-1}$ does generate a $C_0$-semigroup, the inverse semigroup can be
unbounded.  That second example independently rules out a universal boundedness
conclusion; Theorem~1.2(i) is the exact theorem needed for the full negative
answer.

\section*{4. Timing and classification}

The source preprint was submitted in September 2018.  The resolving preprint,
arXiv:2608.06272v1, was submitted on 6 August 2026.  Thus this packet records a
very recent literature resolution rather than a new proof by the present run.
The classification is therefore \emph{literature already answered}, with the
additional review caveat that the resolving source is currently a first arXiv
version.

\section*{Conclusion}

The Hilbert-space inverse generator problem highlighted in arXiv:1809.04743 is
fully resolved in the negative by arXiv:2608.06272, Theorem~1.2(i).
