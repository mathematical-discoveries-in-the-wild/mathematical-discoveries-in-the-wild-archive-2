\section{Source question and setup}

Alpay and Salomon prove in Theorem 3.6 that convolution is jointly continuous
for the strong topology of a V{\aa}ge space.  Immediately afterward they ask
whether it is continuous in the weak topology \cite[p.~13]{AS}.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/source_question_crop.png}
\caption{The strong-continuity theorem and the open weak-topology question on
page 13 of the source.}
\end{figure}

We recall only the needed definitions.  Let $A\subseteq\N$ and
\[
 \ell_A=\N_0^{(A)}=\bigoplus_{j\in A}\N_0e_j
\]
be the free commutative monoid.  For a regular admissible superexponential
weight $a=(a_\alpha)_{\alpha\in\ell_A}$, so that
\[
 a_0=1,\qquad a_{e_j}>1,\qquad
 a_\alpha a_\beta\le a_{\alpha+\beta},
\]
the source defines
\[
 \Fa=\bigcap_{p\in\N}\ell^2_{a^p},\qquad
 \Fa'=\bigcup_{p\in\N}\ell^2_{a^{-p}}.
\]
The convolution on $\Fa'$ is formal-series multiplication,
\[
 (fg)_\gamma=\sum_{\alpha+\beta=\gamma}f_\alpha g_\beta.
\]
The weak topology in the question is $\sigma(\Fa',\Fa)$, as is also clear
from Proposition 3.7 and its proof in the source.

\section{Proof intuition}

Weak neighborhoods see only finitely many linear functionals.  Consequently,
a scalar bilinear form that is jointly continuous for two weak topologies must
factor through finite-dimensional quotients; in particular, it has finite
algebraic rank.

If convolution were jointly weakly continuous, pairing the product $fg$ with
any test vector $\varphi\in\Fa$ would therefore give a finite-rank bilinear
form.  Along any one generator $e\in A$, that form has the Hankel matrix
$(\varphi_{(m+n)e})_{m,n\ge0}$.  We choose $\varphi$ to decay so rapidly that
it belongs to $\Fa$ but its Hankel matrix has infinite rank.  This contradicts
weak joint continuity.

\section{Negative answer}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:finite-rank}
Let $(E,F)$ be a separating dual pair.  Every scalar bilinear form
$B:E\times E\to\C$ that is jointly continuous on
$\sigma(E,F)\times\sigma(E,F)$ has finite algebraic rank.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Joint continuity at $(0,0)$ gives basic weak neighborhoods
\[
 U=\{x:|u_i(x)|<\varepsilon,\ 1\le i\le r\},\qquad
 V=\{y:|v_j(y)|<\delta,\ 1\le j\le s\}
\]
such that $|B(x,y)|\le1$ on $U\times V$, where $u_i,v_j\in F$.
If $x\in\bigcap_{i=1}^r\ker u_i$, then $tx\in U$ for every scalar $t$.
For $y\in V$, boundedness of $tB(x,y)$ for every $t$ forces $B(x,y)=0$.
Since $V$ is absorbing, this holds for every $y\in E$.  Thus
\[
 \bigcap_{i=1}^r\ker u_i
 \subseteq\{x:B(x,y)=0\text{ for all }y\in E\}.
\]
The map $x\mapsto B(x,\cdot)$ therefore factors through a quotient of
dimension at most $r$, and $B$ has finite rank.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $\Fa'$ be a V{\aa}ge space associated with a regular admissible
superexponential weight on $\ell_A$.  The convolution map
\[
 *:(\Fa',\sigma(\Fa',\Fa))\times(\Fa',\sigma(\Fa',\Fa))
 \longrightarrow(\Fa',\sigma(\Fa',\Fa))
\]
is jointly continuous if and only if $A=\varnothing$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $A=\varnothing$, then $\ell_A=\{0\}$ and $\Fa'=\C$, so the assertion is
immediate.

Suppose that $A\ne\varnothing$ and fix a generator $e=e_j$.  Put
$c=a_e>1$.  Superexponentiality gives, by induction,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growth}
 a_{ne}\ge c^n\qquad(n\ge0).
\end{equation}
Define a test vector $\varphi=(\varphi_\alpha)_{\alpha\in\ell_A}$ by
\[
 \varphi_0=1,\qquad
 \varphi_{ne}=a_{ne}^{-n}\quad(n\ge1),\qquad
 \varphi_\alpha=0\quad\text{otherwise}.
\]
For each fixed $p\in\N$,
\[
 \sum_{\alpha\in\ell_A}|\varphi_\alpha|^2a_\alpha^p
 =1+\sum_{n\ge1}a_{ne}^{p-2n}<\infty.
\]
Indeed, only finitely many terms have $2n\le p$, while \eqref{eq:growth}
gives
\[
 a_{ne}^{p-2n}\le c^{-n(2n-p)}
 \qquad(2n>p).
\]
Hence $\varphi\in\Fa$.

Assume for contradiction that convolution is jointly weakly continuous.
The continuous functional
\[
 \Lambda_\varphi(h)=\sum_{\alpha\in\ell_A}h_\alpha
 \overline{\varphi_\alpha}
\]
then makes
\[
 B_\varphi(f,g)=\Lambda_\varphi(fg)
\]
a jointly weakly continuous scalar bilinear form.  Lemma
\ref{lem:finite-rank} says that $B_\varphi$ has finite rank.

Let $\delta_\alpha$ denote the coordinate vector at $\alpha$ and set
\[
 s_n=\varphi_{ne}\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
On the linear span of $(\delta_{ne})_{n\ge0}$, the matrix of
$B_\varphi$ is the Hankel matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hankel}
 B_\varphi(\delta_{me},\delta_{ne})=s_{m+n}.
\end{equation}
This matrix has infinite rank.  Otherwise, finitely many of its columns would
be linearly dependent, so there would be constants $d_0,\ldots,d_r$, not all
zero and with $d_r\ne0$, such that
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^r d_ks_{n+k}=0\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
Thus the ordinary generating function $S(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}s_nz^n$ would be
rational.  On the other hand,
\[
 s_n^{1/n}=a_{ne}^{-1}\le c^{-n}\longrightarrow0,
\]
so $S$ is entire.  A rational entire function is a polynomial, which would
make $s_n=0$ eventually.  This contradicts $s_n=a_{ne}^{-n}>0$ for every
$n\ge1$.  Hence \eqref{eq:hankel} has infinite rank, contradicting Lemma
\ref{lem:finite-rank}.
\end{proof}

\section{Concrete one-generator witness}

Take $A=\{1\}$ and $a_n=2^n$.  This is an exponential regular admissible
weight and hence defines a V{\aa}ge space by Theorem 3.3 of the source.  The
test vector above becomes $\varphi_n=2^{-n^2}$ and lies in $\Fa$ because
\[
 \sum_{n\ge0}|\varphi_n|^2a_n^p
 =\sum_{n\ge0}2^{-2n^2+pn}<\infty
 \qquad(p\in\N).
\]
Every leading Hankel minor is visibly nonzero.  With $q=1/4$,
\[
 2^{-(m+n)^2}=2^{-m^2}q^{mn}2^{-n^2},
\]
and therefore the $(N+1)\times(N+1)$ determinant is a nonzero diagonal
multiple of
\[
 \det(q^{mn})_{m,n=0}^N
 =\prod_{0\le j<k\le N}(q^k-q^j)\ne0.
\]
The accompanying exact checker verifies this factorization through size
seven; the displayed Vandermonde identity is the proof for all sizes.

\section{Strength, scope, and novelty}

The result is stronger than a single counterexample: it rules out joint weak
continuity for every V{\aa}ge space in the source whose free monoid has at
least one generator, and identifies the sole trivial exception.  It does not
assert failure of separate weak continuity or of weak sequential continuity;
these are strictly weaker properties and are not the question answered here.

Bounded searches on 11 August 2026 covered the local run indexes and the web,
using the arXiv identifier, paper title, the exact quoted sentence, and the
phrases ``V{\aa}ge space'', ``convolution'', and ``weak topology''.  They found
the source and adjacent work on other convolution topologies, but no later
answer to this question or this finite-rank Hankel obstruction.  Novelty
confidence is moderate because the weak-bilinear finite-rank lemma is
elementary and may be known independently.
