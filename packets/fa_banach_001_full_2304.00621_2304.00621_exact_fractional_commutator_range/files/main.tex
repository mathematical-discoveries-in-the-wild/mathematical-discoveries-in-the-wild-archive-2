\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Candidate full solution, likely valid; human review is needed.  The result
determines the half-open interval and the literal supremum asked for in the
source.  It does not decide boundedness at the non-diagonal boundary itself,
which is immaterial to both requested quantities.

\section{Source question}

Let
\[
 \gamma(t)=
 \begin{cases}
  (\eps_1|t|^{\beta_1},\ldots,\eps_n|t|^{\beta_n}),&t>0,\\
  (\delta_1|t|^{\beta_1},\ldots,\delta_n|t|^{\beta_n}),&t<0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad 0<\beta_1<\cdots<\beta_n,
\]
be one of the monomial curves in Oikari's source paper \cite{Oikari}, and put
\[
 H_\gamma f(x)=\operatorname{p.v.}\int_\R f(x-\gamma(t))\,\frac{dt}{t}.
\]
For $p\le q<\infty$, set
\[
 B:=|\beta|=\sum_{j=1}^n\beta_j,
 \qquad \alpha=B\left(\frac1p-\frac1q\right).
\]
The estimate under investigation is
\begin{equation}\label{E:pq}
 \|[b,H_\gamma]\|_{L^p(\R^n)\to L^q(\R^n)}
 \le C_{p,q,\gamma}\|b\|_{\BMO^{\beta,\alpha}(\R^n)}
 \qquad\text{for every }b\in\BMO^{\beta,\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Question 1.15 asks for the maximal initial interval of $q$ for which
\eqref{E:pq} holds and defines
\[
 p_{\max}(n,\beta)=\sup\{q\ge p:\eqref{E:pq}\text{ holds}\}.
\]

\begin{figure}[H]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=.98\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
 \caption{Question 1.15 and its surrounding sharpness discussion
 \cite[p.~5]{Oikari}.}
\end{figure}

There is a small logical issue in the question: a supremum over a disconnected
validity set need not be the endpoint of a maximal interval.  The theorem
below determines both notions and shows that they really do diverge for some
parameters.

\section{The answer}

Define
\[
 p_1(n):=\frac{n(n+1)}{n^2-n+2},\qquad
 p_2(n):=\frac{n+1}{2},
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{E:Q}
 Q_n(p):=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{(n-1)p}{n-p},&1<p\le p_1(n),\\[6pt]
 \displaystyle\frac{n(n+1)p}{n(n+1)-2p},&p_1(n)\le p\le p_2(n),\\[6pt]
 \displaystyle\frac{np}{n-1},&p\ge p_2(n).
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
At the shared endpoints the formulas agree.  When $n=2$, the middle interval
collapses and
\[
 Q_2(p)=\begin{cases}p/(2-p),&1<p\le3/2,\\2p,&p\ge3/2.\end{cases}
\]

\begin{theorem}[exact range]\label{T:main}
For every dimension $n\ge2$, every monomial exponent tuple $\beta$, every
allowed sign pattern, and every $1<p<\infty$, the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item The estimate \eqref{E:pq} holds for $p\le q<Q_n(p)$.
 \item If $q>Q_n(p)$ and $\alpha\le\beta_n$, then \eqref{E:pq} fails.
 \item If $\alpha>\beta_n$, then $\BMO^{\beta,\alpha}(\R^n)$ consists only
 of constants modulo null sets, and \eqref{E:pq} holds trivially.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, the maximal initial half-open interval is exactly
\[
                         [p,Q_n(p)).
\]
For the literal supremum displayed in Question 1.15,
\begin{equation}\label{E:pmax}
 p_{\max}(n,\beta)=
 \begin{cases}
 Q_n(p),&p\ge B/\beta_n,\\
 \infty,&1<p<B/\beta_n.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In the second case, if
\begin{equation}\label{E:qtriv}
 q_{\mathrm{triv}}(p,\beta):=\frac{pB}{B-p\beta_n},
\end{equation}
then \eqref{E:pq} fails for
$Q_n(p)<q\le q_{\mathrm{triv}}(p,\beta)$ and holds trivially for every
$q>q_{\mathrm{triv}}(p,\beta)$.  Thus the literal value $\infty$ does
\emph{not} mean that $[p,\infty)$ is an interval of validity.
\end{theorem}

The theorem leaves open only whether \eqref{E:pq} holds at
$q=Q_n(p)$.  The source asks for a half-open interval and a supremum, so this
endpoint question does not change the answer.

\section{Idea of the proof}

The source proves sufficiency in the interior of a polytope $\Omega(n)$.
Cladek and Ou proved that the same polytope is sharp for the positive
single-scale curve average \cite[Sec.~4.2]{CladekOu}: outside $\Omega(n)$
there are normalized inputs supported as close to the origin as desired whose
averages blow up, with dual witnesses supported near a fixed curve arc.

The key observation is that those separated supports can be encoded into the
symbol.  Choose $b(x)=\eta(x_n)$, zero on the input neighborhood and one on
the output neighborhood.  Then $bf=0$ and $bg=g$, so the commutator pairing
is exactly the Hilbert-transform pairing.  If $\alpha\le\beta_n$, this
separator is anisotropically $\alpha$-Hölder and hence belongs to
$\BMO^{\beta,\alpha}$.  Every sharp single-scale obstruction therefore
becomes a commutator obstruction.  This transfers all facets of
$\Omega(n)$ at once, including the middle facet present when $n>2$.

There is one genuine twist.  If $\alpha>\beta_n$, anisotropic
$\alpha$-Hölder regularity has ordinary exponent greater than one along every
coordinate line.  Subdividing a coordinate segment forces zero variation,
so every symbol is constant.  This creates a trivial high-$q$ validity tail
and explains the distinction between a maximal initial interval and the
literal supremum in the source.

\section{The improving polytope and its vertical boundary}

Let $\Omega(n)$ be the closed convex hull of
\begin{align*}
 &(0,0),\quad (1,1),\\
 &A_n=\left(\frac{n^2-n+2}{n(n+1)},\frac{n-1}{n+1}\right),\qquad
 D_n=\left(\frac{2}{n+1},\frac{2(n-1)}{n(n+1)}\right).
\end{align*}
These are the coordinates $(1/p,1/q)$ used in the source.  In the half-plane
$1/p\ge1/q$, its lower boundary consists of the three line segments
\begin{equation}\label{E:facets}
 y=\frac{n-1}{n}x,\qquad
 y=x-\frac{2}{n(n+1)},\qquad
 y=\frac{nx-1}{n-1}.
\end{equation}
Their $x$-intervals are respectively
\[
 \left[0,\frac{2}{n+1}\right],\quad
 \left[\frac{2}{n+1},\frac{n^2-n+2}{n(n+1)}\right],\quad
 \left[\frac{n^2-n+2}{n(n+1)},1\right].
\]
Putting $x=1/p$ and $y=1/q$ in \eqref{E:facets} gives exactly
\eqref{E:Q}.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{E:vertical}
 Q_n(p)=\sup\{q\ge p:(1/p,1/q)\in\Omega(n)\}.
\end{equation}
Oikari's Theorem 1.10 proves \eqref{E:pq} whenever
$(1/p,1/q)$ is in the interior of $\Omega(n)$, as well as on the diagonal
$q=p$ \cite{Oikari}.  This proves part 1 of Theorem~\ref{T:main}.

\section{The separating symbol}

Use the anisotropic quasi-distance
\[
 \rho_\beta(x,y)\asymp\max_{1\le j\le n}|x_j-y_j|^{1/\beta_j}.
\]
The source's Lemma 3.8 gives
\begin{equation}\label{E:holderbmo}
 \|b\|_{\BMO^{\beta,\alpha}}\asymp
 \sup_{x\ne y}\frac{|b(x)-b(y)|}{\rho_\beta(x,y)^\alpha}
 \qquad(\alpha>0).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[separator]\label{L:separator}
Let $0<\alpha\le\beta_n$.  There is a smooth bounded function
$b(x)=\eta(x_n)$ which is zero on a neighborhood of the origin, one on a
neighborhood of the positive curve arc
\[
 \Gamma_+=\{\gamma(t):1/2\le t\le1\},
\]
and belongs to $\BMO^{\beta,\alpha}(\R^n)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the positive branch, the last coordinate of $\Gamma_+$ lies in the signed
interval with absolute values between $2^{-\beta_n}$ and $1$.  It is therefore
separated from zero.  Choose $\eta\in C^\infty(\R)$ which is zero on a small
interval about zero and one on a small neighborhood of that signed interval.

Since $\eta$ is bounded and Lipschitz,
\[
 |\eta(x_n)-\eta(y_n)|\lesssim\min(1,|x_n-y_n|).
\]
If $\rho_\beta(x,y)\le1$, then
$|x_n-y_n|\lesssim\rho_\beta(x,y)^{\beta_n}\le
\rho_\beta(x,y)^\alpha$.  If $\rho_\beta(x,y)\ge1$, boundedness of $\eta$
gives the same conclusion.  Now use \eqref{E:holderbmo}.
\end{proof}

\section{Transfer of the sharp single-scale obstruction}

\begin{lemma}[separated-support transfer]\label{L:transfer}
If $(1/p,1/q)$ lies strictly outside $\Omega(n)$ and
$0<\alpha\le\beta_n$, then \eqref{E:pq} fails.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the positive single-scale operator
\[
 \widetilde A_1^\gamma f(x)=\int_{1/2\le t<1}
 f(x-\gamma(t))\,dt,
\]
Cladek and Ou prove the following sharpness statement
\cite[Sec.~4.2]{CladekOu}.  For every sufficiently small neighborhood $U$ of
the origin there are nonnegative $f_k$ supported in $U$ with
$\|f_k\|_p=1$ and
\[
                 \|\widetilde A_1^\gamma f_k\|_q\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
By duality there are nonnegative $g_k$, with $\|g_k\|_{q'}=1$, supported in
an arbitrarily small neighborhood $V$ of $\Gamma_+$, such that the
corresponding Hilbert-transform pairings tend to infinity.  Their support
argument isolates the positive branch and the finitely many relevant dyadic
scales, so in particular
\begin{equation}\label{E:CO}
                 \ip{H_\gamma f_k}{g_k}\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{equation}

Choose $U,V$ small enough for the symbol in Lemma~\ref{L:separator} to
satisfy $b=0$ on $U$ and $b=1$ on $V$.  Then $bf_k=0$ and $bg_k=g_k$.
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
 \ip{[b,H_\gamma]f_k}{g_k}
 &=\ip{bH_\gamma f_k}{g_k}-\ip{H_\gamma(bf_k)}{g_k}\\
 &=\ip{H_\gamma f_k}{g_k}\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{align*}
If \eqref{E:pq} held, Hölder duality and the normalizations would instead
give
\[
 |\ip{[b,H_\gamma]f_k}{g_k}|
 \le C_{p,q,\gamma}\|b\|_{\BMO^{\beta,\alpha}}
 \quad\text{uniformly in }k,
\]
a contradiction.
\end{proof}

For $q>Q_n(p)$, equation \eqref{E:vertical} places $(1/p,1/q)$ strictly
outside $\Omega(n)$.  Lemma~\ref{L:transfer} proves part 2 of
Theorem~\ref{T:main}.

\section{Collapse above the largest anisotropic exponent}

\begin{lemma}[triviality]\label{L:trivial}
If $\alpha>\beta_n$, every element of
$\BMO^{\beta,\alpha}(\R^n)$ is constant modulo null sets.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{E:holderbmo}, choose the anisotropic Hölder representative of $b$.
Along the $j$th coordinate line,
\[
 |b(x+he_j)-b(x)|\lesssim |h|^{\alpha/\beta_j}.
\]
Here $\alpha/\beta_j>1$ for every $j$.  Divide the segment from $x$ to
$x+he_j$ into $N$ equal pieces.  The triangle inequality gives
\[
 |b(x+he_j)-b(x)|
 \lesssim N\left(\frac{|h|}{N}\right)^{\alpha/\beta_j}
 =|h|^{\alpha/\beta_j}N^{1-\alpha/\beta_j}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus $b$ is constant on every coordinate line, hence on $\R^n$.
\end{proof}

Constants commute with $H_\gamma$, proving part 3.  It remains only to
extract \eqref{E:pmax}.  If $p\ge B/\beta_n$, then for every finite $q$,
\[
 \alpha=B(1/p-1/q)<B/p\le\beta_n.
\]
Part 2 therefore excludes every $q>Q_n(p)$, and the supremum is $Q_n(p)$.

If $p<B/\beta_n$, equation $\alpha=\beta_n$ has the finite solution
\eqref{E:qtriv}.  For $q>q_{\mathrm{triv}}$, Lemma~\ref{L:trivial} makes
\eqref{E:pq} trivial, so the literal supremum is infinity.  Moreover,
$q_{\mathrm{triv}}>Q_n(p)$.  Indeed, throughout $\Omega(n)$ one has
\[
 0\le x-y\le\frac{2}{n(n+1)},
\]
and, because the exponents are strictly increasing,
\[
 B\frac{2}{n(n+1)}<\frac{2\beta_n}{n+1}<\beta_n.
\]
Thus $\alpha<\beta_n$ at the vertical lower boundary, while $\alpha$ grows
strictly with $q$ and reaches $\beta_n$ only at $q_{\mathrm{triv}}$.
Part 2 now gives failure on
$Q_n(p)<q\le q_{\mathrm{triv}}$, completing the proof of
Theorem~\ref{T:main}.

\section{Verification, upgrade audit, and novelty bounds}

The proof was developed through six focused routes.  A direct ball/tube test
first recovered both facets for the parabola.  The adjoint test and a
one-coordinate separator extended this to every planar monomial curve.  In
higher dimensions the same test recovered only the two outer facets and
missed the middle one.  The successful upgrade was to transfer the complete
sharp single-scale sequence from Cladek--Ou, which supplies every facet in
every dimension.  A separate audit of high Hölder orders found the collapse
of the symbol space and the resulting defect in the literal supremum.  The
remaining boundary case was checked and is not needed for the half-open
interval or its supremum.

The run indexes were searched for arXiv:2304.00621, ``monomial curve
commutator'', ``maximal interval'', ``fractional BMO'', and the exact wording
of Question 1.15.  Bounded primary-source web and arXiv searches through
13 August 2026 checked the source, arXiv:1704.07810, and later curved
commutator work including arXiv:2403.08338.  The latter completes the planar
$p=q$ lower-bound theory, not the fractional sufficiency range.  No paper was
found that states the theorem above or answers Question 1.15.  Novelty
confidence is moderate because the literature search was bounded.

Promote for expert review.  The most important review point is that the
precise support localization in Cladek--Ou Section 4.2 is strong enough to
choose one separator with $bf_k=0$ and $bg_k=g_k$ throughout the sequence.
The second point is the distinction between the initial interval endpoint and
the unrestricted supremum when the Campanato class becomes constant.
