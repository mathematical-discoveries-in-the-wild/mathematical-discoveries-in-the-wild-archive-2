\section*{Source Question and Answer}

Gol\'enia and Jecko derive a limiting absorption principle (LAP) from a
strict weighted Mourre estimate by a localised Putnam argument, without
differential inequalities.  They ask whether this approach gives continuity
of the norm boundary values of the sandwiched resolvent \cite[pp.~3--4]{GJ}.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.91\linewidth]{figures/open_question_crop.png}\\[-2pt]
\includegraphics[width=0.91\linewidth]{figures/open_question_continuation.png}
\end{center}

We give a negative answer at the exact level of their general weighted
Mourre theorem: all its strict-estimate, localisation, and conjugation
hypotheses can hold while a norm boundary value fails to exist.  Thus neither
existence nor continuity follows from that estimate and energy argument
alone.  Status: \emph{candidate full counterexample, likely valid}, subject
to human review.

\section*{The One-Block Model}

On $\Kc=L^2(\R)$ let $Q$ be multiplication by $x$ and
$P=-i\,d/dx$.  Set
\[
 D=(1+P^2)^{-1},\qquad
 g(t)=\frac12\left(\arctan t+\frac{t}{1+t^2}\right),\qquad
 b(t)=\frac\pi4-g(t),\qquad B_0=b(P).
 \tag{1}\label{eq:def}
\]
The function $b$ is bounded and nonnegative.  Since
$g'(t)=(1+t^2)^{-2}$ and $[Q,f(P)]=if'(P)$,
\[
 [Q,iB_0]=-b'(P)=(1+P^2)^{-2}=D^2.
 \tag{2}\label{eq:comm}
\]
For $z\notin\R$ write
\[
 S(z)=D(Q-z)^{-1}D.
 \tag{3}\label{eq:S}
\]
The positive-commutator energy estimate gives
$\sup_{z\notin\R}\|S(z)\|<\infty$; this is also the elementary free
one-dimensional LAP with two derivatives of smoothing.

We need two endpoint facts, including the first one in operator norm.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:boundary}
There is a nonzero $S_+\in\mathcal B(\Kc)$ such that
\[
 \lim_{\tau\downarrow0}\|S(i\tau)-S_+\|=0,
 \qquad
 \|S(iy)\|\leq y^{-1}\quad(y>0).
 \tag{4}\label{eq:endpoints}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $u=Df$, $v=Dh$, with $f,h\in L^2(\R)$,
\[
 \langle f,S(i\tau)h\rangle
   =\int_{\R}\frac{\overline{u(x)}v(x)}{x-i\tau}\,dx.
 \tag{5}\label{eq:form}
\]
Here $D:L^2(\R)\to H^2(\R)$ is bounded.  If
$F=\overline uv$, then
\[
 \|F\|_1+\|F\|_\infty+\operatorname{Lip}(F)
 \leq C\|f\|_2\|h\|_2
 \tag{6}\label{eq:product}
\]
by the one-dimensional Sobolev embedding.  Splitting the integrals into
$|x|\leq\tau$, $\tau<|x|\leq1$, and $|x|>1$ in
\[
 \frac1{x-i\tau}=\frac{x}{x^2+\tau^2}
       +i\frac{\tau}{x^2+\tau^2}
\]
gives, uniformly for the functions in \eqref{eq:product},
\[
 \int\frac{F(x)}{x-i\tau}\,dx
 =\operatorname{pv}\!\int\frac{F(x)}x\,dx+i\pi F(0)
   +O\!\left(\tau\log\frac2\tau\right).
 \tag{7}\label{eq:plemelj}
\]
Thus the right side without the error defines a bounded sesquilinear form,
and \eqref{eq:plemelj} proves operator-norm convergence to its operator
$S_+$.  This operator is nonzero: choosing $u\in H^2(\R)$ with $u(0)\ne0$
and $f=(1+P^2)u$ gives
\[
 \operatorname{Im}\langle f,S_+f\rangle=\pi|u(0)|^2>0.
\]
Finally, the spectral theorem yields
$\|S(iy)\|\leq\|D\|^2\|(Q-iy)^{-1}\|\leq y^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\section*{The Direct-Sum Counterexample}

Let $\delta_n=2^{-n}$ and, on a copy $\Kc_n$ of $\Kc$, define
\[
 H_n=\delta_nQ,\qquad B_n=B_0,
 \qquad C_n=\delta_n^{1/2}D.
\]
On $\Hc=\bigoplus_{n\geq1}\Kc_n$, take the natural direct sums
\[
 H=\bigoplus_{n\geq1}H_n,\qquad
 B=\bigoplus_{n\geq1}B_n,\qquad
 C=\bigoplus_{n\geq1}C_n.
 \tag{8}\label{eq:sums}
\]
The operator $H$ is self-adjoint on its direct-sum graph domain, $B$ is
bounded self-adjoint and nonnegative, and $C$ is bounded, positive, and
injective.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:counterexample}
The triple $(H,B,C)$ satisfies the strict weighted projected Mourre estimate
and every auxiliary hypothesis of \cite[Theorem~3.4]{GJ} on
$I=(-2,2)$ and $I'=[-1,1]$.  Its sandwiched resolvent satisfies the uniform
LAP there, but
\[
 \lim_{\eta\downarrow0}C(H-i\eta)^{-1}C
 \quad\text{does not exist in operator norm}.
 \tag{9}\label{eq:no-limit}
\]
In particular, the weighted Mourre hypotheses do not imply continuous norm
boundary values.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each $H_n$ is a rescaled multiplication operator and has no eigenvalues, so
the pure-point projection is zero.  Equations \eqref{eq:comm} and
\eqref{eq:sums} give the global form identity
\[
 [H,iB]=\bigoplus_n\delta_nD^2=C^2.
 \tag{10}\label{eq:strict}
\]
Consequently the strict weighted projected estimate holds as equality after
localisation to any interval.

It remains to check the two conjugations in the abstract theorem.  Since
$B_0$ and $D$ are functions of $P$, $CBC^{-1}$ agrees with $B$ on
$\Ran C$ and extends boundedly.  Choose
$\chi\in C_c^\infty((-2,2))$ equal to one on $[-1,1]$.  The $n$th block of
$C\chi(H)C^{-1}$ is $D\chi(\delta_nQ)D^{-1}$.  For
$f_n(x)=\chi(\delta_nx)$, the commutation rule with $P$ gives the exact
identity
\[
 Df_n(Q)D^{-1}
 =f_n(Q)+2iDPf_n'(Q)-Df_n''(Q).
 \tag{11}\label{eq:cutoff}
\]
Because $\|DP\|=1/2$, $\|D\|=1$, and
$\|f_n^{(k)}\|_\infty\leq\delta_n^k\|\chi^{(k)}\|_\infty$, these blocks
are bounded uniformly in $n$.  Thus all hypotheses of the cited theorem
hold.  They imply the uniform LAP; directly, scaling also gives
\[
 C_n(H_n-z)^{-1}C_n=S(z/\delta_n).
 \tag{12}\label{eq:scale}
\]

Let $m=\|S_+\|>0$.  From \eqref{eq:scale},
\[
 T(\eta):=C(H-i\eta)^{-1}C
   =\bigoplus_{n\geq1}S(i\eta/\delta_n).
 \tag{13}\label{eq:T}
\]
If $T(\eta)$ converged in norm, compression to every fixed block and
Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary} would force its limit to be
$T_+=\bigoplus_nS_+$.  For every $\eta>0$, however, one can choose $n$ so
large that $y:=\eta/\delta_n\geq2/m$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary} then gives
\[
 \|T(\eta)-T_+\|
 \geq\|S(iy)-S_+\|
 \geq m-\|S(iy)\|
 \geq\frac m2.
\]
This contradicts norm convergence and proves \eqref{eq:no-limit}.
\end{proof}

\section*{Scope, Novelty Check, and Verification}

The counterexample satisfies the source's \emph{general} weighted Mourre
theorem, from which its principal LAP theorem is derived.  It proves that
the strict weighted estimate plus the localised Putnam energy argument alone
cannot furnish norm boundary values.  It does not exclude an additional
theorem that uses, simultaneously across all scales, the narrower structure
$C=\langle A\rangle^{-s}$ and $B=\varphi(A)$ occurring in Theorem~1.1.

The same continuity limitation was stated explicitly in a 2017 follow-up
\cite{JM}.  A 2020 sequel says that the weighted-Mourre contradiction proof
provides no continuity description \cite{GM}.  Cheap indexes and bounded
searches through 17 August 2026 found no later answer.  This is not a
priority claim.

the dyadic escape mechanism.  The proof above is exact; the computation is
only an auxiliary transcription check.
