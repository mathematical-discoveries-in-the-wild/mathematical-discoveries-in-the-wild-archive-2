\section*{Source question}

Daptari and Paul \cite{DP} work over real Banach spaces. In the paragraph
containing Theorems 2.2.2 and 2.2.3 they fix a probability space
$(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)$, a closed subspace $Y\subset X$, and assume that $X^*$
has the Radon--Nikodym property (RNP). They prove the implications from the
function-space property back to $Y\subset X$, then ask whether their converses
hold. The exact source passage is on source PDF page 7 (source lines 278--293).

``We do not know the validity of the converse of Theorem 2.2.2, 2.2.3.''

\section*{Definitions}

A closed subspace $Y$ has \emph{property-$\wU$ in $X$} if every
norm-attaining $y^*\in Y^*$ has a unique norm-preserving Hahn--Banach extension
to $X$. Here $L^p(\mu;X)$ is the Bochner space and $C(K;X)$ is the space of
continuous $X$-valued functions on a compact Hausdorff space $K$.

\section*{Proof intuition}

The mechanism is pointwise. A norm-attaining functional on a Bochner space has
a dual density $g(\omega)$. Equality in the two inequalities used to estimate
its value forces $g(\omega)$ itself to be norm-attaining almost everywhere.
If $h(\omega)$ represents a norm-preserving extension, restriction gives
$\|h(\omega)\|\geq\|g(\omega)\|$, while equality of the global norms forces
pointwise equality. Property-$\wU$ then identifies all extension densities.

For $C(K;X)$, the same argument is performed with representing vector measures.
Restriction decreases total variation. Since a norm-preserving extension has
the same total mass, its variation measure must equal that of the original
functional. RNP densities then reduce uniqueness to property-$\wU$ pointwise.

\section*{Result}

\begin{theorem}[Both converse questions]
Let $X$ be a real Banach space, let $Y\subset X$ be a closed subspace, and
assume that $X^*$ has the RNP.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item If $(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)$ is a probability space and $1<p<\infty$, then
\[
Y\text{ has property-}\wU\text{ in }X
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
L^p(\mu;Y)\text{ has property-}\wU\text{ in }L^p(\mu;X).
\]
\item If $K$ is a nonempty compact Hausdorff space, then
\[
Y\text{ has property-}\wU\text{ in }X
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
C(K;Y)\text{ has property-}\wU\text{ in }C(K;X).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first record the easy implications from right to left, which are the
directions proved in \cite[Theorems 2.2.2--2.2.3]{DP}. If property-$\wU$ fails
for $Y\subset X$, choose a norm-one $y^*\in Y^*$, a point $y_0\in S_Y$ with
$y^*(y_0)=1$, and two distinct norm-one extensions $x_1^*,x_2^*\in X^*$.

For the $L^p$ assertion, the functionals
\[
F_j(f)=\int_\Omega x_j^*(f(\omega))\dd\mu(\omega),\qquad j=1,2,
\]
are distinct norm-one extensions of the same functional on $L^p(\mu;Y)$ and
attain their norm at the constant function $y_0$. For $C(K)$, fix $t_0\in K$
and use $F_j(f)=x_j^*(f(t_0))$. Thus the function-space property implies the
base-space property.

It remains to prove the two converse implications.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Bochner $L^p$ spaces.}
Let $q=p/(p-1)$. The RNP passes to the quotient $Y^*$ of $X^*$, so the standard
Bochner duality theorem \cite{DU} gives the isometric representations
\[
(L^p(\mu;X))^*=L^q(\mu;X^*),\qquad
(L^p(\mu;Y))^*=L^q(\mu;Y^*).
\]
Assume that $Y$ has property-$\wU$ in $X$. Let a nonzero functional on
$L^p(\mu;Y)$ be represented by $g\in L^q(\mu;Y^*)$ and attain its norm at
$f\in S_{L^p(\mu;Y)}$. Replacing $f$ by $-f$ if needed, we have
\begin{align*}
\|g\|_q
 &=\int_\Omega g(\omega)(f(\omega))\dd\mu(\omega)\\
 &\leq\int_\Omega \|g(\omega)\|\,\|f(\omega)\|\dd\mu(\omega)
 \leq \|g\|_q\|f\|_p=\|g\|_q.
\end{align*}
Both inequalities are equalities. The first gives
\[
g(\omega)(f(\omega))=\|g(\omega)\|\,\|f(\omega)\|
\quad\text{for a.e. }\omega.
\]
Equality in scalar Holder, with $1<p,q<\infty$, shows that
$f(\omega)\neq0$ wherever $g(\omega)\neq0$, up to a null set. Consequently
$g(\omega)$ is norm-attaining for almost every $\omega$ on which it is
nonzero.

Now let two norm-preserving extensions be represented by
$h_1,h_2\in L^q(\mu;X^*)$. If $r:X^*\to Y^*$ is restriction, uniqueness of the
Bochner representation gives
\[
r(h_j(\omega))=g(\omega)\quad\text{a.e.},\qquad j=1,2.
\]
Hence $\|h_j(\omega)\|\geq\|g(\omega)\|$ almost everywhere. But
\[
\int\|h_j\|^q\dd\mu=\|h_j\|_q^q=\|g\|_q^q
=\int\|g\|^q\dd\mu,
\]
so $\|h_j(\omega)\|=\|g(\omega)\|$ almost everywhere. On the nonzero set,
$h_j(\omega)$ is therefore a norm-preserving extension of the norm-attaining
functional $g(\omega)$; property-$\wU$ yields $h_1(\omega)=h_2(\omega)$. On
the zero set, norm equality forces both densities to vanish. Thus the two
global extensions coincide. The zero functional has only the zero
norm-preserving extension, completing (i).

\medskip
\noindent\emph{$C(K)$ spaces.}
Again assume that $Y$ has property-$\wU$ in $X$. By the vector-measure
representation theorem \cite{DU}, let a nonzero norm-attaining functional on
$C(K;Y)$ correspond to a regular $Y^*$-valued measure $m$ of bounded variation.
Since $Y^*$ has the RNP, write
\[
m(A)=\int_A g(t)\dd|m|(t),\qquad \|g(t)\|=1\quad |m|\text{-a.e.}
\]
Let $f\in S_{C(K;Y)}$ be chosen with the functional taking the value
$\|m\|=|m|(K)$ at $f$. Then
\[
|m|(K)=\int_K g(t)(f(t))\dd|m|(t)
\leq\int_K\|f(t)\|\dd|m|(t)\leq|m|(K).
\]
Thus $g(t)(f(t))=1$ for $|m|$-almost every $t$. In particular, $g(t)$ is a
norm-attaining member of $Y^*$ almost everywhere.

Let $n$ be the regular $X^*$-valued measure representing any norm-preserving
extension. Equality on $C(K;Y)$ gives $r\circ n=m$. Therefore, for every Borel
set $A$,
\[
|m|(A)\leq |n|(A).
\]
The total masses are the functional norms, so
$|n|(K)=|m|(K)$. The positive measure $|n|-|m|$ has total mass zero, whence
\[
|n|=|m|.
\]
The RNP of $X^*$ now gives a Bochner density
\[
n(A)=\int_A h(t)\dd|m|(t),\qquad \|h(t)\|=1\quad |m|\text{-a.e.}
\]
The identity $r\circ n=m$ implies $r(h(t))=g(t)$ almost everywhere. Thus
$h(t)$ is a norm-preserving extension of the norm-attaining $g(t)$, and
property-$\wU$ determines it uniquely almost everywhere. Any two representing
extension measures therefore coincide. This proves (ii).
\end{proof}

\section*{Verification and scope}

The proof retains every standing hypothesis from the source. It uses no
measurable selection: the densities of the extensions are already supplied by
the dual and vector-measure representation theorems. The zero functional,
null sets, and both directions of each equivalence are handled explicitly.

The result is not asserted at $p=1$ or $p=\infty$, without the RNP hypothesis,
or over complex scalars. The last case should admit a phase-normalized variant,
but it is outside the source's stated real setting.

\section*{Novelty check}

Bounded searches on 9 August 2026 used the exact title and arXiv id, the exact
open-question sentence, `property-(wU)' together with $L^p(\mu;Y)$ and
$C(K;Y)$, and `weak Hahn--Banach extension property' with vector-valued
function spaces. They found the source and mirrors, but no later paper claiming
either converse. This is a bounded search, not a definitive priority claim.

\section*{Human-review recommendation}

Review the two standard representation inputs and, in the $C(K)$ proof, the
step $|m|\leq|n|$ plus equality of total masses. Once those are accepted, the
remaining argument is pointwise and direct.
