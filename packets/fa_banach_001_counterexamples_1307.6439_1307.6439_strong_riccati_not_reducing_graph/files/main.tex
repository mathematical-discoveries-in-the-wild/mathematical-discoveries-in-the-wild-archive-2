\section{Source problem}

Makarov, Schmitz, and Seelmann~\cite{MSS13} consider
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}A_0&0\\0&A_1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 V=\begin{pmatrix}0&W\\W^*&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-X^*\\X&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
where $A_0,A_1$ are self-adjoint, $X$ is bounded,
$\Dom(W)\supset\Dom(A_1)$, and $\Dom(W^*)\supset\Dom(A_0)$.  They compare

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\cG(\cH_0,X)$ is reducing for $A+V$;
\item $Y$ is a strong solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:riccati}
 AY-YA-YVY+V=0.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

On page 3 they state that they have no counterexample in which only one of
these requirements holds, and ask whether the two are equivalent or
logically independent.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{The open problem in the introduction of~\cite[p.~3]{MSS13}.}
  \label{fig:problem}
\end{figure}

The associated graph is
$\cG(\cH_0,X)=\{f\oplus Xf:f\in\cH_0\}$.  ``Reducing'' includes both
invariance of this graph and its orthogonal complement and the domain
splitting by their intersections with $\Dom(A+V)$.  ``Strong'' means that
$Y\Dom(A)\subset\Dom(A)$ and \eqref{eq:riccati} holds throughout
$\Dom(A)$.

\section{Proof intuition}

Domain preservation by a bounded operator need not pass to its bounded
inverse when the domain carries a much stronger graph norm.  We exploit that
gap with a Toeplitz operator $X$ which has Hilbert-space norm at most $1/20$
but whose realization on a rapidly weighted domain has $i$ in its spectrum.
Thus $X$ preserves the domain while $(I+X^2)^{-1}$ does not.  The first fact
is exactly the domain part of strong Riccati solvability; the second obstructs
the domain splitting required for a reducing graph.

It remains to choose the off-diagonal operator.  Writing
$C=AX-XA$, the Riccati equation reduces to
$W+XWX=C$.  Because $X$ is tiny in the Hilbert norm even though it is large
in the graph norm, the alternating series
$\sum_{n\ge0}(-1)^nX^nCX^n$ converges on the weighted domain, telescopes to
the required identity, and is skew-symmetric.  This supplies $W$ and $W^*$
with exactly the domains required by the source hypotheses.

\section{Counterexample theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There are separable Hilbert spaces $\cH_0=\cH_1$, self-adjoint operators
$A_0=A_1$, a densely defined operator $W$ satisfying
\[
 \Dom(W)\supset\Dom(A_1),\qquad
 \Dom(W^*)\supset\Dom(A_0),
\]
and a bounded positive self-adjoint $X:\cH_0\to\cH_1$ such that the operator
$Y$ above is a strong solution of \eqref{eq:riccati}, but
$\cG(\cH_0,X)$ is not reducing for $A+V$.

Consequently requirement \emph{(ii)} in the source problem does not imply
requirement \emph{(i)}, and the two requirements are not equivalent in the
generality of Hypotheses 2.1--2.2 of~\cite{MSS13}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $K=\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)$, with standard basis $(e_n)_{n\ge0}$, and let
$S e_n=e_{n+1}$ be the unilateral shift.  Define
\[
 A e_n=200^n e_n,
 \qquad
 \cD:=\Dom(A)
 =\left\{u=(u_n):\sum_{n\ge0}200^{2n}|u_n|^2<\infty\right\}.
\]
Then $A$ is self-adjoint, $A\ge I$, and $A:\cD\to K$ is an isometric
isomorphism when $\cD$ is given the norm $\|u\|_{\cD}=\|Au\|$.

Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:X}
 X=\frac3{100}I+\frac1{100}(S+S^*).
\end{equation}
Since $-2I\le S+S^*\le2I$, the operator $X$ is positive and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
 \|X\|\le\frac1{20}=:q.
\end{equation}
Both $S$ and $S^*$ preserve $\cD$, so $X\cD\subset\cD$.  Conjugating its
restriction to the graph-norm space gives the bounded operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z}
 Z:=AXA^{-1}
 =\frac3{100}I+2S+\frac1{20000}S^*,
 \qquad
 \|Z\|\le\frac{40601}{20000}=:M.
\end{equation}

We first prove the crucial failure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:failure}
 (I+X^2)^{-1}\cD\not\subset\cD.
\end{equation}
Indeed, consider the quadratic polynomial
\[
 p(z)=2z^2+\left(\frac3{100}+i\right)z+\frac1{20000}.
\]
On $|z|=1$,
\[
 \left|\left(\frac3{100}+i\right)z+\frac1{20000}\right|
 \le \sqrt{1+\frac9{10000}}+\frac1{20000}<2=|2z^2|.
\]
Rouch\'e's theorem puts both roots of $p$ in the open unit disk.  Set
$v_{-1}=0$, $v_0=1$, and recursively
\[
 2v_{n+1}+\left(\frac3{100}+i\right)v_n
   +\frac1{20000}v_{n-1}=0.
\]
Since both characteristic roots are in the open disk, the nonzero sequence
$v=(v_n)$ belongs to $\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)$.  Formula \eqref{eq:Z} gives
$(Z^*+i)v=0$, whence $i\in\spec(Z)$.  By polynomial spectral mapping,
$I+Z^2$ is not invertible.

If $R:=(I+X^2)^{-1}$ mapped $\cD$ into itself, then its restriction on the
Banach space $\cD$ would be bounded by the closed graph theorem: convergence
in $\cD$ implies convergence in $K$, where $R$ is bounded.  Hence
$ARA^{-1}$ would be a bounded two-sided inverse of $I+Z^2$, because
$AX^2A^{-1}=Z^2$.  This contradiction proves \eqref{eq:failure}.

Now define on $\cD$
\[
 C:=AX-XA,
 \qquad
 W:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^nX^nCX^n.
\]
This series converges in $K$ for every $u\in\cD$.  In fact,
\begin{align*}
 \|X^nCX^nu\|
 &\le q^n\bigl(\|AX^{n+1}u\|+q\|AX^nu\|\bigr)\\
 &\le (M+q)(qM)^n\|Au\|,
\end{align*}
and
\[
 qM=\frac{40601}{400000}<1.
\]
Thus $W:\cD\to K$ is well-defined and graph-norm bounded.  The operator
$C$ is skew-symmetric on $\cD$, and so is every $X^nCX^n$; absolute
convergence of the scalar products shows that $W$ is skew-symmetric.  In
particular $W$ is densely defined and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjoint}
 \cD\subset\Dom(W^*),\qquad W^*u=-Wu\quad(u\in\cD).
\end{equation}
Moreover, adding the two convergent series and shifting the index yields the
telescoping identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stein}
 W+XWX=C=AX-XA\quad\hbox{on }\cD.
\end{equation}

Take $\cH_0=\cH_1=K$, $A_0=A_1=A$, and $\Dom(W)=\cD$, and form
\[
 \mathbf A=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\0&A\end{pmatrix},\quad
 V=\begin{pmatrix}0&W\\W^*&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-X\\X&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The domain hypotheses on $W$ follow from \eqref{eq:adjoint}.  Since
$X\cD\subset\cD$, the operator $Y$ maps
$\Dom(\mathbf A)=\cD\oplus\cD$ into itself.  The two off-diagonal block
equations in \eqref{eq:riccati} both reduce, using \eqref{eq:adjoint}, to
\[
 AX-XA-XWX-W=0,
\]
which is exactly \eqref{eq:stein}.  Hence $Y$ is a strong Riccati solution.

It remains to disprove reduction.  The Riccati identity already makes
$\cG(K,X)$ and $\cG(K,-X)$ invariant for $\mathbf A+V$.  Their domain
intersections sum to
\[
 T(\cD\oplus\cD),\qquad
 T=I+Y=\begin{pmatrix}I&-X\\X&I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Because $X=X^*$,
\[
 T^{-1}=
 \begin{pmatrix}R&XR\\-XR&R\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad R=(I+X^2)^{-1}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:failure}, some $f\in\cD$ has $Rf\notin\cD$.  Therefore
$T^{-1}(f\oplus0)\notin\cD\oplus\cD$, so
$T(\cD\oplus\cD)\ne\cD\oplus\cD$.  The domain does not split along the
two invariant graphs, and $\cG(K,X)$ is not reducing.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The proof is exact.  The deterministic script
\path{code/check_parameters.py} verifies the rational contraction and
geometric-series bounds, the strict Rouch\'e inequality, and the two
characteristic roots.  It is an illustration rather than a substitute for
the operator proof.  A separate sign-by-sign block multiplication and the
domain-transfer argument are recorded in \path{VERIFICATION.md}.

This example completely disproves the implication ``strong solution
$\Rightarrow$ reducing graph'' and therefore disproves general equivalence.
It does not disprove the converse implication.  Thus, if ``logical
independence'' is reserved for failure of both implications, one half of that
stronger statement remains open.

The local run indexes were searched for arXiv:1307.6439, the exact title, and
the core Riccati/graph terms.  Bounded web searches on August 9, 2026 used the
exact open-problem sentence, the exact title and authors, and combinations of
``strong solution,'' ``reducing graph,'' and ``counterexample.''  They found
the source paper and the authors' later paper~\cite{MSS16}, which still calls
the corresponding domain-splitting/domain-invariance question open, but no
later resolution or this construction.  Novelty confidence is positive but
conservative pending expert literature review.

Human review is strongly recommended.  The decisive checks are the passage
from $i\in\spec(Z)$ to failure of domain preservation, convergence and skew
symmetry of the series defining $W$, and the signs in the two Riccati blocks.
