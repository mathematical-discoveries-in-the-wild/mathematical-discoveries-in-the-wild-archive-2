\section*{Original questions}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Open problem 3.} Find out a criterion for the Nash inequality in
dimension one.

\textbf{Open question 4.} Does there exist a variational formula in the form
of part (2) of Theorem 4.1 for the logarithmic Sobolev or Nash inequality?
\end{quote}
The source's Added in Proof excludes these two questions from the problems
solved before publication \cite{Chen01}.

\section*{Problem 3: explicit later answer}
Mao's introduction explicitly says that the paper addresses the question
posed in Chen's source and describes the answer as complete for birth--death
processes \cite{Mao02}.  For an ergodic birth--death process with invariant
probability masses $\mu_k$, death rates $a_k$, and $\nu>2$, Theorem 2.2(ii)
states that the centered Nash inequality holds if and only if
\[
 \sup_{n\ge1}
 \left(\sum_{k\ge n}\mu_k\right)^{(\nu-2)/\nu}
 \sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac1{\mu_k a_k}<\infty.
\]
This is the source table's proposed condition after using
$\mu_k a_k=\mu_{k-1}b_{k-1}$, with the stated epsilon gap removed.

For the ergodic half-line diffusion
$L=a(x)d^2/dx^2+b(x)d/dx$, put
$C(x)=\int_0^x b/a$ and normalize
$d\mu=Z^{-1}a^{-1}e^Cdx$.  Mao's Theorem 3.1(ii) gives the corresponding
if-and-only-if criterion
\[
 \sup_{x>0}
 \left(\int_x^\infty\frac{e^{C(y)}}{a(y)}\,dy\right)^{(\nu-2)/\nu}
 \int_0^x e^{-C(y)}\,dy<\infty.
\]
The same paper treats transient versions and explicit two-sided bounds for
the optimal constants.  The supporting authors therefore knew they were
answering the extracted problem; this is not an agent-inferred implication.

\section*{Question 4: substantial but scoped later progress}
Chen's 2002 paper extends one-dimensional Poincar\'e variational methods to a
large class of Banach/Orlicz norms, motivated explicitly by Nash and
logarithmic-Sobolev inequalities \cite{Chen02}.  The resulting theorem gives
an exact finiteness criterion, explicit factor bounds, and two inf--sup
expressions that are labeled formulas for \emph{upper bounds}.  The 2003
birth--death analogue gives the discrete criteria and bounds \cite{Chen03}.
Specializing the Orlicz norm yields the Nash and logarithmic-Sobolev criteria.

This is an affirmative answer only under a weakened reading of ``variational
formula.''  The papers do not identify the sharp centered Nash or entropy
constant with both a sup--inf and an inf--sup expression, as Theorem 4.1(2)
does for the ordinary Poincar\'e constant.  The exact dual equality should
therefore not be marked literature-closed on this evidence.
