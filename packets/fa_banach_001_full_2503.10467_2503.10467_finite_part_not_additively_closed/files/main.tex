\noindent\textbf{Status: candidate full counterexample.}

\section{The question}

For an element $v$ of a cone $\C$, Gigli defines its part at infinity by
\[
 \epsp v:=\sup\{w\in\C:w\leq \lambda v\text{ for every }\lambda>0\},
 \qquad
 \Fin:=\{v\in\C:\epsp v=0\}.
\]
Remark~3.6 of \cite{Gigli} asks whether $\Fin$ is always closed under
addition and says that no counterexample is known (source PDF page~38).
The preceding discussion proves only
$\epsp(v+w)\geq\epsp v+\epsp w$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is a cone $\C$ for which $\Fin$ is not closed under addition.  In fact,
$\C$ admits a hyperbolic norm that is positive on every nonzero element and
finite on every element of $\Fin$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Construction and proof intuition}

Take two copies of the open positive ray, join their origins, and put one
common top above them.  Addition behaves normally along either ray, but a
sum involving both rays ``crashes'' into the top.  Shrinking an element along
one ray therefore leaves no nonzero common lower bound, whereas shrinking a
mixed sum does nothing because the top is fixed by every positive scalar.
This is exactly the strict-superadditivity mechanism that the question
allows but does not exhibit.

Formally, put
\[
 \C=\{0,\topel\}\cup\{a_s:s>0\}\cup\{b_t:t>0\}.
\]
Define $0+x=x$, $\topel+x=\topel$, and, for positive parameters,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:addition}
 a_s+a_t=a_{s+t},\qquad b_s+b_t=b_{s+t},\qquad a_s+b_t=\topel.
\end{equation}
For $\lambda>0$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalars}
 \lambda a_s=a_{\lambda s},\qquad
 \lambda b_t=b_{\lambda t},\qquad
 \lambda\topel=\topel,
\end{equation}
and put $0x=0$ for every $x\in\C$.

\section{Verification of the cone axioms}

The operations are commutative.  Associativity is ordinary when all
nonzero finite summands lie on one ray; as soon as both ray types occur,
both parenthesizations equal $\topel$.  The scalar laws and both
distributive laws follow from the same dichotomy.  For instance, if
$\lambda>0$, then
\[
 \lambda(a_s+b_t)=\lambda\topel=\topel
 =a_{\lambda s}+b_{\lambda t};
\]
the case $\lambda=0$ gives zero on both sides.  Thus $\C$ is a prewedge.

Its algebraic order $x\leq y\iff x+z=y$ for some $z$ is completely
described as follows:
\begin{align*}
 &0\leq x\leq\topel &&(x\in\C),\\
 &a_s\leq a_t\iff s\leq t,\qquad
   b_s\leq b_t\iff s\leq t,\\
 &a_s\text{ and }b_t\text{ are incomparable} &&(s,t>0).
\end{align*}
It is therefore antisymmetric.  A directed subset not containing
$\topel$ cannot meet both positive rays, since their only common upper bound
is $\topel$.  Hence it lies in one ray (apart from $0$).  If its parameters
are bounded, its supremum is the point with the usual real supremum; if they
are unbounded, its only upper bound is $\topel$.  A directed subset
containing $\topel$ plainly has supremum $\topel$.  Thus the order is
directed complete.

Finally,
\[
 a_s=\sup\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{a_{\eta s}:0<\eta<1\}\bigr),\qquad
 b_t=\sup\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{b_{\eta t}:0<\eta<1\}\bigr),
 \qquad \topel=\sup_{0\leq\eta<1}\eta\topel.
\]
Any $\eta\in(0,1)$ already gives $\eta\topel=\topel$.  The wedge
compatibility axiom holds, so $\C$ is a cone.

\section{The finite part}

Fix $s>0$.  If $w\leq\lambda a_s$ for every $\lambda>0$, the order
classification forces either $w=0$ or $w=a_r$ with
$r\leq\lambda s$ for every $\lambda>0$.  The latter is impossible for
$r>0$.  Hence
\[
 \epsp a_s=0,\qquad \epsp b_t=0\quad(s,t>0).
\]
On the other hand, $\lambda\topel=\topel$ for every $\lambda>0$ and every
element lies below $\topel$, whence $\epsp\topel=\topel$.  Consequently
\[
 \Fin=\{0\}\cup\{a_s:s>0\}\cup\{b_t:t>0\},
 \qquad a_1,b_1\in\Fin,\qquad a_1+b_1=\topel\notin\Fin.
\]
This proves the negative answer.

\section{A genuine hyperbolic Banach space}

Define
\[
 \lVert0\rVert=0,\qquad
 \lVert a_s\rVert=s,\qquad
 \lVert b_t\rVert=t,\qquad
 \lVert\topel\rVert=+\infty.
\]
For a nonnegative linear combination of two elements, if all positive terms
lie on one finite ray, the hyperbolic norm inequality is an equality.  In
every other nonzero case involving both rays or $\topel$, the resulting
element is $\topel$ and the left side is $+\infty$, so
\[
 \lVert\lambda x+\eta y\rVert
 \geq\lambda\lVert x\rVert+\eta\lVert y\rVert
 \qquad(\lambda,\eta\geq0).
\]
Thus $(\C,\lVert\cdot\rVert)$ is a hyperbolic Banach space under the
paper's definition, with a positive norm finite on all of $\Fin$.

\section{Three neighboring obstructions}

The same example sharpens the diagnosis preceding Remark~3.6:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The part at infinity is strictly superadditive, maximally so:
\[
 \epsp(a_1+b_1)=\topel
 \quad\text{but}\quad
 \epsp a_1+\epsp b_1=0.
\]
\item The desired Riesz-style decomposition of a bound fails.  Although
$a_2\leq\topel=a_1+b_1$, there are no $z_1\leq a_1$ and $z_2\leq b_1$
with $a_2=z_1+z_2$: if both are nonzero their sum is $\topel$, while if
one vanishes the other cannot equal $a_2$ under the stated bounds.
\item Translation need not preserve infima.  With
$A=\{a_{1/n}:n\geq1\}$, one has $\inf A=0$, but
\[
 A+b_1=\{\topel\},\qquad
 \inf(A+b_1)=\topel\ne b_1=b_1+\inf A.
\]
\end{enumerate}
In particular, the construction pinpoints why additional lattice or
decomposition hypotheses can repair the theory, while the axioms of a cone
alone cannot.

\section*{Novelty and scope}

The latest source revision available to this run (11 December 2025) still
states the question and the absence of counterexamples.  Bounded searches
of the run registry and of exact phrases, title, author, arXiv identifier,
and citations found no later answer.  Novelty is therefore provisional.
The result makes no claim for the paper's stronger subclasses, such as cones
with joins or cones satisfying directed decomposition.
