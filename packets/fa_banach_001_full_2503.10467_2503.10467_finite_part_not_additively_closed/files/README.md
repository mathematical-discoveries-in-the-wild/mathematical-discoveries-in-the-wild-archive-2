# The finite part of a hyperbolic cone need not be a wedge

This packet gives a full negative answer to Remark 3.6 of arXiv:2503.10467.
The counterexample has two ordinary positive rays and one absorbing top.
Each ray element has zero part at infinity, but the sum of nonzero elements
from different rays is the top, whose part at infinity is itself.

The same construction shows that the part-at-infinity map can be strictly
superadditive, the proposed Riesz-style decomposition of a bound can fail,
and translation need not preserve infima.  A natural extended-valued
hyperbolic norm makes it an example within the paper's titular class.
