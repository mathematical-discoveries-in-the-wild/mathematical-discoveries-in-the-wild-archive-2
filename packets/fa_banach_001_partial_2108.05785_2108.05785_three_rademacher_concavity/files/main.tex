\section{Problem and exact scope}

Zhang observes in Section 4, arXiv PDF page 9, that a proposed matrix
concavity statement would imply the long-open assertion that the Rademacher
sequence has concavity constant one~\cite{zhang,schechtman}.  In his notation
the exponent is $1/p$; we use the conventional moment exponent $p>2$.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The matrix statement is disproved later in Zhang's paper, so that route does
not answer the scalar problem.  Jenkins and Tkocz subsequently isolate the
precise remaining real range $2<p<3$ and the equivalent formulation below
\cite[Section 3.2]{jenkins}.

\begin{sourceproblem}
For $2<p<3$, is
\[
 \Phi_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
   :=\E\left|\sum_{k=1}^n x_k^{1/p}\eps_k\right|^p
\tag{1}\label{eq:phi}
\]
concave on $\R_+^n$ for every $n$, where the $\eps_k$ are independent
Rademacher variables?
\end{sourceproblem}

Schechtman's equivalences turn this into the constant-one inequality
\[
 \E\left\|\sum_{k=1}^n \eps_k f_k\right\|_p^p
 \leq
 \E\left|\sum_{k=1}^n \eps_k\|f_k\|_p\right|^p
\tag{2}\label{eq:hanner}
\]
for arbitrary real $L_p$ functions $f_k$~\cite{schechtman,jenkins}.  The
two-variable case is classical Hanner.  The theorem below settles the next
case $n=3$.

\section{Proof intuition}

The Hessian formula of Schechtman, in the convenient form recorded by
Jenkins--Tkocz, writes minus the Hessian as the Laplacian of a weighted
complete graph.  For three variables this graph is a triangle.  After the
three coefficients are ordered, two edges have nonnegative weight, while
only the edge joining the two smaller coefficients can be negative.

A weighted triangle with positive weights $a,b$ and a negative weight $-c$
has a positive semidefinite Laplacian exactly when the bad conductance $c$ is
at most the effective conductance $ab/(a+b)$ of the other two edges.  The
required estimate is elementary but not termwise: in the genuine triangle
regime it follows from two secant-ratio inequalities for $t^q$, $0<q<1$.
Both reduce to the fact that
\[
 g(a,h)=a\big((a+h)^q-a^q\big)
\]
is increasing in each variable.  Thus the elusive ``coarser grouping'' of
the Hessian terms becomes exact effective-resistance grouping when $n=3$.

\section{Laplacian reduction}

We begin with an elementary criterion.

\begin{lemma}[weighted triangle]\label{lem:triangle}
Let $a,b>0$ and $c\geq0$.  The quadratic form
\[
 Q(z)=a(z_1-z_2)^2+b(z_1-z_3)^2-c(z_2-z_3)^2
\]
is nonnegative on $\R^3$ if and only if
\[
 c(a+b)\leq ab.
\tag{3}\label{eq:conductance}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed value of $z_2-z_3$, minimize the first two terms over $z_1$.
Their minimum is $ab(z_2-z_3)^2/(a+b)$.  This gives
\eqref{eq:conductance}.
\end{proof}

For $x_k>0$, put $r_k=x_k^{1/p}$ and $S=\sum r_k\eps_k$.  The Hessian
identity from~\cite[Lemma 3]{jenkins} says that for every $h\in\R^n$,
\[
 h^{\mathsf T}D^2\Phi_n(x)h
 =-\frac{p-1}{p}\sum_{k<\ell}
 \left(\frac{h_k}{x_k}-\frac{h_\ell}{x_\ell}\right)^2
 r_kr_\ell\E\big[|S|^{p-2}\eps_k\eps_\ell\big].
\tag{4}\label{eq:hessian}
\]
Thus $\Phi_n$ is concave exactly when the associated weighted Laplacian is
positive semidefinite.

\section{Main theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $2<p<3$ and let $\eps_1,\eps_2,\eps_3$ be independent Rademacher
variables.  Then
\[
 (x_1,x_2,x_3)\longmapsto
 \E\left|x_1^{1/p}\eps_1+x_2^{1/p}\eps_2+x_3^{1/p}\eps_3\right|^p
\]
is concave on $\R_+^3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
It suffices first to work on the positive orthant.  Put $q=p-2\in(0,1)$ and,
after permuting indices, write the three root coefficients as
\[
 r\geq u\geq v>0.
\]
Define
\[
 \begin{array}{llll}
 A=r+u+v,&B=r+u-v,&C=r-u+v,&D=|r-u-v|,\\
 U=A^q,&V=B^q,&W=C^q,&X=D^q,
 \end{array}
\]
and
\[
 P=U+V-W-X,\qquad Q=U-V+W-X,\qquad
 R=U-V-W+X.
\tag{5}\label{eq:PQR}
\]
Direct averaging over the eight signs gives the three weights in
\eqref{eq:hessian}:
\[
 w_{12}=\frac{ru}{4}P,\qquad
 w_{13}=\frac{rv}{4}Q,\qquad
 w_{23}=\frac{uv}{4}R.
\tag{6}\label{eq:weights}
\]
Because $A>C$ and $B\geq D$, the decomposition
$P=(U-W)+(V-X)$ gives $P>0$.  Similarly, $A>B$ and $C\geq D$ give
$Q=(U-V)+(W-X)>0$.  If $R\geq0$, all three weights are
nonnegative and there is nothing to prove.  Hence assume $R<0$ and put
\[
 m=-R=-U+V+W-X>0.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:triangle} and \eqref{eq:weights} reduce positive
semidefiniteness to
\[
 m(uP+vQ)\leq rPQ.
\tag{7}\label{eq:key}
\]

First suppose $r\geq u+v$.  From \eqref{eq:PQR},
\[
 P-m=2(U-W)\geq0,\qquad Q-m=2(U-V)\geq0.
\]
Therefore
\[
 m(uP+vQ)\leq uPQ+vPQ\leq rPQ,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:key} in the dominant-coefficient cone.

It remains to consider the triangle regime $u\leq r<u+v$.  Here
$D=u+v-r$.  We claim the sharper pair of estimates
\[
 \frac mQ\leq\frac{r-v}{u},\qquad
 \frac mP\leq\frac{r-u}{v}.
\tag{8}\label{eq:ratios}
\]
Adding $u$ times the first and $v$ times the second gives exactly
\[
 m\left(\frac uQ+\frac vP\right)\leq(r-v)+(r-u)<r,
\]
where the last inequality is $r<u+v$.  Multiplication by $PQ$ proves
\eqref{eq:key}.

We prove \eqref{eq:ratios}.  The first inequality is equivalent, after
substituting \eqref{eq:PQR}, to
\[
 (r+u-v)(U-V)\geq(u+v-r)(W-X),
\tag{9}\label{eq:ratio1}
\]
and the second to
\[
 (r-u+v)(U-W)\geq(u+v-r)(V-X).
\tag{10}\label{eq:ratio2}
\]
For $a,h>0$ set
\[
 g(a,h)=a\big((a+h)^q-a^q\big).
\]
Clearly $g$ is increasing in $h$.  It is also increasing in $a$, because
with $t=h/a$,
\[
 \frac{\partial g}{\partial a}
 =a^q\big((1+q)((1+t)^q-1)-qt(1+t)^{q-1}\big)
 =a^q\big((1+t)^{q-1}(1+q+t)-(1+q)\big)>0.
\]
Indeed $F(t)=(1+t)^{q-1}(1+q+t)$ satisfies
$F(0)=1+q$ and $F'(t)=q(1+t)^{q-2}(q+t)>0$.

Now \eqref{eq:ratio1} is
\[
 g(B,2v)\geq g(D,2(r-u)),
\]
which follows from $B\geq D$ and $v\geq r-u$.  Likewise
\eqref{eq:ratio2} is
\[
 g(C,2u)\geq g(D,2(r-v)),
\]
which follows from $C\geq D$ and $u\geq r-v$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:ratios}, hence \eqref{eq:key}, and therefore the Hessian is
negative semidefinite throughout $(0,\infty)^3$.

Finally, $\Phi_3$ is continuous on the closed positive orthant.  Concavity on
the open orthant extends to the boundary by approximation, completing the
proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:hanner}
For $2<p<3$ and arbitrary real $f_1,f_2,f_3\in L_p$,
\[
 \E\left\|\eps_1f_1+\eps_2f_2+\eps_3f_3\right\|_p^p
 \leq
 \E\left|\eps_1\|f_1\|_p+\eps_2\|f_2\|_p+\eps_3\|f_3\|_p\right|^p.
\]
Thus the three-dimensional Rademacher subspace has $p$-concavity constant
one.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is the $n=3$ instance of Schechtman's equivalence between concavity of
\eqref{eq:phi} and \eqref{eq:hanner}; see~\cite{schechtman,jenkins}.
\end{proof}

\section{Eight focused upgrade attempts}

After the live problem was identified, eight materially different or
deepening routes were pursued.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact-status audit.}  Zhang's stronger matrix conjecture is
refuted in the source itself.  Jenkins--Tkocz confirms that the scalar real
problem remains open precisely for $2<p<3$.

\item \textbf{Hessian/Laplacian reduction.}  Formula \eqref{eq:hessian}
turns the sign problem into positive semidefiniteness of a signed graph
Laplacian.  This is the successful structural reduction used above.

\item \textbf{Counterexample search.}  Exact sign enumeration and
eigenvalue tests over broad logarithmic coefficient samples for
$3\leq n\leq9$ found no contradiction, while confirming that individual
edge weights can be negative.

\item \textbf{Bernstein integral representation.}  Representing $|S|^q$
by a Bernstein integral does not prove positivity pointwise: sampled
integrand Laplacians can be indefinite.  Integration must exploit
cancellation.

\item \textbf{Dominant three-variable cone.}  The effective-conductance
criterion and the simple inequalities $m\leq P,Q$ prove the case
$r\geq u+v$ directly.

\item \textbf{Triangle three-variable cone.}  The sharp ratio bounds
\eqref{eq:ratios}, proved by monotonicity of $g(a,h)$, complete the new
$n=3$ theorem.

\item \textbf{Interpolation from $p=2$ and $p=3$.}  The Hessian depends on
both the power $q=p-2$ and the nonlinear coordinates $x_k^{1/p}$; neither
matrix convexity in $p$ nor a suitable complex interpolation family was
found.  Endpoint positivity alone does not control the interior signed
edges.

\item \textbf{Extension by triangle decomposition.}  A tempting upgrade is
to decompose the $n$-vertex Laplacian into positive three-vertex Laplacians.
This fails termwise: after conditioning on the other signs, a three-vertex
sub-Laplacian may have all three edge weights negative.  A genuinely global
grouping remains necessary for $n\geq4$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verification, novelty, and limitations}

The formal proof uses only the cited Hessian identity, the elementary
weighted-triangle lemma, and explicit inequalities for $t^q$.  The included
script \texttt{code/check\_hessian.py} independently enumerates all eight
signs, checks \eqref{eq:weights}, and tests the Laplacian over 200,000 fixed
random samples spanning eight exponents and twelve logarithmic orders of
magnitude.  Its worst scaled minimum eigenvalue is
$-4.24\cdot10^{-16}$ (roundoff), and the maximum relative formula discrepancy
is $1.44\cdot10^{-13}$.  This computation is a contradiction check, not part
of the proof.

A bounded novelty search covered the run's four cheap indexes, the source
paper, Schechtman's cited problem, Jenkins--Tkocz and its references, and
focused arXiv/web queries combining ``Rademacher'', ``$p$-concavity'',
``$2<p<3$'', ``three variables'', and Hanner's inequality.  No prior
three-variable statement or proof was found.  The search is not exhaustive,
so novelty confidence is moderate rather than definitive.

The result does not settle arbitrary $n$.  Human review should prioritize:
the sign claims $P,Q\geq0$; the algebra converting \eqref{eq:ratios} to
\eqref{eq:ratio1}--\eqref{eq:ratio2}; monotonicity of $g$; and the precise use
of Schechtman's equivalence in Corollary~\ref{cor:hanner}.
