\section{Source question}
The source is M.~Goffeng and B.~Mesland, ``Spectral triples and finite
summability on Cuntz--Krieger algebras,'' arXiv:1401.2123, published in
\emph{Documenta Mathematica} 20 (2015), 89--170 \cite{GM}.  For
$x\in K^i(A)$ they define $\sd(x)$ to be the infimum of $p>0$ for which $x$
has a $p$-summable bounded Fredholm-module representative, and
\[
 \sd_i(A)=\sup\{\sd(x):x\in K^i(A)\}.
\]
On source PDF page~6 they ask whether this is a homotopy invariant.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

We answer this negatively already in odd degree, with homotopy-equivalent
separable C$^*$-algebras whose degrees are $\infty$ and $0$.

\section{Proof intuition}
The source paper supplies both extremes needed for a counterexample: a
$c_0$-sum of growing odd spheres carrying a class of infinite degree, and
Cuntz--Krieger algebras whose every odd class has degree zero.  The missing
ingredient is to make those algebras homotopy equivalent without losing the
analytic contrast.

The mapping cylinder does exactly this.  Given $f:\cA\to\cB$, its mapping
cylinder $M_f$ is homotopy equivalent to $\cA$, but evaluation at the free
endpoint is a surjection $M_f\twoheadrightarrow\cB$.  Summability pulls back
along surjections: the inverse image of a dense smooth subalgebra is dense.
We choose $\cB$ to be a $c_0$-sum of one rapidly summable Cuntz--Krieger
algebra and choose $f$ to induce an isomorphism on odd K-homology.  Then every
class on $M_f$ comes from $\cB$ and has degree zero, although $M_f$ is
homotopy equivalent to the infinite-degree sphere sum.

\section{Two elementary lemmas}
We use the source convention for $p$-summable bounded Fredholm modules.  All
cycles used below may be chosen normalized, with $F=F^*$ and $F^2=1$, so only
the commutator condition is visible.

\begin{lemma}[Surjective pullback]\label{lem:surj}
Let $q:C\twoheadrightarrow D$ be a surjective $*$-homomorphism.  If
$y\in K^i(D)$ has a $p$-summable representative, then $q^*y\in K^i(C)$ has a
$p$-summable representative.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $(\pi,H,F)$ represent $y$, and let $D_p\subseteq D$ be a dense
$*$-subalgebra on which the Schatten conditions hold.  The pulled-back cycle
is $(\pi q,H,F)$, and the same conditions hold on $q^{-1}(D_p)$.

It remains only to check density.  Given $c\in C$, choose $d_n\in D_p$ with
$d_n\to q(c)$.  Since a surjective bounded linear map between Banach spaces
is open, there are $u_n\in C$ with
\[
 q(u_n)=d_n-q(c),\qquad
 \|u_n\|\le 2\|d_n-q(c)\|.
\]
Then $c+u_n\to c$ and $q(c+u_n)=d_n\in D_p$.  Thus $q^{-1}(D_p)$ is dense.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A $c_0$-sum of degree-zero targets]\label{lem:c0}
Let $D$ be a C$^*$-algebra such that every odd K-homology class has a
normalized $p$-summable representative for every $p>0$.  Then
\[
 \cB:=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}D
\]
has $\sd_1(\cB)=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By countable additivity of KK-theory in the first variable,
$K^1(\cB)\cong\prod_jK^1(D)$.  Fix $y=(y_j)$ and $p>0$.  Choose a normalized
$p$-summable cycle $(\pi_j,H_j,F_j)$ for each $y_j$, and take their Hilbert
direct sum.  For $b=(b_j)\in\cB$,
\[
 [F,\pi(b)]=\bigoplus_j[F_j,\pi_j(b_j)].
\]
Every block is compact and its norm is at most $2\|b_j\|\to0$, so this direct
sum is compact.  The algebraic direct sum of the coordinate smooth
subalgebras is a dense $*$-subalgebra of $\cB$; on it the commutator is a
finite direct sum of Schatten-$p$ operators.  The defects vanish because the
cycles are normalized.  Thus $y$ is $p$-summable.  Since $p>0$ and $y$ were
arbitrary, $\sd_1(\cB)=0$.
\end{proof}

\section{The Cuntz--Krieger target and the KK-equivalences}
Consider the $0$--$1$ matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q}
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Its directed graph is strongly connected and is not a permutation graph, so
$D:=O_Q$ is a unital simple purely infinite Cuntz--Krieger algebra
\cite{CK}.  Moreover,
\[
 I-Q^{\mathsf T}
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&-1\\-1&0&0\\-1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The relations given by the last two columns kill the first coordinate and
identify the third generator with the negative of the second.  Thus the
cokernel is $\mathbb Z$.  The kernel is generated by $(0,1,-1)$, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Kgroups}
 K_0(D)=\operatorname{coker}(I-Q^{\mathsf T})\cong\mathbb Z,
 \qquad
 K_1(D)=\ker(I-Q^{\mathsf T})\cong\mathbb Z.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the Smith invariants are $(1,1,0)$.

Goffeng--Mesland's Theorem~7 says that $\sd_1(O_Q)=0$ and, more precisely,
constructs normalized $p$-summable analytic K-cycles for every $p>0$ and
every odd class \cite{GM}.  Thus $D$ satisfies Lemma~\ref{lem:c0}.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/source_extremes_crop.png}
\end{center}

Put $A_j=C(S^{2j-1})$.  Both $A_j$ and $D$ belong to the UCT class and have
free graded K-groups $(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z)$.  The UCT therefore lifts any
graded K-theory isomorphism to an invertible element
\[
 \kappa_j\in KK(A_j,D) \cite{RS}.
\]
Kirchberg's homomorphism realization theorem, in the form of
Dadarlat's Theorem~2.3(i), applies because $A_j$ is separable, nuclear, exact,
and unital and $D$ is separable, unital, and properly infinite.  It gives a
nuclear $*$-homomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fj}
 f_j:A_j\longrightarrow D,
 \qquad KK(f_j)=\kappa_j \cite{Dadarlat}.
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/kirchberg_realization_crop.png}
\end{center}

Now set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ABf}
 \cA=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}A_j,
 \qquad
 \cB=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}D,
 \qquad
 f=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}f_j:\cA\to\cB.
\end{equation}
The map is well-defined because each $f_j$ is contractive.  Countable
additivity gives
\[
 K^1(\cB)\cong\prod_jK^1(D),
 \qquad
 K^1(\cA)\cong\prod_jK^1(A_j),
\]
and $f^*$ is the product of the isomorphisms $f_j^*$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fstar}
 f^*:K^1(\cB)\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}K^1(\cA).
\end{equation}

\section{The counterexample}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The odd degree of summability is not invariant under C$^*$-homotopy
equivalence.  More precisely, there are separable homotopy-equivalent
C$^*$-algebras $\cA$ and $M_f$ such that
\[
 \sd_1(\cA)=\infty,
 \qquad
 \sd_1(M_f)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use $\cA,\cB,f$ from \eqref{eq:ABf}, and form the mapping cylinder
\[
 M_f=\{(a,h)\in\cA\oplus C([0,1],\cB):h(0)=f(a)\}.
\]
Let
\[
 q:M_f\to\cA,\quad q(a,h)=a,
 \qquad
 s:\cA\to M_f,\quad s(a)=(a,\text{constant path }f(a)).
\]
Then $qs=\operatorname{id}_{\cA}$.  For $0\le t\le1$, define
\[
 H_t(a,h)=(a,h_t),\qquad h_t(r)=h(tr).
\]
Each $H_t$ is a $*$-homomorphism, the path is point-norm continuous,
$H_0=sq$, and $H_1=\operatorname{id}_{M_f}$.  Thus $q$ is a homotopy
equivalence with inverse $s$.

Let
\[
 e:M_f\longrightarrow\cB,\qquad e(a,h)=h(1).
\]
This is surjective: for $b\in\cB$, the element $(0,h)$ with $h(r)=rb$ maps
to $b$.  Also $es=f$.  Since $s^*:K^1(M_f)\to K^1(\cA)$ is an isomorphism,
\eqref{eq:fstar} implies that $e^*:K^1(\cB)\to K^1(M_f)$ is surjective.
Indeed, for $z\in K^1(M_f)$ choose $y\in K^1(\cB)$ with
$f^*y=s^*z$; then
\[
 s^*(e^*y)=(es)^*y=f^*y=s^*z,
\]
and injectivity of $s^*$ gives $e^*y=z$.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:c0}, every $y\in K^1(\cB)$ is $p$-summable for every
$p>0$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:surj}, applied to the surjection $e$, shows that every
$z\in K^1(M_f)$ is $p$-summable for every $p>0$.  Hence
$\sd_1(M_f)=0$.

On the other hand, $\cA$ is exactly the $c_0$-sum used in Goffeng--Mesland's
Lemma~6.  The product class whose $j$th coordinate is the fundamental class
of $S^{2j-1}$ has infinite degree of summability \cite{GM}.  Therefore
$\sd_1(\cA)=\infty$.  Since $M_f$ and $\cA$ are homotopy equivalent, this is
the required counterexample.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, verification, and novelty}
The theorem gives a full negative answer in odd degree; one parity is enough
to disprove homotopy invariance.  It does not assert an even counterexample
and does not solve the source's separate broad problem of classifying all
obstructions to finite summability.

The matrix calculation was checked both by hand and by the packet's exact
integer verifier.  The proof was audited for nonunital $c_0$-sums,
Schatten exponents $0<p<1$, compactness of infinite block diagonals,
contravariance of K-homology, and point-norm continuity of the cylinder
contraction.  A bounded exact-phrase and mapping-cylinder literature search
found later repetitions of the question but no prior solution.  Exhaustive
priority is not claimed.  Human review should concentrate on
\eqref{eq:fj}, Lemma~\ref{lem:c0}, and the equality $es=f$ in the final
K-homology diagram.
