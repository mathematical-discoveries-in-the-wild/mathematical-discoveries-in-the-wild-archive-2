\section*{Source Question and Status}

Kadets and Seliutin ask in Problem 4.6 of their preprint whether a free
filter generated by a shift-invariant statistical measure can equal the
intersection of a countable family of free ultrafilters
\cite[p.~15]{preprint}.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figures/open_question_crop.png}
\end{center}

The substantially rebuilt published article, now with Tryba, explicitly
answers several other preprint questions but retains this exact question as
Problem 4.2 \cite[Sec.~4.2]{published}.  This packet gives an affirmative
answer in ZFC.  More strongly, the countable family can be the full shift
orbit of one ultrafilter.  Status: \emph{candidate full solution, likely
valid}, subject to human review.

\section*{Stone--\v Cech Setup}

Put $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$ and $\N^*=\beta\N\setminus\N$.  Its points are the
free ultrafilters on $\N$.  For $A\subset\N$, write
\[
 A^*=\{p\in\N^*:A\in p\}.
\]
These sets are clopen, $(A\cup B)^*=A^*\cup B^*$, and they are disjoint
when $A$ and $B$ are disjoint.

Let $s(n)=n+1$.  The extension $\beta s$ restricts to a homeomorphism
$T:\N^*\to\N^*$.  Indeed, the extension of a predecessor map is its
inverse on $\N^*$: the two compositions differ from the identity on at
most one natural number, and maps which agree off a finite set have the
same extension on free ultrafilters.  With the convention of the source,
\begin{equation}
 T(p)=p+1,\qquad T(A^*)=(A+1)^*.
 \label{eq:shift}
\end{equation}

\section*{Main Result}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There are a shift-invariant statistical measure $\mu$ on $\N$ and a
countably infinite family of pairwise distinct free ultrafilters
$\{\U+n:n\in\mathbb Z\}$ such that
\[
 \F_\mu=\{A\subset\N:\mu(A)=1\}
       =\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb Z}(\U+n).
\]
Consequently, preprint Problem 4.6 and published Problem 4.2 have an
affirmative answer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a nonempty minimal closed $T$-invariant subset $K$ of $\N^*$.
Such a set exists: start with the closure of any two-sided orbit and apply
Zorn's lemma to the nonempty closed invariant subsets; intersections of
descending chains remain nonempty by compactness.  Fix $\U\in K$.
Minimality gives
\begin{equation}
 K=\overline{\{T^n\U:n\in\mathbb Z\}}.
 \label{eq:denseorbit}
\end{equation}

For $N\geq1$, let
\[
 \nu_N=\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\delta_{T^j\U}.
\]
The regular Borel probability measures on $K$ form a weak-* compact subset
of $C(K)^*$, so $(\nu_N)$ has a weak-* convergent subnet; call its limit
$\nu$.  For every $f\in C(K)$,
\[
 \int f\!\circ T\,d\nu_N-\int f\,d\nu_N
 =\frac{f(T^N\U)-f(\U)}{N}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus $\nu$ is $T$-invariant.

Its support is a nonempty closed $T$-invariant subset of $K$.  Nonemptiness
does not require metrizability: if every point had a null open
neighborhood, compactness would give a finite null cover of $K$, contrary
to $\nu(K)=1$.  Minimality therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{supp}\nu=K.
 \label{eq:fullsupport}
\end{equation}

Define, for every $A\subset\N$,
\begin{equation}
 \mu(A)=\nu(A^*\cap K).
 \label{eq:mu}
\end{equation}
The clopen identities above show finite additivity and nonnegativity, and
$\mu(\N)=1$.  If $A$ is finite then $A^*=\varnothing$, so in particular
$\mu(\{k\})=0$ for every $k$.  Hence $\mu$ is a statistical measure.
Equation \eqref{eq:shift}, invariance of $K$, and invariance of $\nu$ give
\[
 \mu(A+1)=\nu\bigl(T(A^*\cap K)\bigr)=\nu(A^*\cap K)=\mu(A),
\]
so it is shift-invariant.

Full support is exactly what identifies its measure-one filter.  Namely,
\begin{equation}
 \mu(A)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K\subset A^*.
 \label{eq:one}
\end{equation}
The forward implication holds because, if $K\setminus A^*$ were nonempty,
it would be a nonempty relatively open subset of the full-support space
$K$ and hence would have positive $\nu$-measure.  The reverse implication
is immediate.  Since $A^*$ is closed, \eqref{eq:denseorbit} and
\eqref{eq:one} now give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A\in\F_\mu
 &\Longleftrightarrow K\subset A^*\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow T^n\U\in A^*\quad(n\in\mathbb Z)\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow A\in\U+n\quad(n\in\mathbb Z).
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves the asserted equality.  Every $\U+n$ is free, and their
intersection contains the Fr\'echet filter, so $\F_\mu$ is free.

It remains only to check that the displayed family has no repetitions, as
the source uses ``family'' to mean a set.  Suppose $T^m\U=\U$ for some
$m\geq1$.  Let
\[
 E=\bigcup_{k\geq0}\{2km+1,\ldots,(2k+1)m\}.
\]
Then $E-m:=\{r:r+m\in E\}=\N\setminus E$.  But $T^m\U=\U$ would imply
$E\in\U$ if and only if $E-m\in\U$, contradicting the ultrafilter
dichotomy.  Thus the shifts are pairwise distinct.
\end{proof}

\section*{Why the Construction Works}

A naive positive weighted sum of the orbit ultrafilters has exactly the
desired measure-one filter, but no positive probability weights on an
infinite orbit are translation invariant.  Passing from the orbit to its
compact closure repairs invariance; passing to a minimal orbit closure
forces every nonempty relative open set to have positive measure.  That
full-support property preserves the measure-one filter exactly, so no
ultrafilters are lost in the compactification step.

The same proof gives a useful criterion: whenever the orbit closure of a
free ultrafilter carries a full-support invariant probability, its
two-sided shift intersection is generated by a shift-invariant statistical
measure.  Minimal points guarantee the criterion in ZFC.

\section*{Scope, Novelty Search, and Verification}

This is an existence result, exactly as the word ``May'' in the source
asks.  It does not assert that every shift-invariant statistical-measure
filter has a countable representation; the source already records examples
showing otherwise.

Cheap run indexes contained no answer.  A bounded search through
17 August 2026 used the arXiv id, exact question, published title and DOI,
and combinations of \emph{shift-invariant statistical measure},
\emph{countable ultrafilter intersection}, \emph{minimal dynamics}, and
$\N^*$.  It found the source and the 2022 article retaining the problem,
but no later answer.  This is not a priority claim.

The proof was audited for the source's shift orientation, finite
additivity, vanishing on finite sets, weak-* compactness without
metrizability, full support, freeness, and distinctness of the orbit
ultrafilters.  No numerical verification is relevant.

\section*{Human Review Recommendation}

Check equation \eqref{eq:shift}, the existence of the invariant weak-* subnet,
and the equivalence \eqref{eq:one}.  Those are the three structural passage
points; the remaining argument is direct Stone duality.
