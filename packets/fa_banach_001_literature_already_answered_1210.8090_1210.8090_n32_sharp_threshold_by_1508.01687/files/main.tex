\paragraph{Status.}
The sharpness conjecture in arXiv:1210.8090 is answered affirmatively by the
general two-step lower bound of Martini--Müller, arXiv:1508.01687,
Theorem~3.  The later paper also explicitly lists the $N_{3,2}$ theorem among
the earlier results that this lower bound makes sharp.

Let $L$ be the standard homogeneous sub-Laplacian on the free two-step group
$N_{3,2}$.  Its topological and homogeneous dimensions are respectively
$d=6$ and $Q=9$.  Write $\varsigma(L)$ for the infimum of all $s\geq0$ for
which a scale-invariant local $W_2^s$ norm controls the weak-$(1,1)$ norm of
$F(L)$.  The later paper also defines $\varsigma_-(L)$ using the formally
weaker requirement of $L^p$ boundedness for all $1<p<\infty$, controlled by
a local $W_\infty^s$ norm; standard interpolation gives
$\varsigma_-(L)\leq\varsigma(L)$.

\begin{source}[Martini--Müller, arXiv:1210.8090, Theorem 1]
If
\[
 \sup_{t>0}\|\eta F(t\,\cdot)\|_{W_2^s}<\infty
 \qquad\text{for some }s>3,
\]
then $F(L)$ is of weak type $(1,1)$ and bounded on every $L^p(N_{3,2})$,
$1<p<\infty$.  The authors conjecture that the condition $s>d/2=3$ is
sharp.  Equivalently, their theorem gives $\varsigma(L)\leq3$.
\end{source}

\begin{answer}[Martini--Müller, arXiv:1508.01687, Theorem 3]
For every homogeneous sub-Laplacian $\mathcal L$ on a two-step stratified
group of topological dimension $d$ and homogeneous dimension $Q$,
\[
 \frac d2\leq\varsigma_-(\mathcal L)
 \leq\varsigma(\mathcal L)
 \leq\varsigma_+(\mathcal L)<\frac Q2.
\]
\end{answer}

Applying the answering theorem with $d=6$ yields
\[
 3\leq\varsigma_-(L)\leq\varsigma(L).
\]
Together with the source upper bound, this proves the exact characterization
\[
 \boxed{\varsigma_-(L)=\varsigma(L)=3.}
\]
Thus no multiplier theorem of the indicated uniform type can hold below
$s=3$, even after dropping weak type $(1,1)$ and replacing the $W_2^s$
hypothesis by the stronger local $W_\infty^s$ hypothesis.

\paragraph{Scope caveat.}
The source's broader question---whether topological dimension always replaces
homogeneous dimension for arbitrary stratified groups---is not settled here.
The answering paper covers all two-step groups and explicitly leaves the
higher-step extension as an open problem requiring new ideas.
