\section*{Original problem}

Let $s$ be the Fr\'echet space of rapidly decreasing sequences and let
$L(s',s)$ be the Fr\'echet $^*$-algebra of continuous operators from its strong
dual $s'$ into $s$.  In \emph{On the algebra of smooth operators}, Cia\'s asks
the following on page 16 of the local source compilation
\cite[Open Problem~4.13]{source}:
\begin{quote}
Is every closed commutative $^*$-subalgebra of $L(s',s)$ isomorphic to some
closed $^*$-subalgebra of $s$ with pointwise multiplication?
\end{quote}
The source gives an equivalent K\"othe-algebra formulation using an
$s$-complete orthonormal system $(e_n)$ and
$\lambda^\infty(|e_n|_q)$.

\section*{Explicit later answer}

The separate later paper \emph{Commutative subalgebras of the algebra of
smooth operators} says immediately before its decisive theorem that the next
result ``solves in negative'' Open Problem~4.13 of the 2013 paper
\cite[p.~14]{answer}.  Its Theorem~6.10 states:
\begin{quote}
There is a closed commutative $^*$-subalgebra of $L(s',s)$ which is not
isomorphic to any closed $^*$-subalgebra of $s$.
\end{quote}
This is a direct counterexample to the universal assertion in the original
problem, not merely an implication identified after the fact.

The later paper also gives the structural boundary.  Theorem~6.2 proves that
an infinite-dimensional closed commutative $^*$-subalgebra $E\subset L(s',s)$
is isomorphic to a closed $^*$-subalgebra of $s$ exactly when $E$ has Vogt's
property $(\Omega)$ (equivalently, when it is isomorphic as a Fr\'echet space
to a complemented subspace of $s$).  In the notation of its canonical basis,
this is further equivalent to an explicit polynomial domination condition on
the associated weights.  Theorem~6.10 then constructs a system whose weights
violate that condition.

\section*{Scope and provenance}

The 2013 yes/no problem is fully answered: the answer is \emph{no}.  The 2015
paper supplies both a characterization of the positive cases and an explicit
negative example.  The supporting author is the author of the source paper,
cites the source as reference~[3], and explicitly identifies Open
Problem~4.13 as the problem being solved.  This therefore belongs in the
literature-already-answered category and is not a new result of this run.
