The essential-supremum problem for arbitrary measurable functions remains

\section{Source question}

Kumar, Rastogi, and Tripathi ask whether the Douglas--Rudin theorem extends
to measurable unimodular functions on the distinguished boundary $\T^d$ of
the polydisc~\cite{KRT}.  McDonald proved the uniform theorem for
\emph{continuous} unimodular functions, with rational inner factors
\cite{McDonald}.  The source observes that a measurable scalar theorem would
also feed into its operator-valued method.  Its concluding formulation (PDF
page~10) is reproduced below.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Write $m_d$ for normalized Haar measure on $\T^d$.  If $\Phi,\Psi$ are
polydisc inner functions, their boundary quotient means
$\Phi\Psi^{-1}=\Phi\overline\Psi$ almost everywhere.

\section{Idea of the proofs}

There are two different ways to retain a complete theorem without claiming
the open uniform statement.

First, Haar regularity lets us replace a measurable phase, outside a set of
arbitrarily small measure, by a continuous phase.  McDonald's theorem then
gives a rational-inner quotient uniformly close to that continuous phase.
This proves density in every finite $L^r$ and, after choosing summable errors,
almost-everywhere convergence.

Second, suppose all measurable dependence is carried by finitely many analytic
characters $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^a$.  Apply the one-variable Douglas--Rudin theorem
to each one-variable phase and compose its inner factors with $z\mapsto z^a$.
Those compositions are polydisc inner, and multiplication assembles the
factors without any exceptional set.  This gives genuine essential-supremum
approximation for a broad class containing all coordinate-separable phases.

\section{Finite-$L^r$ density for every measurable phase}

Let $\Q_{\mathrm{rat}}(\T^d)$ denote the boundary quotients
$\Phi\overline\Psi$ with $\Phi,\Psi$ rational inner on $\D^d$.

\begin{theorem}[finite-norm Douglas--Rudin approximation]
\label{thm:Lr}
Let $d\geq1$, let $0<r<\infty$, and let
$f:\T^d\to\T$ be measurable.  For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is
$q\in\Q_{\mathrm{rat}}(\T^d)$ such that
\[
                 \|f-q\|_{L^r(m_d)}<\varepsilon.
\]
Moreover, there is a sequence $(q_n)\subset\Q_{\mathrm{rat}}(\T^d)$
such that $q_n(\zeta)\to f(\zeta)$ for almost every $\zeta\in\T^d$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix small $\eta,\delta>0$.  Choose a finite $\eta$-net
$\{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N\}\subset\T$ and a measurable partition
$\T^d=E_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup E_N$ such that the simple phase
$s=\sum_j\lambda_j\mathbf1_{E_j}$ satisfies $|f-s|<\eta$ everywhere after
changing representatives on a null set.

By inner regularity, choose compact $K_j\subset E_j$ with
\[
       m_d\!\left(\T^d\setminus\bigcup_j K_j\right)<\delta.
\]
The finitely many disjoint compact sets have pairwise disjoint open
neighborhoods $U_j$.  Choose continuous $0\leq\rho_j\leq1$ supported in
$U_j$ and equal to $1$ on $K_j$.  Writing $\lambda_j=e^{i\theta_j}$, define
\[
                c(\zeta)=\exp\!\left(i\sum_j\theta_j\rho_j(\zeta)\right).
\]
Then $c$ is continuous and unimodular, and $c=\lambda_j$ on $K_j$.

McDonald's polydisc theorem gives $q\in\Q_{\mathrm{rat}}(\T^d)$ with
$\|c-q\|_\infty<\eta$.  On $K=\bigcup_jK_j$ we have
$|f-q|<2\eta$, while everywhere $|f-q|\leq2$.  Hence
\[
       \int_{\T^d}|f-q|^r\,dm_d
       \leq (2\eta)^r+2^r\delta.
\]
Taking $\eta$ and $\delta$ sufficiently small proves density for every finite
$r$, including $0<r<1$.

For the final assertion, apply the proved case $r=1$ with
$\|f-q_n\|_1<2^{-n}$.  Tonelli's theorem gives
$\sum_n|f-q_n|<\infty$ almost everywhere, and therefore $q_n\to f$ almost
everywhere.
\end{proof}

\section{A uniform measurable subclass}

For $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_d)\in\mathbb N_0^d$, put
$z^a=z_1^{a_1}\cdots z_d^{a_d}$ and $|a|=\sum_\ell a_\ell$.

\begin{theorem}[analytic-character pullbacks]
\label{thm:pullback}
Let $a_1,\ldots,a_N\in\mathbb N_0^d\setminus\{0\}$ and let
$g_1,\ldots,g_N:\T\to\T$ be arbitrary measurable functions.  Define
\[
               f(\zeta)=\prod_{j=1}^N g_j(\zeta^{a_j})
               \qquad(\zeta\in\T^d).
\]
For every $\varepsilon>0$ there are inner functions $\Phi,\Psi$ on $\D^d$
such that
\[
        \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\zeta\in\T^d}
        |f(\zeta)-\Phi(\zeta)\overline{\Psi(\zeta)}|<\varepsilon.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each $j$, the one-variable Douglas--Rudin theorem~\cite{DouglasRudin}
provides inner $\varphi_j,\psi_j$ on $\D$ such that
\[
 \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{w\in\T}
 |g_j(w)-\varphi_j(w)\overline{\psi_j(w)}|<\varepsilon/N.
\]
The character $\chi_{a_j}(\zeta)=\zeta^{a_j}$ pushes Haar measure on
$\T^d$ forward to Haar measure on $\T$.  Consequently, the exceptional null
set in this estimate has null preimage under $\chi_{a_j}$.

The functions $\varphi_j(z^{a_j})$ and $\psi_j(z^{a_j})$ are inner on
$\D^d$.  Indeed they are bounded and holomorphic; for almost every $\zeta$,
radial approach gives
\[
 \varphi_j((r\zeta)^{a_j})
 =\varphi_j(r^{|a_j|}\zeta^{a_j})
 \longrightarrow \varphi_j^*(\zeta^{a_j}),
\]
whose modulus is one, and similarly for $\psi_j$.
Thus
\[
   \Phi(z)=\prod_j\varphi_j(z^{a_j}),\qquad
   \Psi(z)=\prod_j\psi_j(z^{a_j})
\]
are polydisc inner.  A telescoping product estimate for unit-modulus factors
now gives
\[
 \|f-\Phi\overline\Psi\|_\infty
 \leq\sum_{j=1}^N
 \|g_j-(\varphi_j\overline{\psi_j})\|_\infty<\varepsilon.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
Every coordinate-separable measurable phase
$f(\zeta)=\prod_{k=1}^d g_k(\zeta_k)$ satisfies the full uniform conclusion.
The same holds for every finite product of measurable phases of analytic
monomial characters.
\end{corollary}

\section{Boundary of the result}

Uniform phase quantization reduces the full problem to the signs
$2\mathbf1_E-1$ for arbitrary measurable $E\subset\T^d$.  Theorem
\ref{thm:Lr} cannot remove a set of small positive measure because the target
norm is essential supremum.  Theorem~\ref{thm:pullback} depends on exact finite
multiplicative separation through analytic characters, which a general set
$E$ need not possess.  Eight distinct attempts at closing this gap---simple
phase assembly, Hankel and Toeplitz obstructions, topology, slicing,
operator-valued iteration, exceptional-set removal, and closure invariants---
are documented in the associated attempt note; none settles the arbitrary
sign.

A bounded novelty search on 11 August 2026 used the exact arXiv identifier and
title, measurable-polydisc Douglas--Rudin phrases, rational-inner quotient
density in finite $L^r$, and monomial-pullback phrases.  It found the source
and McDonald's continuous result, but no later primary-source resolution of
the measurable uniform question and no exact match for the two theorems above.
Novelty confidence is moderate rather than definitive.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Verify the citation-level formulation of McDonald's theorem and the Haar
pushforward step in Theorem~\ref{thm:pullback}.  The proofs otherwise use only
measure regularity, Urysohn separation, the classical one-variable theorem,
and a telescoping inequality.
