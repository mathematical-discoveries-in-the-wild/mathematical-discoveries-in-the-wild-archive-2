\section*{Source question and intended conventions}

Section 6 of P. Portal, \emph{Using wave packet decompositions to construct
function spaces}, arXiv:2602.03952, considers
\[
 H=-L=-\Delta+V\quad\hbox{on }\R^d,\qquad d>2,
\]
where \(V\geq0\) belongs to \(RH_q\) for some \(q>d/2\). It asks for the
class of such potentials for which the kernel hypothesis (6.3) holds and
suggests that the answer should be all of \(RH_q\). We prove that suggestion.

We use the standard critical radius
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
 \rho(x)=\sup\left\{r>0:r^{2-d}\int_{B(x,r)}V\leq1\right\}.
\end{equation}
The source prints the word min in this definition, but its examples
\(V=1\) and \(V(x)=|x|^2\), as well as the cited literature, force the
standard supremum in \eqref{eq:rho}. We also write the source multiplier as
\[
 T_\sigma=\psi(\sigma\sqrt H).
\]
This is the operator meant by its displayed wave formula and its finite-speed
scaling, although it is denoted there by \(\psi(\sigma^2L)\).

The source takes \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty((0,\infty))\) and defines the even
Schwartz function \(\psi\) by
\(\widehat\psi(\xi)=\varphi(|\xi|)\). Put
\(R_\psi=\sup\{|s|:s\in\operatorname{supp}\widehat\psi\}\).

\section*{Full result}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every \(A>0\), \(T_\sigma\) has an integral kernel \(K_\sigma\) satisfying,
for almost every \(x,y\in\R^d\) and every \(\sigma>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong}
 |K_\sigma(x,y)|\leq C_A\sigma^{-d}
 \1_{\{|x-y|\leq R_\psi\sigma\}}
 \left(1+\frac{\sigma}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-A}
 \left(1+\frac{\sigma}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-A}.
\end{equation}
Consequently the kernel hypothesis (6.3) of arXiv:2602.03952 holds for every
\(V\in RH_q\), \(q>d/2\). In fact, the estimate holds at all scales, not only
when \(\sigma\geq\rho(x_j)\), and the boundedness of \(\rho\) is not needed for
this kernel conclusion.
\end{theorem}

The proof uses one standard input. If \(p_t(x,y)\) is the heat kernel of
\(e^{-tH}\), then for every \(N>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heat}
 0\leq p_t(x,y)\leq C_Nt^{-d/2}e^{-|x-y|^2/(ct)}
 \left(1+\frac{\sqrt t}{\rho(x)}+
              \frac{\sqrt t}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-N}.
\end{equation}
This is Theorem 4.10 of J. Dziubanski and J. Zienkiewicz,
\emph{\(H^p\) spaces associated with Schrodinger operators with potentials
from reverse Holder classes}, Colloq. Math. 98 (2003), 5--38, in their
notation \(m=1/\rho\). It is also restated as Proposition 29 in the included
2021 supporting paper.

\section*{Weighted resolvent columns}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:columns}
Given \(A>0\), there is an integer \(m\) such that the kernel \(a_\sigma\) of
\(A_\sigma=(I+\sigma^2H)^{-m}\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:column}
 \|a_\sigma(\cdot,y)\|_2
 \leq C_A\sigma^{-d/2}
 \left(1+\frac{\sigma}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-A}.
\end{equation}
The same estimate holds for the row \(a_\sigma(x,\cdot)\), with \(y\) replaced
by \(x\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(m\) so that
\(\gamma=2m-d/2>A\). Positivity, self-adjointness, and the semigroup formula
give
\begin{align*}
 \|a_\sigma(\cdot,y)\|_2^2
 &= (I+\sigma^2H)^{-2m}(y,y)\\
 &=\frac1{\Gamma(2m)}\int_0^\infty
     s^{2m-1}e^{-s}p_{s\sigma^2}(y,y)\,ds.
\end{align*}
Apply \eqref{eq:heat} on the diagonal with an exponent \(N>2\gamma\). If
\(a=\sigma/\rho(y)\), the right-hand side is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
 C\sigma^{-d}\int_0^\infty
 s^{\gamma-1}e^{-s}(1+a\sqrt s)^{-N}\,ds.
\end{equation}
For \(a\leq1\), the integral is bounded. For \(a>1\), split it at
\(a^{-2}\) and \(1\). The three pieces are respectively bounded by
\[
 C a^{-2\gamma},\qquad C a^{-2\gamma},\qquad C a^{-N}.
\]
Since \(\gamma>A\) and \(N>2\gamma\), \eqref{eq:J} is at most
\(C_A\sigma^{-d}(1+a)^{-2A}\). Taking square roots proves
\eqref{eq:column}. Symmetry gives the row estimate.
\end{proof}

\section*{Resolvent sandwich and finite propagation}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}]
Fix \(A>0\), choose \(m\) from Lemma \ref{lem:columns}, and define
\[
 b(u)=(1+u^2)^{2m}\psi(u),\qquad B_\sigma=b(\sigma\sqrt H).
\]
The function \(b\) is bounded because \(\psi\) is Schwartz. The spectral
theorem gives the exact factorization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sandwich}
 T_\sigma=A_\sigma B_\sigma A_\sigma,
 \qquad \|B_\sigma\|_{2\to2}\leq\|b\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{eq:sandwich} has the measurable kernel
\[
 K_\sigma(x,y)
 =\left\langle B_\sigma a_\sigma(\cdot,y),
                      a_\sigma(\cdot,x)\right\rangle_{L^2},
\]
up to the immaterial choice of complex conjugation convention. By
Cauchy--Schwarz, \eqref{eq:column}, and its row version,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:amplitude}
 |K_\sigma(x,y)|\leq C_A\sigma^{-d}
 \left(1+\frac{\sigma}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-A}
 \left(1+\frac{\sigma}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-A}.
\end{equation}

It remains only to recover the sharp spatial localization. Since \(\psi\)
and \(\widehat\psi\) are even,
\[
 T_\sigma=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_{-R_\psi}^{R_\psi}
 \widehat\psi(t)\cos(t\sigma\sqrt H)\,dt.
\]
The wave equation for \(H=-\Delta+V\) has propagation speed one; the
zeroth-order potential does not enlarge supports. Hence the kernel of
\(T_\sigma\) vanishes when \(|x-y|>R_\psi\sigma\). Combining this with
\eqref{eq:amplitude} proves \eqref{eq:strong}.

Finally, the standard critical-radius comparability theorem gives
\(\rho(y)\simeq\rho(x_j)\) whenever \(y\in Q_j\), because \(Q_j\) is a
critical cube. Choose any \(A>1\), and set
\[
 g(r)=C_A\1_{(0,R_\psi]}(r),\qquad h(s)=(1+s)^{-A}.
\]
Both functions belong to \(L^1((0,\infty))\). Dropping the \(x\)-weight in
\eqref{eq:strong} and replacing \(\rho(y)\) by the comparable
\(\rho(x_j)\) yields, simultaneously for all \(j,k\),
\[
 \left|\1_{Q_k}(x)K_\sigma(x,y)\1_{Q_j}(y)\right|
 \leq\sigma^{-d}g\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\sigma}\right)
 h\left(\frac{\sigma}{\rho(x_j)}\right).
\]
This is exactly (6.3), under the source's intended notation.
\end{proof}

\section*{Checks, strengthening, and novelty}

The direct heat-semigroup route is not enough by itself: a general Schwartz
wave multiplier need not be a Laplace transform. A one-sided resolvent
factorization still leaves a pointwise-kernel gap. The two-sided sandwich
\eqref{eq:sandwich} is what converts the known diagonal heat estimate into a
pointwise bound by pairing two \(L^2\) columns. Arbitrarily high resolvent
order gives arbitrarily high critical-radius decay.

The proof establishes more than requested: symmetric decay in both critical
radii, exact finite propagation, validity for every \(\sigma>0\), and no use
of the source's global assumption \(\rho\in L^\infty\) for the kernel estimate
itself. A bounded search on 13 August 2026 for the source title, arXiv id,
the named upcoming collaborators, and combinations of wave packets, reverse
Holder potentials, and critical radii found no posted follow-up or prior
statement of this resolution.
