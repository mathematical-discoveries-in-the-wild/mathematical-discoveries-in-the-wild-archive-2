## Exact criterion audit

- In a complete metric space, a non-relatively-compact subset is not totally
  bounded and therefore contains a uniformly separated sequence.
- Radius-`delta/3` bump supports around a `delta`-separated sequence are
  pairwise separated. The family is locally finite and at most one bump is
  nonzero at each point.
- The bump-mode sum is globally jointly continuous, bounded by one, and has
  the relatively dense exact period set `{tau: tau_1 in 2 pi Z}`.
- Evaluation at the separated point `x_m` isolates `exp(i m t_1)`.
- For a finite frequency set one may choose `m e_1` outside it. Large-cube
  means of all remaining nontrivial characters vanish, proving uniform error
  at least one.
- This establishes necessity for an arbitrary family member that is not
  relatively compact, not only for the unit ball of `ell_2`.

## Positive direction audit

- If `K=closure(B)` is compact, joint continuity on `{t_0} times K` implies
  continuity of `t -> F(t,.)|_K` in the `C(K;Y)` norm.
- For a continuous function of `x`, the supremum over `B` equals the supremum
  over its closure `K`; hence the source Bohr estimate is exactly the
  Banach-valued Bohr estimate.
- Banach-valued Bohr approximation produces coefficients in `C(K;Y)`.
- `K` is closed in the metric space `X`, and Dugundji extends every
  coefficient continuously from `K` to the locally convex space `Y`.
- Source Proposition 2.24(iv) supplies the reverse strong-to-Bohr implication
  when the parameter sets are bounded; relative compactness already implies
  boundedness.

## Convex-polyhedral audit

- The cone is the source's explicit domain generated by a basis with

  Theorem 2.36 to the target `C(K;Y)` and then uses Dugundji extension.
- In the negative proof, the dual coordinate `ell_1` maps the ray `s v_1` to

## Diagonal audit

- `exp(i(t_1-t_2)^2)` is bounded, continuous, and unchanged by every
  diagonal translation.
- Its restriction to `(s,0,...,0)` is `exp(i s^2)`.
- With `a_k=sqrt(2 pi k)` and `b_k=sqrt(2 pi k+pi)`, the input distance tends
  to zero while the function values differ by two, proving failure of uniform
  continuity.
- A uniform limit of finite trigonometric polynomials is uniformly
  continuous, so strong approximation is impossible.
