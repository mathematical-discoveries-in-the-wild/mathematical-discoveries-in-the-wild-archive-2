\begin{abstract}
Let $p\in(7/3,8/3)$ and $s=3p/2-2\in(3/2,2)$.  Knappmann,
Schumacher, Steenebruegge and von der Mosel constructed a global Hilbert
gradient flow for $\IntM$ projected to preserve the pointwise speed.  They
proved decay of the projected gradient and weak $H^s$ subconvergence, but left
full convergence open.  We prove that every one of their trajectories
converges strongly in $H^s$ to a projected critical knot.  The energy is real
analytic by a normalized-chord $L^2$ factorization.  At a critical knot the
Blatt--Reiter principal Fourier multiplier is an isomorphism modulo
translations and the differentiated remainder is compact, giving a
Lojasiewicz--Simon inequality.  A Palais--Smale argument supplies a strong
accumulation point; the gradient inequality then makes the tail have finite
length.
\end{abstract}

\section{The source question}

The source is J.~Knappmann, H.~Schumacher, D.~Steenebruegge and
H.~von der Mosel, \emph{A speed preserving Hilbert gradient flow for
generalized integral Menger curvature}, arXiv:2103.10408, later published in
\emph{Advances in Calculus of Variations} \textbf{16} (2023), 597--635
\cite{KSSM}.  Immediately before Corollary~1.4, the authors write that it is
not clear whether their solution curves converge to a projected critical
point as $t\to\infty$.

For $p\in(7/3,8/3)$ put
\[
 s=\frac32p-2\in\left(\frac32,2\right),\qquad
 \E(\gamma)=\IntM(\gamma).
\]
The source flow is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flow}
 \dot\gamma(t)=-\nabla_{\Sigma}\E(\gamma(t)),\qquad
 \Sigma(\gamma)=\log|\gamma'|,
\end{equation}
where the gradient is taken in $H^s$ and orthogonally projected onto
$\ker D\Sigma(\gamma)$.  The source proves global existence, preservation of
$|\gamma'|$, preservation of the barycenter and knot class, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-decay}
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\E(\gamma(t))
 =-\|\nabla_\Sigma\E(\gamma(t))\|_{H^s}^{2},\qquad
 \|\nabla_\Sigma\E(\gamma(t))\|_{H^s}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}

\section{Main theorem and proof intuition}

\begin{theorem}[Full convergence]\label{thm:main}
Let $p\in(7/3,8/3)$ and let
$\gamma_0\in H^s(\T;\R^n)$, $s=3p/2-2$, be a regular embedded closed
curve.  The global solution of \eqref{eq:flow} constructed in \cite{KSSM}
converges strongly in $H^s$ as $t\to\infty$ to a curve $\gamma_\infty$.
Moreover,
\[
 \nabla_\Sigma\E(\gamma_\infty)=0,
 \quad |\gamma_\infty'|=|\gamma_0'|,
 \quad \overline{\gamma_\infty}=\overline{\gamma_0},
 \quad [\gamma_\infty]=[\gamma_0].
\]
Thus $\gamma_\infty$ is a projected critical knot in the same fixed-speed
level and knot class as the initial curve.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{Proof intuition}

The flow already lives on the correct constraint manifold: its projection is
the intrinsic gradient of $\E$ on the level set where speed and barycenter are
fixed.  The source gives a global bounded trajectory whose gradient tends to
zero, but only weak accumulation.  The Blatt--Reiter decomposition of
$D\E$ has a coercive Fourier-multiplier part of order $2s$ and a genuinely
lower-order remainder.  It first upgrades every Palais--Smale sequence to a
strongly convergent one.  At the resulting smooth critical accumulation point,
the same decomposition says that the Hessian is an elliptic isomorphism plus a
compact operator.  Since the energy itself is analytic, the abstract
Lojasiewicz--Simon theorem applies.  Its gradient inequality bounds the length
of the remaining trajectory by a power of the remaining energy drop, so the
trajectory can no longer leave a neighborhood of the accumulation point and
must converge to it.

\section{The preserved constraint manifold}

Write $v=|\gamma_0'|>0$ and $b=\int_\T\gamma_0$.  Let
\[
 \M_{v,b}=\left\{\gamma\in H^s_{\rm ir}(\T;\R^n):
 |\gamma'|=v,\ \int_\T\gamma=b\right\}.
\]
The logarithmic-strain results in \cite[Section 5]{KSSM} show that this is a
smooth Hilbert submanifold.  Its tangent space is
\[
 T_\gamma\M_{v,b}=\left\{h\in H^s:
 \langle\gamma',h'\rangle=0,\ \int_\T h=0\right\}.
\]
Translation invariance gives mean zero for both the ambient and projected
gradients \cite[Lemma 3.2]{KSSM}.  Consequently the right side of
\eqref{eq:flow} is exactly $-\grad_{\M_{v,b}}\E$.  Thus projected critical
points are precisely critical points of $\E|_{\M_{v,b}}$.

There is no loss in proving the analytic and Fredholm assertions for constant
speed.  Indeed, with $\ell=\int_\T v$ and
\[
 \phi(u)=\ell^{-1}\int_0^u v(r)\dd r,
\]
composition with the one fixed Sobolev diffeomorphism $\phi$ maps the
constant-speed level onto the $v$-speed level.  Because $s>3/2$, composition
by $\phi$ and $\phi^{-1}$ is a bounded isomorphism of $H^s$.  Pulling back the
ambient inner product therefore gives an equivalent, time-independent Hilbert
inner product.  Analyticity, compactness, Fredholm index, Palais--Smale
compactness and strong convergence are invariant under this fixed linear
change of coordinates.  Below we write the constant-speed formulas; the
same conclusions hold on $\M_{v,b}$.

\section{Analyticity of the energy}

The next lemma is the first energy-specific ingredient not recorded in the
source literature.

\begin{lemma}[Analyticity]\label{lem:analytic}
The map
\[
 \E:H^s_{\rm ir}(\T;\R^n)\longrightarrow\R
\]
is real analytic.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We give the factorization because it also explains why the special Hilbert
case $q=2$ is decisive.  Let $D\subset(-1/2,0)\times(0,1/2)$ be the standard
Blatt--Reiter integration triangle.  For $a\ne0$ set
\[
 A_a\gamma(u)=\frac{\gamma(u+a)-\gamma(u)}a,
\]
with the continuous extension $A_0\gamma=\gamma'$.  Put
\[
 \dd\mu(u,v,w)=|v|^{2-p}|w|^{2-p}|v-w|^{-p}\dd u\dd v\dd w.
\]
The prototype fractional estimate in \cite[Lemma 4.4]{BR} says precisely that
\[
 B(\gamma)(u,v,w)=A_w\gamma(u)\wedge A_v\gamma(u)
\]
is a continuous quadratic map from $H^s$ to
$L^2(\T\times D,\mu;\bigwedge^2\R^n)$.  This also follows directly from
$A_w\gamma\wedge A_v\gamma=(A_w\gamma-A_v\gamma)\wedge A_v\gamma$
and the Gagliardo characterization of $H^{s-1}$, using
$2s=3p-4$.

For an embedded regular curve, every normalized chord is uniformly separated
from zero.  On a sufficiently small $H^s$ neighborhood of a fixed
$\gamma_*$ there is therefore a $c_*>0$ such that
\[
 |A_a\gamma(u)|\ge c_*
 \quad (u\in\T,\ |a|\le1/2).
\]
Define the bounded multiplier
\begin{align*}
 C(\gamma)(u,v,w)={}&
 |A_w\gamma(u)|^{-p/2}|A_v\gamma(u)|^{-p/2}
 |A_{v-w}\gamma(u+w)|^{-p/2}\\
 &\times |\gamma'(u)\gamma'(u+v)\gamma'(u+w)|^{1/2}.
\end{align*}
The maps $\gamma\mapsto A_a\gamma$ and $\gamma\mapsto\gamma'$ are linear.
Since $H^{s-1}$ is a Banach algebra and embeds into $C^0$, the analytic
Nemytskii maps $z\mapsto |z|^{-p/2}$ away from zero and
$z\mapsto |z|^{1/2}$ away from zero show that
\[
 C:H^s_{\rm ir}\longrightarrow L^\infty(\T\times D)
\]
is analytic locally at $\gamma_*$.  Multiplication
$L^\infty\times L^2(\mu)\to L^2(\mu)$ is continuous bilinear.  After the
change of variables used in \cite[Section 4]{BR}, the exact energy identity is
\[
 \E(\gamma)=6\,\|C(\gamma)B(\gamma)\|_{L^2(\mu)}^2.
\]
The right side is a composition of analytic maps.  Since $\gamma_*$ was
arbitrary, $\E$ is analytic on all of $H^s_{\rm ir}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Ellipticity and the constrained Hessian}

For a constant-speed curve, Blatt and Reiter split the first variation as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split}
 D\E(\gamma)h=12Q(\gamma,h)+12R(\gamma,h).
\end{equation}
Here $Q$ is independent of the base curve and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qsymbol}
 Q(f,h)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\rho_k
 \langle\widehat f_k,\widehat h_k\rangle,\qquad
 \rho_0=0,\qquad \rho_k=c_p|k|^{3p-4}+o(|k|^{3p-4}),
\end{equation}
with $c_p>0$ \cite[Proposition 4.1]{BR}.  As $3p-4=2s$, there are constants
$c,C>0$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coercive}
 c\|h\|_{H^s}^2\le Q(h,h)\le C\|h\|_{H^s}^2
\end{equation}
on every closed subspace transverse to the constants, in particular on the
mean-zero tangent spaces considered here.

\begin{lemma}[Compact differentiated remainder]\label{lem:compactR}
Let $\gamma$ be a smooth embedded constant-speed curve.  There is a
$\delta>0$ such that the bilinear form
\[
 (k,h)\longmapsto D_\gamma R(\gamma,h)[k]
\]
extends continuously to $H^s\times H^{s-\delta}$.  Consequently it induces
a compact operator $H^s\to H^{-s}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We spell out where the gain occurs.  Lemma~4.2 of \cite{BR} writes $R$ as a
finite sum of integrals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rstructure}
 \int_{\T\times D\times[0,1]^N}
 G\!\left(\frac{|\Delta_w\gamma|}{|w|},
           \frac{|\Delta_v\gamma|}{|v|},
           \frac{|\Delta_{v-w}\gamma|}{|v-w|}\right)
 \Gamma_\gamma\otimes h'(u+av+bw),
\end{equation}
where $G$ is analytic on $(0,\infty)^3$, the remaining factors are shifted
tangents, and $\Gamma_\gamma$ contains at least two tangent differences with
the singular weight
$|v|^{2-p}|w|^{2-p}|v-w|^{-p}$.  The normalized chords remain in a compact
subset of $(0,\infty)$.

Differentiate \eqref{eq:Rstructure} in direction $k$.  Each summand either
(i) differentiates a bounded analytic chord multiplier, (ii) replaces one
smooth shifted tangent by $k'$, or (iii) replaces one of the two tangent
differences in $\Gamma_\gamma$ by the corresponding difference of $k'$.
The fractional product estimate used in the proof of
\cite[Proposition 4.3]{BR}, applied with one smooth factor, gives for every
\[
 0<\delta<\min\{s-3/2,\,2-s\}
\]
the uniform bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rgain}
 |D_\gamma R(\gamma,h)[k]|
 \le C_{\gamma,\delta}\|k\|_{H^s}\|h\|_{H^{s-\delta}}.
\end{equation}
For completeness, the power count is the same as in the principal estimate:
the two tangent differences absorb the weight of order $2s-2$, while the
smooth coefficient permits $\delta$ derivatives to be shifted away from the
test factor.  Cases (i)--(iii) have the same bound; no differentiated term
loses both tangent differences.  This is also the one-variation version of
the differentiated-remainder estimates in \cite[Sections 3--4]{SV}.

Thus the induced operator takes $H^s$ boundedly into
$H^{-s+\delta}$.  The embedding $H^{-s+\delta}\hookrightarrow H^{-s}$ is
compact on $\T$, proving the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fredholm constrained Hessian]\label{prop:fredholm}
At every critical point $\gamma_*$ of $\E|_{\M_{v,b}}$, the Riemannian
Hessian
\[
 \operatorname{Hess}_{\M_{v,b}}\E(\gamma_*):
 T_{\gamma_*}\M_{v,b}\longrightarrow T_{\gamma_*}^*\M_{v,b}
\]
is Fredholm of index zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Critical points on the pointwise-speed manifold are also critical under a
fixed-length constraint.  Indeed, reparametrization invariance annihilates
all tangential variations, and a length-preserving variation can be corrected
by a periodic tangential variation so that its first-order logarithmic strain
vanishes.  Hence for some constant $\lambda$ the curve is critical for
$\E+\lambda\mathcal L$ on the ambient space.  The regularity theorem of
Blatt--Reiter makes it smooth \cite[Theorem 4]{BR}.

The constrained Hessian is the restriction of
$D^2(\E+\lambda\mathcal L)(\gamma_*)$ to the tangent space.  By
\eqref{eq:split} it is $12Q$ plus the operator in
Lemma~\ref{lem:compactR}, plus the second variation of length.  The latter has
order at most two derivatives and is compact relative to the order-$2s$
operator because $s>3/2$.  The mean constraint eliminates constants, so
\eqref{eq:coercive} makes $Q$ an isomorphism from the tangent space to its
dual.  The Hessian is therefore an isomorphism plus a compact operator.  It is
Fredholm, and symmetry gives index zero.
\end{proof}

Combining Lemma~\ref{lem:analytic}, Proposition~\ref{prop:fredholm}, and the
abstract Hilbert-manifold Lojasiewicz--Simon theorem
\cite[Theorem 1.4]{DF} yields the following.

\begin{proposition}[Gradient inequality]\label{prop:LS}
If $\gamma_*$ is critical for $\E|_{\M_{v,b}}$, then there exist
$Z>0$, $\varepsilon>0$ and $\theta\in[1/2,1)$ such that
\[
 \|\grad_{\M_{v,b}}\E(\eta)\|_{H^s}
 \ge Z|\E(\eta)-\E(\gamma_*)|^\theta
\]
whenever $\eta\in\M_{v,b}$ and
$\|\eta-\gamma_*\|_{H^s}<\varepsilon$.
\end{proposition}

\section{Palais--Smale compactness}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:PS}
The restriction $\E|_{\M_{v,b}}$ satisfies the Palais--Smale condition.
Equivalently, if $(\gamma_j)\subset\M_{v,b}$ has bounded energy and
\[\|\grad_{\M_{v,b}}\E(\gamma_j)\|_{H^s}\to0,\]
then a subsequence converges strongly in $H^s$ to a critical point.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We again work after the fixed-speed pullback.  The energy bound gives a
uniform bilipschitz lower bound and a uniform $H^s$ bound by
\cite[Theorem 1 and Proposition 2.1]{BR}.  The barycenter constraint controls
the constant Fourier mode.  Passing to a subsequence,
\[
 \gamma_j\rightharpoonup\gamma_\infty\quad\text{in }H^s,
 \qquad \gamma_j\to\gamma_\infty\quad\text{in }C^1.
\]
The bilipschitz estimate, speed and barycenter pass to the limit, so
$\gamma_\infty\in\M_{v,b}$.

Set $h_j=\gamma_j-\gamma_\infty$.  Let $P_j$ be the uniformly bounded
projection onto $T_{\gamma_j}\M_{v,b}$ furnished by the logarithmic-strain
right inverse in \cite[Section 5]{KSSM}.  Since the constrained gradients tend
to zero,
\[
 D\E(\gamma_j)(P_jh_j)=o(1).
\]
Fix $r\in(3/2,s)$.  The same structural estimate as
\eqref{eq:Rstructure}, together with the explicit strain right inverse, gives
on this energy-controlled set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect}
 |D\E(\gamma_j)((I-P_j)h_j)|
 \le C_r\|h_j\|_{H^r}=o(1).
\end{equation}
This is the Menger counterpart of the projection-defect estimate used in the
Palais--Smale proof for tangent-point energy in \cite[Section 4]{FSZM}; it
follows term by term because $D\E$ contains only differences and derivatives
of its test function and the right inverses are uniformly bounded under the
energy and speed bounds.  The final equality in \eqref{eq:defect} uses the
compact embedding $H^s\hookrightarrow H^r$ together with weak $H^s$
convergence.  Hence $D\E(\gamma_j)h_j=o(1)$.

Using \eqref{eq:split}, bilinearity of $Q$, and weak convergence,
\[
 Q(\gamma_j,h_j)=Q(h_j,h_j)+Q(\gamma_\infty,h_j)
                 =Q(h_j,h_j)+o(1).
\]
The remainder bound in \cite[Lemma 4.2 and Proposition 4.3]{BR} gives
$|R(\gamma_j,h_j)|\le C\|h_j\|_{C^1}=o(1)$.  Therefore
$Q(h_j,h_j)\to0$.  The barycenter constraint removes constants, so
\eqref{eq:coercive} implies $\|h_j\|_{H^s}\to0$.

Finally, continuity of the constrained gradient shows that the strong limit
is critical.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of full convergence}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
The source estimates make the trajectory bounded in $H^s$, and
\eqref{eq:source-decay} says its constrained gradient tends to zero.  Choose
any $t_j\to\infty$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:PS} provides a subsequence, still
denoted $t_j$, and a critical $\gamma_\infty\in\M_{v,b}$ such that
\[
 \gamma(t_j)\longrightarrow\gamma_\infty\quad\text{strongly in }H^s.
\]
In particular $\E(\gamma_\infty)=E_\infty:=\lim_{t\to\infty}\E(\gamma(t))$.

Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:LS} near $\gamma_\infty$.  Put
$e(t)=\E(\gamma(t))-E_\infty\ge0$.  Whenever the trajectory is in the
Lojasiewicz--Simon neighborhood and $e(t)>0$,
\begin{align*}
 -\frac{\dd}{\dd t}e(t)^{1-\theta}
 &= (1-\theta)e(t)^{-\theta}
    \|\grad_{\M_{v,b}}\E(\gamma(t))\|_{H^s}^2\\
 &\ge (1-\theta)Z
    \|\grad_{\M_{v,b}}\E(\gamma(t))\|_{H^s}
  =(1-\theta)Z\|\dot\gamma(t)\|_{H^s}.
\end{align*}
Choose $t_j$ so large that $\gamma(t_j)$ lies in the half-sized
neighborhood and $e(t_j)^{1-\theta}$ is smaller than the distance to its
boundary times $(1-\theta)Z$.  Integrating the displayed inequality shows
that the trajectory cannot reach the boundary.  Thus it remains in the
neighborhood forever and
\[
 \int_{t_j}^{\infty}\|\dot\gamma(t)\|_{H^s}\dd t
 \le \frac{e(t_j)^{1-\theta}}{(1-\theta)Z}<\infty.
\]
The tail is Cauchy in $H^s$ and hence converges strongly.  Its already known
subsequential limit identifies the limit as $\gamma_\infty$.

The speed and barycenter identities pass to the strong limit.  The source
paper proves that every finite-time curve is in the initial knot class and
gives a uniform energy-dependent bilipschitz bound; strong $C^1$ convergence
therefore keeps the limiting curve embedded and in the same ambient isotopy
class.  This proves all claims.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and novelty}

The theorem concerns exactly the projected flow \eqref{eq:flow}.  It does not
show that the ordinary ambient flow $\dot\gamma=-\nabla\E(\gamma)$ exists for
all time.  The tempting idea that the two flows differ only by a
reparametrization is false at the formal level: the normal correction belongs
to $\operatorname{ran}D\Sigma(\gamma)^*$ for the nonlocal $H^s$ inner product
and need not be a pointwise tangential vector field.

A bounded literature search through 13 August 2026 used the exact source title
and combinations of ``full convergence'', ``integral Menger curvature'',
``Lojasiewicz--Simon'', and ``projected critical point''.  The closest later
work is Freches--Schumacher--Steenebruegge--von der Mosel
\cite{FSZM}, which develops Palais--Smale and strong-subconvergence machinery,
and Doehrer--Freches \cite{DF}, which proves full convergence for
\emph{tangent-point} energy.  No full-convergence theorem for the projected
generalized-integral-Menger flow was found.  Novelty confidence is moderate
pending specialist review.
