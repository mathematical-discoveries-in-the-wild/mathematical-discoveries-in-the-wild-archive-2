\section{Original question}

Monteil and Van Schaftingen ask in Section~1.3, arXiv PDF page~4, whether the
condition $sp\notin[1,m)$ is necessary for the lifting property when
$0<s<1$ and the compact target $\mathcal N$ has a nontrivial finite
fundamental group~\cite{source}.  The surrounding discussion concerns the
universal covering and a simply-connected domain manifold $\mathcal M$.

Equivalently, the unresolved range included
\[
  2\le sp<m,
\]
where the target's universal cover is compact because $\pi_1(\mathcal N)$ is
finite.

\section{Separate later answer}

Mironescu and Van Schaftingen subsequently proved a complete compact-cover
classification in \emph{Lifting in compact covering spaces for fractional
Sobolev mappings}, arXiv:1907.01373~\cite{answerpaper}.  Their assumptions are
a nontrivial Riemannian covering
\(\pi:\widetilde{\mathcal N}\to\mathcal N\) with both manifolds compact and
connected, and an $m$-dimensional compact simply-connected domain
$\mathcal M$, possibly with boundary.

\begin{answer}
For $0<s<1$ and $m\ge2$, every map
$u\in W^{s,p}(\mathcal M,\mathcal N)$ has a lifting
$\widetilde u\in W^{s,p}(\mathcal M,\widetilde{\mathcal N})$ if and only if
$sp\notin[1,2)$.
\end{answer}

This is Theorem~1 on arXiv PDF page~2.  Its positive part includes the full
range $2\le sp<m$ that remained uncertain in the source.  Its negative part
states that not every map lifts when $1\le sp<2$.  The abstract explicitly
says that the paper settles completely the Sobolev lifting question over
covering spaces.

\section{Identification and scope}

If $\mathcal N$ is compact and $\pi_1(\mathcal N)$ is finite, its universal
cover is a finite-sheeted cover of a compact space and is therefore compact.
Hence the later theorem applies directly to the source question.

For $m\ge3$, the answer to the proposed necessity is negative: maps lift for
every $2\le sp<m$, even though these exponents lie in $[1,m)$.  The correct
exceptional interval is $[1,2)$.  When $m=2$, the intervals $[1,2)$ and
$[1,m)$ coincide.  The packet does not assert anything about
nonsimply-connected domains or cases outside the hypotheses of the covering
theorem.

This is an exact literature answer, not an original result.  The answering
paper cites the source paper, shares Jean Van Schaftingen as an author, and
explicitly presents its theorem as completing the covering-space lifting
problem.
