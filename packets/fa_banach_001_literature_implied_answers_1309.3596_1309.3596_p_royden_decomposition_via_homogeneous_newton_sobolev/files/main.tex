\begin{abstract}
Lucia and Puls conjectured that the Dirichlet problem is solvable on the
$p$-harmonic boundary of every complete doubling PI space in their standing
class, without a global $(p,p)$-Sobolev inequality.  Their exhaustion already
produces the candidate $p$-harmonic function, but they could not prove that
the error lies in the $BD^p$-closure of compactly supported functions.  We
show that Theorem~1.1 of Shanmugalingam, arXiv:2311.17356, supplies exactly
the missing compactness: tail convex combinations of the exhaustion errors
converge in the homogeneous Newton--Sobolev norm.  A local gluing lemma turns
the quasi-continuous errors into genuine members of $BD_c^p(X)$.  This gives
the required decomposition and then the full continuous-data statement by
Stone--Weierstrass and comparison.
\end{abstract}

\section{The source conjecture}

Fix $1<p<\infty$.  The standing assumptions of Lucia--Puls are that
$(X,d,\mu)$ is locally compact, noncompact and complete, that $\mu$ is
doubling with positive finite mass on every ball, and that $X$ supports a
$(1,p)$-Poincar\'e inequality.  In particular, $X$ is proper and connected.

For $u\in\DN$, write
\[
 \|u\|_{D^{1,p}}=\left(\int_X g_u^p\,d\mu\right)^{1/p},
\]
where $g_u$ is the minimal $p$-weak upper gradient.  The Royden algebra
$\BD$ consists of the bounded continuous elements of $\DN$.
Convergence in its $BD^p$-topology means uniform boundedness, locally uniform
convergence, and convergence in the displayed Dirichlet seminorm.  Let
\[
 \mathcal B_0:=\overline{\BDc}^{\,BD^p}.
\]
The Royden compactification is denoted by $\Rbar$, and
\[
 \DeltaP=\{\xi\in\Rbar\setminus X:u(\xi)=0
            \text{ for every }u\in\mathcal B_0\}.
\]

On source PDF page~12, Conjecture~5.1 asks whether every continuous function
on $\DeltaP$ has a $p$-harmonic extension to $X$, and additionally whether
every $f\in\BD$ has such an extension in $\HBD$ agreeing with $f$ on
$\DeltaP$~\cite{LP}.  The source immediately says that a Royden
decomposition $f=g+h$, with $g\in\mathcal B_0$ and $h\in\HBD$, would imply
the conjecture.

\section{The later input}

The homogeneous Newton--Sobolev space is the quotient
\[
 \HN=\DN/\!\sim,
 \qquad u\sim v\iff \|u-v\|_{D^{1,p}}=0.
\]
The Poincar\'e inequality says that equivalent representatives differ almost
everywhere by a constant.  Shanmugalingam proved the following theorem under
exactly the doubling and Poincar\'e hypotheses used here~\cite{Sh}:

\begin{quote}
$\HN$ is a Banach space.  If $1<p<\infty$, every bounded sequence in $\HN$
has a strongly convergent convex-combination subsequence, where its $k$th
term is a convex combination of a finite tail of the original sequence.
\end{quote}

This is Theorem~1.1 on supporting PDF page~2:
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/supporting_theorem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The tail condition is essential below: a tail convex combination preserves
the locally uniform limit of the Lucia--Puls exhaustion.

\section{Proof intuition}

For $f\in\BD$, solve the ordinary Dirichlet problem on an exhaustion
$\Omega_i\Subset X$, extend the solution $h_i$ by $f$ outside $\Omega_i$,
and take a locally uniform limit $h$.  The errors $u_i=f-h_i$ have compact
support, converge locally uniformly to $g=f-h$, and are uniformly bounded in
the homogeneous energy norm.  The later theorem therefore gives tail convex
combinations $v_k$ converging strongly in homogeneous energy.  Local uniform
convergence pins down the otherwise free additive constants, so the strong
limit class is exactly $[g]$.

The only regularity nuisance is that $u_i$ is initially a compactly supported
Newtonian function and may fail to be continuous at the boundary of
$\Omega_i$.  Each $v_k$ is nevertheless continuous far inside $\Omega_k$.
Approximate it in Newtonian norm by a compactly supported Lipschitz function,
then glue the approximation to $v_k$ on a growing interior compact set.  The
glued function lies in $\BDc$, agrees with $v_k$ on larger and larger
compacta, and changes its energy by an arbitrarily small amount.  Hence
$g\in\mathcal B_0$.

\section{A compact-support gluing lemma}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:glue}
Let $K_k\Subset\Omega_k$ exhaust $X$, with the open sets $\Omega_k$
increasing.  Suppose that $(v_k)$ is uniformly bounded, each $v_k$ is a
compactly supported member of $N^{1,p}(X)$ and is continuous on $\Omega_k$,
and
\[
 v_k\longrightarrow g\quad\text{locally uniformly},
 \qquad
 \|v_k-g\|_{D^{1,p}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Then $g\in\mathcal B_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On a complete doubling PI space, bounded compactly supported Newtonian
functions admit bounded compactly supported Lipschitz approximations in
$N^{1,p}$; this is the standard Lipschitz-density theorem, followed by
truncation and a compactly supported cutoff~\cite{BB,Sh0}.  Thus choose
$\phi_k\in\operatorname{Lip}_c(X)$, uniformly bounded by a common constant,
so close to $v_k$ in $N^{1,p}(X)$ that the estimate below is less than
$2^{-k}$.

Choose a Lipschitz cutoff $0\leq\chi_k\leq1$ with $\chi_k=1$ on $K_k$ and
$\operatorname{supp}\chi_k\Subset\Omega_k$, and set
\[
 w_k=\chi_kv_k+(1-\chi_k)\phi_k.
\]
Because $v_k$ is continuous wherever $\chi_k$ is nonzero, $w_k$ is a
bounded continuous compactly supported function.  Hence $w_k\in\BDc$, and
$w_k=v_k$ on $K_k$.  Moreover,
\[
 w_k-v_k=(1-\chi_k)(\phi_k-v_k),
\]
so the product rule for weak upper gradients gives
\[
 \|w_k-v_k\|_{D^{1,p}}
 \leq \|\phi_k-v_k\|_{D^{1,p}}
      +\|g_{\chi_k}|\phi_k-v_k|\|_{L^p}<2^{-k}.
\]
The sequence $(w_k)$ is uniformly bounded, converges to $g$ locally
uniformly, and converges to $g$ in Dirichlet seminorm.  This is precisely
$BD^p$ convergence, proving $g\in\mathcal B_0$.
\end{proof}

\section{The literature-implied answer}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Under the standing assumptions above:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every $f\in\BD$ admits a decomposition
\[
 f=g+h,
 \qquad g\in\mathcal B_0,
 \qquad h\in\HBD.
\]
Consequently $h=f$ on $\DeltaP$.
\item Every real-valued $\varphi\in C(\DeltaP)$ has a bounded $p$-harmonic
extension $h$ to $X$ satisfying
\[
 \lim_{X\ni x\to\xi}h(x)=\varphi(\xi)
 \qquad(\xi\in\DeltaP),
\]
where the limit is taken in the Royden compactification.
\end{enumerate}
Thus Conjecture~5.1 of Lucia--Puls holds in its standing metric-measure-space
class, without a global $(p,p)$-Sobolev inequality or a uniform lower volume
bound on fixed-radius balls.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textit{Step 1: the exhaustion and its homogeneous bound.}
Choose increasing relatively compact domains $\Omega_i$ exhausting $X$.
For $f\in\BD$, let $h_i$ be the bounded $p$-harmonic solution on $\Omega_i$
with boundary values $f$ quasi-everywhere, extended by $f$ off $\Omega_i$.
Lucia--Puls, Proposition~4.2, gives a subsequence, relabelled $(h_i)$, that
converges locally uniformly to some $h\in\HBD$~\cite{LP}.  Put
\[
 u_i=f-h_i,
 \qquad g=f-h.
\]
Then $u_i\to g$ locally uniformly and $u_i$ has compact support in the
Newtonian sense.  Energy minimality on $\Omega_i$ and equality with $f$
outside give
\[
 \|h_i\|_{D^{1,p}}\leq\|f\|_{D^{1,p}},
 \qquad
 \|u_i\|_{D^{1,p}}
 \leq2\|f\|_{D^{1,p}}.
\]
Thus $([u_i])$ is bounded in $\HN$.

\textit{Step 2: strong homogeneous convergence and identification of the
limit.}
By Shanmugalingam's theorem, there are coefficients $a_{k,i}\geq0$ with
$\sum_{i=k}^{N_k}a_{k,i}=1$ such that
\[
 v_k:=\sum_{i=k}^{N_k}a_{k,i}u_i
\]
converges strongly in $\HN$ to some class $[v]$.  Since the combinations use
tails and $u_i\to g$ locally uniformly, also $v_k\to g$ locally uniformly.

We verify that $[v]=[g]$, which is the point at which additive constants in
the homogeneous quotient must be controlled.  Fix a ball $B_0$.  For a
representative $v$ of the limit, the Poincar\'e inequality applied on any
fixed larger ball shows that
\[
 (v_k-v)-(v_k-v)_{B_0}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in }L^p_{\mathrm{loc}}(X).
\]
On $B_0$, the independent convergence $v_k\to g$ forces the constants
$(v_k-v)_{B_0}$ to converge to a scalar $c$.  Applying the same display on
every larger ball gives $g=v+c$ almost everywhere on all of $X$.  Hence
$[g]=[v]$, and therefore
\[
 \|v_k-g\|_{D^{1,p}}\longrightarrow0.                 \tag{1}
\]

\textit{Step 3: membership in the compact-support closure.}
Choose compact sets $K_k\Subset\Omega_k$ exhausting $X$.  Every term $u_i$
with $i\geq k$ is continuous on $\Omega_k$, because there $h_i$ is
$p$-harmonic and $f$ is continuous.  Thus $v_k$ is continuous on
$\Omega_k$.  The maximum principle gives a common bound for $h_i$, hence for
$u_i$ and $v_k$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:glue}, local uniform convergence, and (1)
now yield $g\in\mathcal B_0$.  This proves the decomposition.  By the
definition of $\DeltaP$, $g$ vanishes there, so the continuous extensions of
$f$ and $h$ to $\Rbar$ satisfy $h=f$ on $\DeltaP$.

\textit{Step 4: arbitrary continuous boundary data.}
If $\DeltaP$ is empty, the assertion is vacuous.  Otherwise it is a closed
subset of the compact Hausdorff space $\Rbar$.  Extend
$\varphi\in C(\DeltaP)$ to $F\in C(\Rbar)$ by Tietze's theorem.  Since the
Royden algebra separates points and contains the constants,
Stone--Weierstrass gives $f_n\in\BD$ with
$\|f_n-F\|_{C(\Rbar)}\to0$.

For every $n$ and exhaustion index $i$, form the finite-domain solution
$h_{i,n}$ with data $f_n$.  A diagonal Arzel\`a--Ascoli extraction gives one
subsequence of exhaustion indices along which $h_{i,n}$ converges locally
uniformly to a function $h_n$ for every $n$.  The first three steps, applied
separately for each $n$ along this common subsequence, show that
$h_n\in\HBD$ and $h_n=f_n$ on $\DeltaP$.

The finite-domain comparison principle, including the extensions outside
$\Omega_i$, gives
\[
 \|h_{i,n}-h_{i,m}\|_{L^\infty(X)}
 \leq\|f_n-f_m\|_{L^\infty(X)}.
\]
Passing to the local limits pointwise preserves the inequality.  Thus
$(h_n)$ is uniformly Cauchy on $X$ and has a uniform limit $h$.  Local
uniform stability of $p$-harmonic functions makes $h$ bounded and
$p$-harmonic.

Finally fix $\xi\in\DeltaP$ and $\varepsilon>0$.  Choose $n$ so that both
$\|h-h_n\|_\infty$ and $\|F-f_n\|_
\infty$ are less than $\varepsilon/3$.  Since the continuous extension of
$h_n$ satisfies $h_n(\xi)=f_n(\xi)$, points $x\in X$ sufficiently close to
$\xi$ satisfy $|h_n(x)-f_n(\xi)|<\varepsilon/3$.  The triangle inequality
then gives $|h(x)-\varphi(\xi)|<\varepsilon$.  This proves the asserted
boundary limit and completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and literature status}

The proof uses two external ingredients: the exhaustion and comparison
machinery already proved in Lucia--Puls, and the strong tail-convex-combination
theorem of Shanmugalingam.  The additional arguments in this packet are the
identification of the homogeneous limit class, the compact-support gluing
lemma, and the simultaneous diagonalization needed for continuous boundary
data.  No global $L^p$ estimate for $f-h_i$ is used.

The supporting theorem is stated explicitly in the abstract and as
Theorem~1.1 of arXiv:2311.17356, but that paper neither cites arXiv:1309.3596
nor states the Royden-boundary consequence.  Searches covered the run's
solution, attempt, ledger, and parsed-arXiv indexes; the exact source arXiv id
and conjecture phrase; and arXiv searches for ``$p$-Royden decomposition'',
``$p$-harmonic boundary'', and ``homogeneous Newton--Sobolev''.  The related
later paper arXiv:2407.18315 was also checked at title/abstract level.  No
explicit prior resolution was found.  This bounded search is not an
exhaustive bibliographic guarantee, so the durable classification is
\emph{literature-implied answer}, not a novelty claim.

The theorem proves the existence assertions of Conjecture~5.1.  It does not
assert uniqueness of $g+h$ for nonlinear $p$-harmonic functions, a stronger
statement mentioned speculatively after the conjecture but unnecessary for
the conjecture itself.  Human review should focus on (i) bounded
compactly-supported Lipschitz density as used in Lemma~\ref{lem:glue},
(ii) pinning constants in the passage from $\HN$ convergence to (1), and
(iii) the common-subsequence comparison argument for arbitrary continuous
data.
