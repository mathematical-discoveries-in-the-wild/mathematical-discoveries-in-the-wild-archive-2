\section{The source question}

For a Ritt operator $T$ on a Banach function space, Hults and
Reinhold-Larsson define
\[
 \mathbf Q^T_{\alpha,s,r}f
 =\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^\alpha
       |T^n(I-T)^rf|^s\right)^{1/s}.
\]
Their Theorem 1.7 proves strong boundedness when $sr>\alpha+1$.  Open
Question 1 asks whether $\mathbf Q^T_{\alpha,s,r}$ is of weak type $(1,1)$
on the endpoint $sr=\alpha+1$ \cite[p.~3]{HultsReinhold}.  The exact question
and its context are reproduced in Figure~\ref{fig:question}.

\section{Proof intuition}

A Ritt estimate controls each time increment in operator norm, but it does not
force weak-$(1,1)$ control after absolute values are accumulated over many
widely separated time scales.  We assign the $m$th dyadic martingale
difference the eigenvalue $1-10^{-6m}$.  Its contribution to the square
function is concentrated near time $10^{6m}$, so disjoint time blocks recover
one uniformly positive contribution from each martingale level.  The extreme
lacunarity makes all other levels negligible in that block.

For the Rademacher polynomial $f_N=r_1+\cdots+r_N$, this produces a pointwise
lower bound of order $N^{1/s}$.  Yet $\norm{f_N}_1\leq\sqrt N$.  The two rates
contradict weak type $(1,1)$ whenever $s<2$.  The same lacunarity also makes
the operator easy to audit: bounded-variation formulas for martingale
multipliers prove both power boundedness and the Ritt difference estimate.

\section{Construction and operator estimates}

Let
\[
 \Omega=\{-1,1\}^{\N}
\]
with product probability measure.  Write $r_m(\omega)=\omega_m$, let
$\mathcal F_m=\sigma(r_1,\ldots,r_m)$, and denote conditional expectation by
$E_m=\E(\,\cdot\mid\mathcal F_m)$, with $E_0=\E$.  Put
$D_m=E_m-E_{m-1}$.

We first record the elementary multiplier estimate used throughout.

\begin{lemma}[bounded-variation martingale multipliers]\label{lem:bv}
If $a_m\to0$ and $\sum_m|a_m-a_{m+1}|<\infty$, then
\[
 M_a=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_mD_m
\]
converges in operator norm on $L^1(\Omega)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bv}
 \norm{M_a}_{1\to1}
 \leq |a_1|+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}|a_m-a_{m+1}|.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a finite truncation, summation by parts gives
\[
 \sum_{m=1}^Ma_mD_m
 =a_ME_M+\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}(a_m-a_{m+1})E_m-a_1E_0.
\]
Every $E_m$ is a contraction on $L^1$.  The displayed formula is Cauchy in
operator norm and yields \eqref{eq:bv}.
\end{proof}

Fix
\[
 q=10^{-6},\qquad \delta_m=q^m,\qquad \lambda_m=1-\delta_m,
\]
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T}
 T=I-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\delta_mD_m
   =E_0+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_mD_m.
\end{equation}
The first series is norm convergent by Lemma~\ref{lem:bv}.  The martingale
decomposition shows that $T^n$ has eigenvalue $1$ on constants and
$\lambda_m^n$ on $D_mL^1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ritt}
The operator $T$ is Ritt on $L^1(\Omega)$; more precisely,
\[
 \sup_{n\geq1}\norm{T^n}_{1\to1}\leq3,
 \qquad
 \sup_{n\geq1}n\norm{T^n-T^{n+1}}_{1\to1}<\frac2e.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The coefficients $c_m=1-\lambda_m^n$ decrease to zero.  Lemma~\ref{lem:bv}
therefore gives $\norm{I-T^n}\leq2c_1\leq2$, proving the first estimate.

The multiplier coefficients of $nT^n(I-T)$ are
\[
 b_m=n\delta_m(1-\delta_m)^n.
\]
The scalar function $x\mapsto nx(1-x)^n$ has a unique maximum at
$x=1/(n+1)$.  Since $(\delta_m)$ decreases, $(b_m)_m$ is unimodal and tends
to zero.  Lemma~\ref{lem:bv} and total variation give
\[
 n\norm{T^n(I-T)}
 \leq2\max_m b_m
 \leq2\max_{0\leq x\leq1}nx(1-x)^n
 =2\left(\frac n{n+1}\right)^{n+1}<\frac2e.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Endpoint failure}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For the single Ritt operator $T$ in \eqref{eq:T} and every $1\leq s<2$,
the endpoint square function
\[
 \mathbf Q^T_{s-1,s,1}f
 =\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{s-1}|T^n(I-T)f|^s\right)^{1/s}
\]
is not of weak type $(1,1)$.  In particular, Open Question 1 has a negative
answer for general Ritt operators on $L^1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $L_m=\delta_m^{-1}=10^{6m}$ and introduce the disjoint integer blocks
\[
 B_m=\{L_m,L_m+1,\ldots,2L_m-1\}.
\]
For $N\geq1$, put $f_N=\sum_{j=1}^Nr_j$.  Since $D_jf_N=r_j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockexpansion}
 T^n(I-T)f_N=\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_j\lambda_j^n r_j.
\end{equation}

Fix $m\leq N$.  The weighted $\ell^s(B_m)$ norm of the $m$th summand is at
least $1/16$.  Indeed, $n\geq L_m$, and
\[
 \lambda_m^{L_m}=(1-L_m^{-1})^{L_m}>\frac14
\]
because $L_m\geq2$.  Hence $\lambda_m^n\geq\lambda_m^{2L_m}\geq1/16$ on
$B_m$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 \left(\sum_{n\in B_m}n^{s-1}
       |\delta_m\lambda_m^n|^s\right)^{1/s}\geq\frac1{16}.
\end{equation}

We bound interference in the same norm.  If $j>m$, then $\lambda_j^n\leq1$
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:future}
 \left(\sum_{n\in B_m}n^{s-1}
       |\delta_j\lambda_j^n|^s\right)^{1/s}
 \leq2L_m\delta_j=2q^{j-m}.
\end{equation}
If $j<m$, set $R=\delta_j/\delta_m=q^{-(m-j)}$.  Since
$\lambda_j^n\leq e^{-n\delta_j}\leq e^{-R}$ on $B_m$, the same calculation
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:past}
 \left(\sum_{n\in B_m}n^{s-1}
       |\delta_j\lambda_j^n|^s\right)^{1/s}
 \leq2Re^{-R}.
\end{equation}
The bounds are uniform for $1\leq s<2$.  Moreover,
\[
 2\sum_{k\geq1}q^k+2\sum_{k\geq1}q^{-k}e^{-q^{-k}}<10^{-5}<\frac1{32}.
\]
For the second sum one may use $q^{-k}\geq k/q$ and monotonicity of
$xe^{-x}$ for $x\geq1$.

The reverse triangle inequality in weighted $\ell^s(B_m)$, applied to
\eqref{eq:blockexpansion}--\eqref{eq:past}, now gives a lower bound $1/32$
for every block and every $\omega\in\Omega$.  Since the blocks are disjoint,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pointwise}
 \mathbf Q^T_{s-1,s,1}f_N(\omega)\geq\frac{N^{1/s}}{32}
 \qquad(\omega\in\Omega).
\end{equation}

On the other hand, Rademacher orthogonality and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:l1}
 \norm{f_N}_1\leq\norm{f_N}_2=\sqrt N.
\end{equation}
If the square function were weak type $(1,1)$ with constant $C$, applying the
distributional estimate at $t=N^{1/s}/64$ would yield
\[
 1\leq \frac{C\norm{f_N}_1}{t}
 \leq64C N^{1/2-1/s}.
\]
This is impossible as $N\to\infty$ because $s<2$.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, verification, and novelty}

The same fixed operator settles an interval of endpoint parameters, not only
one triple: $r=1$, $1\leq s<2$, and $\alpha=s-1$ always satisfy
$sr=\alpha+1$.  Thus it refutes the universal question as written.  The
argument becomes scale-balanced at $s=2$, and $T$ is not asserted to be a
positive contraction or a convolution operator.  Accordingly, this packet
does not settle $s=2$ or the structured subclasses for which the source gives
positive results.

Eight focused audits separately checked norm convergence of \eqref{eq:T},
power boundedness, the Ritt estimate, target-block mass, past and future
interference, the Rademacher weak-type contradiction, parameter scope, and
novelty.  The included checker validates the scalar constants and sampled
multiplier variations.  It is a sanity check, not part of the proof.

Novelty is provisional.  Searches through 13 August 2026 covered the exact
question, title, DOI and arXiv id; Ritt/$L^1$/endpoint/weak-type/counterexample
phrases; the local run corpus; the published ETDS version; and nearby Ritt
square-function papers.  The accepted February 2026 revision still asks the
question, and no later answer or matching martingale construction was found.
Priority is not claimed.  Human review should first check
Lemma~\ref{lem:bv} and the interference bounds
\eqref{eq:future}--\eqref{eq:past}.
