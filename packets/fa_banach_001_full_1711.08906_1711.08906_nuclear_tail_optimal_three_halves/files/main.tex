\section{Source question}

Hamhalter and Kalenda study bounded subsets of
\(\Nuc(\ell^q(\Lambda),\ell^p(J))\), the nuclear operators from
\(\ell^q(\Lambda)\) to \(\ell^p(J)\), with \(p,q\in(1,\infty)\)
\cite{HK}.  If \(P_C\) and \(Q_D\) are the canonical coordinate
projections for finite \(C\subset J\) and \(D\subset\Lambda\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 \Tail_{p,q}(A)
 :=\inf_{C,D\ \mathrm{finite}}
   \sup_{T\in A}\|T-P_CTQ_D\|_{N}.
\end{equation}
Their Theorem 2.1(c), page 5, proves
\[
  \chi(A)\le \Tail_{p,q}(A)\le 2\chi(A),
\]
where \(\chi\) is the Hausdorff measure of norm noncompactness, and then
asks whether the constant \(2\) is optimal.

\section{Answer and proof intuition}

\textbf{Answer.} The constant \(2\) is not optimal.  It can be replaced by
\(3/2\), and \(3/2\) is the best constant valid simultaneously for all
\(p,q\in(1,\infty)\).

\subsection*{Proof intuition}

The source proof estimates the complement of the corner projection
\(T\mapsto P_CTQ_D\) by the generic bound \(1+1=2\).  Coordinate
projections have extra symmetry.  The operators \(U=2P_C-I\) and
\(V=2Q_D-I\) are sign-change isometries, and averaging their four actions
gives the exact formula
\[
 T-P_CTQ_D=\frac14(3T-UT-TV-UTV).
\]
The sum of the absolute coefficients is \(3/2\), giving the improved bound.

Sharpness is a finite-dimensional projection phenomenon.  In the trace-dual
operator space, deleting one corner sends a \(2\times2\) matrix of
\(\ell^\infty\!\to\ell^1\) norm \(2\) to one of norm \(3\).  Finite-dimensional
continuity moves this ratio inside the allowed range \(p,q\in(1,\infty)\).
A replication argument then turns a kernel vector for the corner projection
into an infinite bounded set whose ratio \(\Tail(A)/\chi(A)\) is the same
projection-quotient ratio.

\section{The improved inequality}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let \(J,\Lambda\) be infinite sets, \(p,q\in(1,\infty)\), and let
\(A\subset \Nuc(\ell^q(\Lambda),\ell^p(J))\) be bounded.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 \chi(A)\le \Tail_{p,q}(A)\le \frac32\chi(A).
\end{equation}
Moreover, \(3/2\) is the least constant for which the second inequality in
\eqref{eq:main} holds for all choices of \(p,q,J,\Lambda,A\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[corner-complement norm]\label{lem:corner}
For arbitrary coordinate projections \(P=P_C\) on \(\ell^p(J)\) and
\(Q=Q_D\) on \(\ell^q(\Lambda)\), the operator
\[
 \Psi_{C,D}:\Nuc(\ell^q(\Lambda),\ell^p(J))\longrightarrow
 \Nuc(\ell^q(\Lambda),\ell^p(J)),\qquad
 \Psi_{C,D}(T)=T-PTQ,
\]
satisfies \(\|\Psi_{C,D}\|\le 3/2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set \(U=2P-I\) and \(V=2Q-I\).  These are surjective linear isometries on
the respective \(\ell^p\)- and \(\ell^q\)-spaces.  Left or right composition
of a nuclear operator by a surjective isometry preserves its nuclear norm:
one inequality follows by composing a nuclear representation, and the reverse
inequality follows by composing with the inverse isometry.  Since
\[
 PTQ=\frac14(I+U)T(I+V)
     =\frac14(T+UT+TV+UTV),
\]
we have
\[
 \Psi_{C,D}(T)=\frac14(3T-UT-TV-UTV).
\]
The triangle inequality and invariance of the nuclear norm under the three
sign changes give
\[
 \|\Psi_{C,D}(T)\|_N
 \le \frac14(3+1+1+1)\|T\|_N
 =\frac32\|T\|_N. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the inequalities in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
For fixed finite \(C,D\), the set
\(\{P_CTQ_D:T\in A\}\) is bounded in a finite-dimensional space.  It is
therefore relatively compact and lies uniformly within
\(\sup_{T\in A}\|T-P_CTQ_D\|_N\) of \(A\).  Taking the infimum gives
\(\chi(A)\le\Tail_{p,q}(A)\).

Fix \(\varepsilon>0\).  Choose a finite set \(F\) in the nuclear-operator
space such that every \(T\in A\) has some \(S\in F\) with
\[
 \|T-S\|_N<\chi(A)+\varepsilon.
\]
Finite coordinate truncations converge in nuclear norm, so common finite sets
\(C\subset J\), \(D\subset\Lambda\) may be chosen with
\(\|S-P_CS Q_D\|_N<\varepsilon\) for all \(S\in F\).  For \(T\in A\), choose
such an \(S\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:corner} yields
\begin{align*}
 \|T-P_CTQ_D\|_N
 &\le \|\Psi_{C,D}(T-S)\|_N+\|S-P_CS Q_D\|_N\\
 &<\frac32(\chi(A)+\varepsilon)+\varepsilon.
\end{align*}
Taking the supremum over \(A\), then the infimum over \(C,D\), and finally
letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), proves the upper inequality.
\end{proof}

\section{Sharpness of \texorpdfstring{$3/2$}{3/2}}

We first record a standard elementary fact about projections.

\begin{lemma}[projection as a quotient section]\label{lem:quotient}
If \(\Phi\) is a bounded projection on a normed space \(X\) and
\(\Psi=I-\Phi\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient}
 \|\Psi\|=
 \sup_{0\ne b\in\ker\Phi}
 \frac{\|b\|}{\dist(b,\operatorname{ran}\Phi)}.
\end{equation}
In finite dimension the supremum is attained.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(x\in X\) and \(b=\Psi x\), then \(-\Phi x\in\operatorname{ran}\Phi\)
and \(b-(-\Phi x)=x\), so
\(\dist(b,\operatorname{ran}\Phi)\le\|x\|\).  This gives the inequality
``\(\ge\)'' in \eqref{eq:quotient}.  Conversely, for
\(b\in\ker\Phi\) and \(e\in\operatorname{ran}\Phi\),
\(\Psi(b-e)=b\), so \(\|b\|\le\|\Psi\|\|b-e\|\).  Taking the infimum over
\(e\) gives the reverse inequality.  Compactness of the finite-dimensional
quotient unit sphere gives attainment.
\end{proof}

For \(p,q\in(1,\infty)\), put
\(Z_{p,q}=\Nuc(\ell^q_2,\ell^p_2)\), and let \(\Phi_{p,q}\) keep only the
\((1,1)\) matrix entry.  By the trace duality used in the source paper
\cite[Proposition~3.1(a)]{HK},
\[
 Z_{p,q}^*=\mathcal L(\ell^p_2,\ell^q_2)
\]
isometrically.  The adjoint of \(\Phi_{p,q}\) is the same \((1,1)\)-corner
projection, hence the adjoint of \(\Psi_{p,q}=I-\Phi_{p,q}\) deletes that
entry.

Consider
\[
 H=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 H_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
At the finite-dimensional endpoint \(p=\infty,q=1\),
\begin{align*}
 \|H\|_{\ell^\infty_2\to\ell^1_2}
 &=\max_{|x_1|,|x_2|\le1}
    (|x_1-x_2|+|x_1+x_2|)=2,\\
 \|H_0\|_{\ell^\infty_2\to\ell^1_2}
 &=\max_{|x_1|,|x_2|\le1}
    (|x_2|+|x_1+x_2|)=3.
\end{align*}
The norms of a fixed \(2\times2\) matrix from \(\ell^p_2\) to \(\ell^q_2\)
depend continuously on \((p,q)\in[1,\infty]^2\).  Thus, for example with
\(p_m=m\) and \(q_m=1+1/m\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-limit}
 \|\Psi_{p_m,q_m}\|
 =\|\Psi_{p_m,q_m}^*\|
 \ge
 \frac{\|H_0\|_{\ell^{p_m}_2\to\ell^{q_m}_2}}
      {\|H\|_{\ell^{p_m}_2\to\ell^{q_m}_2}}
 \longrightarrow \frac32.
\end{equation}

It remains to turn these finite-dimensional projection norms into ratios of
the two quantities in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  Fix \(p,q\) and choose, using
Lemma~\ref{lem:quotient}, a nonzero \(b\in\ker\Phi_{p,q}\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:b}
 \frac{\|b\|_N}{\delta}=\|\Psi_{p,q}\|,
 \qquad
 \delta:=\dist(b,\operatorname{ran}\Phi_{p,q}).
\end{equation}
For each \(n\ge2\), embed \(b\) as a nuclear operator \(T_n\) supported on
the coordinates \(\{1,n\}\) in both \(\ell^q(\mathbb N)\) and
\(\ell^p(\mathbb N)\), using coordinate \(1\) for the first coordinate of
\(b\).  Let \(E_{11}x=x_1e_1\), choose \(L>\|b\|_N\), and set
\[
 \mathcal A_b=\{T_n:n\ge2\}\cup\{LE_{11}\}.
\]

We claim
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-equalities}
 \chi(\mathcal A_b)=\delta,
 \qquad
 \Tail_{p,q}(\mathcal A_b)=\|b\|_N.
\end{equation}
For the first upper bound, choose an element \(e\) of the one-dimensional
space \(\operatorname{ran}\Phi_{p,q}\) with \(\|b-e\|_N=\delta\).  Under
every embedding it is the same scalar multiple of \(E_{11}\), so the
two-point set \(\{e,LE_{11}\}\) lies within \(\delta\) of \(\mathcal A_b\).
Hence \(\chi(\mathcal A_b)\le\delta\).

Conversely, let \(F\) be any finite subset of the infinite nuclear-operator
space and let \(\varepsilon>0\).  Choose common finite coordinate sets
\(C,D\), both containing \(1\), such that
\(\|S-P_CS Q_D\|_N<\varepsilon\) for every \(S\in F\), and choose
\(n\notin C\cup D\).  Compression to the rows and columns \(\{1,n\}\) is
contractive in nuclear norm.  The compression of \(P_CS Q_D\) belongs to
the \((1,1)\)-corner, so the compression of \(S\) is within \(\varepsilon\)
of \(\operatorname{ran}\Phi_{p,q}\).  Therefore, for every \(S\in F\),
\[
 \|T_n-S\|_N\ge\delta-\varepsilon.
\]
This proves \(\chi(\mathcal A_b)\ge\delta\).

For the tail equality, if either \(1\notin C\) or \(1\notin D\), then the
tail of \(LE_{11}\) has norm \(L>\|b\|_N\).  If \(1\in C\cap D\), choose
\(n\notin C\cup D\).  Since \(b\in\ker\Phi_{p,q}\), its \((1,1)\) entry is
zero, hence \(P_CT_nQ_D=0\), and the tail norm is \(\|b\|_N\).  This proves
the lower bound for \(\Tail\).  For the reverse bound, take increasing finite
sets \(C=D=\{1,\ldots,N\}\): the finitely many \(T_n\) supported inside the
corner have zero tail, and every remaining \(T_n\) has tail norm \(\|b\|_N\).
Thus \eqref{eq:two-equalities} holds.

Combining \eqref{eq:b}, \eqref{eq:two-equalities}, and
\eqref{eq:dual-limit} gives bounded sets with
\(\Tail_{p_m,q_m}(\mathcal A_{b_m})/\chi(\mathcal A_{b_m})\to3/2\).
No smaller universal constant can hold.  This finishes the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The reusable script \texttt{code/check\_corner\_constant.py} checks the
sign-flip identity on random matrices and exactly enumerates the four vertices
needed for the endpoint norms \(2\) and \(3\).  It reports endpoint ratio
\(1.5\).  These checks are diagnostic only; no numerical statement is used in
the proof.

The result determines the constant uniform over all \(p,q\).  It does not
determine the potentially smaller optimal constant for a fixed pair
\((p,q)\).  It also does not address the source paper's distinct problems on
\(C(K)\), compact operators, general \(C^*\)-algebras, or general von Neumann
preduals.

The run's registry, solution, attempt, and proof-gap indexes were searched by
arXiv id, exact title, ``constant 2 optimal,'' ``three halves,'' ``corner,''
and ``nuclear.''  Web/arXiv searches used the exact source sentence, source
title and authors, and close nuclear-operator constant phrases.  They found
the source paper but no later paper explicitly resolving this question or
containing this \(3/2\) argument.  This is a bounded novelty check, not a
claim of exhaustive bibliographic certification.

\textbf{Human-review recommendation.} Verify especially the orientation of
the trace-dual corner map and the two equalities in
\eqref{eq:two-equalities}.  Both are isolated above so the packet can be
audited without reconstructing the discovery process.
