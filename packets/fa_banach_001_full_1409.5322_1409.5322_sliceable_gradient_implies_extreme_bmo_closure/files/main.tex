\section*{Source question}

In Remark 6.4(3) of S. Geiss and J. Ylinen,
\emph{Decoupling on the Wiener Space, Related Besov Spaces, and Applications
to BSDEs}, arXiv:1409.5322, the authors ask for conditions on the BSDE
gradient \(Z\) which guarantee
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 M:=\int_0^\cdot c_s\,d\overline W_s
 \in \overline{L^\infty}^{\,\BMO_2}.
\end{equation}
Here \(c\) is the comparison coefficient constructed in the proof of their
Theorem 6.3. Membership in \eqref{eq:target} is the extreme case in which the
Dolean--Dade exponential of \(M\) satisfies reverse Holder inequalities for
every exponent. We give two answers: a quantitative sliceability criterion
and a broader exponential-energy criterion.

We use the source notation. In particular,
\(\slc_N^{S_2,\F}(u)\) is the \(N\)-sliceable number of a predictable process
\(u\), and \((s_N)\) is a non-increasing sequence such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sN}
 \slc_N^{S_2,\F}(|Z|^\theta)\leq s_N,
 \qquad s_\infty=\lim_{N\to\infty}s_N.
\end{equation}
The generator constant is \(L_Z\). The spaces \(L^\infty\) and \(H^\infty\)
denote respectively bounded continuous martingales and continuous
martingales with bounded terminal quadratic variation.

\section*{Full sufficient-condition theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Assume the setting and hypotheses of Theorem 6.3 of arXiv:1409.5322, with
\(0\leq\theta\leq1\), and let \(M\) be its comparison martingale.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item One always has the quantitative estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance}
 \dist_{\BMO_2}\!\left(M,
       \overline{L^\infty}^{\,\BMO_2}\right)
 \leq 2\sqrt2\,L_Zs_\infty.
\end{equation}
Consequently, sliceability of \(|Z|^\theta\), namely \(s_\infty=0\), implies
\eqref{eq:target}.

\item The conclusion holds automatically for \(\theta=0\). For
\(0<\theta\leq1\), it also holds whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:higher}
 Z\in\bmo(S_{2\eta})\qquad\hbox{for some }\eta>\theta.
\end{equation}
More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate}
 \slc_N^{S_2,\F}(|Z|^\theta)
 \leq \left(\frac TN\right)^{\frac12(1-\theta/\eta)}
       \|Z\|_{\bmo(S_{2\eta})}^{\theta}.
\end{equation}
Unlike the displayed example in the source, \eqref{eq:higher} allows
\(\eta>1\), and therefore includes the quadratic endpoint \(\theta=1\).

\item There is a sufficient condition which does not require
\(s_\infty=0\). Define, for \(\lambda>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \mathfrak C_\lambda(Z):=
 \sup_\tau\left\|
 \E\left[
   \exp\!\left(\lambda\int_\tau^T|Z_s|^{2\theta}\,ds\right)
   \middle|\F_\tau\right]\right\|_\infty,
\end{equation}
where the supremum is over stopping times. If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:allrates}
 \mathfrak C_\lambda(Z)<\infty\qquad\hbox{for every }\lambda>0,
\end{equation}
then \eqref{eq:target} holds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section*{The quantitative closure argument}

For continuous martingales, Corollary 3 of L. Yan and N. Kazamaki,
\emph{On the distance between \(\langle X\rangle\) and \(L^\infty\) in the
space of continuous BMO-martingales} (2005), gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inclusion}
 \overline{H^\infty}^{\,\BMO}
 \subseteq \overline{L^\infty}^{\,\BMO}.
\end{equation}
All martingale BMO norms are equivalent, so the closures in
\eqref{eq:inclusion} are unchanged when the norm is \(\BMO_2\).
Lemma 5.5(4) of the source, based on Schachermayer's characterization, says
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schach}
 \slc_\infty(M)=\dist_{\BMO_2}(M,H^\infty).
\end{equation}

The proof of source Theorem 6.3 establishes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sourcebound}
 \slc_{3N-2}^{S_2,\overline\F}(|c|)
 \leq \sqrt2 L_Z\left[\sqrt{\frac TN}+2s_N\right].
\end{equation}
The BMO norm of a stochastic integral is exactly the corresponding
\(\bmo(S_2)\) norm of its integrand. Hence the left side of
\eqref{eq:sourcebound} is \(\slc_{3N-2}(M)\). Letting \(N\to\infty\) and
using \eqref{eq:inclusion}--\eqref{eq:schach} gives
\[
 \dist_{\BMO_2}\!\left(M,
       \overline{L^\infty}^{\,\BMO_2}\right)
 \leq\dist_{\BMO_2}(M,H^\infty)
 =\slc_\infty(M)
 \leq2\sqrt2L_Zs_\infty,
\]
which proves Theorem \ref{thm:main}(i).

For part (ii), let \(0<\theta<\eta\), with no upper restriction on
\(\eta\). Conditional Holder followed by conditional Jensen gives, for a
deterministic interval \(I=(a,b]\),
\begin{align*}
 &\E\left[\int_t^T\mathbf1_I(s)|Z_s|^{2\theta}\,ds
              \middle|\F_t\right]\\
 &\quad\leq (b-a)^{1-\theta/\eta}
 \left(\E\left[\int_t^T\mathbf1_I(s)|Z_s|^{2\eta}\,ds
              \middle|\F_t\right]\right)^{\theta/\eta}.
\end{align*}
Taking essential suprema and square roots, and then choosing the equidistant
\(N\)-partition, proves \eqref{eq:rate}. Thus \(s_\infty=0\). The
\(\theta=0\) estimate
\(\slc_N^{S_2,\F}(1)\leq\sqrt{T/N}\) is immediate. This proves (ii).

\section*{A broader, direct extreme-class criterion}

We prove part (iii). The canonical decoupling identities used in steps (c)
and (d) of the source proof transfer \eqref{eq:energy} with the same constant
to each transformed gradient \(Z^\rho\),
\(\rho\in\{\varphi,\psi\}\), relative to the enlarged filtration.
The source comparison coefficient satisfies
\[
 |c_s|\leq\sqrt2L_Z
 \bigl(1+|Z_s^\psi|^\theta+|Z_s^\varphi|^\theta\bigr).
\]
Writing \(Q_\tau=\int_\tau^T|c_s|^2ds\), we therefore have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qbound}
 Q_\tau\leq6L_Z^2\left[T+
 \int_\tau^T|Z_s^\psi|^{2\theta}ds+
 \int_\tau^T|Z_s^\varphi|^{2\theta}ds\right].
\end{equation}
Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz and \eqref{eq:allrates} imply, for every
\(\lambda>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qexp}
 \sup_\tau\left\|\E[e^{\lambda Q_\tau}\mid\overline\F_\tau]
              \right\|_\infty
 \leq e^{6\lambda L_Z^2T}
       \mathfrak C_{12\lambda L_Z^2}(Z)<\infty.
\end{equation}
In particular \(M\in\BMO\).

Let \(R=\mathcal E(M)\) and fix \(q>1\). Since \(2qM\in\BMO\), its
stochastic exponential is a uniformly integrable martingale. For every
stopping time \(\tau\), Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\begin{align*}
 \E\left[\left(\frac{R_T}{R_\tau}\right)^q
       \middle|\overline\F_\tau\right]
 &=\E\left[e^{q(M_T-M_\tau)-\frac q2Q_\tau}
       \middle|\overline\F_\tau\right]\\
 &\leq
 \E\left[\frac{\mathcal E(2qM)_T}
                    {\mathcal E(2qM)_\tau}
       \middle|\overline\F_\tau\right]^{1/2}
 \E\left[e^{(2q^2-q)Q_\tau}
       \middle|\overline\F_\tau\right]^{1/2}\\
 &\leq
 \left[e^{6(2q^2-q)L_Z^2T}
 \mathfrak C_{12(2q^2-q)L_Z^2}(Z)\right]^{1/2}<\infty.
\end{align*}
Thus \(R\in\RH_q\) for every \(q>1\). Kazamaki's extreme-class
characterization, quoted immediately after Proposition 5.24 in the source,
is precisely that this is equivalent to
\(M\in\overline{L^\infty}^{\,\BMO_2}\). This proves (iii).

\section*{Strength, boundary, and novelty}

Condition \eqref{eq:allrates} genuinely reaches beyond the sliceable class.
Schachermayer's 1996 example has infinite conditional quadratic-variation
critical exponent, hence the every-rate property, but is not in the BMO
closure of \(H^\infty\). The new direct argument only seeks the larger
\(L^\infty\)-closure requested by the source.

An exact necessary condition expressed solely through \(|Z|\) cannot hold
uniformly over all generators: if the generator is independent of its
\(z\)-variable, then \(c=0\) and \eqref{eq:target} holds for every admissible
\(Z\). The theorem therefore answers the source's request at its natural
level---broad, checkable sufficient conditions---and adds a quantitative
distance estimate.
