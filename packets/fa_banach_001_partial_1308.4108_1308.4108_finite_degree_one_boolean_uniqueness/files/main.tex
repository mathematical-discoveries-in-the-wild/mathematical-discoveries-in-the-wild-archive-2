\paragraph{Status.}
Substantial partial result, likely valid.  For Boolean-valued limit objects
depending on finitely many degree-one coordinates, all affine-system densities
determine a canonical finite aperiodic quotient up to affine isomorphism.  The
general fractional, higher-degree, and infinite-coordinate question remains
open here.

\section{Source question}

The source is H.~Hatami, P.~Hatami, and J.~Hirst, \emph{Limits of Boolean
Functions on $\F_p^n$}, arXiv:1308.4108 \cite{source}.  In the concluding
remarks on source PDF page~12, it asks:

\begin{quote}
Suppose that two limit objects $\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2$ satisfy
$t_{\mathcal L}(\Gamma_1)=t_{\mathcal L}(\Gamma_2)$ for every affine system
of linear forms $\mathcal L$.  Then how do $\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_2$ relate
to each other?
\end{quote}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The source compares the desired relation with the common-factor
characterization of graphons.  The theorem below gives exactly such a common
finite factor in a natural deterministic cylinder subclass.

\section{Definitions and result}

Fix a prime $p$.  The source domain is
\[
 \G_\infty=
 \prod_{j\geq1}\prod_{0\leq k\leq\lfloor(j-1)/(p-1)\rfloor}
 \mathbb U_{k+1}^{\mathbb N}.
\]
A \emph{finite degree-one Boolean cylinder} is a measurable
$\Gamma:\G_\infty\to\{0,1\}$ of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cylinder}
 \Gamma(x)=f(\pi(x)),
 \qquad f:V\to\{0,1\},\quad V=\F_p^r,
\end{equation}
where $\pi$ selects finitely many coordinates of degree $1$ and depth $0$
(identifying $\mathbb U_1$ with $\F_p$).  Redundant selected coordinates are
allowed.

For a function $f:V\to\{0,1\}$ define its translation-period subgroup
\[
 H_f=\{h\in V:f(x+h)=f(x)\text{ for every }x\in V\}.
\]
It descends to a unique function
$\bar f:W_f:=V/H_f\to\{0,1\}$.  This descended function is
\emph{aperiodic}, meaning $H_{\bar f}=\{0\}$.  We call $(W_f,\bar f)$ the
\emph{minimal affine core} of the cylinder.

\begin{theorem}[Finite degree-one uniqueness]\label{thm:main}
Let $\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2$ be finite degree-one Boolean cylinders, with minimal
affine cores $(W_1,\bar f_1)$ and $(W_2,\bar f_2)$.  The following are
equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every affine system of linear forms $\mathcal L$,
\[
 t_{\mathcal L}(\Gamma_1)=t_{\mathcal L}(\Gamma_2).
\]
\item There is an affine bijection $T:W_1\to W_2$ such that
\[
 \bar f_1=\bar f_2\circ T.
\]
\item The two cylinders factor through a common finite affine Boolean object:
there are a finite affine space $W$, a Boolean function $h:W\to\{0,1\}$,
and surjective affine maps $q_i$ from their finite coordinate spaces to $W$
such that $f_i=h\circ q_i$ for $i=1,2$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the affine densities determine both the minimal core dimension and its
affine-isomorphism class.
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof intuition}

Use as a test system all points of $\F_p^m$.  On degree-one coordinates, a
consistent sample of this system is exactly a uniformly random affine map
$A:\F_p^m\to W$.  Since the cylinder is Boolean-valued, the source's
auxiliary Bernoulli sampling is deterministic, so the induced random object is
the entire restriction array $\bar f\circ A$.

The translation periods of this array expose the rank of $A$.  If $\bar f$ is
aperiodic and $A$ is onto, the period subgroup of $\bar f\circ A$ is exactly
the kernel of the linear part of $A$.  Consequently the smallest quotient
dimension is detected by the first dimension in which an aperiodic restriction
appears with positive probability.  At that dimension, the aperiodic
restrictions are precisely the affine orbit of $\bar f$.  Equal densities
give equal restriction laws, so they force equal dimensions and the same
affine orbit.

\section{Proof}

For $m\geq0$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fullsystem}
 \mathcal A_m=
 \{L_a:a\in\F_p^m\},
 \qquad
 L_a(x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_m)=x_0+\sum_{j=1}^m a_jx_j.
\end{equation}
This is the affine system consisting of every point of the affine $m$-cube.

\begin{lemma}[Restriction law]\label{lem:law}
Let $\Gamma$ have minimal core $\bar f:W\to\{0,1\}$.  The random Boolean
array on $\F_p^m$ induced by $\Gamma$ and $\mathcal A_m$ has the same law as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:array}
 (\bar f(Aa))_{a\in\F_p^m},
\end{equation}
where $A:\F_p^m\to W$ is a uniformly random affine map.

Moreover, if two limit objects have equal $t_{\mathcal L}$ for every affine
system $\mathcal L$, then their arrays \eqref{eq:array} have the same law for
every $m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each selected degree-one coordinate, consistency with
$\mathcal A_m$ means precisely that its values are
\[
 a\longmapsto c_0+\sum_{j=1}^m a_jc_j
\]
for uniformly random independent coefficients $c_0,\ldots,c_m\in\F_p$.
Combining the finitely many coordinates gives a uniformly random affine map
into the original coordinate space.  Passing through the quotient by $H_f$
still gives a uniformly random affine map into $W$, because a surjective
linear quotient sends uniform independent coefficients to uniform independent
coefficients.  Since $\Gamma$ takes only the values zero and one, the
independent Bernoulli variables in the source definition equal their
parameters almost surely.  This proves \eqref{eq:array}.

For the final assertion, let $X=(X_a)_{a\in\F_p^m}$ be an induced Boolean
array.  For each $\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\F_p^m}$, inclusion--exclusion gives
\begin{align*}
 \Pr[X=\omega]
 &=\mathbb E\!\left[
   \prod_{\omega(a)=1}X_a\prod_{\omega(a)=0}(1-X_a)\right]\\
 &=\sum_{T\subseteq\{a:\omega(a)=0\}}(-1)^{|T|}
   t_{\{L_a:\omega(a)=1\text{ or }a\in T\}}(\Gamma).
\end{align*}
Every system on the right is affine.  Equality of all source densities
therefore gives equality of every atom probability of the finite array law.
\end{proof}

For a function $g:\F_p^m\to\{0,1\}$ write
\[
 \Per(g)=\{v\in\F_p^m:g(x+v)=g(x)\text{ for every }x\in\F_p^m\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Periods detect full rank]\label{lem:period}
Let $h:W\to\{0,1\}$ be aperiodic, and let
$A(x)=b+Lx:\F_p^m\to W$ be affine.
Then
\[
 \ker L\subseteq\Per(h\circ A).
\]
If $L$ is surjective, equality holds.  In particular, when
$m=\dim W$, the restriction $h\circ A$ is aperiodic if and only if $L$ is
invertible.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first inclusion is immediate.  If $L$ is surjective and
$v\in\Per(h\circ A)$, then
\[
 h(y+Lv)=h(y)\qquad\text{for every }y\in W,
\]
because $b+Lx$ ranges over all of $W$.  Thus $Lv\in H_h=\{0\}$ and
$v\in\ker L$.  The last assertion follows because a square linear map is
surjective exactly when it is invertible.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Assume (1), and set $r_i=\dim W_i$.  Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
$r_1<r_2$, and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:law} with $m=r_2$.  Every linear map
$\F_p^{r_2}\to W_1$ has a nonzero kernel, so every restriction array from
$\bar f_1$ has a nonzero period.  For $\bar f_2$, the linear part is invertible
with positive probability
\[
 \frac{|\operatorname{GL}(r_2,p)|}{p^{r_2^2}}>0,
\]
and Lemma~\ref{lem:period} says that the corresponding array is aperiodic.
The two finite array laws cannot be equal, contradicting
Lemma~\ref{lem:law}.  By symmetry $r_1=r_2=:r$.

Use $m=r$.  For each core, Lemma~\ref{lem:period} says that the aperiodic
arrays are exactly those obtained from affine bijections
$A:\F_p^r\to W_i$.  Conditional on this positive-probability event, the
support of the array law is therefore the affine orbit
\[
 \{\bar f_i\circ A:A\in\Aff(\F_p^r,W_i)\text{ is bijective}\}.
\]
The conditional distribution is uniform on that orbit: the affine group acts
transitively, and every orbit element has the same number of representing
bijections (the stabilizer size).  Equality of the full array laws forces the
two orbit supports to coincide after identifying $W_i$ with $\F_p^r$.  Hence
there is an affine bijection $T:W_1\to W_2$ with
$\bar f_1=\bar f_2\circ T$.  This proves (2).

Statement (2) is equivalent to (3) by taking the common space to be either
minimal core and composing one quotient map with $T$ or $T^{-1}$.

Finally, (2) implies (1): composition with a fixed affine bijection sends a
uniform random affine map to a uniform random affine map.  Lemma~\ref{lem:law}
therefore gives identical restriction laws for every affine system, and in
particular identical densities.
\end{proof}

The constant-core case $r=0$ is included: the only cores are the constant
zero and constant one functions, and their one-point densities distinguish
them.

\section{Upgrade attempts and limitations}

The proof was pushed in two directions beyond the promoted theorem.

\paragraph{Fractional finite cylinders.}
If $\Gamma$ takes values in $[0,1]$, a random affine map produces a mixture of
independent product-Bernoulli arrays with parameter vector
$(\Gamma(Aa))_a$, not that parameter vector itself.  The event that the
parameter array has trivial period is no longer directly observable.  The
full-rank extraction above becomes a latent-mixture identifiability problem;
no unconditional separation argument was obtained.

\paragraph{Arbitrary Boolean measurable objects.}
Finite cylinders approximate such objects in measure, but the affine
identifications furnished at successive finite scales need not be coherent.
A full argument would require a coupling or inverse-limit theorem aligning
the finite quotient cores.

Candela, Gonz\'alez-S\'anchez, and Szegedy give a deep representation of
$\F_2^\omega$-affine-exchangeable laws and a universal 2-adic limit domain in
arXiv:2203.08915 \cite{CGS}.  They explicitly cite the source and clarify the
law-level landscape, but the bounded search did not locate a theorem turning
two particular source parameter functions with the same law into the direct
graphon-style common-factor relation asked here.  The present theorem is
independent of their machinery and works for every prime $p$.

\section{Verification and novelty}

The proof was checked at the three points where information could be lost:
the inclusion--exclusion reconstruction of the whole Boolean array law, the
period/kernel equality for surjective maps, and the isolation of the affine
orbit from the full-rank stratum.  No computation is used as proof.

The four lightweight run indexes had no entry for arXiv:1308.4108 or this
finite-cylinder classification.  Bounded primary-arXiv searches on 13 August
2026 used the exact title, exact uniqueness wording, finite-field function
limits, nilspace graphons, and affine exchangeability.  They found the source,
the 2014 thesis version, and arXiv:2203.08915, but no direct finite-core theorem
or full answer in the source parametrization.  Novelty confidence is moderate.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Review as a clean substantial partial theorem.  The main checks are the
identification of consistent degree-one samples with uniform affine maps and
the claim that equality of all $t_{\mathcal L}$ values supplies every subset
density needed by inclusion--exclusion.
