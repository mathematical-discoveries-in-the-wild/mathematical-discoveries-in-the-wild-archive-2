\noindent\textbf{Status.}
\emph{Literature-implied answer (complete real simplex tight-frame
subfamily).}  The exact formula below is an agent-identified translation of
Cheon--Shader's extremal theorem for sparse orthogonal matrices.  It is not a
claim of a new theorem.  The translation settles the global optimal-sparsity
problem for every real unit-norm tight frame of $n+1$ vectors in $\R^n$.

\section{Source problem and later algorithmic status}

Casazza et al., \emph{Optimally Sparse Frames}
(arXiv:1009.3663), Section 5, printed pages 10--11 (PDF pages 10--11), asks
whether and how Spectral Tetris extends when some eigenvalues are below $2$.
It notes that larger DFT blocks do not always give globally optimally sparse
frames, even for tight frames, and asks for an extension appropriate ``in the
sense of optimal sparsity.''  The two source pages are reproduced below.

Casazza--Fickus--Heinecke--Wang--Zhou (arXiv:1108.4061) subsequently removed
the spectral restriction.  Their Theorem 3.12 constructs a unit-norm frame
with any positive prescribed spectrum satisfying the trace condition.  Their
Section 3.2 explicitly warns, however, that the general construction does not
in general produce the sparsest possible frame.  Thus the construction part
of the source question was answered, while global optimal sparsity remained a
separate issue.

\section{Exact simplex result}

For a synthesis matrix $F$, write $\spn(F)$ for its number of nonzero
entries.  Changing the ambient orthonormal basis left-multiplies $F$ by an
orthogonal matrix, so minimizing over all frames in the standard basis is
equivalent to minimizing the source's basis-relative sparsity.

\begin{theorem}[Exact simplex sparsity]
Let $n\geq 1$, let $m=n+1$, and put
\[
 r=\lceil\log_2m\rceil.
\]
Among all real unit-norm tight frames of $m$ vectors in $\R^n$, the minimum
number of nonzero synthesis-matrix entries is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simplex}
 \sigma(m)=m(r+1)-2^r.
\end{equation}
A balanced (generally non-dyadic) Haar basis of the zero-sum hyperplane
$\mathbf 1^\perp\subset\R^m$ attains the minimum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $F\in\R^{(m-1)\times m}$ be a unit-norm tight-frame synthesis matrix and
set
\[
 U=\sqrt{\frac{m-1}{m}}F.
\]
Then $UU^{\mathsf T}=I_{m-1}$.  The matrix $P=U^{\mathsf T}U$ is a rank
$m-1$ orthogonal projection whose diagonal entries are all $(m-1)/m$.
Consequently $I-P=vv^{\mathsf T}$ for a unit vector $v$ with
$|v_j|=m^{-1/2}$ for every $j$.  Choose a diagonal sign matrix $D$ such that
$D\mathbf 1/\sqrt m=v$.  The matrix
\[
 Q=\begin{bmatrix}UD\\ m^{-1/2}\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\end{bmatrix}
\]
is orthogonal, has a full constant last row, and satisfies
$\spn(Q)=\spn(F)+m$.

Conversely, if an orthogonal $m\times m$ matrix has last row
$m^{-1/2}\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}$ and first $m-1$ rows $U$, then
$F=\sqrt{m/(m-1)}U$ is a unit-norm tight-frame synthesis matrix: its rows are
orthogonal with the required common square norm, and every column has norm
one.  Therefore the frame problem is exactly the orthogonal-matrix problem,
with the $m$ entries of the constant row removed.

Cheon--Shader's main extremal theorem for orthogonal matrices with a full row
(equivalently, after transposition, a full column) states that the minimum
number of nonzero entries is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cheon}
 f(m)=(\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor+3)m
       -2^{\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor+1}
     =m(r+2)-2^r.
\end{equation}
Their extremizers are Haar-type matrices, and the bound is attained with the
full row constant.  Subtracting that row gives
$\sigma(m)=f(m)-m$, which is \eqref{eq:simplex}.

For completeness, the matching construction can be described directly.
Take a full binary tree with $m$ leaves whose leaf depths differ by at most
one.  For every internal node with leaf set $S=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B$, use the
unit vector supported on $S$ which is constant on $A$, constant with opposite
sign on $B$, and has mean zero.  These $m-1$ vectors form an orthonormal basis
of $\mathbf 1^\perp$.  If $r=\lceil\log_2m\rceil$, the tree has
$2^r-m$ leaves at depth $r-1$ and $2m-2^r$ leaves at depth $r$.  The total
support of its internal-node vectors equals the sum of the leaf depths,
\[
 (2^r-m)(r-1)+(2m-2^r)r=m(r+1)-2^r.
\]
Thus the construction realizes the lower bound with a constant full row.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences for the low-redundancy question}

The tight-frame eigenvalue is $m/(m-1)<2$ whenever $m\geq3$, precisely the
regime excluded by the source paper's optimality theorem.

If one formally extrapolates the source's high-redundancy tight-frame formula
$N+2(n-\gcd(N,n))$ to $N=m$, $n=m-1$, it becomes $3m-4$.  The exact simplex
formula agrees with it for $m=2,3,4$ and is strictly larger for every
$m\geq5$.  Indeed,
\[
 \sigma(m)-(3m-4)=m(r-2)-2^r+4>0\qquad(m\geq5).
\]
Hence the high-redundancy formula cannot extend verbatim below redundancy
$2$, even in the most rigid real tight-frame family.

For the first new case, $m=5$ and $n=4$, the exact optimum is $12$, whereas
the formal extrapolation is $11$.  An optimal orthonormal basis of
$\mathbf 1^\perp\subset\R^5$ consists of the four vectors supported,
respectively, on
\[
 \{1,2,3,4,5\},\quad \{1,2\},\quad \{3,4,5\},\quad \{4,5\},
\]
with the balanced Haar coefficients.  Scaling these rows by $\sqrt{5/4}$
gives the desired five-vector unit-norm tight frame in $\R^4$ with
$5+2+3+2=12$ nonzero entries.

\section{Provenance, limitations, and review focus}

The source paper does not cite Cheon--Shader, and Cheon--Shader predates the
frame question.  The relation is the projection-completion identification in
the proof above.  The supporting authors did not know they were answering the
source problem.

The exact result is limited to real simplex frames ($N=n+1$).  It does not
settle optimal sparsity for arbitrary spectra below $2$, for general
low-redundancy tight frames, for complex frames, or for the source's other
questions about equiangularity and compressibility.  A bounded search covered
the four run indexes, exact-title and formula searches, arXiv:1108.4061, and
the later frame survey literature.  No frame-theory source explicitly making
this simplex/Haar identification was found, but the decisive extremal theorem
has been known since 2000.  The packet is therefore classified by literature
provenance, not as a new partial solution.

Human review should check the equivalence between simplex frames and
orthogonal completions with a constant row, and the use of the exact
Cheon--Shader extremal formula.  The latter is independently restated on page
213 of Cheon--Hwang--Rim--Shader--Song (2003), which attributes it to the 2000
Haar paper.
