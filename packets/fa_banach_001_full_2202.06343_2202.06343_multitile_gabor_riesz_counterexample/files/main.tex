\begin{abstract}
Let
\[
  \Omega=[0,2)\cup[3,4),\qquad \Lambda=\Gamma=\Z.
\]
Then $\Omega$ is a level-three multi-tile, but not a tile, with respect to
$\Z$. Nevertheless, the critical Gabor system
$\cG(\chi_\Omega,\Z\times\Z)$ is a Riesz basis for $L^2(\R)$. On the Zak
unit square its multiplier is $1+z+z^3$, and
\[
 \frac{47-14\sqrt7}{27}\leq |1+z+z^3|^2\leq 9
 \qquad (|z|=1).
\]
This gives a positive answer to Problem 6.2, even with $\Gamma$ a lattice,
and a negative answer to the intended tiling-necessity question in Problem
6.1. It also contradicts Theorems 1.3 and 1.4 of the source as stated. The
source proof incorrectly promotes an almost-everywhere multi-tiling property
to continuity of the Zak transform across lattice-cell boundaries.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definitions and proof intuition}

For $g\in L^2(\R)$ and $m,n\in\Z$, set
\[
  M_mg(t)=e^{2\pi i mt}g(t),\qquad T_ng(t)=g(t-n).
\]
The critical integer Gabor system is
$\cG(g,\Z\times\Z)=\{M_mT_ng:m,n\in\Z\}$.
A sequence $(f_j)$ is a Riesz basis if it is the image of an orthonormal
basis under a boundedly invertible operator. Equivalently, it is complete
and there are $0<A\leq B<\infty$ such that
\[
 A\sum_j|c_j|^2\leq\left\|\sum_jc_jf_j\right\|_2^2
 \leq B\sum_j|c_j|^2
\]
for every finitely supported scalar family $(c_j)$.

The Zak transform, with the convention used in the source, is
\[
  Zf(x,\xi)=\sum_{k\in\Z}f(x+k)e^{2\pi i k\xi},
  \qquad (x,\xi)\in[0,1)^2.
\]
It extends to a unitary map from $L^2(\R)$ onto $L^2([0,1)^2)$.
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covariance}
 Z(M_mT_nf)(x,\xi)=e^{2\pi i(mx+n\xi)}Zf(x,\xi).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Intuition.}
If $\Omega$ is made from finitely many integer translates of $[0,1)$, then
$Z\chi_\Omega$ is a finite $0/1$ Laurent polynomial in
$z=e^{2\pi i\xi}$. At critical density, equation \eqref{eq:covariance}
says that the Gabor system is exactly the standard Fourier basis of the Zak
square multiplied by this polynomial. It is therefore a Riesz basis if and
only if the polynomial never vanishes on the unit circle. Two-term masks
always vanish, but the three-term mask $1+z+z^3$ does not.

\section{A finite-mask criterion}

For a nonempty finite set $A\subset\Z$, define, modulo null endpoints,
\[
  \Omega_A=\bigcup_{a\in A}([0,1)+a),
  \qquad p_A(z)=\sum_{a\in A}z^a.
\]

\begin{proposition}[finite lattice-fiber masks]\label{prop:mask}
The set $\Omega_A$ multi-tiles $\R$ by $\Z$ at level $|A|$. Furthermore,
\[
 \cG(\chi_{\Omega_A},\Z\times\Z)
 \text{ is a Riesz basis for }L^2(\R)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 p_A(z)\neq0\ \text{for every }z\in\T.
\]
When these conditions hold, the optimal squared Riesz bounds are
\[
 A_0=\min_{|z|=1}|p_A(z)|^2,
 \qquad B_0=\max_{|z|=1}|p_A(z)|^2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix a noninteger $t$ and write $t=x+j$ with $0<x<1$ and $j\in\Z$.
For each $a\in A$, precisely the translate
$\Omega_A+(j-a)$ contains $t$. These $|A|$ translates are distinct, and no
other integer translate contains $t$. Hence the multi-tiling level is $|A|$
almost everywhere.

For $0\leq x<1$, again away from irrelevant endpoints,
\[
 Z\chi_{\Omega_A}(x,\xi)
 =\sum_{k\in\Z}\chi_{\Omega_A}(x+k)e^{2\pi i k\xi}
 =\sum_{a\in A}e^{2\pi i a\xi}
 =p_A(e^{2\pi i\xi}).
\]
Let $E_{m,n}(x,\xi)=e^{2\pi i(mx+n\xi)}$. The family
$(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\Z}$ is an orthonormal basis of $L^2([0,1)^2)$.
By \eqref{eq:covariance}, the Zak transform carries the proposed Gabor
system to $(p_A(e^{2\pi i\xi})E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\Z}$.

If $p_A$ is zero-free on $\T$, compactness gives $0<A_0\leq B_0<\infty$.
Thus multiplication by $p_A(e^{2\pi i\xi})$ is boundedly invertible on the
Zak square. Its image of the Fourier orthonormal basis is a Riesz basis,
with the displayed bounds. Conversely, if $p_A$ has a unit-circle zero,
continuity makes its essential infimum zero. Multiplication by it is not
bounded below, so its image of the Fourier basis cannot be a Riesz basis.
\end{proof}

\section{The counterexample}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:counterexample}
Let
\[
 \Omega=[0,2)\cup[3,4),\qquad \Lambda=\Gamma=\Z,
 \qquad g=\chi_\Omega.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Omega$ multi-tiles $\R$ by $\Lambda$ at level three and does not tile
      $\R$ by $\Lambda$ at level one;
\item $\cG(g,\Lambda\times\Gamma)$ is a Riesz basis for $L^2(\R)$;
\item its optimal squared Riesz bounds are
\[
  \frac{47-14\sqrt7}{27}\quad\text{and}\quad 9.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Consequently Problem 6.2 has a positive answer, even with $\Gamma$ a lattice,
and tiling by $\Lambda$ is not necessary in Problem 6.1.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Here $\Omega=\Omega_A$ for $A=\{0,1,3\}$, so Proposition
\ref{prop:mask} gives the level-three assertion and reduces the Riesz-basis
claim to the polynomial
\[
 p(z)=1+z+z^3.
\]
Suppose $z=e^{i\theta}$ and $p(z)=0$. Taking imaginary parts yields
\[
 0=\sin\theta+\sin3\theta
  =2\sin2\theta\cos\theta
  =4\sin\theta\cos^2\theta.
\]
Thus $\theta$ is congruent modulo $2\pi$ to one of
$0,\pi,\pi/2,3\pi/2$. Direct substitution gives, respectively,
$p(z)=3,-1,1,1$, a contradiction. Hence $p$ is zero-free on $\T$, proving
the Riesz-basis assertion.

For the exact bounds, put $c=\cos\theta$. A direct expansion gives
\begin{align*}
 |1+e^{i\theta}+e^{3i\theta}|^2
 &=3+2(\cos\theta+\cos2\theta+\cos3\theta)\\
 &=8c^3+4c^2-4c+1=:F(c).
\end{align*}
On $[-1,1]$, the only interior critical points are
\[
 c_\pm=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt7}{6}.
\]
Using $6c_\pm^2+2c_\pm-1=0$ gives
\[
 F(c_+)=\frac{47-14\sqrt7}{27},\qquad
 F(c_-)=\frac{47+14\sqrt7}{27}.
\]
Together with $F(-1)=1$ and $F(1)=9$, this proves the displayed minimum and
maximum. Finally, the level-three count rules out level-one tiling by the
same lattice $\Lambda=\Z$.
\end{proof}

\section{Why the source's obstruction does not apply}

The example is not merely outside Theorem 1.3 of the source: for $M=N=1$ it
meets that theorem's stated hypotheses and contradicts its conclusion.
The same is true of the equivalence asserted in Theorem 1.4.

The standard critical-density statement used by the source is sound: if a
Zak transform is continuous on all of $\R^2$ with the usual
quasi-periodicity, then it must vanish. But an arbitrary multi-tiling
indicator need not have a continuous Zak transform. In the present example,
for $0<x<1$ its lattice fiber is $\{0,1,3\}$, whereas for $1<x<2$ the fiber
is $\{-1,0,2\}$. Thus
\[
 Zg(x,\xi)=p(e^{2\pi i\xi})\quad(0<x<1),
\]
while crossing the boundary $x=1$ changes the expression by the nontrivial
quasi-periodic phase $e^{-2\pi i\xi}$. Except at integer $\xi$, the two
one-sided limits differ.

The source's Lemma 5.3 argues, after saying \emph{without loss of generality,
assume that $\Omega$ is open}, that the finite lattice fiber is locally
constant at every point. Neither step follows from an almost-everywhere
multi-tiling hypothesis. Replacing $\Omega$ by the open representative
$(0,2)\cup(3,4)$ changes no $L^2$ or tiling assertion, but the fiber still
jumps at integer cell boundaries. The claimed continuity in Theorem 5.4
therefore fails, and the zero theorem cannot be invoked.

\section{Independent checks, novelty, and limitations}

The companion script \texttt{verify\_counterexample.py} checks the exact
critical values symbolically, samples the multi-tiling count away from the
null boundary set, and performs a million-point numerical audit of the mask.
The proof above is independent of that computation.

The polynomial $1+z+z^3$ and the same lower bound occur in an unrelated
positive-definite Toeplitz quadratic-form example in arXiv:math/0507185.
Searches of the local arXiv corpus and the web by the source title, exact
problem wording, and mask polynomial found no published correction of
arXiv:2202.06343 or use of this mask to answer Problems 6.1--6.2. This is a
novelty search, not a proof of novelty.

The packet does not address Problem 6.3 about balls, does not classify all
separable Gabor Riesz bases, and does not claim that every level-$k$
multi-tile works. Because the result directly challenges stated theorems in
the source preprint, a specialist should verify the Zak convention and then
notify the authors before dissemination.
