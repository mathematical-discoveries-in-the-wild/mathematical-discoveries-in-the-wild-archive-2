\section{Source conjecture and provenance}

Mu\~noz-Fern\'andez, Pellegrino, and Seoane-Sep\'ulveda introduced explicit
Bohnenblust--Hille upper estimates involving an even-indexed gamma product
$r_n$, but left its convergence unproved \cite{MPS}.  Serrano-Rodr\'iguez
subsequently corrected a finite-index imprecision and, on PDF page 5,
conjectured that the corrected product satisfies \cite{SR}
\[
             \lim_{n\to\infty}r_n
             =\frac{\e^{1-\gamma/2}}{\sqrt2},
\]
where $n$ tends to infinity through the even integers and $\gamma$ is the
Euler--Mascheroni constant.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{The complete conjecture on source PDF page 5 of arXiv:1205.4735.}
\end{figure}

For even $n>14$, the corrected formula (equation (2.3) of the source) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected}
 r_n=\frac{
 \pi^{(n^2-196)/(8n)}}{
 2^{(n^2-2n-192)/(4n)}
 \left[\displaystyle\prod_{k=7}^{(n-2)/2}
 \Gamma\!\left(\frac{6k+1}{4k+2}\right)^{2k+1}
 \right]^{1/n}}.
\end{equation}

\section{Proof intuition}

The apparently complicated product is a Ces\`aro average in disguise.  The
gamma arguments obey
\[
       \frac{6k+1}{4k+2}=\frac32-\frac1{2k+1}.
\]
After inserting $\Gamma(3/2)=\sqrt\pi/2$, all terms of macroscopic size cancel
exactly.  Each remaining summand is
\[
 (2k+1)\left[
 \log\Gamma\!\left(\frac32-\frac1{2k+1}\right)
 -\log\Gamma\!\left(\frac32\right)\right],
\]
which tends to $-\psi(3/2)$.  Its average therefore gives the limit, and the
next Taylor term gives the sharper $-(\psi_1(3/2)/4)(\log n)/n$ correction.
The 2012 correction deletes only six product factors and changes powers by
$O(1/n)$ in the logarithm, so it cannot affect the limit or its logarithmic
leading error.

\section{Full theorem}

Write $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$ and $\psi_1=\psi'$.  Let $r_n$ be the corrected
quantity in \eqref{eq:corrected}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
As $n\to\infty$ through the even integers,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refined}
 \log r_n
 =1-\frac\gamma2-\frac12\log2
  -\frac{\psi_1(3/2)}{4n}\log n+O\!\left(\frac1n\right).
\end{equation}
Consequently
\[
             \lim_{n\to\infty}r_n
             =\frac{\e^{1-\gamma/2}}{\sqrt2}.
\]
The same asymptotic expansion holds for the original, uncorrected product in
arXiv:1107.4814.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
It is convenient first to use the original product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:old}
 \widetilde r_n=
 \frac{\pi^{(n^2-4)/(8n)}}{
 2^{(n-2)/4}
 \left[\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{(n-2)/2}
 \Gamma\!\left(\frac{6k+1}{4k+2}\right)^{2k+1}
 \right]^{1/n}}.
\end{equation}
Put $m=(n-2)/2$ and
\[
 f(x)=\log\Gamma\!\left(\frac32-x\right),\qquad f_0=f(0).
\]
Since $\Gamma(3/2)=\sqrt\pi/2$ and
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{m}(2k+1)=m(m+2)=\frac{n^2-4}{4},
\]
the powers of $\pi$ and the constant gamma contributions cancel exactly in
the logarithm of \eqref{eq:old}.  What remains is the identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cancel}
 \log\widetilde r_n
 =\frac{n-2}{2n}\log2
  -\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^{m}(2k+1)
  \left[f\!\left(\frac1{2k+1}\right)-f_0\right].
\end{equation}

Taylor's theorem at zero gives, uniformly as $t\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:taylor}
 t\,[f(1/t)-f_0]
 =f'(0)+\frac{f''(0)}{2t}+O(t^{-2}).
\end{equation}
Here
\[
 f'(0)=-\psi(3/2),\qquad f''(0)=\psi_1(3/2).
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac1{2k+1}=\frac12\log n+O(1),
 \qquad
 \sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac1{(2k+1)^2}=O(1).
\]
Substitution of \eqref{eq:taylor} in \eqref{eq:cancel}, using
$m/n=1/2-1/n$, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:old-expansion}
 \log\widetilde r_n
 =\frac12\log2+\frac12\psi(3/2)
  -\frac{\psi_1(3/2)}{4n}\log n+O(n^{-1}).
\end{equation}
The classical identity
\[
          \psi(3/2)=2-\gamma-2\log2
\]
turns the constant in \eqref{eq:old-expansion} into
$1-\gamma/2-(\log2)/2$.

It remains to check the corrected formula rather than merely the original
one.  Direct comparison of \eqref{eq:corrected} and \eqref{eq:old} gives the
exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-correction}
 \log r_n-\log\widetilde r_n=\frac{\kappa}{n},
\end{equation}
where the constant independent of $n$ is
\[
 \kappa=48\log2-24\log\pi
 +\sum_{k=1}^{6}(2k+1)
 \log\Gamma\!\left(\frac32-\frac1{2k+1}\right).
\]
Indeed, the corrected powers differ by $-24/n$ for $\pi$ and $-48/n$
in the denominator exponent of $2$, while its product omits precisely the
indices $1,\ldots,6$.  Equation \eqref{eq:finite-correction} transfers
\eqref{eq:old-expansion} unchanged at its displayed precision and proves the
theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{Upgrade: the formerly conditional ratio limit}

The source arXiv:1107.4814 also considered the real recursive estimates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
 C_2=\sqrt2,\quad C_3=2^{5/6},\qquad
 C_n=\sqrt2\left(\frac{C_{n-2}}
 {A_{(2n-4)/(n-1)}^2}\right)^{(n-2)/n}\quad(n>3),
\end{equation}
where $A_p$ is the sharp lower Khinchin constant.  The paper derived a
one-step ratio limit conditional on convergence of its $r_n$.  The same local
calculation proves a stronger unconditional asymptotic for the entire
recursive sequence.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:recurrence}
For the estimates in \eqref{eq:recurrence},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-asymptotic}
 \log C_n
 =\frac n8\log2+1-\frac\gamma2-\frac14\log2
  -\frac{\psi_1(3/2)}{4n}\log n+O\!\left(\frac1n\right).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\[
 \frac{C_n}{2^{n/8}}\longrightarrow
 \frac{\e^{1-\gamma/2}}{2^{1/4}},
 \qquad
 \frac{C_n}{C_{n-1}}\longrightarrow2^{1/8}.
\]
These statements concern the explicit upper estimates $C_n$, not the unknown
optimal Bohnenblust--Hille constants.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Set $s_n=n\log C_n$.  Once
$p_n=(2n-4)/(n-1)$ lies in Haagerup's gamma branch,
\[
 A_{p_n}=\sqrt2\left(
 \frac{\Gamma(3/2-1/(n-1))}{\sqrt\pi}
 \right)^{1/p_n}.
\]
All earlier indices contribute only a bounded parity-dependent constant to
$s_n$.  Multiplying the logarithm of \eqref{eq:recurrence} by $n$ and using
$f_0=\frac12\log\pi-\log2$ gives, for all sufficiently large $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:increment}
 s_n-s_{n-2}
 =\left(\frac n2+1\right)\log2
 -(n-1)\left[f\!\left(\frac1{n-1}\right)-f_0\right].
\end{equation}
Sum \eqref{eq:increment} separately on the even and odd subsequences.  On
either parity,
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{j\le n}\left(\frac j2+1\right)
   &=\frac{n^2}{8}+\frac{3n}{4}+O(1),\\
 \#\{j\le n\}&=\frac n2+O(1),\\
 \sum_{j\le n}\frac1{j-1}&=\frac12\log n+O(1),
\end{align*}
where all sums are restricted to the relevant parity and may begin at any
fixed sufficiently large index.  Applying \eqref{eq:taylor} in
\eqref{eq:increment} therefore gives
\[
 s_n=\frac{n^2}{8}\log2
 +n\left(\frac34\log2-\frac12f'(0)\right)
 -\frac{f''(0)}4\log n+O(1).
\]
Divide by $n$, insert $f'(0)=-\psi(3/2)$ and
$f''(0)=\psi_1(3/2)$, and use the displayed digamma identity.  This is
\eqref{eq:C-asymptotic}.  Its error tends to zero, so subtracting the formulas
for $n$ and $n-1$ proves the ratio limit.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The proof uses only exact algebra, Taylor's theorem for $\log\Gamma$ near
$3/2$, the elementary harmonic-sum asymptotic, and the standard identities
$\Gamma(3/2)=\sqrt\pi/2$ and
$\psi(3/2)=2-\gamma-2\log2$.  The included verifier checks the finite-correction
identity to high precision, the predicted first-order error, and the recursive
corollary.  These computations are sanity checks, not proof steps.

The result completely answers the explicit conjecture in arXiv:1205.4735 and
removes the convergence hypothesis attached to the classical recursive
estimates in arXiv:1107.4814.  It does \emph{not} determine the optimal
Bohnenblust--Hille constants; later work already replaced these classical
estimates by much slower-growing families.

A bounded novelty audit on 11 August 2026 searched the run's four lightweight
indexes; exact web phrases containing the conjectured constant and $r_n$;
Crossref; OpenAlex's eight works then recorded as citing DOI
\texttt{10.1016/j.laa.2012.11.028}; and locally available arXiv full text for
the citing Bohnenblust--Hille corpus (including arXiv:1205.2385, 1207.0124,
1302.3026, 1503.07306, 1506.00159, and 1604.06323).  No proof of the exact
gamma-product conjecture was found.  ArXiv:1108.1550 computes the related
single-factor Khinchin limit that explains the same constant, but does not
state or prove convergence of this product.  Novelty is plausible, not
certified.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Check the exact cancellation in \eqref{eq:cancel}, the factor $1/4$ in the
trigamma correction, and the finite correction \eqref{eq:finite-correction}.
For the upgrade, independently check the increment identity
\eqref{eq:increment}.  If these pass, classify the conjecture itself as fully
solved and the recursive asymptotic as a valid strengthening.
