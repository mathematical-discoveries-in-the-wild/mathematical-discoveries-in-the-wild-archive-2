\noindent\textbf{Status.} Candidate partial result, likely valid. This note

\section{Source question}

Skalski and Viselter ask in Question 2.20 of \cite{skalskiviselter}, PDF
page 31, whether the following converse is true. If $(\mu_i)$ is a net of
states on $C_0^u(\G)$ and
\[
  R_{\mu_i}\longrightarrow \id
  \quad\text{in }B(L^\infty(\G))\text{-norm},
\]
must $\mu_i\to\epsilon$ in $C_0^u(\G)^*$-norm? Here $\epsilon$ is the
universal counit. The source observes that the answer is affirmative when
$\G$ is co-amenable.

\section{Co-commutative specialization}

Let $H$ be a locally compact group and put $\G=\widehat H$. Then
\[
  C_0^u(\widehat H)=C^*(H),
  \qquad L^\infty(\widehat H)=VN(H).
\]
Write $u_s\in M(C^*(H))$ for the canonical universal unitary and
$\lambda_s\in VN(H)$ for the left-regular unitary associated with $s\in H$.
If $\mu$ is a state on $C^*(H)$, its strictly continuous extension to the
multiplier algebra defines the normalized continuous positive-definite
function
\[
  \varphi(s)=\mu(u_s).
\]
Since the coproduct is group-like on the canonical unitaries, the reduced
right convolution operator satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplier}
  R_\mu(\lambda_s)=\varphi(s)\lambda_s \qquad(s\in H).
\end{equation}

\section{Idea of the proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:multiplier} converts norm closeness of $R_\mu$ to the
identity into uniform closeness of every positive-definite coefficient
$\varphi(s)$ to $1$. In the GNS representation, the orbit of the cyclic
vector therefore lies in a thin spherical cap. The unique minimum-norm point
in the closed convex hull of that orbit is fixed by the whole group. More
precisely, it is the orthogonal projection of the cyclic vector onto the
invariant subspace, and the spherical-cap estimate forces this projection to
have squared norm at least $1-\delta$.

Normalizing the projection realizes the counit as a vector state inside the
same representation as $\mu$. The elementary distance formula for two vector
states then gives a $2\sqrt\delta$ bound. No averaging measure or invariant
mean is used, so the argument remains valid when $H$ is nonamenable.

\section{Partial theorem and proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $H$ be a locally compact group. For every state $\mu$ on $C^*(H)$, let
$R_\mu$ be its reduced right convolution operator on $VN(H)$ and let
$\epsilon$ be the counit, equivalently the state induced by the trivial
representation of $H$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mainbound}
  \|\mu-\epsilon\|
  \le 2\sqrt{\|R_\mu-\id\|_{B(VN(H))}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set
\[
  \delta=\|R_\mu-\id\|_{B(VN(H))}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:multiplier} and $\|\lambda_s\|=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient}
  |\varphi(s)-1|
  =\|(R_\mu-\id)(\lambda_s)\|
  \le\delta
  \qquad(s\in H).
\end{equation}

Let $(\pi,K,\xi)$ be the GNS representation of $\mu$, with $\|\xi\|=1$, so
that $\varphi(s)=\langle\pi(u_s)\xi,\xi\rangle$. Let $\C$ be the closed
convex hull of the orbit $\{\pi(u_s)\xi:s\in H\}$. The Hilbert-space norm has
a unique minimum on the nonempty closed convex set $\C$; denote its minimizer
by $\eta$. Each $\pi(u_t)$ maps $\C$ onto itself and preserves the norm, so
uniqueness gives
\[
  \pi(u_t)\eta=\eta \qquad(t\in H).
\]
Thus $\eta$ is invariant.

Let $K^H$ be the invariant subspace and let $P$ be the orthogonal projection
onto $K^H$. For every $v\in K^H$, every orbit vector has the same inner
product with $v$ as $\xi$ does. By convexity and closure,
\[
  \langle\eta,v\rangle=\langle\xi,v\rangle
  \qquad(v\in K^H).
\]
Since $\eta\in K^H$, this identity says exactly that $\eta=P\xi$.

From \eqref{eq:coefficient}, every orbit vector $w=\pi(u_s)\xi$ satisfies
$\operatorname{Re}\langle w,\xi\rangle\ge1-\delta$. The same inequality
holds throughout $\C$, hence at $P\xi$. Because $P$ is an orthogonal
projection,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection}
  \|P\xi\|^2=\langle P\xi,\xi\rangle\ge1-\delta.
\end{equation}

Suppose first that $\delta<1$. Then $P\xi\ne0$, and
\[
  \zeta=\frac{P\xi}{\|P\xi\|}
\]
is a unit invariant vector. Its vector state on $C^*(H)$ is the state of the
trivial representation, hence it is $\epsilon$. Thus $\mu$ and $\epsilon$
are the restrictions to $\pi(C^*(H))$ of the vector states of $\xi$ and
$\zeta$. The distance of the restrictions is bounded by the distance of the
two vector states on $B(K)$. The latter is the trace norm of the difference
of the two rank-one projections and equals
\[
  2\sqrt{1-|\langle\xi,\zeta\rangle|^2}.
\]
Since $P$ is an orthogonal projection,
$|\langle\xi,\zeta\rangle|=\|P\xi\|$. Therefore \eqref{eq:projection} gives
\[
  \|\mu-\epsilon\|
  \le 2\sqrt{1-\|P\xi\|^2}
  \le 2\sqrt\delta.
\]

If $\delta\ge1$, then \eqref{eq:mainbound} follows from the elementary bound
$\|\mu-\epsilon\|\le2\le2\sqrt\delta$ for two states. This proves the
theorem in all cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:net}
For every locally compact group $H$ and every net $(\mu_i)$ of states on
$C^*(H)$,
\[
  R_{\mu_i}\longrightarrow\id
  \quad\text{in }B(VN(H))\text{-norm}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \mu_i\longrightarrow\epsilon
  \quad\text{in }C^*(H)^*\text{-norm}.
\]
Consequently Question 2.20 has an affirmative answer for every
co-commutative locally compact quantum group $\widehat H$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main} to each $\mu_i$ and pass to the limit.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification notes}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The proof uses no amenability hypothesis. In particular, if $H$ is
  nonamenable, then $\widehat H$ is not co-amenable, so the corollary goes
  beyond the case recorded in the source paper.
  \item The closed-convex-hull argument does not invoke a Følner net or an
  invariant mean. Invariance follows solely from uniqueness of the
  minimum-norm point.
  \item The identity $\eta=P\xi$ is not assumed: it follows because all orbit
  vectors, and therefore every point of $\C$, have the same inner products
  with invariant vectors.
  \item The vector-state estimate is applied first on $B(K)$ and then
  restricted to $\pi(C^*(H))$, so no purity assertion about the states on
  $C^*(H)$ is needed.
  \item No numerical or symbolic computation is used.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Verifier verdict:} likely valid. The two most useful checks
are the multiplier formula \eqref{eq:multiplier} under the source's right
convolution convention and the rank-one vector-state calculation in the last
paragraph of the proof.

\section{Scope and novelty status}

The argument depends crucially on the group-like unitaries $\lambda_s$ and
does not provide an analogous orbit for a general locally compact quantum
group. The full Question 2.20 therefore remains open in this packet.

On 11 August 2026, the local lightweight indexes were searched for the exact
arXiv id, the question, and its core terms. The locally parsed arXiv corpus was
searched for later citations to arXiv:1709.04873. Bounded official-arXiv
searches used the exact id, title, and combinations of ``right convolution'',
``counit'', ``operator norm'', ``co-amenable'', and ``co-commutative''. No
later paper explicitly answering Question 2.20 and no statement of the
quantitative co-commutative subcase was found. Novelty confidence is moderate,
because the argument is elementary after specialization to group duals.
