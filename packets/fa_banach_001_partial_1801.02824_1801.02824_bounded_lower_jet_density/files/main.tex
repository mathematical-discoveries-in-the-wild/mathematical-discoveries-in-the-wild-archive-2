\section{Source question and partial answer}

Nandi, Rajala, and Schultz prove that
$W^{k,\infty}(\Om)\cap C^\infty(\Om)$ is dense in the homogeneous space
$L^{k,p}(\Om)$ whenever $\Om\subset\R^2$ is bounded and simply connected
\cite{NRS}.  They obtain density in the full $W^{k,p}$ norm when $\Om$ is
additionally a $p$-Poincar\'e domain, and ask whether this extra assumption can
be dropped (PDF page 2, after Corollary 1.2).

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{The source question in arXiv:1801.02824, PDF page 2.}
\end{figure}

Here and below $W^{0,\infty}=L^\infty$.  We prove the following proper
subcase of the question.

\begin{theorem}[bounded-lower-jet density]\label{thm:main}
Let $k\in\mathbb N$, $1\leq p<\infty$, and let
$\Om\subset\R^2$ be a bounded simply connected domain.  Then
\[
 W^{k,\infty}(\Om)\cap C^\infty(\Om)
 \quad\hbox{is dense in}\quad
 W^{k,p}(\Om)\cap W^{k-1,\infty}(\Om)
\]
with respect to the $W^{k,p}(\Om)$ norm.
\end{theorem}

For $k=1$ this recovers only the bounded-data reduction inside the known full
first-order theorem.  The new content is for $k\geq2$: no Poincar\'e,
extension-domain, or boundary regularity assumption is imposed.

\section{Proof intuition}

The source approximant keeps the function on a compact Whitney core and puts
one moment-normalized polynomial on each complementary boundary tail.  At
order $k$, the polynomial itself disappears and only differences of nearby
polynomials remain; this is why the published proof is homogeneous.  At a
lower order, the polynomial survives on the entire tail and can be large far
from its anchoring square.

Boundedness of the complete lower jet neutralizes precisely this failure.
The moment conditions then bound every coefficient of every anchoring
polynomial, uniformly in the Whitney scale.  Since the domain is bounded, all
these polynomials and their lower derivatives are uniformly bounded on the
whole domain.  Their remaining $L^p$ contribution is supported in a boundary
tail whose measure tends to zero.  A variable-radius interior smoothing lemma
lets us make the datum smooth without losing the bounded lower jet.

\section{Two lemmas}

For a square $Q\Subset\Om$, let $P_Q=P_k(u,Q)$ be the polynomial of degree at
most $k-1$ determined by
\[
 \int_Q \D^\alpha(u-P_Q)=0,
 \qquad |\alpha|\leq k-1.                                      \tag{1}
\]
This is the normalization used in \cite{NRS}.

\begin{lemma}[uniform polynomial extrapolation]\label{lem:poly}
Suppose $u\in W^{k-1,\infty}(\Om)$ and $P_Q$ is defined by (1).  If
$d=\operatorname{diam}\Om$, then, for $0\leq m<k$,
\[
 \|\D^mP_Q\|_{L^\infty(\Om)}
 \leq C(k,d)\max_{|\gamma|\leq k-1}
               \|\D^\gamma u\|_{L^\infty(\Om)}.              \tag{2}
\]
The constant is independent of $Q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $c_Q$ and $\ell(Q)$ be the center and side length of $Q$.  Write $P_Q$ in
Taylor form about $c_Q$.  For $|\gamma|=k-1$, (1) says that the constant
$\D^\gamma P_Q$ is the average of $\D^\gamma u$ on $Q$, so its coefficient is
bounded by the right side of (2).  Descend on $|\gamma|$.  The average
$|Q|^{-1}\int_Q\D^\gamma P_Q$ equals
$|Q|^{-1}\int_Q\D^\gamma u$, while its difference from
$\D^\gamma P_Q(c_Q)$ is a fixed linear combination of already controlled
higher coefficients multiplied by powers of $\ell(Q)$.  Since
$\ell(Q)\leq d$, every Taylor coefficient is bounded by $C(k,d)$ times the
right side of (2).  Finally $|x-c_Q|\leq d$ for $x\in\Om$, and evaluation of
the Taylor polynomial gives (2).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[bounded-jet interior smoothing]\label{lem:smooth}
For every $u\in W^{k,p}(\Om)\cap W^{k-1,\infty}(\Om)$ there are
$v_j\in C^\infty(\Om)\cap W^{k,p}(\Om)\cap W^{k-1,\infty}(\Om)$ such that
$v_j\to u$ in $W^{k,p}(\Om)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a positive $r\in C^\infty(\Om)$ such that
$r(x)<\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Om)/4$ and every derivative of $r$ is
bounded.  Such an $r$ is obtained by composing a regularized distance with a
positive smooth function flat at zero and then rescaling.  For a standard
mollifier $\rho$ supported in the unit disk, set
\[
 v_\eta(x)=\int_{B(0,1)}\rho(y)u(x-\eta r(x)y)\,dy.              \tag{3}
\]
For small $\eta$, all sampled points lie in $\Om$, and the smooth kernel form
of (3) shows that $v_\eta\in C^\infty(\Om)$.

For fixed $y$, $F_{\eta,y}(x)=x-\eta r(x)y$ is uniformly bi-Lipschitz,
$F_{\eta,y}\to\mathrm{id}$ uniformly, and its Jacobian tends uniformly to
one.  Hence $f\circ F_{\eta,y}\to f$ in $L^p(\Om)$ for every $f\in L^p$,
uniformly for $|y|\leq1$ after the usual density argument.  Differentiating
(3) weakly through order $k$, the principal term at order $m$ is the average
of $(\D^m u)\circ F_{\eta,y}$ multiplied by tensors tending uniformly to the
identity.  Every other term contains a derivative of $\eta r$ and a derivative
of $u$ of order at most $m-1$.  Those coefficients are $O(\eta)$; for
$m\leq k$ the accompanying lower derivative is in $L^p$, and for
$m\leq k-1$ it is also bounded.  Dominated convergence therefore gives
$v_\eta\to u$ in $W^{k,p}$, while the same formula gives
$\|v_\eta\|_{W^{k-1,\infty}}<\infty$ for every fixed $\eta$.  Take
$\eta\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth} and a diagonal argument, it is enough to treat
\[
 u\in C^\infty(\Om)\cap W^{k,p}(\Om)\cap W^{k-1,\infty}(\Om).           \tag{4}
\]
Use exactly the Whitney cores $D_n$, boundary regions $H_i$, partition
$(\psi_i)_{i=0}^{l_n}$, associated squares $Q_i$, and polynomials
$P_i=P_{Q_i}$ from Sections 2--4 of \cite{NRS}, and put
\[
 u_n=u\psi_0+\sum_{i=1}^{l_n}\psi_iP_i.                               \tag{5}
\]
The source proves that $u_n\in C^\infty(\Om)\cap W^{k,\infty}(\Om)$,
$u_n=u$ on $D_{n-1}$, and
\[
                    \|\D^k(u_n-u)\|_{L^p(\Om)}\longrightarrow0.       \tag{6}
\]

We record what the same calculation gives below top order.  Let
$R_n=\Om\setminus D_{n-1}$ and let $R_n^*$ denote the union of the bounded-
overlap boundary-square chains used in equations (4.3)--(4.8) of \cite{NRS}.
Then $R_n,R_n^*$ escape every compact subset of $\Om$.  For $0\leq m<k$,
Leibniz' rule, the cutoff bounds
$|\D^a\psi_i|\lesssim2^{n|a|}$, the local polynomial Poincar\'e estimate,
and the bounded-length chaining lemma of the source give
\begin{align}
 \|\D^m(u_n-u)\|_{L^p(\Om)}
 &\leq C\|\D^m u\|_{L^p(R_n)}
       +C\Big(\sum_i\|\D^mP_i\|_{L^p(H_i)}^p\Big)^{1/p} \notag\\
 &\hspace{1.1cm}+C2^{-n(k-m)}\|\D^ku\|_{L^p(R_n^*)}.                  \tag{7}
\end{align}
Indeed, the last term contains all products in which a derivative lands on a
cutoff: after subtracting a neighboring polynomial, the chaining estimate
contributes $2^{-n(k-|\beta|)}$, while the cutoff contributes
$2^{n(m-|\beta|)}$.  The only terms not covered by this cancellation are the
plain $\D^mP_i$ terms displayed explicitly in (7).

Set
$M=\max_{|\gamma|\leq k-1}\|\D^\gamma u\|_{L^\infty(\Om)}$.  The regions
$H_i$ have overlap bounded independently of $n$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:poly}
therefore yields
\[
 \Big(\sum_i\|\D^mP_i\|_{L^p(H_i)}^p\Big)^{1/p}
 \leq C(k,\Om)M\,|R_n|^{1/p}.                                       \tag{8}
\]
The exhaustion $D_n\uparrow\Om$ implies $|R_n|\to0$ and absolute continuity
of the integral gives
$\|\D^m u\|_{L^p(R_n)}\to0$.  The final term in (7) is bounded by
$C2^{-n(k-m)}\|\D^ku\|_{L^p(\Om)}$ and also tends to zero.  Equations
(6)--(8), for every $m<k$, prove $u_n\to u$ in $W^{k,p}(\Om)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and status}

The theorem does not resolve the source question for arbitrary $W^{k,p}$
data.  Without a bounded lower jet, (8) is unavailable: a polynomial fixed on
a tiny associated square may extrapolate a large lower-order value throughout
a geometrically large tail.  This is exactly the obstruction that the global
Poincar\'e inequality suppresses in the source corollary.

Eight focused upgrade attempts tested direct lower-order estimates, abstract
jet density, room-and-corridor counterexamples, higher-order truncation,
intrinsic compatibility, and adaptive Whitney trees.  No full proof or
counterexample survived.  Bounded exact-phrase, citation, title, and author
searches through 13 August 2026 found no later resolution.  The 2021
Gromov-hyperbolic generalization remains homogeneous and retains a Poincar\'e
hypothesis for the full norm \cite{Nandi}.  The candidate theorem appears new,
but novelty confidence is only moderate pending expert literature review.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Check the lower-order bookkeeping leading to (7), especially the bounded
overlap of the regions used for the plain-polynomial term, and check the weak
chain rule in Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth}.  These are the two load-bearing points.
