\section{Source questions and current status}

Schneider records the open question whether every topological group is either
precompact or ambitable \cite[Section~5]{Schneider}.  In the formulation used
below, a group is ambitable if, for every continuous invariant pseudometric
$\Delta$, some $h\in\Ub(G)$ has
\[
 \BLip(\Delta)\subseteq\overline{\orb(h)}^{\,G},       \tag{1.1}
\]
where the bar denotes pointwise closure and $\BLip(\Delta)$ is the ball of
real-valued functions $f$ with $\lVert f\rVert_\infty\le1$ and
$|f(x)-f(y)|\le\Delta(x,y)$.  This is the standard Lipschitz-ball
characterization used by Pachl \cite{PachlBook,Pachl2026}.

The same section asks whether the Samuel compactification of every
nonprecompact group contains two disjoint closed invariant nonempty subsets.

Pachl's June 2026 note explicitly says that the first question remains open
and proves ambitability for one exceptional group: the finite-support
$\mathbb Z/2$-sum on $\omega_\omega$, layered by the cardinals $\aleph_k$
\cite{Pachl2026}.  The theorem below extracts and strengthens the exact
combinatorial mechanism of that construction.

\section{Idea of the proof}

The topology has a countable sequence of projection kernels.  Any continuous
invariant pseudometric is dominated by a step ultrametric on these kernels, so
it is enough to code finite Lipschitz patterns for one such metric.  At layer
$k+1$, a pattern must use a prescribed fiber in order to remain coherent with
its restriction to layer $k$.  The fiber has cardinality $\kappa_{k+1}$,
while all previously placed blocks forbid at most $\kappa_k$ points plus
fewer than $\kappa_{k+1}$ same-level points.  Strict cardinal growth leaves a
fresh translate.  Coherence then makes the disjoint-block code uniformly
continuous: nearby code points have the same lower projection, hence lie over
the same restricted pattern.  The orbit of its extension realizes all finite
patterns.

This proves a broad class of new examples, but it does not solve the full
source question: a general nonprecompact group need not possess coherent
homomorphic projections with successively larger fibers.

\section{The generalized layered theorem}

Let
\[
 \kappa_0<\kappa_1<\kappa_2<\cdots                     \tag{3.1}
\]
be arbitrary infinite cardinals.  Choose nested sets
$I_0\subset I_1\subset\cdots$ with $|I_k|=\kappa_k$, put
$I=\bigcup_k I_k$, and fix a nontrivial finite abelian group $A$.  Write
\[
 G=A^{(I)},\qquad G_k=A^{(I_k)},
\]
for the finite-support direct sums, and let $\pi_k:G\to G_k$ be coordinate
restriction.  For $x\ne0$ define
\[
 m(x):=\min\{k:\supp(x)\cap I_k\ne\varnothing\}.       \tag{3.2}
\]
Given any strictly decreasing positive sequence $s_k\to0$, set
\[
 \rho(x,y)=s_{m(x-y)}\quad(x\ne y),\qquad \rho(x,x)=0. \tag{3.3}
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The metric group $(G,\rho)$ is nonprecompact and ambitable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first occupied layer cannot move downward under addition, so $\rho$ is an
invariant ultrametric.  Moreover,
\[
 \rho(x,y)\le s_{k+1}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \pi_k(x)=\pi_k(y).                                  \tag{3.4}
\]

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 1: reduction to a step ultrametric.}
Let $\Delta$ be a continuous invariant pseudometric.  Replacing it by
$\min\{2,\Delta\}$ does not change the required conclusion.  Put $r_0=2$ and
for $k\ge1$ put
\[
 r_k:=\sup\{\Delta(0,x):\pi_{k-1}(x)=0\}.
\]
The projection kernels form a neighborhood basis at zero, so $r_k\downarrow0$.
Then $t_k=r_k+2^{-k}$ is strictly decreasing to zero.  The invariant step
ultrametric
\[
 d(x,y)=t_{m(x-y)}\quad(x\ne y)                       \tag{3.5}
\]
dominates $\Delta$.  Hence $\BLip(\Delta)\subseteq\BLip(d)$, and it suffices
to construct one orbit whose pointwise closure contains $\BLip(d)$.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 2: finite patterns.}
Fix a countable dense set $Q\subset[-1,1]$.  Let $\Phi$ be the family of
nonempty finite partial functions $\varphi:G\dashrightarrow Q$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $\varphi$ is the restriction of a function with sup norm at most two
 and Lipschitz constant at most two for $d$;
 \item $\pi_j(\operatorname{dom}\varphi)\subseteq
 \operatorname{dom}\varphi$ for every $j$.
\end{enumerate}
Put
\[
 \Phi_k=\{\varphi\in\Phi:\operatorname{dom}\varphi\subseteq G_k\}.
\]
Projection closure preserves finiteness: the projections of a finite-support
point change only at the finitely many layers occupied by its support.  Thus
every finite test set can be enlarged to a finite projection-closed one.
Rational approximation, followed by the real McShane extension, shows that a
family realizing every $\varphi\in\Phi$ is pointwise dense in $\BLip(d)$.
Also
\[
 |\Phi_k|=|G_k|=\kappa_k.                              \tag{3.6}
\]
The upper bound is finite-pattern counting.  For the lower bound, associate
to each $x\in G_k$ a constant-valued pattern on the finite projection closure
of $\{x\}$.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 3: coherent disjoint translations.}
For $\varphi\in\Phi$, write $\varphi/j$ for its restriction to $G_j$.
We claim that translations $z_\varphi$ can be chosen so that
\[
 \varphi\in\Phi_k\Rightarrow z_\varphi\in G_k,
 \qquad \pi_j(z_\varphi)=z_{\varphi/j},               \tag{3.7}
\]
and all finite blocks
\[
 B_\varphi:=\operatorname{dom}(\varphi)+z_\varphi     \tag{3.8}
\]
are pairwise disjoint.

At level zero, well-order $\Phi_0$ in type $\kappa_0$.  Before any stage
$\alpha<\kappa_0$, fewer than $\kappa_0$ finite blocks have been placed, and
each block forbids only finitely many translations, so a new translation in
$G_0$ remains.

Inductively suppose all patterns in $\Phi_k$ have been placed.  Well-order
$\Phi_{k+1}\setminus\Phi_k$ in type $\kappa_{k+1}$.  For a current pattern
$\chi$, coherence requires
\[
 z_\chi\in F_\chi:=G_{k+1}\cap
 \pi_k^{-1}(z_{\chi/k}).                              \tag{3.9}
\]
Since $|I_{k+1}\setminus I_k|=\kappa_{k+1}$ and $A$ is finite nontrivial,
$|F_\chi|=\kappa_{k+1}$.  Each earlier finite block forbids only finitely many
members of $F_\chi$: explicitly, an intersection
$(u+z_\chi)=(v+z_\varphi)$ forces
$z_\chi=v+z_\varphi-u$.  At a same-level stage
$\alpha<\kappa_{k+1}$, the total forbidden set therefore has cardinal at most
\[
 \max\{\kappa_k,|\alpha|\}<\kappa_{k+1}.              \tag{3.10}
\]
Choose a remaining point of $F_\chi$.  This proves (3.7)--(3.8) by recursion.
No regularity or successor assumption on $\kappa_{k+1}$ was used.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 4: uniform compatibility of the code.}
On the disjoint union $D=\bigcup_{\varphi\in\Phi}B_\varphi$, define
\[
 \widetilde h(x+z_\varphi)=\varphi(x).                \tag{3.11}
\]
Suppose
$d(x+z_\varphi,y+z_\psi)\le t_{k+1}$.  From (3.4), applied to $d$, and
coherence,
\[
 \pi_k(x)+z_{\varphi/k}=\pi_k(y)+z_{\psi/k}.          \tag{3.12}
\]
The two sides lie in the translated blocks belonging to the restricted
patterns.  Disjointness forces $\varphi/k=\psi/k$, and then
$\pi_k(x)=\pi_k(y)$.  Projection closure and the two-Lipschitz property give
\begin{align*}
 |\varphi(x)-\psi(y)|
 &\le |\varphi(x)-\varphi(\pi_kx)|
      +|\psi(\pi_ky)-\psi(y)|\\
 &\le 2d(x,\pi_kx)+2d(y,\pi_ky)
 \le4t_{k+1}.                                        \tag{3.13}
\end{align*}
Thus $\widetilde h$ is bounded and uniformly continuous.  The standard
uniform extension theorem for bounded real functions on metric subspaces
extends it to $h\in\Ub(G)$.  For every $\varphi$, the translate
$z_\varphi\mathbin{\cdot}h$, defined by
$(z\mathbin{\cdot}h)(x)=h(x+z)$, realizes $\varphi$.  Step~2 now yields
\[
 \BLip(d)\subseteq\overline{\orb(h)}^{\,G}.
\]
Since $\Delta$ was arbitrary, $G$ is ambitable.

Finally, $\ker\pi_0$ is open and
$G/\ker\pi_0\cong G_0$ is infinite.  The corresponding right-uniform
entourage has no finite cover, so $G$ is not precompact.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:cardinals}
For every singular cardinal $\kappa$ of countable cofinality and every
nontrivial finite abelian group $A$, there is a nonprecompact ambitable group
of cardinality $\kappa$ of the form in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  Its Samuel
compactification contains continuum many pairwise disjoint closed invariant
nonempty subsets.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose any strictly increasing cofinal sequence of infinite cardinals
$\kappa_k$ below $\kappa$.  Then $|A^{(I)}|=|I|=\kappa$.  Schneider's
Lemma~5.3 says that the Samuel compactification of an ambitable group contains
a continuum of pairwise disjoint closed invariant nonempty subsets
\cite{Schneider}.
\end{proof}

Pachl's 2026 example is recovered by taking $A=\mathbb Z/2$,
$I_k=\omega_k$, and $\kappa_k=\aleph_k$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} allows every
countable strictly growing cardinal tower and every finite nontrivial abelian
coefficient group.

\section{Upgrade attempts and remaining obstruction}

Eight focused upgrades were audited.  First, the displayed Pestov
concentration problem in the source was discarded because the same paper
answers it negatively.  Second, the June 2026 status check confirmed that the
ambitability problem itself remains open.  Third, the cardinal recursion was
abstracted successfully: strict growth, not regularity or a successor step,
is the required resource.  Fourth, the coefficient group was generalized
from $\mathbb Z/2$ to every finite nontrivial abelian group by using general
difference avoidance rather than characteristic two.

The remaining routes did not close the full problem.  If consecutive layers
have equal cardinality, the prescribed fiber may be exhausted by earlier
patterns.  With an uncountable cofinal tower, projection closure of a finite
domain can become infinite, breaking the finite-pattern recursion.  An
arbitrary nonprecompact group supplies separated sets but not coherent
homomorphic retractions, so equality of close code-point projections no
longer reduces to one lower-level block.  Finally, a direct attempt on the
weaker Samuel problem gives the strong conclusion in
Corollary~\ref{cor:cardinals}, but outside the layered class invariant closures
of two pieces of a uniformly discrete set can still meet the same minimal
subflow.

These are structural failures of the present method, not counterexamples.
The unrestricted questions therefore remain open.

\section{Verification, novelty bounds, and review recommendation}

The proof is self-contained after the standard Lipschitz-ball
characterization of ambitability and the bounded uniform-extension theorem.
The recursion was checked at arbitrary (including singular) layer cardinals:
for every initial stage $\alpha<\kappa_{k+1}$ one has
$|\alpha|<\kappa_{k+1}$, which is all (3.10) needs.  Finite projection closure,
fiber cardinality, the disjointness criterion, and the modulus (3.13) were
checked separately.  No computation is part of the proof.

The run's cheap registry, solution, attempt, proof-gap, and ledger indexes were
searched for arXiv:2204.09427, arXiv:2606.06000, ``precompact or ambitable'',
``singular ambitable'', and the core cardinal-tower terms.  Pachl's 4 June
2026 primary paper was also checked directly.  The bounded search found the
single $\mathbb Z/2,\aleph_\omega$ example but no arbitrary-tower or
finite-coefficient statement.  Novelty confidence is therefore moderate, not
definitive.

Promote as a substantial partial result.  Human review should focus on the
finite-pattern density convention and the coherent-translation recursion,
especially (3.9)--(3.13).
