\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Substantial partial result, likely valid.  Both source questions have a full
positive answer when the composition symbol is a finite Blaschke product.
For an arbitrary symbol, the finite-zero hypothesis can also be removed when
the zero multiplicities admit an analytic $p/2$-power; in particular this
covers every even integer $p$.  The unrestricted questions remain open here.

\section{Source questions}

The source is Chalendar and Partington, \emph{The reproducing kernel thesis
for lower bounds of weighted composition operators}~\cite{source}.
Remark~3.2 on source PDF page~8 asks whether the kernel thesis extends from
$A^2$ to the standard weighted Bergman spaces $A^2_\alpha$, $\alpha>-1$:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=.94\textwidth]{figures/weighted_question_crop.png}
\end{center}

After Theorem~3.4, on source PDF page~9, the authors ask whether their $A^p$
theorem extends to weights with infinitely many zeros:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=.94\textwidth]{figures/infinite_zero_question_crop.png}
\end{center}

Write $dA_\alpha(z)=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^2)^\alpha dA(z)$ and let
$A^p_\alpha$ be the corresponding analytic $L^p$ space.  We use $A^p=A^p_0$.
For holomorphic $h$ and $\psi:\D\to\D$, set
\[
 W_{h,\psi}f=h(f\circ\psi).
\]
The normalized $A^2_\alpha$ kernel is
\[
 k_w^\alpha(z)=
 \frac{(1-|w|^2)^{(\alpha+2)/2}}
 {(1-\overline wz)^{\alpha+2}},
\]
and the source's normalized $A^p$ tests are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:test}
 \ell_w^{(p)}(z)=
 \frac{(1-|w|^2)^{2/p}}{(1-\overline wz)^{4/p}}.
\end{equation}

\section{Results}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-Blaschke extension]\label{thm:blaschke}
Let $B$ be a finite Blaschke product.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $1\le p<\infty$, let $h\in A^p$, and suppose
      $W_{h,B}:A^p\to A^p$ is bounded.  With no restriction on the zero set
      of $h$, the operator is bounded below if and only if
      \[
      \inf_{w\in\D}\|W_{h,B}\ell_w^{(p)}\|_{A^p}>0.
      \]
\item Let $\alpha>-1$, let $h\in A^2_\alpha$, and suppose
      $W_{h,B}:A^2_\alpha\to A^2_\alpha$ is bounded.  Then it is bounded
      below if and only if
      \[
      \inf_{w\in\D}\|W_{h,B}k_w^\alpha\|_{A^2_\alpha}>0.
      \]
\end{enumerate}
Thus the two source questions have positive answers for every proper
holomorphic self-map of the disk.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Compatible infinite zero sets]\label{thm:orders}
Let $1\le p<\infty$, let $\psi:\D\to\D$ be holomorphic, and suppose
$W_{h,\psi}$ is bounded on $A^p$.  Assume that, with at most finitely many
exceptions, every zero $a$ of $h$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:order}
 \frac p2\operatorname{ord}_a(h)\in\mathbb N.
\end{equation}
Then the conclusion of the source's Theorem~3.4 holds without requiring
finitely many zeros: $W_{h,\psi}$ is bounded below if and only if it is
uniformly bounded below on the tests \eqref{eq:test}.

In particular, the conclusion holds for every even integer $p$ and an
arbitrary infinite zero set.  If $p=2a/b$ in lowest terms, it holds whenever
all but finitely many zero multiplicities are divisible by $b$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof intuition}

The pullback measure of a weighted composition operator converts both the
operator inequality and the kernel test into measure statements.  A general
Berezin lower bound is not enough: singular counterexamples are now
known~\cite{CLZ}.  The finite-Blaschke hypothesis supplies the missing
regularity.  All critical values lie in a compact subdisk, while near the
boundary the pullback density is a finite sum of moduli of analytic functions.
Its Carleson upper bound and the submean inequality force that density to be
bounded.  The compact inner part disappears asymptotically from normalized
kernels, and a Fredholm argument reduces the problem to Luecking's theorem
for bounded densities.

The second theorem uses a different mechanism.  Condition \eqref{eq:order}
is exactly the monodromy condition for $h^{p/2}$ to be analytic.  Once
$|H|^2=|h|^p$ with analytic $H$, the $A^p$ problem is literally the source's
established $A^2$ problem for the same pullback measure.

\section{A tail-bounded density lemma}

For a positive measure $\mu$ that is Carleson for $A^2_\alpha$, write
\[
 \widetilde\mu_\alpha(w)
   =\int_\D |k_w^\alpha(z)|^2\,d\mu(z).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Tail-bounded Berezin principle]\label{lem:tail}
Suppose $d\mu=g\,dA_\alpha$, $g\ge0$, and $g$ is essentially bounded on
$\{z:|z|>r_0\}$ for some $r_0<1$.  If
$\inf_{w\in\D}\widetilde\mu_\alpha(w)>0$, then $\mu$ is a reverse
Carleson measure for $A^2_\alpha$.

For $\alpha=0$, the outer bounded-density part also dominates $A^p$ for
every $0<p<\infty$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $r_1>r_0$ and split
\[
 \mu=\nu+\sigma,
 \qquad d\nu=\mathbf1_{\{|z|>r_1\}}g\,dA_\alpha.
\]
The finite measure $\sigma$ is supported in a compact subdisk.  Since the
normalized kernels tend to zero uniformly on compact subsets,
$\widetilde\sigma_\alpha(w)\to0$ as $|w|\to1$.  Hence
$\widetilde\nu_\alpha$ is bounded below for $|w|$ sufficiently close to one.

Add to the density of $\nu$ the indicator of a fixed compact disk.  The new
density $q$ is bounded, and $\widetilde{q,dA_\alpha}_\alpha$ is bounded below
on all of $\D$: near the boundary this comes from $\nu$, and on the remaining
compact set it comes from the added disk.  The bounded-density
Douglas--Luecking theorem says that $T_q$ is invertible.  For completeness,
its geometric core is short: normalized kernel mass outside a sufficiently
large fixed pseudohyperbolic disk is uniformly small; on the disk the kernel
density is comparable to reciprocal weighted area.  A lower Berezin bound
and $q\in L^\infty$ therefore make a level set $\{q>\delta\}$ occupy a fixed
positive proportion of every such disk.  Luecking's dominating-set theorem
then gives the reverse embedding~\cite{L81,L84,CLZ}.

The difference $T_q-T_\nu$ has compactly supported bounded symbol and is
compact.  Thus $T_\nu$ is Fredholm.  Moreover $\nu$ is nonzero, since its
Berezin transform is bounded below near the boundary.  If $T_\nu f=0$, then
\[
 0=\langle T_\nu f,f\rangle
   =\int |f|^2\,d\nu.
\]
A nonzero analytic function cannot vanish on the positive-area set on which
the density of $\nu$ is positive, so $f=0$.  The self-adjoint Fredholm
operator $T_\nu$ is therefore invertible.  Hence $\nu$, and a fortiori
$\mu$, is reverse Carleson.

When $\alpha=0$, Luecking's bounded-symbol characterization yields a level
set of the density of $\nu$ that is dominating.  The geometric density
condition for that set is independent of $p$, so the same set dominates
every $A^p$; this is also the exponent-independence used in the source's
proof of Theorem~3.4.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:blaschke}}

\begin{proof}
We give the unweighted $A^p$ argument first.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mu}
 \mu(E)=\int_{B^{-1}(E)}|h(z)|^p\,dA(z).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm}
 \|W_{h,B}f\|_{A^p}^p=\int_\D |f|^p\,d\mu.
\end{equation}
Boundedness of the operator makes $\mu$ a Bergman Carleson measure.  Since
$|\ell_w^{(p)}|^p=|k_w^0|^2$, the assumed test lower bound is precisely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:berezin}
 \inf_{w\in\D}\widetilde\mu_0(w)>0.
\end{equation}

The area formula gives, almost everywhere,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
 d\mu(u)=g(u)dA(u),\qquad
 g(u)=\sum_{B(z)=u}\frac{|h(z)|^p}{|B'(z)|^2}.
\end{equation}
All critical values of $B$ lie in a compact subdisk.  Near any sufficiently
outer $u$, choose local inverse branches $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_N$.  On a small
disk there is a holomorphic branch of $(\phi_j')^{2/p}$, and hence
\[
 g(v)=\sum_{j=1}^N
 \left|(h\circ\phi_j)(v)(\phi_j'(v))^{2/p}\right|^p.
\]
Thus $g$ has a locally subharmonic representative in an outer annulus.
For a fixed small $c>0$, the disk
$D(u,c(1-|u|))$ remains in an inverse-branch chart when $|u|$ is sufficiently
large.  The submean inequality and the Carleson disk estimate give
\[
 g(u)\lesssim \frac{\mu(D(u,c(1-|u|)))}{(1-|u|)^2}lesssim1.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}, \eqref{eq:berezin}, and its $p$-independence statement
now show that \eqref{eq:norm} is bounded below.  The reverse implication is
immediate because the tests have norm one.

For $A^2_\alpha$, use
\[
 \mu_\alpha(E)=\int_{B^{-1}(E)}|h(z)|^2\,dA_\alpha(z).
\]
Its density relative to $dA_\alpha$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-density}
 g_\alpha(u)=\sum_{j=1}^N |h(\phi_j(u))|^2|\phi_j'(u)|^2
 \left(\frac{1-|\phi_j(u)|^2}{1-|u|^2}\right)^\alpha
\end{equation}
in the outer annulus.  Because a finite Blaschke product extends across the
circle and has nonzero boundary derivative, the derivative and ratio factors
in \eqref{eq:weighted-density} are uniformly bounded above and below on
outer Bergman disks.  Apply the submean inequality to
$|h\circ\phi_j|^2$, then the weighted Carleson disk upper bound, to obtain
$g_\alpha(u)\lesssim1$ near the boundary.  Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} completes
the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:orders}}

\begin{proof}
First remove the finitely many exceptional zeros by the bounded finite
contractive divisor used in the source.  Multiplication by that divisor is
bounded above and below onto its closed finite-codimensional range, so it
preserves boundedness below both on the whole space and on the test family.
We may therefore suppose \eqref{eq:order} holds at every zero.

On the punctured disk, analytically continue $h^{p/2}$.  A circuit around a
zero of order $m$ multiplies a branch by $e^{2\pi i(pm/2)}$, which equals one
by \eqref{eq:order}.  All monodromy is therefore trivial.  The resulting
single-valued function extends across each zero with order $pm/2$, giving
an analytic $H$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 |H|^2=|h|^p.
\end{equation}
In particular $H\in A^2$.

The pullback measure induced by $W_{H,\psi}$ on $A^2$ is exactly the measure
induced by $W_{h,\psi}$ on $A^p$.  The standard forward Bergman Carleson
criterion is exponent-independent, so the former operator is bounded.
Moreover
\[
 (\ell_w^{(p)})^{p/2}=k_w^0,
 \qquad
 \|W_{H,\psi}k_w^0\|_{A^2}^2
 =\|W_{h,\psi}\ell_w^{(p)}\|_{A^p}^p.
\]
By Theorem~3.1 of the source, this operator is bounded below.  If $p=2m$ is
an even integer, the transfer back is completely direct: for every $f\in
A^{2m}$, put $F=f^m\in A^2$.  Then
\[
 \|W_{H,\psi}F\|_{A^2}^2=\|W_{h,\psi}f\|_{A^{2m}}^{2m},
 \qquad \|F\|_{A^2}^2=\|f\|_{A^{2m}}^{2m}.
\]
For the remaining compatible exponents, the exponent-independent reverse
Carleson step used in the source's own proof transfers the common-measure
inequality back to $A^p$.  This proves the forward implication; necessity is
immediate.
\end{proof}

\section{Upgrade attempts and remaining obstruction}

Six focused upgrades were audited.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A direct arbitrary-measure argument fails: arXiv:2405.05412 constructs
      a Carleson measure with Berezin transform bounded below but a
      noninvertible Toeplitz operator~\cite{CLZ}.
\item Exact scalar or vector Hilbertization cannot cross an incompatible zero
      multiplicity: the leading vanishing order of a squared holomorphic norm
      is an even integer, forcing \eqref{eq:order}.
\item Finite branched covers resolve finitely many rational-power branch
      points, but infinitely many prescribed branch points require an
      infinite cover and destroy the finite area-change identity.
\item The tail-density proof extends beyond finite Blaschke products whenever
      the critical values are compactly confined and outer valence/inverse
      charts are uniformly controlled.
\item For a general symbol, critical values and valence may accumulate at the
      boundary.  Carleson disk bounds then allow narrow unbounded density
      spikes, so the decisive outer $L^\infty$ estimate is unavailable.
\item Interpolation between even exponents changes the effective analytic
      weight; attempting to take fractional powers simply reproduces the
      monodromy obstruction.
\end{enumerate}
The strongest remaining route is therefore either a new analytic
anti-concentration theorem for general weighted pullbacks or a realization
of a Douglas-type spiky measure as such a pullback.

\section{Verification and novelty}

The proof was audited at the area formula, local subharmonicity, uniform
outer inverse charts, compact-tail Berezin limit, Fredholm injectivity, and
zero-monodromy steps.  No computation is used as proof.

The four lightweight run indexes had no entry for the source paper or these
extensions.  Bounded primary-source searches on 13 August 2026 used its exact
title, the two exact open-problem phrases, finite Blaschke symbols, contractive
divisors, and lower reproducing-kernel theses.  They found the source and
general Berezin/Toeplitz work, but no later paper stating either theorem.
Novelty confidence is moderate.
