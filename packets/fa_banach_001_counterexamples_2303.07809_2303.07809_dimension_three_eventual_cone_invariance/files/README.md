# Dimension three already separates individual and uniform eventual cone invariance

Status: **candidate full counterexample and minimal-dimension resolution; likely
valid; send to human review**.

Source: Jochen Glück and Julian Hölz, *Eventual cone invariance revisited*,
arXiv:2303.07809; Linear Algebra and its Applications 675 (2023), 274--293.
The dimension-three question follows Example 3.1 on source PDF page 7.

## Main result

On the three-dimensional cone of real `2 x 2` positive-semidefinite matrices,

`K = {(x1,x2,x3): x1,x3 >= 0 and x2^2 <= x1*x3}`, 

take

`A = [[0,4,0],[0,0,1],[0,0,0]]`.

The semigroup generated by `A` is individually eventually `K`-nonnegative,
but it is not uniformly eventually nonnegative.  In fact, `exp(tA)` fails to
preserve `K` for every `t>0`, witnessed by the boundary vector
`(t^2,-t,1)`.

All proper cones with interior in dimension two are polyhedral, where
individual eventual nonnegativity implies uniform eventual nonnegativity by
checking finitely many generating rays.  Therefore dimension three is the
smallest possible dimension.

## Bonus result

Restricting the source's left-multiplication construction to a carefully
chosen invariant ordered subspace gives a weakly but not individually
eventually nonnegative semigroup in dimension six, improving the source's
dimension-16 example.  No minimality claim is made for six.
