\section*{Source question}

Albrecht Böttcher, Lenny Fukshansky, Stephan Ramon Garcia, Hiren Maharaj,
and Deanna Needell ask in the introduction of
\emph{Lattices from tight equiangular frames} (arXiv:1607.05350; locally
compiled source PDF page~1):
\[
 \boxed{\text{When is the integer span \(\Lambda(G)\) of a real unit ETF a lattice?}}
\]
Writing its Gram matrix as
\[
 G^{\mathsf T}G=I+\alpha^{-1}C
\]
with \(C\) a sign matrix, source Proposition~2.1 proves the necessary
condition that \(\alpha\) be rational. The paper then treats particular
families and low-dimensional cases, but does not state the converse.

\section*{Exact later answer}

Böttcher and Fukshansky explicitly correct this omission in
\emph{Lattices and quadratic forms from tight frames in Euclidean spaces}
(arXiv:1704.07735; published as an addendum in Linear Algebra and its
Applications 531 (2017), 592--601). Its abstract says the earlier paper
overlooked a simple argument in the reverse direction. Proposition~1
proves that any rational spanning family has lattice integer span, since
after clearing a common denominator \(d\), the norm form satisfies
\[
 d\|Ga\|^2\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}\qquad(a\in\mathbb Z^n).
\]
For a unit ETF, rationality of the Gram matrix is equivalent to rationality
of \(\alpha\) (and the ETF parameter restrictions force such an \(\alpha\)
to be a positive integer). Theorem~3 therefore gives
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \Lambda(G)\text{ is a lattice}
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\
 \alpha\in\mathbb Z_{>0}
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\
 Q_G\text{ has separated values}.
 \;}
\]

\section*{Scope and provenance}

This completely answers the source's lattice-existence question for real
unit equiangular tight frames. It does not settle the source's separate,
open-ended request to determine all geometric properties of every resulting
lattice. The identification is explicit: the addendum names the source
paper, describes the missing reverse implication, and packages the
equivalence as Theorem~3.
