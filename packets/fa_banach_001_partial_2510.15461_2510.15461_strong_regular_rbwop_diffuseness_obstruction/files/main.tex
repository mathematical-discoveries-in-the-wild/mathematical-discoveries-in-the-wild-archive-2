\section*{Source Question}

Lopez-Perez, Martinez Vano, and Rueda Zoca introduce the r-big weak open
property and ask in Remark 3.3(3):
\begin{quote}
``A natural question, whose answer we do not know, is whether there are
Banach spaces with the r-BWOP and being strongly regular.''
\end{quote}
The question is on PDF page 14 of arXiv:2510.15461 and at line 671 of the
parsed source file
\path{Big_weak_open_radius_versus_big_slice_diameter.tex} \cite{LMRZ}.

\section*{Definitions}

For a bounded set $A$ in a Banach space $X$, write
\[
 r(A)=\inf\{t>0:A\subseteq x+tB_X\text{ for some }x\in X\}.
\]
The space $X$ has the \emph{r-big weak open property} (r-BWOP) when every
nonempty relatively weakly open subset of $B_X$ has radius one
\cite[Definition 2.3]{LMRZ}. Recall that
\[
 r(A)\leq \diameter(A).
\]

The space $X$ is \emph{strongly regular} when every nonempty bounded convex
subset admits finite convex combinations of slices of arbitrarily small
diameter. We use the standard localization of this definition: if $X$ is
strongly regular, $W$ is a nonempty relatively weakly open subset of $B_X$,
and $\varepsilon>0$, then there are nonempty relatively weakly open subsets
$U_1,\ldots,U_n$ of $B_X$, all contained in $W$, and positive weights
$\lambda_i$ summing to one such that
\[
 \diameter\left(\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_iU_i\right)<\varepsilon.
\]
This localization is recorded, in the more general topological formulation,
in \cite[Lemma 2.2]{LPM}.

Finally, $X$ has property $\Pbar$ when, for every finite convex combination
$C$ of slices of $B_X$ and every $x\in C$, there is a relatively weakly open
neighborhood $V$ of $x$ in $B_X$ such that $V\subseteq\overline C$, where the
bar denotes norm closure \cite[Definition 2.1]{BFP}.

\section*{Proof Intuition}

Each weak opening $U_i$ has radius one and therefore diameter at least one.
If all other coordinates in a Minkowski convex combination are frozen, the
combination contains a translated copy of $\lambda_iU_i$. Thus a combination
of diameter $d$ cannot assign any constituent weight larger than $d$.
Arbitrarily small strong-regularity witnesses must consequently spread their
mass over arbitrarily many weak openings. Moreover, a combination of diameter
below one cannot contain a weak opening even after norm closure, because that
opening would have radius below one. Property $\Pbar$ forces exactly such an
opening for every convex combination of slices, so it is incompatible with
strong regularity and r-BWOP together. The original unrestricted existence
question remains open.

\section*{Main Partial Result}

\begin{theorem}[Diffuseness and weak-interior obstruction]\label{thm:main}
Let $X$ have the r-BWOP. Let $U_1,\ldots,U_n$ be nonempty relatively weakly
open subsets of $B_X$, let $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n>0$ satisfy
$\sum_i\lambda_i=1$, and put
\[
 C=\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_iU_i.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\diameter(C)\geq\max_i\lambda_i$;
\item if $\diameter(C)<\varepsilon$, then
      $\lambda_i<\varepsilon$ for every $i$ and $n>1/\varepsilon$;
\item if $\diameter(C)<1$, then $\overline C$ has empty interior in the
      relative weak topology of $B_X$.
\end{enumerate}

If, in addition, $X$ is strongly regular, then for every nonempty relatively
weakly open $W\subseteq B_X$ and every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ there is such a
combination with $U_i\subseteq W$ and diameter below $\varepsilon$; every one
of its coefficients is below $\varepsilon$, it uses more than
$1/\varepsilon$ terms, and its norm closure has empty relative weak interior.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $i$. For every $j\neq i$, choose a point $u_j\in U_j$. If $u,v\in U_i$,
then both
\[
 \lambda_i u+\sum_{j\neq i}\lambda_j u_j
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \lambda_i v+\sum_{j\neq i}\lambda_j u_j
\]
belong to $C$, and their distance is $\lambda_i\lVert u-v\rVert$. Hence
\[
 \diameter(C)\geq\lambda_i\diameter(U_i)
 \geq\lambda_i r(U_i)=\lambda_i,
\]
where the last equality is the r-BWOP. Taking the maximum over $i$ proves
\textup{(i)}.

If $\diameter(C)<\varepsilon$, part \textup{(i)} gives
$\lambda_i<\varepsilon$ for every $i$. Therefore
\[
 1=\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i<n\varepsilon,
\]
which proves \textup{(ii)}.

For \textup{(iii)}, suppose that a nonempty relatively weakly open subset
$V$ of $B_X$ were contained in $\overline C$. Then
\[
 1=r(V)\leq\diameter(V)\leq\diameter(\overline C)
   =\diameter(C)<1,
\]
a contradiction.

Now assume that $X$ is strongly regular. Apply the standard localization
statement above inside $W$ to obtain nonempty relatively weakly open
$U_i\subseteq W$ whose convex combination has diameter below $\varepsilon$.
The remaining assertions follow from \textup{(ii)} and \textup{(iii)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A broad no-go class]\label{thm:pbar}
No Banach space can simultaneously be strongly regular, have the r-BWOP, and
have property $\Pbar$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that $X$ has all three properties. Strong regularity applied to $B_X$
gives a finite convex combination $C$ of slices of $B_X$ with
$\diameter(C)<1$. Choose $x\in C$. Property $\Pbar$ gives a nonempty
relatively weakly open neighborhood $V$ of $x$ in $B_X$ with
$V\subseteq\overline C$. Therefore
\[
 1=r(V)\leq\diameter(V)\leq\diameter(\overline C)
   =\diameter(C)<1,
\]
which is impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:l1}
No isometric predual of $\ell_1(\Gamma)$ can be both strongly regular and
have the r-BWOP.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every isometric predual of an $\ell_1$ space has property $\Pbar$
\cite[Corollary 2.8]{BFP}. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:pbar}.
\end{proof}

\section*{Verification Notes}

The core estimate is an exact scaled-copy argument; it does not use compactness,
separability, attainment of the diameter, or scalar restrictions. The closure
in Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and \ref{thm:pbar} is norm closure. Since $C$ is
convex, its norm closure is also its weak closure, but that identification is
not needed: only equality of diameters under norm closure is used.

No computation is required. The two imported facts to check are the standard
localization of strong regularity and the theorem that isometric
$\ell_1$-preduals have $\Pbar$; both are cited at their source locations.

\section*{Limitations and Novelty Check}

This is a partial result only. It neither supplies a strongly regular r-BWOP
space nor rules one out without an additional local-interior property. The
direct full contradiction stops precisely because arbitrary convex combinations
of slices need not contain relatively weakly open subsets, even up to closure.

The run registry and solution indexes were searched for arXiv:2510.15461 and
the phrases ``r-BWOP strongly regular,'' ``r-big weak open property strongly
regular,'' and close variants. A bounded external search of arXiv and the
June 2026 version-of-record page was also performed through 9 August 2026. No
later answer to the exact question was found, and the version of record still
states it as open. The $\Pbar$ obstruction is a direct new synthesis of the
source question with the known local-interior property; novelty confidence is
moderate, and the packet makes no claim of priority.
