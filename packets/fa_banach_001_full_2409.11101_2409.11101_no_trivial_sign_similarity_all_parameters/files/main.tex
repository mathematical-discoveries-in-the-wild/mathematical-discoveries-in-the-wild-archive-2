\section{Source question and scope}

Biswas, Ghosh, Narayanan, and Shyam Roy ask in Section~4.3 of
\emph{Contractive Hilbert modules on quotient domains}, arXiv:2409.11101
\cite{source}, whether the trivial and sign submodules
$\mathbb A_{\mathrm{triv}}^{(\lambda)}(\D^n)$ and
$\mathbb A_{\mathrm{sign}}^{(\lambda)}(\D^n)$ are similar over the symmetric
polynomial ring.  They prove non-similarity only for $n=2$ and
$\lambda=1,2$.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

On PDF page~21, Remark~4.22 asks the corresponding general division question
for the Vandermonde $V_n$ and again records only the case $n=2$,
$\lambda=1,2$.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/division_question_crop.png}
\end{center}

We answer both questions for all $n\geq2$ and $\lambda\geq1$.  In fact, the
kernel argument below works for every $\lambda>0$ for which the RKHS with the
displayed kernel is considered.

\section{Definitions and proof intuition}

Let $\cH=\mathbb A^{(\lambda)}(\D^n)$ be the reproducing-kernel Hilbert space
with kernel
\[
 K_\lambda(z,w)=\prod_{j=1}^n(1-z_j\overline{w_j})^{-\lambda}.
\]
Let $\cH_+$ and $\cH_-$ denote its symmetric and anti-symmetric subspaces.
They are the trivial and sign reducing submodules for the action of $\Sn$.
Put
\[
 \Delta(z)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(z_i-z_j),
 \qquad N=\frac{n(n-1)}2.
\]
Every anti-symmetric holomorphic function is divisible by $\Delta$, with a
symmetric holomorphic quotient.  We therefore introduce
\[
 \widetilde{\cH}_-
 =\{g\text{ symmetric and holomorphic}:\Delta g\in\cH_-\},
 \qquad \|g\|_{\widetilde{\cH}_-}=\|\Delta g\|_{\cH}.
\]
Multiplication by $\Delta$ is a unitary from $\widetilde{\cH}_-$ onto
$\cH_-$ and respects multiplication by symmetric polynomials.

\subsection*{Proof intuition}

The two divided modules have dramatically different point-evaluation sizes
near the boundary of the full diagonal.  At
$\diagpt a=(a,\ldots,a)$, the kernel norm in $\widetilde{\cH}_-$ exceeds the
one in $\cH_+$ by the exact factor
$(1-|a|^2)^{-N}$, up to a fixed constant.  A module similarity must be
multiplication by a holomorphic function $\psi$, and its inverse must be
multiplication by $h=1/\psi$.  Kernel-vector estimates then force
$|h(\diagpt a)|\lesssim(1-|a|^2)^N$ uniformly in the argument of $a$.
Maximum modulus says that the holomorphic function $a\mapsto h(a,\ldots,a)$
must be zero, contradicting $h\psi=1$.

\section{The diagonal kernel calculation}

Let $L_+$ and $L_-$ be the reproducing kernels of $\cH_+$ and
$\widetilde{\cH}_-$, respectively.

\begin{lemma}\label{kernel-lemma}
For $a\in\D$,
\begin{align*}
 L_+(\diagpt a,\diagpt a)
   &=(1-|a|^2)^{-n\lambda},\\
 L_-(\diagpt a,\diagpt a)
   &=c_{n,\lambda}(1-|a|^2)^{-n\lambda-2N},
\end{align*}
where
\[
 c_{n,\lambda}
 =\frac1{n!}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{(\lambda)_j}{j!}>0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \left(\frac{L_-(\diagpt a,\diagpt a)}
 {L_+(\diagpt a,\diagpt a)}\right)^{1/2}
 =\sqrt{c_{n,\lambda}}(1-|a|^2)^{-N}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The symmetric and anti-symmetric projection formulas give
\begin{align}
 L_+(z,w)
 &=\frac1{n!}\operatorname{per}
    \big[(1-z_i\overline{w_j})^{-\lambda}\big]_{i,j=1}^n,
 \label{plus-kernel}\\
 L_-(z,w)
 &=\frac1{n!}\frac{\det
    \big[(1-z_i\overline{w_j})^{-\lambda}\big]_{i,j=1}^n}
    {\Delta(z)\overline{\Delta(w)}}.
 \label{minus-kernel}
\end{align}
The quotient in \eqref{minus-kernel} extends holomorphically across the
collision sets because the numerator is alternating separately in $z$ and
$\overline w$.  At $w=\diagpt a$, every summand of
\eqref{plus-kernel} agrees, which gives the first formula.

For the second formula, first coalesce all variables at zero.  The power
series
\[
 (1-zu)^{-\lambda}=\sum_{k\geq0}
 \frac{(\lambda)_k}{k!}z^ku^k
\]
and the Cauchy--Binet expansion show that the lowest nonzero alternating
bidegree of the determinant uses $k=0,1,\ldots,n-1$.  Dividing by the two
Vandermondes and letting all variables tend to zero yields
\[
 \prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{(\lambda)_j}{j!}.
\]

For a general $a$, apply the disc automorphism
$\phi_a(z)=(z-a)/(1-\overline a z)$ to every coordinate.  The identities
\[
 1-\phi_a(z)\overline{\phi_a(w)}
 =\frac{(1-|a|^2)(1-z\overline w)}
 {(1-\overline a z)(1-a\overline w)}
\]
and
\[
 \phi_a(z_i)-\phi_a(z_j)
 =\frac{(1-|a|^2)(z_i-z_j)}
 {(1-\overline a z_i)(1-\overline a z_j)}
\]
transform the coalesced determinant quotient at zero into
$(1-|a|^2)^{-n\lambda-2N}$ times the same constant.  The factor $1/n!$ in
\eqref{minus-kernel} completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{No module similarity}

\begin{theorem}\label{main-theorem}
For every $n\geq2$ and $\lambda\geq1$, the Hilbert modules
$\mathbb A_{\mathrm{triv}}^{(\lambda)}(\D^n)$ and
$\mathbb A_{\mathrm{sign}}^{(\lambda)}(\D^n)$ are not similar over
$\C[z_1,\ldots,z_n]^{\Sn}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Via division by $\Delta$, it suffices to rule out a bounded invertible module
map $X:\cH_+\to\widetilde{\cH}_-$.  Symmetric polynomials are dense in
$\cH_+$, so $1$ is cyclic.  They are also dense in
$\widetilde{\cH}_-$: anti-symmetric polynomials are dense in $\cH_-$, and
every such polynomial is $\Delta$ times a symmetric polynomial.

Set $\psi=X1$.  For every symmetric polynomial $p$, the module relation gives
$Xp=p\psi$.  Density and continuity of point evaluations extend this identity
to every $f\in\cH_+$, so $X=M_\psi$.  Applying the same argument to
$Y=X^{-1}$ gives $Y=M_h$ for a symmetric holomorphic $h$.  Hence
\[
 h\psi=1.
\]

Let $k_w^+$ and $k_w^-$ be the kernel vectors of $\cH_+$ and
$\widetilde{\cH}_-$.  Adjointing the two multiplier identities gives
\[
 M_\psi^*k_w^-=\overline{\psi(w)}k_w^+,
 \qquad
 M_h^*k_w^+=\overline{h(w)}k_w^-.
\]
Therefore, with
$q(w)=\sqrt{L_-(w,w)/L_+(w,w)}$,
\[
 \frac{q(w)}{\|Y\|}\leq |\psi(w)|\leq \|X\|q(w).
\]
Put $w=\diagpt a$ and use Lemma~\ref{kernel-lemma}.  Since $h=1/\psi$,
\[
 |h(a,\ldots,a)|
 \leq \frac{\|Y\|}{\sqrt{c_{n,\lambda}}}(1-|a|^2)^N.
\]
The function $g(a)=h(a,\ldots,a)$ is holomorphic on $\D$.  For every
$0<r<1$, the preceding estimate holds uniformly on $|a|=r$, and its
right-hand side tends to zero as $r\uparrow1$.  By the maximum-modulus
principle, $g$ vanishes identically.  This contradicts $h\psi=1$ on the full
diagonal.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{division-corollary}
For every $n\geq2$ and $\lambda\geq1$, multiplication by $\Delta$ from
$\cH_+$ to $\cH_-$ is not onto.  Equivalently, there exists
$f\in\mathbb A^{(\lambda)}(\D^n)$ such that $f/\Delta$ is holomorphic on
$\D^n$ but $f/\Delta\notin\mathbb A^{(\lambda)}(\D^n)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the divided model, multiplication by $\Delta$ becomes the identity map
$J:\cH_+\to\widetilde{\cH}_-$.  It is bounded because $\Delta$ is a
polynomial multiplier.  If it were onto, the open mapping theorem would make
$J^{-1}$ bounded.  Applying $(J^{-1})^*$ to kernel vectors would force
$L_-(w,w)\leq\|J^{-1}\|^2L_+(w,w)$, contrary to Lemma~\ref{kernel-lemma}
as $w=\diagpt a$ and $|a|\uparrow1$.  Finally, every anti-symmetric
holomorphic function is holomorphically divisible by $\Delta$, so failure of
surjectivity has exactly the stated division interpretation.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, limitations, and novelty}

The accompanying script forms the mixed-derivative confluent matrix of
$(1-zu)^{-\lambda}$ and verifies the determinant formula in
Lemma~\ref{kernel-lemma} for eleven exact cases with $2\leq n\leq5$ and
$\lambda\in\{1,2,5/2\}$.  This audits the constant and exponent; it is not
used as a proof.

The conclusion concerns the trivial and sign representations of $\Sn$ and
the full parameter range in the source question.  It does not classify
similarity among higher-dimensional irreducible-representation summands or
among modules for other reflection groups.

A bounded novelty search on 17 August 2026 used arXiv, the local full-source
corpus, exact title/author searches, the phrases ``trivial sign weighted
Bergman similarity'', ``Vandermonde division polydisc'', and citation searches
for arXiv:2409.11101.  It found the source paper and earlier unitary
inequivalence work, but no all-parameter similarity or division answer.  Of
particular significance, Sarkar's December 2025 survey
\cite{survey} still presents Vandermonde surjectivity/division on $\D^n$ as
an open problem.  Thus the packet should receive expert review as a plausible
new full result.
