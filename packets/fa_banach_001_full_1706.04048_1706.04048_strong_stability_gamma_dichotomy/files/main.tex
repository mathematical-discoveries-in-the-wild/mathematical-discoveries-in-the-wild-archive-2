\section*{Source and Open Question}

This packet concerns Remark~7.6 on page~15 of Chen and
\"Oktem~\cite{ChenOktem}, arXiv:1706.04048v2.  In the Hilbert space
\[
                \Hspace=L^2([0,1],V),
\]
the source minimizes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:objective}
 \Jfun_{g,\gamma}(\nu)
 =\gamma\norm{\nu}_{\Hspace}^{2}
  +\Ddisc\bigl(\Amap(\nu),g\bigr),
 \qquad
 \Amap(\nu):=T\bigl(\mathcal V(\nu,I)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal V(\nu,I)$ is the deformed template, $T$ is the forward
operator, and $\Ddisc$ is the data discrepancy.  Theorem~7.5 proves that for
$g_k\to g$, a sequence of minimizers of $\Jfun_{g_k,\gamma}$ has a weakly
convergent subsequence whose limit minimizes $\Jfun_{g,\gamma}$.  Remark~7.6
asks whether a strongly convergent subsequence always exists.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The proof of Theorem~7.5 uses the convergence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resconv}
 \Ddisc(\Amap(\nu_k),g_k)
 \longrightarrow \Ddisc(\Amap(\widehat\nu),g)
 \quad\text{whenever }\nu_k\rightharpoonup\widehat\nu, g_k\to g.
\end{equation}
This follows from Corollary~7.3 and continuity of $T$ when the discrepancy is
jointly continuous.  We retain precisely this proof-effective continuity
hypothesis below.

\section*{Proof Intuition}

The source proof already contains almost all of the strong-convergence
argument.  Weak convergence gives the lower bound
$\norm{\widehat\nu}\leq\liminf\norm{\nu_k}$.  To obtain the missing upper
bound, use the weak limit itself as a fixed competitor in the minimizing
inequality.  The discrepancy on both sides converges to the same number by
\eqref{eq:resconv}, so it cancels in the limit and leaves
\(\limsup\norm{\nu_k}^2\leq\norm{\widehat\nu}^2\), provided $\gamma>0$.
Weak convergence plus convergence of norms is strong convergence in a Hilbert
space.

This mechanism necessarily disappears at $\gamma=0$.  A zero template and
zero forward data make the discrepancy independent of the velocity, so every
velocity is a minimizer.  An orthonormal sequence then gives weak convergence
without any strongly convergent subsequence.

\section*{Sharp Resolution}

\begin{theorem}[Strong stability for positive regularization]\label{thm:positive}
Assume the hypotheses and the discrepancy convergence used in the proof of
Theorem~7.5 of the source paper.  Let $\gamma>0$, let $g_k\to g$, and choose
\[
                  \nu_k\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{\nu\in\Hspace}
                  \Jfun_{g_k,\gamma}(\nu).
\]
Then some subsequence converges strongly in $\Hspace$ to a minimizer of
$\Jfun_{g,\gamma}$.

More precisely, every subsequence satisfying
$\nu_k\rightharpoonup\widehat\nu$ and \eqref{eq:resconv} satisfies
$\nu_k\to\widehat\nu$ strongly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $\gamma>0$, the coercive estimate in the source proof is valid and
shows that $(\nu_k)$ is bounded.  Passing to a subsequence, Theorem~7.5 gives
\[
                  \nu_k\rightharpoonup\widehat\nu
                  \quad\text{in }\Hspace,
\]
where $\widehat\nu$ minimizes $\Jfun_{g,\gamma}$.  Put
\[
 r_k:=\Ddisc(\Amap(\nu_k),g_k),
 \qquad r:=\Ddisc(\Amap(\widehat\nu),g).
\]
By \eqref{eq:resconv}, $r_k\to r$.  Continuity in the data variable also gives
\[
 \Ddisc(\Amap(\widehat\nu),g_k)\longrightarrow r.
\]

Use $\widehat\nu$ as a competitor for the $g_k$ problem.  Minimality gives
\[
 \gamma\norm{\nu_k}_{\Hspace}^{2}+r_k
 \leq
 \gamma\norm{\widehat\nu}_{\Hspace}^{2}
 +\Ddisc(\Amap(\widehat\nu),g_k).
\]
Taking the upper limit and cancelling the common discrepancy limit yields
\[
 \limsup_{k\to\infty}\norm{\nu_k}_{\Hspace}^{2}
 \leq\norm{\widehat\nu}_{\Hspace}^{2}.
\]
Weak lower semicontinuity gives the reverse inequality
\[
 \norm{\widehat\nu}_{\Hspace}^{2}
 \leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\norm{\nu_k}_{\Hspace}^{2}.
\]
Thus $\norm{\nu_k}_{\Hspace}\to\norm{\widehat\nu}_{\Hspace}$.  The Radon--Riesz
property of Hilbert spaces now implies
$\nu_k\to\widehat\nu$ strongly.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Failure at zero regularization]\label{prop:zero}
The strong conclusion in Remark~7.6 is false for $\gamma=0$ under the printed
hypotheses.  In fact, the weak compactness conclusion of Theorem~7.5 can also
fail at $\gamma=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose any nonzero admissible Hilbert space $V$ of vector fields and set
$\Hspace=L^2([0,1],V)$.  Take the template $I\equiv0$.  Both the geometric and
mass-preserving LDDMM actions keep this template equal to zero under every
velocity.  Let the data space be $Y=\mathbb R$, let $T\equiv0$, take
$g_k=g=0$, and use the standard squared discrepancy
\[
                         \Ddisc(y,g)=|y-g|^2.
\]
All continuity and regularity hypotheses in Theorem~7.5 are satisfied.  At
$\gamma=0$, however, \eqref{eq:objective} is identically zero, so every
$\nu\in\Hspace$ is a minimizer.

Fix a unit vector field $z\in V$ and let
\[
                 \nu_k(t)=\sqrt2\sin(2\pi kt)z.
\]
The sequence $(\nu_k)$ is orthonormal in $\Hspace$.  Hence
$\nu_k\rightharpoonup0$, and $0$ is a minimizer, but no subsequence converges
strongly because
$\norm{\nu_j-\nu_k}_{\Hspace}=\sqrt2$ for $j\neq k$.  This disproves the
strong statement at $\gamma=0$.

Moreover, since every velocity is a minimizer, the sequence $k\nu_1$ is an
unbounded sequence of minimizers and has no weakly convergent subsequence.
Thus Theorem~7.5 itself requires $\gamma>0$ (or a separate bounded-selection
hypothesis); its displayed coercive estimate contains $1/\gamma$ and is not
valid at zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact answer]
For the regularization regime actually supported by the source proof,
$\gamma>0$, Remark~7.6 has an affirmative answer without uniqueness or
convexity.  For the additional value $\gamma=0$ allowed by the printed theorem,
the answer is negative.  This dichotomy is sharp.
\end{remark}

\section*{Verification, Scope, and Novelty}

The positive proof was checked line by line against page~15 of the source.
It uses only the weak subsequence and discrepancy convergence established
there, minimality with one fixed competitor, and the Hilbert-space
weak-plus-norm criterion.  The counterexample uses the source's own LDDMM
state space and the standard squared discrepancy; it is not an abstract
pathology.

There are two statement-level points for human review.  First, the source
prints $\gamma\geq0$ but divides by $\gamma$ in its first stability estimate.
Second, its theorem states continuity with the limiting datum fixed, while its
proof uses continuity along $(\Amap(\nu_k),g_k)$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:positive}
assumes exactly the latter convergence, as the source proof does; joint
continuity of $\Ddisc$ is a standard sufficient condition.

A bounded novelty check on August 11, 2026 searched the run indexes, exact
title and exact-question phrases, and the 32 citing works indexed by OpenAlex.
No later paper was found that states this positive/zero dichotomy or closes
Remark~7.6.  A targeted inspection of the related arXiv:1810.08596 found a
weak stability proposition, not the norm-convergence upgrade.  Novelty
confidence is moderate pending specialist review.
