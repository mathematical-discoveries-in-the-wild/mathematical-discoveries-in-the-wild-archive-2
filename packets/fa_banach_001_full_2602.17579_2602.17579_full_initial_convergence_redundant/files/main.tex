\section*{Source conjecture}

The source is B. Hilder, P. van Meurs, and U. Sharma,
\emph{Non-equilibrium functional inequalities for finite Markov chains},
arXiv:2602.17579. Its Theorem 5.6 concerns a finite scale-separated Markov
chain on \(\X=\Y\times\Zs\), with generator
\[
 L^\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-1}Q+G,
\]
coarse marginal \(\widehat\mu_t^\varepsilon\), and effective law
\(\eta_t^\varepsilon\). Among its hypotheses is the convergence, as
\(\varepsilon\to0\), of the microscopic initial law
\(\mu_0^\varepsilon\) and the two coarse initial laws
\(\widehat\mu_0^\varepsilon,\eta_0^\varepsilon\).

Remark 5.7 says that convergence of \(\mu_0^\varepsilon\) is used only to
obtain a uniform bound on
\(\Ent(\mu_0^\varepsilon\mid\rho^\varepsilon)\), where
\(\rho^\varepsilon\) is the stationary law of \(L^\varepsilon\), and
conjectures that this assumption can be removed.

\section*{Result and proof intuition}

We prove more: the entire convergence bullet is unnecessary. The two uses of
convergence in the cited proof have direct uniform replacements.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Since the finite-state stationary laws \(\rho^\varepsilon\) converge to
a strictly positive law, their smallest coordinates are uniformly positive.
The elementary estimate
\[
 \Ent(\mu\mid\rho^\varepsilon)
 \leq \log\frac1{\min_x\rho^\varepsilon(x)}
\]
is then uniform over \emph{all} probability laws \(\mu\).
\item Uniform positivity of the two coarse initial laws propagates on every
fixed time interval. Indeed, cancellation of the fast within-cluster
generator gives a scalar inequality \(p_y'(t)\geq-Rp_y(t)\), where \(R\)
depends only on the bounded inter-cluster rates.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
In Theorem 5.6 of arXiv:2602.17579, delete the hypothesis that
\[
 \mu_0^\varepsilon,\qquad
 \widehat\mu_0^\varepsilon,\qquad
 \eta_0^\varepsilon
\]
converge as \(\varepsilon\to0\). Retain all the other hypotheses, in
particular uniform strict positivity of
\(\widehat\mu_0^\varepsilon,\eta_0^\varepsilon\) and the initial
\(O(\varepsilon)\) coarse relative-entropy estimate. Then the conclusions
(60) and (61) of that theorem remain valid, with constants independent of
sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\).
\end{theorem}

\section*{Proof}

We use the notation and assumptions of Theorem 5.6. The state spaces are
finite. Hilder--Sharma, \emph{Quantitative Coarse-Graining of Markov Chains},
arXiv:2201.10256, Lemma 4.3, proves
\[
 \rho^\varepsilon\longrightarrow\rho\in\Pp_+(\X)
 \qquad(\varepsilon\to0).
\]
Consequently, after decreasing \(\varepsilon_0\) if necessary, there is
\(r>0\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
 \min_{x\in\X}\rho^\varepsilon(x)\geq r
 \qquad(0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0).
\end{equation}
For every \(\mu\in\Pp(\X)\), with \(0\log0=0\),
\begin{align}
 \Ent(\mu\mid\rho^\varepsilon)
 &=\sum_{x\in\X}\mu(x)\log\frac{\mu(x)}{\rho^\varepsilon(x)}
 \nonumber\\
 &\leq \sum_{x\in\X}\mu(x)\log\frac1{\rho^\varepsilon(x)}
 \leq \log\frac1r.                                      \label{eq:entropy}
\end{align}
Here we used \(\mu(x)\leq1\), hence \(\mu(x)\log\mu(x)\leq0\).
This gives exactly the uniform microscopic entropy bound for which
convergence of \(\mu_0^\varepsilon\) is invoked in Remark 5.7 and in the
last paragraph of the proof of Hilder--Sharma Theorem 4.5.

We next replace the use of convergence of the two coarse initial laws. For
\(y\in\Y\), let
\[
 R_y=\max_{z\in\Zs}\sum_{z'\in\Zs}G_{y,1-y}(z,z'),
 \qquad R=\max_{y\in\Y}R_y.
\]
These numbers are finite and independent of \(\varepsilon\). Summing the
microscopic forward equation over the fiber
\(\Lambda_y=\{y\}\times\Zs\), all terms from
\(\varepsilon^{-1}Q_y\) cancel because \(Q_y\) is a Markov generator.
The remaining inflow from the other fiber is nonnegative, while the outflow
is at most \(R_y\widehat\mu_t^\varepsilon(y)\). Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coarse-diff}
 \frac{d}{dt}\widehat\mu_t^\varepsilon(y)
 \geq -R_y\widehat\mu_t^\varepsilon(y).
\end{equation}

The effective off-diagonal rate is an average of the same microscopic
inter-fiber rates:
\[
 N^\varepsilon(y,1-y)
 =\sum_{z,z'\in\Zs}
   \rho^\varepsilon(z\mid y)G_{y,1-y}(z,z')
 \leq R_y.
\]
The effective forward equation therefore similarly gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-diff}
 \frac{d}{dt}\eta_t^\varepsilon(y)
 \geq -R_y\eta_t^\varepsilon(y).
\end{equation}
The retained first bullet of Theorem 5.6 supplies a constant \(c_0>0\)
such that both initial coarse laws are at least \(c_0\). Gronwall's
inequality applied to \eqref{eq:coarse-diff} and
\eqref{eq:effective-diff} yields, for every \(T<\infty\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive}
 \inf_{\substack{0\leq t\leq T\\y\in\Y\\0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0}}
 \left\{
 \widehat\mu_t^\varepsilon(y),\eta_t^\varepsilon(y)
 \right\}
 \geq c_0e^{-RT}>0.
\end{equation}
This is precisely the finite-time, \(\varepsilon\)-uniform positivity used
in the proof of Hilder--Sharma Theorem 4.5 to control the logarithm. The
cited proof originally obtains it from convergence of the coarse initial
laws to averaged dynamics; \eqref{eq:positive} supplies it directly.

We have now replaced every use of the deleted convergence bullet. The
source paper already removes the formerly assumed uniform log-Sobolev
constant by continuity of its generalized log-Sobolev constant. Applying
the rest of the proof of Theorem 5.6 unchanged gives the finite-time
\(O(\varepsilon)\) estimate. In that estimate the entropy dissipation is
bounded by its initial value, which is uniformly bounded by
\eqref{eq:entropy}. The source's long-time generalized log-Sobolev argument
then combines with the finite-time estimate exactly as written to give
\[
 \Ent_{\eta_t^\varepsilon}
 \left(\frac{\widehat\mu_t^\varepsilon}{\eta_t^\varepsilon}\right)
 \leq C_1\varepsilon e^{-\beta t}\qquad(t\geq0),
\]
and hence its time-uniform consequence. This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. \(\square\)

\section*{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The source conjecture and convergence bullet were checked on PDF page 25.
The exact use of microscopic initial convergence was checked on page 21 of
the supporting paper: it is invoked only to bound
\(\Ent(\mu_0^\varepsilon\mid\rho^\varepsilon)\). Its Lemma 4.3 proves the
finite-state convergence to a positive stationary law used in
\eqref{eq:rho}. The fiber summation leading to
\eqref{eq:coarse-diff} is also the cancellation used throughout both papers'
scale-separated setup.

The result retains uniform strict positivity at time zero. Removing that
condition while keeping an estimate from \(t=0\) is different: the logarithm
used in the relative-entropy comparison is singular at zero. The cited paper
does give delayed-time estimates on \([\delta,T]\) without this positivity,
but no all-time strengthening is claimed here.

Before promotion, the run indexes were searched for arXiv:2602.17579, its
title, Theorem 5.6, and initial-data convergence phrases. A bounded arXiv/web
search on 13 August 2026 used the exact conjecture sentence and close
variants. It found the source and arXiv:2201.10256, but no later answer.
