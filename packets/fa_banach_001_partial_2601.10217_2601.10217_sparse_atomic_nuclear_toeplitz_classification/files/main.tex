\textbf{Status.} Candidate substantial partial result, likely valid.  The
arbitrary-measure problem remains open, but the theorem below gives a complete
criterion on an explicit infinite family of positive measures and produces
nuclear examples of infinite total mass.

\section*{Source question}

Ma, Lu, and Zu ask for a necessary and sufficient condition for a positive
measure $\mu$ to induce a nuclear map
$T_\mu:F_\alpha^p\to F_\alpha^q$ when $1\le p<q\le\infty$
\cite{MaLuZu}.

\section*{Proof intuition}

Widely separated normalized Fock kernels behave like the unit-vector basis of
$\ell^t$, simultaneously for all $1\le t\le\infty$.  For points $z_j=jR$, the
analysis--synthesis Gram matrix has entries
$e^{-\alpha R^2(i-j)^2/2}$.  If $R$ is large, its off-diagonal part has norm
less than one on every $\ell^t$, so the Fock analysis and synthesis maps have
bounded one-sided inverses.

An atomic Toeplitz operator on these points is therefore, up to these bounded
maps, exactly the diagonal operator $D_c:\ell^p\to\ell^q$.  Tong's classical
diagonal theorem identifies nuclearity of $D_c$ with an $\ell^s$ condition,
where $1/s=1-1/p+1/q$.  The inverse factorization makes this condition
necessary as well as sufficient.  Since $s>1$ away from the extreme endpoint,
the criterion allows non-summable weights and hence measures of infinite mass.

\section*{Statement}

Fix $\alpha>0$ and $1\le p<q\le\infty$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s}
             \frac1s=1-\frac1p+\frac1q.
\end{equation}
Thus $1<s<\infty$ unless $(p,q)=(1,\infty)$, when $s=\infty$.
Choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R}
                 R>\sqrt{\frac{2\log 3}{\alpha}},
        \qquad z_j=jR\quad(j\ge1),
\end{equation}
and let $c_j\ge0$.  Set $\mu=\sum_{j\ge1}c_j\delta_{z_j}$.

\begin{theorem}[exact sparse-atomic criterion]\label{thm:main}
The measure $\mu$ satisfies the defining kernel-integrability condition of
\cite{MaLuZu} whenever $c$ belongs to the space below, and
\[
 T_\mu\in\Nuc(F_\alpha^p,F_\alpha^q)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \begin{cases}
 c\in\ell^s,&(p,q)\ne(1,\infty),\\
 c\in c_0,&(p,q)=(1,\infty).
 \end{cases}
\]
Moreover, with constants depending only on $p,q,\alpha,R$,
\[
 \nu(T_\mu)\asymp
 \begin{cases}
 \|c\|_{\ell^s},&(p,q)\ne(1,\infty),\\
 \|c\|_{\ell^\infty},&(p,q)=(1,\infty),\ c\in c_0,
 \end{cases}
\]
where $\nu$ denotes the nuclear norm.
\end{theorem}

\section*{Analysis, synthesis, and the Gram inverse}

The normalized kernel is
\[
 k_w(z)=e^{\alpha\bar w z-\alpha|w|^2/2}.
\]
For $1\le t\le\infty$, define
\[
 A_t f=\bigl(f(z_j)e^{-\alpha|z_j|^2/2}\bigr)_{j\ge1},
 \qquad
 S_t a=\sum_{j\ge1}a_jk_{z_j}.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:maps}
$A_t:F_\alpha^t\to\ell^t$ and $S_t:\ell^t\to F_\alpha^t$ are bounded.  Their
composition $G_t=A_tS_t$ is invertible on $\ell^t$.  Consequently
\[
       R_t:=S_tG_t^{-1}\quad\hbox{satisfies}\quad A_tR_t=I_{\ell^t},
\]
and
\[
       L_t:=G_t^{-1}A_t\quad\hbox{satisfies}\quad L_tS_t=I_{\ell^t}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $0<\rho<R/2$.  The standard submean estimate, applied after a Fock
translation, gives for $t<\infty$
\[
 |f(z_j)|^t e^{-t\alpha|z_j|^2/2}
 \le C_{t,\alpha,\rho}
 \int_{D(z_j,\rho)}|f(z)|^t e^{-t\alpha|z|^2/2}\,dA(z).
\]
The disks are disjoint, so summation proves boundedness of $A_t$.  For
$t=\infty$ it follows directly from the Fock norm.

For synthesis, pointwise
\[
 |S_ta(z)|e^{-\alpha|z|^2/2}
 \le\sum_j|a_j|e^{-\alpha|z-z_j|^2/2}.
\]
Integration gives the $\ell^1\to F_\alpha^1$ bound, while the uniformly bounded
Gaussian sum gives the $\ell^\infty\to F_\alpha^\infty$ bound.  Interpolation
(or the same discrete convolution estimate) gives all intermediate $t$.

Because all $z_j$ are real,
\[
 (G_ta)_i=\sum_{j\ge1}e^{-\alpha R^2(i-j)^2/2}a_j.
\]
Put $u=\alpha R^2/2>\log3$.  The off-diagonal row and column sums are at most
\[
 2\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-un^2}
 \le \frac{2e^{-u}}{1-e^{-u}}<1.
\]
Young's inequality (including its endpoint forms) therefore gives
$\|G_t-I\|_{\ell^t\to\ell^t}<1$ for every $t$.  The Neumann series supplies
$G_t^{-1}$, and the one-sided inverse identities follow.
\end{proof}

\section*{Proof of the classification}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
First, the relevant coefficient spaces are contained in $\ell^\infty$.  For
each fixed $z\in\C$, completing the square shows
\[
 \sum_j c_j|K_\alpha(z,z_j)|e^{-\alpha|z_j|^2}
 \le \|c\|_\infty e^{\alpha(\operatorname{Re}z)^2/4}
       \sum_j e^{-\alpha(z_j-\operatorname{Re}z/2)^2}<\infty.
\]
Thus $T_\mu$ is defined on the kernel span.

Let $D_c:\ell^p\to\ell^q$ be the diagonal map
$D_c(a_j)=(c_ja_j)$.  Direct substitution into the definition of $T_\mu$
gives the exact factorization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor}
                 T_\mu=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}S_qD_cA_p.
\end{equation}
Tong's diagonal nuclearity theorem \cite{Tong,HLS} says
\[
 D_c\in\Nuc(\ell^p,\ell^q)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \begin{cases}
 c\in\ell^s,&(p,q)\ne(1,\infty),\\
 c\in c_0,&(p,q)=(1,\infty),
 \end{cases}
\]
and its nuclear norm is the displayed sequence norm.  Hence the stated
condition implies nuclearity of $T_\mu$ by \eqref{eq:factor} and the operator
ideal property.

Conversely, Lemma~\ref{lem:maps} and \eqref{eq:factor} give
\[
                 D_c=\frac{\pi}{\alpha}L_qT_\mu R_p.
\]
If $T_\mu$ is nuclear, so is $D_c$, and Tong's theorem gives the necessary
sequence condition.  Applying the nuclear ideal norm inequality to both
factorizations yields the two-sided norm estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[positive infinite-mass nuclear symbols]\label{cor:mass}
For every $1\le p<q\le\infty$, there is a positive measure $\mu$ with
$\mu(\C)=\infty$ such that
$T_\mu\in\Nuc(F_\alpha^p,F_\alpha^q)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $s<\infty$, choose $a$ with $1/s<a\le1$ and set $c_j=j^{-a}$; then
$c\in\ell^s\setminus\ell^1$.  At $(p,q)=(1,\infty)$ take $c_j=1/j$, which
belongs to $c_0\setminus\ell^1$.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

\section*{Scope, verification, and novelty}

The source proves that finite total mass is sufficient for all $p,q$ and is
necessary when $q\le p$.  Corollary~\ref{cor:mass} proves that this criterion
cannot extend to $p<q$.  The exponent in \eqref{eq:s} also matches the source's
necessary $L^s$ Berezin-transform condition.  The proof extends to any
normalized-kernel sequence for which the corresponding analysis and synthesis
maps are complemented; arbitrary non-atomic and densely atomic measures remain
open because their local cell operators need not reduce to scalar diagonal
blocks.

All normalization constants and inverse identities were checked directly; no
computation is used.  A bounded search on 11 August 2026 covered the run indexes,
the exact wording of Question 3.8, the July 2026 accepted manuscript, and
keyword searches for nuclear positive-measure Fock Toeplitz maps in the $p<q$
range.  No later answer was found.  Tong's theorem is classical prior
background; novelty confidence for the Fock reduction and infinite-mass
consequence is moderate-high.

\textbf{Human-review recommendation.} Check the endpoint statement of Tong's
theorem and the factor $\alpha/\pi$ in \eqref{eq:factor}.  The Gram estimate is
uniform in $t$ and includes both endpoints.
