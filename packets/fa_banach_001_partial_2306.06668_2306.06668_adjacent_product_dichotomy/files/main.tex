\title{Adjacent product-derivative inequalities:\\
a finite-exponent dichotomy and endpoint obstructions}
\author{Run \texttt{fa\_banach\_001} --- candidate substantial partial result}
\date{August 11, 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Open Problem~1.10 of Marbach asks when a mean-order Sobolev norm is
controlled by the norm of a pointwise product of derivatives.  We completely
classify the subfamily whose integer derivative orders are supported on three
consecutive values: the desired inequality holds for every finite Lebesgue
exponent, with an explicit constant, and fails at the supremum endpoint.  We
also prove the $q=1$ estimate for every symmetric two-factor gap and give two
sharp endpoint results for the first fractional pair $(0,1)$.  General tuples
remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source problem and statement of the partial result}

The source is Fr\'ed\'eric Marbach, \emph{A family of interpolation
inequalities involving products of low-order derivatives},
arXiv:2306.06668~\cite{Marbach}.  Open Problem~1.10 on PDF page~4 asks for
which $q\in[1,\infty]$ and integers
\(0\le k_1\le\cdots\le k_\kappa\), with
\(\bar k=\kappa^{-1}\sum_i k_i\), one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
 \|D^{\bar k}u\|_{L^{q\kappa}(\R)}
 \lesssim
 \|D^{k_1}u\cdots D^{k_\kappa}u\|_{L^q(\R)}^{1/\kappa}
 \qquad (u\in C_c^\infty(\R;\R)),
\end{equation}
with the usual fractional interpretation when $\bar k\notin\mathbb N$.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.91\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop_page4.png}
\caption{Open Problem~1.10 and the source paper's three examples
(arXiv:2306.06668, PDF page~4).}
\end{figure}

Our main partial answer is the following exact dichotomy.

\begin{theorem}[Adjacent integer orders]\label{thm:adjacent}
Let $j\ge1$, $a\ge1$, and $b\ge0$ be integers, and set
$\kappa=2a+b$.
For every $1\le q<\infty$ and $u\in C_c^\infty(\R;\R)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjacent}
 \|D^j u\|_{L^{q\kappa}}
 \le (q\kappa-1)^{a/\kappa}
 \big\|(D^{j-1}u)^a(D^ju)^b(D^{j+1}u)^a\big\|_{L^q}^{1/\kappa}.
\end{equation}
For $q=\infty$, there is no finite constant $C$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjacent-infty}
 \|D^ju\|_{L^\infty}
 \le C
 \big\|(D^{j-1}u)^a(D^ju)^b(D^{j+1}u)^a\big\|_{L^\infty}^{1/\kappa}
\end{equation}
for every $u\in C_c^\infty(\R;\R)$.
\end{theorem}

The derivative multiset in this theorem has $a$ copies of $j-1$, $b$ copies
of $j$, and $a$ copies of $j+1$.  Its mean is $j$.  Conversely, every
mean-$j$ multiset supported on $\{j-1,j,j+1\}$ has exactly this form.  Thus
Theorem~\ref{thm:adjacent} classifies the entire adjacent-order subfamily of
the source problem.

\begin{theorem}[All symmetric gaps at $q=1$]\label{thm:gaps}
Let $j\ge d\ge1$ be integers.  Then every $u\in C_c^\infty(\R;\R)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaps}
 \|D^j u\|_{L^2}
 \le \big\|D^{j-d}u\,D^{j+d}u\big\|_{L^1}^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Finally, the first fractional pair already shows different endpoint
behavior.  We use the standard Gagliardo seminorm at finite exponent and the
homogeneous H\"older interpretation
\(\seminorm{u}=\sup_{x\ne y}|u(x)-u(y)|/|x-y|^{1/2}\) at infinity.

\begin{theorem}[The fractional pair $(0,1)$]\label{thm:fractional}
There is no constant $C$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frac-one}
 [u]_{\dot W^{1/2,2}(\R)}
 \le C\|uu'\|_{L^1}^{1/2}
 \qquad(u\in C_c^\infty(\R;\R)).
\end{equation}
At the supremum endpoint, however, every $u\in C_c^\infty(\R;\R)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frac-infty}
 \seminorm{u}\le2\|uu'\|_{L^\infty}^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The source proves the same pair at $q=2$ (its Lemma~1.11).  We do not settle
the other finite exponents here.

\section{Idea of the proof}

For the adjacent family, put $f=D^{j-1}u$.  The prescribed product becomes
$f^a(f')^b(f'')^a$.  One integration by parts writes the $L^{q\kappa}$
energy of $f'$ as an integral containing one copy of $ff''$.  Taking the
$a$-th root of the full prescribed product and applying H\"older closes
exactly: the identity $\kappa=2a+b$ is precisely the exponent balance.

The divergence of the resulting constant as $q\to\infty$ is not an artifact.
The autonomous equation $y''=A(y)/y$ keeps $|yy''|$ bounded while its energy
lets $|y'|^2$ accumulate a logarithm over a large range of values.  A cutoff
made in the \emph{value variable} turns one periodic orbit into a smooth
compactly supported bump without losing either feature.  Separated finite
differences impose the vanishing moments needed to make that bump a high
derivative of another compactly supported function.

For the fractional $q=1$ obstruction, smooth approximations to an interval
indicator have bounded variation of $u^2$, hence bounded $\|uu'\|_1$, but
critical $H^{1/2}$ energy growing like the logarithm of the inverse transition
width.  At $q=\infty$, the elementary identity $(u^2)'=2uu'$ gives the
opposite conclusion after separating same-sign and opposite-sign pairs.

\section{Finite exponents and symmetric gaps}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the finite-$q$ part of Theorem~\ref{thm:adjacent}]
Set
\[
 f=D^{j-1}u,\qquad P=q\kappa,\qquad
 G=|f|^a|f'|^b|f''|^a,
 \qquad I=\int_\R |f'|^P.
\]
Since $P\ge2$, integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ibp}
 I=-(P-1)\int_\R f f''|f'|^{P-2}
 \le(P-1)\int_\R G^{1/a}|f'|^{P-2-b/a}.
\end{equation}
Suppose first that $aq>1$.  H\"older's inequality with conjugate exponents
$aq$ and $aq/(aq-1)$ applies because
\begin{equation}\label{eq:holder-id}
 \left(P-2-\frac ba\right)\frac{aq}{aq-1}=P;
\end{equation}
indeed $P=q(2a+b)$.  Thus
\[
 I\le(P-1)
 \left(\int_\R G^q\right)^{1/(aq)} I^{(aq-1)/(aq)}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 I^{1/q}\le(P-1)^a\|G\|_{L^q}.
\]
But $I^{1/q}=\|f'\|_{L^P}^{P/q}=\|f'\|_{L^P}^{\kappa}$, so taking
$\kappa$-th roots proves \eqref{eq:adjacent}.

If $aq=1$, then $a=q=1$ and $P=2+b$.  The residual exponent in
\eqref{eq:ibp} is $P-2-b=0$, so the same conclusion follows directly.  This
also covers the only limiting case omitted from the H\"older calculation.
\end{proof}

For $a=1$ and $b=0$, the argument is the familiar nonlinear
Gagliardo--Nirenberg/Opial inequality.  Ka{\l}amajska--Peszek give this
finite-exponent inequality, in greater weighted generality, in
arXiv:1104.1967~\cite{KalamajskaPeszek}.  The point here is the exact repeated
factor extension and its placement in the source problem.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gaps}]
Integrating by parts $d$ times gives the exact identity
\[
 \int_\R D^{j-d}u\,D^{j+d}u
 =(-1)^d\int_\R (D^ju)^2.
\]
Taking absolute values and then applying the triangle inequality proves
\eqref{eq:gaps}.
\end{proof}

\section{The supremum counterexample}

We first construct the basic bad bump.

\begin{lemma}[Phase-plane bump]\label{lem:bump}
There is a universal $K<\infty$ and a family of nonnegative functions
$f_\varepsilon\in C_c^\infty(\R)$, $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bump}
 \|f_\varepsilon f_\varepsilon''\|_{L^\infty}\le K,
 \qquad
 \|f_\varepsilon'\|_{L^\infty}^2
 \ge2\log\frac1\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $A\in C^\infty((0,\infty);[-1,1])$ such that $A(y)=1$ for $y\le1$
and $A(y)=-1$ for $y\ge e$.  Define
\[
 H_\varepsilon(y)=2\int_\varepsilon^y\frac{A(t)}t\,\dd t.
\]
For small $\varepsilon$, $H_\varepsilon$ is positive immediately to the
right of $\varepsilon$, satisfies
$H_\varepsilon(1)=2\log(1/\varepsilon)$, and has a first zero
$Y_\varepsilon>e$.  The solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ode}
 y''=\frac{A(y)}y,\qquad y(0)=\varepsilon,\qquad y'(0)=0
\end{equation}
obeys the conserved energy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 (y')^2=H_\varepsilon(y).
\end{equation}
It travels from the minimum $\varepsilon$ to the turning point
$Y_\varepsilon$ in finite time and, by time reversal, returns smoothly to
$\varepsilon$.  Let $[0,T_\varepsilon]$ be this min-to-min period.

Choose a smooth step $\rho$ which is zero on $(-\infty,0]$, one on
$[1,\infty)$, and flat at both endpoints, and set
\[
 \chi_\varepsilon(y)=y\rho\left(\frac{y-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right).
\]
Define $f_\varepsilon(x)=\chi_\varepsilon(y(x))$ on
$[0,T_\varepsilon]$ and zero outside.  Flatness of $\rho$ at zero makes this
extension $C^\infty$.

When $y\ge2\varepsilon$, one has $\chi_\varepsilon(y)=y$, hence
$|f_\varepsilon f_\varepsilon''|=|yy''|=|A(y)|\le1$.  In the transition
strip $\varepsilon\le y\le2\varepsilon$,
\[
 |\chi_\varepsilon|\le2\varepsilon,\qquad
 |\chi_\varepsilon'|\le C,\qquad
 |\chi_\varepsilon''|\le C/\varepsilon,\qquad
 (y')^2=2\log(y/\varepsilon)\le2\log2,\qquad
 |y''|\le1/\varepsilon.
\]
The chain rule
$f_\varepsilon''=\chi_\varepsilon''(y)(y')^2+\chi_\varepsilon'(y)y''$
therefore gives a universal bound $K$ in \eqref{eq:bump}.  At $y=1$, the
cutoff is the identity and \eqref{eq:energy} gives
$|f_\varepsilon'|^2=2\log(1/\varepsilon)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the $q=\infty$ part of Theorem~\ref{thm:adjacent}]
For $j=1$, take $u=f_\varepsilon$.  Writing
$M_\varepsilon=\|f_\varepsilon'\|_\infty$ and using
Lemma~\ref{lem:bump},
\[
 \|f_\varepsilon^a(f_\varepsilon')^b(f_\varepsilon'')^a\|_\infty
 \le K^aM_\varepsilon^b.
\]
The ratio of the left side of \eqref{eq:adjacent-infty} to its product term
is therefore at least
\[
 K^{-a/\kappa}M_\varepsilon^{1-b/\kappa}
 =\left(\frac{M_\varepsilon^2}{K}\right)^{a/\kappa}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Now let $j>1$ and put $n=j-1$.  Choose $h$ larger than the diameter of the
support of $f_\varepsilon$, and form the separated finite difference
\[
 g_\varepsilon(x)=\sum_{r=0}^n(-1)^r\binom nr
 f_\varepsilon(x-rh).
\]
Its translated supports are disjoint.  Moreover, for $0\le m<n$,
\[
 \int_\R x^m g_\varepsilon(x)\,\dd x
 =\int_\R f_\varepsilon(t)
   \sum_{r=0}^n(-1)^r\binom nr(t+rh)^m\,\dd t=0,
\]
because an $n$-th finite difference annihilates polynomials of degree below
$n$.  Hence there exists $u_\varepsilon\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ with
$D^n u_\varepsilon=g_\varepsilon$; explicitly, for $n\ge1$, take
\[
 u_\varepsilon(x)=\frac1{(n-1)!}
 \int_{-\infty}^x(x-t)^{n-1}g_\varepsilon(t)\,\dd t.
\]
The vanishing moments make this primitive zero beyond the support.

On each translated component, $g_\varepsilon$ and its first two derivatives
are the same binomial coefficient times the corresponding derivatives of
$f_\varepsilon$.  The coefficient cancels from the homogeneous ratio above.
Thus the same divergent ratio holds with
$(D^{j-1}u_\varepsilon,D^ju_\varepsilon,D^{j+1}u_\varepsilon)
=(g_\varepsilon,g_\varepsilon',g_\varepsilon'')$, completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{The first fractional pair}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the failure in Theorem~\ref{thm:fractional}]
Fix a smooth increasing step $\eta$ from zero to one on $[0,1]$, flat at
both endpoints.  For $0<\delta<1/8$, let $u_\delta$ be zero outside $[0,1]$,
equal to $\eta(x/\delta)$ on $[0,\delta]$, equal to one on
$[\delta,1-\delta]$, and equal to
$\eta((1-x)/\delta)$ on $[1-\delta,1]$.  Then $u_\delta\in C_c^\infty$ and
\[
 \|u_\delta u_\delta'\|_{L^1}
 =\frac12\operatorname{Var}(u_\delta^2)=1.
\]
On the other hand, $u_\delta(x)=1$ for $x\in[2\delta,1/2]$ and
$u_\delta(y)=0$ for $y\le0$.  Consequently
\begin{align*}
 [u_\delta]_{\dot W^{1/2,2}}^2
 &\ge \int_{2\delta}^{1/2}\int_{-x}^{0}
       \frac{1}{(x-y)^2}\,\dd y\,\dd x \\
 &=\frac12\int_{2\delta}^{1/2}\frac{\dd x}{x}
 =\frac12\log\frac1{4\delta}\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{align*}
This contradicts any uniform form of \eqref{eq:frac-one}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the positive endpoint in Theorem~\ref{thm:fractional}]
Let $M=\|uu'\|_\infty$.  Since $(u^2)'=2uu'$, the function $u^2$ is
$2M$-Lipschitz.  If $u(x)$ and $u(y)$ have the same sign, then
\[
 |u(x)-u(y)|^2
 \le |u(x)^2-u(y)^2|\le2M|x-y|.
\]
If their signs differ, continuity gives $z$ between $x$ and $y$ with
$u(z)=0$.  Therefore
\[
 |u(x)-u(y)|^2=(|u(x)|+|u(y)|)^2
 \le2(|u(x)|^2+|u(y)|^2)
 \le4M|x-y|.
\]
Taking the supremum over $x\ne y$ proves \eqref{eq:frac-infty}.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, limitations, and novelty}

The deterministic checker evaluates Theorem~\ref{thm:adjacent} on five
Schwartz-function parameter sets, checks the signed integration-by-parts
identity in Theorem~\ref{thm:gaps} for five gaps through $d=5$, and samples
the phase-plane construction for four values of
$\log(1/\varepsilon)$.  All checks pass.  They are sanity tests, not parts of
the proof.

The full source problem remains open.  In particular, the present argument
does not control wider gaps at $q>1$: further integrations by parts generate
intermediate derivative monomials absent from the prescribed product.  Apart
from the two endpoints in Theorem~\ref{thm:fractional}, general noninteger
mean orders are also untouched.

The bounded novelty check covered the run's registry, solution, attempt, and
proof-gap indexes; the exact source id/title and Open Problem~1.10; searches
for the displayed adjacent and supremum inequalities; the nonlinear
Gagliardo--Nirenberg paper of Ka{\l}amajska--Peszek; and the later
higher-order Opial paper of Klaassen~\cite{Klaassen}.  The known
$a=1,b=0$ finite-exponent case is explicitly credited above.  No located
source states the repeated-factor adjacent dichotomy, the compactly supported
supremum obstruction, or their identification with Marbach's problem.
Novelty is therefore plausible but provisional.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Review as a substantial partial result.  The most sensitive points are the
uniform transition-strip estimate in Lemma~\ref{lem:bump} and the moment
argument ensuring that the finite-difference primitive is compactly
supported.  The finite-$q$ proof and both fractional endpoint arguments are
elementary.
