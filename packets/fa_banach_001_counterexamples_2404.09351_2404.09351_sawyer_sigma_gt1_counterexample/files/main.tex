\section{Source question}

The source is E. Roure-Perdices~\cite{Roure}.  The paper is titled
\emph{Extrapolation via Sawyer-type inequalities}.  Its arXiv identifier is
2404.09351v1, and the question is Conjecture~5.11 on pp.~31--32.  The complete source
statement is reproduced below as two full-width crops because it crosses a
page boundary.

For clarity, write
\[
 \varrho=1+\varsigma(q-1).
\]
The unrestricted form asserts that if $u^\varsigma\in\AR_{\varrho}$ and
$u^\varsigma v^\varrho\in\AR_r$ for some $r\geq1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conjecture}
 \left\|\frac{M\bigl(|f|^{q/\varrho}
 u^{(1-\varsigma)/\varrho}\bigr)}{v}\right\|^
 {\varrho}_{L^{\varrho,\infty}(u^\varsigma v^\varrho)}
 \leq
 \phi\!\left([u^\varsigma]_{\AR_{\varrho}},
 [u^\varsigma v^\varrho]_{\AR_r}\right)
 \|f\|_{L^{q,1}(u)}^q,
\end{equation}
with $\phi$ independent of $f$.

We use the source normalization
\[
 \|g\|_{L^{p,1}(w)}=p\int_0^\infty
 \lambda_g^w(t)^{1/p}\,dt,
 \qquad
 \|g\|_{L^{p,\infty}(w)}
 =\sup_{t>0}t\lambda_g^w(t)^{1/p}.
\]

\section{Proof intuition}

At the sharp endpoint, the power weight $|x|^{p-1}$ is in the restricted
class $\AR_p$ even though it is not a classical $A_p$ weight.  This lets us
make the maximal-function input exactly $x^{-1}\mathbf1_{[1,N]}$.  Its
average on $[0,N]$ is $(\log N)/N$, and the endpoint weight gives
$[0,N]$ mass of order $N^\varrho$; hence the weak target quasi-norm to the
$\varrho$-th power grows like $(\log N)^\varrho$.  The source Lorentz norm
of $x^{-1}\mathbf1_{[1,N]}$ grows only like $\log N$, so the right side of
\eqref{eq:conjecture} grows like $(\log N)^q$.  The exponent gap is
\(\varrho-q=(\varsigma-1)(q-1)\), which is positive precisely in the
refuted range $\varsigma>1$.

\section{The counterexample}

We first verify the only endpoint-weight fact needed in the construction.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:endpoint}
For every $p>1$, the weight $w_p(x)=|x|^{p-1}$ on $\R$ belongs to
$\AR_p$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We use the equivalent defining condition from the source:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restricted-def}
 \sup_I\sup_{E\subseteq I}
 \frac{|E|}{|I|}\left(\frac{w_p(I)}{w_p(E)}\right)^{1/p}<\infty,
\end{equation}
where $I$ ranges over intervals and $E$ over measurable subsets of positive
measure.

Put $L=|I|$ and $m=|E|$.  Among all measurable subsets of $\R$ of measure
$m$, the integral of the even increasing rearrangement $|x|^{p-1}$ is
minimized by $[-m/2,m/2]$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-lower}
 w_p(E)\geq \frac{m^p}{p,2^{p-1}}.
\end{equation}
If $I$ meets the origin, then $|x|\leq L$ on $I$, so $w_p(I)\leq L^p$;
\eqref{eq:global-lower} gives
$w_p(E)/w_p(I)\geq (p2^{p-1})^{-1}(m/L)^p$.

Suppose next that $I=(a,b)$ lies in $(0,\infty)$; the negative case is
symmetric.  If $a<L$, then $b<2L$, whence
$w_p(I)\leq2^{p-1}L^p$ and the preceding argument applies with another
constant depending only on $p$.  If $a\geq L$, then $b\leq2a$ and
\[
 \frac{w_p(E)}{w_p(I)}
 \geq \frac{a^{p-1}m}{b^{p-1}L}
 \geq 2^{1-p}\frac mL
 \geq 2^{1-p}\left(\frac mL\right)^p.
\]
Thus in every case $w_p(E)/w_p(I)\geq c_p(m/L)^p$, which is exactly the
finiteness of \eqref{eq:restricted-def}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:counterexample}
For every $q>1$ and $\varsigma>1$, the estimate in the unrestricted
$\varsigma>0$ form of Conjecture~5.11 is false.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $q>1$ and $\varsigma>1$, and set
\[
 \varrho=1+\varsigma(q-1)>q,
 \qquad u(x)=|x|^{q-1},\qquad v(x)=1.
\]
Then
\[
 u^\varsigma(x)=|x|^{\varsigma(q-1)}=|x|^{\varrho-1}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint}, with $p=\varrho$, shows that
$u^\varsigma\in\AR_{\varrho}$.  Since $v=1$, the second hypothesis also
holds by choosing $r=\varrho$.  Both characteristics appearing in $\phi$
are fixed independently of the parameter below.

For $N>2$, define
\[
 f_N(x)=x^{-1}\mathbf1_{[1,N]}(x).
\]
The input to the maximal operator simplifies exactly:
\begin{align*}
 |f_N(x)|^{q/\varrho}u(x)^{(1-\varsigma)/\varrho}
 &=x^{[-q+(q-1)(1-\varsigma)]/\varrho}\mathbf1_{[1,N]}(x)\\
 &=x^{-1}\mathbf1_{[1,N]}(x),
\end{align*}
because $-q+(q-1)(1-\varsigma)=-\varrho$.
For every $x\in[0,N]$, the interval $[0,N]$ is admissible in the uncentered
Hardy--Littlewood maximal operator, and hence
\[
 M(x^{-1}\mathbf1_{[1,N]})(x)\geq\frac{\log N}{N}.
\]
Taking a threshold half this value and using
$u^\varsigma([0,N])=N^\varrho/\varrho$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lhs}
 \bigl\|M(x^{-1}\mathbf1_{[1,N]})\bigr\|^
 {\varrho}_{L^{\varrho,\infty}(u^\varsigma)}
 \geq \frac{(\log N)^\varrho}{2^\varrho\varrho}.
\end{equation}

It remains to bound the source norm.  With respect to $u(x)dx$, the
distribution function of $f_N$ satisfies
\[
 \lambda_{f_N}^u(t)\leq
 \begin{cases}
 N^q/q,&0<t<N^{-1},\\
 (qt^q)^{-1},&N^{-1}\leq t<1,\\
 0,&t\geq1.
 \end{cases}
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rhs}
 \|f_N\|_{L^{q,1}(u)}
 =q\int_0^\infty\lambda_{f_N}^u(t)^{1/q}\,dt
 \leq q^{1-1/q}(1+\log N).
\end{equation}
If \eqref{eq:conjecture} held, then \eqref{eq:lhs} and
\eqref{eq:rhs}, with the fixed weight characteristics absorbed into a
constant, would imply
\[
 (\log N)^\varrho\leq C_{q,\varsigma}(1+\log N)^q
 \qquad(N>2).
\]
This is impossible because
$\varrho-q=(\varsigma-1)(q-1)>0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, stress tests, and verification}

The construction disproves exactly the unrestricted clause $\varsigma>0$.
It does not disprove the parenthetical alternative $0<\varsigma\leq1$.
Indeed the same calculation has target/source logarithmic exponent gap
$(\varsigma-1)(q-1)$, so it is neutral at $\varsigma=1$ (the case the source
already records as true) and points in the non-obstructive direction for
$\varsigma<1$.

Three variants were checked.  Adding logarithmic factors to the endpoint
power weight changes both sides by the same slowly varying contribution and
does not move the decisive gap.  Replacing $v=1$ by a power weight introduces
extra compatibility constraints but does not extend this counterexample into
$\varsigma\leq1$.  Finally, the second conjecture in Section~8.2 of the source
has a different weighted-measure geometry and is not claimed to follow or
fail from this example.

The separate \texttt{verification.md} audits each exponent and the direct
$\AR_p$ proof.  The script \texttt{code/check\_growth.py} prints finite-scale
ratios for representative parameters; it is illustrative and is not used as
proof.

\section{Novelty audit and human-review recommendation}

A bounded search on 13 August 2026 covered the exact paper title and arXiv id,
``Conjecture 5.11'', ``Sawyer-type inequalities'', ``restricted weak type'',
and the notation $A_p^{\mathcal R}$, including the authors' earlier
Sawyer-type paper~\cite{PerezRoure}.  No later arXiv paper proving or refuting
this exact conjecture, and no occurrence of this endpoint power-weight
counterexample, was located.  The novelty claim is therefore provisional.

Human review is recommended.  The two points deserving the closest check are
Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint} and the Lorentz normalization in
\eqref{eq:rhs}.  Both are elementary and fully reproduced, so verification
does not depend on an external classification theorem.
