\section{Source questions}

The paper \cite{Henderson} asks:
\begin{enumerate}
\item whether Sobolev small-ball problems can be handled using spectral
properties of the covariance, such as its nuclear norm;
\item whether every Gaussian measure on \(W^{m,p}(\mathcal D)\) is induced
by some Gaussian process.
\end{enumerate}
It notes that its Proposition~2.9 gives the second statement for \(m=0\),
that is, on \(L^p(\mathcal D)\).  We completely answer (ii), and give exact
limitations on the nuclear-norm suggestion in (i).

\section{Idea of the proof}

Forget the derivatives and push the Sobolev Gaussian measure into
\(L^p(\mathcal D)\).  Proposition~2.9 realizes that pushforward as the law
of the sample classes of a jointly measurable Gaussian process.  Those
classes are supported on the Sobolev image.  Although the image need not be
closed in \(L^p\), the Lusin--Souslin theorem makes it Borel and makes the
inverse embedding Borel.  Applying that inverse recovers exactly the
original Sobolev-valued law.

\section{An abstract realization theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:abstract}
Let \(1\le p<\infty\), let \(\mathcal D\subset\mathbb R^d\) be open, and
let \(X\) be a separable Banach space equipped with a continuous injective
linear map
\[
 J:X\longrightarrow L^p(\mathcal D).
\]
Assume the \(L^p\)-realization statement of Proposition~2.9 of
\cite{Henderson}.  Then every Gaussian Borel probability measure \(\mu\)
on \(X\) is induced by a jointly measurable Gaussian process
\((U(x))_{x\in\mathcal D}\): its sample-class map is almost surely in
\(J(X)\), the map
\[
 \omega\longmapsto J^{-1}[U_\omega]\in X
\]
is Borel measurable after an arbitrary definition on a null set, and its
law is \(\mu\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First suppose that \(\mu\) is centered and put \(\nu=J_*\mu\).  This is a
centered Gaussian measure on \(L^p(\mathcal D)\): for every
\(\ell\in(L^p)^*\), the pullback \(\ell\circ J\) belongs to \(X^*\), so the
\(\ell\)-marginal of \(\nu\) is Gaussian.

By Proposition~2.9, there is a jointly measurable centered Gaussian
process \(U:\Omega\times\mathcal D\to\mathbb R\) such that its sample paths
lie in \(L^p\) almost surely, the class map
\[
 F:\Omega\longrightarrow L^p(\mathcal D),\qquad F(\omega)=[U_\omega],
\]
is Borel, and \(F_*\mathbb P=\nu\).

The spaces \(X\) and \(L^p(\mathcal D)\) are Polish.  Since \(J\) is Borel
and injective, the Lusin--Souslin theorem implies that
\[
 C:=J(X)
\]
is Borel in \(L^p\), that \(J^{-1}:C\to X\) is Borel, and, more generally,
that \(J(A)\) is Borel in \(L^p\) for every Borel \(A\subset X\).
Furthermore,
\[
 \mathbb P(F\in C)=\nu(C)=\mu(X)=1.
\]
Define \(G=J^{-1}\circ F\) on this full-probability event and define it
arbitrarily on its complement.  Then \(G:\Omega\to X\) is Borel.  For
every Borel \(A\subset X\),
\[
 \mathbb P(G\in A)
 =\mathbb P(F\in J(A))
 =\nu(J(A))
 =\mu(A).
\]
Thus \(G\) has law \(\mu\), and \(F=J(G)\) almost surely.  In particular,
the sample class of \(U\) lies in \(J(X)\) almost surely.

For a noncentered Gaussian measure, its mean \(a\) belongs to \(X\) because
\(X\) is separable.  Apply the centered case to the translate of \(\mu\)
by \(-a\), choose a measurable representative \(\widehat{J(a)}\) of
\(J(a)\), and replace \(U(\omega,x)\) by
\(U(\omega,x)+\widehat{J(a)}(x)\).  Joint measurability and Gaussianity are
preserved, and the resulting \(X\)-valued law is \(\mu\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:sobolev}
Let \(\mathcal D\subset\mathbb R^d\) be open, let \(m\in\mathbb N_0\), and
let \(1\le p<\infty\).  Every Gaussian Borel probability measure on
\(W^{m,p}(\mathcal D)\) is induced by a jointly measurable Gaussian
process with almost surely \(W^{m,p}\) sample classes, and the
Sobolev-valued sample-class map has exactly the prescribed law.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The Sobolev space is a separable Banach space for finite \(p\), and the
canonical map
\[
 J:W^{m,p}(\mathcal D)\hookrightarrow L^p(\mathcal D)
\]
is continuous and injective.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract}.
\end{proof}

The result includes \(p=1\), beyond the range \(1<p<\infty\) of the main
Sobolev theorems in the source, because its \(L^p\)-realization
Proposition~2.9 is valid for \(1\le p<\infty\).  The argument does not
cover \(p=\infty\), where both separability and the cited realization
theorem fail in general.

\section{What nuclear norm cannot say about small balls}

Question (i) is broad, but its suggested scalar invariant has an exact
negative answer.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:trace}
Nuclear norm alone does not determine norm-small-ball probabilities, even
for Gaussian measures on a Hilbert Sobolev space.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(H=W^{m,2}(\mathcal D)\) have dimension at least two and choose
orthonormal \(f_1,f_2\in C_c^\infty(\mathcal D)\) (start with two
independent test functions and apply Gram--Schmidt in \(H\)).  With
independent standard normal variables,
set
\[
 X_1=\xi f_1,\qquad
 X_2=\frac{\xi_1f_1+\xi_2f_2}{\sqrt2}.
\]
Their covariance operators on \(H\) are
\[
 Q_1=f_1\otimes f_1,\qquad
 Q_2=\tfrac12(f_1\otimes f_1+f_2\otimes f_2),
\]
so both have trace and nuclear norm one.  Nevertheless, for
\(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P(\|X_1\|_H\le\varepsilon)
 =2\Phi(\varepsilon)-1
 \sim\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\varepsilon,
\]
whereas
\[
 \mathbb P(\|X_2\|_H\le\varepsilon)
 =\mathbb P(\xi_1^2+\xi_2^2\le2\varepsilon^2)
 =1-e^{-\varepsilon^2}
 \sim\varepsilon^2.
\]
The small-ball orders themselves differ.
\end{proof}

Even the complete eigenvalue list of the covariance on the
\emph{underlying} \(L^2\) space is insufficient for a Sobolev norm.  On
\(\mathcal D=(0,2\pi)\), let
\[
 e_0=(2\pi)^{-1/2},\qquad e_N(x)=\pi^{-1/2}\sin(Nx).
\]
Both are \(L^2\)-unit vectors, so the \(L^2\)-covariance operators of
\(\xi e_0\) and \(\xi e_N\) have the identical spectrum
\(\{1,0,0,\ldots\}\).  But
\[
 \|e_0\|_{H^1}=1,\qquad \|e_N\|_{H^1}=\sqrt{1+N^2},
\]
and hence their \(H^1\) small-ball probabilities are respectively
\[
 2\Phi(\varepsilon)-1
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 2\Phi\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{1+N^2}}\right)-1.
\]
Thus the Sobolev geometry of the covariance eigenfunctions matters.  By
contrast, the entire spectrum of the covariance \emph{on the Hilbert
Sobolev space itself} does determine the norm-small-ball law through the
distribution of \(\sum_j\lambda_j\xi_j^2\).

\section{Verification and novelty notes}

The realization proof is measure-theoretic and uses no computation.  The
accompanying script checks the exact small-ball formulas at a sample value
of \(\varepsilon\); those formulas are proved analytically above.

question, source title and author, and combinations of the phrases
\emph{Gaussian measure Sobolev process realization},
\emph{Lusin--Souslin}, and \emph{small ball nuclear norm}.  It found the
source but no later answer to these exact questions.  This is not an
