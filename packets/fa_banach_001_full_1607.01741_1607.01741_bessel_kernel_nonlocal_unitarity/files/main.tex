\noindent\textbf{Status.} Candidate full affirmative solution, subject to
expert review.  If $\Omega\subset\R^n$ is nonempty and open and
$\operatorname{int}(\Omega^c)\ne\varnothing$, then
\[
 |_{\Omega}:\widetilde H^s(\Omega)\longrightarrow H^s_0(\Omega)
 \quad\text{is unitary if and only if}\quad s\in\N_0.
\]
This proves the conjecture in Remark 2.13 of Hewett--Moiola \cite{HM}.

\section{Source conjecture}

With the unitary Fourier transform, the source uses
\[
 (u,v)_{H^s(\R^n)}
 =\int_{\R^n}(1+|\xi|^2)^s\widehat u(\xi)
          \overline{\widehat v(\xi)}\,d\xi.
 \tag{1}\label{eq:inner}
\]
Its Lemma 2.10 says that the restriction is unitary exactly when
\[
 \D(\Omega)\subset (H^s_{\Omega^c})^\perp.
 \tag{2}\label{eq:orthogonality}
\]
Proposition 2.12 proves the claimed classification when $\Omega$ is bounded.
Remark 2.13 on printed page 6 asks whether it holds whenever $\Omega^c$ has
nonempty interior:

\section{Proof intuition}

Integer Sobolev norms are local because their multiplier
$(1+|\xi|^2)^m$ is a polynomial; its inverse Fourier transform is supported
at the origin.  For every noninteger $s$, the opposite is true in the
strongest possible form.  Away from the origin, the distribution kernel of
$(1-\Delta)^s$ is a nonzero constant times a strictly positive function.
This follows directly from the heat-kernel/Gamma integral.

Choose one nonnegative test function inside $\Omega$ and another inside
$\operatorname{int}(\Omega^c)$.  Their supports are separated, so their
$H^s$ inner product is an ordinary double integral of that off-diagonal
kernel.  The integrand has one strict sign, and the inner product cannot
vanish.  Condition \eqref{eq:orthogonality} therefore fails for every
$s\notin\N_0$, without any geometric assumption beyond the existence of the
two open regions.

\section{The off-diagonal multiplier kernel}

For $s\in\mathbb C$, let $\kappa_s$ denote the distribution kernel with
Fourier multiplier $(1+|\xi|^2)^s$, normalized so that for separated test
functions
\[
 (\phi,\psi)_{H^s(\R^n)}
 =\iint_{\R^n\times\R^n}
   \kappa_s(y-x)\phi(x)\overline{\psi(y)}\,dx\,dy.
 \tag{3}\label{eq:kernel-pairing}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Off-diagonal Bessel kernel]\label{lem:kernel}
For $x\ne0$ and real $s$,
\[
 \kappa_s(x)
 =\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n/2}\Gamma(-s)}
   \int_0^\infty
   t^{-s-n/2-1}\exp\!\left(-t-\frac{|x|^2}{4t}\right)dt.
 \tag{4}\label{eq:bessel-kernel}
\]
Consequently $\kappa_m(x)=0$ off the origin for $m\in\N_0$, while for every
real $s\notin\N_0$ the function $\kappa_s$ is smooth, nowhere zero, and has
the constant sign of $1/\Gamma(-s)$ on $\R^n\setminus\{0\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First suppose $\operatorname{Re}s<0$.  The Gamma identity gives
\[
 (1+|\xi|^2)^s
 =\frac1{\Gamma(-s)}\int_0^\infty
   t^{-s-1}e^{-t}e^{-t|\xi|^2}\,dt.
\]
The inverse Fourier kernel under the normalization in
\eqref{eq:kernel-pairing} is
\[
 (2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\R^n}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{-t|\xi|^2}\,d\xi
 =(4\pi t)^{-n/2}e^{-|x|^2/(4t)}.
\]
Fubini's theorem yields \eqref{eq:bessel-kernel} in this half-plane.

For fixed $x\ne0$, the integral in \eqref{eq:bessel-kernel} converges
locally uniformly for every complex $s$: $e^{-t}$ controls infinity and
$e^{-|x|^2/(4t)}$ controls zero.  Hence its product with the entire function
$1/\Gamma(-s)$ is entire in $s$.  On the other hand,
$\F^{-1}((1+|\xi|^2)^s)$, restricted to test functions supported away from
the origin, is also an entire distribution-valued function of $s$; on
compact $s$-sets this follows from the polynomial growth of the multiplier
and rapid decay of test-function Fourier transforms.  Analytic continuation
therefore proves \eqref{eq:bessel-kernel} for all $s$ off the origin.

For real $s$ the integral is strictly positive.  The reciprocal Gamma
factor vanishes exactly when $s\in\N_0$ and is finite and nonzero otherwise.
This proves every assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{Main theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $\Omega\subset\R^n$ be nonempty and open, with
$\operatorname{int}(\Omega^c)\ne\varnothing$.  Then
\[
 |_{\Omega}:\widetilde H^s(\Omega)\longrightarrow H^s_0(\Omega)
\]
is a unitary isomorphism if and only if $s$ is a nonnegative integer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $s=m\in\N_0$, the $H^m$ inner product is local: by expanding
$(1+|\xi|^2)^m$ and applying Plancherel, it is a finite sum of $L^2$ inner
products of matching derivatives.  Hence test functions supported in
$\Omega$ are orthogonal to $H^m$ distributions supported in $\Omega^c$.
Condition \eqref{eq:orthogonality}, and therefore unitarity, follows.  This
is also Lemma 2.2(iii) of \cite{HM}.

Now let $s\notin\N_0$.  Choose nonempty balls $U,V$ with
\[
 \overline U\subset\Omega,
 \qquad
 \overline V\subset\operatorname{int}(\Omega^c),
\]
and choose nonzero, nonnegative real functions
$\phi\in\D(U)$ and $\psi\in\D(V)$.  The compact supports are disjoint and
have positive distance.  Thus \eqref{eq:kernel-pairing} is an ordinary
absolutely convergent integral, and Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel} gives
\[
 (\phi,\psi)_{H^s(\R^n)}
 =\iint_{U\times V}\kappa_s(y-x)\phi(x)\psi(y)\,dx\,dy\ne0,
\]
because the integrand has one strict sign wherever
$\phi(x)\psi(y)>0$.  But $\psi\in\D(V)\subset H^s_{\Omega^c}$, so
$\phi\notin(H^s_{\Omega^c})^\perp$.  Condition
\eqref{eq:orthogonality} fails, and the restriction cannot be unitary.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The decisive normalization check is $n=1,s=-1$: formula
\eqref{eq:bessel-kernel} gives
$\kappa_{-1}(x)=\tfrac12e^{-|x|}$, agreeing with the source's explicit
$H^{-1}(\R)$ kernel.  The proof uses only separated compact supports, so no
singularity at the diagonal or distributional local term enters the double
integral.  The Gamma factor explains the sharp threshold: it vanishes
precisely at nonnegative integers, exactly where the multiplier is a
polynomial and the norm is local.

The theorem settles only the unitarity conjecture in Remark 2.13.  It does
not characterize surjectivity for rough open sets, nor the half-integer
images mentioned elsewhere in the paper.

A bounded novelty search through 11 August 2026 checked the run registry and
all lightweight indexes; the arXiv source and published DOI
\texttt{10.1515/jaa-2017-0001}; exact title, authors, proposition, conjecture,
restriction-unitarity, nonempty-interior, Bessel-kernel, and citation
searches.  No later paper or independent resolution was found.  Novelty
