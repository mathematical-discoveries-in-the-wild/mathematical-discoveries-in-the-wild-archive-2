{\Large Literature-status packet}\\[4pt]
{\large Mean Li--Yorke chaos without distributional chaos}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{literature\_already\_answered}.  This note records a direct,
explicit resolution in a separate later paper.

\paragraph{Original question.}
N.~C. Bernardes Jr., A. Bonilla, and A. Peris,
\emph{Mean Li--Yorke chaos in Banach spaces},
arXiv:1804.03900, Question 16 on PDF page 10, ask:
\begin{quote}
Is there a Banach (or Hilbert) space operator which is mean Li--Yorke
chaotic but is not distributionally chaotic?
\end{quote}
The introduction of arXiv:2511.06207 repeats this question as open.

\paragraph{Decisive later answer.}
Jian Li and Jie Li,
\emph{Power growth of mean-L-stable operators on Banach spaces},
arXiv:2608.09694, Proposition 6.1 on PDF pages 23--24, prove that their
operator \(T_1\) on \(X_1\) is densely mean Li--Yorke chaotic but not
distributionally chaotic.  They prove the stronger statement that every pair
in \(X_1\times X_1\) is distributionally asymptotic.  Immediately after the
proof they state that Proposition 6.1 answers Question 16.

\paragraph{Answer.}
The answer is affirmative on Banach spaces.  The same later paper also gives
the Hilbert-space contrast: Proposition 6.2, combined with its Hilbert-space
mean-L-stability theorem, forces densely mean Li--Yorke chaotic Hilbert-space
operators to be densely distributionally chaotic.

\paragraph{Provenance and scope.}
This packet records retrieval of a literature answer posted in August 2026;
it does not claim a new proof.  The identification is exact because the
supporting paper cites the original Question 16 and explicitly says its
Proposition 6.1 answers it.
