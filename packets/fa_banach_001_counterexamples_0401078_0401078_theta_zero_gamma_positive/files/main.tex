\textbf{Outcome.}  For every $m\ge2$ and $0\le k\le m-2$, there are
one-dimensional vector subspaces $\cA\subset W^{m,p}(Q)$ for which
\[
  \Theta_{m,k,p}^{\alpha}(\cA)=0
  \quad\text{but}\quad
  \Gamma_{m,k,p}(\cA)>0.
\]
The positive $\Gamma$ value may be made arbitrarily small.  This supplies the
instances requested in the source and shows that no uniform two-sided
equivalence can hold below the already-known case $k=m-1$.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

\section*{1. The question and the definitions}

For a cube $Q$, let $\cP_k$ be the polynomials of degree at most $k$.  In the
notation of the source,
\[
 U_{P,\cA}=\{w\in W_0^{m,p}(2Q):w|_Q-P\in\cA\}
\]
and Maz'ya's polynomial capacity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma}
 \Gamma_{m,k,p}(\cA)=
 \inf_{0\ne P\in\cP_k}\ \inf_{w\in U_{P,\cA}}
 \frac{\norm{\nabla^m w}_{L^p(2Q)}^p}{\norm P_{L^p(Q)}^p}.
\end{equation}
The source's $\Theta^\alpha$ first restricts to those $u\in\cA$ for which
the degree-$\le k$ part of the polynomial projection dominates its
higher-degree complement by the factor $\alpha$, and whose nonpolynomial
remainder is at most half the polynomial part.  It then takes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta}
 \Theta_{m,k,p}^{\alpha}(\cA)=
 \min\left\{1,\inf_u
 \frac{\norm{\nabla^m u}_{L^p(Q)}^p}
 {\norm{\Pi_{m-1,k,p}u}_{L^p(Q)}^p}\right\}.
\end{equation}

\section*{2. A separation for every nontrivial index}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:general}
Let $Q$ be a cube, $1\le p<\infty$, $m\ge2$, $0\le k\le m-2$, and
$\alpha>0$.  There are one-dimensional vector subspaces $\cA_\varepsilon$
such that
\[
 \Theta_{m,k,p}^{\alpha}(\cA_\varepsilon)=0
 <\Gamma_{m,k,p}(\cA_\varepsilon)<\infty.
\]
Moreover $\Gamma_{m,k,p}(\cA_\varepsilon)\le C\varepsilon^p$, so the
positive capacity can tend to zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose $0\ne P_0\in\cP_k$ and a polynomial
$H\in\cP_{m-1}\setminus\cP_k$ having no terms of degree at most $k$; a
homogeneous polynomial of degree $k+1$ will do.  Put
\[
 a_\varepsilon=P_0+\varepsilon H,
 \qquad \cA_\varepsilon=\operatorname{span}\{a_\varepsilon\},
\]
where $\varepsilon>0$ is small enough that
$\norm{P_0}_p\ge\alpha\varepsilon\norm H_p$.

For every nonzero $t$, the exact polynomial $t a_\varepsilon$ has projection
itself, degree-$\le k$ part $tP_0$, complement $t\varepsilon H$, and zero
nonpolynomial remainder.  It therefore belongs to the restricted test set in
\eqref{eq:theta}.  Since $\deg a_\varepsilon\le m-1$,
$\nabla^m a_\varepsilon=0$, and hence
\[
 \Theta_{m,k,p}^{\alpha}(\cA_\varepsilon)=0.
\]

We prove that $\Gamma>0$.  The higher-order Poincaré inequality on $2Q$
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poincare}
 \norm{\nabla^m w}_{L^p(2Q)}\ge c_Q\norm w_{L^p(Q)}
 \qquad(w\in W_0^{m,p}(2Q)).
\end{equation}
Define the finite-dimensional angle
\[
 \delta_\varepsilon=
 \inf_{\substack{P\in\cP_k,\ \norm P_p=1\\t\in\R}}
 \norm{P+t(P_0+\varepsilon H)}_{L^p(Q)}.
\]
This number is positive.  Indeed, if a minimizing sequence tended to zero,
passing to the quotient $\cP_{m-1}/\cP_k$ would first force
$t\varepsilon[H]\to0$, hence $t\to0$; the original relation would then force
$P\to0$, contradicting $\norm P_p=1$.

For $w\in U_{P,\cA_\varepsilon}$ we have
$w|_Q=P+t a_\varepsilon$ for some $t$.  Scaling $P$ and using
\eqref{eq:poincare} therefore shows
\[
 \frac{\norm{\nabla^m w}_p^p}{\norm P_p^p}
 \ge c_Q^p\delta_\varepsilon^p>0.
\]
Taking the two infima in \eqref{eq:gamma} proves positivity.

Finally, let $\eta\in C_c^\infty(2Q)$ equal one on $Q$.  Take
$P=-P_0$ and $w=\varepsilon\eta H$.  Then
$w|_Q-P=a_\varepsilon\in\cA_\varepsilon$, so this is admissible and gives
\[
 \Gamma_{m,k,p}(\cA_\varepsilon)
 \le\varepsilon^p
 \frac{\norm{\nabla^m(\eta H)}_{L^p(2Q)}^p}
 {\norm{P_0}_{L^p(Q)}^p}.
\]
This also proves finiteness and the asserted small-capacity estimate.
\end{proof}

\section*{3. The smallest explicit example}

\begin{example}
Take
\[
 Q=(-1,1),\quad m=2,\quad k=0,\quad p=2,\quad\alpha=1,
 \qquad \cA=\operatorname{span}\{1+x/2\}.
\]
The constant and linear parts have squared norms $2$ and $1/6$, so the
$\Theta$ dominance test holds, and $\Theta_{2,0,2}^1(\cA)=0$.

For the $\Gamma$ lower bound, write a constant polynomial as $P=c$ and the
line parameter as $t$.  After dividing by $c\ne0$, the normalized squared
trace norm is
\[
 \frac{\norm{1+t(1+x/2)}_{L^2(-1,1)}^2}{\norm1_{L^2(-1,1)}^2}
 =(1+t)^2+\frac{t^2}{12}.
\]
Its minimum is $1/13$.  Thus \eqref{eq:poincare} gives the explicit strict
bound
\[
 \Gamma_{2,0,2}(\cA)\ge c_Q^2/13>0.
\]
\end{example}

\section*{4. Boundary and novelty}

The construction works throughout $k\le m-2$.  At $k=m-1$ there is no
higher polynomial component available, and the source already proves
$\Theta\asymp\Gamma$; the example therefore separates exactly the remaining
index range.  It uses a vector subspace, not an artificial non-vector set.

The result answers the capacities question as defined for arbitrary subsets
of $W^{m,p}(Q)$.  It does not decide whether the capacities might be
equivalent on narrower geometric families such as restrictions of
$W_0^{m,p}(K^c)$ used in spectral synthesis.

Bounded primary-source searches through 17 August 2026 found the source and
its 2004 follow-up.  The latter repeats the equivalence question; no later
source recording this separation was found.  External expert review is still
recommended before dissemination.
