\textbf{Status.} Candidate substantial partial result for Question 1.3 of
arXiv:1602.05750.  Likely valid; expert review requested.  The theorem below
proves the full strict-derivative clause and the full global Lipschitz clause
for separable real Hilbert ambient spaces.  It also proves a uniform
scaled-local Lipschitz clause.  It does not prove the source's exact local
conclusion on every $B(a,r)$ with $r<R$.

\section{Source question}

Koc and Kol\'a\v r consider a closed set $F$ in a normed space $X$, an
arbitrary map $f:F\to Y$, and a Baire-one prescribed derivative
$L:F\to\cL(X,Y)$ \cite{KK}.  Their Theorem 1.1 constructs an extension
$\bar f:X\to Y$ preserving continuity, pointwise H\"older behavior, and every
relative Fr\'echet derivative represented by $L$; the extension is smooth off
$F$ whenever $X$ has smooth partitions of unity.  If $\dim X<\infty$, it also
has the following two properties:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item[(vii)] At every $a\in F$ where $L$ is continuous and $L(a)$ is a
relative strict derivative of $f$, the derivatives of $\bar f$ off $F$
converge to $L(a)$, and $L(a)$ is the strict derivative of $\bar f$ at $a$.
\item[(viii)] If $f$ is Lipschitz and $L$ bounded on $B(a,R)\cap F$, then
$\bar f$ is Lipschitz on $B(a,r)$ for every $r<R$; if the hypotheses hold
globally on $F$, then $\bar f$ is globally Lipschitz.
\end{enumerate}

Question 1.3 on page 2 asks:

\begin{quote}
Can the condition $\dim X<\infty$ in Theorem 1.1 (vii) and (viii) be
replaced by the condition $\dim_NF<\infty$?
\end{quote}

Here finite Nagata dimension means that $F$ satisfies
$\operatorname{Nagata}(n,c)$ for some integer $n$ and constant $c$: at every
scale it has a uniformly bounded cover of scale multiplicity at most $n+1$.
We use Basso's normalization of this condition \cite{Basso}.

\section{Claimed contribution}

All Hilbert spaces below are real.  Balls are open.

\begin{theorem}[Hilbert--Nagata extension theorem]\label{thm:main}
Let $H$ be a separable Hilbert space, let $\varnothing\ne F\subset H$ be
closed and satisfy $\operatorname{Nagata}(n,c)$, let $Y$ be a normed linear
space, let $f:F\to Y$ be arbitrary, and let
$L:F\to\cL(H,Y)$ be Baire one.  There is an extension
$\bar f:H\to Y$ with all of the following properties.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $\bar f=f$ on $F$ and $\bar f\in C^\infty(H\setminus F,Y)$.
\item At every $a\in F$, continuity and pointwise $\alpha$-H\"older
continuity of $f$ at $a$ are inherited by $\bar f$, for $0<\alpha\le1$.
\item If $L(a)$ is a relative Fr\'echet derivative of $f$ at $a$, then
$\bar f$ is Fr\'echet differentiable at $a$ and
$D\bar f(a)=L(a)$.
\item If $L$ is continuous at $a\in F$ and $L(a)$ is a relative strict
derivative of $f$ at $a$, then
\[
 D\bar f(x)\longrightarrow L(a)
 \quad(x\to a,\ x\in H\setminus F),
\]
and $L(a)$ is the strict derivative of $\bar f$ at $a$ with respect to $H$.
\item If $f$ is globally Lipschitz on $F$ and $L$ is bounded on $F$, then
$\bar f$ is globally Lipschitz on $H$.
\item There is $\theta=\theta(n,c)\in(0,1)$ such that, whenever $a\in F$,
$R>0$, $f$ is Lipschitz on $B(a,R)\cap F$, and $L$ is bounded there, the
extension $\bar f$ is Lipschitz on $B(a,\theta R)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Thus part (d) is the full conclusion of source item (vii), and part (e) is the
global half of source item (viii).  Part (f) is weaker than the local half of
(viii), but its radius loss is uniform and depends only on the Nagata data.

\section{Proof intuition}

Finite dimensionality enters Koc--Kol\'a\v r's proof through a smooth Whitney
partition with bounded overlap and
\[
 \sum_i\|D\phi_i(x)\|\lesssim \dist(x,F)^{-1}.
\]
Finite Nagata dimension supplies the bounded-overlap Whitney cover even in an
infinite-dimensional ambient metric space \cite[Proposition 3.1]{Basso}.
The missing operation is to smooth its distance cutoffs without destroying
their Lipschitz constants.  On a separable Hilbert space this is supplied by
the smooth Lipschitz approximation theorem of Azagra, Ferrera,
L\'opez-Mesas, and Rangel \cite[Theorem 1]{AFLR}.  A positive-depth cutoff also
turns pointwise bounded multiplicity into genuine local finiteness.

We then use the same affine jets as the source.  A Baire-one extension
$A:H\setminus F\to\cL(H,Y)$ of $L$ is sampled at Whitney centers $z_i$,
and
\[
 \bar f(x)=\sum_i\phi_i(x)
 \bigl[f(a_i)+A(z_i)(x-a_i)\bigr].
\]
Near a strict point, the jet discrepancies are
$o(\dist(x,F))$; the partition derivative costs precisely
$O(\dist(x,F)^{-1})$, so $D\bar f(x)\to L(a)$.  Bounded data give a uniform
derivative bound off $F$.  A one-dimensional lemma along line segments glues
that bound to the corresponding Lipschitz estimate on the portions of the
segment lying in $F$.

\section{A smooth finite-Nagata Whitney partition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:partition}
Under the hypotheses on $H$ and $F$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, there are an
index set $I$, points $z_i\in H\setminus F$, anchors $a_i\in F$, and a locally
finite $C^\infty$ partition of unity $(\phi_i)_{i\in I}$ on $H\setminus F$
such that, for constants $M,C_0,C_1$ depending only on $(n,c)$, if
$d=\dist(x,F)$ and $\phi_i(x)\ne0$, then
\begin{align}
 \#\{i:\phi_i(x)\ne0\}&\le M,\label{eq:mult}\\
 C_0^{-1}d\le \dist(z_i,F)&\le C_0d,\label{eq:scale}\\
 \|x-z_i\|+\|x-a_i\|&\le C_0d,\label{eq:geometry}\\
 \sum_i\|D\phi_i(x)\|&\le \frac{C_1}{d}.
 \label{eq:derivative-sum}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition 3.1 of Basso \cite{Basso} with fixed numerical parameter
$q>1$.  It gives a cover $(B_i)_{i\in I}$ of $H\setminus F$.  Put
$r_i=\dist(B_i,F)$.  Basso's neighborhoods
$N_{\delta r_i}(B_i)$ have bounded multiplicity, where $\delta>0$ depends
only on the chosen $q$.  To avoid any convention about open versus closed
neighborhoods, set
\[
 U_i=\{x:\dist(x,B_i)<\delta r_i/2\}.
\]
There are constants $\alpha,\beta$ depending only on $(c,q)$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{diam}B_i\le\alpha r_i,
 \qquad \sup_{b\in B_i}\dist(b,F)\le\beta r_i,
\]
Every point belongs to at most $M=3(n+1)$ of the sets $U_i$.

Choose $z_i\in B_i$ and then $a_i\in F$ with
$\|z_i-a_i\|\le(\beta+1)r_i$.  If $x\in U_i$, then
\[
 (1-\delta/2)r_i\le\dist(x,F)
 \le(\alpha+\beta+\delta/2)r_i,
 \qquad
 \|x-z_i\|+\|x-a_i\|\le C r_i.
\]
These elementary inequalities already give
\eqref{eq:scale}--\eqref{eq:geometry} for functions supported in $U_i$.

Let
\[
 g_i(x)=\dist(x,H\setminus U_i),
 \qquad \varepsilon_i=\frac{\delta r_i}{32}.
\]
The function $g_i$ is $1$-Lipschitz, vanishes outside $U_i$, and is at least
$\delta r_i/2$ on $B_i$.  By Theorem 1 of
Azagra--Ferrera--L\'opez-Mesas--Rangel \cite{AFLR}, there is a
$C^\infty$ function $s_i:H\to\mathbb R$ such that
\[
 |s_i-g_i|<\varepsilon_i,
 \qquad \Lip(s_i)\le2.
\]
Fix $\eta\in C^\infty(\mathbb R,[0,1])$ with
$\eta=0$ on $(-\infty,2]$ and $\eta=1$ on $[4,\infty)$, and set
\[
 h_i(x)=\eta\bigl(s_i(x)/\varepsilon_i\bigr).
\]
Then $h_i=1$ on $B_i$,
\[
 \supp h_i\subset\{g_i\ge\varepsilon_i\}\subset U_i,
 \qquad
 \|Dh_i\|\le \frac{C}{r_i}.
\]

The supports just constructed are locally finite, a point that is stronger than mere
point-finiteness.  Otherwise distinct supports would meet points
$y_k\to x\in H\setminus F$.  By the preceding scale comparison, their scales $r_{i_k}$ are bounded above
and below by positive multiples of $\dist(x,F)$.  Since
$g_{i_k}(y_k)\ge\varepsilon_{i_k}$ and $g_{i_k}$ is $1$-Lipschitz, for all
large $k$ one would have $g_{i_k}(x)>0$, hence $x\in U_{i_k}$.  This
contradicts the multiplicity bound $M$.

The locally finite sum $S=\sum_i h_i$ satisfies $S\ge1$, because the sets
$B_i$ cover and $h_i=1$ on $B_i$.  Define $\phi_i=h_i/S$.  At most $M$ terms
are active, and direct differentiation gives
\[
 \sum_i\|D\phi_i(x)\|
 \le2\sum_{i:h_i(x)\ne0}\|Dh_i(x)\|
 \le \frac{C'}{\dist(x,F)},
\]
using the scale comparison and the derivative bound above.  This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\section{The affine-jet extension}

We use the following source theorem.  Theorem 1.6 of Koc--Kol\'a\v r
\cite{KK} says that for a Baire-one map
$L:F\to\cL(H,Y)$ there is a continuous map
$A:H\setminus F\to\cL(H,Y)$ such that, for every $a\in\partial F$,
\begin{equation}
 \|A(x)-L(a)\|\frac{\dist(x,F)}{\|x-a\|}\longrightarrow0
 \quad(x\to a,\ x\notin F),
 \label{eq:NT}
\end{equation}
such that $A(x)\to L(a)$ whenever $L$ is continuous at $a$, and such that
\begin{equation}
 L\text{ bounded on }B(a,12r)\cap F
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 A\text{ bounded on }B(a,r)\setminus F.
 \label{eq:B}
\end{equation}

Take $A$ from that theorem and the data from Lemma~\ref{lem:partition}.  Put
\begin{equation}
 P_i(x)=f(a_i)+A(z_i)(x-a_i),
 \qquad
 \bar f(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 f(x),&x\in F,\\[2mm]
 \displaystyle\sum_i\phi_i(x)P_i(x),&x\notin F.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:extension}
\end{equation}
The sum is locally finite, so $\bar f$ is $C^\infty$ on $H\setminus F$.

We record the boundary estimates, both to establish parts (a)--(c) and for
later use.  If $x\to a\in F$ and $\phi_i(x)\ne0$, then
\eqref{eq:geometry} gives
\begin{equation}
 a_i,z_i\to a,
 \qquad
 \|x-a_i\|=O(\dist(x,F)),
 \qquad
 \|z_i-a\|=O(\|x-a\|).
 \label{eq:boundary-geometry}
\end{equation}
Moreover, \eqref{eq:scale}, \eqref{eq:NT}, and
\eqref{eq:boundary-geometry} imply
\begin{equation}
 \|(A(z_i)-L(a))(x-a_i)\|=o(\|x-a\|)
 \label{eq:NT-use}
\end{equation}
uniformly over active indices.  Uniformity follows because the active anchor
and center lie in fixed constant multiples of the shrinking ball about $a$.

If $f$ is continuous at $a$, equations \eqref{eq:extension}--\eqref{eq:NT-use}
show $\bar f(x)\to f(a)$.  If $f$ is pointwise $\alpha$-H\"older at $a$,
then
$\|f(a_i)-f(a)\|=O(\|x-a\|^\alpha)$; the $L(a)$ term in
$A(z_i)(x-a_i)$ is $O(\|x-a\|)$, and the remaining term is
$o(\|x-a\|)$, proving the same pointwise $\alpha$-H\"older bound for
$\bar f$.

Finally, if $L(a)$ is a relative Fr\'echet derivative of $f$ at $a$, then
\begin{align*}
 \bar f(x)-f(a)-L(a)(x-a)
 =\sum_i\phi_i(x)\bigl[&f(a_i)-f(a)-L(a)(a_i-a)\\
 &+(A(z_i)-L(a))(x-a_i)\bigr].
\end{align*}
The first bracket is $o(\|x-a\|)$ by relative differentiability and
\eqref{eq:boundary-geometry}, and the second is $o(\|x-a\|)$ by
\eqref{eq:NT-use}.  Hence
$D\bar f(a)=L(a)$.  This proves parts (a)--(c) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Strict differentiability}

For $x\notin F$, differentiating \eqref{eq:extension} and using
$\sum_iD\phi_i(x)=0$ gives, for any affine map $Q:H\to Y$,
\begin{equation}
 D\bar f(x)=\sum_i\phi_i(x)A(z_i)
 +\sum_i D\phi_i(x)\otimes\bigl(P_i(x)-Q(x)\bigr),
 \label{eq:derivative}
\end{equation}
where $(\lambda\otimes y)(v)=\lambda(v)y$.

Suppose $L$ is continuous at $a\in F$ and $L(a)$ is a relative strict
derivative of $f$ at $a$.  The interior case is immediate, so assume
$a\in\partial F$.  For each $x\notin F$, choose $b_x\in F$ with
\begin{equation}
 \|x-b_x\|<2\dist(x,F).
 \label{eq:approx-nearest}
\end{equation}
No nearest-point projection is being assumed.  In
\eqref{eq:derivative} choose
$Q(t)=f(b_x)+L(a)(t-b_x)$.  Then
\begin{align}
 P_i(x)-Q(x)
 ={}&f(a_i)-f(b_x)-L(a)(a_i-b_x)\nonumber\\
 &+(A(z_i)-L(a))(x-a_i).
 \label{eq:jet-error}
\end{align}

As $x\to a$, all active $a_i,z_i,b_x$ tend to $a$.  If
$d=\dist(x,F)$, strict differentiability and the geometry lemma make the first
line of \eqref{eq:jet-error} equal to $o(d)$, uniformly over active $i$.
Continuity of $L$ and property (C) of $A$ give
$A(z_i)-L(a)=o(1)$, so the second line is also $o(d)$.  Combining
\eqref{eq:derivative}, \eqref{eq:derivative-sum}, and
\eqref{eq:jet-error} yields
\begin{equation}
 \|D\bar f(x)-L(a)\|\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:D-convergence}
\end{equation}

It remains to upgrade derivative convergence off $F$ to a strict derivative
across arbitrary pairs.  We isolate the elementary device.

\begin{lemma}[segment gluing]\label{lem:segment}
Let $u:[0,1]\to Y$ be continuous and let $E\subset[0,1]$ be closed.  Suppose
$u|_E$ is $K$-Lipschitz and that $u$ is differentiable on
$[0,1]\setminus E$ with $\|u'(t)\|\le K$.  Then $u$ is $K$-Lipschitz.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On every component interval of $[0,1]\setminus E$, scalarization by
$y^*\in Y^*$ and the real mean-value theorem give the $K$-Lipschitz estimate;
Hahn--Banach recovers the norm estimate.  Given $s<t$, if
$E\cap[s,t]=\varnothing$ we are done.  Otherwise let $p$ and $q$ be its first
and last points.  Use the component estimate from $s$ to $p$ and from $q$ to
$t$, the hypothesis on $E$ from $p$ to $q$, and add the three bounds.
\end{proof}

Apply the lemma to
\[
 u(t)=\bar f(x+t(y-x))-L(a)(x+t(y-x))
\]
and $E=\{t:x+t(y-x)\in F\}$.  By \eqref{eq:D-convergence}, for every
$\varepsilon>0$ the derivative of $u$ has norm at most
$\varepsilon\|y-x\|$ off $E$, provided $x,y$ are sufficiently close to $a$.
Relative strict differentiability gives the same Lipschitz bound on $E$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:segment} therefore gives
\[
 \|\bar f(y)-\bar f(x)-L(a)(y-x)\|
 \le\varepsilon\|y-x\|.
\]
This proves part (d) of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Global and scaled-local Lipschitz bounds}

Assume first that $f$ is globally $K$-Lipschitz on $F$ and that $L$ is
bounded there.  Taking $r=\infty$ in \eqref{eq:B}, the operator field $A$ is
globally bounded, say $\|A\|\le B$.  For $x\notin F$, choose $b_x$ as in
\eqref{eq:approx-nearest} and use $Q\equiv f(b_x)$ in
\eqref{eq:derivative}.  The geometry lemma gives
\[
 \|P_i(x)-f(b_x)\|
 \le K\|a_i-b_x\|+B\|x-a_i\|
 \le C(K+B)\dist(x,F).
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:derivative-sum}, this yields
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{x\in H\setminus F}\|D\bar f(x)\|
 \le C'(K+B)<\infty.
 \label{eq:global-D}
\end{equation}
For any line segment in $H$, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:segment} with the closed
subset where the segment meets $F$.  On that subset $\bar f=f$ is
$K$-Lipschitz, and on its complement \eqref{eq:global-D} bounds the
derivative.  Thus $\bar f$ is globally Lipschitz, proving part (e).

Now let the hypotheses hold only on $B(a,R)\cap F$.  Property
\eqref{eq:B} bounds $A$ on $B(a,R/12)\setminus F$.  Choose
$\theta=\theta(n,c)>0$ small enough that for every
$x\in B(a,\theta R)\setminus F$ and every active $i$,
\[
 z_i\in B(a,R/12),
 \qquad a_i,b_x\in B(a,R).
\]
Such a choice follows directly from \eqref{eq:geometry} and
\eqref{eq:approx-nearest}, since
$\dist(x,F)\le\|x-a\|$.  The preceding derivative estimate now applies on
$B(a,\theta R)\setminus F$, with the local Lipschitz constant of $f$ and the
local bound for $A$.  Every segment between two points of
$B(a,\theta R)$ stays in that ball; on its intersection with $F$, the local
Lipschitz hypothesis applies.  A final use of Lemma~\ref{lem:segment} proves
part (f).

\section{Scope, obstruction, and novelty check}

The proof uses separability only through the quantitative $C^\infty$ smooth
approximation theorem \cite{AFLR}.  The Nagata cover itself works in an
arbitrary metric ambient space.  A comparable smooth approximation theorem
would therefore extend the argument to other Banach classes.

The exact local clause from the source remains open in this packet.  The
finite-dimensional proof first establishes a fixed-radius estimate and then
uses compactness to pass from local bounds to every smaller concentric ball
\cite[proof of Theorem 4.1]{KK}.  In infinite-dimensional Hilbert space,
$\overline{B(a,r)}$ is not compact.  Continuity of the auxiliary field $A$
does not bound it on the noncompact part of $B(a,r)$ lying a positive distance
from $F$.  The present argument avoids this only by shrinking far enough that
property (B) supplies a uniform bound.  Closing the radius gap appears to
require either an exact-local boundedness refinement of the Baire-one extender
or a new jet selection in the deep interior.

A bounded novelty search on 11 August 2026 covered the four lightweight run
indexes and external searches for the exact title with ``Nagata dimension'',
fragments of Question 1.3, ``strict derivative finite Nagata'', and ``Whitney
extension Hilbert finite Nagata''.  No exact answer to Question 1.3 or this
separable-Hilbert subcase was found.  Basso's finite-Nagata Whitney cover and
the Azagra--Ferrera--L\'opez-Mesas--Rangel smooth approximation theorem are
proof inputs; neither source identifies the theorem above.
