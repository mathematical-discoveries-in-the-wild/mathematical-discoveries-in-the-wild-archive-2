\section*{Source question}

Yang, Gimeno, and de la Llave prove persistence and smooth parameter dependence
of periodic orbits for functional differential equations close to an ODE.
After observing that analytic state-dependent-delay data bootstrap the orbit to
$C^\infty$, Remark~6.5 asks whether the parameterization $K$ must be analytic
and conjectures that it may fail to be so \cite{source}.  The remark is reproduced
from PDF page 18.

\section*{The counterexample}

For $X=(u,v,z)\in\R^3$, take
\begin{align}
 \dot u&=-v+(1-u^2-v^2)u,\label{eq:clock1}\\
 \dot v&= u+(1-u^2-v^2)v,\label{eq:clock2}\\
 \dot z&=-z+v+\e\,z\bigl(t-r(X(t))\bigr),
 &r(u,v,z)&=2\pi-\arctan v.\label{eq:z}
\end{align}
This has precisely the source's form
\[
 \dot X(t)=f(X(t))+\e P\bigl(X(t),X(t-r(X(t)))\bigr),
 \qquad P(X,Y)=(0,0,Y_3).
\]
The maps $f,P,r$ are real analytic, and the delay is globally bounded and
strictly positive:
\(
3\pi/2<r<5\pi/2.
\)

At $\e=0$ there is the analytic $2\pi$-periodic orbit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K0}
 K_0(t)=\left(\cos t,\sin t,\frac{\sin t-\cos t}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
Its Floquet multipliers are
\[
 1,\qquad e^{-4\pi},\qquad e^{-2\pi}.
\]
Indeed, the first two equations have radial equation
$\dot\rho=\rho(1-\rho^2)$ and angular equation $\dot t=1$; the $z$-variation
is triangular with diagonal exponent $-1$.  Thus the unit multiplier is
simple and the orbit is nondegenerate in the source's sense.

The clock equations are unaffected by the perturbation.  A nearby orbit with
the phase fixed by $(u,v)=(\cos t,\sin t)$ therefore has third component
$y=y_\e$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar}
 y'(t)+y(t)=\sin t+\e y(\eta(t)),
 \qquad \eta(t)=t+\arctan(\sin t),
\end{equation}
where periodicity was used to replace
$t-2\pi+\arctan(\sin t)$ by $\eta(t)$.

\begin{lemma}[Existence, uniqueness, and smoothness]\label{lem:fixed}
For every $|\e|<1$, equation \eqref{eq:scalar} has a unique continuous
$2\pi$-periodic solution.  It is $C^\infty$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sup}
 \norm{y_\e}_\infty\le \frac1{1-|\e|}.
\end{equation}
Moreover $y_\e\to y_0=(\sin t-\cos t)/2$ as $\e\to0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $A=D+1$ on periodic functions and let $C y=y\circ\eta$.  The unique
periodic solution of $Ay=g$ is
\[
 (A^{-1}g)(t)=\frac1{1-e^{-2\pi}}
 \int_{t-2\pi}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}g(s)\,ds,
\]
so $\norm{A^{-1}g}_\infty\le\norm{g}_\infty$ (also for complex-valued
functions), while $\norm C=1$.  Hence
\[
 y=A^{-1}\sin+\e A^{-1}Cy
\]
is a contraction for $|\e|<1$, and \eqref{eq:sup} follows.  The differential
equation then bootstraps a continuous solution successively to every finite
differentiability order.  The fixed-point equation also gives convergence to
$A^{-1}\sin=y_0$ as $\e\to0$.
\end{proof}

It remains to prove that suitable arbitrarily small $\e>0$ give a nonanalytic
solution.  The expansive fixed point $\eta(0)=0$, $\eta'(0)=2$ supplies the
obstruction.

\section*{A certified Taylor obstruction}

Write the formal Taylor series at zero as
\[
 y(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}y_nt^n,
 \qquad e_{k,n}=[t^n]\eta(t)^k,
\]
and, for $\e\ne0$, normalize
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wdef}
 w_n=\frac{n!\,y_n}{\e^n2^{n(n-1)/2}}.
\end{equation}
Since $\eta(t)=2t+O(t^3)$, coefficient comparison in
\eqref{eq:scalar} gives
\begin{align}
 w_{n+1}-w_n
 &=-\frac{w_n}{\e2^n}
   +\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}A_{n,k}w_k+G_n,\label{eq:wrec}\\
 A_{n,k}
 &=e_{k,n}\frac{n!}{k!}\e^{k-n}
   2^{k(k-1)/2-n(n+1)/2},\label{eq:Ank}\\
 G_n&=\frac{n![t^n]\sin t}{\e^{n+1}2^{n(n+1)/2}}.\label{eq:Gn}
\end{align}
Only even differences $d=n-k\ge2$ occur in the sum because $\eta$ is odd.

We record a fully explicit convergence estimate specialized to
$\e=1/10$.  Put $\eta(t)=2t(1+u(t))$.  On $|t|=1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ubound}
 |u(t)|\le
 \frac{\operatorname{arctanh}(\sinh(1/2))-1/2}{2(1/2)}<\frac1{10}.
\end{equation}
The first inequality follows by majorizing separately the power series of
$\arctan z-z$ and $\sin t-t$; the second follows, for example, from
$\sinh(1/2)<8/15$ and
$\operatorname{arctanh}(8/15)=\tfrac12\log(23/7)<3/5$.
Thus Cauchy's estimate and $n!/k!\le n^{n-k}$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Tnd}
 |A_{n,n-d}|\le T_{n,d}:=\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^{n-d}
 (20n)^d2^{-d(2n-d+1)/2}.
\end{equation}
As a function of even $d$, $\log T_{n,d}$ is convex.  For $n\ge30$ its
maximum is therefore at $d=2$ or at the last admissible endpoint.  Exact
comparison at $n=30,31$, followed by
\[
 \frac{(T_{n+2,d_{\max}}/T_{n+2,2})}
 {(T_{n,d_{\max}}/T_{n,2})}
 <\frac{6400n^2\,8}{2^{2n+3}}<1,
\]
shows that the first endpoint always dominates.  Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:georatio}
 \frac{\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor T_{n+1,2}}
 {\lfloor n/2\rfloor T_{n,2}}
 \le \frac{31}{29}\frac{11}{10}
       \left(\frac{31}{30}\right)^2\frac14<\frac13.
\end{equation}
Consequently the sum of all row perturbations after row 30 satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rtail}
 S:=\sum_{n\ge30}\left(\frac{10}{2^n}+
       \sum_{k<n}|A_{n,k}|\right)
 <1.883\times10^{-8}.
\end{equation}
The forcing occurs only for odd $n$, and its two-step ratio is
$100\,2^{-(2n+3)}$; hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ftail}
 H:=\sum_{n\ge30}|G_n|<9.78\times10^{-118}.
\end{equation}

All coefficients through order 30 are rational: the Taylor coefficients of
$\sin$, $\arctan$, and \eqref{eq:scalar} are rational when $\e=1/10$.
The included exact-arithmetic certificate computes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finitecert}
 w_{30}=a_{30}y(0)+b_{30},\qquad
 a_{30}\doteq109.81990128428,\quad
 b_{30}\doteq349.05750538187.
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed}, $|y(0)|\le10/9$.  Exact rational comparisons in
the script prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separation}
 |w_{30}|>227,\qquad
 \max_{k\le30}|w_k|<735.
\end{equation}
If $W_N=\max_{k\le N}|w_k|$, equations
\eqref{eq:wrec}--\eqref{eq:Ftail} imply that all later terms are bounded by
$(W_{30}+H)/(1-S)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:displacement}
 |w_\infty-w_{30}|\le
 \frac{S(W_{30}+H)}{1-S}+H<10^{-3}.
\end{equation}
Thus $w_n$ converges and $|w_\infty|>226.999$ for the unique periodic
solution at $\e=1/10$.

If this solution were analytic at zero, its Taylor coefficients would have
bounded $n$-th roots.  Instead, \eqref{eq:wdef} and
$w_n\to w_\infty\ne0$ give
\[
 |y_n|^{1/n}
 =|\e|\frac{2^{(n-1)/2}}{(n!)^{1/n}}|w_n|^{1/n}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
a contradiction.  Hence $y_{1/10}$ is $C^\infty$ but nonanalytic.

\section*{Why the parameter can be arbitrarily small}

The source's persistence radius need not contain $1/10$, so one final point
is essential.  On the complex Banach space of continuous periodic functions,
\[
 y_\e=(I-\e A^{-1}C)^{-1}A^{-1}\sin
\]
is holomorphic for $|\e|<1$.  On every compact subset of the punctured unit
disc, the estimates above (with $|\e|$ bounded away from zero) make the
sequence $w_n(\e)$ locally uniformly convergent.  Therefore
\(
 W(\e):=\lim_n w_n(\e)
\)
is holomorphic on $0<|\e|<1$.  Since the exact certificate proves
$W(1/10)\ne0$, the identity theorem says that $W$ cannot vanish on any
interval $(0,\delta)$.  There are consequently positive parameters
$\e\to0$ for which $W(\e)\ne0$ and $y_\e$ is nonanalytic.

Choose such an $\e$ below the source theorem's persistence threshold.
The clock subsystem has only the unit-circle periodic orbit nearby, up to
phase, and Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed} makes its $z$ component unique.  Hence
$K_\e=(\cos t,\sin t,y_\e(t))$ is exactly the locally persisted branch (after
the source's phase normalization).  It is $C^\infty$, by the bootstrap, but
not analytic.  We have proved the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:answer}
There are real-analytic $f,P,r$ satisfying the state-dependent-delay setup
of arXiv:2103.05203 and arbitrarily small positive perturbation parameters
$\e$ for which the periodic orbit persisted from a nondegenerate analytic
ODE orbit is $C^\infty$ but not real analytic.  Thus the natural question in
Remark~6.5 has a negative answer, and the authors' conjectured failure of
analyticity occurs already in dimension three with one bounded positive
delay.
\end{theorem}

\section*{Verification and literature boundary}

Run the exact certificate from this directory with
\begin{verbatim}
conda run --no-capture-output -n sandbox python code/rigorous_certificate.py
\end{verbatim}
It uses \texttt{fractions.Fraction}; floating-point numbers are used only for
display after every decisive inequality has been checked exactly.

Mallet-Paret and Nussbaum established analogous expansive-fixed-point
obstructions for analytic equations with prescribed time-dependent delays
\cite{MPN}, and their paper explicitly separates the state-dependent case.
Hu proves analyticity for a special state-dependent system under a monotone
time-map hypothesis \cite{Hu}; that hypothesis excludes the expanding fixed
point used here.  Searches through 12 August 2026 found no paper resolving
Remark~6.5 itself.  This is a bounded novelty check, not a claim of exhaustive
bibliographic priority.
