\section*{Status and source question}

This is a \texttt{candidate\_partial\_likely\_valid} result for Roman
Drnov\v sek, \emph{Triangularizability of trace-class operators with
increasing spectrum}, arXiv:1508.07766 \cite{Drnovsek}.  Question~3 on PDF
page~3 asks:

\begin{quote}
Suppose that $K$ is a compact operator on $L^2(X,\mu)$ that has increasing
spectrum relative to standard compressions. Does $K$ admit a standard
triangularization (which need not be multiplicity-free)? In particular, is
$K$ decomposable?
\end{quote}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.94\linewidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

For a measurable $E\subseteq[0,1]$, write $P_E$ for multiplication by
$\mathbf1_E$.  Increasing spectrum means
\[
 \sigma(P_EK|_{P_EL^2})\subseteq\sigma(P_FK|_{P_FL^2})
 \qquad(E\subseteq F).
\]
The source's Theorem~5 answers the interval case when $K$ is trace class,
its kernel is continuous, and its lattice modulus is also trace class.  The
result below removes all three restrictions, retaining only the strictly
weaker Hilbert--Schmidt hypothesis.

\section*{Main result}

\begin{theorem}[Hilbert--Schmidt interval case]\label{thm:main}
Let $K\in\Stwo(L^2[0,1])$ have increasing spectrum relative to standard
compressions.  Then $K$ is quasinilpotent and admits a standard
triangularization.

More precisely, if $k\in L^2([0,1]^2)$ is the kernel of $K$ and $T$ is the
positive Hilbert--Schmidt operator with kernel $|k|$, then $T$ is
quasinilpotent.  A standard triangularizing chain supplied for $T$ by the
ideal-triangularization form of de Pagter's theorem consists of
$K$-invariant standard subspaces as well.
\end{theorem}

\section*{Proof intuition}

The trace of $K$ need not exist, and a measurable kernel has no meaningful
diagonal values.  Both apparent losses are avoided by starting with the
second power.  Every Hilbert--Schmidt compression has trace-class powers
from exponent two onward, and increasing spectrum makes all of their power
traces zero.

For $n$ disjoint localization sets, take an alternating sum over all their
unions.  Boolean inclusion--exclusion cancels every block word that misses a
set; a word of length $n$ that visits all $n$ sets is necessarily a
permutation.  Lebesgue differentiation therefore says that the sum of all
oriented $n$-cycle products is zero almost everywhere.  A shortest nonzero
cycle cannot cancel in this sum: all its backward chords except the closing
edge would create shorter cycles, so only its $n$ rotations remain.

Thus the absolute kernel $|k|$ has zero cyclic moments of every order at
least two.  A separate spectral lemma shows that an Hilbert--Schmidt operator
with all those power traces zero must be quasinilpotent.  De Pagter's theorem
then provides the standard chain, and $|Kf|\leq T|f|$ transfers its
invariance to $K$.  The full compact problem remains open because a compact
operator need not possess either an $L^2$ kernel or any trace-class power.

\section*{Three lemmas}

We first isolate the only spectral fact needed at the end.

\begin{lemma}[vanishing power traces]\label{lem:power}
If $A\in\Stwo$ and
\[
 \tr(A^n)=0\qquad(n\geq2),
\]
then $A$ is quasinilpotent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $(\lambda_j)$ be the nonzero eigenvalues of $A$, repeated according to
algebraic multiplicity.  Weyl's inequality gives
$\sum_j|\lambda_j|^2<\infty$, and Lidskii's theorem applied to $A^n\in\Sone$
gives
\[
 0=\tr(A^n)=\sum_j\lambda_j^n\qquad(n\geq2).
\]
Suppose that $r=r(A)>0$, and list the finitely many peripheral eigenvalues
as $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m$.  Simultaneous recurrence on the compact
torus gives integers $n_q\to\infty$ such that
$(\lambda_j/r)^{n_q}\to1$ for every $j\leq m$.

The remaining eigenvalues have modulus at most some $\rho<r$ outside an
$\ell_2$ tail of modulus at most $r/2$.  Consequently
\[
 r^{-n_q}\sum_{j>m}|\lambda_j|^{n_q}\longrightarrow0:
\]
the finite part is $O((\rho/r)^{n_q})$, while the tail is at most
$r^{-2}2^{-(n_q-2)}\sum_j|\lambda_j|^2$.  Dividing the zero power sum by
$r^{n_q}$ and passing to the limit therefore gives $0=m$, a contradiction.
Thus $r(A)=0$.
\end{proof}

For $n\geq2$, define the cyclic product
\[
 c_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=
 k(x_1,x_2)k(x_2,x_3)\cdots k(x_n,x_1)
\]
and its complete symmetrization
\[
 F_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=
 \sum_{\pi\in S_n}c_n(x_{\pi(1)},\ldots,x_{\pi(n)}).
\]
Products of Hilbert--Schmidt kernels around a finite cycle are integrable;
this follows first for finite simple kernels from the trace formula and then
for $L^2$ kernels by the Schatten H\"older bound.  Hence $F_n\in L^1$.

\begin{lemma}[localized inclusion--exclusion]\label{lem:sym}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main},
\[
 F_n=0\quad\text{almost everywhere on }[0,1]^n
 \qquad(n\geq2).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Drnov\v sek's Theorem~4 \cite{Drnovsek} uses only compactness and the
compressions $P_{[0,t]}$ to show that $K$ is quasinilpotent.  For every
measurable $E$, increasing spectrum then gives
\[
 \sigma(P_EKP_E|_{P_EL^2})\subseteq\sigma(K)=\{0\}.
\]
Thus $K_E=P_EKP_E$ is quasinilpotent.  Since $K_E\in\Stwo$, Lidskii gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localtraces}
 \tr(K_E^n)=0\qquad(n\geq2).
\end{equation}

Fix pairwise disjoint measurable sets $E_1,\ldots,E_n$.  For
$J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$, put $P_J=P_{\cup_{j\in J}E_j}$.  Taking the
Boolean alternating sum of \eqref{eq:localtraces} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boolean}
 0=\sum_{J\subseteq[n]}(-1)^{n-|J|}
       \tr\bigl((P_JKP_J)^n\bigr).
\end{equation}
Expand each trace into block words.  If a word uses precisely the index set
$S$, its coefficient in \eqref{eq:boolean} is
\[
 \sum_{J\supseteq S}(-1)^{n-|J|}
 =\begin{cases}1,&S=[n],\\0,&S\ne[n].\end{cases}
\]
A surviving word has length $n$ and uses every one of the $n$ indices, so
its index word is a permutation.  The standard trace formula for cyclic
products of Hilbert--Schmidt kernel operators therefore turns
\eqref{eq:boolean} into
\[
 \int_{E_1\times\cdots\times E_n}F_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
 \,dx_1\cdots dx_n=0.
\]

Take the $E_i$ to be equal-radius intervals centered at distinct points
$x_i$.  They are disjoint for all sufficiently small radii.  The ordinary
Lebesgue differentiation theorem on $[0,1]^n$, applied to the integrable
function $F_n$, gives $F_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=0$ for almost every tuple with
distinct coordinates.  The collision diagonals have measure zero, proving
the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[cycle elimination]\label{lem:cycles}
For every $n\geq2$, $c_n=0$ almost everywhere.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We induct on $n$.  Since
$F_2(x_1,x_2)=2c_2(x_1,x_2)$, the base case follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:sym}.

Assume the assertion through length $n-1$.  If $c_n$ were nonzero on a set
of positive measure, we could choose a tuple of distinct coordinates
$(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ such that $c_n\ne0$, $F_n=0$, and every shorter cycle on
every subtuple vanishes.  This simultaneous choice is legitimate because
only finitely many null sets and their coordinate pullbacks are excluded.

For $1\leq i<j\leq n$ with $j-i\leq n-2$, the forward path
\[
 k(x_i,x_{i+1})\cdots k(x_{j-1},x_j)
\]
is nonzero.  Closing it with $k(x_j,x_i)$ gives a cycle of length at most
$n-1$, so the induction hypothesis forces $k(x_j,x_i)=0$.  In any directed
Hamiltonian cycle on the ordered vertices $1,\ldots,n$, avoiding all such
backward edges leaves only
\[
 1\to2\to\cdots\to n\to1
\]
and its $n$ cyclic rotations: after the unique allowed backward edge
$n\to1$, every other edge must move forward, and visiting all vertices
forces their natural order.  Hence every summand of $F_n$ vanishes except
those $n$ rotations, all equal to $c_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$.  This gives
$0=F_n=n c_n\ne0$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\section*{Proof of the theorem}

The quasinilpotence of $K$ was already obtained in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:sym}.  Let $T$ be the positive integral operator with kernel
$|k|$.  Since $|k|\in L^2([0,1]^2)$, $T\in\Stwo$ and is compact.  For every
$n\geq2$, $T^n\in\Sone$, and the Hilbert--Schmidt cyclic trace formula plus
Lemma~\ref{lem:cycles} gives
\[
 \tr(T^n)=\int_{[0,1]^n}
 |k(x_1,x_2)|\cdots|k(x_n,x_1)|\,dx_1\cdots dx_n=0.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:power} shows that $T$ is quasinilpotent.

The ideal-triangularization corollary of de Pagter's theorem for positive
compact quasinilpotent operators \cite{dePagter,RadjaviRosenthal} supplies a
maximal standard chain invariant under $T$.  If $L^2(E)$ belongs to this
chain and $f$ is supported in $E$, then
\[
 |Kf|(x)\leq\int_0^1|k(x,y)|\,|f(y)|\,dy=(T|f|)(x).
\]
The right side is supported in $E$, so $Kf$ is supported in $E$.  Thus the
same standard chain is invariant under $K$, proving the asserted standard
triangularization.  \qed

\section*{Verification, upgrade attempts, and limitations}

The proof uses no numerical approximation.  A finite script included as
\path{code/check_cycle_combinatorics.py} verifies through $n=8$ both the
Boolean coefficient in Lemma~\ref{lem:sym} and that exactly the $n$ cyclic
rotations survive the forbidden-backward-edge condition.  It is a regression
check, not a substitute for the displayed proofs.

Four focused upgrades were attempted.  First, the source's trace-class
assumption on the modulus was replaced by Hilbert--Schmidt power traces.
Second, trace class of $K$ itself was removed, initially for continuous
kernels.  Third, Boolean localization removed continuity and produced the
full theorem above.  Fourth, the route was tested against arbitrary compact
operators.  That last extension stops for a structural reason: a compact
operator can belong to no $\mathcal S_p$ with finite $p$, so no positive
power need have a trace; it also need not possess an $L^2$ kernel whose
absolute value defines a positive compact majorant.  Norm approximation by
finite-rank operators does not preserve increasing spectra or invariant
standard subspaces.  Consequently the source question for arbitrary compact
operators remains open.
