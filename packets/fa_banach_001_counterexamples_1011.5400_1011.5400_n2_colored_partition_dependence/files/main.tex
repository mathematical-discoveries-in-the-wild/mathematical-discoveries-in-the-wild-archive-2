\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{The linear-independence clause of Theorem~3.5 in
arXiv:1011.5400 is false as printed.}
For $s=5$, $n=2$, and $(k,l)=(0,6)$, eleven distinct diagrams in
$D_5(0,6)$ satisfy the explicit nonzero linear relation below.  In fact the
same relation belongs to $D_s(0,6)$ for every $s\geq3$.

This does not challenge the theorem's spanning statement.  It shows that the
argument counting diagrams as a basis, and conclusions using that count at
$n=2$, require a small-rank correction.

\section*{The source claim and Gram question}
The source works on $H=\C^{2n}$ and assigns a map
$T_\pi:H^{\otimes k}\to H^{\otimes l}$ to each admissibly bicolored
noncrossing partition $\pi\in D_s(k,l)$.  Source PDF page~10 states:

\begin{center}
\fbox{\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{source_theorem_crop.png}}
\end{center}

After the proof, source PDF page~12 asks for the Gram determinant of these
maps and suggests proving independence by showing that determinant is
nonzero.  The counterexample occurs at the first degree where the relevant
determinant has a factor $2n-4$.

\section*{Notation for the eleven diagrams}
Put
\[
 X=\{1,2\}\times\{0,1\},\qquad
 \overline{(i,a)}=(i,1-a),\qquad H=\C^X.
\]
Write $c_0=wb$ and $c_1=bw$.  For a colored partition $\pi$ of six
lower points, set $\xiP_\pi=T_\pi(1)\in H^{\otimes6}$.  The source's
definition says that a tensor coordinate of $\xiP_\pi$ is one exactly when,
in every block, all black indices equal some $I\in X$ and all white indices
equal $\bar I$.

We specify a block by its increasing list of positions followed by its color
word.  For $a,b\in\{0,1\}$ define:
\begin{align*}
 A_{ab}&=\{123456[c_0c_ac_b]\},\\
 B_{12}^{a}&=\{1234[c_0c_a]\}\{56[c_0]\},\\
 B_{13}^{a}&=\{1256[c_0c_a]\}\{34[c_0]\},\\
 B_{23}^{a}&=\{12[c_0]\}\{3456[c_0c_a]\},\\
 C&=\{12[c_0]\}\{34[c_0]\}\{56[c_0]\}.
\end{align*}
For example, $A_{10}$ has color word $wbbwwb$, while $B_{13}^1$
has the outer block $\{1,2,5,6\}$ colored $wbbw$ and the inner block
$\{3,4\}$ colored $wb$.

Every displayed partition is noncrossing.  Every block contains equally many
black and white legs, so the defining congruence holds for every $s$; the
eleven diagrams are distinct even after the source's blockwise color-swap
identification.

\begin{theorem}
On $H=\C^4$ one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relation}
 2\sum_{a,b\in\{0,1\}}\xiP_{A_{ab}}
 -\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}}
   \bigl(\xiP_{B_{12}^{a}}+\xiP_{B_{13}^{a}}+\xiP_{B_{23}^{a}}\bigr)
 +\xiP_C=0.
\end{equation}
Consequently the maps indexed by $D_5(0,6)$ are not linearly independent
when $n=2$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof intuition.}
All eleven tensors live on coordinates in which each adjacent pair has
opposite colors.  Such a coordinate is determined by three indices
$x_1,x_2,x_3\in X$.  Joining two pair-blocks forgets their second coordinates
and tests only whether their first coordinates in $\{1,2\}$ agree.  Joining
all three tests whether all three first coordinates agree.  The relation is
then just the pigeonhole fact that among three elements of a two-point set,
either all three are equal or exactly one pair is equal.

\begin{proof}
Fix a basis coordinate $(I_1,\ldots,I_6)\in X^6$.  Every term in
\eqref{eq:relation} vanishes unless
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairs}
 I_2=\bar I_1,\qquad I_4=\bar I_3,\qquad I_6=\bar I_5.
\end{equation}
Assume \eqref{eq:pairs}, put $x_j=I_{2j-1}$, and let
$p_j\in\{1,2\}$ be the first coordinate of $x_j$.

For a fixed pair of pair-blocks, summing the two orientations $c_0,c_1$
gives the indicator that their first coordinates agree.  Thus
\[
 \sum_a\xiP_{B_{12}^a}=\one_{p_1=p_2},\quad
 \sum_a\xiP_{B_{13}^a}=\one_{p_1=p_3},\quad
 \sum_a\xiP_{B_{23}^a}=\one_{p_2=p_3}
\]
at the chosen tensor coordinate.  Similarly,
$\sum_{a,b}\xiP_{A_{ab}}=\one_{p_1=p_2=p_3}$, while $\xiP_C=1$.
The coefficient of that coordinate on the left of \eqref{eq:relation} is
therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pigeon}
 2\one_{p_1=p_2=p_3}
 -\one_{p_1=p_2}-\one_{p_1=p_3}-\one_{p_2=p_3}+1.
\end{equation}
If all three $p_j$ are equal, \eqref{eq:pigeon} is $2-3+1=0$.
Otherwise, because $p_j\in\{1,2\}$, exactly one pair is equal, and it is
$0-1+1=0$.  Hence every tensor coordinate vanishes, proving
\eqref{eq:relation}.  Its eleven coefficients are not all zero, so it is a
genuine dependence among distinct elements of $D_5(0,6)$.
\end{proof}

\section*{Exact Gram audit}
Let $N=2n$.  An independent signed-union-find enumeration constructs all
33 diagrams in $D_5(0,6)$ and their exact polynomial Gram matrix.  It gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:det}
 \det G_{5,6}(N)=N^{33}(N-2)^{26}(N-4)^2.
\end{equation}
At $n=2$, the $33\times33$ matrix has exact rank $31$.  Thus the hand
relation is corroborated by the full Gram computation, which also provides a
concrete low-degree answer to the source's determinant question.  The proof
of the counterexample itself does not depend on the enumeration.

The exhaustive coordinate checker verifies \eqref{eq:relation} on all
$4^6=4096$ basis coordinates and confirms that every diagram occurs
nontrivially.  Both exact programs are included in the packet.
