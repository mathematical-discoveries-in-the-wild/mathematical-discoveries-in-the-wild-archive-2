\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{Literature-implied answer, complete for the infinite-dimensional
complex $L_p(\mu)$ subfamily.}  This is an identification of an older theorem,
not a new proof and not a characterization of arbitrary complex Banach spaces.

\section*{Source problem}
Chatzakou and Sarantopoulos ask in Problem~17 of arXiv:2003.11002 (official
PDF page~17) for a characterization of the complex Banach spaces $X$ such
that every continuous $m$-homogeneous polynomial $P=\widehat L$ satisfies
\[
              \|DP\|\le m\|P\|,
              \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
              \|L\|=\|P\|,
\]
for every degree $m$ \cite{CS}.  In the usual notation this says
$c(m,X)=1$ for all $m\ge1$.

\section*{Supporting theorem and identification}
Dimant, Galicer, and Rodriguez call $c(2,X)=1$ the symmetric operator norm
property.  Their Theorem~3.1 states that an infinite-dimensional complex
Banach space with this property must satisfy
\[
                         p(X)=q(X)=2,
\]
where $p(X)$ and $q(X)$ are its optimal type and cotype exponents
\cite[Theorem~3.1]{DGR}.

\begin{theorem}
Let $L_p(\mu)$ be infinite dimensional and complex, with $1\le p\le\infty$.
Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $c(m,L_p(\mu))=1$ for every $m\ge1$;
\item Bernstein's inequality $\|DP\|\le m\|P\|$ holds for every continuous
      homogeneous polynomial and every degree;
\item $p=2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Identification.}
The standard optimal exponents of an infinite-dimensional $L_p$ space are
\[
                 p(L_p)=\min\{p,2\},\qquad
                 q(L_p)=\max\{p,2\},
\]
with the usual endpoint interpretation.  The all-degree property implies
$c(2,L_p)=1$, so the supporting theorem forces both exponents to equal two
and hence $p=2$.  Conversely, $L_2(\mu)$ is a complex Hilbert space and
Banach's polarization theorem gives $c(m,L_2)=1$ in every degree.  The source
records the derivative/polarization equivalence, giving (1)$\Leftrightarrow$(2).

\section*{Provenance and scope}
The supporting paper predates the source problem and does not identify itself
as an answer to Problem~17.  The relation is therefore an agent-identified
implication.  It settles only the infinite-dimensional complex $L_p$ family.
It does not classify finite-dimensional spaces, arbitrary complex Banach
spaces with $c(2,X)=1$, or spaces $H_1\oplus_\infty H_2$.

The official source page and supporting Theorem~3.1 (including its proof)
were independently inspected rather than inferred from the interrupted
temporary PDF.  Exact-id, theorem-name, polarization-constant, and
type/cotype searches found no full arbitrary-space classification through
21 August 2026.  A separate attempt record documents the unsuccessful full
upgrade.
