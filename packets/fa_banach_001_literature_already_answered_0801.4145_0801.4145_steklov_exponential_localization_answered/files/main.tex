important correction to the source formulation.

\section*{Source question}

Zagrebnov's Conjecture 2.7 on PDF page 6 of \cite{Zagrebnov} attributes to
Hislop and Lutzer the claim that harmonic extensions of
Dirichlet-to-Neumann eigenfunctions have exponential interior decay.  The
display in that paper reads
\[
 O\!\left(\exp[-k\,\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal C,\partial\Omega)]\right).
\]

\section*{Explicit later answer}

Galkowski and Toth formulate the spectral problem semiclassically as
\[
 (h\mathcal D-1)\varphi_h=0,
\]
so the spectral frequency is $h^{-1}$.  In their introduction they explicitly
write that their main result proves the Hislop--Lutzer conjecture in arbitrary
dimension \cite{GT}.  Their Theorem 1, on PDF pages 2--3, proves for compact
real-analytic manifolds with real-analytic boundary a sharp near-boundary
estimate of the form
\[
 |\partial_x^\alpha u_h(x)|
 \leq C_{\alpha,\delta}h^{-N_\alpha}
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{d(x)}h\right)
\]
in the stated tubular region, together with a global interior estimate.  This
is the intended exponential localization, with rate governed by the
Dirichlet-to-Neumann eigenvalue.

\section*{Statement correction and scope}

The standalone wording in \cite{Zagrebnov} omits the real-analytic hypothesis
present in the Hislop--Lutzer conjecture and uses $k$ immediately after $k$
has denoted ordinal eigenvalue position.  Those are material differences:

\begin{itemize}
\item Galkowski--Toth stress that analyticity is needed for exponential
  accuracy; smooth calculus gives only $O(h^\infty)$ control.
\item The decay rate is spectral frequency $h^{-1}=\lambda$, not ordinal
  index.  On a three-dimensional ball, degree-$l$ spherical harmonics have
  eigenvalue $l/R$ with multiplicity $2l+1$, so ordinal index is of order
  $l^2$, while the harmonic lifting decays like $(r/R)^l$.
\end{itemize}

Indeed, the ball example immediately following Conjecture 2.7 in
\cite{Zagrebnov} obtains $O(e^{-l(R-r)/R})$, which is consistent with spectral
frequency and not with the ordinal index after multiplicities are counted.
Accordingly, the analytically corrected intended conjecture is answered by
\cite{GT}; the literal smooth/index sentence in the 2008 source should not be
quoted as the theorem they proved.

\section*{Other extracted signal}

The trace-norm product-approximation conjecture later in \cite{Zagrebnov} is
followed in the same section by the statement that the displayed argument
proves it ``at least for the ball.''  That ball case is a same-paper
ask-and-answer passage, not a separate literature-answer target.  Any genuinely
general-domain version would require a new precise formulation of the
approximating family and uniform trace estimates.
