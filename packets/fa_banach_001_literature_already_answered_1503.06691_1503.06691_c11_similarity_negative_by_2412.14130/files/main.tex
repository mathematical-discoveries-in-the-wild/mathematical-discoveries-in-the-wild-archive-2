\textbf{Status: literature already answered, negatively.}
Question~18 of K\'erchy asks whether a polynomially bounded operator of class
$C_{11}$ must be similar to a contraction.  Gamal' explicitly states that her
construction answers this question negatively: it is polynomially bounded and
quasisimilar to a unitary, but is not similar to a contraction.
}}
\end{center}

\section{Original question}

L\'aszl\'o K\'erchy, \emph{Quasianalytic polynomially bounded operators},
arXiv:1503.06691~\cite{source}, asks on arXiv PDF page~18:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Question 18.} If the polynomially bounded operator $T$ is of class
$C_{11}$, is $T$ similar to a contraction?
\end{quote}
Here $C_{11}$ means that neither $T$ nor $T^*$ has a nonzero stable vector.
The source notes immediately before the question that such an operator is
quasisimilar to its unitary asymptote.

\section{Separate later answer}

Maria F. Gamal', \emph{Examples of cyclic polynomially bounded operators that
are not similar to contractions}, arXiv:2412.14130~\cite{answerpaper}, states
on arXiv PDF page~2 that the paper provides a negative answer to Question~18
of K\'erchy~\cite{source}.  The same paragraph says that the construction
produces polynomially bounded operators which are not similar to contractions
and are quasisimilar to the bilateral shift and to cyclic reductive unitaries.
It derives these examples through the Section~2 transfer construction and the
main Theorem~7.1 (PDF page~24).

For completeness, quasisimilarity to a unitary indeed places the example in
the source's class $C_{11}$.  Suppose quasiaffinities $X,Y$ satisfy
\[
 XT=UX,\qquad YU=TY.
\]
If $T^nh\to0$, then $U^nXh=XT^nh\to0$.  Unitarity gives $Xh=0$, and
injectivity of $X$ gives $h=0$.  Taking adjoints in $YU=TY$ gives
$U^*Y^*=Y^*T^*$; since $Y$ has dense range, $Y^*$ is injective, and the same
argument shows that $(T^*)^nh\to0$ implies $h=0$.  Thus $T$ is $C_{11}$,
while Gamal's construction is not similar to a contraction.

\section{Provenance and scope}

This is an author-explicit literature answer: the supporting paper cites
K\'erchy's source as reference~[K], quotes Question~18, and says the answer is
negative.  No new mathematical result is claimed here.

The identification settles only Question~18.  The source's later questions
about whether every polynomially bounded operator is quasisimilar to a
contraction, the power-bounded singular-unitary problem, and invariant
subspaces for full residual spectrum are not resolved by this status note.
