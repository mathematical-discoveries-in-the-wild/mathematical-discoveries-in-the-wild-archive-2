\section{Source question}

Jabbari, Ebadian, and Eshaghi Gordji introduce $\varphi$-amenability for
topological semigroups in \cite{JEG}.  For a continuous character
$\varphi:S\to\T$, they write
\[
 P(\varphi)=\{s\in S:\varphi(s)=1\}.
\]
Corollary~5.2(ii) is the assertion that every continuous affine action of
$P(\varphi)$ on a nonempty compact convex subset of a locally convex space has
a common fixed point.  Remark~5.3 asks whether this condition implies that
$S$ is $\varphi$-amenable.  The source defines $\varphi$-amenability to mean
\emph{both} left and right $\varphi$-amenability.

\section{Idea of the proof}

The fixed-point condition is one-sided.  A right-zero semigroup
$\R=\{0,1\}$, whose product is $ij=j$, has exactly the required affine
fixed-point property: a fixed point of either one of the action maps is
automatically fixed by both.  On the opposite side, however, right
translations by the two idempotents collapse $\R$ onto two different fibers.
No normalized invariant functional can survive both collapses.

To make the character nontrivial, attach a cyclic sign coordinate.  Thus
$S=C_2\times\R$ and $\varphi$ is the sign character on $C_2$.  Its kernel
$P(\varphi)$ is precisely the right-zero factor.  A short quotient-module
argument derives the needed invariant functional directly from the definition
of right $\varphi$-amenability; two indicator functions then give the
contradiction.

\section{A necessary functional for right $\varphi$-amenability}

For $s\in S$, let $R_s$ denote right translation on $C(S)$,
\[
 (R_sf)(t)=f(ts).
\]
The next finite-dimensional lemma is included so that the counterexample does
not rely on a separate mean-characterization theorem from the source.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:mean}
Let $S$ be a finite discrete semigroup and let $\varphi:S\to\T$ be a
character.  If $S$ is right $\varphi$-amenable in the sense of \cite{JEG},
then there is $m\in C(S)^*$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean}
 m(\varphi)=1,
 \qquad m(R_sf)=\varphi(s)m(f)
 \quad(s\in S,\ f\in C(S)).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $E=C(S)$ and make it an $S$-bimodule by
\[
 (s\cdot f)(t)=f(ts),
 \qquad (f\cdot s)(t)=\varphi(s)f(t).
\]
The line $\C\varphi$ is an $S$-submodule, because
$s\cdot\varphi=\varphi\cdot s=\varphi(s)\varphi$.  Hence
$X=E/\C\varphi$ is a Banach $S$-bimodule whose right action is
$x\cdot s=\varphi(s)x$, exactly as required in the definition of right
$\varphi$-amenability.  Identify
\[
 X^*=Y=\{F\in E^*:F(\varphi)=0\}.
\]

Choose $F_0\in E^*$ with $F_0(\varphi)=1$.  With the usual dual module
actions, define
\[
 D(s)=s\cdot F_0-F_0\cdot s.
\]
This is an inner derivation in $E^*$ and it takes values in $Y$, since its two
terms have the same value $\varphi(s)$ on $\varphi$.  Because $S$ is finite
and discrete, $D$ is bounded and continuous.  Right $\varphi$-amenability
therefore supplies $F\in Y$ such that
\[
 D(s)=s\cdot F-F\cdot s\qquad(s\in S).
\]
Set $m=F_0-F$.  Then $m(\varphi)=1$ and $s\cdot m=m\cdot s$.  Evaluating this
identity at $f\in E$ gives
\[
 \varphi(s)m(f)=m(f\cdot s)=(s\cdot m)(f)
 =(m\cdot s)(f)=m(s\cdot f)=m(R_sf),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:mean}.
\end{proof}

\section{Counterexample}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The converse asked for in Remark~5.3 of \cite{JEG} is false, even when $S$ is
a four-element discrete semigroup and $\varphi$ is nontrivial.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $C_2=\{0,1\}$ with addition modulo two and let
$\R=\{0,1\}$ be the right-zero semigroup, so $ij=j$.  On
\[
 S=C_2\times\R
\]
the direct-product multiplication is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product}
 (\varepsilon,i)(\delta,j)=(\varepsilon+\delta\!\!\pmod 2,j).
\end{equation}
Give $S$ the discrete topology and define
\[
 \varphi(\varepsilon,i)=(-1)^\varepsilon.
\]
Associativity follows from the direct-product construction, and
\eqref{eq:product} immediately gives
$\varphi(xy)=\varphi(x)\varphi(y)$.  Thus $\varphi$ is a continuous nontrivial
character.  Moreover,
\[
 P(\varphi)=\{p_0,p_1\},
 \qquad p_i=(0,i),
 \qquad p_ip_j=p_j.
\]

We first verify Corollary~5.2(ii).  Let $T_p$ be any continuous affine action
of $P(\varphi)$ on a nonempty compact convex set $K$ in a locally convex
space.  By the Schauder--Tychonoff fixed-point theorem \cite{Tychonoff}, the
map $T_{p_0}$ has a fixed point $y\in K$.  For either $p\in P(\varphi)$,
\[
 T_py=T_pT_{p_0}y=T_{pp_0}y=T_{p_0}y=y,
\]
because $pp_0=p_0$.  Hence $y$ is a common fixed point, and condition (ii)
holds.

It remains to show that $S$ is not $\varphi$-amenable.  Suppose instead that
$S$ were right $\varphi$-amenable.  Lemma~\ref{lem:mean} would give a
functional $m$ satisfying \eqref{eq:mean}.  For $i=0,1$, let
\[
 u_i(\varepsilon,j)=\begin{cases}1,&j=i,\\0,&j\ne i.\end{cases}
\]
Right multiplication by $p_i$ sends $(\varepsilon,j)$ to
$(\varepsilon,i)$.  Consequently
\[
 R_{p_i}(\varphi u_{1-i})=0.
\]
Since $\varphi(p_i)=1$, equation \eqref{eq:mean} implies
\[
 m(\varphi u_{1-i})=m(R_{p_i}(\varphi u_{1-i}))=0.
\]
Using $i=0$ and $i=1$ yields
$m(\varphi u_0)=m(\varphi u_1)=0$.  But $u_0+u_1=1$, so
\[
 1=m(\varphi)=m(\varphi u_0)+m(\varphi u_1)=0,
\]
a contradiction.  Thus $S$ is not right $\varphi$-amenable and therefore is
not $\varphi$-amenable.  This disproves the converse.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A two-element version]
If the trivial character is allowed, take $S=\R$ itself and
$\varphi\equiv1$.  The same fixed-point proof applies.  If a functional from
Lemma~\ref{lem:mean} existed, right translation by $i$ would annihilate
$u_{1-i}$, forcing $m(u_0)=m(u_1)=0$ and contradicting $m(1)=1$.  Thus two
elements already suffice for the literal question; Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
shows that triviality of the character is not the issue.
\end{remark}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The proof is exact and finite-dimensional.  The accompanying verifier checks
all $64$ associativity triples, all $16$ character identities, the right-zero
kernel, and the complete invariant-functional system over the rational
numbers.  That system has coefficient rank $4$ and augmented rank $5$.

The left/right distinction is essential.  Corollary~5.2(ii) is a one-sided
fixed-point property of $P(\varphi)$, whereas the source's unqualified term
``$\varphi$-amenable'' is explicitly two-sided.  The theorem completely
answers Remark~5.3 as written.  It does not resolve the repaired question
whether condition (ii) forces \emph{left} $\varphi$-amenability.

The run indexes contained no matching result, attempt, or proof-gap packet.
Bounded web/arXiv searches on 9 August 2026 used the exact Remark~5.3 wording,
the title, DOI and authors, and combinations of $P(\varphi)$, fixed points,
right-zero semigroups, and character amenability.  They found the source and
bibliographic records but no later answer, correction, or matching
counterexample.  This is not an exhaustive novelty claim.
