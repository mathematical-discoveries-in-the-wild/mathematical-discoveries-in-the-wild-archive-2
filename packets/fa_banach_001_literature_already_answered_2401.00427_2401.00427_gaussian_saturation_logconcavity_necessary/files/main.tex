\textbf{Status: literature already answered.}
The universal all-even Gaussian-saturation statement proposed as equation
(3.5) of the source is false.  A later theorem proves the statement under
log-concavity, and a subsequent paper explains why log-concavity cannot be
omitted.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

\section{The source target}

For inverse Brascamp--Lieb data $(L,c,Q)$, the source defines
\[
 \Lambda(L,c,Q;\mathbf f)
 =\int_{\mathbb R^d}e^{-\pi\langle x,Qx\rangle}
   \prod_{i=1}^m f_i(L_i x)^{c_i}\,dx.
\]
In Section 3.2, PDF page 15, it proposes equation (3.5): for every datum,
the infimum over normalized nonnegative even inputs should equal the infimum
over centered Gaussian inputs.  The statement is offered as a degenerate
extension of Barthe--Wolff Gaussian saturation and as a route to the
Kolesnikov--Werner many-function Santal\'o conjecture.

\section{What the later literature proves}

Nakamura and Tsuji subsequently proved a sharp positive theorem.  Their
Theorem 1.3 in arXiv:2409.13611 states that for positive exponents and an
arbitrary symmetric quadratic kernel, centered Gaussians exhaust the
inverse Brascamp--Lieb lower bound when all inputs are nonnegative, even,
integrable, and \emph{log-concave}.  Their Theorem 4.1 also establishes the
linked Kolesnikov--Werner exponential-kernel inequality.

Milman then showed in Section 1.1 of arXiv:2501.11018 that log-concavity is
essential.  If the same lower bound held for all even inputs, his reduction
would imply
\[
 \mathbb P(X_1\in K,\,X_2\in L)
 \geq \mathbb P(X_1\in K)\mathbb P(X_2\in L)             \tag{1}
\]
for every centered correlated Gaussian pair and every origin-symmetric
measurable $K,L$.  Applying (1) both to $L$ and to its complement forces
equality, hence independence of those two events, which is false for a
generic correlated pair.

Milman records the resulting inverse Brascamp--Lieb counterexample with
$N=2$, $m=2$, $n_1=n_2=1$, $c_1=c_2=1$,
\[
 h_1=\mathbf 1_{[-1,1]},\qquad
 h_2=\mathbf 1_{\mathbb R\setminus[-1,1]},
\]
and the quadratic data induced by a generic positive-definite covariance
matrix $\Sigma$.  Concretely one may take a standard Gaussian pair with
correlation $1/2$: strict positive association of the two interval events
makes the interval/complement version of (1) fail.

\section{Disposition and scope}

The source's unrestricted ``for any datum, all even inputs'' conjecture is
therefore answered negatively in the later literature.  Its natural
log-concave repair is answered affirmatively.  This status identification
does not claim that every broader version of the original
Kolesnikov--Werner conjecture for arbitrary radial kernels follows from
these papers; it resolves precisely the Gaussian-saturation route proposed
in equation (3.5) and its cited exponential-kernel application.
