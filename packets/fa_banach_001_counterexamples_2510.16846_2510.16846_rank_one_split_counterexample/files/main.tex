\section{Source problem}

Tang and Zhang~\cite{TangZhang} define $c_p(m)$ to be the least constant such
that
\[
 \left\|\sum_{k=1}^m A_k\right\|_p
 \le c_p(m)\left\|\sum_{k=1}^m |A_k|\right\|_p
 \qquad(A_k\in M_n(\C)),
\]
where $|A|=(A^*A)^{1/2}$ and $\|\cdot\|_p$ is the Schatten $p$-norm.
Their Conjecture 3.1 proposes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-conjecture}
 c_p(m)=
 \frac{\sqrt{x_{p,m}(x_{p,m}+m-1)}}
 {(x_{p,m}^{,p}+m-1)^{1/p}},
 \qquad x_{p,m}^{,p}=2x_{p,m}+m-1.
\end{equation}
The conjecture and its rank-one heuristic occur on page 4 of the source PDF.

\section{Proof intuition}

The source heuristic uses matrices $A_k=u v_k^*$ with a \emph{common} left
vector $u$.  Their sum therefore has rank one.  That is stable at the
Hilbert--Schmidt exponent $p=2$, but it cannot be extremal below $2$.

Indeed, rotate one left vector away from the other by a small amount
$\delta$.  The principal singular value suffers only a linear loss in
$\delta$, while a new singular value is born with squared size proportional
to $\delta$.  Its contribution to the $p$-th power of the Schatten norm is
therefore proportional to $\delta^{p/2}$.  When $1<p<2$, we have $p/2<1$, so
$\delta^{p/2}$ dominates the linear loss.  The denominator is unchanged,
because it depends only on the right vectors.  This turns every proposed
two-matrix extremizer into a strict violation.

\section{Counterexample theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $1<p<2$, the value proposed by \eqref{eq:source-conjecture} is
strictly smaller than $c_p(2)$.  More precisely, there exist real rank-one
matrices $A_1,A_2\in M_2(\R)$ such that
\[
 \frac{\|A_1+A_2\|_p}{\||A_1|+|A_2|\|_p}
 > \frac{\sqrt{x(x+1)}}{(x^p+1)^{1/p}},
 \qquad x^p=2x+1.
\]
Consequently Conjecture 3.1 of Tang--Zhang is false.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $1<p<2$, put $q=p/2\in(1/2,1)$, and let $x>1$ be the positive solution
of $x^p=2x+1$.  Set
\[
 \beta=\frac{x-1}{x+1}\in(0,1).
\]
For a parameter $\alpha\in(0,1]$, take unit vectors in $\R^2$
\[
 u_1=v_1=e_1,\qquad
 u_2=\alpha e_1+\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}\,e_2,\qquad
 v_2=\beta e_1+\sqrt{1-\beta^2}\,e_2
\]
and define
\[
 A_1=u_1v_1^{\mathsf T},\qquad A_2=u_2v_2^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Since $u_i$ and $v_i$ are unit vectors,
\[
 A_i^*A_i=v_iv_i^{\mathsf T},\qquad |A_i|=v_iv_i^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The two eigenvalues of $|A_1|+|A_2|$ are $1+\beta$ and $1-\beta$.

Write $U=[u_1\ u_2]$ and $V=[v_1\ v_2]$.  Then
$A_1+A_2=UV^{\mathsf T}$, and the left and right Gram matrices are
\[
 G_U=U^{\mathsf T}U=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\\ \alpha&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 G_V=V^{\mathsf T}V=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&\beta\\ \beta&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The squared singular values of $UV^{\mathsf T}$ are the eigenvalues of
$G_UG_V$.  These Gram matrices are simultaneously diagonalized by
$(1,1)^{\mathsf T}$ and $(1,-1)^{\mathsf T}$, so the squared singular values
are
\[
 (1+\alpha)(1+\beta),\qquad (1-\alpha)(1-\beta).
\]
It follows that the $p$-th power of the ratio is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
 \mathcal R_p(\alpha,\beta)^p=
 \frac{((1+\alpha)(1+\beta))^q+
       ((1-\alpha)(1-\beta))^q}
 {(1+\beta)^p+(1-\beta)^p}.
\end{equation}

At $\alpha=1$, the substitution $(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)=x$ gives directly
\[
 \mathcal R_p(1,\beta)
 =\frac{\sqrt{x(x+1)}}{(x^p+1)^{1/p}},
\]
which is exactly the conjectured value for $m=2$.

We now split the common left vector.  Let $\alpha=1-\delta$ with
$0<\delta<1$.  The change in the numerator of \eqref{eq:ratio} is
\begin{align*}
 D(\delta)
 &= (1-\beta)^q\delta^q
   -(1+\beta)^q\bigl(2^q-(2-\delta)^q\bigr).
\end{align*}
For $0<q<1$ and $0<\delta<1$,
\[
 2^q-(2-\delta)^q
 =\int_{2-\delta}^{2}q t^{q-1}\,dt
 \le q\delta,
\]
because $t\ge1$ throughout the interval.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gain}
 D(\delta)\ge
 (1-\beta)^q\delta^q-q(1+\beta)^q\delta.
\end{equation}
Define
\[
 T=\left[
 \frac{1}{q}\left(\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}\right)^q
 \right]^{1/(1-q)},
 \qquad
 \delta=\frac12\min\{1,T\}.
\]
Then $0<\delta<1$ and $\delta<T$.  The right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:gain} is therefore strictly positive.  The denominator in
\eqref{eq:ratio} has not changed, so
\[
 \mathcal R_p(1-\delta,\beta)>\mathcal R_p(1,\beta).
\]
The matrices constructed above consequently violate the proposed constant.
\end{proof}

\section{Numerical illustration and verification}

The proof is exact and requires no computation.  As a transparent numerical
illustration, take $p=4/3$, $\alpha=9/10$, and $\beta=13/20$.  Thus
\[
 A_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A_2=\frac1{200}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 117&9\sqrt{231}\\
 13\sqrt{19}&\sqrt{4389}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The squared singular values of $A_1+A_2$ are $627/200$ and $7/200$, while
the eigenvalues of $|A_1|+|A_2|$ are $33/20$ and $7/20$.  A 100-digit
evaluation gives a ratio
\[
 1.01791549015466577995682703563,
\]
whereas the conjectured value is
\[
 1.01375834364417921376942970795.
\]
The deterministic script \path{code/verify_rank_one_split.py} checks the
closed formulas at six representative exponents and this illustration.  The
seeded exploratory script \path{code/lee_cp_extremal_search.py} searches
arbitrary small complex matrices; it is included only to document how the
analytic pattern was found.

\section{Scope, novelty, and review recommendation}

The theorem disproves the proposed formula for every $1<p<2$ already at
$m=n=2$.  It does not determine the true constant $c_p(2)$, decide the
formula for $p>2$, or determine $c_p(m)$ for general $m$.

The local run indexes were searched for the arXiv id, exact title, Lee's
conjecture, and the scalar equation in \eqref{eq:source-conjecture}.  Bounded
web searches on August 9, 2026 used the exact title, the DOI together with
``counterexample,'' the title together with ``counterexample Schatten p,''
and the exact scalar equation.  They located the accepted source version and
publisher record but no later resolution or this rank-one-splitting argument.
Novelty confidence is therefore positive but conservative pending expert
literature review.

Human review is strongly recommended.  The key points to check are the two
Gram-matrix spectral calculations and the scalar estimate
$2^q-(2-\delta)^q\le q\delta$.
