\section{Source conjecture}

For $p>1$ and $-1<N,D<1$, Benhida, Curto, and Exner associate the
geometrically regular weighted shift $W_\alpha$ with squared weights
\begin{equation}\label{weights}
 a_n:=\alpha_n^2=\frac{p^n+N}{p^n+D},\qquad n\geq0.
\end{equation}
Their Conjecture~1.3 asks whether: every nonboundary point of Sector~II fails
Bernstein interpolation; every nonboundary point of Sector~III fails moment
infinite divisibility; and the listed loci exhaust all $\MID$, subnormal, and
completely hyperexpansive shifts in the parameter square \cite{GRWS}.

The source says ``no $\alpha$ is interpolated'' in the first clause.  Its
immediately preceding theorem and the discussion following Theorem~2.5 make
clear that this refers to the squared-weight sequence $a=(\alpha_n^2)$; we
follow that established convention.

\section{Proof intuition}

Put $q=p^{-1}\in(0,1)$.  The two difficult sequence conditions become signed
atomic Hausdorff moment expansions supported at $q,q^2,\ldots$.  In
Sector~II, the complement $1-a_n$ has a negative mass at $q^2$, preventing
complete alternation and hence Bernstein interpolation.  In Sector~III,
$-\log a_n$ has negative masses at every even atom, preventing the log
complete alternation equivalent to $\MID$.  Compact Hausdorff moment measures
are unique, so these negative masses cannot be hidden by another positive
representation.

For complete hyperexpansivity, the first-difference moment sequence must be
completely monotone and therefore log-convex.  Its exact successive ratio is
known from the source.  Requiring those ratios to increase forces $D\geq pN$,
which cuts out precisely the sector the source already proved sufficient.
The source's Sector-IV determinant theorem and the elementary monotonicity of
the original weights then finish the global subnormal and $\MID$
classifications.

\section{Statement of the complete classification}

\begin{theorem}\label{classification}
Fix $p>1$ and $-1<N,D<1$, and define $W_\alpha$ by \eqref{weights}.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The squared-weight sequence $(a_n)$ is interpolated by a Bernstein
function if and only if
\[
 D=N\qquad\text{or}\qquad N<D\leq0.
\]
\item $W_\alpha$ is moment infinitely divisible if and only if
\[
 D=N\qquad\text{or}\qquad N<0\ \text{ and }\ N<D\leq -N.
\]
\item $W_\alpha$ is subnormal if and only if one of the following holds:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 D=N;\qquad N\leq0\ \text{ and }\ D>N;\\
 0<N<D\ \text{ and }\ D=p^kN\ \text{for some }k\in\Nats.
 \end{gathered}
\]
\item $W_\alpha$ is completely hyperexpansive if and only if
\[
 D=N\qquad\text{or}\qquad N<0\ \text{ and }\ pN\leq D<N.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Consequently all three clauses of Conjecture~1.3 in \cite{GRWS} hold.
\end{theorem}

\section{A signed Hausdorff uniqueness lemma}

We use the forward difference convention
$\nabla x_n=x_n-x_{n+1}$.  A sequence is completely monotone if
$\nabla^j x_n\geq0$ for all $j,n\geq0$; it is completely alternating if
$\nabla^j x_n\leq0$ for all $j\geq1$ and $n\geq0$.

\begin{lemma}[negative-atom obstruction]\label{negative-atom}
Let $r_k\in[0,1]$ be distinct and let
$\sum_k|c_k|<\infty$.  If
\[
 x_n=\sum_k c_kr_k^n\qquad(n\geq0)
\]
is completely monotone, then every $c_k\geq0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the Hausdorff moment theorem \cite[Chapter~III]{Akhiezer}, complete
monotonicity gives a finite positive measure $\mu$ on $[0,1]$ with moments
$x_n$.  The finite signed measure $\nu=\sum_kc_k\delta_{r_k}$ has the same
moments.  Hence $\mu-\nu$ annihilates every polynomial.  Polynomials are
uniformly dense in $C([0,1])$, so the Riesz representation theorem implies
$\mu-\nu=0$.  Thus $\nu=\mu$ is positive, and every atomic coefficient is
nonnegative.
\end{proof}

This compact signed-measure uniqueness principle is also emphasized in the
later Berger-charge work of the same authors \cite{Charges}.

\section{Bernstein interpolation}

The sign of the first weight difference follows directly from \eqref{weights}:
\begin{equation}\label{weight-difference}
 a_{n+1}-a_n
 =\frac{q^n(1-q)(D-N)}
 {(1+Dq^n)(1+Dq^{n+1})}.
\end{equation}
If $D<N$, the sequence decreases, so it cannot be sampled from a Bernstein
function, which is increasing.  If $D=N$, it is the constant sequence one.

It remains to treat $N<D$.  Suppose first that $D>0$.  Since $|D|<1$,
\begin{align}\label{bernstein-charge}
 1-a_n
 &=\frac{(D-N)q^n}{1+Dq^n}\\
 &=\sum_{k=1}^\infty (D-N)(-D)^{k-1}(q^k)^n.
\end{align}
This is an absolutely summable signed atomic representation.  Its coefficient
at $q^2$ is $-(D-N)D<0$.  Were $(a_n)$ completely alternating, the
nonnegative sequence $(1-a_n)$ would be completely monotone, contradicting
Lemma~\ref{negative-atom}.  Samples of a Bernstein function are completely
alternating, so no Bernstein interpolant exists.

For $N<D\leq0$, the source constructs a Bernstein interpolant
\cite[Corollary~2.6]{GRWS}.  These cases and the constant diagonal prove part
(1) of Theorem~\ref{classification}.  In particular, throughout Sector~II
away from its $D=0$ boundary, $D>0$ and Bernstein interpolation fails.  This
proves Conjecture~1.3(1), and \eqref{bernstein-charge} also rules out such
interpolation in Sectors~III and IV.

\section{Moment infinite divisibility}

For a contractive weighted shift, the source recalls that $\MID$ is equivalent
to log complete alternation of the squared weights.  When $a_n\leq1$, this is
equivalent to complete monotonicity of
\[
 t_n=-\log a_n.
\]

In Sector~III write $N=-u$ and $D=v$, where $0\leq u<v<1$.  Absolute
convergence of the logarithmic series gives
\begin{align}\label{log-charge}
 t_n
 &=\log\!\left(\frac{1+vq^n}{1-uq^n}\right)\\
 &=\sum_{k=1}^\infty
 \frac{u^k+(-1)^{k+1}v^k}{k}(q^k)^n.
\end{align}
For every even $k$ the coefficient is
$(u^k-v^k)/k<0$.  Lemma~\ref{negative-atom} shows that $(t_n)$ is not
completely monotone.  Hence no interior Sector-III shift is $\MID$.

On the boundary $v=u$, all even coefficients in \eqref{log-charge} vanish
and the odd coefficients are positive, consistently with the source's
Sector-II $\MID$ theorem.  Thus Conjecture~1.3(2) holds, including the edge
$u=0<v$.

To get the global equivalence in Theorem~\ref{classification}(2), combine
this obstruction with the source's $\MID$ results in Sectors~I and II, its
non-$\MID$ result for the subnormal Sector-IV rays
\cite[Theorem~2.9]{GRWS}, and the fact that $\MID$ implies subnormality.

\section{Subnormality}

A subnormal weighted shift is hyponormal, and a unilateral weighted shift is
hyponormal exactly when its weights are nondecreasing.  Thus
\eqref{weight-difference} excludes every point with $D<N$, except the
constant diagonal.

For the remaining half of the square, the source proves subnormality in
Sectors~I--III, including a countably atomic Berger measure for Sector~III
\cite[Theorem~2.8]{GRWS}.  In Sector~IV, Theorem~2.9 proves subnormality on
the rays $D=p^kN$, $k\geq1$.  Theorem~2.16 proves that every point off these
rays fails some finite $k$-hyponormality condition and hence cannot be
subnormal.  Algebraically, these are exactly the alternatives in part (3) of
Theorem~\ref{classification}.

\section{Complete hyperexpansivity}

Let $(\gamma_n)$ be the moment sequence of $W_\alpha$, so
$\gamma_{n+1}=a_n\gamma_n$, and put
\[
 \delta_n=\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_n.
\]
Complete hyperexpansivity is equivalent to complete monotonicity of
$(\delta_n)$ \cite[Theorem~2.10 and its preliminaries]{GRWS}.  If $D>N$,
then $a_0<1$ and $\delta_0<0$, which is impossible.  The diagonal is the
unweighted shift, so assume $D<N$; then every $\delta_n>0$.

Every positive completely monotone sequence is a Hausdorff moment sequence
and is log-convex by Cauchy--Schwarz.  Consequently the ratios
$r_n=\delta_{n+1}/\delta_n$ must be nondecreasing.  Directly (and as computed
in the source),
\begin{equation}\label{delta-ratio}
 r_n=\frac1p\frac{p^n+N}{p^n+D/p}
 =q\frac{1+Nq^n}{1+Dq^{n+1}}.
\end{equation}
A second exact subtraction gives
\begin{equation}\label{ratio-difference}
 r_{n+1}-r_n
 =\frac{q^{n+1}(1-q)(Dq-N)}
 {(1+Dq^{n+1})(1+Dq^{n+2})}.
\end{equation}
Therefore log-convexity forces $Dq-N\geq0$, equivalently $D\geq pN$.
Together with $D<N$, this is possible only when $N<0$ and
\[
 pN\leq D<N.
\]
This is precisely Sector~VIIIA.  Source Theorem~2.10 proves the entire sector
completely hyperexpansive, including its boundary.  Adding the diagonal
proves part (4) of Theorem~\ref{classification} and supplies the last missing
necessity statement in Conjecture~1.3(3).

\section{Assembly, verification, and novelty}

The four equivalences cover every point in the open parameter square.  The
only special boundaries are transparent in the signed expansions:
$D=0$ leaves a positive one-atom measure in \eqref{bernstein-charge};
$D=-N$ deletes every even atom in \eqref{log-charge}; and $D=pN<N<0$
makes \eqref{ratio-difference} an equality.  The positive Sector-IV rays and
the diagonal are separately covered by the source theorems cited above.

The bundled script \texttt{code/verify\_q\_series.py} symbolically verifies
\eqref{weight-difference}, the coefficients in
\eqref{bernstein-charge}--\eqref{log-charge}, and
\eqref{ratio-difference}.  It is an algebra check, not a replacement for the
Hausdorff uniqueness proof.

A bounded novelty search on 13 August 2026 covered local run indexes, the
exact title and conjecture label, and web/arXiv combinations of the sector and
property names.  The later safe-quotients paper \cite{SafeQuotients} and
signed-charge paper \cite{Charges} repeat only the partial classification;
the 2026-updated source v2 still states Conjecture~1.3.  No later claimed
solution or these exact necessity arguments were found.  Novelty confidence
is therefore moderate, not definitive.

Human review should focus on Lemma~\ref{negative-atom}, the contractive
$\MID$ equivalence used before \eqref{log-charge}, and the log-convexity
necessity leading to \eqref{ratio-difference}.  No unproved dependency is
currently known.
