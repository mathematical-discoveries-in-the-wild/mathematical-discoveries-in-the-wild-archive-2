\section{Source question}

The source is Ronald G. Douglas and Piotr W. Nowak,
\emph{Every finitely generated group is weakly exact}, arXiv:1109.0313
\cite{DouglasNowak}.  In Section~III, printed page~10, Definitions~15--16
introduce Hopf $A$-bimodules and call the predual $A=M_*$ of a
Hopf--von Neumann algebra weakly exact when
\[
  \mathcal H^1(A,\E)=0
\]
for every weak-$*$ operator closed Hopf $A$-bimodule of the displayed form.
The paper then asks:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Question 17.} Is every Banach algebra $A$ as above weakly exact?
\end{quote}

The source page, including the definitions, module actions, and question, is
shown below.

\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Convention note.}
Immediately before Definition~15 the source takes $X$ to be a \emph{right}
$A$-module and defines
\[
  (a\mathbin{\cdot}T)(x)=T(xa),
  \qquad
  (T\mathbin{\cdot}a)(x)=T(x)a.
  \tag{1}
\]
Definition~16 later says ``left $A$-module,'' although (1) uses the right
action.  We use the displayed convention.  Our $X$ carries the same
one-dimensional action on both sides, so it is also a left module if the
later word is read literally.

\section{Definitions and the finite monoid}

Let $S=\{0,1,2\}$ with multiplication
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 \cdot&0&1&2\\ \hline
 0&0&0&2\\
 1&0&1&2\\
 2&0&2&2
\end{array}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus $1$ is the identity, and $st=t$ whenever $t\in\{0,2\}$.
Put
\[
  M=\ell_\infty(S),
  \qquad
  (\Delta f)(s,t)=f(st).
\tag{3}
\]
Then $M$ is a finite-dimensional commutative von Neumann algebra.  Its
predual $A=M_*=\ell_1(S)$ has the convolution product
\[
  \delta_s\delta_t=\delta_{st}.
\tag{4}
\]

For the natural dual $A$-bimodule structure on $M$, we use
\[
 \langle h\mathbin{\cdot}a,b\rangle=\langle h,ab\rangle
 \quad(a,b\in A,\ h\in M).
\tag{5}
\]
In particular,
\[
  (h\mathbin{\cdot}\delta_s)(r)=h(sr).
\tag{6}
\]
A bounded linear map $D:A\to\E$ is a derivation when
\[
  D(ab)=a\mathbin{\cdot}D(b)+D(a)\mathbin{\cdot}b.
\tag{7}
\]
It is inner when it has the form
$a\mapsto a\mathbin{\cdot}\xi-\xi\mathbin{\cdot}a$ for some $\xi\in\E$.

\section{Proof intuition}

The monoid (2) has two different characters on its semigroup algebra.  The
augmentation character $\varphi$ is constantly one on the three point
masses, while the coefficient-at-the-identity character $\psi$ is one only
at $\delta_1$.  The coordinate line in $M$ supported at the identity is a
Hopf $A$-bimodule whose left action is $\varphi$ and whose right action is
$\psi$.

Derivations into this line are therefore $(\varphi,\psi)$-derivations.  The
asymmetry in the right-zero ideal $\{0,2\}$ lets a derivation distinguish
$0$ from $2$: it takes values $(1,0,0)$ on
$(\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$.  Inner derivations cannot make that
distinction; all of them have values proportional to $(1,0,1)$.  Hence the
first Hochschild cohomology is nonzero.  All ambient spaces are finite
dimensional, so the required operator weak-$*$ closedness is automatic.

\section{Counterexample theorem}

\begin{theorem}
There is a finite-dimensional commutative Hopf--von Neumann algebra $M$ whose
predual Banach algebra $A=M_*$ is not weakly exact in the sense of
Douglas--Nowak.  Consequently, the answer to their Question~17 is negative.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We verify each hypothesis and then exhibit a non-inner derivation.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{1. The Hopf--von Neumann algebra.}
The table (2) is associative.  Indeed, if the final factor $u$ is $0$ or
$2$, then both $(st)u$ and $s(tu)$ equal $u$; if $u=1$, both equal $st$.
The element $1$ is visibly a two-sided identity.

It follows that the map $\Delta$ in (3) is a unital $*$-homomorphism and is
coassociative.  It is normal because $M$ is finite dimensional.  It is also
injective: if $\Delta f=0$, then
\[
  f(t)=(\Delta f)(1,t)=0 \qquad(t\in S).
\]
Thus the example satisfies even the convention in which injectivity is
included in the definition of a Hopf--von Neumann coproduct.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{2. Two characters.}
For $a=\sum_{s\in S}a_s\delta_s\in A$, define
\[
  \varphi(a)=a_0+a_1+a_2,
  \qquad
  \psi(a)=a_1.
\tag{8}
\]
The augmentation $\varphi$ is multiplicative.  The functional $\psi$ is
multiplicative as well, because $st=1$ in $S$ if and only if $s=t=1$.

Let $X=\C$ with $x\mathbin{\cdot}a=\varphi(a)x$; give it the same left
action as noted above.  Identify $\mathcal L(X,M)$ with $M$ via
$T\leftrightarrow T(1)$.  Let
\[
  e=\1_{\{1\}}\in M,
  \qquad
  \E=\C e.
\tag{9}
\]

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{3. $\E$ is an admissible Hopf $A$-bimodule.}
For the left action in (1),
\[
  a\mathbin{\cdot}e=\varphi(a)e.
\tag{10}
\]
For the right action, (6) and the fact that $sr=1$ only when $s=r=1$
give
\[
  e\mathbin{\cdot}a=\psi(a)e.
\tag{11}
\]
Hence $\E$ is an $A$-bimodule.  It is also an $M$-module under pointwise
multiplication, since $he=h(1)e$ for every $h\in M$.  Finally $\E$ is
finite dimensional, and hence is closed in the weak-$*$ operator topology.
It is therefore a Hopf $A$-bimodule of exactly the type quantified over in
Definition~16 of the source.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{4. A derivation.}
Define $D:A\to\E$ on the point masses by
\[
  D(\delta_0)=e,
  \qquad
  D(\delta_1)=D(\delta_2)=0.
\tag{12}
\]
Write $d(0)=1$ and $d(1)=d(2)=0$, so that
$D(\delta_s)=d(s)e$.  By (10)--(11), the derivation identity on point
masses is
\[
  d(st)=d(t)+d(s)\psi(\delta_t).
\tag{13}
\]
If $t=0$, both sides of (13) are $1$; if $t=2$, both are $0$; and if
$t=1$, both are $d(s)$.  Thus (13) holds for every $s,t\in S$.
Bilinearity proves (7) for arbitrary $a,b\in A$.  The map $D$ is bounded
because $A$ and $\E$ are finite dimensional.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{5. The derivation is not inner.}
Every $\xi\in\E$ is $\lambda e$ for some $\lambda\in\C$.  By
(10)--(11), the corresponding inner derivation has point-mass values
\[
 \lambda\bigl(\varphi(\delta_s)-\psi(\delta_s)\bigr)e,
 \qquad s=0,1,2,
\]
that is, the coefficient vector
\[
  \lambda(1,0,1).
\tag{14}
\]
The derivation (12) has coefficient vector $(1,0,0)$, which is not of the
form (14).  Therefore $D$ is not inner, so
\[
  \mathcal H^1(A,\E)\ne0.
\]
The predual $A$ is not weakly exact.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and discovery audit}

The proof above is independent of computation.  The included direct verifier
checks all $27$ associativity
identities, the identity element, surjectivity of multiplication (and hence
injectivity of $\Delta$), multiplicativity of $\varphi$ and $\psi$, all nine
basis instances of (13), and the incompatible inner/derivation vectors.
It exits successfully.

The example was found by the included finite-semigroup search, which uses
exact rational linear algebra.  The exhaustive order-two run checks all $16$ tables, finds
$8$ associative ones and $14$ stable coordinate-module cases, and finds no
nonzero cohomology.  The order-three run checks all $3^9=19683$ tables,
finds $113$ associative tables and $341$ stable coordinate-module cases,
with $27$ hits.  For the unital example (2) with support $\{1\}$ it computes
\[
  \dim\operatorname{Der}(A,\E)=2,
  \quad
  \dim\operatorname{Inn}(A,\E)=1,
  \quad
  \dim\mathcal H^1(A,\E)=1.
\]
This enumeration is a discovery and sanity-check aid, not a premise of the
proof.

\section{Novelty bounds, limitations, and review recommendation}

On 9 August 2026, the run's registry, solution, attempt, and proof-gap
indexes were searched for arXiv:1109.0313 and the core phrases.  The local
full-source arXiv corpus and exact official-arXiv searches for
\emph{``weakly exact Banach algebra,''}
\emph{``Is every Banach algebra weakly exact,''} and
\emph{``Hopf $A$-bimodule weakly exact''} found the source paper but no later
answer to Question~17.  Within those bounded searches, this appears to be a
new counterexample; publication-level novelty is not certified.

The theorem answers the broad question under the Hopf--von Neumann and Hopf
module definitions printed in the source.  The example is finite
dimensional, unital, commutative, and has an injective coproduct.  It is not a
group algebra and is not claimed to satisfy the extra antipode, cancellation,
Haar-weight, or locally compact quantum-group axioms that some narrower
frameworks impose.

Human review should focus on (i) the orientation of the natural dual action
in (5)--(6), (ii) the source's right/left-module wording inconsistency and
the fact that $X$ carries both actions here, and (iii) whether the intended
scope of ``Hopf--von Neumann algebra'' in Question~17 silently includes any
axioms beyond a normal unital coassociative coproduct.  No such extra axioms
are stated in the question's setup, and injectivity is verified explicitly.
