\section{Source conjecture}

A (closed) real plank of Euclidean width $w$ is a set
\[
 P=\{x\in\R^d:a\le \langle u,x\rangle\le b\},
 \qquad \|u\|=1,\quad w=b-a.
\]
Conjecture 1.15 of Glazyrin--Karasev--Polyanskii~\cite{GKPextensions},
originally Conjecture 1.17 of~\cite{GKPcovering}, asks whether, for $d\ge4$,
every finite plank cover of the unit sphere $S^{d-1}$ has total width at least
$2$.  The source statement is reproduced below.

\section{Why the small-cardinality case becomes a ball problem}

The complement of finitely many planks is a union of open polyhedral sign
cells.  If a point $x$ is outside every plank, choose, for each plank, the
one of its two outer open halfspaces containing $x$.  Their intersection is
the sign cell containing $x$.

With fewer than $d$ independent normals, every such cell contains a line.
With exactly $d$ independent signed normals, solve one square linear system
to find a direction that strictly increases all defining linear forms.  Thus,
for at most $d$ planks, every nonempty sign cell has a ray to infinity.  A ray
starting inside the unit ball must cross its sphere.  Consequently, covering
the sphere already forces covering the whole ball, where Bang's plank theorem
applies.

\section{The partial theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $P_1,\ldots,P_N$ be real planks covering $S^{d-1}\subset\R^d$, and let
$u_i$ be a unit normal of $P_i$.  If either
\begin{enumerate}
\item $N\le d$, or
\item $\operatorname{span}\{u_1,\ldots,u_N\}\ne\R^d$,
\end{enumerate}
then the same planks cover the closed unit ball $\B$.  In particular,
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^N \operatorname{width}(P_i)\ge2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 P_i=\{y\in\R^d:a_i\le\langle u_i,y\rangle\le b_i\}.
\]
Suppose, for a contradiction, that some $x\in\B$ is outside every $P_i$.
Because the sphere is covered, $\|x\|<1$.  For each $i$, exactly one of the
strict inequalities
\[
 \langle u_i,x\rangle>b_i,
 \qquad
 \langle -u_i,x\rangle>-a_i
\]
holds.  Choose $v_i\in\{u_i,-u_i\}$ and $c_i\in\{b_i,-a_i\}$ so that
$\langle v_i,x\rangle>c_i$.  The open sign cell
\[
 C=\{y\in\R^d:\langle v_i,y\rangle>c_i\text{ for every }i\}
\]
therefore contains $x$ and is disjoint from every plank.

First suppose the normals span a proper subspace.  Choose a nonzero vector
$v$ orthogonal to all $v_i$.  Then $x+tv\in C$ for every $t\ge0$.

It remains to treat the first hypothesis when the normals span $\R^d$.
Since $N\le d$, necessarily $N=d$, and $v_1,\ldots,v_d$ are linearly
independent.  Hence the square system
\[
 \langle v_i,v\rangle=1\qquad(1\le i\le d)
\]
has a solution $v$.  For every $t\ge0$,
\[
 \langle v_i,x+tv\rangle=\langle v_i,x\rangle+t>c_i,
\]
so again $x+tv\in C$.

In either case $C$ contains a ray $x+tv$ with $v\ne0$.  Its norm starts below
$1$ and tends to infinity.  By continuity the ray meets $S^{d-1}$, at a point
still in $C$ and hence outside every plank.  This contradicts sphere coverage.
Thus the planks cover $\B$.

Bang's plank theorem~\cite{Bang} says that planks covering a convex body have
total width at least its minimal width.  The unit ball has minimal width $2$,
which proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:four}
In $\R^4$, Conjecture 1.15 holds for every cover of $S^3$ by at most four
planks.  More generally, in every dimension it holds for at most $d$ planks.
Any counterexample must use at least $d+1$ planks whose normals span $\R^d$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The positive claims are Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  Its contrapositive gives the
two necessary conditions on a counterexample.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, obstruction, and verification}

The argument does not settle the full conjecture.  Already $d+1$ suitably
oriented open halfspaces can have a bounded simplex as their intersection.
Equivalently, the selected signed normals can positively span $\R^d$, so the
common recession direction used above need not exist.  A direct attempt to
compare the facet distances of such a bounded uncovered cell with the widths
of the original planks does not close: the opposite boundary of each plank
generally cuts a different neighboring cell.  The two polynomial-plank
conjectures in the source are also unaffected.

No computation enters the proof.  The only external theorem is Bang's
classical plank theorem.  Strict inequalities are preserved along the ray, so
there is no boundary or closure issue.

\paragraph{Bounded novelty check.}
The original conjecture paper records the $d=3$ case and, for $d\ge4$, a
reduction when there are at most three planks~\cite[Remark after Conjecture
1.17]{GKPcovering}.  A current survey still lists the conjecture as open and
records the same $S^3$ at-most-three-plank subcase~\cite{Verreault}.  The run's
registry, solution, attempt, and proof-gap indexes, plus bounded exact-phrase,
boundary-covering, four-plank, cardinality, title, DOI, and citing-work
searches through 11 August 2026, found no statement of the $N\le d$ theorem or
the proper-normal-span extension.  Novelty confidence is moderate; this short
polyhedral observation could be folklore, and no priority claim is made.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Check the sign-cell construction, the square system in the full-rank $N=d$
case, and the ray--sphere crossing.  Then independently search the classical
spherical-plank literature for this cardinality reduction.  Subject to that
audit, classify the result as a likely valid substantial partial advance, not
as a full solution.
