\section{Source question}

Let $A$ be a $C^*$-algebra and let $(\phi_m)\subset A^*$ be a bounded
sequence spanning $\ell_1$ almost isometrically.  Proposition 1.3 of
Pfitzner~\cite{Pfi02} says that, for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, one can
find a subsequence $(\phi_{m_n})$ and pairwise orthogonal positive normalized
elements $(a_n)$ and $(b_n)$ in $A$ such that
\[
 \|\phi_{m_n}-b_n\phi_{m_n}a_n\|<\varepsilon
 \qquad(n\in\mathbb N).
\]
The second question in Section 6 asks whether these objects can instead be
chosen so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:question}
 \|\phi_{m_n}-b_n\phi_{m_n}a_n\|\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Here $(b\phi a)(x)=\phi(axb)$.  The theorem below proves this for every
compact $C^*$-algebra, meaning a $c_0$-direct sum of elementary compact
operator algebras.

\section{Finite-rank cutdowns of orthogonal functionals}

We use the standard description
\[
 A=\bigoplus_{i\in I}^{c_0}\K(H_i),\qquad
 A^*=\bigoplus_{i\in I}^{\ell_1}\Sone(H_i),\qquad
 A^{**}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}^{\ell_\infty}B(H_i).
\]
A functional $\psi\in A^*$ is represented by a trace-class family
$T=(T_i)$, with $\psi(x)=\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(T_i x_i)$ and
$\|\psi\|=\sum_i\|T_i\|_1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cut}
Let $(\psi_n)$ be pairwise orthogonal functionals in $A^*$, and let
$(\eta_n)$ be positive.  For every $n$ with $\psi_n\ne0$ there are
finite-rank projections $e_n,f_n\in A$ such that both families are pairwise
orthogonal and
\[
 \|\psi_n-f_n\psi_ne_n\|<\eta_n.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $T^{(n)}=(T_i^{(n)})$ for the trace-class family representing
$\psi_n$.  Choose a finite set of coordinates and, in each chosen
coordinate, finitely many singular vectors so that the corresponding finite
singular-value truncation $S^{(n)}$ satisfies
$\|T^{(n)}-S^{(n)}\|_1<\eta_n$.  If $e_n$ and $f_n$ are respectively the
right and left support projections of $S^{(n)}$, then they have finite
support in $I$, are finite-rank in every coordinate, and hence belong to
$A$.  They satisfy
\[
 f_nT^{(n)}e_n=S^{(n)},
 \]
after taking the truncation in a singular-value decomposition.

Orthogonality of two normal functionals is equivalent to orthogonality of
their right support projections and of their left support projections in
$A^{**}$.  Since $e_n$ and $f_n$ are subprojections of those supports, the
$e_n$ are pairwise orthogonal, as are the $f_n$.  Finally, the functional
$f_n\psi_ne_n$ is represented by $f_nT^{(n)}e_n=S^{(n)}$, so the asserted
norm estimate is exactly the trace-norm estimate above.
\end{proof}

\section{The partial answer}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $A=\bigoplus_{i\in I}^{c_0}\K(H_i)$ be a compact $C^*$-algebra, and let
$(\phi_m)\subset A^*$ be a bounded sequence spanning $\ell_1$ almost
isometrically.  Then there are a subsequence $(\phi_{m_n})$ and pairwise
orthogonal positive normalized elements $(a_n)$ and $(b_n)$ in $A$ such
that
\[
 \|\phi_{m_n}-b_n\phi_{m_n}a_n\|\longrightarrow0.
\]
The elements $a_n,b_n$ may be chosen to be finite-rank projections.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The von Neumann algebra $A^{**}$ has predual canonically equal to $A^*$.
Pfitzner's orthogonalization theorem~\cite[Theorem 1.2]{Pfi02} (equivalently,
the JBW$^*$-triple extension~\cite[Theorem 4.1]{PP16}) yields a subsequence,
still denoted $(\phi_{m_n})$, and pairwise orthogonal functionals
$\psi_n\in A^*$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orthapprox}
 \|\phi_{m_n}-\psi_n\|\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}

First suppose, after discarding finitely many terms, that $\psi_n\ne0$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:cut} with $\eta_n\downarrow0$, and put
$a_n=e_n$, $b_n=f_n$.  Left and right multiplication by contractions are
contractive on $A^*$, whence
\begin{align*}
 \|\phi_{m_n}-b_n\phi_{m_n}a_n\|
 &\le \|\phi_{m_n}-\psi_n\|
      +\|\psi_n-b_n\psi_na_n\|
      +\|b_n(\psi_n-\phi_{m_n})a_n\|\\
 &<2\|\phi_{m_n}-\psi_n\|+\eta_n\longrightarrow0.
\end{align*}

It remains only to handle the possible zero terms.  If infinitely many
$\psi_n$ vanish, then along that subsequence \eqref{eq:orthapprox} gives
$\|\phi_{m_n}\|\to0$.  The normalized sequence
$(\phi_m/\|\phi_m\|)$ spans $\ell_1$ isomorphically, so $A^*$, and hence
$A$, is infinite-dimensional.  An infinite-dimensional compact
$C^*$-algebra contains an infinite sequence of pairwise orthogonal
finite-rank projections; choose two such sequences $(a_n)$ and $(b_n)$.
Then
\[
 \|\phi_{m_n}-b_n\phi_{m_n}a_n\|
 \le 2\|\phi_{m_n}\|\longrightarrow0.
\]
This completes both cases.
\end{proof}

\section{Why this does not settle the general case}

The proof first orthogonalizes in the von Neumann envelope $A^{**}$ and then
cuts the orthogonal support projections down inside $A$.  In a compact
$C^*$-algebra, finite singular-value truncation supplies these inner
cutdowns while preserving pairwise orthogonality.  For a general
$C^*$-algebra the support projections remain in $A^{**}$, and the missing
step is a countable separation theorem producing pairwise orthogonal
elements of $A$ with individual support errors tending to zero.

A direct attempt to let the distortion parameter tend to zero in the proof
of Proposition 1.3 encounters the same issue in quantitative form: leakage
into all previously chosen closed supports accumulates to a fixed loss.  A
summable leakage schedule makes that loss small but does not make it vanish.
Thus the compact-operator truncation above is a genuine additional input,
not merely a reparameterization of the fixed-$\varepsilon$ proof.

\section{Literature scope}

The exact title, question phrase, and citation trail were searched.  The
later paper~\cite{PP16} proves the normal orthogonal-functional theorem for
JBW$^*$-triples and a fixed-accuracy non-normal theorem, but it does not state
\eqref{eq:question} for arbitrary $C^*$-algebras.  No source stating the
compact-algebra theorem above was located in the bounded search.  Novelty
confidence is therefore limited rather than definitive.
