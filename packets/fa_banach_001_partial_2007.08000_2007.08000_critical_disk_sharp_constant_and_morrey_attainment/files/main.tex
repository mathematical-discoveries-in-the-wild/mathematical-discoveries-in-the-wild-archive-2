\section{Source question and decomposition into branches}

Brasco, G\'omez-Castro, and V\'azquez characterize homogeneous fractional
Sobolev spaces in arXiv:2007.08000v2~\cite{source}.  Section~6 on arXiv PDF
page~22 asks:

\begin{sourcequestion}
What are the optimal constants, and are they achieved by extremal functions,
in the subcritical fractional Sobolev inequality, the critical
Poincar\'e--Wirtinger inequality, and the supercritical fractional Morrey
inequality?
\end{sourcequestion}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_question_crop.png}
\end{center}

The question is a program rather than a single yes/no assertion.  The source
itself recalls that subcritical extremals are already studied and that the
sharp subcritical constant is explicit for $p=2$ but unknown for general
$p$.  Below we obtain an exact critical case, then record a later answer to
the Morrey-attainment branch.

\section{New result: the sharp critical constant on a planar disk}

Write $j=j_{0,1}=2.404825557\ldots$ for the first positive zero of the Bessel
function $J_0$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:disk}
For every $u\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2)$, every $x_0\in\mathbb R^2$, and
$R>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp}
 \int_{B_R(x_0)}|u-u_{x_0,R}|^2\,dx
 \le \frac{R^2}{j_{0,1}^2}\int_{\mathbb R^2}|\nabla u|^2\,dx.
\end{equation}
The constant $j_{0,1}^{-2}=0.1729150690\ldots$ is sharp.  It is attained in
the homogeneous completion $\mathcal D^{1,2}(\mathbb R^2)$ modulo constants.

For $B_1(0)$, every nonconstant extremal is, for some $(A,B)\ne(0,0)$ and
$c\in\mathbb R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extremal}
U(r,\theta)=c+
\begin{cases}
J_1(jr)(A\cos\theta+B\sin\theta),&0\le r\le1,\\
J_1(j)r^{-1}(A\cos\theta+B\sin\theta),&r\ge1.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The extremals for other disks follow by translation and scaling.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{Proof intuition}

The source uses the whole-space Dirichlet energy, not merely the energy inside
the disk.  For a prescribed boundary Fourier mode, minimize the exterior
energy first.  The mode of angular frequency $m\ge1$ continues as $r^{-m}$
and contributes exactly a boundary penalty $m|f(1)|^2$.  The resulting first
interior mode has a Robin condition.  Its Bessel equation reduces that
condition to $J_0(j)=0$, which is lower than both the radial mean-zero mode and
all higher angular modes.

\begin{proof}
By translation and scaling it is enough to prove the assertion for
$B=B_1(0)$.  Since both sides ignore additive constants, replace $u$ by
$u-u_B$.  Thus $u_B=0$.

Expand in polar Fourier modes.  Apart from the harmless normalization factors
$2\pi$ for the radial mode and $\pi$ for the other modes, the denominator is
the sum of
\[
 \int_0^1 |f(r)|^2r\,dr,
\]
while the Dirichlet energy of an angular mode $m\ge1$ is
\[
 \int_0^\infty\left(|f'|^2+\frac{m^2}{r^2}|f|^2\right)r\,dr.
\]
For the exterior part, completing a square gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:exterior}
 \int_1^\infty\left(|f'|^2+\frac{m^2}{r^2}|f|^2\right)r\,dr
 &=m|f(1)|^2+
 \int_1^\infty\left|f'+\frac mr f\right|^2r\,dr\\
 &\ge m|f(1)|^2.\notag
\end{align}
Equality holds exactly for the finite-energy harmonic continuation
$f(r)=f(1)r^{-m}$.

It follows that the least Rayleigh quotient in mode $m\ge1$ is bounded below
by the first eigenvalue of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode}
 Q_m(f)=\int_0^1\left(|f'|^2+\frac{m^2}{r^2}|f|^2\right)r\,dr
 +m|f(1)|^2.
\end{equation}
Since $Q_m(f)\ge Q_1(f)$ for every $m\ge1$, it suffices to solve the first
mode.  Its Euler--Lagrange equation and natural boundary condition are
\[
 -(rf')'+\frac1r f=\lambda r f,
 \qquad f'(1)+f(1)=0,
\]
with the regular condition at $0$.  Thus $f(r)=J_1(\sqrt\lambda,r)$, and
\[
 \sqrt\lambda J_1'(\sqrt\lambda)+J_1(\sqrt\lambda)
 =\sqrt\lambda J_0(\sqrt\lambda)=0.
\]
The first eigenvalue is therefore $j_{0,1}^2$.

For the radial mode, the mean-zero condition reads
$\int_0^1 f(r)r\,dr=0$.  The exterior energy is nonnegative, and the radial
Neumann Poincar\'e inequality gives
\[
 \int_0^1|f'|^2r\,dr\ge j_{1,1}^2\int_0^1|f|^2r\,dr.
\]
The standard interlacing of Bessel zeros gives $j_{1,1}>j_{0,1}$.  Hence the
radial mode is strictly more expensive.  Higher modes are no cheaper by
$Q_m\ge Q_1$.  Summing all modes proves~\eqref{eq:sharp} for $R=1$.

Equality forces the first angular mode, the first Robin eigenfunction, and
equality in~\eqref{eq:exterior}.  These conditions give exactly
\eqref{eq:extremal}.  The identity
$jJ_1'(j)+J_1(j)=jJ_0(j)=0$ makes the radial derivative continuous at $r=1$,
so $U$ is a global weak extremal.  It has finite Dirichlet energy.  Truncating
its $r^{-1}$ tail and then mollifying approximates it by $C_c^\infty$
functions in Dirichlet energy and locally in $L^2$.  Therefore the constant is
sharp already for the source's test class, and attained in its homogeneous
completion.  Scaling gives the factor $R^2$ for a general disk.
\end{proof}

\section{Upgrade attempts and the remaining obstruction}

The compactness part actually extends beyond the Hilbertian disk: if
$s p=n$ with $s<n$, the critical homogeneous quotient is reflexive and its
restriction to a fixed ball, normalized to have mean zero, is compact in
$L^p$ by local Rellich compactness.  Hence the sharp critical
Poincar\'e--Wirtinger quotient is attained in the homogeneous completion.
This gives attainment, but not a usable closed form for the constant.

The exact upgrade above works because $(n,s,p)=(2,1,2)$ is simultaneously
local, linear, and rotationally separable.  For local $p=n\ne2$, the angular
decomposition does not diagonalize the nonlinear $p$-energy.  In the other
Hilbertian critical case $(n,s,p)=(1,1/2,2)$, restriction to an interval leads
to a nonlocal exterior Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator rather than the scalar
penalty in~\eqref{eq:exterior}.  These are structural obstructions to turning
the present calculation into a full all-parameter formula, not merely missing
algebra.

\section{Later literature: Morrey extremals do exist}

Brasco, Prinari, and Sk explicitly cite the source paper and study the sharp
fractional Morrey constant in arXiv:2309.06058v1~\cite{later}.  Their
Theorem~6.1 on arXiv PDF page~23 proves, for $N\ge1$, $0<s\le1$, $1<p<\infty$
and $sp>N$, that the sharp variational infimum over the homogeneous Morrey
space is attained.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/morrey_theorem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Thus the attainment half of the source's supercritical branch has a positive
literature answer.  The later paper explicitly says that the numerical value
of the sharp constant remains unknown, already in much of the local theory;
it supplies existence, regularity, and sharp asymptotics rather than a closed
formula.

\section{Novelty check and scope}

The four lightweight run indexes contained no treatment of either arXiv id.
A bounded exact-title, formula, Beppo--Levi, disk, Bessel-zero, and
Poincar\'e--Wirtinger search through 12 August 2026 found the source, the later
Morrey papers, standard bounded-domain Poincar\'e constants, and general
exterior-energy references, but no source recording the whole-space disk
constant $j_{0,1}^{-2}$ or the extremals~\eqref{eq:extremal}.  The proof is
short enough to audit directly, but bibliographic novelty remains subject to
human review.

This packet does not claim the full three-regime program.  It gives a complete
sharp answer for one critical parameter choice, a general critical attainment
argument without an explicit constant, and the exact later provenance for
Morrey attainment.  The general nonlinear/fractional constants remain open.
