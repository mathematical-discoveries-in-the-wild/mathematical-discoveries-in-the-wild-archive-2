\textbf{Status: literature already answered.}
Garcia--Wogen prove the stronger statement that every partial isometry of
rank at most two is complex symmetric, and also that every partial isometry
in dimension at most four is complex symmetric.
}}
\end{center}

\section*{Source conjecture}

Conjecture~1 on PDF page~9 of Tener's
\emph{Unitary equivalence to a complex symmetric matrix: an algorithm}
\cite{Tener} states:
\begin{quote}
Every rank-two $4\times4$ partial isometry is unitarily equivalent to a
complex symmetric matrix.
\end{quote}

\section*{Decisive literature answer}

Garcia and Wogen's separate paper \emph{Complex symmetric partial isometries}
\cite{GW} explicitly discusses Tener's numerical evidence for this exact
four-dimensional assertion.  On PDF page~2, their Theorem~2 characterizes
complex symmetric partial isometries via compression to the initial space,
and they immediately derive:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Corollary 1.} Every partial isometry of rank $\leq2$ is complex
symmetric.

\textbf{Corollary 2.} Every partial isometry on a Hilbert space of dimension
$\leq4$ is complex symmetric.
\end{quote}
Both corollaries contain Tener's rank-two $4\times4$ case.  In finite
dimension, an operator is complex symmetric exactly when some orthonormal
basis gives it a complex symmetric matrix, so this is the source's UECSM
property.

\section*{Provenance, chronology, and scope}

The supporting arXiv posting is dated 26 July 2009, shortly before Tener's
15 August 2009 arXiv posting, but Garcia--Wogen cite Tener's earlier manuscript
as reference~[13] and explicitly identify the same assertion.  Thus this is
an explicitly linked independent/separate-paper answer, not a same-paper
ask-and-answer passage.  The answer is affirmative and complete; no part of
Conjecture~1 remains unresolved.

and complex-symmetric partial-isometry searches found this decisive match.
