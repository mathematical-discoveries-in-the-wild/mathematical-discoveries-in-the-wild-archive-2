\section{Source question}

Let $b$ be in the closed unit ball of $H^\infty(\Cplus)$.  Its
de Branges--Rovnyak kernel, viewed through its boundary values, is
\[
 k_z^b(t)=\frac{1-\overline{b(z)}b(t)}{t-\overline z},
 \qquad z\in\Cplus,\quad t\in\R.
\]
Baranov, Fricain, and Mashreghi proved for inner $b$ that, for $q>1$,
there is a constant depending only on $q$ such that
\[
 \lVert k_{x+iy_1}^b\rVert_q
 \le C(q)\lVert k_{x+iy_2}^b\rVert_q
 \qquad(x\in\R,\ 0\le y_2\le y_1),                 \tag{6.9}
\]
and ask in Question~6.6 whether this remains true for a general $b$
\cite[Question~6.6, PDF pp.~27--28]{source}.

\section{Idea of the proof}

The kernel at the higher point is exactly the lower kernel multiplied by a
boundary contraction, plus one ordinary Cauchy kernel.  The coefficient of
that error is $b(z_1)-b(z_2)$.  The elementary geometry of the unit disk
bounds this coefficient by $|1-\overline{b(z_2)}b(z_1)|$.  But the latter
quantity is precisely the value of the lower kernel at $z_1$, times
$|z_1-\overline{z_2}|$.  A single $H^q$ point-evaluation estimate therefore
absorbs the entire error into the lower kernel norm.  No innerness or boundary
modulus-one property of $b$ is used.

\section{The theorem}

For $1<s<\infty$, put
\[
 A_s:=\left(\int_{\R}(1+u^2)^{-s/2}\,du\right)^{1/s}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[vertical kernel monotonicity]\label{thm:main}
Let $b\in H^\infty(\Cplus)$ satisfy $\lVert b\rVert_\infty\le1$, let
$1<q<\infty$, and let $q'=q/(q-1)$.  For every $x\in\R$ and
$0<y_2\le y_1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 \lVert k_{x+iy_1}^b\rVert_{L^q(\R)}
 \le
 \left(1+\frac{A_qA_{q'}}{\pi}\right)
 \lVert k_{x+iy_2}^b\rVert_{L^q(\R)}.
\end{equation}
The same conclusion holds for $y_2=0$ whenever the boundary kernel
$k_x^b$ exists as an $H^q(\Cplus)$ function.  If its extended $L^q$ norm is
infinite, the inequality is automatic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 z_j=x+iy_j,\qquad b_j=b(z_j),\qquad k_j=k_{z_j}^b
 \quad(j=1,2).
\]
For real $t$, direct subtraction of the two kernel numerators gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
 k_1(t)
 =\frac{t-\overline{z_2}}{t-\overline{z_1}}\,k_2(t)
  +\frac{(\overline{b_2}-\overline{b_1})b(t)}
         {t-\overline{z_1}}.
\end{equation}
Because $0\le y_2\le y_1$,
\[
 \left|\frac{t-\overline{z_2}}{t-\overline{z_1}}\right|^2
 =\frac{(t-x)^2+y_2^2}{(t-x)^2+y_1^2}\le1,
\]
and $|b(t)|\le1$ almost everywhere.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:firstbound}
 \lVert k_1\rVert_q
 \le \lVert k_2\rVert_q
 +|b_1-b_2|\left\lVert\frac1{t-\overline{z_1}}\right\rVert_q
 =\lVert k_2\rVert_q+A_q|b_1-b_2|y_1^{1/q-1}.
\end{equation}

We next absorb the second term.  The elementary identity
\[
 |1-\overline a w|^2-|w-a|^2
 =(1-|a|^2)(1-|w|^2)\ge0
 \]
for $a,w$ in the closed unit disk gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disk}
 |b_1-b_2|\le |1-\overline{b_2}b_1|.
\end{equation}

For completeness, the standard $H^q$ point-evaluation estimate follows
immediately from the Cauchy formula and H\"older's inequality: if
$f\in H^q(\Cplus)$ and $z=x+iy$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:point}
 |f(z)|
 \le \frac1{2\pi}\lVert f\rVert_q
       \left\lVert\frac1{t-z}\right\rVert_{q'}
 =\frac{A_{q'}}{2\pi}y^{-1/q}\lVert f\rVert_q.
\end{equation}
Apply this to $f=k_2$ at $z_1$.  Since
\[
 k_2(z_1)=\frac{1-\overline{b_2}b_1}{z_1-\overline{z_2}},
 \qquad |z_1-\overline{z_2}|=y_1+y_2\le2y_1,
\]
estimate \eqref{eq:point} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:absorb}
 |1-\overline{b_2}b_1|y_1^{1/q-1}
 \le \frac{A_{q'}}{\pi}\lVert k_2\rVert_q.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:firstbound}, \eqref{eq:disk}, and
\eqref{eq:absorb} proves \eqref{eq:main}.

When $y_2=0$ and $k_x^b\in H^q$, the same proof is literal: set
$z_2=x$ and let $b_2=b(x)$ in the boundary-kernel definition.  The multiplier
in \eqref{eq:identity} still has modulus at most one, and now
$|z_1-\overline{z_2}|=y_1$.  This proves the boundary case as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
Question 6.6 of the source has an affirmative answer.  In particular, the
constant is independent of $b$, $x$, $y_1$, and $y_2$, and the conclusion
holds throughout the source's range $q\ge2$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Stress tests and scope}

The proof was checked against the following edge cases.
For a unimodular constant $b$, all kernels vanish.  For a constant
$|b|<1$, both sides have the expected scaling $y^{1/q-1}$.  At $q=2$,
$A_2=\sqrt\pi$, so the displayed constant is $2$, consistent with the known
case though not asserted to be sharp.  The proof remains uniform as
$y_2\downarrow0$ whenever a finite $H^q$ boundary kernel is present.

No computational evidence is used.  Question 6.6 is solved in full, but this
packet makes no claim about the distinct boundary-integrability Question 3.3
in the source.  The source's Question 6.4 was answered later by
Aleman--Malman; that separate literature fact is not used in this proof.

\section{Novelty check and human review}

A bounded run-index and web/arXiv search on 17 August 2026 used arXiv id
0802.0789, the exact wording of Question 6.6, and combinations of
``de Branges--Rovnyak'', ``vertical rays'', ``reproducing kernel'',
``$L^q$ norm'', and ``monotonicity''.  The search found the source and
adjacent kernel literature but no later statement of the general-$b$
inequality or this two-kernel proof.  Novelty confidence is moderate rather
than conclusive.

Human review is recommended.  The decisive checks are the kernel identity
\eqref{eq:identity}, the normalization in the Cauchy estimate
\eqref{eq:point}, and the source's convention for a boundary kernel when
$y_2=0$.
