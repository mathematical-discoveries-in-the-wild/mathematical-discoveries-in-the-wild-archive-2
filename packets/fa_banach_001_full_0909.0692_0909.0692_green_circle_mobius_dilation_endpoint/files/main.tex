\begin{abstract}
Adimurthi and Tintarev ask for a sharp critical nonlinearity on
$H_0^1(\B)$ invariant under the product symmetry suggested by M\"obius
shifts and the radial dilations
$h_su(r)=s^{-1/2}u(r^s)$.  We give a positive answer in the exact sense
in which the source defines these actions.  A maximal normalized
$L^2$-mean over all Green-function level circles defines a norm
$\|\cdot\|_{\G}$ on $H_0^1(\B)$.  It is invariant under every disk
automorphism and satisfies the sharp inequality
\[
 \|u\|_{\G}\le \frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\|\nabla u\|_2.
\]
On radial functions it is exactly
$\sup_{0<r<1}|u(r)|/\sqrt{\log(1/r)}$, the source's sharp
dilation-invariant endpoint.  We also show that no nonzero pointwise
integral against hyperbolic area can possess both symmetries, explaining
why the common endpoint must be nonlocal.
\end{abstract}

\section{The source question}

Let $\B=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$.  The source defines the M\"obius
maps
\[
 \eta_a(z)=\frac{z-a}{1-\bar a z}
\]
on $H_0^1(\B)$ and, only on the radial subspace $H_{0,r}^1(\B)$,
the group
\begin{equation}\label{source-dilation}
 h_su(r)=s^{-1/2}u(r^s),\qquad s>0.
\end{equation}
It identifies the sharp radial endpoint
\begin{equation}\label{source-endpoint}
 \|u\|_{\infty,\log}
 =\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{|u(r)|}{\sqrt{\log(1/r)}}
\end{equation}
and asks:
\begin{quote}
\emph{It remains an open problem, however, to find a sharp critical
nonlinearity that is invariant with respect to the product group.}
\end{quote}
The exact source page, including \eqref{source-dilation} and
\eqref{source-endpoint}, is reproduced below.
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Because \eqref{source-dilation} is defined only for radial functions, the
literal common-extension problem is to find a M\"obius-invariant quantity
on all of $H_0^1(\B)$ whose radial restriction is
\eqref{source-endpoint}.  We solve precisely that problem.

\section{Definition and intuition}

Write
\[
 \phi_a(w)=\eta_a^{-1}(w)=\frac{a+w}{1+\bar a w}.
\]
The curve $\phi_a(|w|=e^{-t})$ is the level set
\[
 \log\frac1{|\eta_a(z)|}=t
\]
of the Green function of the disk with pole at $a$.  Uniform angular
measure in the $w$-coordinate is harmonic measure on this circle seen
from its center.

For $u\in C_c^\infty(\B)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{Gnorm}
 \boxed{\quad
 \|u\|_{\G}
 =
 \sup_{\substack{a\in\B\\ t>0}}
 \left(
 \frac1{2\pi t}\int_0^{2\pi}
 \left|u\!\left(\phi_a(e^{-t+i\theta})\right)\right|^2
 \,d\theta
 \right)^{1/2}.
 \quad}
\end{equation}
The factor $t^{-1/2}$ is forced by the one-dimensional radial
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and is exactly the scaling in
\eqref{source-endpoint}.  The supremum over centers is what restores
M\"obius invariance.

\section{The full endpoint theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{main}
Formula \eqref{Gnorm} extends to a norm on $H_0^1(\B)$ with the following
properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $u\in H_0^1(\B)$,
\begin{equation}\label{sharp-embedding}
 \|u\|_{\G}\le\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(\B)},
\end{equation}
and the constant $1/\sqrt{2\pi}$ is optimal.
\item For every $T\in\Aut(\B)$,
\begin{equation}\label{mobius-invariance}
 \|u\circ T\|_{\G}=\|u\|_{\G}.
\end{equation}
\item If $u$ is radial, then
\begin{equation}\label{radial-identity}
 \|u\|_{\G}
 =\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{|u(r)|}{\sqrt{\log(1/r)}}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\|h_su\|_{\G}=\|u\|_{\G}$ for every $s>0$.
\item More generally, on the subspace of functions radial about any
$a\in\B$, the norm is invariant under the conjugate dilation centered
at $a$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $a\in\B$ and set $v=u\circ\phi_a$.  For $r=e^{-t}$, the zero
boundary trace and Cauchy--Schwarz on almost every radial line give
\[
 |v(r,\theta)|^2
 =\left|\int_r^1\partial_\rho v(\rho,\theta)\,d\rho\right|^2
 \le
 \left(\int_r^1|\partial_\rho v|^2\rho\,d\rho\right)
 \left(\int_r^1\frac{d\rho}{\rho}\right).
\]
The last factor is $t$.  Average in $\theta$, divide by $2\pi t$,
and use conformal invariance of Dirichlet energy:
\[
 \frac1{2\pi t}\int_0^{2\pi}|v(e^{-t},\theta)|^2\,d\theta
 \le \frac1{2\pi}\int_{\B}|\nabla v|^2
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\B}|\nabla u|^2.
\]
Taking the supremum proves \eqref{sharp-embedding}.  Minkowski's
inequality on every circle gives the triangle inequality, and the bound
makes \eqref{Gnorm} continuous in the Dirichlet norm, hence it extends
from $C_c^\infty(\B)$ to $H_0^1(\B)$.  The trace formula holds for the
extension with an essential supremum.  If the extended value is zero,
the terms with $a=0$ show that $u$ vanishes on almost every centered
circle, and hence $u=0$ almost everywhere.  Thus it is a norm.

For M\"obius invariance, take $T\in\Aut(\B)$.  Both
$T\circ\phi_a$ and $\phi_{T(a)}$ map $0$ to $T(a)$.  Therefore, for
some rotation $R_\gamma(w)=e^{i\gamma}w$,
\begin{equation}\label{factorization}
 T\circ\phi_a=\phi_{T(a)}\circ R_\gamma.
\end{equation}
Rotation preserves angular measure.  Apply \eqref{factorization} inside
the circle mean and use that $a\mapsto T(a)$ is a bijection of $\B$.
This proves \eqref{mobius-invariance}.

Now let $u$ be radial and put
\[
 M=\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{|u(r)|}{\sqrt{\log(1/r)}}.
\]
The center $a=0$ in \eqref{Gnorm} gives $\|u\|_{\G}\ge M$.  Conversely,
$|u(z)|^2\le M^2\log(1/|z|)$.  Jensen's formula yields, for
$0<r<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{jensen}
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
 -\log|\phi_a(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
 =
 -\log\max\{|a|,r\}
 \le -\log r.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the mean of $\log|a+re^{i\theta}|$ is
$\log\max\{|a|,r\}$, while the mean of
$\log|1+\bar a r e^{i\theta}|$ is zero.  Substituting the pointwise
bound into every circle average and using \eqref{jensen} proves the
reverse inequality $\|u\|_{\G}\le M$, hence \eqref{radial-identity}.

If $q=r^s$, direct substitution in \eqref{source-endpoint} gives
\[
 \sup_r\frac{s^{-1/2}|u(r^s)|}{\sqrt{\log(1/r)}}
 =
 \sup_q\frac{|u(q)|}{\sqrt{\log(1/q)}}.
\]
This proves radial dilation invariance.  Finally, a function radial
about $a$ becomes radial about $0$ after composition with $\phi_a$.
Conjugate the preceding identity and use \eqref{mobius-invariance}.

It remains to prove sharpness.  For $T>0$ define the Moser hat
\begin{equation}\label{moser-hat}
 m_T(r)=\frac{\min\{\log(1/r),T\}}{\sqrt{2\pi T}}.
\end{equation}
In the variable $t=\log(1/r)$,
\[
 \|\nabla m_T\|_2^2
 =2\pi\int_0^\infty |m_T'(t)|^2\,dt=1.
\]
Formula \eqref{radial-identity} gives
$\|m_T\|_{\G}=1/\sqrt{2\pi}$, proving that the constant in
\eqref{sharp-embedding} is exact.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Endpoint noncompactness]\label{noncompact}
The hats satisfy $h_sm_T=m_{T/s}$.  As $T\to\infty$ they converge weakly
to zero in $H_0^1(\B)$, while both their Dirichlet norm and
$\G$-norm remain fixed.  Thus the embedding \eqref{sharp-embedding} is
noncompact along exactly the source dilation symmetry.  M\"obius shifts
move the same concentration to an arbitrary center.
\end{remark}

\section{Why a pointwise integral cannot work}

The source's M\"obius-invariant Trudinger--Moser functional is a local
integral against hyperbolic area
\[
 d\mu(z)=\frac{dx\,dy}{(1-|z|^2)^2}.
\]
The next proposition shows that no nonzero functional in this natural
local class can also be dilation invariant.

\begin{proposition}\label{local-no-go}
Let $F:\R\to\R$ be continuous with $F(0)=0$, and set
\[
 J_F(u)=\int_{\B}F(u)\,d\mu.
\]
If, for some fixed $s\ne1$,
\[
 J_F(h_su)=J_F(u)
\]
for every radial $u\in C_c^\infty(\B)$, then $F\equiv0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In polar coordinates,
\[
 J_F(u)=2\pi\int_0^1F(u(r))\frac{r}{(1-r^2)^2}\,dr.
\]
In $J_F(h_su)$ make the change of variable $q=r^s$.  This gives
\[
 J_F(h_su)=2\pi\int_0^1F(s^{-1/2}u(q))
 \frac1s\frac{q^{2/s-1}}{(1-q^{2/s})^2}\,dq.
\]
Use smooth radial plateaus converging to an arbitrary constant $c$ on
an arbitrary compact subinterval of $(0,1)$, then apply Lebesgue
differentiation.  Invariance forces
\begin{equation}\label{weight-equality}
 F(s^{-1/2}c)\frac1s\frac{q^{2/s-1}}{(1-q^{2/s})^2}
 =
 F(c)\frac{q}{(1-q^2)^2}
\end{equation}
for almost every $q\in(0,1)$.  If $F(c)\ne0$, then
\eqref{weight-equality} says that
\[
 s q^{2-2/s}\frac{(1-q^{2/s})^2}{(1-q^2)^2}
\]
is constant in $q$.  It is not constant when $s\ne1$ (compare its
limits at $q=0$ and $q=1$).  Hence $F(c)=0$.  Since $c$ was arbitrary,
$F\equiv0$.
\end{proof}

The nonlocal Green-circle average in \eqref{Gnorm} is therefore not an
ornament: within the natural pointwise hyperbolic class, nonlocality is
forced.

\section{Verification, novelty, and limitations}

\paragraph{Independent checks.}
The script \texttt{verify\_green\_circle\_norm.py} performs deterministic
quadrature and complex-algebra checks of:
\begin{itemize}
\item the Jensen identity \eqref{jensen} on off-center circles;
\item the factorization \eqref{factorization} pointwise;
\item the radial endpoint bound on sampled Moser-type profiles;
\item the dilation scaling and the exact constant $1/\sqrt{2\pi}$;
\item the nonconstant radial-weight ratio in Proposition~\ref{local-no-go}.
\end{itemize}
The proof itself is exact and does not depend on the numerical checks.

\paragraph{Literature audit.}
Exact-phrase searches, the run's cheap indexes, searches for
M\"obius-invariant circular-mean Sobolev norms, and inspection of the
related later papers \cite{ATcompact,TintarevRemainder,YangZhu} did not
locate \eqref{Gnorm} or a prior resolution of this question.  This is
evidence, not a definitive novelty certification.

\paragraph{Interpretation and limitation.}
The result is a full positive answer under the source's own terminology:
the source calls the radial norm \eqref{source-endpoint} an
$L^\infty$-nonlinearity, and \eqref{Gnorm} extends it exactly.  The
source dilation is not defined on arbitrary nonradial functions, so no
claim of such an action is made.  Instead, the result supplies full
M\"obius invariance and dilation invariance on every centered radial
subspace, which is the strongest literal common action available.
The result does not assert that $\|\cdot\|_{\G}$ is a pointwise integral;
Proposition~\ref{local-no-go} rules out that more restrictive form.

\paragraph{Human review recommendation.}
Check the trace extension, \eqref{jensen}, and the intended meaning of
product group.  Search the conformally invariant Dirichlet-space and
maximal-function literature for the Green-circle norm.  If both the
proof and novelty survive, the source authors should be contacted.
