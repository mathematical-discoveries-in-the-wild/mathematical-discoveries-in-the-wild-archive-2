\section{The source question}

Let \(A=A(\overline{\D}^{,n})\) be the polydisc algebra: the continuous
functions on \(\overline{\D}^{,n}\) which are holomorphic on
\(\D^n\).  Its real-symmetric real form is
\[
 A_r=\{f\in A:f(z)=\overline{f(\bar z)}\}.
\]
A commutative unital ring is \emph{projective free} when every finitely
generated projective module is free; equivalently, every idempotent matrix
over the ring is similar over that ring to
\(\operatorname{diag}(I_\ell,0)\).

Sasane \cite{Sasane} proves in Theorem~1.3 that, for every positive integer
\(N\), the smoother real-symmetric algebra \(\partial^{-N}A_r\) is
projective free.  At the end of the paper he asks whether \(A_r\) itself is
projective free.

\section{Proof intuition}

The missing boundary derivatives can be created without perturbing the
idempotent equation.  If \(P(z)^2=P(z)\), then
\(P_r(z):=P(rz)\) is still idempotent.  For \(r<1\), it is holomorphic on a
larger polydisc and therefore belongs to every smoother algebra in the
source theorem.  Meanwhile \(P_r\to P\) uniformly.

Idempotents that are sufficiently close in a Banach algebra are similar.
Here no abstract stability theorem is needed: the matrix
\(W=QP+(I-Q)(I-P)\) satisfies \(WP=QW\), and it approaches the identity as
\(Q\to P\).  Taking \(Q=P_r\) transports the source theorem's
diagonalization of \(P_r\) to \(P\).

\section{The nearby-idempotent mechanism}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:nearby}
Let \(P,Q\) be idempotents in a unital Banach algebra.  If
\[
 \|Q-P\|\,\|2P-I\|<1,                                   \tag{1}
\]
then \(P\) and \(Q\) are similar.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set
\[
 W=QP+(I-Q)(I-P).
\]
Using only \(P^2=P\) and \(Q^2=Q\), one obtains
\[
 WP=QP=QW.                                               \tag{2}
\]
Also
\[
 W-I=2QP-Q-P=(Q-P)(2P-I).                               \tag{3}
\]
Condition (1) and the Neumann lemma make \(W\) invertible.  Equation (2)
then gives \(Q=WPW^{-1}\).
\end{proof}

\section{Full solution}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:main}
For every positive integer \(n\), the real-symmetric polydisc algebra
\(A_r(\overline{\D}^{,n})\) is projective free.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(P\in M_k(A_r)\) be idempotent.  For \(0<r<1\), define
\[
 P_r(z)=P(rz),\qquad z\in\overline{\D}^{,n}.             \tag{4}
\]
The algebraic identity is preserved pointwise, so \(P_r^2=P_r\).  Since
\(r\) is real and \(P\) is real symmetric,
\[
 P_r(z)=P(rz)=\overline{P(r\bar z)}
       =\overline{P_r(\bar z)}.                          \tag{5}
\]
Moreover, the same formula extends \(P_r\) holomorphically to the open
polydisc of radius \(1/r>1\).  All of its complex partial derivatives are
therefore continuous on \(\overline{\D}^{,n}\).  In particular,
\[
 P_r\in M_k(\partial^{-1}A_r).                           \tag{6}
\]

Because \(P\) is uniformly continuous on the compact closed polydisc,
\[
 \|P_r-P\|_\infty\longrightarrow0\qquad(r\uparrow1).     \tag{7}
\]
Choose \(r<1\) close enough that
\(\|P_r-P\|\,\|2P-I\|<1\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:nearby}, applied in the
real Banach algebra \(M_k(A_r)\), gives an invertible \(W\in M_k(A_r)\)
such that
\[
 P_r=WPW^{-1}.                                           \tag{8}
\]

By Sasane's Theorem~1.3 for \(N=1\), there are
\(S_r\in GL_k(\partial^{-1}A_r)\) and an integer \(\ell\) such that
\[
 S_r^{-1}P_rS_r=\begin{bmatrix}I_\ell&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}. \tag{9}
\]
Regard \(S_r\) as an invertible matrix over \(A_r\) and set
\(S=W^{-1}S_r\).  Equations (8)--(9) yield
\[
 S^{-1}PS
 =S_r^{-1}WPW^{-1}S_r
 =S_r^{-1}P_rS_r
 =\begin{bmatrix}I_\ell&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}.           \tag{10}
\]
Thus every idempotent matrix over \(A_r\) has the required constant
diagonal form, proving projective freeness.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The proof answers the source question affirmatively for all dimensions.  It
uses the source's proved Theorem~1.3, not an unproved extension of it.  The
only transfer ingredients are radial dilation, uniform continuity, and the
identities (2)--(3), all valid in the real Banach algebra itself.

The main review points are: (i) formula (4) extends holomorphically to a
strictly larger polydisc, which establishes (6); (ii) convergence (7) is in
the matrix norm used for the Neumann lemma; and (iii) the order of factors in
(8)--(10) is correct.  No numerical or computer-assisted claim is used.

for the arXiv identifier, exact source question, title, author, `real
symmetric polydisc algebra', and `projective free' found no later explicit
resolution.  Novelty confidence is moderate-high.
