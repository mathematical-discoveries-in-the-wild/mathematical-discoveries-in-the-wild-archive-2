\section{Source problem}

The target is Section 6 (PDF pp.~17--18) of Gerdjikov--Nikolov,
\emph{Some inequalities for norms in $\R^n$ and $\C^n$},
arXiv:2303.03210 \cite{source}.  Their greedy minimal and maximal bases give
the sharp-for-that-strategy constant $2^n-1$.  They ask how this exponential
constant can be improved, define the optimal orthonormal-basis constant
$c_n^\perp$, the unrestricted unit-basis constant $c_n^\angle$, and the
interpolating constants $c_n^\rho$ under
$|\langle b_i,b_j\rangle|\le\rho$.

We answer the growth question by replacing $2^n-1$ with $n$.  The basis is
explicit once a classical finite-dimensional convex optimization---the John
ellipsoid---has been performed.

\section{Definitions}

Let $V=\F^n$, where $\F=\R$ or $\C$, with its fixed Euclidean or Hermitian
inner product.  For a norm $f$ and $0\le\rho\le1$, let $C_\rho(f)$ be the
infimum of all $C$ for which there is a Euclidean-unit basis
$(b_1,\ldots,b_n)$ satisfying
\[
 |\langle b_i,b_j\rangle|\le\rho\quad(i\ne j)
\]
and
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n |a_i|f(b_i)
 \le C f\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i b_i\right)
 \quad(a_1,\ldots,a_n\in\F).
\]
Then the source's constant is
$c_n^\rho(\F^n)=\sup_f C_\rho(f)$,
$c_n^0=c_n^\perp$, and $c_n^1=c_n^\angle$.

The unit ball of $f$ is denoted by $K$.  An ellipsoid $E$ has a quadratic
gauge
\[
 q(x)=\inf\{t>0:x\in tE\}=\|Tx\|_2
\]
for a positive definite self-adjoint operator $T$.

\subsection*{Proof intuition}

The exponential loss in the source comes from recursively projecting away
one direction and paying a factor close to two at each step.  Instead, put
the entire norm ball into its best ellipsoidal position at once.  John's
theorem supplies
\(E\subset K\subset\sqrt n E\).  Diagonalizing $E$ uses an orthonormal
basis in the original inner product.  Along that basis the numerator is a
weighted $\ell_1$ norm and the ellipsoid is the corresponding weighted
$\ell_2$ norm.  Cauchy--Schwarz costs $\sqrt n$, and the John sandwich costs
another $\sqrt n$, producing $n$ rather than $2^n-1$.  The Euclidean norm
forces at least $\sqrt n$ by averaging over signs.  When the original norm is
already ellipsoidal, the sandwich has no distortion and the exact constant is
$\sqrt n$.

\section{The John sandwich over both scalar fields}

\begin{lemma}[Real and complex symmetric John sandwich]\label{lem:john}
Let $K$ be the unit ball of a norm on $\F^n$.  There is an ellipsoid $E$,
defined by a positive definite self-adjoint $\F$-linear operator, such that
\[
 E\subset K\subset\sqrt n\,E.
\]
For $\F=\C$, the ellipsoid can be taken Hermitian relative to the fixed
complex structure.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\F=\R$, this is the centrally symmetric form of John's theorem
\cite{john}.  We recall the contact-point argument because it also resolves
the scalar-field issue.  After a linear change of coordinates puts the John
ellipsoid in the form $E=B_2^n$, there are contact points
$u_j\in\partial K\cap S^{n-1}$ and weights $d_j>0$ with
\[
 I=\sum_j d_j u_j\otimes u_j,
 \qquad \sum_jd_j=\operatorname{tr}I=n.
\]
At a contact point, the supporting functional of $K$ is the Euclidean
functional represented by $u_j$; central symmetry gives
$|\langle x,u_j\rangle|\le1$ for $x\in K$.  Hence
\[
 \|x\|_2^2=\sum_jd_j|\langle x,u_j\rangle|^2
 \le\sum_jd_j=n,
\]
which is $K\subset\sqrt n B_2^n$.

Now let $\F=\C$ and regard $K$ as a centrally symmetric body in
$\R^{2n}$.  Its real John ellipsoid is unique.  Since $K$ is invariant under
every scalar phase $R_t:x\mapsto e^{it}x$, uniqueness gives $R_tE=E$.
Thus the positive operator defining $E$ commutes with the complex structure,
so $E$ is Hermitian.  Normalize again to $E=B_2^n$ and use the real John
decomposition
\[
 I_{\R^{2n}}=\sum_jd_j u_j\otimes_{\R}u_j,
 \qquad \sum_jd_j=2n.
\]
Every $R_tu_j$ is again a contact point.  The supporting-functional argument
and phase invariance of $K$ imply
\[
 |\langle x,u_j\rangle_{\C}|\le1\qquad(x\in K).
\]
Average the real quadratic decomposition over $t\in[0,2\pi]$.  For every
$x\in\C^n$,
\[
 \|x\|_2^2
 =\frac12\sum_jd_j|\langle x,u_j\rangle_{\C}|^2
 \le\frac12\sum_jd_j=n.
\]
Thus $K\subset\sqrt nB_2^n$ also in the complex case.  Undoing the
complex-linear normalization proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Polynomial upper bounds and matching lower bound}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform polynomial bound]\label{thm:main}
For $\F\in\{\R,\C\}$, $n\ge1$, and every $0\le\rho\le1$,
\[
 \boxed{\qquad \sqrt n\ \le\ c_n^\rho(\F^n)\ \le\ n.\qquad}
\]
More explicitly, for every norm $f$ there is an orthonormal basis
$(b_1,\ldots,b_n)$ such that
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n|a_i|f(b_i)
 \le n f\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i b_i\right)
 \quad(a_i\in\F).
\]
One may take an eigenbasis of the positive operator defining the John
ellipsoid of the $f$-unit ball.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take $E$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:john} and write its gauge as
$q(x)=\|Tx\|_2$.  The inclusions are equivalent to
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n}q(x)\le f(x)\le q(x)\qquad(x\in V).
\]
Choose an orthonormal eigenbasis $b_i$ of $T$, say $Tb_i=\lambda_i b_i$
with $\lambda_i>0$.  Then $f(b_i)\le\lambda_i$.  If
$x=\sum_i a_i b_i$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum_i|a_i|f(b_i)
 &\le\sum_i|a_i|\lambda_i\\
 &\le\sqrt n\left(\sum_i|a_i|^2\lambda_i^2\right)^{1/2}
 =\sqrt n\,q(x)
 \le n f(x).
\end{align*}
The basis is orthonormal, hence it is admissible for every $\rho\ge0$.

For the lower bound, use the Euclidean norm $f=\|\cdot\|_2$.  Let
$(b_i)$ be any admissible unit-vector basis.  For independent Rademacher
signs $\varepsilon_i$,
\[
 \mathbb E\left\|\sum_i\varepsilon_i b_i\right\|_2^2
 =\sum_i\|b_i\|_2^2=n.
\]
Therefore some sign choice has norm at most $\sqrt n$, whereas
$\sum_i|\varepsilon_i|f(b_i)=n$.  The constant for that basis is at least
$\sqrt n$.  This holds for every admissible basis and every $\rho$.
\end{proof}

In particular,
\[
 \sqrt n\le c_n^\perp(\F^n)\le n,
 \qquad
 \sqrt n\le c_n^\angle(\F^n)\le n.
\]
For the orthonormal problem this replaces the source's exponential
$2^n-1$ by $n$.  The cited planar identity
$c_2^\perp(\R^2)=2$ shows that the new upper bound is exact when $n=2$.

\section{Exact solution for ellipsoidal norms}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ellipsoid}
If $f(x)=\|Ax\|_2$ for a positive definite self-adjoint operator $A$, then
\[
 C_\rho(f)=\sqrt n\qquad(0\le\rho\le1)
\]
over both scalar fields.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $(b_i)$ be a Euclidean orthonormal eigenbasis,
$Ab_i=\lambda_i b_i$.  It is admissible for every $\rho$, and
\[
 \sup_{a\ne0}
 \frac{\sum_i|a_i|f(b_i)}{f(\sum_i a_i b_i)}
 =\sup_{a\ne0}
 \frac{\sum_i|a_i|\lambda_i}
 {(\sum_i|a_i|^2\lambda_i^2)^{1/2}}
 =\sqrt n.
\]
Equality is attained by $|a_i|=1/\lambda_i$.

Conversely, take any unit-vector basis $(v_i)$ and put
$x_i=v_i/f(v_i)$, so $f(x_i)=1$.  Independent signs satisfy
\[
 \mathbb E f\!\left(\sum_i\varepsilon_i x_i\right)^2
 =\mathbb E\left\|\sum_i\varepsilon_i Ax_i\right\|_2^2
 =\sum_i\|Ax_i\|_2^2=n.
\]
This rescaling leaves the coefficient ratio unchanged: choose
$a_i=\varepsilon_i/f(v_i)$ in the original basis.
For some sign choice the denominator is at most $\sqrt n$, while the
weighted coefficient numerator is $n$.  Thus no basis has constant below
$\sqrt n$.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, novelty, and limitations}

The script \texttt{code/check\_bounds.py} checks the exact ellipsoidal
witness, exhausts all signs for random real and complex bases through
dimension eight, and reports $28{,}032$ deterministic or seeded-random sign,
ellipsoid, and coefficient checks, including synthetic norms satisfying the
John sandwich.  Run from the
packet directory with
\begin{center}
\texttt{conda run --no-capture-output -n sandbox python code/check\_bounds.py}.
\end{center}
These computations are sanity checks only; the proof is the preceding
ellipsoid argument.

\paragraph{Novelty search.}
On 17 August 2026, bounded searches covered the run's registry, solution,
attempt, and proof-gap indexes; the exact title and arXiv id; the symbols and
phrases for $c_n^\perp$, $c_n^\angle$, and $c_n^\rho$; and arXiv searches
combining John ellipsoids, orthonormal bases, weighted coefficient
inequalities, and crosspolytope containment.  No later paper explicitly
answering the source problem was located.  The result is an elementary
corollary of classical John theory, so novelty confidence is moderate rather
than high.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The theorem fully answers the source's request to improve the exponential
constant by giving a uniform linear bound and a concrete basis-selection
principle.  It does not determine the exact values of $c_n^\rho$ in general
for $n>2$, nor does it prove that the upper bound $n$ is asymptotically sharp.
John ellipsoid computation is a standard convex optimization, but the packet
does not analyze its numerical complexity or give a closed formula for an
arbitrary input norm.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Verify the complex part of Lemma~\ref{lem:john}, especially phase averaging
of the real contact decomposition, and the gauge direction in
$E\subset K\subset\sqrt nE$.  Then assess whether the durable result should
be described as a full answer to the exponential-growth question or as a
substantial partial answer to the broader exact-constant problem.
