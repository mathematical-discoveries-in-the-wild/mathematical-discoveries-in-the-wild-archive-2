\section{Source questions}

Garc\'ia-Cuerva and Parcet define $\Gamma$-type and
$\Gamma$-cotype for the quantized orthonormal system formed by the irreducible
unitary representations of a compact group $G$~\cite{GP}.  At the end of the
paper they ask:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Does there exist a noncommutative compact group $G$ such that either
 $\Gamma$-type $2$ or $\Gamma$-cotype $2$ forces an operator space to be
 completely isomorphic to an $\OH$ space?
 \item If the irreducible dimensions $\mathbf d_\Gamma$ are unbounded, must
 Banach $\Gamma$-type $2$ coincide with $\Gamma$-type $2$, and similarly for
 cotype?
\end{enumerate}
The paper recalls immediately beforehand that the one-sided autoduality in
the first question holds for the torus $\mathbb T$ and the Cantor group.

\section{Proof Intuition}

One-sided autoduality passes upward through compact-group quotients.  Indeed,
if $G\twoheadrightarrow Q$, every irreducible representation of $Q$ pulls
back to an irreducible representation of $G$, and the corresponding Fourier
subsystem on $G$ is exactly the Fourier system on $Q$.  Thus type or cotype
for the full dual of $G$ implies the same property for the dual of $Q$.

This makes $G=\mathbb T\times SU(2)$ decisive.  Its torus quotient supplies
autoduality, while its $SU(2)$ factor makes the group noncommutative and gives
irreducible representations of arbitrarily large dimension.  The same
example then separates Banach from complete type and cotype using the
operator spaces $\Rp_p$ already analyzed in the source.

\section{Quotient inheritance}

\begin{lemma}[Autoduality lifts through quotients]\label{lem:quotient}
Let $\pi:G\to Q$ be a continuous surjective homomorphism of compact groups.
Let $E$ be an operator space.  If $E$ has $\widehat G$-type $2$, then it has
$\widehat Q$-type $2$; if it has $\widehat G$-cotype $2$, then it has
$\widehat Q$-cotype $2$.  The corresponding constants do not increase.
Consequently, if $Q$ has one-sided Kwapie\'n autoduality, then so does $G$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The map
\[
 \widehat Q\longrightarrow\widehat G,\qquad
 [\sigma]\longmapsto[\sigma\circ\pi]
\]
is injective, preserves irreducibility, and preserves representation
dimensions.  Its image is the family of irreducible representations of $G$
that are trivial on $\ker\pi$.

Normalized Haar measure on $G$ pushes forward to normalized Haar measure on
$Q$.  Hence pullback $f\mapsto f\circ\pi$ identifies the $E$-valued
$L^2$ spaces on $Q$ completely isometrically with the subspace of functions
on $G$ that are constant on cosets of $\ker\pi$.  Under this identification,
the Fourier and inverse Fourier transforms for $Q$ are precisely the
restrictions of the transforms for $G$ to the subsystem
$\{\sigma\circ\pi:\sigma\in\widehat Q\}$.

The definitions of $\Gamma$-type and $\Gamma$-cotype take suprema over all
finite subsets of the dual system.  Restricting those suprema to this
subsystem proves both asserted inequalities.  If either property for
$\widehat Q$ forces $E\simeq_{cb}\OH$, the same is therefore true for
$\widehat G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Affirmative answer to the first question]\label{thm:example}
The compact group
\[
 G=\mathbb T\times SU(2)
\]
is noncommutative, has unbounded irreducible dimensions, and satisfies:
if an operator space $E$ has $\widehat G$-type $2$ or
$\widehat G$-cotype $2$, then $E$ is completely isomorphic to an $\OH$
operator space.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Projection onto the first factor is a compact-group quotient
$G\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb T$.  The source records the one-sided
autoduality of the torus in exactly the operator-space sense used here.
Lemma~\ref{lem:quotient} therefore proves the conclusion.

The group is noncommutative because $SU(2)$ is.  Its irreducible
representations include $1\otimes\rho_m$, where $\rho_m$ is the
$(m+1)$-dimensional irreducible representation of $SU(2)$.  Thus
$\mathbf d_{\widehat G}$ is unbounded.
\end{proof}

The same proof gives a family rather than an isolated example: every
noncommutative compact group admitting the torus or the Cantor group as a
quotient has the requested autoduality.

\section{Banach type and complete type are not equivalent}

\begin{theorem}[Negative answer to the second question]\label{thm:separation}
Unboundedness of $\mathbf d_\Gamma$ does not imply equivalence between
Banach $\Gamma$-type $2$ and $\Gamma$-type $2$, nor between the corresponding
cotype notions.  This already fails for
$\Gamma=\widehat{\mathbb T\times SU(2)}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Rp_p$ be Pisier's operator-space structure on the closed Rademacher
span used in the source.  Point $(\mathrm a_1)$ of the final example in
\cite{GP} states that for every uniformly bounded quantized orthonormal
system with unbounded dimensions, $\Rp_p$ has Banach type $2$ whenever
$2\leq p<\infty$.  Choose $p>2$ and use the complete dual system of
$G=\mathbb T\times SU(2)$.  If $\Rp_p$ also had $\widehat G$-type $2$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:example} would force $\Rp_p\simeq_{cb}\OH$.  The source
explicitly recalls that this is impossible unless $p=2$.  Thus Banach
$\widehat G$-type $2$ does not imply complete $\widehat G$-type $2$.

Similarly, point $(\mathrm a_2)$ states that $\Rp_p$ has Banach cotype $2$
for $1\leq p\leq2$.  Choosing $p<2$, complete $\widehat G$-cotype $2$ would
again imply $\Rp_p\simeq_{cb}\OH$, a contradiction.  This separates the two
cotype notions as well.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and novelty}

The construction answers both questions exactly as written and even keeps
the surrounding unbounded-dimension hypothesis.  It also exposes a natural
stronger surviving problem: require the compact noncommutative group to have
no torus or Cantor quotient (for example, finite abelianization), or ask for
autoduality generated intrinsically by its higher-dimensional irreducibles.
Nothing here settles that strengthened version, such as the case of $SU(2)$
alone.

A bounded search through 11 August 2026 covered the run's result and attempt
indexes and searches for the exact title, the two final questions,
``autoduality,'' quantized type/cotype, and later papers on sharp type and
cotype.  It found related developments but no source stating this quotient
construction or resolving the exact questions.  Novelty confidence is
moderate pending specialist review.  The proof itself is a direct consequence
of the definitions and the torus result already recorded in the source.
