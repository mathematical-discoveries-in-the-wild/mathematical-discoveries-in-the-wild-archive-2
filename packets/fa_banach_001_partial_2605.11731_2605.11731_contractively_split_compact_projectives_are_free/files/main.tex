\section{Source question}

Clausen and Scholze prove that every compact projective condensed abelian
group is a retract of $\Z[S]$ for some extremally disconnected profinite set
$S$, and then ask the following question \cite[Question 3.6, p.~26]{CS}:

\begin{quote}
Are all compact projective condensed abelian groups isomorphic to $\Z[S]$
for some $S\in\Extr$?
\end{quote}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The source emphasizes that the unrestricted question is closely related to
an old question on the structure of injective Banach spaces
\cite[Section 1.6.1]{ASC}.

\section{Definitions and statement}

Write a profinite set as $S=\varprojlim_i S_i$, with each $S_i$ finite.  The
explicit description used in the source is
\[
 \Z[S]=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\Ball_n(S),\qquad
 \Ball_n(S):=\varprojlim_i
 \{a\in\Z[S_i]:\lVert a\rVert_1\leq n\}.
\]
An endomorphism $e:\Z[S]\to\Z[S]$ is determined, under the
free/forgetful adjunction, by its \emph{generator map}
\[
 g_e:S\longrightarrow \Z[S].
\]
We call $e$ \emph{generator-contractive} if $g_e$ factors through
$\Ball_1(S)$.  This is the integral radius-one condition in the canonical
$\ell^1$ filtration; it does not mean that every presentation of the same
retract has this property.

\begin{theorem}[Radius-one classification]\label{thm:main}
Let $S\in\Extr$ and let $e:\Z[S]\to\Z[S]$ be a
generator-contractive idempotent.  There is an extremally disconnected
profinite retract $T\subseteq S$ such that
\[
 \im(e)\cong \Z[T].
\]
More precisely, there is a clopen partition
$S=S_0\sqcup S_+\sqcup S_-$ and a continuous map
$r:S_+\sqcup S_-\to T$, with $T\subseteq S_+$ and $r|_T=\mathrm{id}_T$,
for which
\[
 g_e(s)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&s\in S_0,\\
 [r(s)],&s\in S_+,\\
 -[r(s)],&s\in S_-.
 \end{cases}
\]
Conversely, every signed partial retraction of this form induces a
generator-contractive idempotent with image $\Z[T]$.
\end{theorem}

Consequently, Question 3.6 has an affirmative answer for every compact
projective which admits a presentation as the image of a
generator-contractive idempotent on some $\Z[S]$.

\section{Proof intuition}

At integral $\ell^1$-radius one there are only three kinds of elements: zero,
a positive basis vector, and a negative basis vector.  Hence the generator
map of $e$ is a continuous signed partial map from $S$ back to $S$.
Idempotence is unexpectedly rigid: every point hit by this partial map must
be hit positively and fixed.  Those fixed points form a retract $T$ of $S$.
The signed partial map then linearizes to a retraction $\Z[S]\to\Z[T]$, and
the inclusion $T\hookrightarrow S$ linearizes to its section.

This mechanism is special to radius one.  Starting at radius two, a generator
may map to a sum or difference of basis vectors, so there is no point-valued
map whose fixed set can be extracted.

\section{Proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ball}
For every profinite set $S$, there is a natural isomorphism of profinite sets
\[
 \Ball_1(S)\cong \{0\}\sqcup S^+\sqcup S^-,
\]
where the two copies of $S$ send $s$ to $[s]$ and $-[s]$ respectively.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a finite set $S_i$, an integral vector of $\ell^1$-norm at most one is
exactly $0$, $[s_i]$, or $-[s_i]$.  Under every transition map between finite
quotients, zero remains zero and the sign and total coefficient of a nonzero
vector remain fixed.  A compatible family of positive (respectively
negative) basis vectors is exactly a point of
$S=\varprojlim_i S_i$.  Taking the inverse limit gives the claimed natural
decomposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:ball}, the continuous map $g_e:S\to\Ball_1(S)$ determines
a clopen partition
\[
 S=S_0\sqcup S_+\sqcup S_-
\]
and a continuous map $r:U:=S_+\sqcup S_-\to S$ such that
$g_e(s)=0,[r(s)],-[r(s)]$ on the respective pieces.

Evaluate $e^2=e$ on the universal generator $[s]$.  If $s\in S_+$ and
$t=r(s)$, then
\[
 g_e(t)=e([t])=e(g_e(s))=g_e(s)=[t].
\]
If $s\in S_-$ and $t=r(s)$, then
\[
 -g_e(t)=e(-[t])=e(g_e(s))=g_e(s)=-[t],
\]
so again $g_e(t)=[t]$.  Thus every point in $r(U)$ is positively fixed.
Put
\[
 T:=\{t\in S_+:r(t)=t\}.
\]
The preceding calculation gives $r(U)\subseteq T$, while the reverse
inclusion follows from $r(t)=t$ for $t\in T$.  Hence $r(U)=T$ and
$r|_T=\mathrm{id}_T$.  The set $T$ is closed, being an equalizer inside the
clopen profinite set $S_+$.

If $T$ is empty, then $U$ is empty and $e=0$, giving $\im(e)=\Z[\varnothing]$.
Otherwise choose $t_0\in T$.  Extend $r$ across the clopen piece $S_0$ by the
constant value $t_0$.  This produces a continuous retraction $S\to T$.
A retract of an extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff space is
extremally disconnected: for an open $V\subseteq T$, the closure of $V$ in
$T$ is the intersection with $T$ of the closure of its inverse image under
the retraction, and is therefore open.  Thus $T\in\Extr$.

Let $i:\Z[T]\to\Z[S]$ be induced by the inclusion.  Define
$p:\Z[S]\to\Z[T]$ from the generator map
\[
 s\longmapsto
 \begin{cases}
 0,&s\in S_0,\\
 [r(s)],&s\in S_+,\\
 -[r(s)],&s\in S_-.
 \end{cases}
\]
This map is continuous by the clopen decomposition and Lemma~\ref{lem:ball}
applied to $T$.  On universal generators, $i\circ p$ agrees with $e$, while
$p\circ i$ is the identity because every $t\in T$ lies in $S_+$ and is fixed
by $r$.  Hence $e=i\circ p$, $p\circ i=\mathrm{id}_{\Z[T]}$, and the image of
$e$ is isomorphic to $\Z[T]$.

Conversely, the same formulas attached to any signed partial retraction give
$p\circ i=\mathrm{id}$, so $e=i\circ p$ is an idempotent, is
generator-contractive, and has image $\Z[T]$.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and stress tests}

The argument is exact and uses no computation.  As a finite sanity check, the
script \path{code/check_finite_contractive_idempotents.py} enumerates every map
from an $n$-point set to $\{0\}\sqcup S^+\sqcup S^-$ for $1\leq n\leq5$,
keeps the idempotents, and checks that every nonzero output targets a
positively fixed point.  It checked respectively
\[
 2,\quad 8,\quad 44,\quad 288,\quad 2192
\]
idempotents.  This is corroboration of the pointwise algebra only; it is not
a proof of the continuity or extremal-disconnectedness steps.

The most useful hand checks are:
\begin{itemize}
\item Negative outputs cause no sign error: $e(-[t])=-e([t])$, so
idempotence still forces $e([t])=[t]$.
\item If $e\neq0$, then $T\neq\varnothing$, because every nonzero output
lands in $T$.
\item The extension over $S_0$ is continuous because $S_0$ is clopen.
\item Equality of the constructed morphisms can be checked on the universal
generator by the free/forgetful adjunction.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations and full-result frontier}

Every compact projective is the image of some idempotent on $\Z[S]$, and the
quasicompactness of $S$ places its generator map in $\Ball_N(S)$ for some
finite $N$.  The theorem settles the sharp first layer $N=1$.

The proof does not extend formally to $N=2$.  Already on two points, the
idempotent
\[
 e([a])=[a]+[b],\qquad e([b])=0
\]
has generator bound two.  Its image is free in this finite example, but it has
no positively fixed \emph{basis point}; the fixed object is instead generated
by the vector $[a]+[b]$.  In a profinite family, the missing step is to choose
such integral image bases continuously.  That is exactly the new structural
issue at higher radius, and the source's comparison with complemented
subspaces/injective Banach spaces cautions against treating it as routine.

A bounded literature search on 9 August 2026 used the exact wording of
Question 3.6 and combinations of ``compact projective condensed abelian
group'', ``idempotent'', ``contractive'', ``$\ell^1$'', and ``retract''.
It covered the source notes, recent condensed-mathematics notes, and the cited
injective-Banach context.  No matching radius-one classification was found;
novelty is therefore provisional.

\section{Human-review recommendation}

The candidate is likely valid and merits review as a partial result.  Review
