\section{Source question}

Farkas and R\'ev\'esz ask whether the optimal constant in a bounded continuous
periodic decomposition can always be chosen to be one
\cite[problem following Remark 3.5]{FR}.  More precisely, if
\[
 \Pcal_\alpha=\{g\in BC(\R):g(x+\alpha)=g(x)\},\qquad \alpha\ne0,
\]
and $f\in\Pcal_{\alpha_1}+\cdots+\Pcal_{\alpha_n}$, must there be
$f_j\in\Pcal_{\alpha_j}$ with
\[
 f=\sum_{j=1}^n f_j,
 \qquad \max_j\|f_j\|_\infty\le\|f\|_\infty?
\]
The source proves existence of decompositions and records the sharp constant
one for two periods.  The exact source problem is reproduced in
\texttt{source\_excerpt.pdf}.

\section{Results}

Two nonzero reals are called \emph{commensurable} when their ratio is
rational.

\begin{theorem}[Rationally independent reciprocal periods]\label{th:generic}
Suppose $\alpha_1^{-1},\ldots,\alpha_n^{-1}$ are linearly independent over
$\Q$.  Every $f\in\sum_{j=1}^n\Pcal_{\alpha_j}$ has a decomposition
$f=\sum_j g_j$, $g_j\in\Pcal_{\alpha_j}$, such that
\[
 \|g_j\|_\infty\le\|f\|_\infty\qquad(j=1,\ldots,n).
\]
The constant one is sharp.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Three periods]\label{th:three}
Suppose $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$ are not all commensurable.  Every
$f\in\Pcal_{\alpha_1}+\Pcal_{\alpha_2}+\Pcal_{\alpha_3}$ has a
decomposition $f=g_1+g_2+g_3$, $g_j\in\Pcal_{\alpha_j}$, with
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le3}\|g_j\|_\infty\le\|f\|_\infty.
\]
Again the constant one is sharp.
\end{theorem}

The second theorem goes beyond Theorem~\ref{th:generic}: three pairwise
incommensurable reciprocal periods can satisfy an integer relation, so their
orbit closure can be a proper two-dimensional subtorus of $\T^3$.

\section{The full-product argument}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{th:generic}]
Write $\beta_j=\alpha_j^{-1}$ and choose an initial decomposition
\[
 f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^n F_j(\beta_jx\bmod1),\qquad F_j\in C(\T,\R).
\]
Kronecker density says that $x\mapsto(\beta_1x,\ldots,\beta_nx)\bmod1$
is dense in $\T^n$.  Put
\[
 a_j=\min F_j,\quad b_j=\max F_j,\quad
 c_j=\frac{a_j+b_j}{2},\quad r_j=\frac{b_j-a_j}{2},
\]
and $c=\sum_jc_j$, $R=\sum_jr_j$.  Density gives
\[
 \min f=c-R,\qquad \max f=c+R,
 \qquad M:=\|f\|_\infty=|c|+R.
\]
There are real numbers $d_j$ such that
\[
 \sum_jd_j=c,\qquad |d_j|\le M-r_j.
\]
Indeed, the symmetric intervals of radii $M-r_j$ have Minkowski sum of
radius
\[
 \sum_j(M-r_j)=nM-R\ge |c|.
\]
Now set
\[
 G_j=F_j-c_j+d_j.
\]
Then $\sum_jG_j(\beta_jx)=f(x)$ and
$\|G_j\|_\infty=r_j+|d_j|\le M$.

For sharpness take $f(x)=\cos(2\pi\beta_1x)$.  The nonzero Fourier spectra of
the periodic summand spaces are the disjoint lattices
$\beta_j\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$.  Uniqueness of Fourier coefficients forces
the first summand in any decomposition to be
$\cos(2\pi\beta_1x)$ plus a constant, so its norm is at least one.
\end{proof}

\section{The rank-two three-period argument}

We first handle the genuinely new case in which the three periods are
pairwise incommensurable.  Take an initial decomposition and let
\[
 K=\overline{\{(\beta_1x,\beta_2x,\beta_3x)\bmod1:x\in\R\}}\subset\T^3.
\]
Every two-coordinate projection of $K$ is onto $\T^2$.  Let $\mu$ be Haar
measure on $K$, put $m_j=\int_\T F_j$, and $m=\sum_jm_j=\int_K f$.
Define
\[
 h_j=F_j+m-m_j.
\]
The pullback of $h_j$ to $K$ is the conditional Haar average of $f$ over the
fibres of the $j$th coordinate: the other two coordinates are Haar-uniform
because the corresponding two-coordinate projections are onto.  Therefore,
with $M=\|f\|_\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounds}
 -M\le h_j\le M,\qquad \int_\T h_j=m,
 \qquad h_1+h_2+h_3=f+2m.
\end{equation}

Write $\ell_j=\min h_j$ and $u_j=\max h_j$.  If $m\ge0$, choose a point of
$K$ at which any prescribed pair $h_i,h_j$ attains its two minima; this is
possible by surjectivity of the $(i,j)$ coordinate projection.  At that point
$f\ge-M$, while the remaining $h_k\le M$.  From \eqref{eq:bounds},
\[
 \ell_i+\ell_j\ge2m-2M.
\]
Adding the three pair inequalities yields
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^3(\ell_j+M)\ge3m\ge2m.
\]
Thus there are $q_j\in[0,\ell_j+M]$ with $\sum_jq_j=2m$.
The functions $g_j=h_j-q_j$ sum to $f$ and remain in $[-M,M]$.

If $m\le0$, apply the symmetric argument at simultaneous pairwise maxima.
It gives
\[
 u_i+u_j\le2m+2M,\qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^3(M-u_j)\ge-3m\ge-2m.
\]
Choose $q_j\in[0,M-u_j]$ with $\sum_jq_j=-2m$ and put
$g_j=h_j+q_j$.  Again the summands add to $f$ and have norm at most $M$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{th:three}]
The pairwise incommensurable case is proved above.  Otherwise exactly two
periods, say $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$, are commensurable and the third is
incommensurable with both.  The sum of the first two initial components has a
common period $L$.  Apply Theorem~\ref{th:generic} for two periods to this
aggregate and the $\alpha_3$-periodic component.  Its construction only moves
a constant, so it produces
\[
 f=H+g_3,\qquad \|H\|_\infty,\|g_3\|_\infty\le M,
\]
with $H\in\Pcal_{\alpha_1}+\Pcal_{\alpha_2}$.  The source's sharp
two-period result decomposes $H=g_1+g_2$ with
$\|g_1\|_\infty,\|g_2\|_\infty\le\|H\|_\infty\le M$.

Sharpness follows as in Theorem~\ref{th:generic}, using a cosine whose
frequency belongs to the singleton commensurability class in the second case.
\end{proof}

\section{Boundary of the result}

The only three-period case not covered is the fully commensurable one.  It
reduces fibrewise to a norm-one splitting problem for three coset partitions
of a finite cyclic group.  Exhaustive small vertex computations and broader
sampled searches found quotient norm one, but no uniform dual certificate or
continuous selection argument was obtained.  For four or more distinct
commensurability classes, higher rational relations create the analogous
proper-subtorus obstruction.

No external record settling the exact constant question was located in the
bounded search performed for this packet.  The local parsed corpus contains
the question only in arXiv:1312.3798; the adjacent arXiv:1401.1226 develops
semigroup decomposition results but does not settle this norm-one problem.

\textbf{Human-review recommendation.} Check the fibre conditional-average
identity, the pair-extrema inequalities, and the reduction with one
commensurable pair.
