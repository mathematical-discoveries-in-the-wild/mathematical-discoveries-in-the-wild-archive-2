\section*{Status and sources}

\textbf{Status:} \texttt{literature\_already\_answered (Open Problem 2 only)}.

The original question is Open Problem~2 on PDF page~24 of Marina Ghisi and
Massimo Gobbino's \emph{Residual pathologies}, arXiv:1908.09496
\cite{Residual}.  Their distinct later paper \emph{Critical counterexamples
for linear wave equations with time-dependent propagation speed},
arXiv:1909.10020 \cite{Critical}, answers it in Theorem~3.2 on PDF page~11;
the relevant initial-data construction is on PDF pages~23--24.

\section*{Source question}

The source fixes the abstract wave equation with zero initial displacement,
\[
 u''(t)+c(t)Au(t)=0,
 \qquad u(0)=0,
 \qquad u'(0)=\psi,
\]
and asks whether there exist
\begin{itemize}
\item an $\alpha$-H\"older speed $c(t)$, $0<\alpha<1$,
\item a critical-order initial velocity
  $\psi\in\mathcal G_{s,r_0}(A)$ with
  $s=(1-\alpha)^{-1}$ and $r_0>0$, and
\item a time $t_0>0$,
\end{itemize}
such that the corresponding solution has severe derivative loss, or an
equivalent form of it, for every $t>t_0$.

\section*{Exact later answer}

Theorem~3.2 of \cite{Critical} assumes an unbounded nonnegative discrete
multiplication operator $A$, an $\alpha$-H\"older strictly positive speed,
and
\[
 s=S=\frac1{1-\alpha},\qquad r_0>0.
\]
It allows $\delta=0$ in the more general equation containing a damping term,
and sets the explicit finite time
\[
 t_0=\frac{32\mu_2^{(1+\alpha)/2}}{H}\,r_0.
\]
It proves that a residual set of admissible propagation speeds admits a
solution whose initial data lie in the critical finite-radius Gevrey class
and such that
\[
 (u(t),u'(t))\notin
 \mathcal G_{-S,\log,1/2}(A)\times
 \mathcal G_{-S,\log,0}(A)
 \qquad\text{for every }t>t_0.
\]
The implication following the theorem shows that this is the largest loss
compatible with the known lower regularity bound.

The proof on PDF page~23 removes the only possible formulation ambiguity: it
starts with
\[
 u(0)=0,
 \qquad
 u'(0)=\sum_i a_i e_i,
\]
then chooses
\[
 a_i=\lambda_i^{-1}\exp(-r_0\lambda_i^{1/s})
\]
for Theorem~3.2.  Thus the result supplies precisely the initial velocity
requested in Open Problem~2, not merely an arbitrary pair of critical initial
data.

\section*{Why this is an explicit literature answer}

The later paper's abstract says it treats critical cases left open by the
previous literature.  Its introduction identifies the critical
$s=(1-\alpha)^{-1}$ case as the first such case, says Theorem~3.2 proves
finite-time severe loss, includes the nondissipative case $\delta=0$, and
cites \cite{Residual}.  The relation is therefore known to the supporting
authors and belongs in \texttt{literature\_already\_answered}, not in an
agent-generated proof category.
