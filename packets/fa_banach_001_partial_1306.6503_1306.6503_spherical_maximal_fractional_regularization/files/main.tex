\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Candidate substantial partial result, likely valid; human review is needed.
The full low-exponent $W^{1,p}\to\dot W^{1,p}$ conjecture remains open.

\section{Source question}

For normalized surface measure $\sigma$ on $S^{n-1}$, write
\[
 \Sph u(x):=\sup_{r>0}
 \left|\int_{S^{n-1}}u(x-r\omega)\,d\sigma(\omega)\right|.
\]
The same argument applies to the positive convention with $|u|$ inside the
average.  Hajlasz and Liu ask whether
\[
 \Sph:W^{1,p}(\R^n)\longrightarrow\dot W^{1,p}(\R^n) \tag{1.1}
\]
is bounded for every $n\ge2$ and $1<p<n$ \cite{HL}.  Stein--Bourgain
settles (1.1) for $p>p_c:=n/(n-1)$, so the unresolved range is
$1<p\le p_c$.

\begin{figure}[ht]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/source_question_crop.png}
 \caption{Question~1.4 in the source paper \cite{HL}, PDF page 5.}
\end{figure}

The source proves the pointwise growth estimate
$\Sph u\lesssim I_1(|\nabla u|)$, hence $\Sph u\in L^{p^*}$ for
$p^*=np/(n-p)$, but notes that this does not control oscillation.  The next
result supplies scale-sharp positive fractional regularity in the entire
unresolved range.

\section{Strong fractional regularity}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:strong}
Let $1<p<n$, $p^*=np/(n-p)$, and
\[
 \max\{p,p_c\}<q<p^*.
\]
Set
\[
 \alpha:=1-n\left(\frac1p-\frac1q\right)\in(0,1).
\]
Then every $u\in W^{1,p}(\R^n)$ satisfies
\[
 \sup_{h\ne0}|h|^{-\alpha}
 \norm{\tauh\Sph u-\Sph u}_{L^q}
 \le C_{n,p,q}\norm{\nabla u}_{L^p}.                 \tag{2.1}
\]
Equivalently, $\Sph u\in\dot B^\alpha_{q,\infty}$ with the displayed
homogeneous bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Translation invariance and the triangle inequality for the supremum give
the pointwise estimate
\[
 |\tauh\Sph u-\Sph u|\le \Sph(\tauh u-u).             \tag{2.2}
\]
Put $g_h=\tauh u-u$.  The fundamental theorem on lines and Sobolev
embedding yield
\[
 \norm{g_h}_{p}\le |h|\norm{\nabla u}_{p},\qquad
 \norm{g_h}_{p^*}\le2\norm{u}_{p^*}le C_{n,p}\norm{\nabla u}_{p}.
                                                                    \tag{2.3}
\]
Because
\[
 \frac1q=\frac\alpha p+\frac{1-\alpha}{p^*},
\]
interpolation of the two norms in (2.3) gives
\[
 \norm{g_h}_{q}\le C|h|^\alpha\norm{\nabla u}_{p}.     \tag{2.4}
\]
Finally, $q>p_c$, so the strong spherical-maximal theorem applied to
(2.2)--(2.4) proves (2.1).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:frac}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:strong}, for every $0<s<\alpha$,
\[
 \Sph:W^{1,p}(\R^n)\longrightarrow W^{s,q}(\R^n)
\]
is bounded.  In particular, if $p=p_c$, every order $s<1$ is available
with $q>p_c$ sufficiently close to $p_c$.  If $1<p<p_c$, every order
\[
 0<s<n\left(1-\frac1p\right)                           \tag{2.5}
\]
is available with a suitable $q>p_c$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $p\le q\le p^*$ and $u\in W^{1,p}$, interpolation gives $u\in L^q$;
the strong maximal theorem gives $\Sph u\in L^q$.  In the difference
quotient formula for $[\Sph u]_{W^{s,q}}$, (2.1) controls $|h|<1$ and
the $L^q$ norm controls $|h|\ge1$.  The radial $h$-integral near zero is
$\int_0^1 r^{q(\alpha-s)-1}\,dr<\infty$.
\end{proof}

\section{Critical Lorentz refinement}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lorentz}
Suppose $n\ge3$ and $1<p<p_c$.  Put
\[
 \beta:=n\left(1-\frac1p\right)\in(0,1).
\]
Then
\[
 \sup_{h\ne0}|h|^{-\beta}
 \norm{\tauh\Sph u-\Sph u}_{L^{p_c,\infty}}
 \le C_{n,p}\norm{\nabla u}_{L^p}.                   \tag{3.1}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Bourgain's endpoint estimate in dimensions $n\ge3$ is
\[
 \Sph:L^{p_c,1}\longrightarrow L^{p_c,\infty}.        \tag{3.2}
\]
Real interpolation of (2.3), using
$(L^p,L^{p^*})_{\theta,1}=L^{p_c,1}$, gives
\[
 \norm{g_h}_{L^{p_c,1}}
 \le C|h|^\beta\norm{\nabla u}_{p};                   \tag{3.3}
\]
indeed the weight of the order-one $L^p$ estimate is
$1-n(1/p-1/p_c)=\beta$.  Combine (2.2), (3.2), and (3.3).
\end{proof}

The full endpoint Lorentz estimate for the circular maximal operator is
false, so Theorem~\ref{thm:lorentz} is correctly restricted to $n\ge3$;
see \cite{STW} and its discussion of the endpoint obstruction.

\section{Sharpness and why this is not yet one derivative}

For $u_\lambda(x)=u(\lambda x)$, the right side of (2.1) scales as
$\lambda^{1-n/p}$.  A $\dot B^\gamma_{q,\infty}$ seminorm scales as
$\lambda^{\gamma-n/q}$.  Hence any homogeneous estimate of this form
forces
\[
 \gamma-\frac nq=1-\frac np,
\]
which is exactly $\gamma=\alpha$.  Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:strong} loses no
smoothness beyond the integrability tradeoff needed to enter the known
spherical-maximal range.

That tradeoff is nevertheless decisive.  On the critical line,
$W^{1,p}$ embeds into $B^\alpha_{q,\infty}$; the reverse embedding needed
to deduce (1.1) is false.  In frequency language, the Besov conclusion has
only dyadic $\ell^\infty$ control, while a full Sobolev derivative requires
additional scale summability.

\section{Eight upgrade attempts and remaining obstruction}

Eight focused routes were audited.  Direct $L^p$ difference quotients are
circular below $p_c$.  Interpolation before applying the maximal theorem is
the successful proof above.  Endpoint real interpolation gives
Theorem~\ref{thm:lorentz}, but not a strong derivative.  Critical Besov
embedding runs in the wrong direction.  In the companion maximal-potential
representation, differentiating a truncation leaves a boundary spherical
average, precisely the unavailable low-exponent term.  Stationarity of a
maximizing radius cancels only the scalar radial mean, not its first
spherical moment.  The source's logarithmic radial counterexample at $p=1$
does not extend to $p>1$: the tangent-sphere singularity becomes integrable,
and the radial vector field has an additional odd cancellation.  Finally,
an upgrade via vector-valued or variation estimates would need scale
summability not supplied by known scalar strong or endpoint bounds.

The associated attempt note records these calculations in detail.  No full
proof or counterexample survived the audit.  The new result should therefore
be retained as a substantial partial answer, not labeled a solution of
Question~1.4.

\section{Verification and literature status}

The proof uses only: the line-translation estimate, homogeneous Sobolev
embedding, elementary interpolation, the classical strong spherical-maximal
theorem, and Bourgain's endpoint Lorentz theorem.  The exponent identities
were checked directly and by dilation.  The argument works for both common
absolute-value conventions for $\Sph$.

Cheap run indexes and bounded searches through 12 August 2026 found no later
paper resolving the exact low-exponent Sobolev conjecture.  A later
Hajlasz--Liu paper still calls it a conjecture, and recent pointwise-potential
work recalls the growth estimate rather than a derivative bound.  Novelty
confidence is moderate because the literature search was bounded; the
mathematical argument here is self-contained once the classical maximal
theorems are accepted.
