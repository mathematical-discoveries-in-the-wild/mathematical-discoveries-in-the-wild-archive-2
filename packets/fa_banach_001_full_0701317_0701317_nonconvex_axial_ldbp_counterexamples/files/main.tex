\begin{abstract}
Rubin asked whether convexity is essential in the positive part of his
lower-dimensional Busemann--Petty theorem for bodies with generalized axial
symmetry.  We prove the conjectured negative statement for every admissible
parameter.  The construction starts from an explicit smooth
$K_\ell$-symmetric star body $B$ whose $(n-i)$th radial power is a constant
plus a degree-two spherical harmonic.  The inverse generalized cosine
transform multiplies that harmonic by $-i/(n-i)$, producing a negative
representing density.  Rubin's Radon--cosine perturbation then gives a nearby
star body $A$ with all $i$-dimensional central sections no larger than those
of $B$, but with larger $n$-volume.  The same parameter inequality gives
strictly negative curvature in a coordinate two-plane, and this persists
under the perturbation, so $A$ is nonconvex as required.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source conjecture and scope}

The source is Boris Rubin, \emph{The Lower Dimensional Busemann-Petty
Problem for Bodies with the Generalized Axial Symmetry},
arXiv:math/0701317v2 (2007), later published in the \emph{Israel Journal of
Mathematics} \cite{Rubin}.  The conjecture appears on source PDF page~4,
after Theorem~1.1.

In paraphrase, Rubin fixes the parameters from Theorem~1.1(b), namely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
  1\leq \ell\leq n/2,\qquad i+\ell\leq n,
  \qquad i=\ell+1\ \text{or}\ i=\ell+2,
\end{equation}
and asks for a nonconvex $K_\ell$-symmetric body $A$ and an
origin-symmetric star body $B$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 \vol_i(A\cap\xi)\leq \vol_i(B\cap\xi)\quad(\xi\in G_{n,i}),
 \qquad \vol_n(A)>\vol_n(B).
\end{equation}
Here $K_\ell=O(n-\ell)\times O(\ell)$ acts block-diagonally on
$\R^{n-\ell}\oplus\R^\ell$.

\section{Definitions and source identities}

Write $k=n-i$.  For an origin-symmetric star body $K$, let $\rho_K$
denote its radial function.  In Rubin's normalization, the generalized cosine
transform is
\[
 (M^\alpha f)(u)=\gamma_n(\alpha)
 \int_{S^{n-1}} f(\theta)|\theta\cdot u|^{\alpha-1}\,d\theta,
\]
initially where the integral converges and then by meromorphic continuation.
The parameters used below are not poles.  On even smooth functions,
Rubin's Lemma~4.1 gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse}
 M^\alpha M^\beta f=f
 \quad\text{when}\quad \alpha+\beta=2-n.
\end{equation}
The transform is self-adjoint.  Rubin's Lemma~4.2 gives, for a positive
constant $c_{n,i}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intertwine}
 R_iM^{1-i}h(\xi)=c_{n,i}R_kh(\xi^\perp),
 \qquad \xi\in G_{n,i}.
\end{equation}

An origin-symmetric star body $K$ is a $k$-intersection body precisely when
there is a nonnegative finite even Borel measure $\mu$ on $S^{n-1}$ such
that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intersection}
 \rho_K^k=M^{1-k}\mu
\end{equation}
in the distributional sense \cite[Definition~4.5]{Rubin}.

We use one elementary Funk--Hecke calculation.  If $Y$ is a spherical
harmonic of degree two, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multipliers}
 M^{1-i}1=c_0,
 \qquad
 M^{1-i}Y=-c_0\frac{i}{k}Y,
 \qquad
 c_0=\sigma_{n-1}\frac{\Gamma(i/2)}{\Gamma(k/2)}>0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, for general $\alpha$, the degree-two multiplier divided by the
constant multiplier is
\[
 -\frac{1-\alpha}{n-1+\alpha};
\]
substitution of $\alpha=1-i$ gives $-i/k$.

\section{Idea of the proof}

The positive theorem in the source is powered by positivity of an
intersection-body representing density.  We force exactly that density to
change sign with the lowest nonconstant harmonic compatible with
$K_\ell$-symmetry.  Put
\[
 Y(\theta)=|\theta''|^2-\frac\ell n,
 \qquad \theta=(\theta',\theta'')\in
 \R^{n-\ell}\oplus\R^\ell.
\]
This is an even $K_\ell$-invariant degree-two harmonic.  If
$\rho_B^k=1+\varepsilon Y$, formula \eqref{eq:multipliers} says that
the representing density is proportional to
$1-\varepsilon(i/k)Y$.  There is a nonempty interval of $\varepsilon$
for which the radial function stays positive but this density is negative
near the $\ell$-block axis.

Once this sign change is available, a nonnegative bump $h$ supported in the
negative region feeds directly into Rubin's perturbation
$\rho_A^i=\rho_B^i-\delta M^{1-i}h$.  Identity
\eqref{eq:intertwine} gives the section inequalities on the entire
Grassmannian, while self-adjointness and \eqref{eq:inverse} make the mixed
radial integral increase.  Holder's inequality then forces the strict volume
reversal.  A short polar-curvature calculation shows that $B$ is already
strictly nonconvex, and an arbitrarily small perturbation preserves this
strict defect for $A$.

\section{The counterexamples}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Assume \eqref{eq:parameters}.  Then there are infinitely smooth,
origin-symmetric, $K_\ell$-symmetric star bodies $A,B\subset\R^n$ such that
$A$ and $B$ are nonconvex and \eqref{eq:target} holds.  In particular,
Rubin's conjecture is true for every parameter choice in its stated scope.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $k=n-i$.  The assumptions give $k\geq\ell\geq1$.  On $S^{n-1}$ write
\[
 t=|\theta''|^2,
 \qquad Y(\theta)=t-\frac\ell n.
\]
The polynomial $|x''|^2-(\ell/n)|x|^2$ is harmonic, so $Y$ is an even
degree-two spherical harmonic.  It is also $K_\ell$-invariant, and its range
is
\[
 -\frac\ell n\leq Y\leq1-\frac\ell n.
\]

Choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epsilon}
 \frac{kn}{i(n-\ell)}<\varepsilon<\frac n\ell.
\end{equation}
The interval is nonempty: after canceling positive factors, its nonemptiness
is exactly $k\ell<i(n-\ell)$, which reduces to $\ell<i$.
Define $B$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B}
 \rho_B(\theta)^k=1+\varepsilon Y(\theta).
\end{equation}
The upper bound in \eqref{eq:epsilon} makes the right-hand side strictly
positive.  Thus $B$ is an infinitely smooth, origin-symmetric,
$K_\ell$-symmetric star body.

Let
\[
 \varphi=M^{1-i}(\rho_B^k).
\]
By \eqref{eq:multipliers},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi}
 \varphi(\theta)=c_0\left[1-\varepsilon\frac{i}{k}
 \left(t-\frac\ell n\right)\right].
\end{equation}
At $t=1$, the lower bound in \eqref{eq:epsilon} gives
\[
 \varphi=c_0\left[1-\varepsilon\frac{i(n-\ell)}{kn}\right]<0.
\]
Consequently, $\varphi$ is negative on a nonempty open
$K_\ell$-invariant subset of the sphere.

Since $(1-k)+(1-i)=2-n$, identity \eqref{eq:inverse} gives
\[
 \rho_B^k=M^{1-k}\varphi.
\]
This representing distribution is unique.  Indeed, on the even spherical
harmonic of degree $2j$, the multiplier of $M^{1-k}$ is a nonzero constant
times
\[
 (-1)^j\frac{\Gamma(j+k/2)}{\Gamma(j+i/2)},
\]
so $M^{1-k}$ is injective on even distributions.  Therefore no nonnegative
measure can represent \eqref{eq:B}, because the unique representing measure
has the smooth density \eqref{eq:phi}, which is negative on an open set.
Thus $B$ is not a $k$-intersection body.

We also need a strict, stable proof of nonconvexity.  Choose unit vectors
$u\in\R^{n-\ell}$ and $v\in\R^\ell$, and examine the radial curve of
$B\cap\operatorname{span}\{u,v\}$.  With $\omega$ measured from $u$ toward
$v$, it is
\[
 r(\omega)=\bigl(a+\varepsilon\sin^2\omega\bigr)^{1/k},
 \qquad a=1-\frac{\varepsilon\ell}{n}>0.
\]
For a smooth polar curve, the numerator determining signed curvature is
$r^2+2(r')^2-rr''$.  At $\omega=0$ this equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curvature}
 a^{2/k-1}\left(a-\frac{2\varepsilon}{k}\right).
\end{equation}
The last factor is negative exactly when
\[
 \varepsilon>\frac{nk}{k\ell+2n}.
\]
Put $s=i-\ell\in\{1,2\}$.  Since $k=n-\ell-s$,
\[
 (k\ell+2n)-i(n-\ell)=n(2-s)\geq0.
\]
Hence the lower bound in \eqref{eq:epsilon} is at least the curvature
threshold, and the inequality is strict.  Expression
\eqref{eq:curvature} is negative, so this planar section is not locally
convex.  Therefore $B$ is nonconvex.

Let $\Omega=\{\theta:\varphi(\theta)<0\}$.  Choose a nonzero smooth,
even, $K_\ell$-invariant function $h\geq0$ supported in $\Omega$.
For $\delta>0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
 \rho_A^i=\rho_B^i-\delta M^{1-i}h.
\end{equation}
For all sufficiently small $\delta$, the right-hand side is positive, so it
defines an infinitely smooth, origin-symmetric, $K_\ell$-symmetric star body
$A$.  Moreover, $\rho_A\to\rho_B$ in $C^\infty$ as $\delta\to0$.
The curvature in \eqref{eq:curvature} is strictly negative, so it remains
negative for all sufficiently small $\delta$.  Thus $A$ is nonconvex.

For every $\xi\in G_{n,i}$, \eqref{eq:intertwine} yields
\[
 R_i\rho_A^i(\xi)
 =R_i\rho_B^i(\xi)-\delta c_{n,i}R_kh(\xi^\perp)
 \leq R_i\rho_B^i(\xi).
\]
Since $\vol_i(K\cap\xi)=i^{-1}R_i\rho_K^i(\xi)$, this is the first
inequality in \eqref{eq:target} on the full Grassmannian.

Finally, self-adjointness of the cosine transform and
\eqref{eq:inverse} give
\begin{align*}
 \ip{\rho_B^k}{\rho_B^i-\rho_A^i}
 &=\delta\ip{M^{1-k}\varphi}{M^{1-i}h}\\
 &=\delta\ip{\varphi}{M^{1-k}M^{1-i}h}\\
 &=\delta\ip{\varphi}{h}<0.
\end{align*}
Thus
\[
 \int_{S^{n-1}}\rho_B^n
 <\int_{S^{n-1}}\rho_B^k\rho_A^i.
\]
Holder's inequality, with exponents $n/k$ and $n/i$, gives
\[
 \int_{S^{n-1}}\rho_B^k\rho_A^i
 \leq
 \left(\int_{S^{n-1}}\rho_B^n\right)^{k/n}
 \left(\int_{S^{n-1}}\rho_A^n\right)^{i/n}.
\]
It follows that $\int\rho_B^n<\int\rho_A^n$, and the polar volume
formula proves $\vol_n(B)<\vol_n(A)$.  This completes the construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is explicit]
The base body $B$ is given explicitly by \eqref{eq:B}, with any
$\varepsilon$ in \eqref{eq:epsilon}.  The body $A$ is an existence-level
smooth perturbation: any nonzero invariant bump supported where
\eqref{eq:phi} is negative works once $\delta$ is sufficiently small.
No unproved analytic or numerical dependency remains.
\end{remark}

\section{Verification}

The accompanying script \texttt{code/verifier.py} enumerates every admissible
triple $(n,\ell,i)$ with $3\leq n\leq100$, chooses the midpoint of
\eqref{eq:epsilon}, and checks positivity of $\rho_B^k$, negativity of
\eqref{eq:phi} at $t=1$, negativity of the curvature factor in
\eqref{eq:curvature}, and a 201-point angular sampling of the radial-power
profile.  All tested cases pass.  This code is only an algebraic sanity
check; the proof uses the exact inequalities and transform identities above.

The main expert-review points are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the normalization and degree-two multiplier in
  \eqref{eq:multipliers};
\item injectivity of $M^{1-k}$ on even distributions at the stated integer
  parameters;
\item the index substitution in Rubin's inversion and intertwining lemmas;
\item persistence of the strict curvature defect under \eqref{eq:A}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Novelty search and limitations}

A bounded search on 11 August 2026 covered the run's registry, solution,
attempt, and proof-gap indexes; the exact arXiv id and title; phrases from the
source conjecture; the published DOI; and later work on intersection bodies
with symmetries, including arXiv:1307.3206.  The existing run packet for
arXiv:0704.0061 concerns a different $(q,\ell)$-ball
$\lambda$-intersection-body question and merely lists the present source as
related literature.  No explicit later resolution or this degree-two
construction was found.  The search is not exhaustive, so originality is
provisional.

This theorem addresses the conjecture after Theorem~1.1(b), under the
source's condition $i+\ell\leq n$.  It does not address the separate convex
exceptional cases with $i+\ell>n$ discussed immediately before the conjecture;
those remain tied to the open two- and three-dimensional-section problem.
