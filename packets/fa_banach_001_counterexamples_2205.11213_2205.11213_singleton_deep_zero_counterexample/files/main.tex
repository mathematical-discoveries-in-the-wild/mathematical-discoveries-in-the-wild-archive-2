\section*{Source problem and answer}

In Problem~1.1 of \cite{Hedenmalm}, Hedenmalm asks the following.  Given
$\mathcal E\subset\mathbb N_0$, put
$\mathcal E^c=\mathbb N_0\setminus\mathcal E$.  If $f$ belongs to the
Bargmann--Fock space and
\[
 f^{(j)}(0)=0\quad(j\in\mathcal E),
 \qquad
 (U_\beta f)^{(j)}(0)=0\quad(j\in\mathcal E^c),
\]
must $f$ vanish identically?  Here, in the source's convention,
\begin{equation}
 U_\alpha h(z)=e^{-\frac12|\alpha|^2+\bar\alpha z}h(z-\alpha).       \tag{1}
\end{equation}

The answer is \emph{no}, already when $\mathcal E$ has one element.

\section*{Counterexample}

\paragraph{Idea of the proof.}
Put all allowed Taylor data of $U_\beta f$ into one monomial and translate
back.  A suitable displacement makes the corresponding diagonal Taylor
coefficient vanish.  At the first level the cancellation is simply
$1-|\beta|^2=0$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let
\[
 \mathcal E=\{1\},\qquad \beta=1,
 \qquad f(z)=e^{-1/2-z}(z+1).
\]
Then $0\ne f\in\F$ and
\[
 f'(0)=0,
 \qquad
 (U_1f)^{(j)}(0)=0\quad\text{for every }j\ne1.
\]
Thus Problem~1.1 has a full negative answer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $g(z)=z$.  Formula (1) gives
\[
 U_{-1}g(z)=e^{-1/2-z}g(z+1)=e^{-1/2-z}(z+1)=f(z).
\]
Since $g$ is a polynomial in $\F$ and $U_{-1}$ is unitary, $f\in\F$.
Moreover,
\[
 f'(z)=-ze^{-1/2-z},
\]
so the sole condition indexed by $\mathcal E$ holds.  The Weyl composition
law has no phase for the opposite pair $1,-1$, hence
\[
 U_1f=U_1U_{-1}g=g=z.
\]
Every derivative of $z$ at zero except the first is zero, exactly as required
for $j\in\mathcal E^c$.  Finally, $f(0)=e^{-1/2}\ne0$.
\end{proof}

The cancellation is not isolated.  For $m\ge1$ and $t=|\beta|^2$, the
coefficient of $z^m$ in $U_{-\beta}(z^m)$ is
\begin{equation}
 e^{-t/2}P_m(t),
 \qquad
 P_m(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{m}\binom mr\frac{(-t)^r}{r!}.                   \tag{2}
\end{equation}
Indeed, expand
$e^{-\bar\beta z}(z+\beta)^m$ and collect degree $m$.  The polynomial
$P_m$ is the Laguerre polynomial $L_m$.  If $m$ is odd, then
$P_m(0)=1$ and its leading coefficient is $-1/m!$, so it has a positive
zero $t_m$.  Choosing $|\beta|^2=t_m$, setting
$\mathcal E=\{m\}$, and taking $f=U_{-\beta}(z^m)$ gives the same
counterexample.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is the case
$P_1(t)=1-t$.

\section*{Scope and literature boundary}

This counterexample does not conflict with the paper's Theorem~1.4, where
$\mathcal E$ is the set of \emph{all} even integers or all odd integers.
It answers the preceding arbitrary-subset Problem~1.1 as stated.

The run's cheap indexes and a bounded current exact-title/exact-phrase search
through 11 August 2026 found no prior occurrence of this singleton example.
The later paper \cite{LLZ} proves affirmative results for Hedenmalm's distinct
Problem~5.2 and congruence-class analogues via the HRT conjecture; it does not
address this finite-set obstruction.  The proof above is elementary and does
not depend on that later work.

\section*{Verification and review recommendation}

The source convention for $U_\alpha$, the inverse identity, both derivative
conditions, and Fock-space membership were checked directly.  Formula (2)
was independently re-expanded, including the factorial and phase.  The
$m=1$ counterexample needs no asymptotic or external theorem.  Human review is
recommended, with primary attention to matching the source's sign convention
for the Fock translate.  Confidence is very high.
