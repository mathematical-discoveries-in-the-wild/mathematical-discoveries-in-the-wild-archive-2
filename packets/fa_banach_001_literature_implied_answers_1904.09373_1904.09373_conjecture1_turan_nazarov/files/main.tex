\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{Literature-implied answer (full answer to Conjecture 1 only).}
The implication below is a direct application of the classical measurable
Tur\'an--Nazarov inequality.  It is not presented as a new theorem.  The
supporting authors did not know they were answering Lawton's later question.

\paragraph{Source question.}
Wayne M. Lawton, \emph{Distribution of Small Values of Bohr Almost Periodic
Functions with Bounded Spectrum}, arXiv:1904.09373 (2019), Conjecture 1.1 on
page 2 of the locally compiled source PDF, asks whether the sequence $C_n$ in
\begin{equation}\label{lawton}
 J_f(u)\le C_n H_f^{-1/n}u^{1/n},\qquad u>0,
\end{equation}
can be replaced by a bounded sequence.  Here
$f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_j e^{i\omega_jx}$ has distinct real frequencies,
$H_f=\max_j|a_j|$, and
\[
 J_f(u)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}
 \bigl|\{x\in[-T,T]:|f(x)|<u\}\bigr|.
\]

\paragraph{Supporting theorem.}
Friedland and Yomdin quote Nazarov's measurable Tur\'an--Nazarov theorem as
Theorem 1.1 on page 1 of arXiv:1107.0039.  In the purely imaginary exponent
case needed here, it says that an absolute constant $A\ge1$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{TN}
 \sup_I|f|\le
 \left(\frac{A|I|}{|E|}\right)^n\sup_E|f|
\end{equation}
for every interval $I\subset\R$ and every measurable $E\subset I$ of
positive measure.  Crucially, the exponent is the number of terms minus one
and $A$ is independent of the frequencies and of $n$.

\begin{theorem}
For every trigonometric polynomial with at most $n+1$ distinct real
frequencies and every $u>0$,
\[
 J_f(u)\le A H_f^{-1/n}u^{1/n}.
\]
Consequently Lawton's Conjecture 1.1 is true, with the bounded sequence
$C_n=A$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The case $f=0$ is excluded in the source statement.  Write
$I_T=[-T,T]$, set
\[
 E_T=\{x\in I_T:|f(x)|<u\},\qquad
 \delta_T=\frac{|E_T|}{|I_T|},\qquad
 M_T=\sup_{I_T}|f|.
\]
If $|E_T|=0$, then $\delta_T=0$.  Otherwise, applying
\eqref{TN} to $E_T\subset I_T$ and using $\sup_{E_T}|f|\le u$ gives
\[
 M_T\le \left(\frac A{\delta_T}\right)^n u,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
 \delta_T\le A\left(\frac u{M_T}\right)^{1/n}.
\]

Choose $j_0$ with $|a_{j_0}|=H_f$.  Orthogonality of distinct real
frequencies in the Bohr mean gives
\[
 a_{j_0}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1{2T}
 \int_{-T}^{T}f(x)e^{-i\omega_{j_0}x}\,dx.
\]
The absolute value of each average on the right is at most $M_T$; therefore
$H_f\le\liminf_{T\to\infty}M_T$.  Since $J_f(u)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\delta_T$
exists, taking the upper limit in the preceding sublevel estimate yields
\[
 J_f(u)\le A\left(\frac u{H_f}\right)^{1/n}.
\]
This is exactly \eqref{lawton} with $C_n=A$ for every $n$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Identification and scope.}
The original 1993 Nazarov theorem predates arXiv:1904.09373, and
Friedland--Yomdin's 2011 restatement does not cite Lawton.  Thus this is an
agent-identified corollary rather than a later paper explicitly resolving the
conjecture.  No claim is made here about Conjectures 2 or 3 of Lawton's paper.

\paragraph{Search evidence.}
The bounded search used the exact source title, the exact bounded-$C_n$
conjecture phrase, the author name, and the terms ``small values,''
