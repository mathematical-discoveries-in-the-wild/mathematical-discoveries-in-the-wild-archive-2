\section*{1. The residual question and reduction}

Let $\varphi(t)=2^{1/4}e^{-\pi t^2}$.  With $d=1/a>0$, the source uses
\[
 f_\pm=\varphi\pm i\gamma T_d\varphi,
 \qquad |\mathcal Gf_+|=|\mathcal Gf_-|
 \quad\text{on }\R\times a\mathbb Z.
\]
These functions are not equal up to global phase.  The paper proves a local
Lipschitz bound, uniform in $a$ and the ball radius $R$, after choosing
$\gamma$ depending on $R$.  Its discussion asks for a fixed $\gamma>0$ giving
a whole-plane bound.  The global stability theorem of Grohs--Rathmair reduces
that upgrade to a uniform analytic weighted Poincar\'e estimate for
$|\mathcal Gf_\pm|^p$, $1\le p<2$.

Up to an irrelevant positive normalization, the source's exact formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight}
 w_\pm(x,y)=e^{-q(x^2+y^2)}
 \left|1\pm i\gamma e^{\pi d(x-iy)-\pi d^2/2}\right|^p,
 \qquad q=\frac{p\pi}{2}.
\end{equation}
For a vertical half-plane, define its boundary density and vertical Cheeger
constant by
\[
 \rho_\pm(t)=\int_\R w_\pm(t,y)\,dy,
 \qquad
 h_{\rm vert}(w_\pm)=\inf_{t\in\R}
 \frac{\rho_\pm(t)}{
 \min\{\int_{-\infty}^t\rho_\pm,\int_t^\infty\rho_\pm\}}.
\]

\section*{2. Exact vertical marginal comparison}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:marginal}
For $1\le p\le2$, $d>0$, and $\gamma>0$, set
\[
 m_{d,\gamma}(x)=e^{-qx^2}+\gamma^p e^{-q(x-d)^2}.
\]
There are constants depending only on $p$ such that, simultaneously for both
signs,
\[
 \frac{Z_q}{2}m_{d,\gamma}(x)
 \le \rho_\pm(x)\le Z_q2^{p-1}m_{d,\gamma}(x),
 \qquad Z_q=\int_\R e^{-qy^2}\,dy.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x$ and write $s=\gamma e^{\pi dx-\pi d^2/2}$.  With expectation taken
under the even probability density $Z_q^{-1}e^{-qy^2}$, put
$X(y)=|1\pm is e^{-i\pi dy}|$.  Oddness of the sine gives the exact identity
\[
 \mathbb E X^2=1+s^2.
\]
Since $X\le1+s$ and $p\le2$,
\[
 \mathbb E X^p\ge
 \frac{\mathbb E X^2}{(1+s)^{2-p}}
 =\frac{1+s^2}{(1+s)^2}(1+s)^p
 \ge\frac12(1+s)^p.
\]
The reverse bound $\mathbb E X^p\le(1+s)^p$ is immediate.  Finally,
$1+s^p\le(1+s)^p\le2^{p-1}(1+s^p)$, and
\[
 e^{-qx^2}s^p=\gamma^p e^{-q(x-d)^2}.
\]
Substitution into \eqref{eq:weight} proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\section*{3. Uniform exclusion of the two-bump cut}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:mixture}
For every $q>0$ there is $c_q>0$ such that, for all $d>0$ and
$0<\varepsilon\le e^{-qd^2}$, the density
\[
 m(x)=e^{-qx^2}+\varepsilon e^{-q(x-d)^2}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \inf_{t\in\R}\frac{m(t)}{
 \min\{\int_{-\infty}^t m,\int_t^\infty m\}}\ge c_q.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $G(u)=e^{-qu^2}$ there is $C_q<\infty$ such that
\[
 \int_t^\infty G\le C_qG(t)\quad(t\ge0),
 \qquad
 \int_{-\infty}^tG\le C_qG(t)\quad(t\le0).
\]
If $t\le0$, apply the second inequality to $t$ and $t-d$; the left mass of
$m$ is at most $C_qm(t)$.  If $t\ge d$, apply the first inequality to those
two arguments; the right mass is at most $C_qm(t)$.  Finally, if
$0\le t\le d$, then $\varepsilon\le e^{-qd^2}\le G(t)$, so
\[
 \int_t^\infty m
 \le C_qG(t)+\varepsilon\int_\R G
 \le\left(C_q+\int_\R G\right)G(t)
 \le C'_qm(t).
\]
In every case one of the two masses is bounded by a fixed multiple of
$m(t)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $1\le p<2$.  There is $c_p>0$ such that for every $d>0$ and every
\[
 0<\gamma\le e^{-\pi d^2/2},
\]
both weights in \eqref{eq:weight} satisfy
$h_{\rm vert}(w_\pm)\ge c_p$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $\varepsilon=\gamma^p$.  Because $q=p\pi/2$, the hypothesis is exactly
$\varepsilon\le e^{-qd^2}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:mixture} gives a uniform Cheeger
bound for $m_{d,\gamma}$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:marginal} transfers it to
$\rho_\pm$ with constants depending only on $p$.
\end{proof}

\section*{4. Meaning and remaining obstruction}

The theorem rigorously eliminates the apparent vertical ``cut between the
two bumps'' as a source of deteriorating stability, with the natural scaling
$\gamma\lesssim e^{-\pi d^2/2}$.  This materially strengthens the source's
finite-ball comparison, whose coefficient must shrink with the ball radius.

It does not yet prove the full negative answer.  The Cheeger constant used in
the phase-retrieval theorem is an infimum over arbitrary smooth cuts, not only
vertical half-planes.  On the equal-amplitude line the weight has a periodic
set of isolated zeros.  Consequently it is neither pointwise comparable to
the Gaussian mixture nor log-concave, and no justified half-space
isoperimetry principle applies.  Ordinary conditional tensorization also
fails at the zero line.  A full proof now reduces to a geometric lemma showing
that curved cuts cannot exploit this isolated zero comb at vanishing cost.

Exact-title and keyword searches through 13 August 2026 found no later primary
source resolving this whole-plane step.  Eight focused upgrade attempts,
including the failed exhaustion and tensorization routes, are recorded with
the run attempt.
