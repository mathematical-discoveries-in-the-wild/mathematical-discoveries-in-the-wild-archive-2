\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Candidate partial result; likely valid, subject to expert review.  The theorem
settles the source question for separable SIN topologies and gives a stronger
algebraic obstruction to locally compact target groups.  It does not settle
uniqueness among arbitrary separable group topologies.

\section{Source question and partial answer}

Dowerk asks in Section~5, pages~11--12 of \cite{Dowerk} whether
$\U(M)$ or $\PU(M)$ has a unique nontrivial separable group topology when
$M$ is a separable full von Neumann algebra, and whether a coarsest separable
group topology exists more generally.  For first-countable topologies on
$\PU(M)$ he reformulates this as completeness of every bounded compatible
left-invariant metric inducing a separable topology.

The result below concerns $\PU(M)=\U(M)/\T$.  Recall that a topological
group is \emph{SIN} if it has a neighbourhood basis at the identity consisting
of conjugacy-invariant sets, and is \emph{precompact} if its completion is
compact.

\begin{theorem}[SIN rigidity]\label{thm:sin}
Let $M$ be a factor with separable predual and put $G=\PU(M)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $M$ is of type $\mathrm{II}_1$, the strong operator topology is the
unique nontrivial separable SIN group topology on the abstract group $G$.
\item If $M$ is of type $\mathrm{III}$, the abstract group $G$ admits no
nontrivial separable SIN group topology.
\item In either case, $G$ admits no Hausdorff precompact group topology.
Equivalently, every abstract homomorphism from $G$ to a compact Hausdorff
group is trivial.
\end{enumerate}
None of these conclusions requires fullness.
\end{theorem}

The first assertion upgrades the known uniqueness of the Polish topology to
uniqueness among all separable SIN topologies, including incomplete ones.  It
also gives the following direct partial answer to the metric reformulation.

\begin{corollary}[the SIN metric case]\label{cor:metric}
Let $M$ be a separable $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor.  If a compatible
left-invariant metric $d$ on $\PU(M)$ induces a nontrivial separable SIN
topology, then $d$ is complete (and its topology is the strong operator
topology).  For a type $\mathrm{III}$ factor there is no such metric topology.
\end{corollary}

The target paper's strong uncountable cofinality theorem converts the last
part of Theorem~\ref{thm:sin} into a target-group result beyond SIN groups.

\begin{corollary}[locally compact targets]\label{cor:lc}
Let $M$ be a factor with separable predual, of type $\mathrm{II}_1$ or
$\mathrm{III}$.  Every abstract
homomorphism
\[
              \PU(M)\longrightarrow H
\]
to a second-countable locally compact Hausdorff group $H$ is trivial.  No
continuity of the homomorphism is assumed.
\end{corollary}

\section{Proof intuition}

The normal-generation estimates of Dowerk--Thom say uniformly that an element
which stays a fixed positive distance from the scalars normally generates the
whole projective unitary group in a bounded number of conjugates.  In a SIN
topology, sufficiently small neighbourhoods are invariant under conjugation.
Such a neighbourhood therefore cannot contain an element bounded away from
the scalars: otherwise a fixed power of the neighbourhood would be the whole
group.  Thus every SIN topology must be at least as fine as the canonical
projective length topology.

For a $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor this length topology is the strong topology.
Invariant automatic continuity supplies the opposite inclusion for every
separable SIN topology, giving equality.  For a type $\mathrm{III}$ factor the
canonical length is the projective operator norm, whose topology is
nonseparable, so no separable SIN topology can refine it.

A precompact Hausdorff group topology is automatically SIN.  If it refined
one of the two canonical length topologies, that length topology would itself
be precompact; explicit families of symmetries show that it is not.  Hence the
abstract groups have no nontrivial compact images.  Finally, strong
uncountable cofinality makes the image of any homomorphism into a group with a
proper left-invariant metric bounded.  In a locally compact second-countable
target the image therefore has compact closure, and the compact-image result
forces it to be trivial.

\section{Proof}

We isolate the general mechanism.  An invariant length on a group $G$ is a
map $\ell:G\to[0,L]$ such that $\ell(1)=0$, $\ell(g^{-1})=\ell(g)$,
$\ell(gh)\leq\ell(g)+\ell(h)$, and
$\ell(hgh^{-1})=\ell(g)$.  We say that it has \emph{uniform normal generation
away from the identity} if, for every $\varepsilon>0$, there is
$N_\varepsilon\in\mathbb N$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ung}
 \ell(g)\geq\varepsilon
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 G=(g^G\cup g^{-G})^{N_\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[SIN refinement]\label{lem:refine}
Suppose that $\ell$ is a Hausdorff invariant length satisfying
\eqref{eq:ung}.  Every Hausdorff SIN group topology $\tau$ on $G$ refines the
topology defined by $\ell$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $0<\varepsilon<L$ and put $N=N_\varepsilon$.  Choose $a\neq1$.  Since
$\tau$ is Hausdorff, there is a $\tau$-neighbourhood $O$ of $1$ with
$a\notin O$.  Continuity of multiplication gives a symmetric neighbourhood
$U$ with $U^N\subseteq O$.  The SIN property gives a symmetric
conjugacy-invariant neighbourhood $V\subseteq U$.

If $v\in V$ and $\ell(v)\geq\varepsilon$, then
$v^G\cup v^{-G}\subseteq V$, so \eqref{eq:ung} yields
$G\subseteq V^N\subseteq O$, contradicting $a\notin O$.  Hence
$V\subseteq\{g:\ell(g)<\varepsilon\}$.  Every $\ell$-ball about $1$ is
therefore a $\tau$-neighbourhood, which is the desired refinement.
\end{proof}

For a $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor with normalized trace $\tau_M$, define on
$G=\PU(M)$
\[
 \ell_1([u])=\inf_{\lambda\in\T}\tau_M(|1-\lambda u|),\qquad
 \ell_2([u])=\inf_{\lambda\in\T}\|1-\lambda u\|_2.
\]
These define the same topology because
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normcompare}
                \ell_1([u])\leq\ell_2([u]),
        \qquad \ell_2([u])^2\leq2\ell_1([u]).
\end{equation}
This is precisely the quotient strong operator topology.  Dowerk--Thom's
normal-generation function for $\PU(M)$ \cite[Theorem~1.3]{DTfinite}
implies \eqref{eq:ung} for $\ell_1$: their bound is locally bounded when
$\ell_1$ is bounded below by a positive constant.

For a type $\mathrm{III}$ factor define
\[
                  \ell_\infty([u])
        =\inf_{\lambda\in\T}\|1-\lambda u\|.
\]
The optimal normal-generation estimate
$2048/\ell_\infty$ from \cite[Theorem~1.3]{DTinfinite} gives
\eqref{eq:ung} for $\ell_\infty$.

We also need two elementary noncompactness facts.  A $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor
contains a trace-preserving diffuse abelian von Neumann algebra and hence
Rademacher symmetries $r_n$.  For $n\neq m$, the symmetry $r_nr_m$ takes the
values $1$ and $-1$, each on a projection of trace $1/2$, and therefore
\[
 \ell_1([r_n^{-1}r_m])
 =\inf_{\lambda\in\T}\frac{|1-\lambda|+|1+\lambda|}{2}=1.
\]
Thus the strong topology on $\PU(M)$ is not precompact.

Every type $\mathrm{III}$ factor also contains a diffuse abelian von Neumann
algebra.  Choose a continuous increasing family of projections
$(p_t)_{t\in(1/4,3/4)}$ there and put $s_t=1-2p_t$.  If $s\neq t$, then
$s_s s_t$ has both $1$ and $-1$ in its spectrum, so
\[
 \ell_\infty([s_s^{-1}s_t])
 =\inf_{\lambda\in\T}\max\{|1-\lambda|,|1+\lambda|\}=\sqrt2.
\]
This uncountable separated family shows that the projective norm topology is
nonseparable and, in particular, not precompact.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sin}]
The groups under consideration are algebraically simple
\cite{DTfinite,DTinfinite}; consequently every nontrivial group topology is
Hausdorff.

In the $\mathrm{II}_1$ case, Lemma~\ref{lem:refine} says that every Hausdorff
SIN topology refines the strong topology.  If the topology is also separable,
invariant automatic continuity \cite[Theorem~1.4]{DTfinite}, applied to the
identity homomorphism from $G$ with its strong topology to $G$ with the new
topology, gives the reverse refinement.  The two topologies coincide.

In the type $\mathrm{III}$ case, Lemma~\ref{lem:refine} says that every
Hausdorff SIN topology refines the projective norm topology.  A countable
dense set for the finer topology would then be dense for the norm topology,
contradicting the uncountable $\sqrt2$-separated family above.

Finally, every Hausdorff precompact group topology is SIN, because it embeds
densely into its compact completion and compact groups are SIN.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:refine} it refines the appropriate canonical length topology.
Precompactness passes to a coarser group topology: for every canonical length
ball $B$, which is open in the finer topology, finitely many translates of
$B$ cover $G$.  This contradicts the separated families above.  Hence no
Hausdorff precompact topology exists.

If $\pi:G\to K$ is a nontrivial homomorphism to a compact Hausdorff group,
algebraic simplicity makes $\pi$ injective.  Pulling the topology of $K$ back
along $\pi$ gives a Hausdorff precompact group topology on $G$, which has just
been ruled out.  Thus $\pi$ is trivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:metric}]
Theorem~\ref{thm:sin} identifies the metric topology with the strong topology.
The latter is induced by the complete bi-invariant projective $2$-metric, so
its left uniformity is complete.  Any compatible left-invariant metric induces
that same left uniformity and is therefore complete.  The type
$\mathrm{III}$ assertion is Theorem~\ref{thm:sin}(2).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:lc}]
By Struble's theorem \cite{Struble}, $H$ admits a proper compatible
left-invariant metric $d$.  Let $G$ act on $(H,d)$ by
$g\cdot h=\pi(g)h$.  This is an isometric action.  Strong uncountable
cofinality for $G$, proved in the source paper \cite[Theorems~1.1 and
1.2]{Dowerk} and inherited by projective quotients, implies that every orbit
of every isometric action is bounded.  Hence $\pi(G)$ is bounded in $H$.
Properness of $d$ makes its closure compact.  The homomorphism into that
compact closure is trivial by Theorem~\ref{thm:sin}(3), so $\pi$ is trivial.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, upgrade attempts, and novelty check}

The source problem remains open for arbitrary separable non-SIN topologies.
The proof identifies two sharp restrictions on any counterexample: its
completion must be non-locally-compact, and the topology cannot be SIN.
Fullness does not bridge this gap: its spectral-gap formulation controls
strong-operator central sequences, whereas an arbitrary neighbourhood need
not have an invariant interior.

Six focused upgrade attempts were made.  The successful ones were the SIN
refinement lemma, the complete SIN classification, and the exclusion of all
locally compact targets.  Attempts to invariantize an arbitrary separable
neighbourhood, use fullness to bound the support size in the known Steinhaus
argument, obtain a countable-index-subgroup contradiction, and compactify an
arbitrary bounded metric completion all fail at the same non-SIN obstruction.
In particular, intersecting all conjugates of a neighbourhood need not leave
interior, and strong uncountable cofinality gives boundedness rather than total
boundedness.

Novelty searches through 17 August 2026 used the exact phrases from the source
question, ``unique separable SIN group topology'', ``projective unitary group
locally compact homomorphism'', the source arXiv id, and citations/keywords for
the two Dowerk--Thom normal-generation papers.  No explicit statement of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sin} or Corollary~\ref{cor:lc} was found.  The result is a
short synthesis of known quantitative theorems, so novelty confidence is
moderate even though proof confidence is high.

\section{Verification and recommendation}

The proof is noncomputational.  The verifier should check: (i) that the cited
normal-generation bounds are uniform on every set $\ell\geq\varepsilon$;
(ii) the direction of both topology refinements in the $\mathrm{II}_1$ case;
(iii) inheritance of SIN and precompactness from compact completions; and
(iv) the use of Struble properness plus abstract strong uncountable
cofinality.  Recommended disposition: human review as a likely-valid,
substantial partial result.
