\begin{abstract}
Ganesh--Bose--Rajagopalan leave as future work the unbounded operator
\(f(B)\) for \(f\in H^2\), where \(B\) is the bilateral shift.  We give a
complete canonical realization.  Under the Fourier model
\(\ell_2(\Z)\simeq L^2(\T)\), \(f(B)\) is the maximal multiplication
operator by the \(L^2\) boundary function of \(f\).  It is densely defined,
closed, normal, and has an explicit adjoint, spectrum, and resolvent.
Finitely supported sequences form a graph core, and on that core the Taylor
power series converges in Hilbert norm and closes to the maximal operator.
Bounded spectral truncations converge on the entire maximal domain.  Thus
both the domain and the required meanings of convergence are fixed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Proof intuition}

The bilateral shift is the easiest possible unitary for unbounded functional
calculus.  Fourier transform sends it to multiplication by the coordinate
\(z\) on \(L^2(\T)\).  An \(H^2\) function has an \(L^2\) boundary value
\(F\), so the only natural maximal definition is multiplication by \(F\) on
the vectors for which that product remains square integrable.

This model resolves the two ambiguities noted in the source.  The domain is
exactly a weighted \(L^2\) space, making closedness, normality, the adjoint,
and the resolvent transparent.  For convergence, Taylor polynomials need not
be controlled on every vector of the maximal domain.  They do converge on
the canonical core of finitely supported vectors, and closing that
power-series operator gives the maximal realization.  On the full domain,
bounded spectral truncations provide unconditional graph convergence.

\section{The maximal operator}

Let \(m\) be normalized Haar measure on \(\T\).  On
\(\ell_2(\Z)\), let \((e_k)_{k\in\Z}\) be the standard basis and
\[
 Be_k=e_{k+1}.
\]
The Fourier unitary
\[
 U:\ell_2(\Z)\longrightarrow L^2(\T,m),
 \qquad Ue_k(z)=z^k,
\]
satisfies \(UBU^{-1}=M_z\).

Let
\[
 f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nz^n\in H^2(\D),
\]
and let \(F\in L^2(\T,m)\) be its radial boundary function.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:def}
 \Dom(A_f)=\{x\in\ell_2(\Z):F\,Ux\in L^2(\T)\},
 \qquad
 A_fx=U^{-1}(F\,Ux).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The operator \(A_f\) in \eqref{eq:def} has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=1.1cm]
\item \(A_f\) is densely defined, closed, and normal, and
\[
 A_f^*=U^{-1}M_{\overline F}U,
 \qquad
 \Dom(A_f^*)=\Dom(A_f).
\]
It is bounded exactly when \(f\in H^\infty\), in which case
\(\norm{A_f}=\norm f_{H^\infty}\).
\item Let \(s_N(z)=\sum_{n=0}^Na_nz^n\).  For every
\(x\in c_{00}(\Z)\),
\[
 \sum_{n=0}^Na_nB^nx
 =U^{-1}(s_N\,Ux)\longrightarrow A_fx
 \quad\text{in }\ell_2(\Z).
\]
Moreover \(c_{00}(\Z)\) is a graph core for \(A_f\).  Consequently the
formal power-series operator on \(c_{00}(\Z)\) is closable and its closure is
exactly \(A_f\).
\item If \(F_j=F\,\mathbf 1_{\{|F|\le j\}}\), then
\[
 A_{F_j}:=U^{-1}M_{F_j}U\in\mathcal B(\ell_2(\Z))
\]
and \(A_{F_j}x\to A_fx\) for every \(x\in\Dom(A_f)\).
\item The resolvent and spectrum are
\[
 \lambda\in\rho(A_f)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \essinf_{\zeta\in\T}|F(\zeta)-\lambda|>0,
 \qquad
 \sigma(A_f)=\essran F.
\]
When \(\lambda\in\rho(A_f)\),
\[
 (A_f-\lambda I)^{-1}
 =U^{-1}M_{1/(F-\lambda)}U.
\]
\item Every \(e_k\) lies in \(\Dom(A_f)\), and
\[
 A_fe_k=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_ne_{k+n},
 \qquad
 \norm{A_fe_k}=\norm f_{H^2}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under \(U\), the operator \(A_f\) is the maximal multiplication operator
\(M_F\).  Its graph norm is
\[
 \norm h_{M_F}^2
 =\norm h_2^2+\norm{Fh}_2^2
 =\int_{\T}|h|^2(1+|F|^2)\,dm.
\]
The measure \(d\mu=(1+|F|^2)\,dm\) is finite.  Bounded functions lie in
\(\Dom(M_F)\) and are dense in \(L^2(m)\), so the domain is dense.
Completeness of \(L^2(\mu)\) proves that \(M_F\) is closed.

For \(g,h\) in the appropriate domains,
\[
 \langle M_Fg,h\rangle=\langle g,M_{\overline F}h\rangle.
\]
The standard maximal-domain argument gives
\(M_F^*=M_{\overline F}\).  Since
\(\Dom(M_F)=\Dom(M_{\overline F})\) and
\(M_FM_{\overline F}=M_{\overline F}M_F=M_{|F|^2}\), the operator is
normal.  A multiplication operator is bounded exactly when its multiplier
is essentially bounded, with norm \(\norm F_\infty\).  The Hardy boundary
theorem identifies \(F\in L^\infty\) with \(f\in H^\infty\) and preserves
the norm \cite{Duren}.

Now take \(x\in c_{00}(\Z)\).  Then \(p=Ux\) is a bounded trigonometric
polynomial.  Taylor convergence in \(H^2\) gives
\[
 \norm{(s_N-F)p}_2
 \le \norm p_\infty\norm{s_N-F}_2\longrightarrow0,
\]
which proves the asserted power-series convergence.

It remains to prove that this small domain is a core.  Trigonometric
polynomials are dense in \(L^2(\mu)\): continuous functions are dense for
the finite regular Borel measure \(\mu\), and the trigonometric polynomials
are uniformly dense in \(C(\T)\).  Since the \(L^2(\mu)\)-norm is precisely
the graph norm of \(M_F\), the inverse Fourier image \(c_{00}(\Z)\) is graph
dense in \(\Dom(A_f)\).  The closure claim follows.

For the spectral truncations and \(h\in\Dom(M_F)\),
\[
 \norm{(F_j-F)h}_2^2
 =\int_{\{|F|>j\}}|Fh|^2\,dm\longrightarrow0.
\]
This proves full-domain convergence in (iii).

Finally, \(M_F-\lambda I=M_{F-\lambda}\).  It has an everywhere-defined
bounded inverse exactly when \(1/(F-\lambda)\in L^\infty\), equivalently
when the displayed essential infimum is positive.  This is precisely the
complement of the essential range, proving (iv).  For (v), \(Ue_k=z^k\), so
\[
 Fz^k=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nz^{n+k}
\quad\text{in }L^2(\T),
\]
and Parseval gives the norm identity.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences and convergence boundary}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:invert}
If \(f\ne0\), then \(A_f\) is injective and has dense range.  It is
surjective with a bounded inverse in the closed-operator sense exactly when
\(1/F\in L^\infty(\T)\); then
\[
 A_f^{-1}=U^{-1}M_{1/F}U.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A nonzero \(H^2\) function has nonzero boundary values almost everywhere
\cite{Duren}.  Hence both \(\ker M_F\) and
\(\ker M_{\overline F}=\ker M_F^*\) vanish, proving injectivity and dense
range.  The final assertion is Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(iv) with
\(\lambda=0\).
\end{proof}

The theorem also marks a necessary limitation.  From \(f\in H^2\) alone,
Taylor partial sums are guaranteed to converge on the graph core, not on
every vector of \(\Dom(A_f)\).  Indeed, as maps from the graph-norm domain
to \(L^2(\T)\), their norms are
\[
 \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\T}
 \frac{|s_N|}{\sqrt{1+|F|^2}}.
\]
Whole-domain pointwise convergence would force these quantities to be
uniformly bounded by the uniform boundedness principle.  No such estimate
follows from \(H^2\) membership.  The bounded spectral truncations in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(iii) give the unconditional full-domain replacement.

For stationary-process notation, part~(v) is the essential concrete fact:
the white-noise coordinate vectors are in the domain, and the moving-average
series converges in mean-square geometry exactly because
\((a_n)\in\ell_2\).  Thus the unbounded operator is not merely formal even
though it cannot act on all of \(\ell_2(\Z)\).

\section{Verification, limitations, and novelty}

The proof is unitary-model independent up to the harmless convention
\(Be_k=e_{k+1}\); reversing the shift replaces \(F(z)\) by \(F(\overline z)\).
All domain equalities use maximal multiplication domains.  The graph-core
step was separately checked through the finite weighted measure
\((1+|F|^2)dm\), and the resolvent formula includes the required verification
that \(F/(F-\lambda)=1+\lambda/(F-\lambda)\) is bounded whenever the inverse
multiplier is bounded.

A bounded search on August 13, 2026 covered the run indexes, arXiv:2604.02336,
the exact future-work sentence, and combinations of \(H^2\), unbounded
\(f(B)\), bilateral shift, maximal multiplication operator, graph core, and
unbounded Toeplitz operator.  It found standard accounts of unbounded
multiplication operators and a broader unbounded-Toeplitz literature, but no
later paper explicitly answering this source item and no exact
bilateral-shift domain/core result tied to it.  The result should therefore
be read as a self-contained application and synthesis of classical tools,
with low-to-moderate novelty confidence.  It does not claim universal
Taylor convergence on the maximal domain or analyze stochastic questions
beyond the Hilbert-space moving-average action.
