{\color{navy}\Large\bfseries Low-dimensional Laguerre extremality for mixed
characteristic polynomials}\par
\vspace{0.2em}
{\large A global theorem in dimensions two and three, plus balanced local
stability in every dimension}\par
\vspace{0.35em}
\textbf{Status.} Candidate partial result; human review requested.\hfill
\textbf{Run.} \texttt{fa\_banach\_001}, lane 15

\textbf{Source target.} Marcus--Srivastava, \emph{The Solution of the
Kadison--Singer Problem}, arXiv:1712.08874.  The lecture notes leave an
associated-Laguerre extremality assertion unproved:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.94\linewidth]{figures/lecture_extremality_crop.png}
\end{center}

The precise conjecture appears in Marcus--Spielman--Srivastava,
\emph{Interlacing Families II}.  For positive semidefinite \(d\times d\)
matrices satisfying
\[
  \sum_{i=1}^m A_i=I_d,\qquad \tr A_i\leq\varepsilon,
\]
it proposes that the largest zero of
\[
 \mcp(x)=
 \left.\prod_{i=1}^m(1-\partial_{z_i})
 \det\!\left(xI_d+\sum_{i=1}^m z_iA_i\right)\right|_{z=0}
\]
is maximized by the \emph{capped scalar packing}: as many matrices as possible
equal \((\varepsilon/d)I_d\), one residual scalar matrix, and the rest zero.

\resultbox{\textbf{Results.} The conjecture is globally true for \(d=2\) and
\(d=3\), for every feasible \(m,\varepsilon\).  Moreover, in every dimension,
when \(m\ge d\) and \(\varepsilon=d/m\), the balanced scalar tuple
\(A_i=I_d/m\) is a strict local maximizer along every nonzero affine feasible
direction.}

\begin{theorem}[Global low-dimensional extremality]\label{thm:global}
For \(d\in\{2,3\}\), the capped scalar packing maximizes the largest zero of
\(\mcp\) among all feasible positive semidefinite tuples.
\end{theorem}

\textbf{Trace packing.}
Put \(t_i=\tr A_i\).  The vector
\[
 p=(\underbrace{\varepsilon,\ldots,\varepsilon}_{q\text{ entries}},r,0,\ldots,0),
 \qquad q=\lfloor d/\varepsilon\rfloor,\quad r=d-q\varepsilon,
\]
majorizes every vector with \(0\le t_i\le\varepsilon\) and
\(\sum_i t_i=d\).  Hence it simultaneously maximizes every convex power sum
\(\sum_i t_i^s\), \(s\ge1\).

\textbf{Dimension two.}
The determinant expansion and \(\sum_iA_i=I_2\) give
\[
 \mcp(x)=x^2-2x+1-\sum_i\det A_i.
\]
Its largest zero is \(1+\sqrt{\sum_i\det A_i}\).  If \(A\succeq0\) has trace
\(t\), then
\[
 \det A\leq t^2/4,
\]
with equality precisely at \(A=(t/2)I_2\).  The trace-packing observation now
proves the claim.

\textbf{Dimension three.}
Let \(e_k(A)\) be the \(k\)-th elementary symmetric function of the eigenvalues
and set
\[
 S_2=\sum_i e_2(A_i),\qquad S_3=\sum_i\det A_i.
\]
The quadratic coefficient is \(3-S_2\).  To find the constant coefficient,
expand \(\det(\sum_iA_i)=1\).  For each fixed \(i\), the terms with label \(i\)
twice and another label once sum to
\[
 \tr\!\bigl(\operatorname{adj}(A_i)(I-A_i)\bigr)
 =e_2(A_i)-3\det A_i.
\]
Together with the all-\(i\) term \(\det A_i\), all repeated-label terms sum to
\(S_2-2S_3\).  Thus the distinct-label mixed discriminant is
\(1-S_2+2S_3\), and the exact shifted identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic}
 \mcp(1+y)=y^3-S_2y-2S_3.
\end{equation}

For \(A\succeq0\) of trace \(t\), Maclaurin's inequalities give
\[
 e_2(A)\leq t^2/3,\qquad \det A\leq t^3/27,
\]
with simultaneous equality exactly at \(A=(t/3)I_3\).  The capped trace
packing therefore simultaneously maximizes \(S_2\) and \(S_3\).  Finally, if
\(a,b\ge0\) increase in \(y^3-ay-2b\), evaluating the new polynomial at the
old nonnegative largest zero gives a nonpositive value.  Its new largest zero
cannot be smaller.  This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:global}.

\begin{theorem}[Balanced local stability]\label{thm:local}
Let \(m\ge d\ge2\), \(c=1/m\), and
\[
 A_i(t)=cI_d+tH_i,\qquad H_i=H_i^*,\quad \tr H_i=0,\quad \sum_iH_i=0.
\]
For sufficiently small \(t\), let \(\lambda(t)\) be the largest zero of the
corresponding mixed characteristic polynomial.  If some \(H_i\ne0\), then
\[
 \lambda(t)=\lambda(0)-c_Ht^2+O(t^3),\qquad c_H>0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 P_{a,b}(x)=(1-c\,\partial_x)^b x^a.
\]
For the source's normalized mixed discriminants \(D\), multilinearity and
\[
 D(H,K)=\tr H\,\tr K-\tr(HK)
\]
show that every linear perturbation term vanishes.  In the coefficient indexed
by a \(k\)-element subset, the quadratic terms equal
\[
 c^{k-2}(d-2)_{k-2}\sum_{i<j}-\tr(H_iH_j).
\]
Each pair occurs in \(\binom{m-2}{k-2}\) subsets, while
\[
 \sum_{i<j}\tr(H_iH_j)=-\frac12\sum_i\tr(H_i^2).
\]
Consequently, coefficient by coefficient,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variation}
 \mu_t(x)=P_{d,m}(x)+\frac{t^2}{2}
 \left(\sum_i\tr(H_i^2)\right)P_{d-2,m-2}(x)+O(t^3).
\end{equation}

The two \(P\)'s are monic scaled associated Laguerre polynomials of degrees
\(d\) and \(d-2\), with the same parameter \(m-d\ge0\).  Their zeros strictly
interlace.  If \(\lambda_0\) is the largest zero of \(P_{d,m}\), then
\[
 P'_{d,m}(\lambda_0)>0,
 \qquad P_{d-2,m-2}(\lambda_0)>0.
\]
The largest zero is simple, so the implicit-function theorem and
\eqref{eq:variation} yield
\[
 c_H=
 \frac{\bigl(\sum_i\tr(H_i^2)\bigr)P_{d-2,m-2}(\lambda_0)}
 {2P'_{d,m}(\lambda_0)}>0.
\]
\end{proof}

\textbf{Why this does not yet prove the conjecture in \(d=4\).}
In dimension four, the same shift has the form
\[
 \mcp(1+y)=y^4-S_2y^2-2S_3y+C,
\]
where \(C\) contains both \(-3\sum_i\det A_i\) and positive \((2,2)\)
mixed-discriminant terms coupling distinct matrices.  The capped scalar tuple
maximizes \(S_2,S_3\), but it need not minimize \(C\): the verifier exhibits
negative scalar-comparison gaps for this coefficient.  Thus the global cubic
argument cannot be extended coefficientwise.  A proof in \(d\ge4\) needs a
new coupled inequality evaluated at the candidate Laguerre root.

\textbf{Upgrade audit.}
Five focused routes were completed: exact source/duplicate triage; a direct
determinant evaluator and low-dimensional counterexample search; the global
\(d=2,3\) invariant proof; the failed but diagnostic \(d=4\) coefficient
comparison; and the all-dimensional balanced second variation.  The strongest
reproducible run checked 2,700 normalized commuting, rank-one, and full-rank
tuples in dimensions three and four with no root violation.  This computation
is corroborative only.

\textbf{Scope.}
The global conjecture remains open in dimension four and higher.  Bounded run
index and external exact-phrase/keyword searches found the 2015 conjecture and
adjacent work, but no source stating the two low-dimensional cases or the
balanced Hessian formula.  Novelty is plausible but requires specialist
review because the \(d\le3\) proof becomes elementary after the correct shift.
