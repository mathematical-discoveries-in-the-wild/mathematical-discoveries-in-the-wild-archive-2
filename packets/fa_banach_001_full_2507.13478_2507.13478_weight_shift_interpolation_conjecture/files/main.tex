For every parameter allowed in Remark 4.9, the two complex interpolation

\section{The question}

Let $p\in(1,\infty)$, let $X$ be a UMD Banach space, and put
$w_\gamma(x)=x_1^\gamma$ on the half-space
$\R^d_+=\{x_1>0\}$.  Lindemulder, Lorist, Roodenburg, and
Veraar~\cite{source}, Remark 4.9, conjecture that for
\[
 p-1<\gamma<2p-1,
 \qquad
 0<\theta<\frac13\left(1+\frac{\gamma+1}{p}\right),
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:question}
 [L^p(w_\gamma;X),W^{3,p}_{\Dir}(w_\gamma;X)]_\theta
 =
 [L^p(w_\gamma;X),W^{3,p}_0(w_\gamma;X)]_\theta .
\end{equation}
Here and below all spaces are on $\R^d_+$, and equality means equality of
Banach spaces with equivalent norms.

For the present range of $\gamma$, the trace characterisations in the source
reduce to
\begin{align*}
 W^{3,p}_{\Dir}(w_\gamma;X)
   &=\{f\in W^{3,p}(w_\gamma;X):\Tr_0f=0\},\\
 W^{3,p}_0(w_\gamma;X)
   &=\{f\in W^{3,p}(w_\gamma;X):\Tr_0f=\Tr_1f=0\}.
\end{align*}
Only normal traces are displayed: the tangential first-order traces vanish
automatically when $\Tr_0f=0$.  We write
$\Tr_jf=(\partial_1^jf)|_{x_1=0}$.

\section{Proof intuition}

The missing interval is difficult only because $w_\gamma$ lies outside the
Muckenhoupt range, where fractional Sobolev interpolation is not intrinsically
identified.  Multiplication by $x_1$ changes $w_\gamma$ into
$w_{\gamma-p}$.  It also shifts boundary jets by one order:
Dirichlet data become two zero normal traces, and full-zero data become three.

The latter endpoint shift is a known isomorphism.  For the former, the only
issue is surjectivity.  Given a lower-weight function whose first two traces
vanish, lift its second trace at the original weight, multiply that lift by
$x_1$, and subtract.  The remainder has all three lower-weight traces zero and
can be divided by $x_1$ using the full-zero isomorphism.

After this endpoint identification, both couples live at the Muckenhoupt
weight.  Roodenburg's theorem says that an order-$m$ boundary condition appears
at intermediate smoothness $s$ only when $s>m+(\beta+1)/p$.  Hence the two
systems agree until the order-two trace appears.  That appearance occurs at
exactly the source's upper threshold.

\section{The endpoint weight shift}

Set
\[
 \beta:=\gamma-p,\qquad
 a:=\frac{\beta+1}{p}=\frac{\gamma+1}{p}-1.
\]
Then $-1<\beta<p-1$ and $0<a<1$.  Define the lower-weight trace kernels
\begin{align*}
 E_{01}&:=\{g\in W^{3,p}(w_\beta;X):\Tr_0g=\Tr_1g=0\},\\
 E_{012}&:=\{g\in W^{3,p}(w_\beta;X):
                  \Tr_0g=\Tr_1g=\Tr_2g=0\}.
\end{align*}
The trace characterisation gives $E_{012}=W^{3,p}_0(w_\beta;X)$.

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint shift]\label{lem:shift}
For $(\M f)(x)=x_1f(x)$, the following are Banach-space isomorphisms:
\begin{align*}
 \M &:L^p(w_\gamma;X)\longrightarrow L^p(w_\beta;X),\\
 \M &:W^{3,p}_{\Dir}(w_\gamma;X)\longrightarrow E_{01},\\
 \M &:W^{3,p}_0(w_\gamma;X)\longrightarrow E_{012}.
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first map is an isometry because
\[
 \|\M f\|_{L^p(w_\beta)}^p
 =\int_{\R^d_+}|x_1f(x)|^p x_1^{\gamma-p}\,dx
 =\|f\|_{L^p(w_\gamma)}^p.
\]
The third map is precisely the multiplication isomorphism on full-zero
weighted Sobolev spaces~\cite[Lemma 3.6(ii)]{halfspace}.

For the second map, boundedness into $W^{3,p}(w_\beta;X)$ follows from
\cite[Lemma 3.6(i)]{halfspace}.  The product rule gives, initially on smooth
functions and then by trace continuity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-shift}
 \Tr_0(\M f)=0,\qquad
 \Tr_1(\M f)=\Tr_0f,\qquad
 \Tr_2(\M f)=2\Tr_1f.
\end{equation}
Thus $\M$ maps the Dirichlet endpoint into $E_{01}$.

It remains to prove surjectivity with a bounded inverse.  Let $g\in E_{01}$
and put $q=\Tr_2g$.  The lower-weight trace theorem gives
\[
 q\in B^{,3-2-a}_{p,p}(\R^{d-1};X)
   =B^{,2-(\gamma+1)/p}_{p,p}(\R^{d-1};X).
\]
At the original weight, the universal right inverse for the first normal
trace~\cite[Theorem 4.6]{interpolation} therefore supplies
\[
 e:=\tfrac12\operatorname{ext}_1q\in W^{3,p}(w_\gamma;X),
 \qquad \Tr_0e=0,\quad \Tr_1e=q/2,
\]
with $\|e\|_{W^{3,p}(w_\gamma)}\lesssim\|q\|_{B^{1-a}_{p,p}}$.
By \eqref{eq:trace-shift}, the function $h:=g-\M e$ satisfies
$\Tr_0h=\Tr_1h=\Tr_2h=0$, so $h\in E_{012}$.  The third isomorphism yields a
unique $f_0\in W^{3,p}_0(w_\gamma;X)$ such that $\M f_0=h$.  Consequently
\[
 f:=f_0+e\in W^{3,p}_{\Dir}(w_\gamma;X),
 \qquad \M f=g.
\]
The continuity of $\Tr_2$, of $\operatorname{ext}_1$, of $\M$, and of the
inverse on the full-zero spaces gives
$\|f\|_{W^{3,p}(w_\gamma)}\lesssim\|g\|_{W^{3,p}(w_\beta)}$.
This proves the asserted isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\section{The interpolation identity}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $p\in(1,\infty)$, $p-1<\gamma<2p-1$, and let $X$ be a UMD Banach space.
Then \eqref{eq:question} holds for every
\[
 0<\theta<\frac13\left(1+\frac{\gamma+1}{p}\right).
\]
Thus the conjecture in Remark 4.9 of the source is true.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:shift} and functoriality of complex interpolation, applying
$\M$ to the left and right sides of \eqref{eq:question} gives respectively
\begin{align}
 [L^p(w_\beta;X),E_{01}]_\theta,
 \qquad
 [L^p(w_\beta;X),E_{012}]_\theta.\label{eq:shifted-couples}
\end{align}
Because $-1<\beta<p-1$ and $X$ is UMD, integer-order weighted Bessel-potential
and Sobolev spaces coincide.  We may therefore apply Roodenburg's complex
interpolation theorem for weighted Bessel-potential spaces with normal
boundary systems~\cite[Theorem 6.4]{interpolation}, with
$s_0=0$, $s_1=3$, and $s_\theta=3\theta$.  Its lower-index hypothesis holds
because $0>-1+a$.

First suppose
\[
 3\theta\notin\{a,1+a,2+a\}.
\]
The theorem identifies the first space in \eqref{eq:shifted-couples} with
the lower-weight Bessel-potential space of smoothness $3\theta$ carrying
the trace conditions among $\Tr_0,\Tr_1$ for which
\[
 j+a<3\theta.
\]
It identifies the second space in exactly the same way, except that
$\Tr_2$ is also available at the top endpoint.  But the assumed parameter
range is
\[
 3\theta<1+\frac{\gamma+1}{p}=2+a.
\]
Hence the condition $\Tr_2=0$ has not yet appeared.  The two intermediate
spaces, with their norms, are therefore identical.

Only the critical values $3\theta=a$ and $3\theta=1+a$ remain inside the open
interval.  Fix either such $\theta_0$.  Choose noncritical
$0<r<\theta_0<s<(2+a)/3$ and $\lambda\in(0,1)$ with
$\theta_0=(1-\lambda)r+\lambda s$.  If $I_{01}(t)$ and $I_{012}(t)$ denote
the two shifted interpolation scales, the preceding paragraph gives
$I_{01}(r)=I_{012}(r)$ and $I_{01}(s)=I_{012}(s)$.  Exact complex
reiteration gives
\[
 I_{01}(\theta_0)
 =[I_{01}(r),I_{01}(s)]_\lambda
 =[I_{012}(r),I_{012}(s)]_\lambda
 =I_{012}(\theta_0).
\]
The couples are regular: smooth functions supported in the open half-space
belong to both top endpoints and are dense in the common $L^p$ endpoint.
Thus reiteration applies.  Pulling the equality back through $\M$ completes
the proof for all $\theta$ in the claimed interval.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, novelty, and review priorities}

The theorem answers exactly the half-space interpolation conjecture displayed
in Remark 4.9.  It does not address the source's separate questions about
negative smoothness, Lipschitz domains, or the unshifted Laplacian at
$\mu=0$.  No endpoint assertion is made at
$3\theta=1+(\gamma+1)/p$, where the second normal trace reaches its critical
smoothness.

The local run indexes were searched by arXiv id, title, and core terms.
External exact-phrase, title/citation, author-page, and arXiv searches through
12 August 2026 found no later paper explicitly resolving equation (4.3).
The source already cites both ingredients used here, but does not record the
endpoint identification in Lemma~\ref{lem:shift}.  Novelty is therefore
plausible, not certified.  Human review should focus first on the surjectivity
argument in Lemma~\ref{lem:shift}, and second on reiteration at the two critical
trace indices.
