For each $n\geq1$, the smooth, even, strongly convex functions

\]

\section*{The source conjecture}
For a convex function $\psi:\R^n\to\R$ with $0<\int e^{-\psi}<\infty$, Li, Sch\"utt, and Werner define the affine surface area of $f=e^{-\psi}$ by
\[
 \asa(f)=\int_{\R^n}\bigl(\det\nabla^2\psi(x)\bigr)^{1/(n+2)}e^{-\psi(x)}\,dx.
\]
After proving the inequality for $2$-homogeneous $\psi$, they conjecture that it holds for every convex $\psi$ \cite{Source}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conjecture}
 \asa(f)\leq (2\pi)^{n/(n+2)}
 \left(\int_{\R^n}e^{-\psi(x)}\,dx\right)^{n/(n+2)}.
\end{equation}

We give a variational counterexample that retains the most natural normalizations: $\psi$ is even, $C^\infty$, strongly convex, and has minimum zero at the origin.

\section*{A quartic perturbation of the Gaussian}
Fix $n\geq1$.  For $\varepsilon\geq0$, let
\[
 \psi_\varepsilon(x)=\frac{|x|^2}{2}+\varepsilon x_1^4,
 \qquad
 Z(\varepsilon)=\int_{\R^n}e^{-\psi_\varepsilon(x)}\,dx.
\]
Since
\[
 \nabla^2\psi_\varepsilon(x)
 =\operatorname{diag}(1+12\varepsilon x_1^2,1,\ldots,1),
\]
the corresponding affine surface area is
\[
 A(\varepsilon)
 =\int_{\R^n}(1+12\varepsilon x_1^2)^{1/(n+2)}
 e^{-|x|^2/2-\varepsilon x_1^4}\,dx.
\]
Normalize the two sides of \eqref{eq:conjecture} by setting
\[
 R(\varepsilon)=
 \frac{A(\varepsilon)}{(2\pi)^{n/(n+2)}Z(\varepsilon)^{n/(n+2)}}.
\]
The conjecture asserts $R(\varepsilon)\leq1$.  At $\varepsilon=0$,
\[
 A(0)=Z(0)=(2\pi)^{n/2},
 \qquad R(0)=1.
\]

Both integrals are right-differentiable at zero.  This follows directly from dominated convergence: on any bounded interval of nonnegative $\varepsilon$, the differentiated integrands are bounded by a fixed polynomial times $e^{-|x|^2/2}$.  If $G$ is a standard Gaussian vector, then $\E G_1^2=1$ and $\E G_1^4=3$, and hence
\begin{align*}
 \frac{Z'(0)}{Z(0)}
 &=-\E G_1^4=-3,\\
 \frac{A'(0)}{A(0)}
 &=\E\left(\frac{12}{n+2}G_1^2-G_1^4\right)
 =\frac{12}{n+2}-3.
\end{align*}
It follows that
\begin{align*}
 \left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\log R(\varepsilon)\right|_{0+}
 &=\frac{A'(0)}{A(0)}-\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{Z'(0)}{Z(0)}\\
 &=\frac{12}{n+2}-3+\frac{3n}{n+2}
 =\frac{6}{n+2}>0.
\end{align*}
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expansion}
 R(\varepsilon)=1+\frac{6}{n+2}\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)
 \qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}
For every $n\geq1$, there exists $\varepsilon_n>0$ such that every $0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_n$ gives a smooth, even, strongly convex function
$\psi_\varepsilon(x)=|x|^2/2+\varepsilon x_1^4$ for which
\[
 \asa(e^{-\psi_\varepsilon})>
 (2\pi)^{n/(n+2)}
 \left(\int_{\R^n}e^{-\psi_\varepsilon(x)}\,dx\right)^{n/(n+2)}.
\]
Consequently, the conjecture following Corollary~1 of the source is false in every dimension.
\end{theorem}

Indeed, the conclusion follows immediately from \eqref{eq:expansion}.  Notice that $\psi_\varepsilon(0)=\min\psi_\varepsilon=0$, so the failure cannot be removed merely by fixing the additive constant in the potential.

\section*{A still simpler literal obstruction}
The formulation for general convex functions is also incompatible with additive constants.  Let
\[
 \psi_a(x)=\frac{|x|^2}{2}+a.
\]
Its normalized ratio is
\[
 R_a=e^{-2a/(n+2)}.
\]
Thus every $a<0$ already violates \eqref{eq:conjecture}.  In the source's proved corollary, $2$-homogeneity forces $a=0$ despite the displayed equality description allowing an additive constant.  The quartic family above is the substantive obstruction: it remains after imposing minimum zero and changes the shape rather than the height of the Gaussian.

\section*{Adversarial checks and scope}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item The Hessian is positive definite everywhere for $\varepsilon\geq0$, so no generalized-Hessian or boundary convention is involved.
\item The perturbation is integrable and all differentiated integrands admit Gaussian domination.  The proof uses an exact first variation, not a numerical approximation.
\item Equation \eqref{eq:expansion} is strict in the forbidden direction, so continuity supplies actual positive parameters, not only a formal infinitesimal obstruction.
\item The example is even and centered.  It therefore also survives the common barycenter-at-zero hypothesis used in related functional affine inequalities.
\item The counterexample concerns the source's new affine surface area, whose weight is $e^{-\psi}$.  It does not contradict the different functional affine isoperimetric inequality for expression (16) in the source.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Bounded novelty check}

This is evidence for novelty, not a definitive guarantee.
