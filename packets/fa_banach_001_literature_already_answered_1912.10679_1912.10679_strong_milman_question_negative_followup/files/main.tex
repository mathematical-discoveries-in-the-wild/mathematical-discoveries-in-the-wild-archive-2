\section*{Exact source question}

Question 5.2 on PDF page 20 of Gowers--Wyczesany,
\emph{High-dimensional tennis balls} (arXiv:1912.10679), asks the following.
Fix $k\in\mathbb N$, $C\geq1$, and $\varepsilon>0$.  Does some dimension
$n$ guarantee that every norm $\vertiii{\cdot}$ on $\mathbb R^n$ satisfying
\[
 \lVert x\rVert_2\leq\vertiii{x}\leq C\lVert x\rVert_2
\]
has a $k$-dimensional subspace $U$ for which, for some $\beta>0$,
\[
 \beta\lVert u\rVert_2\leq\vertiii{u}\leq
 \beta(1+\varepsilon)\lVert u\rVert_2 \quad(u\in U),
\]
and the Euclidean orthogonal projection $P_U$ has operator norm at most
$1+\varepsilon$ in $\vertiii{\cdot}$?  In the terminology of the follow-up,
$U$ must be both strongly $(1+\varepsilon)$-Euclidean and strongly
$(1+\varepsilon)$-complemented.  Source PDF page 21 announces that a follow-up
will answer this question negatively.

\section*{The later answer}

The announced follow-up is Gowers--Wyczesany,
\emph{A counterexample to a strengthening of a question of Milman}
(arXiv:2110.03023).  It restates the question as Question 1.3 and proves on
PDF page 2:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Theorem 1.4.}
There are constants $\varepsilon>0$ and $C$ such that, for every sufficiently
large $n$, an $n$-dimensional $C$-Euclidean normed space exists with no
two-dimensional subspace that is both strongly
$(1+\varepsilon)$-Euclidean and strongly
$(1+\varepsilon)$-complemented.
\medskip

Taking $k=2$ directly negates the universal assertion in Question 5.2.
The later paper even supplies explicit final constants (its concluding proof
uses $\varepsilon=2^{-1017}$), but the existence statement of Theorem 1.4 is
already an exact answer.

\section*{Scope warning}

Question 5.1 of arXiv:1912.10679 asks the weaker, coordinate-free Milman
question using arbitrary near-isometries and arbitrary projections.  The
follow-up explicitly says it does not answer that weaker question.  Therefore
this identification closes Question 5.2 only; it makes no claim about
Question 5.1 or the paper's broader topological-subsphere questions.
