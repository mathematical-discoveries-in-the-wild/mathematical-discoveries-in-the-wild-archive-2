\section*{The original future-work direction}

In its concluding paragraph, the original paper says that it would be
interesting to extend its projector results to underrelaxed projectors or
even proximal mappings \cite[p.~25]{ABRW2021}.  It separately asks for an
algorithm computing the difference vector without Seeger's algorithm.  The
two requests must not be conflated.

The paper cited there as initial progress is arXiv:2101.05857
\cite{ABW2021}.  Its theorem-level contents show that it carries out the
proposed proximal-mapping extension, not merely a preliminary special case.

\section*{The later proximal theory}

Let $X$ be a real Hilbert space, let $f_i\in\Gamma_0(X)$ for
$1\leq i\leq m$, put $\mathbf X=X^m$ and
$f=f_1\oplus\cdots\oplus f_m$, and let $R$ be the circular right shift on
$\mathbf X$.  A classical proximal cycle is a vector $z$ satisfying
\[
 z=\Prox_f(Rz),\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
 0\in\partial f(z)+(\Id-R)z.
\]
The later paper systematically studies these cycles, their gap vectors, and
the fixed-point sets of the cyclic proximal compositions.  In particular,
when the classical gap vector $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_m)$ exists, the successive
fixed-point sets are translations by its coordinates.

More importantly, the theory remains meaningful when classical cycles do
not exist.  Write $\Delta$ for the diagonal in $\mathbf X$ and set
\[
 g=\cl(f\mathbin{\square}\ii_\Delta).
\]
Theorem 4.2 (PDF pp.~13--14) proves that the generalized primal and dual
problems
\[
 0\in\partial g(x)+(\Id-R)x,\quad x\in\Delta^\perp,
\]
and
\[
 0\in\partial(f^*+\ii_\Delta^*)(y)+\tfrac12y+Ty
\]
have unique solutions (where $T$ is the skew linear operator associated
with the shift in that paper).  These are the generalized cycle and gap
vector.  Theorems 4.3 and 4.4 (PDF pp.~14--15) show precisely how this
construction recovers classical proximal cycles whenever they exist.

Theorem 7.2 (PDF pp.~23--24) also supplies a forward--backward algorithm.
For $0<\gamma<1$ and standard relaxation parameters, it iterates
\[
 q_n=(1-\gamma)x_n+\gamma Rx_n,
 \qquad
 x_{n+1}=x_n+\lambda_n\bigl(\Prox_{\gamma g}q_n-x_n\bigr).
\]
It proves weak convergence to a generalized cycle and strong convergence of
$Rx_n-x_n$ to the unique generalized gap vector.  Thus the later paper
provides structural, duality, existence, recovery, and computational results
for cyclic compositions of proximal mappings at the same level at which the
original paper treated projectors.

\section*{Why the algorithmic question remains outside this answer}

For projectors onto $C_1,\ldots,C_m$, take
$f=\ii_C$ with $C=C_1\times\cdots\times C_m$.  Then
\[
 g=\cl(\ii_C\mathbin{\square}\ii_\Delta)
   =\ii_{\overline{C+\Delta}},
 \qquad
 \Prox_{\gamma g}=P_{\overline{C+\Delta}}.
\]
Consequently, the later forward--backward scheme still calls the projector
onto $\overline{C+\Delta}$.  The original paper's difficulty was exactly the
lack of an explicit formula for this projector, for which it invoked
Seeger's algorithm.  The proximal extension therefore does not, by itself,
give the requested Seeger-free projector algorithm.

The proper conclusion is scoped: the proximal-mapping extension proposed on
page 25 is answered by arXiv:2101.05857; the adjacent explicit-algorithm
question is not claimed to be resolved.  The original paper itself cites the
later work as promising initial progress, and the present status assignment
comes from checking its subsequent theorems.  This packet makes no new
mathematical claim.
