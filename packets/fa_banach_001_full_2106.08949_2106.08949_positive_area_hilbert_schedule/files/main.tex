\paragraph{Source problem.}
For \(a>0\), put
\[
  w_s(a)=1+\frac{a}{\sqrt{s}},\qquad s\geq1,
\]
and let \(B_{w(a)}\) be the associated weighted backward shift.  Does there
exist a positive-Lebesgue-measure set \(\Lambda\subset\R^2\) such that
\[
 \bigl(B_{w(\lambda)}\times B_{w(\mu)}\bigr)_{(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda}
\]
has a common hypercyclic vector on \(c_0\times c_0\)?  The question appears
on page 6 of the current arXiv PDF.

\section*{Proof intuition}

The logarithm of the first \(n\) weights grows like \(2a\sqrt n\).  If a
later parameter tag moves backwards by \(\delta\), the unwanted contribution
has exponent of the form
\[
 C\delta\sqrt{n_j}-c\sqrt{n_j-n_k}.
\]
Thus a backward move is harmless when
\(\delta^2\lesssim(n_j-n_k)/n_j\).  This is a \emph{quadratic} cost.

A Hilbert space-filling curve has exactly the matching regularity: parameter
time \(r^2\) fills a square of side \(r\).  We partition its unit time
interval into 4-adic pieces of length comparable to \(1/n_j\), where the
visit times \(n_j\) form an arithmetic progression.  Their lengths have a
divergent harmonic sum, so finitely many pieces cover the whole Hilbert
interval.  Hilbert's \(1/2\)-Hölder estimate gives the quadratic cost above,
while each image square has precisely the \(n_j^{-1/2}\) size required by the
common-hypercyclicity characterization.

\section*{The exact characterization}

Set
\[
 f_n(a)=\sum_{s=1}^n\log\left(1+\frac a{\sqrt s}\right),\qquad f_0=0.
\]
Theorem 2.1 of Bayart--Costa--Menet~\cite{BayartCostaMenet} applies to this
family on any compact positive interval.  Specialized to \(c_0^2\), it is
enough to prove the following for every \(\tau>0\), \(N\geq1\), and
\(\varepsilon>0\): there are finitely many tags
\(\lambda_h=(a_h,b_h)\) and integers
\[
 N\leq n_1<n_1+N\leq n_2<\cdots<n_q
\]
whose rectangles of side \(\tau/\lceil\sqrt{n_h}\rceil\), ending at
\(\lambda_h\), cover the parameter set, and such that, for each coordinate,
\begin{align}
 \max_h e^{-f_{n_h}(a_h)}&<\varepsilon,
 \label{eq:direct}\\
 \max_{j>k}
 \exp\!\left(
 f_{n_j+l}(a_k)-f_{n_j-n_k+l}(a_k)
 -f_{n_j+l}(a_j)+f_l(a_j)
 \right)&<\varepsilon
 \label{eq:cross}
\end{align}
for every \(k<q\) and \(0\leq l\leq N\); the same inequalities hold with
\(a_h\) replaced by \(b_h\).  These are exactly clauses (ii) and (iii) of
that theorem because the displayed coefficients occupy distinct coordinates
in \(c_0\).

The hypotheses of the characterization hold with
\(F(n)=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil\).  Indeed, on \(1\leq a,b\leq2\), differentiation
and the mean-value theorem give
\begin{equation}
 \frac13\sqrt n\,|a-b|
 \leq |f_n(a)-f_n(b)|
 \leq2\sqrt n\,|a-b|.
 \label{eq:lipschitz}
\end{equation}
The pointwise weight ratios are also uniformly bounded away from zero.

\section*{A Hilbert scheduling lemma}

We use a standard Hilbert map \(H:[0,1]\to[0,1]^2\).  There is an absolute
constant \(A\geq1\) such that
\begin{equation}
 \|H(s)-H(t)\|_\infty^2\leq A|s-t|.
 \label{eq:hilbert-holder}
\end{equation}
Moreover, every 4-adic interval of length \(4^{-m}\) maps onto one dyadic
square of side \(2^{-m}\).

\begin{lemma}[Harmonic Hilbert schedule]\label{lem:schedule}
For every sufficiently small \(\kappa>0\), there is \(L>0\) with the
following property.  Given \(\tau>0\), an integer gap \(G\geq1\), and a
sufficiently large initial integer, there are finitely many arithmetic times
\(n_h=n_0+hG\), dyadic squares \(Q_h\subset[1,1+L]^2\), and upper-right tags
\(\lambda_h\in Q_h\) such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the \(Q_h\) cover \([1,1+L]^2\);
\item the side of \(Q_h\) is at most \(\tau/(2\sqrt{n_h})\);
\item for every \(k<j\),
\[
 \|\lambda_j-\lambda_k\|_\infty
 \leq\kappa\sqrt{\frac{n_j-n_k}{n_j}}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(L>0\) so small that \(L^2\leq\kappa^2/2\), and put
\[
 \beta=\min\left\{
 \frac{\tau^2}{4L^2},
 \frac{\kappa^2G}{16AL^2}
 \right\}>0.
\]
Increase \(n_0\), if necessary, so that \(\beta/n_1<1\).  Starting at
\(t_0=0\), let \(I_h=[t_{h-1},t_h]\) be the next 4-adic interval whose length
\(\ell_h=4^{-m_h}\) is the largest 4-adic number satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \ell_h\leq\frac{\beta}{n_h}.
 \label{eq:length-choice}
\end{equation}
Because \(\beta/n_h\) decreases, the exponents \(m_h\) do not decrease.
The current endpoint is therefore aligned with the next finer 4-adic grid.
If it is below 1, the next interval fits.  Maximality gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\beta}{4n_h}<\ell_h\leq\frac{\beta}{n_h}.
 \label{eq:length-two-sided}
\end{equation}
If this procedure did not reach 1 after finitely many steps, the disjoint
intervals would have total length at least
\(\frac\beta4\sum_hn_h^{-1}=+\infty\), a contradiction.  Thus they form a
finite partition of \([0,1]\).

Let \(Q_h\) be the affine image in \([1,1+L]^2\) of \(H(I_h)\), and tag it
by its upper-right corner \(\lambda_h\).  Its side is
\(L\sqrt{\ell_h}\leq\tau/(2\sqrt{n_h})\), proving (i)--(ii).

For \(k<j\), the tag points have Hilbert parameters lying in \(I_k\) and
\(I_j\); hence, by \eqref{eq:hilbert-holder},
\begin{equation}
 \|\lambda_j-\lambda_k\|_\infty^2
 \leq AL^2\sum_{h=k}^j\ell_h.
 \label{eq:tag-sum}
\end{equation}
If \(n_j/n_k\leq2\), elementary comparison with an integral gives
\[
 \sum_{h=k}^j\frac1{n_h}
 \leq\frac4G\frac{n_j-n_k}{n_j}.
\]
Equations \eqref{eq:length-choice} and \eqref{eq:tag-sum}, together with the
definition of \(\beta\), yield (iii).  If \(n_j/n_k\geq2\), then
\(\|\lambda_j-\lambda_k\|_\infty^2\leq L^2\), while
\((n_j-n_k)/n_j\geq1/2\); the choice of \(L\) again yields (iii).
\end{proof}

\section*{Main result}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There exists \(L>0\) such that, for
\(\Lambda=[1,1+L]^2\),
\[
 \bigcap_{(a,b)\in\Lambda}
 \HC\bigl(B_{w(a)}\times B_{w(b)}\bigr)\neq\varnothing
 \qquad\text{on }c_0\times c_0.
\]
In particular, Question 2.5 of arXiv:2106.08949 has a positive answer with a
parameter set of positive Lebesgue measure \(L^2\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On \([1,2]\), the elementary bounds
\begin{equation}
 f_m(a)\geq\frac13\sqrt m,
 \qquad
 |f_m(a)-f_m(b)|\leq2\sqrt m\,|a-b|
 \label{eq:weight-bounds}
\end{equation}
hold for \(m\geq1\).  The first follows from
\(\log(1+x)\geq x/(1+x)\), and the second from differentiation.

Fix
\[
 0<\kappa<\frac1{12\sqrt2},
\]
and let \(L\leq1\) be supplied by Lemma~\ref{lem:schedule}.  We verify the
exact characterization for the whole square \(\Lambda\).  Fix
\(\tau>0\), \(N\geq1\), and \(0<\varepsilon<1\).  Put
\[
 C_N=\max_{0\leq l\leq N,\,1\leq a\leq2} f_l(a).
\]
Choose an integer \(G\geq N\) so large that
\begin{equation}
 \exp\left(C_N-\frac16\sqrt G\right)<\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:G-choice}
\end{equation}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:schedule}, enlarging its initial time so that
\(n_1\geq N\) and
\begin{equation}
 e^{-\sqrt{n_1}/3}<\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:n-choice}
\end{equation}
The rectangles ending at \(\lambda_h\) with side
\(\tau/\lceil\sqrt{n_h}\rceil\) contain \(Q_h\), because
\(\lceil\sqrt n\rceil\leq2\sqrt n\).  They therefore cover \(\Lambda\).
The time gaps are \(G\geq N\), and \eqref{eq:n-choice} together with
\eqref{eq:weight-bounds} proves \eqref{eq:direct}.

It remains to check the cross terms.  Fix \(k<j\), \(0\leq l\leq N\), and
one coordinate of the tags.  Writing that coordinate as \(a_h\), the
logarithm in \eqref{eq:cross} is at most
\begin{align*}
 &2\sqrt{n_j+N}\,|a_k-a_j|
 -\frac13\sqrt{n_j-n_k+l}+C_N\\
 &\qquad\leq
 2\sqrt2\,\kappa\sqrt{n_j-n_k}
 -\frac13\sqrt{n_j-n_k}+C_N\\
 &\qquad\leq C_N-\frac16\sqrt{n_j-n_k}
 \leq C_N-\frac16\sqrt G.
\end{align*}
Here we used \(n_j\geq N\) and clause (iii) of the scheduling lemma.  The
choice \eqref{eq:G-choice} proves \eqref{eq:cross}; the other coordinate is
identical.  All clauses of Theorem 2.1 of~\cite{BayartCostaMenet} hold, so the
family has a common hypercyclic vector.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
The same square \(\Lambda\) works on
\(\ell_p\times\ell_p\) for every \(1\leq p<\infty\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For clause (ii) of the characterization, the \(p\)-th powers of the direct
coefficients are bounded by
\(e^{-p\sqrt{n_h}/3}\), whose sum over the arithmetic progression becomes
arbitrarily small when the initial time grows.  For clause (iii), the proof
above bounds the \(p\)-th powers of the cross coefficients by
\[
 e^{pC_N}e^{-p\sqrt{(j-k)G}/6}.
\]
Their sum over \(j>k\) becomes arbitrarily small as \(G\to\infty\), uniformly
in the finite truncation.  These are precisely the relevant \(\ell_p\) norms.
\end{proof}

\section*{Verification, novelty, and limitations}

The proof uses only the exact characterization in
Bayart--Costa--Menet~\cite{BayartCostaMenet} and the standard recursive
properties of the Hilbert curve.  The two endpoint mechanisms are explicit:
the 4-adic cells terminate because their lengths dominate a harmonic series,
and the \(1/2\)-Hölder bound turns Hilbert time into the quadratic parameter
jump needed by the weight ratios.  No numerical assumption is used.

source corpus were searched for arXiv:2106.08949 and the exact positive-area
question.  Exact-phrase and close weighted-shift web searches found the
