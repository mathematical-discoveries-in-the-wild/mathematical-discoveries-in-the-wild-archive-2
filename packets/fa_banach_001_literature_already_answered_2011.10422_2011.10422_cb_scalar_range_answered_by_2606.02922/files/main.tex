\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{Answered affirmatively by Hartz--McCarthy, arXiv:2606.02922.}
If the antilinear map in the completely bounded conjecture takes values in
the scalar multiples of the identity, then the desired sharp bound
\(\|\theta\|_{cb}\le 2\) follows.  This answers the explicit ``we do not
know'' question on source PDF page~9.  It does \emph{not} settle either of
the source paper's two unrestricted abstract conjectures.

\section*{Source question}
Clou\^atre--Ostermann--Ransford formulate the completely bounded conjecture
for a unital completely bounded homomorphism \(\theta\) and a unital
antilinear complete contraction \(\alpha\).  Immediately after their
\(1+\sqrt2\) result, they ask whether the sharp constant \(2\) follows for
the disk algebra when \(\alpha\) takes values in \(\C 1\) [1, p.~9].

\section*{Proof intuition}
When \(\alpha\) has scalar range, its contribution to the symmetrized map is
not an operator-valued perturbation at all: it is a scalar functional times
the identity.  Hartz and McCarthy prove that adding any such scalar
perturbation to a unital homomorphism cannot reduce any matrix-amplified norm
below the homomorphism norm (unless that norm was already at most one).
Applying this comparison at every matrix level turns the hypothesis on
\(\theta_\alpha\) directly into the bound \(2\).

The comparison theorem is powered by an extremal-vector argument.  Near a
norm-attaining sequence for a homomorphism, scalar matrix perturbations are
first-order orthogonal to the image.  Potapov--M\"obius automorphisms of the
operator ball supply the admissible variations; taking a first variation
removes the scalar cross term.

\begin{theorem}[Hartz--McCarthy \cite{answer}]
Let \(\A\) be a unital operator algebra, let
\(\theta:\A\to B(\mathcal H)\) be a continuous unital homomorphism, and let
\(\beta\in\A^*\).  For every \(m\ge1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HM}
 \|\theta^{(m)}\|
 \le \max\!\left\{1,
 \|\theta^{(m)}+\beta^{(m)}I\|\right\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\[
 \|\theta\|_{cb}\le
 \max\{1,\|\theta+\beta I\|_{cb}\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Why the comparison holds.}
Fix \(m\), put \(C=\|\theta^{(m)}\|>1\), and choose contractions
\(a_k\in M_m(\A)\) and unit vectors \(x_k\) with
\(\|R_kx_k\|\to C\), where \(R_k=\theta^{(m)}(a_k)\).
For a scalar matrix \(D\in M_m(\C)\), the Potapov--M\"obius transform
\[
 M_D(X)=(I-DD^*)^{-1/2}(X+D)(I+D^*X)^{-1}(I-D^*D)^{1/2}
\]
preserves the operator unit ball.  The homomorphism identity
\[
 M_{D\otimes I}(R_k)
 =\theta^{(m)}\!\left(M_{D\otimes I}(a_k)\right)
\]
therefore bounds these variations by \(C\).  Expanding at \(tD\) to first
order and using \(\|R_kx_k\|\to C\) gives, uniformly for bounded \(D_k\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orth}
 \langle R_kx_k,(D_k\otimes I)x_k\rangle\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Taking \(D_k=\beta^{(m)}(a_k)\), expanding the squared norm of
\((\theta^{(m)}+\beta^{(m)}I)(a_k)x_k\), and using
\eqref{eq:orth} yields
\[
 \|\theta^{(m)}+\beta^{(m)}I\|^2\ge C^2.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:HM}; the case \(C\le1\) is immediate.

\section*{Application to the source question}
Assume \(\alpha(\A)\subset\C1\).  Write
\(\alpha(f)=\lambda(f)1\), where \(\lambda\) is antilinear, and define the
linear functional \(\beta(f)=\overline{\lambda(f)}\).  Because \(\theta\)
is unital,
\[
 2\theta_\alpha(f)
 =\theta(f)+\theta(\alpha(f))^*
 =\theta(f)+\beta(f)I.
\]
Thus \(\|\theta_\alpha\|_{cb}\le1\) implies
\(\|\theta+\beta I\|_{cb}\le2\).  The theorem gives
\[
 \boxed{\|\theta\|_{cb}\le2}.
\]
This argument works for every unital operator algebra, not only the disk
algebra, and the comparison theorem itself does not require \(\alpha(1)=1\).

\section*{Exact remaining scope}
The unrestricted norm and completely bounded conjectures in [1] allow
operator-valued \(\alpha(f)\), so the reduction to a scalar functional fails.
Lorist--Schwenninger [3] prove the classical Crouzeix conjecture and an
abstract variant under the stronger condition that
\(\tfrac12(\theta+\theta\!\circ\!\alpha{}^*)\) is completely positive.
They explicitly note that their argument does not directly yield the
completely bounded Crouzeix statement.  Hence those 2026 results answer the
scalar-range question recorded here and the classical consequence, but they
do not claim the source paper's unrestricted abstract conjectures.
