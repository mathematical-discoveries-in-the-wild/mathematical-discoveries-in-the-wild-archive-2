\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.9\textwidth}{\textbf{Status: literature already answered
(Question 1.4).} The answer is no. There is a Banach space with continuous
$2$-SCP---indeed, also controlled SCP---which has no projectional skeleton.}}
\end{center}

\section*{Original question}

Kalenda and Kubi\'s ask in Question 1.4 on printed page 3 of
\cite{KalendaKubis}:
\begin{quote}
Let $E$ be a Banach space with the continuous $k$-SCP. Does it have a
projectional skeleton?
\end{quote}
Here continuous $k$-SCP means that a closed cofinal family of separable
subspaces of $E$ consists of $k$-complemented subspaces.

\section*{Separate negative answer}

Ferrer, Koszmider, and Kubi\'s state in Theorem 1.6 of \cite{FerrerEtAl}
(printed page 6): there is an almost disjoint family $\mathcal A$ such that
$C(K_{\mathcal A})$ has controlled SCP and continuous SCP, but has neither a
projectional resolution of the identity nor a projectional skeleton.

The proof in Section 5 (printed pages 17--18) is quantitative enough for the
source hypothesis: it writes $C(K_{\mathcal A})$ as the union of a continuous
chain $(E_\alpha)_{\alpha<\omega_1}$ of separable subspaces and constructs a
projection onto every $E_\alpha$ of norm at most $2$. Thus this space has
continuous $2$-SCP while lacking a projectional skeleton, directly falsifying
Question 1.4.

\section*{Provenance and scope}

The supporting paper cites \cite{KalendaKubis}, uses its continuous-SCP
terminology, and says that its examples decide the missing implications among
the separable complementation properties under discussion. This is therefore
an explicit later literature answer, not a new counterexample from this run.
The identification is limited to Question 1.4: source Question 1.6 about
$\aleph_0$-monolithic dual balls and source Conjecture 4.2 for scattered compact
lines are not resolved by this packet.
