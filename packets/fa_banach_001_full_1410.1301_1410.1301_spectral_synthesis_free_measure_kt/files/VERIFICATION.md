# Verification Report

Verdict: **likely valid candidate full solution**

Claim checked: spectral synthesis can be removed from Theorem 1.2 of
arXiv:1410.1301 under its stated Hilbert-space high-frequency resolvent
hypothesis.

## Adversarial checks

### 1. Does the resolvent assumption really yield the regularity used?

Yes. The source itself records that a Neumann-series extension gives
\(s_0^\infty(A)<0\) from the stated imaginary-axis bound and that, on
Hilbert space, \(\delta(T)=\zeta(T)=s_0^\infty(A)\). From \(\delta(T)<0\),
the proof chooses a norm-continuous approximant with exponentially decaying
error. This gives \(D(r)\to0\), and the semigroup law makes the small
right-increment estimate uniform on the entire tail. Negative increments are
reduced to positive ones at the earlier base point. No global norm continuity
of \(T\) is assumed.

### 2. Is strong continuity falsely asserted on the full ultrapower?

No. The full coordinatewise ultrapower semigroup may fail to be \(C_0\).
The proof restricts it to the closed \(L^1\)-essential subspace generated by
vectors \(\widehat a(\widetilde T)\xi\). Translation continuity in \(L^1\)
gives uniform operator-norm continuity on these generators, hence strong
continuity on their closed span.

### 3. Is the ultrapower spectrum inclusion justified?

Yes. On the essential subspace, Fubini gives
\(\widehat a(S)=\widehat a(\widetilde T)|_{H_c}\), and coordinatewise
ultrapower norms yield
\(\|\widehat a(S)\|\le\|\widehat a(T)\|\). Applying the defining inequality
for the spectrum of a semigroup representation for every \(a\in L^1\)
gives \(\operatorname{Sp}(S)\subset\operatorname{Sp}(T)\), and hence the
same inclusion for unitary spectra.

### 4. Does a finite singular measure commute with the ultrapower quotient?

Not in general, and the proof does not claim that. It uses the particular
smoothed vector \(x=\widehat b(\widetilde T)y\). The map
\(q\mapsto T(q)\widehat b(T)\) is norm-continuous (indeed uniformly so for
small increments), so its finite-measure integral is an operator-norm
Bochner integral approximable by common simple sums. Those sums pass through
the quotient. Thus
\(\widehat\mu(S)x=[\widehat\mu(T)\widehat b(T)y_n]\) is valid. This resolves
the most serious apparent gap in a naive proof.

### 5. Is the non-decay lower bound valid despite averaging?

Yes. The bump \(b\) is nonnegative, has integral one, and is supported in a
short interval. The reverse triangle inequality compares the average with
the single central value. Tail uniform norm continuity bounds the error in
operator norm, uniformly in \(n\). For each fixed ultrapower time \(s\), the
cofinite set with \(t_n\ge s+\tau\) supplies the backward lower bound.

### 6. Does the unitary-asymptote step work for a measure rather than \(L^1\)?

Yes. The bounded intertwiner commutes with the Bochner integral on the
already strongly continuous semigroup \(S\). The unitary group has a spectral
measure, so integrating it against a finite Borel measure gives the spectral
multiplier \(\mathcal F\mu\). Because the unitary-asymptote spectrum lies in
the original unitary spectrum, the vanishing hypothesis makes this operator
zero.

### 7. Is the Fourier sign correct?

Yes. With \((\mathcal F\mu)(s)=\int e^{-ist}\,d\mu(t)\), the unitary
character indexed by \(s\) is \(t\mapsto e^{-ist}\), whose generator is
\(-is\). Thus \(s=i(-is)\), exactly matching the source's real parameter set
\(i\sigma(A)\cap\mathbb R\).

### 8. Is spectral synthesis smuggled into a cited theorem?

No. The only spectral annihilation is for a strongly continuous unitary
group on a Hilbert space and follows directly from the spectral theorem.
The cited companion paper supplies the standard unitary-asymptote
construction and its spectrum inclusion; its Hilbert conclusion at that
stage does not require spectral synthesis.

## Scope and novelty

- The proof answers exactly Remark 1.3/Theorem 1.2, and in fact works under
  the more intrinsic assumption \(\delta(T)<0\).
- It does not remove spectral synthesis for general Banach spaces.
- A bounded 9 August 2026 search found no later explicit solution. Novelty
  still requires expert literature review.
- No numerical or computer-assisted claim is used.

## Recommended human focus

The two most delicate lines are the common simple-sum approximation in the
measure-ultrapower identity and the spectrum inclusion for the minimal
unitary extension. Both are standard once the continuous essential subspace
is used, but they deserve line-by-line review.
