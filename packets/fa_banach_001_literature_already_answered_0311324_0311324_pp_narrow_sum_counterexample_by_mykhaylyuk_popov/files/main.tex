\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{The general-range sum question has a negative answer.}
Mykhaylyuk--Popov constructed a Banach space $Y$ and PP-narrow operators
$T_1,T_2:\Lone\to Y$ for which $T_1+T_2$ is not PP-narrow.  Their result
appeared in \emph{J. Math. Anal. Appl.} 404 (2013), 554--561,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmaa.2013.03.008}{doi:10.1016/j.jmaa.2013.03.008}.
This packet records that later-literature counterexample and makes no novelty
claim.

\section*{The source question}
Let $(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)$ be a nonatomic probability space.  A \emph{sign on
$A\in\Sigma$} is
\[
 f=\mathbf 1_{B_1}-\mathbf 1_{B_2},\qquad
 A=B_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}B_2,\qquad \mu(B_1)=\mu(B_2).
\]
An operator $T:L_1(\Omega)\to X$ is PP-narrow when, for every positive-measure
$A$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, some sign $f$ on $A$ satisfies
$\|Tf\|<\varepsilon$.

The source paper notes on PDF page 4 that PP-narrow operators on $L_p$ are not
closed under sums when $1<p<\infty$, and asks whether closure under sums might
nevertheless hold for $p=1$.  The surrounding setup keeps the range $X$
arbitrary.  Thus the selected question is
\[
 \boxed{\ \text{For every Banach space $X$, must two PP-narrow maps
 $\Lone\to X$ have PP-narrow sum?}\ }
\]

\section*{The later counterexample}
Mykhaylyuk--Popov prove the stronger assertion
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{for every K\"othe Banach space $E$ on $[0,1]$, there are a Banach
 space $Y$ and}\\[-2pt]
 \text{narrow operators $T_1,T_2:E\to Y$ such that $T_1+T_2$ is not narrow.}
 \end{gathered}\tag{MP}
\]
Their publisher abstract explicitly says that this answers the $E=L_1$
question in the negative.  Before publication, the same authors stated the
exact arbitrary-range problem in their 2012 Stefan Banach conference abstract
and announced negative answers.

\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{evidence/conference_answer_crop.png}

PDF page 27 (unaltered crop).}

\section*{Formal deduction from (MP)}
Take $E=\Lone$ in (MP).  The term ``narrow'' used there is the Plichko--Popov
notion called ``PP-narrow'' by the source paper: both definitions quantify over
every measurable $A$, every $\varepsilon>0$, and equal-measure signs on $A$.
Hence the theorem supplies a Banach space $Y$ and two PP-narrow maps
$T_1,T_2:\Lone\to Y$.

The assertion that $T_1+T_2$ is not narrow is precisely the negation of the
source property.  Equivalently, there are a positive-measure set $A$ and
$\varepsilon_0>0$ such that
\[
 \|(T_1+T_2)f\|\ge \varepsilon_0
 \quad\text{for every equal-measure sign $f$ on $A$}.
\]
Thus $T_1,T_2$ answer the displayed source question negatively.  Only two
summands are needed.

\section*{Why there is no contradiction with the $L_1\to L_1$ theorem}
The range is decisive.  For self-maps, the class of PP-narrow operators in
$\mathcal L(L_1)$ is stable even under pointwise unconditional sums.  The
counterexample instead permits a specially chosen Banach range $Y$.

The 2025 monograph by Kadets--Mart\'in--Rueda Zoca--Werner states both facts
side by side and identifies the counterexample as Mykhaylyuk--Popov [236]:

\section*{Proof intuition}
PP-narrowness is an existential small-sign condition.  Given $T_1$ and $T_2$
separately, the sign that makes $T_1$ small need not be the sign that makes
$T_2$ small.  A target norm can preserve this incompatibility: each coordinate
test can be defeated on its own, while the combined operator remains uniformly
large on all signs of one measurable set.  That is the mechanism made possible
by the custom range $Y$ in the 2013 construction.

The source paper's repair is hereditary PP-narrowness.  It demands the same
small-sign property after restriction to every nonatomic sub-$\sigma$-algebra.
One may first choose a sub-$\sigma$-algebra on which one summand is uniformly
small, then use hereditary narrowness of the other inside that same algebra.
This synchronizes the sign choices and yields
\[
 U\ \text{PP-narrow},\quad V\ \text{HPP-narrow}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad U+V\ \text{PP-narrow}.
\]
The later counterexample shows that the hereditary hypothesis is substantive,
not a technical convenience.

\section*{Scope and audit trail}
The 2013 theorem is stronger than needed because it works for every K\"othe
Banach domain $E$; the source question follows by the single specialization
$E=L_1$.  No claim is made here to reproduce the internal construction of $Y$.
The literature implication and the matching of definitions and quantifiers are
complete.
