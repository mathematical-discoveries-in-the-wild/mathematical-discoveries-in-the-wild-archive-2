\section{The question and the answer}

Kadets and Rold\'an prove that if $M$ is $\sigma$-precompact, then every
Banach subspace of $\SNA(M)$ is separable and isomorphically polyhedral.
Question 4.3 asks whether the same conclusion follows from the weaker small
ball property \cite{source}.  The answer is no.  In fact the counterexample
works uniformly over all separable infinite-dimensional reflexive Banach
spaces.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $X$ be a separable infinite-dimensional reflexive real Banach space.
There is a dense pointed metric subspace $M\subset X$ with the small ball
property such that the linear map
\[
 J:X^*\longrightarrow \Lip(M),\qquad
 (Jx^*)(x)=x^*(x)-x^*(o),
\]
where $o\in M$ is fixed, is an isometric embedding and
$J(X^*)\subset\SNA(M)$.

In particular, for $X=\ell_2$, the set $\SNA(M)$ contains a closed subspace
isometric to $\ell_2$, which is not isomorphically polyhedral.
\end{theorem}

\section{A dense small-ball set has every vector as a chord}

We include the short construction underlying Proposition 5.1 of
Behrends--Kadets \cite{bk}; this makes the counterexample self-contained.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:dense}
Every separable Banach space $X$ has a dense $G_\delta$ subset $M$ with the
small ball property.  Moreover, every such dense $G_\delta$ set satisfies
$M-M=X$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a dense sequence $(x_n)$ in $X$ and positive numbers $a_n\to0$.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M}
 M=\bigcap_{k=1}^\infty\ \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty
 B^\circ(x_n,a_{k+n}).
\end{equation}
For each $k$, the displayed union is open and contains the dense set
$\{x_n:n\ge1\}$, so it is dense.  Since $X$ is complete, the Baire category
theorem makes $M$ a dense $G_\delta$ set.

Given $\varepsilon_0>0$, choose $k$ so far out that
$a_{k+n}<\varepsilon_0$ for every $n\ge1$.  Equation \eqref{eq:M} gives
\[
 M\subset \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty B(x_n,a_{k+n}),
\]
where all radii are smaller than $\varepsilon_0$ and tend to zero.  Thus $M$
has the small ball property.

Finally fix $u\in X$.  Both $M$ and $M-u$ are dense $G_\delta$ subsets of
the complete space $X$.  Their intersection is nonempty.  If
$q\in M\cap(M-u)$, then $q\in M$ and $q+u\in M$, so
$u=(q+u)-q\in M-M$.
\end{proof}

\section{Every functional strongly attains on a chord}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Take the set $M$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:dense} and choose a base point $o\in M$.
For $x^*\in X^*$ the function $Jx^*$ vanishes at $o$ and
\[
 |(Jx^*)(p)-(Jx^*)(q)|=|x^*(p-q)|
 \le \|x^*\|\,\|p-q\| \qquad(p,q\in M).
\]
Hence $\|Jx^*\|_{\Lip}\le\|x^*\|$.

Suppose $x^*\ne0$.  Reflexivity of $X$ makes $B_X$ weakly compact, so $x^*$
attains its norm: after changing sign, there is $u\in S_X$ with
$x^*(u)=\|x^*\|$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:dense} gives $p,q\in M$ such that
$p-q=u$.  Therefore
\[
 \frac{|(Jx^*)(p)-(Jx^*)(q)|}{\|p-q\|}
 =\frac{|x^*(u)|}{\|u\|}=\|x^*\|.
\]
Thus $Jx^*$ strongly attains its Lipschitz norm at $(p,q)$ and
$\|Jx^*\|_{\Lip}=\|x^*\|$.  The map $J$ is linear and isometric, so its
range is closed because $X^*$ is complete.

Now take $X=\ell_2$.  Then $J(X^*)$ is isometric to $\ell_2$.  An
infinite-dimensional isomorphically polyhedral Banach space contains an
isomorphic copy of $c_0$ \cite{fonf}.  This is impossible in the reflexive
space $\ell_2$, because closed subspaces of reflexive spaces are reflexive
whereas $c_0$ is not.  Hence $J(\ell_2)$ is not isomorphically polyhedral,
which disproves the assertion in Question 4.3.
\end{proof}

\section{Strength, limitations, and novelty check}

The construction disproves exactly the printed question: that question
assumes only that $M$ is pointed and has the small ball property; it does not
assume that $M$ is complete.  The set in \eqref{eq:M} is a dense
$G_\delta$ in $X$, but need not be complete for the inherited norm metric.
Thus this packet does not decide the stronger variant obtained by adding
metric completeness.

The separability half of the source conclusion is not contradicted: the
displayed bad subspace is $\ell_2$, which is separable.  The counterexample
is decisive because the question asks for both separability and
isomorphic polyhedrality.

On 11 August 2026, exact searches of the run registry and all cheap indexes
found no prior packet or registered answer.  Exact searches across the
locally parsed arXiv corpus for the source title, the small ball property,
strong norm attainment, and polyhedrality found the source and later papers
discussing Questions 4.1--4.2, but no resolution of Question 4.3.  Primary
arXiv:2204.12529 answers Questions 4.1--4.2 by embedding $c_0$ in
$\SNA(M)$ for every infinite complete $M$ \cite{aviles}.  Bounded web
searches likewise returned the source and unrelated uses of ``small ball.''
This is a bounded novelty check, not an exhaustive priority claim.
