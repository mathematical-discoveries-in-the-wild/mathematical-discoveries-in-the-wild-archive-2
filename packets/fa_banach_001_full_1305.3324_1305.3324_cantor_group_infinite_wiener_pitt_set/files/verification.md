## Proof audit

1. **Finite quotient:** for `Gamma_r` generated by the first `r` dual
   coordinates, the pushforward to `D/Gamma_r^perp` has Fourier transform
   equal to the original transform restricted to `Gamma_r`, and its total
   variation norm does not increase.
2. **Lift:** spreading each quotient atom uniformly on its coset preserves
   total variation, preserves the rounded coefficients on `Gamma_r`, and
   makes every coefficient outside `Gamma_r` zero.
3. **Weak-star limit:** uniform norm bounds give a cluster point. Every fixed
   character belongs to all sufficiently large `Gamma_r`, so its coefficient
   is eventually the desired rounded integer.
4. **Recursive order:** at stage `N`, the quantities `q_N`, `B_N`, `E_N`,
   `C_N`, and `T_N` depend only on the already chosen
   `a_1,...,a_N`. Since `q_N(0)=0`, all four upper bounds imposed on
   `a_{N+1}` are compatible.
5. **Arbitrary norm:** for a measure of norm `R`, choose integer `N>=R`.
   The prefix polynomial has norm at most the stage-`N` bound, and the
   isolated-tail residual has norm at most `a_{N+1}^{-1/2}`.
6. **Tail induction:** the ratio condition lets the rounding lemma isolate
   each remaining level and gives
   `||e_n|| <= (3/2)a_n^{-3/2}+1/2`; the `1/9` decay propagates the
   residual bound.
7. **Convergence:** the weighted idempotent series converge for powers two
   and three because their tail norms are bounded by summable multiples of
   `a_n^{1/2}` and `a_n^{3/2}`.
8. **Spectrum:** a multiplicative functional takes values zero or one on each
   idempotent and can select at most one. The square and cube together force
   its value on `mu` to be the corresponding positive `a_n`; the finite-range
   polynomial handles the only case in which zero is not already a Fourier
   limit.
