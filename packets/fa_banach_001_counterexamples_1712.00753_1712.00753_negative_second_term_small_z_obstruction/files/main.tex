\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Candidate counterexample, likely valid, to Open Question 1.8 exactly as
printed in arXiv:1712.00753. The conclusion concerns the all-$z>0$ reading
used by the surrounding estimates.

\section{Source question}
Hassannezhad and Laptev study the mixed Steklov--Dirichlet eigenvalues
$\eta_j$ of a bounded domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$ whose boundary is
split into a free part $\mathcal F$ and a Dirichlet part. For $\gamma>0$ the
Riesz mean is
\[
 R_\gamma(z)=\sum_j (z-\eta_j)_+^\gamma,\qquad z>0.
\]
On page 6, Open Question 1.8 asks whether there is a positive constant
$C(\alpha,\beta)$, depending only on the two corner angles, such that
\begin{equation}\label{proposed}
 R_\gamma(z)\leq C_{n,\gamma}|\mathcal F|z^{n+\gamma-1}
 -C(\alpha,\beta)z^{n+\gamma-2}.
\end{equation}
No lower cutoff in $z$ and no remainder term is stated \cite{HL}.

\section{Idea of the proof}
The Riesz mean can never be negative, because it is a sum of nonnegative
terms. But the suggested two-term polynomial is necessarily negative near
$z=0$: the negative term has one lower power of $z$ than the positive Weyl
term. Factoring out the common positive power makes the contradiction
immediate.

\section{Counterexample theorem}
\begin{theorem}
Fix $n\geq2$, $\gamma>0$, a domain with $|\mathcal F|>0$, and any proposed
constant $C(\alpha,\beta)>0$. Then inequality \eqref{proposed} fails for some
$z>0$. Consequently, no positive $C(\alpha,\beta)$ can make the inequality
in Open Question 1.8 hold for every $z>0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $A=C_{n,\gamma}|\mathcal F|>0$ and $C=C(\alpha,\beta)>0$. The right-hand
side of \eqref{proposed} factors as
\[
 z^{n+\gamma-2}(Az-C).
\]
Choose any
\[
 0<z<\frac{C}{A}.
\]
Because $n\geq2$ and $\gamma>0$, the factor $z^{n+\gamma-2}$ is positive,
while $Az-C<0$. Hence the proposed right-hand side is strictly negative.
On the other hand,
\[
 R_\gamma(z)=\sum_j(z-\eta_j)_+^\gamma\geq0.
\]
Thus \eqref{proposed} would require a nonnegative number to be at most a
strictly negative number, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The argument does not require a specially chosen domain or spectrum. In
particular, if $z$ is also taken below the first mixed
Steklov--Dirichlet eigenvalue, then $R_\gamma(z)=0$ exactly.

\section{Deepening and the necessary repair}
The obstruction shows that a global estimate with a pure negative second
term is impossible. Any corrected version must include at least one of the
following: a lower threshold $z\geq z_0$, a nonnegative lower-order remainder,
or a positive-part truncation of the polynomial right-hand side.

The source's immediately preceding planar estimate already displays the
relevant repair. For right-angle John domains, Theorem 1.7 gives
\[
 R_1(z)\leq \frac{|\mathcal F|}{2\pi}z^2-\frac12z
 +\frac{\pi}{2|\mathcal F|}.
\]
The final positive constant prevents the right-hand side from becoming
negative near the origin. Removing it is not a harmless sharpening at small
$z$.

\begin{remark}
The argument does not decide the charitable asymptotic reformulation: for a
fixed domain, does there exist $z_0$ such that a bound of the displayed form
holds for all $z\geq z_0$? Nor does it determine an optimal explicit positive
remainder. Those are distinct repaired questions.
\end{remark}

\section{Verification and novelty check}
The source formula and its lack of a lower cutoff were checked in the final
arXiv PDF, page 6, Open Question 1.8. The script
\texttt{code/verify\_sign.py} chooses $z=C/(2A)$ and verifies that the proposed
right side is negative while the universal lower bound on the Riesz mean is
zero.

A bounded search on 2026-08-12 used the exact question wording, the source
arXiv id, and combinations of \emph{mixed Steklov--Dirichlet}, \emph{Riesz
mean}, and \emph{negative second term}. It found the source and adjacent later
work on mixed Steklov eigenvalues, but no paper pointing out or repairing this
small-$z$ obstruction. Novelty confidence is moderate; mathematical-validity
confidence for the literal quantifier is high.
