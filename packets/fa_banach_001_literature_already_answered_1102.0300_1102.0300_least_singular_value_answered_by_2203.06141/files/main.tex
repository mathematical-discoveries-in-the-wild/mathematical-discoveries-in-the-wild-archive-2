\section{The question isolated}

Let $H$ be an $n\times n$ real symmetric random matrix with independent
subgaussian entries on and above the diagonal, of mean zero and variance one.
The 2011 paper of Vershynin proved, in a somewhat more general model,
an estimate of the form
\[
  \Pp\!\left\{\smin(H)\leq \varepsilon n^{-1/2}\right\}
       \leq C\varepsilon^{1/9}+2e^{-n^c}.
\]
Its Remark 1.4 conjectured that the singularity-probability term $2e^{-n^c}$
could be replaced by $2e^{-cn}$.  The surrounding discussion also identifies
linear dependence on $\varepsilon$ as the optimal small-ball scale.

\section{The later theorem}

Campos, Jenssen, Michelen and Sahasrabudhe prove the following in
arXiv:2203.06141v3, Theorem 1.1.

\medskip
\textbf{Resolving theorem.}
Let $\zeta$ be a subgaussian random variable with mean zero and variance one,
and let $A$ be a symmetric $n\times n$ matrix whose entries on and above the
diagonal are independent copies of $\zeta$.  There are constants $C,c>0$,
depending only on the law of $\zeta$, such that for every $\varepsilon\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resolving}
  \Pp\!\left\{\smin(A)\leq \varepsilon n^{-1/2}\right\}
       \leq C\varepsilon+e^{-cn}.
\end{equation}

The resolving paper explicitly says that this confirms the folklore
conjecture stated by Vershynin and is sharp up to the constants.  Thus the
identification is not merely an inference based on similar-looking estimates.

Setting $\varepsilon=0$ in \eqref{eq:resolving} gives
\[
  \Pp\{A\text{ is singular}\}\leq e^{-cn},
\]
which is precisely the conjectured exponential-in-$n$ improvement in the
canonical i.i.d. model.  Equation \eqref{eq:resolving} is stronger: it also
upgrades $\varepsilon^{1/9}$ to the optimal linear dependence $\varepsilon$.
The Rademacher law is subgaussian, so symmetric Bernoulli matrices are included.

\section{Scope and logical crosswalk}

The implication is immediate and contains no hidden limit argument:
\[
 \text{Theorem 1.1 of arXiv:2203.06141}
 \quad\mathop{\Longrightarrow}\limits^{\varepsilon=0}\quad
 \text{Vershynin's }e^{-cn}\text{ singularity term}.
\]

There are two scope distinctions worth preserving.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Vershynin's hypothesis \textbf{(H)} allowed the diagonal entries to be
arbitrary (subject in Theorem 1.2 to a size bound), independently of the
off-diagonal entries.  The resolving theorem assumes the entries on and above
the diagonal are i.i.d.  This packet therefore certifies the full canonical
i.i.d. subgaussian formulation---the one the resolving authors themselves call
Vershynin's conjecture---and does not assert an arbitrary-diagonal extension.

\item The older introduction also recalls the sharper conjecture that a
symmetric Bernoulli matrix has singularity probability
$(1/2+o(1))^n$.  An unspecified $e^{-cn}$ upper bound does not determine that
optimal exponential base.  That separate conjecture is not claimed solved
here.
\end{enumerate}

These qualifications do not weaken the answer to the queue's exact
``we conjecture'' signal in Remark 1.4 as interpreted in the later paper; they
prevent conflating it with stronger or more uniform variants.
