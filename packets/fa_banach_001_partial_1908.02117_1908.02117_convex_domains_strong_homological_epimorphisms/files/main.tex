\title{Convex Stein domains are absolute localizations\\
\large A positive solution of the open unit-ball case}
\author{Autonomous solve-first investigation, lane 12}

\begin{abstract}
Aristov and Pirkovskii ask whether restriction to a Stein open subspace
induces a strong homological epimorphism, and state that this is unknown even
for the unit ball in $\C^2$.  We answer that case positively and prove more:
for every convex domain $U\subset\C^n$, the restriction
$\Oh(\C^n)\to\Oh(U)$ is a strong homological epimorphism (Taylor's absolute
localization).  The proof base-changes the finite Koszul resolution of the
diagonal.  Convexity supplies an explicit continuous contracting homotopy on
the resulting holomorphic Koszul complex: in difference coordinates
$v=z-w$, it is $d_v(E+k)^{-1}$ on exterior degree $k$.  No boundary
regularity or boundedness is needed.  The general nonconvex Stein question
is not claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and homological reduction}

All tensor products below are completed projective tensor products.  A
Fréchet algebra homomorphism $A\to B$ is a \emph{strong homological
epimorphism} if, after applying
$B\htens_A(-)\htens_A B$ to a projective $A$-bimodule resolution of $A$,
the resulting augmented complex is admissible, i.e.\ split exact as a
complex of Fréchet spaces \cite{AP}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $U\subset\C^n$ be a nonempty convex domain.  Then restriction
\[
 \rho_U:\Oh(\C^n)\longrightarrow\Oh(U)
\]
is a strong homological epimorphism.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}
For the open Euclidean unit ball $B_n\subset\C^n$, and in particular for the
case $n=2$ singled out in \cite[Question 6.1]{AP},
$\Oh(\C^n)\to\Oh(B_n)$ is a strong homological epimorphism.
\end{corollary}

Put $A=\Oh(\C^n)$ and let $E=\C^n$ with basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$.  Under the
nuclear kernel isomorphism
\[
 A^e=A\htens A\cong\Oh(\C^n\times\C^n),
\]
the standard finite free $A$-bimodule resolution of $A$ is the augmented
Koszul complex
\begin{equation}\label{eq:globalK}
 0\longrightarrow A^e\otimes\Lambda^nE\longrightarrow\cdots
 \longrightarrow A^e\otimes E\xrightarrow{\delta}
 A^e\xrightarrow{\epsilon}A\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon f(w)=f(w,w)$ and
\[
 \delta(f\,e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_k})
 =\sum_{r=1}^k(-1)^{r-1}(z_{i_r}-w_{i_r})f\,
 e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\widehat e_{i_r}\cdots\wedge e_{i_k}.
\]
For completeness, the contracting homotopy below with $U=\C^n$ proves that
\eqref{eq:globalK} is admissible; its terms are finite free $A^e$-modules.

Let $B=\Oh(U)$.  Explicit cancellation of the balanced $A$-actions gives
\[
 B\htens_A(A^e\otimes\Lambda^kE)\htens_A B
 \cong(B\htens B)\otimes\Lambda^kE
 \cong\Oh(U\times U)\otimes\Lambda^kE.
\]
Thus the base change of \eqref{eq:globalK} is the same diagonal Koszul
complex on $U\times U$.  It remains only to prove that this complex has a
continuous contraction.

\section{The radial Koszul contraction}

Use the linear coordinates $(v,w)=(z-w,w)$ and write
\[
 D_U=\{(v,w)\in\C^n\times\C^n:w\in U,\ w+v\in U\}.
\]
Convexity says that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial}
 (v,w)\in D_U,\quad 0\le t\le1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (tv,w)\in D_U.
\end{equation}
Identify a Koszul $k$-chain with a holomorphic exterior $k$-form in the
formal basis $e_j$.  Let
\[
 d_v=\sum_{j=1}^ne_j\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial v_j},
 \qquad E=\sum_{j=1}^nv_j\frac{\partial}{\partial v_j},
\]
and let $\delta=\iota_v$ be contraction by $v$.  On exterior degree $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cartan}
 \delta d_v+d_v\delta=E+k.
\end{equation}

For $k\ge1$ define a continuous linear operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rk}
 (R_k\omega)(v,w)=\int_0^1t^{k-1}\omega(tv,w)\,dt,
\end{equation}
and for functions put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R0}
 (R_0f)(v,w)=\int_0^1\frac{f(tv,w)-f(0,w)}{t}\,dt.
\end{equation}
The integrand in \eqref{eq:R0} has a removable singularity at $t=0$.
Differentiating $t^k\omega(tv,w)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse}
 (E+k)R_k\omega=\omega\quad(k\ge1),\qquad
 ER_0f=f-Pf,
\end{equation}
where $(Pf)(v,w)=f(0,w)$.  Also
\begin{equation}\label{eq:commute}
 R_{k-1}\delta=\delta R_k\qquad(k\ge1);
\end{equation}
for $k=1$, this uses $(\delta\omega)(0,w)=0$.

Set $h_k=d_vR_k$.  Combining \eqref{eq:cartan}--\eqref{eq:commute} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:homotopy}
 \delta h_k+h_{k-1}\delta=I\quad(k\ge1),\qquad
 \delta h_0=I-P.
\end{equation}
Finally, $s:\Oh(U)\to\Oh(D_U)$, $(sg)(v,w)=g(w)$, satisfies
$\epsilon s=I$ and $s\epsilon=P$.  Hence \eqref{eq:homotopy} contracts the
entire augmented Koszul complex.

We record the topological point.  If $K\Subset D_U$, then by
\eqref{eq:radial}
\[
 K^\star=\{(tv,w):(v,w)\in K,\ 0\le t\le1\}
\]
is a compact subset of $D_U$.  For $k\ge1$, compact-open seminorms of
$R_k\omega$ on $K$ are controlled by seminorms of $\omega$ on $K^\star$.
For $R_0$, use
\[
 \frac{f(tv,w)-f(0,w)}t
 =\int_0^1\sum_{j=1}^nv_j
       \frac{\partial f}{\partial v_j}(stv,w)\,ds;
\]
boundedness of $v$ on $K$ and continuity of holomorphic differentiation give
the corresponding seminorm estimate.  Hence every $R_k$ and
$h_k=d_vR_k$ is continuous.  Thus the augmented complex is split exact as a
complex of Fréchet spaces.  By the base-change reduction above, this is
precisely the strong homological epimorphism property and proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Scope}

The proof actually isolates a useful sufficient mechanism: a continuous
contraction of the diagonal Koszul complex after restriction.  Convexity
provides one by the elementary radial formula, so the result covers
unbounded convex domains and requires no boundary regularity.

For a general Stein domain, Hefer division and Cartan theory give algebraic
exactness of related complexes, but strongness asks for continuous linear
splittings in the Fréchet topology.  Exact sequences of nuclear Fréchet
spaces need not split, and no global replacement for \eqref{eq:radial} is
provided here.  Therefore the full Stein-open-subspace question and the
source's questions on singular $C^\infty$-differentiable spaces remain open.

Eight focused upgrade routes were tested.  Bounded exact-question,
unit-ball, absolute-localization, Koszul, Hefer, corrigendum, and citation
searches on 13 August 2026 found no later resolution of the unit-ball case or
the convex-domain theorem.  Novelty is plausible, not certified.
