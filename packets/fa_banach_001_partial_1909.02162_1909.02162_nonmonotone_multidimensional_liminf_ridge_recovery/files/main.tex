\section{Source question}

Brezis and Nguyen prove in one dimension that a broad class of nonlocal,
nonconvex energies Gamma-converges without assuming that the interaction law
is nondecreasing \cite{BN1D}.  On PDF page 3 they ask whether that monotonicity
hypothesis can also be removed when $d>1$.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{The source question on PDF page 3 of \cite{BN1D}.}
\end{figure}

Let $p\geq 1$, let $\Omega\subset\R^d$ be open, and let
$\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ have the regularity assumed in
\cite{BN1D}: it is defined everywhere, continuous except at finitely many
positive points where both one-sided limits exist, and $\varphi(0)=0$.
Assume, for some $\alpha,\beta>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyp}
 \varphi(t)\leq \alpha t^{p+1}\quad(0\leq t\leq1),\qquad
 \varphi(t)\leq\beta\quad(t\geq0),\qquad
 \int_0^\infty\varphi(t)t^{-p-1}\,dt=\frac12.
\end{equation}
No monotonicity is imposed.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fd}
 \F^d_\delta(u;\Omega)
 :=\delta^p\int_\Omega\int_\Omega
 \frac{\varphi(|u(x)-u(y)|/\delta)}{|x-y|^{d+p}}\,dx\,dy.
\end{equation}
Let $\kappa=\kappa(\varphi,p)\in[0,1]$ be the one-dimensional
Gamma-limit constant of \cite[Theorem 1.1]{BN1D}, with the normalization in
\eqref{eq:hyp}, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma}
 \gamma_{d,p}:=\int_{\Sph^{d-1}}|\sigma\cdot e|^p\,d\sigma,
 \qquad c_{d,p}:=\frac{\gamma_{d,p}}2,
\end{equation}
where $e$ is any unit vector.

For $p>1$, write
\[
 \E_p(u;\Omega)=\int_\Omega|\nabla u|^p\,dx
 \quad\text{if }u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega),
\]
and $\E_p=+\infty$ otherwise.  For $p=1$, set
$\E_1(u;\Omega)=|Du|(\Omega)$ for $u\in BV(\Omega)$ and $+\infty$
otherwise.  As in \cite{BN1D}, when $\kappa=0$ the product
$\kappa\E_p$ means the zero functional on all of $L^p$.

\section{Idea of the proof}

The $d$-dimensional energy is exactly one half of the average of the
one-dimensional energies on all oriented lines.  This identity makes the
one-dimensional Gamma-liminf available on almost every slice; Fatou's lemma
then turns the averaged directional Sobolev or BV seminorm into
$\gamma_{d,p}\E_p$.  This proves the lower half of the desired Gamma-limit
without monotonicity.

For recovery, a general multidimensional gluing argument is still missing.
There is nevertheless an exact nontrivial class where no gluing is needed.
If $\Omega=I e+D$ is a cylinder and $u(te+z)=g(t)$, lift a one-dimensional
recovery family for $g$ constantly across $D$.  Integrating the Riesz kernel
over the transverse variables bounds it by
$c_{d,p}|t-s|^{-p-1}$, and the identity $c_{d,p}=\gamma_{d,p}/2$ gives exactly
the same coefficient as the global lower bound.

\section{The partial theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Global lower bound and exact ridge recovery]\label{thm:main}
Assume \eqref{eq:hyp} and let $d\geq2$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item\label{it:lower}
For every open $\Omega\subset\R^d$, every sequence $\delta_n\downarrow0$,
and every $u_n\to u$ in $L^p(\Omega)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\F^d_{\delta_n}(u_n;\Omega)
 \geq c_{d,p}\,\kappa\,\E_p(u;\Omega).
\end{equation}

\item\label{it:ridge}
Let $e\in\Sph^{d-1}$, let $I\subset\R$ be an open interval, and let
$D\subset e^\perp$ be open with finite measure.  Set
$\Omega=\{te+z:t\in I,\ z\in D\}$.  For every $g\in L^p(I)$ and
$u(te+z)=g(t)$, there is $u_\delta\to u$ in $L^p(\Omega)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ridge}
 \limsup_{\delta\downarrow0}\F^d_\delta(u_\delta;\Omega)
 \leq c_{d,p}\,\kappa\,\E_p(u;\Omega).
\end{equation}
Consequently the lower bound in \eqref{eq:lower} is sharp at every such ridge
target, even against arbitrary (not necessarily ridge) perturbing sequences.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof}

\subsection{An exact line decomposition}

Fix $\sigma\in\Sph^{d-1}$ and $z\in\sigma^\perp$, and set
\[
 \Omega_{\sigma,z}:=\{t\in\R:z+t\sigma\in\Omega\},\qquad
 u_{\sigma,z}(t):=u(z+t\sigma).
\]
Denote by $\F^1_\delta(\,cdot\,;J)$ the one-dimensional functional from
\cite{BN1D}.  Polar coordinates $y=x+h\sigma$, followed by the foliation
$x=z+t\sigma$, give the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slicing-energy}
 \F^d_\delta(u;\Omega)
 =\frac12\int_{\Sph^{d-1}}\int_{\sigma^\perp}
 \F^1_\delta(u_{\sigma,z};\Omega_{\sigma,z})\,dz\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the Jacobian $h^{d-1}$ changes $|x-y|^{-d-p}$ into
$h^{-p-1}$, and the factor $1/2$ appears because the full one-dimensional
double integral counts both orientations of every unordered pair.

\subsection{Proof of the lower bound}

It is enough to consider a subsequence along which the left side of
\eqref{eq:lower} is a limit.  Passing to a further subsequence, we may assume
that sliced $L^p$ convergence holds for almost every $(\sigma,z)$.  To see
this, use Fubini and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slice-lp}
 \int_{\Sph^{d-1}}\int_{\sigma^\perp}
 \|(u_n-u)_{\sigma,z}\|_{L^p(\Omega_{\sigma,z})}^p\,dz\,d\sigma
 =|\Sph^{d-1}|\,\|u_n-u\|_{L^p(\Omega)}^p,
\end{equation}
then choose a summable subsequence.

For almost every $(\sigma,z)$, the open set $\Omega_{\sigma,z}$ is a
countable union of disjoint intervals.  On every finite collection of those
components, nonnegativity lets us discard all other and all cross-component
interactions.  The one-dimensional Gamma-liminf theorem
\cite[Theorem 1.1]{BN1D}, followed by monotone convergence over the
components, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:line-lower}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \F^1_{\delta_n}((u_n)_{\sigma,z};\Omega_{\sigma,z})
 \geq \kappa\,\E_p^1(u_{\sigma,z};\Omega_{\sigma,z}).
\end{equation}
Fatou's lemma in \eqref{eq:slicing-energy} now gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dir-lower}
 \liminf_n\F^d_{\delta_n}(u_n;\Omega)
 \geq\frac\kappa2\int_{\Sph^{d-1}}\int_{\sigma^\perp}
 \E_p^1(u_{\sigma,z};\Omega_{\sigma,z})\,dz\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}

For $p>1$, Sobolev slicing and rotation invariance give
\begin{align*}
 \int_{\Sph^{d-1}}\int_{\sigma^\perp}
 \E_p^1(u_{\sigma,z};\Omega_{\sigma,z})\,dz\,d\sigma
 &=\int_\Omega\int_{\Sph^{d-1}}|\nabla u(x)\cdot\sigma|^p
 \,d\sigma\,dx\\
 &=\gamma_{d,p}\int_\Omega|\nabla u|^p\,dx.
\end{align*}
The standard slicing characterization also shows that finite right side in
\eqref{eq:dir-lower}, when $\kappa>0$, forces $u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)$.

For $p=1$, the BV slicing theorem and the polar decomposition
$Du=\nu_u|Du|$ give \cite[Section 3.11]{AFP}
\begin{align*}
 \int_{\Sph^{d-1}}\int_{\sigma^\perp}
 |D(u_{\sigma,z})|(\Omega_{\sigma,z})\,dz\,d\sigma
 &=\int_{\Sph^{d-1}}|D_\sigma u|(\Omega)\,d\sigma\\
 &=\int_\Omega\int_{\Sph^{d-1}}|\nu_u(x)\cdot\sigma|
 \,d\sigma\,d|Du|(x)\\
 &=\gamma_{d,1}|Du|(\Omega).
\end{align*}
Again, finite averaged slice variation characterizes $BV$; if $\kappa=0$,
the source convention makes \eqref{eq:lower} the trivial nonnegative bound.
This proves part~\ref{it:lower}.

\subsection{Proof of ridge recovery}

Rotate coordinates so that $e$ is the first coordinate vector and write
$x=(t,z)\in I\times D$.  By the one-dimensional Gamma-limsup theorem there
is $g_\delta\to g$ in $L^p(I)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-rec}
 \limsup_{\delta\downarrow0}\F^1_\delta(g_\delta;I)
 \leq\kappa\,\E_p^1(g;I).
\end{equation}
Set $u_\delta(t,z)=g_\delta(t)$.  Then
$u_\delta\to u$ in $L^p(I\times D)$, and Tonelli's theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-reduction}
 \F^d_\delta(u_\delta;I\times D)
 =\int_I\int_I \varphi_\delta(|g_\delta(t)-g_\delta(s)|)
 K_D(t-s)\,dt\,ds,
\end{equation}
where $\varphi_\delta(r)=\delta^p\varphi(r/\delta)$ and
\[
 K_D(h)=\int_D\int_D
 \bigl(h^2+|z-w|^2\bigr)^{-(d+p)/2}\,dz\,dw.
\]
For $h\neq0$, enlarge the inner transverse domain to all of $\R^{d-1}$:
\begin{align}
 K_D(h)
 &\leq |D|\int_{\R^{d-1}}
 (h^2+|q|^2)^{-(d+p)/2}\,dq\notag\\
 &=|D|\,\pi^{(d-1)/2}
 \frac{\Gamma((p+1)/2)}{\Gamma((d+p)/2)}|h|^{-p-1}
 =|D|\,c_{d,p}|h|^{-p-1}.\label{eq:marginal}
\end{align}
The last equality follows either from the beta integral or from polar
coordinates:
\[
 \gamma_{d,p}
 =2\pi^{(d-1)/2}\frac{\Gamma((p+1)/2)}{\Gamma((d+p)/2)}.
\]
Combining \eqref{eq:kernel-reduction}, \eqref{eq:marginal}, and
\eqref{eq:1d-rec},
\begin{align*}
 \limsup_{\delta\downarrow0}\F^d_\delta(u_\delta;I\times D)
 &\leq |D|c_{d,p}\kappa\E_p^1(g;I)\\
 &=c_{d,p}\kappa\E_p(u;I\times D).
\end{align*}
This is \eqref{eq:ridge}; part~\ref{it:lower} supplies the matching lower
bound against every multidimensional approximating family.
\qed

\section{Verification and limitations}

The calculation was audited in three independent ways: the polar-coordinate
line formula fixes the factor $1/2$; the beta integral computes the transverse
kernel marginal; and the two constants agree through
$c_{d,p}=\gamma_{d,p}/2$.  The proof uses only nonnegativity and the source's
one-dimensional theorem, never monotonicity of $\varphi$.

This packet does \emph{not} solve the full source question.  Its missing part
is a Gamma-limsup for arbitrary multidimensional targets.  A promising
approach is to extend the source's repeated one-dimensional cell to a scalar
map $q_\delta$ and try $u_\delta=q_\delta\circ u$.  On affine regions this has
the correct bulk mechanism, but the source cell is only controlled in
$L^p$ and may be discontinuous.  Without monotonicity, there is presently no
valid fundamental estimate controlling short-range interactions across
interfaces where affine gradients change.  Boundedness of $\varphi$ alone
produces a nonintegrable short-range majorant, so this point cannot be hidden
inside a routine gluing assertion.

The bounded novelty check searched the run's exact-id/title indexes, its
locally downloaded arXiv-source corpus through the 2026-06 build, and web
queries using the exact question, authors, and nonmonotone interaction-law
terminology.  No later primary-source solution or duplicate of this partial
theorem was located.  Novelty confidence is therefore moderate, not
exhaustive.  Human review should concentrate on the measurable
slice/subsequence step, the zero-$\kappa$ convention, and the factor $1/2$.
