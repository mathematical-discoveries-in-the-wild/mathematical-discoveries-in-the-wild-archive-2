\begin{abstract}
Let \(X_1,\dots,X_n,Y_1,\dots,Y_n\) be independent uniform points of
\([0,1]\), and let
\[
 M_n=\min_{\sigma\in S_n}\sum_{i=1}^n |X_i-Y_{\sigma(i)}|^{1/2}.
\]
Goldman and Trevisan asserted \(\limsup \E M_n/\sqrt{n\log n}<\infty\),
citing a 2020 transport estimate, and conjectured existence of the associated
finite limit.  Bobkov and Ledoux's published 2024 correction states that the
underlying proposition was false and replaces the critical empirical upper
bound by \(O(\log n/\sqrt n)\) for normalized measures.  We prove the matching
lower bound by a signed multiscale dyadic-tent witness in
Kantorovich--Rubinstein duality.  Consequently
\[
 \E M_n\asymp \sqrt n\log n,
 \qquad
 \frac{\E M_n}{\sqrt{n\log n}}\asymp\sqrt{\log n}\longrightarrow+\infty.
\]
The lower bound is elementary and quantitative.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source question and correction}

The source is Goldman and Trevisan, \emph{On the concave one-dimensional
random assignment problem and Young integration theory}, arXiv:2305.09234
\cite{GoldmanTrevisan}.  Item (2) of Section 1.3, split across printed pages 3
and 4, is reproduced below.

\smallskip
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop_part2.png}

In transcription, for iid samples with common law \(\mu\), the source asserts
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-upper}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{\E M_{1/2}((X_i)_{i=1}^n,(Y_i)_{i=1}^n)}{\sqrt{n\log n}}<\infty,
\end{equation}
then says that a natural conjecture is that the corresponding limit always
exists.  It also proves a positive liminf for uniform \(\mu\).

The citation supporting \eqref{eq:source-upper} was later corrected by its
authors.  Bobkov and Ledoux state that their Proposition 6.1 ``is not correct''
and that logarithmic corrections are necessary \cite{BobkovLedouxCorrection}:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/correction_notice_crop.png}
\end{center}

For \(d=1\) and \(\alpha=1/2\), equation (12) of the correction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected-upper}
 \E\,\zeta_{1/2}(\mu_n,\nu_n)
 \le C\frac{\log(2n)}{\sqrt n}.
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/corrected_bound_crop.png}
\end{center}

Since \(M_n=n\zeta_{1/2}(\mu_n,\nu_n)\), the corrected bound is
\(\E M_n\le C\sqrt n\log(2n)\).  It no longer implies
\eqref{eq:source-upper}.

\section{Definitions and result}

For probability measures \(\mu,\nu\) on \([0,1]\), write
\[
 W_d(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}
       \int d(x,y)\,d\pi(x,y),
 \qquad d(x,y)=|x-y|^{1/2}.
\]
The snowflake \(d\) is a metric.  For a function \(f:[0,1]\to\R\), put
\[
 [f]_{1/2}=\sup_{x\ne y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{1/2}}.
\]
Kantorovich--Rubinstein duality on the compact metric space
\(([0,1],d)\) gives \cite[Chapter 1]{Villani}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KR}
 W_d(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{[f]_{1/2}\le1}\int f\,d(\mu-\nu).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp critical order and divergent source normalization]
\label{thm:main}
Let \(X_1,X_2,\ldots,Y_1,Y_2,\ldots\) be independent
\(\Unif[0,1]\) random variables and
\[
 M_n=\min_{\sigma\in S_n}\sum_{i=1}^n|X_i-Y_{\sigma(i)}|^{1/2}.
\]
There are absolute constants \(c,C>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large
\(n\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-order}
 c\sqrt n\log n\le \E M_n\le C\sqrt n\log n.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:divergence}
 \frac{\E M_n}{\sqrt{n\log n}}\longrightarrow+\infty.
\end{equation}
Thus the uniform law contradicts the finite limsup assertion
\eqref{eq:source-upper} and the intended finite-limit conjecture following it.
More precisely, that ratio is \(\Theta(\sqrt{\log n})\).
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof intuition}

The concavity of \(t^{1/2}\) makes the optimal permutation difficult to
describe directly.  The key simplification is that this cost is also a metric.
Duality therefore lets us lower-bound the optimal assignment by exhibiting one
\(1/2\)-Hölder function.

At every dyadic scale, place a unit tent on each cell.  After seeing the two
samples, choose each tent's sign to agree with the corresponding empirical
discrepancy, and multiply all level-\(j\) tents by \(2^{-j/2}\).  This is the
critical coefficient: arbitrary signs and arbitrarily many levels still give
a uniformly \(1/2\)-Hölder sum.  A tent on a cell of length \(h\) sees a
discrepancy of expected size at least a constant times \(\sqrt{nh}\).  There
are \(h^{-1}\) cells and the coefficient is \(\sqrt h\), so each level
contributes order \(\sqrt n\).  The levels from scale one through scale
\(1/n\) then add, producing the missing \(\log n\).

\section{The multiscale lower bound}

Let
\[
 \mu_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{X_i},\qquad
 \nu_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{Y_i}.
\]
The finite transport linear program has an optimal permutation (equivalently,
use the Birkhoff--von Neumann theorem), so \eqref{eq:KR} yields, pointwise in
the samples,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:assignment-dual}
 M_n=nW_d(\mu_n,\nu_n)
 =\sup_{[f]_{1/2}\le1}\sum_{i=1}^n(f(X_i)-f(Y_i)).
\end{equation}

For \(j\ge0\) and \(0\le k<2^j\), let
\[
 I_{j,k}=[k2^{-j},(k+1)2^{-j}]
\]
and define the unit tent
\[
 \phi_{j,k}(x)=
 \max\!\left\{1-2^{j+1}\left|x-(k+\tfrac12)2^{-j}\right|,0\right\}.
\]
These are the usual dyadic Faber--Schauder tents \cite{Schauder}.  At a fixed
level their interiors are disjoint and they vanish at the cell endpoints.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform signed Hölder bound]\label{lem:holder}
For every integer \(J\ge1\) and every choice
\(\varepsilon_{j,k}\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 f_J(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}2^{-j/2}
          \sum_{k=0}^{2^j-1}\varepsilon_{j,k}\phi_{j,k}(x)
\]
satisfies \([f_J]_{1/2}<14\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write the level-\(j\) summand as \(g_j\).  Disjointness within a level gives
\(\|g_j\|_\infty\le2^{-j/2}\), while its continuous piecewise-affine graph
has slopes of absolute value at most \(2^{j/2+1}\).  Hence, for
\(r=|x-y|\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:level-bound}
 |g_j(x)-g_j(y)|
 \le\min\{2\,2^{-j/2},\,2^{j/2+1}r\}.
\end{equation}
If \(0<r\le1\), choose \(m\ge0\) so that
\(2^{-(m+1)}<r\le2^{-m}\).  Using the slope bound for \(j\le m\) and the
amplitude bound for \(j>m\), and harmlessly extending both finite sums to the
corresponding infinite ones, gives
\begin{align*}
 |f_J(x)-f_J(y)|
 &\le 2r\sum_{j=0}^{m}2^{j/2}
      +2\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}2^{-j/2}\\
 &<\left(\frac{2\sqrt2}{\sqrt2-1}
       +\frac{2}{1-2^{-1/2}}\right)\sqrt r
 <14\sqrt r.
\end{align*}
Here \(r2^{m/2}\le\sqrt r\) and
\(2^{-(m+1)/2}<\sqrt r\).  This proves the claim.
\end{proof}

For each cell define the empirical tent discrepancy
\[
 S_{j,k}=\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_{j,k}(X_i)
          -\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_{j,k}(Y_i).
\]
Choose \(\varepsilon_{j,k}=\operatorname{sgn}(S_{j,k})\), with either sign
when \(S_{j,k}=0\).  The test function \(f_J/14\) is admissible in
\eqref{eq:assignment-dual}; crucially, dependence on the realized samples is
legitimate because the supremum is evaluated separately for every realization.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-lower}
 M_n\ge\frac1{14}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}2^{-j/2}
             \sum_{k=0}^{2^j-1}|S_{j,k}|.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[One-cell discrepancy]\label{lem:cell}
There is an absolute constant \(c_0>0\) such that, for any dyadic cell of
length \(h\) and its unit tent \(\phi\),
\[
 \E\left|\sum_{i=1}^n(\phi(X_i)-\phi(Y_i))\right|
 \ge c_0\sqrt{nh}
\]
whenever \(nh\ge12\).  One may take \(c_0=(16\sqrt{12})^{-1}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(U\sim\Unif[0,1]\), direct integration of the triangular function gives
\[
 \E\phi(U)=\frac h2,
 \qquad
 \E\phi(U)^2=\frac h3.
\]
For independent \(U,V\), put \(Z=\phi(U)-\phi(V)\).  Then \(Z\) is centered
and symmetric, \(|Z|\le1\), and
\[
 v:=\E Z^2=2\left(\frac h3-\frac{h^2}{4}\right)
 \ge\frac h6.
\]
For \(S=\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i\), independence and centering give
\[
 \E S^4=n\E Z^4+3n(n-1)v^2
 \le nv+3n^2v^2.
\]
If \(nh\ge12\), then \(nv\ge2\), and therefore
\(\E S^4\le4(nv)^2\).  Applying Paley--Zygmund to \(S^2\) at threshold
\(\tfrac12\E S^2\) gives
\[
 \Pr\{S^2\ge\tfrac12nv\}\ge\frac1{16}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \E|S|\ge\frac1{16}\sqrt{\frac{nv}{2}}
 \ge\frac1{16\sqrt{12}}\sqrt{nh}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}]
For \(n\ge12\), let
\[
 L_n=1+\left\lfloor\log_2(n/12)\right\rfloor
\]
and use \eqref{eq:dual-lower} with \(J=L_n\).  At every included level
\(j\), the cell length \(h=2^{-j}\) satisfies \(nh\ge12\).  Lemma
\ref{lem:cell} and the \(2^j\) cells yield
\begin{align*}
 2^{-j/2}\sum_{k=0}^{2^j-1}\E|S_{j,k}|
 &\ge 2^{-j/2}\,2^j c_0\sqrt{n2^{-j}}\\
 &=c_0\sqrt n.
\end{align*}
Taking expectations in \eqref{eq:dual-lower} and summing levels gives
\[
 \E M_n\ge\frac{c_0}{14}L_n\sqrt n
 \ge c\sqrt n\log n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  This proves the lower bound without any
external asymptotic theorem.

For the upper bound, apply Bobkov--Ledoux's corrected equation (12),
\eqref{eq:corrected-upper}, to the two empirical measures.  Since
\(M_n=n\zeta_{1/2}(\mu_n,\nu_n)\),
\[
 \E M_n\le C\sqrt n\log(2n),
\]
which is \(O(\sqrt n\log n)\).  This proves \eqref{eq:main-order}.  Dividing
by \(\sqrt{n\log n}\) proves \eqref{eq:divergence} and the stated
\(\Theta(\sqrt{\log n})\) refinement.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and audit}

The reusable checker \texttt{code/verify\_multiscale\_witness.py} performs
three supplementary tests:
\begin{enumerate}
\item it checks the exact moment inequalities at fourteen dyadic levels;
\item for twelve random sign systems on ten levels, it samples three million
point pairs on a 4097-point grid and checks the Hölder ratio against 14;
\item for twenty random instances at each of \(n=32,64,128,256\), it compares
the normalized witness against the exact Hungarian assignment cost.
\end{enumerate}
The command and complete output are recorded in \texttt{verification.md}.
These finite checks are regression evidence only; Lemmas \ref{lem:holder} and
\ref{lem:cell} are the proof.

The following proof-sensitive points were audited explicitly.
\begin{itemize}
\item The cost \(|x-y|^{1/2}\) is a metric, so the standard
Kantorovich--Rubinstein duality applies; this would fail for an arbitrary
concave cost.
\item The witness signs may depend on the samples because duality is a
pointwise supremum before expectation.
\item Adjacent tents with opposite signs cause no discontinuity: every tent
vanishes at its cell endpoints, and the global slope bound used in
\eqref{eq:level-bound} remains valid.
\item No independence between different dyadic cells or levels is used.
Linearity of expectation is enough after the one-cell estimate.
\item The supporting upper bound is cited from the 2024 correction, not from
the invalid 2020 formulation used in the source paper.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope, novelty search, and limitations}

The theorem settles the critical order for the uniform law and therefore
supplies a counterexample to the asserted finite limsup and intended finite
limit.  It does not classify all common laws \(\mu\), nor does it claim that
the normalized sequence lacks an extended-real limit.  For this example it
has the extended-real limit \(+\infty\).

A bounded novelty search was conducted through 13 August 2026 using the four
run indexes, arXiv/web exact-title searches, citation searches around
arXiv:2305.09234 and the 2024 correction, and combinations of the phrases
``\(M_{1/2}\)'', ``\(\sqrt n\log n\)'', ``Zolotarev empirical measures'',
and ``Faber--Schauder matching''.  The search found the source, the correction,
and unrelated matching work, but no paper explicitly connecting the
correction to the source conjecture or proving the dyadic-tent lower bound.
This is evidence of novelty, not a guarantee.  A human should repeat the
literature search, check the Hölder constant and fourth-moment step, and decide
whether the source's wording warrants describing the result as a
``counterexample'' or more narrowly as a ``sharp critical-rate correction''.
