\title{Haar-countable sets need not form an ideal:\\
a closed Haar-1 counterexample in $\R^\omega$}
\author{Run \texttt{fa\_banach\_001}, agent lane 9}
\date{17 August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We give a candidate full negative answer to Question 7.5 of Adam Kwela's
\emph{Haar-smallest sets} in the unrestricted class of abelian Polish groups.
In the group $G=\R^\omega$ we construct two closed Haar-1 sets $F_Y,F_Z$ such
that $F_Y\cup F_Z$ is not Haar-countable.  The construction applies a
universal closed-set theorem of Banakh--G\l{}\k{a}b--Jab\l{}o\'nska--Swaczyna
to two Borel families of compacta.  A Lusin--Novikov/perfect-set dichotomy
then defeats every Cantor witness for the union.  The result does not address
the stronger possible interpretation in which the ambient group is required
to be $\R$ or, more generally, locally compact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source and exact question}

The source is Adam Kwela, \emph{Haar-smallest sets}, arXiv:1711.09753
\cite{kwela}.  At the end of Section 7 the author asks:
\begin{quote}\small
``Question 7.5. Is the family of all Haar-countable sets an ideal?''
\end{quote}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth,trim=0 0 72 0,clip]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The paper works with subsets of abelian Polish groups, although many of its
constructions in the surrounding sections lie in $\R$.  We answer the
unrestricted group-level formulation.  Section~\ref{sec:scope} isolates the
remaining scope issue.

\section{Definitions and the universal tool}

A \emph{Cantor set} in a Polish space means a subspace homeomorphic to
$2^\omega$.  A subset $A$ of an abelian Polish group $X$ is
\emph{Haar-countable} if there are a Borel set $B\supseteq A$ and a Cantor
set $C\subseteq X$ such that
\[
       (C+x)\cap B \quad\text{is countable for every }x\in X.
\]
It is \emph{Haar-1} if the same condition holds with
$|(C+x)\cap B|\leq 1$ for every $x$.  Thus every Haar-1 set is
Haar-countable.

For a Polish space $X$, let $\K(X)$ be the Vietoris hyperspace of nonempty
compact subsets of $X$.  We use the following result verbatim in substance.

\begin{theorem}[Universal closed-set theorem \cite{banakh-et-al}]
\label{thm:universal}
For every nonempty analytic subspace $\cA\subseteq\K(\R^\omega)$ there is a
closed set $F\subseteq\R^\omega$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item for every $K\in\cA$ there is $a\in\R^\omega$ with $K+a\subseteq F$;
\item for every $x\in\R^\omega$, the set
$F\cap(x+[0,1]^\omega)$ is contained in $K+d$ for some $K\in\cA$ and
$d\in\R^\omega$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

This is Theorem 6.1 of \cite{banakh-et-al}.  Clause (i) is a universal
\emph{absorption} property; clause (ii) is a local \emph{shape control}
property.  We apply the theorem twice, to complementary shapes.

\section{Proof intuition}

Write $G=\R^\omega=Y\times Z$, where $Y$ consists of the even coordinates and
$Z$ of the odd coordinates.  One universal closed set will locally resemble
compact graphs over $Z$; a Cantor set moving only in the $Y$ coordinates
therefore meets each translate in at most one point.  The other universal set
interchanges $Y$ and $Z$.

Why can their union be large?  Every Cantor set $C\subseteq Y\times Z$
contains a Cantor subset which is a graph in one of the two coordinate
directions.  If an uncountable $Z$-fiber exists, use a Cantor subset of that
fiber, on which the $Y$-projection is injective.  If all $Z$-fibers are
countable, Lusin--Novikov decomposes $C$ into countably many graphs over $Z$;
one graph is uncountable and contains a Cantor set.  Universal absorption then
translates this Cantor subset into one of the two closed sets.  Thus every
candidate witness has a translate with uncountable intersection with the
union.

\section{The two analytic hyperspace families}

Let $\pi_Y:G\to Y$ and $\pi_Z:G\to Z$ be the coordinate projections, and put
\[
 \cK_Z=\{K\in\K(G):\pi_Z|_K\text{ is injective}\},\qquad
 \cK_Y=\{K\in\K(G):\pi_Y|_K\text{ is injective}\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:borel}
The families $\cK_Y$ and $\cK_Z$ are nonempty Borel subspaces of $\K(G)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Nonemptiness is immediate, for instance from singletons.  We prove the Borel
claim for $\cK_Z$; the other case is symmetric.  Fix compatible metrics $d$
on $G$ and $d_Z$ on $Z$.  For $r,s\geq1$, let
\[
 U_{r,s}=\left\{K\in\K(G):
   \forall u,v\in K,\quad d(u,v)<\frac1r\ \text{or}\
   d_Z(\pi_Zu,\pi_Zv)>\frac1s\right\}.
\]
For fixed $r,s$, the displayed disjunction defines an open subset of
$G\times G$.  The set of compacta $K$ whose square is contained in that open
set is open in the Vietoris hyperspace: compactness of $K^2$ gives a finite
product-neighborhood refinement which persists for all sufficiently close
compacta.  Hence every $U_{r,s}$ is open.

We claim
\[
             \cK_Z=\bigcap_{r\geq1}\bigcup_{s\geq1}U_{r,s}.
\]
If $\pi_Z|_K$ is injective, then on the compact set
\(
E_r=\{(u,v)\in K^2:d(u,v)\geq 1/r\}
\)
the continuous function
$d_Z(\pi_Zu,\pi_Zv)$ is strictly positive and therefore has a positive
minimum.  A suitable $s$ gives $K\in U_{r,s}$.  Conversely, if two distinct
points $u,v\in K$ have the same $Z$-projection, choose $r$ with
$d(u,v)\geq1/r$; then $K\notin U_{r,s}$ for every $s$.  The claimed formula
follows, so $\cK_Z$ is Borel and in particular analytic.
\end{proof}

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:universal} to $\cK_Z$ and $\cK_Y$.  We obtain closed
sets $F_Z,F_Y\subseteq G$ such that, for $i\in\{Y,Z\}$,
\begin{align}
&\text{every }K\in\cK_i\text{ has a translate contained in }F_i,
\label{eq:absorb}\\
&F_i\cap(x+[0,1]^\omega)\subseteq K+d
  \text{ for some }K\in\cK_i,d\in G.                 \label{eq:local}
\end{align}

\section{Each universal set is Haar-1}

Identify $Y$ and $Z$ with their corresponding coordinate subgroups of $G$.
Inside $Q=[0,1]^\omega$, define
\[
 C_Y=\{x\in G:x(2n)\in\{0,1\},\ x(2n+1)=0\text{ for all }n\},
\]
and define $C_Z$ symmetrically, with the odd coordinates varying in
$\{0,1\}$ and the even coordinates equal to zero.  Both are Cantor sets.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:haar1}
$F_Z$ is Haar-1, witnessed by $C_Y$, and $F_Y$ is Haar-1, witnessed by $C_Z$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x\in G$.  Since $C_Y+x\subseteq Q+x$, the localization property
\eqref{eq:local} gives
\[
 (C_Y+x)\cap F_Z\subseteq (C_Y+x)\cap(K+d)
\]
for some $K\in\cK_Z$ and $d\in G$.  Every point of $C_Y+x$ has the same
$Z$-projection, while $\pi_Z$ is injective on $K+d$.  The intersection on
the right therefore has at most one point.  Since $F_Z$ is closed and $x$
was arbitrary, $C_Y$ witnesses that $F_Z$ is Haar-1.  Interchanging $Y$ and
$Z$ proves the assertion for $F_Y$.
\end{proof}

\section{Every Cantor set contains one of the two shapes}

\begin{lemma}[Coordinate dichotomy]\label{lem:dichotomy}
Every Cantor set $C\subseteq Y\times Z$ contains a Cantor set
$K\in\cK_Y\cup\cK_Z$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Consider the fibers of $\pi_Z|_C:C\to Z$.

First suppose that some fiber
\[
       C_z=C\cap(Y\times\{z\})
\]
is uncountable.  It is a closed subset of the compact metrizable space $C$,
hence an uncountable Polish space.  By the perfect set theorem it contains a
Cantor set $K$.  The map $\pi_Y$ is injective on $C_z$, so
$K\in\cK_Y$.

Now suppose every fiber of $\pi_Z|_C$ is countable.  Regard $C$, after
swapping the coordinates, as a closed relation in $Z\times Y$ with countable
vertical sections.  The Lusin--Novikov uniformization theorem decomposes
$C$ into countably many Borel partial graphs.  Equivalently, there are Borel
sets $B_n\subseteq C$ with
\[
                   C=\bigcup_{n\in\N}B_n
\]
such that $\pi_Z$ is injective on each $B_n$.  Since $C$ is uncountable, some
$B_n$ is uncountable.  An uncountable Borel subset of a Polish space contains
a Cantor set $K$.  Then $\pi_Z|_K$ is injective, so $K\in\cK_Z$.
\end{proof}

\section{The counterexample}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
In the abelian Polish group $G=\R^\omega$ there are two closed Haar-1 sets
whose union is not Haar-countable.  Consequently, Haar-countable subsets do
not form an ideal in the unrestricted class of abelian Polish groups.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take the closed Haar-1 sets $F_Y,F_Z$ constructed above.  We show that
$F=F_Y\cup F_Z$ is not Haar-countable.

Let $C\subseteq G$ be an arbitrary Cantor set.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:dichotomy}, $C$ contains a Cantor set
$K\in\cK_Y\cup\cK_Z$.  If $K\in\cK_i$, the absorption property
\eqref{eq:absorb} gives a translation vector $a\in G$ such that
$K+a\subseteq F_i$.  Therefore
\[
          (C+a)\cap F\supseteq K+a,
\]
and the intersection is uncountable.

This argument applies to every Cantor set $C$.  Moreover, if $B$ is any
Borel hull of $F$, then $(C+a)\cap B$ contains the same uncountable set
$K+a$.  Thus no Cantor set can witness Haar-countability of $F$, and
$F\notin\HC$.  On the other hand, Lemma~\ref{lem:haar1} gives
$F_Y,F_Z\in\HC$.  Hence $\HC$ is not closed under finite unions and is not an
ideal.
\end{proof}

The conclusion is stronger than the minimum required for non-ideality: both
summands are closed and Haar-1, the smallest nontrivial level in the hierarchy.

\section{Compatibility with the source's Theorem 2.3}
\label{sec:compatibility}

The source paper prints a statement, Theorem 2.3, asserting that a Haar-$n$
set and a Haar-$m$ set have Haar-$(n+m)$ union.  Read literally for arbitrary
subsets of arbitrary abelian Polish groups, that statement would contradict
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  The proof printed immediately below Theorem 2.3,
however, invokes the fact that \emph{$\sigma$-compact} Haar-$k$ sets are
generically Haar-$k$ and then applies this genericity conclusion to the two
sets without having assumed $\sigma$-compactness.

The cited background result appears in the present version of
\cite{banakh-et-al} as Theorem 16.5(3), and it explicitly has the
$\sigma$-compact hypothesis.  Closed subsets of the non-locally-compact
Polish group $\R^\omega$ need not be $\sigma$-compact, and the universal
construction supplies no such conclusion for $F_Y$ or $F_Z$.  Thus the
counterexample is consistent with the proved genericity theorem and exposes
the missing hypothesis in the broader printed finite-union assertion.  This
point deserves particular human scrutiny because it is also an independent
correction to the source paper.

\section{Scope, novelty audit, and review status}
\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{Scope.}
The theorem gives a complete negative answer if Question 7.5 asks whether
Haar-countable sets form an ideal in every abelian Polish group.  It does not
construct a counterexample in $\R$.  The proof uses the non-locally-compact
group $\R^\omega$ and a universal theorem special to that setting.  No
relevant topological group embedding into $\R$ exists, and arbitrary Borel
coding does not preserve Haar-countability.  Therefore the real-line or
locally compact version, if that was the intended question, remains open in
this packet.

\paragraph{Version and literature audit.}
Bounded searches on 13--17 August 2026 used the exact question, the source
title and arXiv identifier, the author's name, and combinations of
\emph{Haar-countable}, \emph{ideal}, and $\R^\omega$.  No later paper or note
explicitly answering Question 7.5 was found.  The current arXiv abstract says
that Haar-countable sets do not form an ideal, while the current source TeX,
the PDF, and the published paper still pose Question 7.5.  We treat the
abstract sentence as a metadata inconsistency rather than as a documented
solution.  The universal theorem itself is published and cited below; the
application through the two coordinate-injective hyperspace families was not
found in the bounded search.

\paragraph{Status and human review.}
This packet is a candidate full, likely valid counterexample for the
unrestricted abelian-Polish-group formulation.  The four points to check most
carefully are Lemma~\ref{lem:borel}, the two applications of
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal}, the Lusin--Novikov step in
Lemma~\ref{lem:dichotomy}, and the intended ambient-group scope of Question
7.5.  Section~\ref{sec:compatibility} should also be checked against the
precise version history of the source's cited background theorem.
