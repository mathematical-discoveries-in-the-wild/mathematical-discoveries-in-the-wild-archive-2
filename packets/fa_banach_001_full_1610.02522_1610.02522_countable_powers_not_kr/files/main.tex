\noindent\fbox{\parbox{0.94\linewidth}{\textbf{Status: candidate full result,
likely valid.}  In ZFC,
\[
   (S_2)^\omega\quad\text{and}\quad(S_\omega)^\omega
\]
are both \emph{not} $k_R$-spaces.  The proof embeds the paper's metric
test space $P_0$ as a closed subspace of a tail of either power.  The
$S_\omega$ case then contradicts the strict closed-fan obstruction already
proved in the source.  The $S_2$ case descends to the same obstruction
through the standard perfect quotient \(S_2\to S_\omega\).}}

\section{The question and idea of the proof}

Lin, Lin, and Liu ask whether the countable powers $(S_2)^\omega$ and
$(S_\omega)^\omega$, known not to be $k$-spaces, might nevertheless be
$k_R$-spaces \cite[Question~5.11]{LLL}.  Here $S_2$ is the Arens space and
$S_\omega$ is the sequential fan.

The source also introduces the metrizable space
\[
  P_0=\{p\}\cup\bigcup_{i\in\N}X_i,
\]
where every $X_i$ is countably infinite, discrete, and open, and
\[
  V_k=\{p\}\cup\bigcup_{i\ge k}X_i\qquad(k\in\N)
\]
is a neighborhood base at $p$.  Its Lemma~3.6 proves
\[
                 S_\omega\times P_0\ \text{is not a }k_R\text{-space}.
\tag{1}\label{eq:obstruction}
\]

The missing observation is that $P_0$ occurs canonically and \emph{closedly}
inside a countable product of any space containing a closed countably
infinite discrete subspace.  Both $S_2$ and $S_\omega$ have such a
subspace.  Stratifiability then makes the closed embedding useful: closed
subspaces of stratifiable $k_R$-spaces are $k_R$ \cite[Prop.~5.11]{BG}.

\section{A universal closed copy of \texorpdfstring{$P_0$}{P0}}

Give $\N$ the discrete topology and distinguish $0\in\N$.  In Baire
space $\N^\omega$, put
\[
 E=\{0^\omega\}\cup\{e_{i,m}:i\in\omega,\ m\in\N\},
 \qquad
 e_{i,m}(j)=
 \begin{cases}
 m,&j=i,\\
 0,&j\ne i.
 \end{cases}
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:P0}
The set $E$ is closed in $\N^\omega$ and is homeomorphic to $P_0$.
Consequently, if a space $T$ contains a closed countably infinite discrete
subspace, then $T^\omega$ contains a closed copy of $P_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The complement of $E$ consists exactly of sequences with at least two
nonzero coordinates.  Specifying those two coordinates gives an open
cylinder disjoint from $E$, so $E$ is closed.

Each $e_{i,m}$ is isolated in $E$.  If
$X_i=\{e_{i,m}:m\in\N\}$ and $p=0^\omega$, then the cylinder fixing the
first $k$ coordinates to zero meets $E$ in
\[
 \{p\}\cup\bigcup_{i\ge k}X_i.
\]
These cylinders form a neighborhood base at $p$, which is precisely the
definition of $P_0$.

If $D\subset T$ is closed, countably infinite, and discrete, then
$D^\omega$ is closed in $T^\omega$ and is homeomorphic to $\N^\omega$.
The displayed $E\subset D^\omega$ is therefore the required closed copy.
\end{proof}

For completeness, the needed discrete sets can be chosen explicitly.  Write
\[
 S_\omega=\{a_0\}\cup\{a(n,m):n,m\in\N\},
\]
where every $a(n,m)$ is isolated and a neighborhood of $a_0$ contains a
tail of every branch.  Then
\[
       D_\omega=\{a(n,1):n\in\N\}
\]
is closed and discrete: the neighborhood with branch threshold $2$
misses all of it.  In the usual notation
\[
 S_2=\{\infty\}\cup\{x_n:n\in\N\}
       \cup\{x_n(m):n,m\in\N\},
\]
the set $D_2=\{x_n(1):n\in\N\}$ is likewise closed and discrete.  Tail
neighborhoods of each $x_n$, followed by the defining neighborhoods of
$\infty$, separate every nonmember from $D_2$.

\section{\texorpdfstring{\hspace{0.35em}}{}The obstruction \texorpdfstring{$S_\omega\times P_0$}{Somega x P0}}

We recall why \eqref{eq:obstruction} holds.  Enumerate
$X_n=\{b(n,m):m\in\N\}$ and set
\[
 F_n=\{(a(n,m),b(n,m)):m\in\N\}\subset S_\omega\times P_0.
\]
Every $F_n$ is clopen.  Indeed, its points are isolated; and since $X_n$ is
clopen in $P_0$, a point outside $F_n$ is separated either from $X_n$ in
the second coordinate or from the unique paired point in the first
coordinate.  The family $\{F_n:n\in\N\}$ is
compact-finite: the projection of a compact subset of $S_\omega$ can meet
only finitely many branches away from $a_0$.  To see the latter fact,
choosing one point from each of infinitely many met branches would produce
an infinite closed discrete subspace, because one may choose the branch
thresholds at $a_0$ to miss all selected points.

On the other hand, the family is not locally finite at $(a_0,p)$.  Given a
neighborhood of this point, choose $n$ beyond the prescribed $P_0$-tail
and then $m$ beyond the prescribed threshold on the $n$-th fan branch;
the neighborhood meets $F_n$.  Because the $F_n$ are clopen, they form a
strict closed fan.  A $k_R$-space contains no strict closed fan
\cite[Lemma~3.2]{LLL}, proving \eqref{eq:obstruction}.

\section{The negative answer}

We use three standard facts, recording their exact role.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Stratifiable spaces are also called $M_3$-spaces, and countable
products of $M_3$-spaces are $M_3$ \cite[Thm.~2.4]{Ceder}.
The sequential fan is a closed image of a metrizable topological sum of
convergent sequences, hence is La\v{s}nev and therefore stratifiable.  The
Arens space $S_2$ is a $1$-paracomplex and hence stratifiable
\cite[Thm.~2.2 and introduction]{Kim}.

\item If $A$ is closed in a stratifiable $k_R$-space $X$, then $A$
is $k_R$ \cite[Prop.~5.11]{BG}.

\item Quotient images of $k_R$-spaces are $k_R$.  Indeed, if
$q:X\to Y$ is quotient and $g:Y\to\mathbb R$ is continuous on compact
subsets, then $g\circ q$ is continuous on each compact $K\subset X$
because $q(K)$ is compact.  Hence $g\circ q$ is continuous, and the
quotient property makes $g$ continuous.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Neither $(S_\omega)^\omega$ nor $(S_2)^\omega$ is a $k_R$-space.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First suppose that $(S_\omega)^\omega$ is $k_R$.  Split off its first
coordinate.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:P0}, its remaining coordinates contain a
closed copy of $P_0$; hence the whole power contains a closed copy of
$S_\omega\times P_0$.  The power is stratifiable by Fact~1, so Fact~2
would make this closed subspace $k_R$, contradicting
\eqref{eq:obstruction}.

Now suppose that $(S_2)^\omega$ is $k_R$.  The same construction gives a
closed copy
\[
                   S_2\times P_0\subset (S_2)^\omega.
\]
Again the ambient power is stratifiable, so $S_2\times P_0$ is $k_R$.

Collapse the compact convergent sequence
$C_0=\{\infty\}\cup\{x_n:n\in\N\}\subset S_2$ to one point.  The resulting
quotient is $S_\omega$.  The quotient map
\(q:S_2\to S_\omega\) is perfect: for a closed $F\subset S_2$, its
saturation is either $F$ or $F\cup C_0$, so $q$ is closed; its exceptional
fiber is the compact set $C_0$, and all other fibers are singletons.  Perfect maps
are stable under products, so
\[
       q\times\operatorname{id}_{P_0}:S_2\times P_0
          \longrightarrow S_\omega\times P_0
\]
is a closed quotient map.  Fact~3 would make $S_\omega\times P_0$ a
$k_R$-space, once more contradicting \eqref{eq:obstruction}.
\end{proof}

\section{Audit, scope, and novelty}

The proof is entirely in ZFC.  It uses no cardinal-characteristic
hypothesis, no unverified compactness classification, and no claim that the
$k_R$-property is closed-hereditary in arbitrary spaces.  Stratifiability
is essential at precisely the two closed-subspace steps.  The quotient step
is also safe: $q\times\operatorname{id}_{P_0}$ is quotient because it is
perfect, not because arbitrary products preserve quotient maps.

The construction answers both parts of Question~5.11, but it does not settle
the source's broader Question~5.9 about arbitrary products of sequential
spaces.  It also does not address the other open questions about free
Abelian topological groups.

Exact-title, exact-question, $S_2^\omega$, $S_\omega^\omega$, $k_R$,
and citation searches through 11 August 2026 did not locate a published
answer to Question~5.11.  The closest later items found study unrelated
properties of $S_2^\omega$, or establish the stratifiability input for
$S_2$, but do not state this $k_R$-conclusion.  The core argument is a
new combination of the source's own obstruction with the elementary closed
$P_0$-embedding.  Novelty confidence is moderate and independent expert
review is requested.
