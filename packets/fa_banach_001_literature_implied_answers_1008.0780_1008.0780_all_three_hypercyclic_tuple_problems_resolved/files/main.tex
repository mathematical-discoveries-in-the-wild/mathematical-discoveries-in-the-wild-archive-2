\section{Problems 1--2: exact Toeplitz counts}

Abels and Manoussos, \emph{Topological generators of abelian Lie groups and
hypercyclic finitely generated abelian semigroups of matrices},
arXiv:1108.1123~\cite{abels}, compute in Table~3 on arXiv PDF page~11 the
minimum number of generators for the groups of invertible $n\times n$
Toeplitz matrices:
\[
 \boxed{2n\ \text{over }\C},\qquad
 \boxed{n+1\ \text{over }\R}.
\]
Their proof identifies the invertible Toeplitz group as an abelian Lie group
with a dense orbit and applies their topological-generator theorem.  The
complex group has real noncompact dimension $2n-1$ (its maximal torus is
$S^1$), while the identity component of the real group has noncompact
dimension $n$; a connected abelian Lie group of noncompact dimension $d$
needs and admits $d+1$ dense semigroup generators.

There is a small identification needed because the source problems do not
require the generators to be invertible.  Let $J$ be the nilpotent backward
shift and write a triangular Toeplitz matrix as
\[
 A=a_0I+a_1J+\cdots+a_{n-1}J^{n-1}.
\]
Its last coordinate acts by multiplication by $a_0$, so $A$ is singular
exactly when $a_0=0$.  If a product uses a singular generator, its image has
last coordinate zero.  Consequently, inside the open set
\[
 U=\{x\in\mathbb K^n:x_n\ne0\},\qquad \mathbb K\in\{\R,\C\},
\]
every point of a tuple orbit comes from the subtuple of invertible
generators.  If the full orbit is dense, that invertible subtuple already has
a dense orbit in $U$.  Conversely, the invertible Toeplitz group acts simply
transitively on $U$ via $A\mapsto Ae_n$, and $U$ is dense in $\mathbb K^n$.
Thus singular generators cannot lower the minimum, and the two entries of
Abels--Manoussos Table~3 answer Problems~1--2 exactly.

\section{Problem 3: a common-eigenline obstruction}

The negative answer follows from the classical simultaneous-eigenvector
argument for commuting complex matrices.

\begin{proposition}
Let $n\ge3$ and let $A_1,\ldots,A_r\in M_n(\R)$ commute.  There is a nonzero
proper real subspace $W\subset\R^n$, of dimension at most two, invariant under
every $A_j$.  Hence no such family can have every nonzero vector hypercyclic.
In particular, the answer to Problem~3 is no.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Complexify the matrices.  A finite commuting family of complex matrices has a
common eigenvector: choose an eigenspace of $A_1$; commutativity makes it
invariant under all remaining matrices, then successively choose eigenspaces
of their restrictions.  Hence there are $v\in\C^n\setminus\{0\}$ and
$\lambda_j\in\C$ such that
\[
 A_jv=\lambda_jv\qquad(1\le j\le r).
\]
Put
\[
 W=\operatorname{span}_{\R}\{\operatorname{Re}v,
                              \operatorname{Im}v\}\subseteq\R^n.
\]
This is nonzero and has dimension at most two.  Since every $A_j$ is real,
the displayed eigenvalue equations show that $A_jW\subseteq W$.  When
$n\ge3$, $W$ is proper.  The orbit of any $x\in W\setminus\{0\}$ under the
generated semigroup remains in the closed proper subspace $W$, so it cannot
be dense in $\R^n$.
\end{proof}
