\section*{Source question and result}

For a rearrangement-invariant quasi-Banach function space $Z\subset L^1(\T)$,
write
\[
 H[Z]=\{f\in Z:\wh f(n)=0\text{ for every }n<0\}
\]
and denote Banach envelopes by a superscript ${}^{\wedge}$.  Question 5.8
of Le\'snik (Question 4 in the arXiv source) asks whether
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
 H[Z]^{\wedge}=H[Z^{\wedge}]
\end{equation}
for every separable rearrangement-invariant quasi-Banach function space
$Z\subset L^1$.  The source proves this when $Z$ has nontrivial Boyd
indices, using boundedness of the Riesz projection on $Z$.

We settle a genuine missing endpoint family.  For $0<q<1$, let
\[
 \|f\|_{1,q}=\left(\int_0^1[t f^*(t)]^q\,\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/q},
 \qquad Z=L^{1,q}(\T).
\]
Here the upper Boyd index is $1$, so the source lemma does not apply.

\begin{theorem}[Lorentz endpoint]\label{thm:main}
For every $0<q<1$, the canonical identity on analytic polynomials extends
to an isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 H[L^{1,q}(\T)]^{\wedge}\simeq H^1(\T)
 =H[(L^{1,q}(\T))^{\wedge}].
\end{equation}
Thus the answer to \eqref{eq:source} is affirmative for all
$Z=L^{1,q}$, $0<q<1$.
\end{theorem}

\section*{Idea of the Proof}

The lattice envelope of $L^{1,q}$ is $L^1$.  The missing point is that the
analytic subspace has no larger envelope.  Project a normalized real
$H^1$ atom onto its nonnegative Fourier modes.  An $L^2$ estimate near its
support and cancellation away from the support give a two-piece decreasing
rearrangement bound.  This bound is uniformly integrable in the
$L^{1,q}$ quasi-norm.  A finite atomic decomposition of the real part of an
analytic polynomial then bounds its analytic envelope norm by its $H^1$
norm.  The opposite inequality is the elementary embedding
$L^{1,q}\hookrightarrow L^1$.

To pass from polynomials to the full $H[L^{1,q}]$, we prove a density lemma
which does not use the Riesz projection.  Inner--outer factorization and an
outer cutoff approximate any $H[Z]$ function by bounded analytic functions;
radial approximation then gives analytic polynomials.

\section*{Two lemmas}

Let $P_+$ denote the nonnegative-frequency projection when it is applied to
a trigonometric polynomial or an $L^2$ function.

\begin{lemma}[outer truncation gives polynomial density]\label{lem:density}
Let $Z\subset L^1(\T)$ be a separable rearrangement-invariant quasi-Banach
function space.  Then the analytic polynomials $\PA$ are dense in $H[Z]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Separability of $Z$ is equivalent to order continuity of its quasi-norm.
Take nonzero $f\in H[Z]\subset H^1$ and write its inner--outer
factorization as $f=IO$.  Put
\[
 w_N=\min\{1,N/|O|\},\qquad u_N=\log w_N\le0,
\]
and let $h_N$ be the outer function with boundary modulus $w_N$, normalized
in the usual way.  Since
\[
 \|u_N\|_1
 =\int_{\{|O|>N\}}\log(|O|/N)\,dm\longrightarrow0,
\]
weak type $(1,1)$ of harmonic conjugation gives
$\widetilde u_N\to0$ in measure.  Hence $h_N\to1$ in measure, while
$|h_N|\le1$.  The functions $f_N=fh_N$ are bounded analytic functions,
because $|f_N|=\min\{|f|,N\}$ almost everywhere.  Moreover,
\[
 |f_N-f|\le2|f|,
\]
so order-continuous dominated convergence gives $\|f_N-f\|_Z\to0$.

For $g\in H^\infty$, the radial functions $g_r(z)=g(rz)$ converge almost
everywhere to $g$ and are dominated by $2\|g\|_\infty$ after subtraction.
The same dominated-convergence argument gives $g_r\to g$ in $Z$.  For fixed
$r<1$, the Taylor polynomials of $g_r$ converge uniformly on $\T$, hence in
$Z$.  Combining the two approximations proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[uniform analytic atom estimate]\label{lem:atom}
Fix $0<q<1$.  There is $C_q<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:atomnorm}
 \|P_+a\|_{1,q}\le C_q
\end{equation}
for every real $(1,2)$ Hardy atom $a$ on $\T$: $a$ is supported on an arc
$I$ of length $\delta$, has integral zero, and
$\|a\|_2\le\delta^{-1/2}$.  The exceptional constant atom obeys the same
conclusion.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $b=P_+a$.  The $L^2$ boundedness of $P_+$ and Chebyshev's inequality
give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small}
 b^*(t)\le C(\delta t)^{-1/2}\qquad(0<t\le C\delta).
\end{equation}
Outside the doubled arc, the atom itself vanishes.  Subtracting the
conjugate-kernel value at the center $c_I$ and using $\int a=0$ gives
\[
 |b(e^{i\theta})|
 \le \frac{C}{\operatorname{dist}(\theta,I)^2}
       \int_I|s-c_I|\,|a(s)|\,ds
 \le \frac{C\delta}{\operatorname{dist}(\theta,I)^2},
\]
because $\|a\|_1\le1$.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:large}
 b^*(t)\le C\delta t^{-2}\qquad(C\delta\le t\le1).
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:small} and \eqref{eq:large},
\begin{align*}
 \|b\|_{1,q}^q
 &\le C^q\int_0^{C\delta}(t/\delta)^{q/2}\frac{dt}{t}
 +C^q\int_{C\delta}^1(\delta/t)^q\frac{dt}{t}
 \le C_q^q.
\end{align*}
The constant atom is immediate.
\end{proof}

\section*{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}}

First, $L^{1,q}\hookrightarrow L^1$.  One quick proof is to discretize the
decreasing rearrangement on dyadic intervals: the $L^1$ norm is comparable
to $\sum_k2^{-k}f^*(2^{-k})$, whereas the $q$-th power of the Lorentz
quasi-norm is comparable to the sum of the $q$-th powers of the same
quantities.  The inequality $\|x\|_{\ell^1}\le\|x\|_{\ell^q}$ for
$q<1$ gives the embedding.

The K\"othe associate of $L^{1,q}$ is $L^\infty$.  One inclusion follows
from the preceding embedding.  For the converse, if $g$ is essentially
unbounded, testing on a normalized characteristic function of a set on
which $|g|$ is arbitrarily large makes the associate norm infinite.  Hence
the K\"othe bidual is $L^1$.  The standard envelope theorem for separable
rearrangement-invariant quasi-Banach lattices contained in $L^1$ now gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:latticeenv}
 (L^{1,q})^{\wedge}=L^1
\end{equation}
with equivalent norms.

It remains to compare the two norms on $\PA$.  The embedding
$L^{1,q}\hookrightarrow L^1$ implies, for every analytic polynomial $p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 \|p\|_1\le C_q\|p\|_{H[L^{1,q}]^{\wedge}}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, apply the embedding to each term of an arbitrary finite
decomposition of $p$ in $H[L^{1,q}]$ and then take the infimum.

For the reverse inequality, put $c=p(0)$ and
$u=\operatorname{Re}(p-c)$.  This is a smooth real $H^1$ function of mean
zero, and its real Hardy norm satisfies
\[
 \|u\|_{H^1_{\rm real}}
 \le C\bigl(\|u\|_1+\|\widetilde u\|_1\bigr)
 \le C\|p\|_1.
\]
The finite atomic characterization of real $H^1$ with $(1,2)$ atoms gives
an exact finite decomposition
\[
 u=\sum_{j=1}^M\lambda_j a_j,
 \qquad \sum_{j=1}^M|\lambda_j|\le C\|p\|_1.
\]
Since $p-c$ is analytic and has zero constant coefficient,
\[
 p=c+2P_+u=c+2\sum_{j=1}^M\lambda_jP_+a_j.
\]
Lemma \ref{lem:atom}, together with the fixed cost of the constant function,
therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper}
 \|p\|_{H[L^{1,q}]^{\wedge}}
 \le C_q\left(|c|+\sum_j|\lambda_j|\right)
 \le C_q'\|p\|_1.
\end{equation}

Thus the envelope norm and the $H^1$ norm are equivalent on $\PA$.
Lemma \ref{lem:density} says that $\PA$ is dense in $H[L^{1,q}]$, while
it is classically dense in $H^1$.  Taking completions in
\eqref{eq:lower}--\eqref{eq:upper} and using \eqref{eq:latticeenv} proves
\eqref{eq:main}. \qed

\section*{A reusable criterion and the remaining obstruction}

The proof actually gives the following criterion.  Suppose $Z$ is
separable, rearrangement invariant, $Z\subset L^1$, $Z^{\wedge}=L^1$, and
\[
 \sup\{\|P_+a\|_Z:a\text{ is a real }(1,2)\ H^1\text{ atom}\}<\infty.
\]
Then the same finite-atomic argument and Lemma \ref{lem:density} prove
$H[Z]^{\wedge}=H^1=H[Z^{\wedge}]$.

This does not settle the source question in full.  For a general $Z$, its
Banach envelope need not be $L^1$; even when it is, the displayed uniform
atom estimate is not a formal consequence of the envelope identity.  A
general proof would need an analytic decomposition adapted to the geometry
of $Z^{\wedge}$, while a counterexample would need a continuous functional
on $H[Z]$ that cannot extend to $H[Z^{\wedge}]$.  Neither is produced here.

Eight focused upgrade routes were pursued: source-proof auditing, a direct
projection removal, dual extension, Fej\'er/spectral convexification,
endpoint counterexample tests, Lorentz atom estimates, outer-truncation
density, and the general atom-criterion upgrade.  The final obstruction is
recorded in the accompanying attempt log.

\section*{Novelty check}

Exact-question, arXiv-id, title, ``Banach envelope Hardy Lorentz,'' and
``density analytic polynomials abstract Hardy spaces'' searches were run
in the local FA/Banach indexes and primary literature through 2026-08-11.
The searches found the source question, classical envelope results for
other Hardy--Lorentz regimes, and later polynomial-density results for
separable \emph{Banach} function spaces, but no answer to Question 5.8 and
no statement of Theorem \ref{thm:main}.  The proof above is self-contained
apart from standard inner--outer factorization, weak $(1,1)$ conjugation,
finite real-$H^1$ atomic decomposition, and the lattice envelope theorem.
