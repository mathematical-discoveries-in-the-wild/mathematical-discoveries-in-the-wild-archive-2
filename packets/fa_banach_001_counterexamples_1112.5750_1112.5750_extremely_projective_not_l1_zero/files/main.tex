\section{Source conjecture}

In Section~3 of A.~Ya.~Helemskii's \emph{Metric freedom and projectivity for
classical and quantum normed modules}, arXiv:1112.5750v1 \cite{Hel12}, the
author conjectures that every extremely projective normed space is
isometrically isomorphic to $\ell_1^0(M)$; see source PDF page~10 and
Remark~3.6 on page~12.  The author's earlier arXiv:1104.2463 states the same
question and characterizes extremely projective normed spaces as near-retracts
of some $\ell_1^0(\Lambda)$ \cite{Hel11}.

We disprove the conjecture by a one-dimensional algebraic enlargement of
$c_{00}$ inside its completion.

\section{Definitions}

A contractive surjection $q:Y\to X$ is \emph{coisometric} if it maps the open
unit ball of $Y$ onto the open unit ball of $X$.  A normed space $P$ is
\emph{extremely projective} if, for every coisometry $q:Y\to X$, bounded
operator $\varphi:P\to X$, and $\varepsilon>0$, there is
$\psi:P\to Y$ such that
\[
 q\psi=\varphi,
 \qquad \|\psi\|<\|\varphi\|+\varepsilon.
\]
We write $c_{00}=\ell_1^0(\N)$ with its usual $\ell_1$ norm.

\section{Statement and proof intuition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $e_j$ be the canonical basis of $\ell_1$, set
\[
 a_j=2^{-2^j},\qquad x=\sum_{j=1}^\infty a_j e_j,
 \qquad P=c_{00}+\C x\subset\ell_1,
\]
and give $P$ the inherited norm.  Then $P$ is extremely projective, but $P$ is
not isometrically isomorphic to $\ell_1^0(M)$ for any set $M$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof intuition.}
The vector $x$ is superlacunary: sufficiently far out, each tail is almost
entirely its first coordinate.  Replace the tail of the canonical basis by
normalized successive tails of $x$.  Tail differences recover the omitted
coordinate vectors, so the new sequence is still a Hamel basis of $P$; and
superlacunarity makes it uniformly as close to the canonical $\ell_1$ basis
as desired.  Hence $P$ is an arbitrarily small distortion of $c_{00}$, which
is enough to transfer the asymptotic lifting property defining extreme
projectivity.

The space nevertheless cannot be isometric to $c_{00}$.  Any such isometry
would extend to an onto isometry of the completions $\ell_1$.  Extreme points
force onto $\ell_1$-isometries to permute the coordinate axes, so they preserve
$c_{00}$ and cannot map it onto the strict enlargement $P$.

\section{Two preliminary lemmas}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:c00}
$c_{00}$ is extremely projective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $q:Y\to X$ be coisometric and $\varphi:c_{00}\to X$ bounded.  Given
$\varepsilon>0$, choose for every $j$ a vector $y_j\in Y$ with
$q(y_j)=\varphi(e_j)$ and
$\|y_j\|<\|\varphi\|+\varepsilon/2$.  This is possible by
coisometry because $\|\varphi(e_j)\|\le\|\varphi\|$.  The formula
$\psi(e_j)=y_j$ defines a bounded operator on $c_{00}$ with
$q\psi=\varphi$ and
\[
 \|\psi\|=\sup_j\|y_j\|\le\|\varphi\|+\varepsilon/2
 <\|\varphi\|+\varepsilon.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Distance-one transfer]\label{lem:transfer}
Suppose $Q$ is extremely projective and, for every $\delta>0$, there is an
onto isomorphism $T:Q\to P$ with
$\|T\|\,\|T^{-1}\|<1+\delta$.  Then $P$ is extremely projective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Rescale $T$ so that $\|T\|=1$.  Let $q:Y\to X$ be coisometric and
$\varphi:P\to X$ bounded.  If $\varphi=0$, take the zero lifting.  Otherwise,
given $\varepsilon>0$, first choose $T$ so close to isometric that
$\|T^{-1}\|\,\|\varphi\|<\|\varphi\|+\varepsilon/2$, and then choose
$\eta>0$ with $\|T^{-1}\|\eta<\varepsilon/2$.  Extreme projectivity of $Q$
gives a lifting $\theta:Q\to Y$ of $\varphi T$ satisfying
\[
 \|\theta\|<\|\varphi T\|+\eta\le\|\varphi\|+\eta.
\]
Then $\psi=\theta T^{-1}$ lifts $\varphi$ and has norm less than
$\|\varphi\|+\varepsilon$.
\end{proof}

\section{The normalized-tail basis}

For $n\ge0$, put
\[
 r_n=\sum_{j>n}a_j e_j,\qquad s_n=\|r_n\|_1,
 \qquad b_n=s_n^{-1}r_n.
\]
For each $N\in\N$, define $U_N:c_{00}\to P$ on the canonical basis by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:UN}
 U_Ne_j=
 \begin{cases}
 e_j,&j\le N,\\
 b_{j-1},&j>N.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:almost}
Each $U_N$ is onto, and
\[
 \|U_N-I\|\le\delta_N\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \|U_N\|\,\|U_N^{-1}\|
 \le\frac{1+\delta_N}{1-\delta_N}\longrightarrow1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every vector in the range of $U_N$ belongs to $P$.  Conversely,
$r_N=s_NU_Ne_{N+1}$, so
\[
 x=\sum_{j=1}^N a_je_j+r_N
\]
belongs to the range.  For every $j>N$,
\[
 e_j=\frac{r_{j-1}-r_j}{a_j}
 =\frac{s_{j-1}U_Ne_j-s_jU_Ne_{j+1}}{a_j},
\]
while $e_1,\ldots,e_N$ are already in the range.  Thus the range contains
$c_{00}+\C x=P$.

Write $R_j=\sum_{k>j}a_k$.  For $j>N$,
\[
 \|b_{j-1}-e_j\|_1=\frac{2R_j}{a_j+R_j}.
\]
Since $a_{j+1}/a_j=2^{-2^j}$ and all later ratios are smaller,
\[
 \frac{R_j}{a_j}
 \le \frac{2^{-2^j}}{1-2^{-2^{j+1}}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \delta_N:=\sup_{j>N}\|b_{j-1}-e_j\|_1\longrightarrow0.
\]
For a finitely supported scalar sequence $(\lambda_j)$,
\[
 \|(U_N-I)\sum_j\lambda_je_j\|_1
 \le\delta_N\sum_j|\lambda_j|.
\]
Hence $\|U_N-I\|\le\delta_N$.  For large $N$, $\delta_N<1$, and
\[
 (1-\delta_N)\|z\|_1\le\|U_Nz\|_1
 \le(1+\delta_N)\|z\|_1.
\]
Together with surjectivity, this gives the asserted distortion bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the counterexample}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Lemmas~\ref{lem:c00}, \ref{lem:transfer}, and \ref{lem:almost} show that $P$
is extremely projective.

Suppose that $P$ is isometrically isomorphic to $\ell_1^0(M)$.  Since $P$ is
separable and infinite-dimensional, $M$ must be countably infinite, so this
would give an onto isometry $J:c_{00}\to P$.  Both spaces are dense in
$\ell_1$, hence $J$ extends uniquely to a surjective linear isometry
$\overline J:\ell_1\to\ell_1$.

The extreme points of the closed unit ball of complex $\ell_1$ are precisely
$\{\lambda e_j:|\lambda|=1,\ j\in\N\}$.  (If a unit vector has two nonzero
coordinates, a small mass can be shifted between them to write it as a
nontrivial midpoint.)  Thus $\overline J$ permutes the coordinate axes up to
unimodular scalars and therefore
$\overline J(c_{00})=c_{00}$.  On the other hand,
$\overline J(c_{00})=J(c_{00})=P$.  This is impossible because
$x\in P\setminus c_{00}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
Extreme projectivity does not coincide with metric projectivity (or metric
freedom) for normed spaces.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~3.5 of \cite{Hel12} says that every metrically projective normed space
is isometric to some $\ell_1^0(M)$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} therefore gives an
extremely projective normed space that is not metrically projective.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The proof is self-contained apart from the last corollary's use of the source
paper's Theorem~3.5.  The included exact-arithmetic script checks finite
truncations of the tail vectors, the formula for their distance to the
canonical basis, the triangular invertibility, and the decay of the error.
These computations are sanity checks, not substitutes for the limiting proof.

On 11 August 2026, the run indexes had no result for arXiv:1112.5750 or the
conjecture.  Exact-phrase and close-keyword searches of the local parsed arXiv
corpus found the question only in arXiv:1104.2463 and 1112.5750.  A local later
citation, arXiv:1309.4974, does not discuss the conjecture.  Bounded external
searches returned no usable records in this environment, so novelty confidence
is moderate.

Lemma~\ref{lem:almost}, the distortion transfer in Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer},
and the completion-isometry obstruction.  No unproved dependency remains.
