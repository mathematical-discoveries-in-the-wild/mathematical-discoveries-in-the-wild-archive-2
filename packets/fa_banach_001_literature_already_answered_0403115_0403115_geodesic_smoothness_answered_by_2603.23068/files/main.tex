\section{The 2004 question}

In Remark~14 of 
Friz and Victoir, \emph{A note on the notion of geometric rough paths},
arXiv:math/0403115 \cite{source}, the authors would like to choose, for each
$g\in G^m(V)$, a shortest Carnot--Caratheodory connection from the identity
to $g$.  They record smoothness of these minimizing geodesics as open for
$m\geq3$, contrasting it with the affirmative step-two theory.

\section{The 2026 negative theorem}

Rossi, Schiavoni Piazza, and Socionovo prove the following in
arXiv:2603.23068v3 \cite{answer}.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.97\textwidth]{figures/supporting_theorem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Their Section~10 constructs the Carnot group explicitly.  Its horizontal
layer has the orthonormal frame $Y_1,Y_2$, and a submetry lifts a minimizing
geodesic that is non-smooth at an interior point while preserving its
regularity and minimality.  Thus the all-geodesics-smooth statement is false
already within Carnot groups.

\section{Transfer to the exact free groups}

The resolving paper does not phrase Theorem~1.3 using rough-path notation,
so we record the short standard implication.

\begin{proposition}[Free lift of a non-smooth minimizer]
Let $G$ be a rank-$r$, step-$s$ Carnot group whose first layer has an
orthonormal generating frame.  If $G$ admits a non-smooth minimizing
geodesic, then the free Carnot group $F_{r,s}=G^s(\mathbb R^r)$ admits one.
The same follows for every $F_{d,m}$ with $d\geq r$ and $m\geq s$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The universal property of $F_{r,s}$ gives a stratified epimorphism
$\pi:F_{r,s}\to G$ mapping the free orthonormal generators to those of $G$.
It is a metric submetry.  Translate a non-smooth minimizing geodesic
$\gamma$ so that it starts at the identity and lift its horizontal control
to a curve $\widetilde\gamma$ starting at the identity of $F_{r,s}$.
The lift preserves length.  Therefore
\[
 d_G(e,\gamma(T))
 \leq d_{F_{r,s}}(e,\widetilde\gamma(T))
 \leq L(\widetilde\gamma)
 =L(\gamma)=d_G(e,\gamma(T)).
\]
All inequalities are equalities, so $\widetilde\gamma$ is minimizing.  If it
were smooth, then $\gamma=\pi\circ\widetilde\gamma$ would be smooth, a
contradiction.  For larger rank or step, apply the same argument to the
natural stratified epimorphism onto $G$.
\end{proof}

In the lowest-regularity construction of \cite{answer}, the parameter is
$b=5$.  The Section~10 frame contains central-coordinate coefficients of
degree at most $b+3$, and the top coefficient requires $b+4$ horizontal
generators under iterated bracketing.  Thus its Carnot group has step
$s=b+4=9$.  The proposition gives a non-smooth minimizing geodesic in
\[
 G^9(\mathbb R^2)=F_{2,9},
\]
and in every $G^m(\mathbb R^d)$ with $m\geq9$ and $d\geq2$.

\section{What is and is not resolved}

This is a decisive negative answer to any uniform claim that minimizing
geodesics in all free groups $G^m(V)$ are smooth once $m\geq3$.  It also
shows that the smooth step-two theory cannot extend without qualification.

Two narrower questions are not answered by this transfer.  First, the
individual free steps $3\leq m\leq8$ are not settled here.  Second, a
non-smooth minimizer between two endpoints does not exclude the possibility
that the same endpoints also admit a different smooth minimizer.  Therefore
the result refutes universal smoothness of geodesics, but does not by itself
exclude every possible rule selecting one smooth shortest connector per
endpoint.

The source's Proposition~13 is unaffected: it deliberately uses a smooth
connector whose length is within a factor two of optimal, rather than an
exact minimizer.
