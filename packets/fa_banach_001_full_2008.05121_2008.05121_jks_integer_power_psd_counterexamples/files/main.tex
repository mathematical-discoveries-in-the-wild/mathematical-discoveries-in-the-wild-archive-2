\section{Source question}

Khare's paper \cite{KhareJKS} studies the symmetric kernel
\[
 K_{\JKS}(x,y):=\max(1+xy,0),\qquad x,y\in\R.
\]
Question~5.5, in Section~5 on printed page~19 of arXiv:2008.05121v2,
asks for every integer $\alpha\geq0$ whether
$K_{\JKS}^{\circ\alpha}$ can be shown not to be
$\TN_{\alpha+3}$ on $\R\times\R$.  Its stronger form asks for a witness with
the same increasing tuple in both arguments, equivalently a symmetric matrix
that is not positive semidefinite.  As in the source, $0^0=0$.

\section{Proof intuition}

The substitution $x=\tan u$ converts the kernel, up to harmless positive
diagonal factors, into the positive part of the cosine difference kernel.
Choose $m+3$ equally spaced angles whose total span is just over $\pi/2$, but
whose every shorter pairwise span is below $\pi/2$.  Truncation then deletes
exactly the two opposite corner entries of the untruncated cosine-power
matrix.

The untruncated matrix has a finite Fourier Gram representation with only
$m+1$ frequencies, hence rank $m+1=(m+3)-2$.  Its middle $(m+1)$-square block
is nevertheless positive definite, by a square Vandermonde determinant.
Expanding after deleting the two corners, the constant and linear terms vanish
by the rank deficiency; the unique quadratic term is the negative of the
middle determinant.  This gives an exact negative determinant in every
degree, including $m=0$.

\section{The construction and determinant identity}

\begin{theorem}[Symmetric witness in every integer degree]\label{thm:main}
Let $m\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$ and put $n=m+3$.  Choose any
\[
 \frac{\pi}{2(n-1)}<\delta<\frac{\pi}{2(n-2)},
 \qquad
 u_j=\left(j-\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\delta,
 \qquad x_j=\tan u_j
 \quad(1\leq j\leq n).
\]
Then $x_1<\cdots<x_n$, and the symmetric matrix
\[
 M=\left(K_{\JKS}(x_j,x_k)^m\right)_{j,k=1}^n
\]
has negative determinant.  Consequently it is not positive semidefinite, and
$K_{\JKS}^{\circ m}$ is not $\TN_{m+3}$ on $\R\times\R$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $L=(n-1)\delta$.  The choice of $\delta$ gives
\[
 \frac\pi2<L<\pi.
\]
Indeed, the second inequality is immediate when $n=3$, and for $n>3$ follows
from $(n-1)/(2(n-2))<1$.  Hence all $u_j$ lie in $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$, so the
$x_j=\tan u_j$ are well-defined and strictly increasing.

For $u,v\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tan}
 K_{\JKS}(\tan u,\tan v)
 =\sec u\,\sec v\,\pos{\cos(u-v)}.
\end{equation}
Define
\[
 A_{jk}:=\pos{\cos(u_j-u_k)}^m,
 \qquad
 C_{jk}:=\cos^m(u_j-u_k).
\]
For $m=0$, the first definition uses the source convention $0^0=0$, while
the second has the usual value $a^0=1$ because all occurring cosines in $C$
are nonzero.  Every pair other than $\{1,n\}$ has separation at most
$(n-2)\delta<\pi/2$, whereas $|u_n-u_1|=L>\pi/2$.  Thus truncation changes
exactly the two opposite corners.  With
\[
 c:=\cos^m L
\]
(so $c=1$ when $m=0$), we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corner}
 A=C-c(E_{1n}+E_{n1}).
\end{equation}

The elementary Fourier expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourier}
 \cos^m t=2^{-m}\sum_{r=0}^m \binom mr e^{i(m-2r)t}
\end{equation}
expresses $C$ as $VDV^*$, where
\[
 V_{jr}=e^{i(m-2r)u_j}\quad(0\leq r\leq m),
 \qquad
 D_{rr}=2^{-m}\binom mr>0.
\]
Therefore $C$ is positive semidefinite and $\operatorname{rank}C\leq m+1=n-2$.

Let
\[
 B=C[\{2,\ldots,n-1\},\{2,\ldots,n-1\}].
\]
This is the Gram matrix from the middle $m+1$ rows of $V$.  That square
submatrix of $V$ is nonsingular: after extracting $e^{imu_j}$ from row $j$,
it is the Vandermonde matrix
\[
 \bigl(z_j^r\bigr)_{j=2,\ldots,n-1;\ r=0,\ldots,m},
 \qquad z_j=e^{-2iu_j}.
\]
The nodes are distinct, since $0<|u_j-u_k|<\pi$ for $j\ne k$.  Hence
$B$ is positive definite and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bpositive}
 \det B>0.
\end{equation}

It remains to expand \eqref{eq:corner}.  Regard the determinant as a polynomial
in the two changes at positions $(1,n)$ and $(n,1)$.  Its constant term is
$\det C=0$.  Each linear coefficient is an $(n-1)$-minor of $C$ and hence
vanishes because $\operatorname{rank}C\leq n-2$.  The coefficient of the
product of the two changes is $-\det B$: a contributing permutation exchanges
the two endpoint columns, producing one minus sign, and its remaining part is
the determinant of the middle block.  Since both changes equal $-c$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:detidentity}
 \det A=-c^2\det B<0.
\end{equation}
Here $c\ne0$ because $L\in(\pi/2,\pi)$; this also covers $m=0$, when
$B=[1]$ and \eqref{eq:detidentity} reads $\det A=-1$.

Finally, \eqref{eq:tan} yields $M=D_m A D_m$ with
$D_m=\operatorname{diag}(\sec^m u_1,\ldots,\sec^m u_n)$.  Thus
\[
 \det M=\left(\prod_{j=1}^n\sec(u_j)^{2m}\right)\det A<0.
\]
A positive-semidefinite matrix has nonnegative determinant, so $M$ is not
positive semidefinite.  Its negative full minor also violates
$\TN_n=\TN_{m+3}$.
\end{proof}

\section{The completed full-plane classification}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:classification}
For every integer $p\geq2$ and every real $\alpha\geq0$,
\[
 K_{\JKS}^{\circ\alpha}\text{ is }\TN_p\text{ on }\R\times\R
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \alpha\geq p-2.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~C(1) of \cite{KhareJKS} proves the forward construction for every
$\alpha\geq p-2$.  Theorem~C(2) there proves that a $\TN_p$ power below
$p-2$ would have to be an integer.  If such an integer is $m$, then
$m\leq p-3$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives an $(m+3)$-minor with negative
determinant.  Since $m+3\leq p$, this contradicts $\TN_p$.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification}

The exact argument was audited at the following points.
\begin{itemize}
 \item The interval for $\delta$ is nonempty and places exactly one unordered
 endpoint pair beyond the cosine cutoff.
 \item Formula \eqref{eq:fourier} gives at most $m+1=n-2$ Gram vectors with
 strictly positive weights.
 \item The middle evaluation matrix is a square Vandermonde matrix on distinct
 unit-circle nodes, so no hidden singularity remains.
 \item A direct determinant expansion confirms both the sign and the middle
 minor in \eqref{eq:detidentity}.
 \item The convention at $m=0$ was checked separately and is also contained in
 the same formula.
\end{itemize}

The reusable script \texttt{code/verify\_jks\_family.py} evaluates the matrices
at 180-digit precision for $m=0,\ldots,16$.  It checks $\det B>0$, $\det A<0$,
and $\det A=-\cos^{2m}(L)\det B$ to relative error below $10^{-70}$.  All 17
cases pass.  This computation is a regression check, not part of the proof.

\section{Novelty search, limitations, and review recommendation}

The run's registry, solution, attempt, and proof-gap indexes were searched for
the arXiv id, title, kernel name, and core formula, with no duplicate found.
A bounded external search through 17 August 2026 used the exact Question~5.5
label, ``Jain--Karlin--Schoenberg kernel'', \texttt{max(1+xy,0)},
``integer powers'', ``alpha+3'', and ``positive semidefinite''.  The current
author-hosted PDF still states the question.  Close later arXiv papers checked
included 2103.12550, 2110.08206, and 2411.03391; none was found to state this
construction or resolve Question~5.5.  Novelty is therefore plausible but is
not certified by this bounded search.

There is no conditional dependency in the proof.  The main human-review focus
should be the two-entry determinant coefficient in
\eqref{eq:detidentity} and the inference in Corollary~\ref{cor:classification}
from an $(m+3)$-minor to failure of $\TN_p$.  Subject to that check and a
specialist literature search, promote as a full solution.
