\section*{Source conjecture}

Chen and Wang's Conjecture 4.1 asks whether the unique optimizer $\hat f$ in
their regularized approximation theorem must have the form
\[
  \hat f=\int_X\phi(x)\,d\nu(x)
\]
for a signed or complex measure $\nu$ supported on $\operatorname{supp}\mu$,
provided every such measure $\xi$ satisfies
\[
 \int_X\|\phi(x)\|_{\Hcal(K)}\,d|\xi|(x)<\infty.
\]
The original statement appears on PDF page 13 of the source paper
\cite[Conjecture 4.1]{source} and is reproduced below.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Theorem 2.6 minimizes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sourceobjective}
 \int_X c(f(x),g(x))\,d\mu(x)+\lambda\|f\|_{\Hcal(K)}^p,
\end{equation}
where $\lambda>0$, $p>1$, $c(0,g(\cdot))\in L^1(\mu)$, and
$c(\cdot,z)$ is lower semicontinuous and convex for each $z$.  Under these
hypotheses the optimizer exists and is unique.

\section*{Proof intuition}

For the delta kernel on $\N$, the RKHS is $\ell^2$ and a measure embedding is
simply the sequence of its atomic masses.  Finite signed measures therefore
produce only $\ell^1$ sequences, although their embeddings are dense in
$\ell^2$.  This is exactly the nonclosed-range phenomenon behind the source's
approximating-sequence theorem.

It remains to make a vector in $\ell^2\setminus\ell^1$ occur as the unique
optimizer without violating the source's integrability assumption.  The
allowed cost may depend on the target value.  We encode at point $n$ a
quadratic loss of weight $2^n$ and take the sampling mass to be $2^{-n}$.
These factors cancel in the objective, and completing the square makes
$(1/n)$ the unique optimizer.  A smooth global formula for the cost realizes
this encoding, so no pathology in the first-variable convexity is involved.

\section*{Counterexample}

Let $X=\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$ with the discrete topology, let
\[
 K(m,n)=\delta_{mn},\qquad \mu(\{n\})=2^{-n},
\]
and work over $\R$.  Thus $X$ is Polish,
$\Hcal(K)=\ell^2(\N)$, and the feature map is $\phi(n)=e_n$.
Set $p=2$ and $\lambda=1$.  For $z\in\R$ write
\[
 q(z)=\sqrt{1+z^2},\qquad
 g(n)=\sqrt{n^2-1},\qquad
 c(u,z)=2^{q(z)}\left(u-\frac{2}{q(z)}\right)^2.
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The data above satisfy all assumptions of Theorem 2.6 and of Conjecture 4.1.
The unique optimizer of \eqref{eq:sourceobjective} is
\[
 \hat f=(1,1/2,1/3,\ldots)\in\ell^2.
\]
There is no finite signed measure $\nu$ on $X$ such that
$\hat f=\int_X\phi\,d\nu$.  Consequently Conjecture 4.1 is false.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The kernel is normalized because $K(n,n)=1$.  Hence for every finite signed
measure $\xi$ on $X$,
\[
 \int_X\|\phi(n)\|_{\ell^2}\,d|\xi|(n)
 =\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\xi(\{n\})|=|\xi|(X)<\infty.
\]
Also every point has positive $\mu$-mass, so
$\operatorname{supp}\mu=X$.  This verifies the extra hypothesis of the
conjecture.

The functions $g$ and $c$ are Borel measurable.  In fact $c$ is continuous
on $\R^2$, and $c(\cdot,z)$ is a nonnegative strictly convex quadratic for
every $z$.  Since $q(g(n))=n$,
\[
 \int_X c(0,g(n))\,d\mu(n)
 =\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}2^n\left(\frac2n\right)^2
 =4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2}<\infty.
\]
Thus all hypotheses of Theorem 2.6 hold.

For $f=(f_n)\in\ell^2$, the objective is finite and equals
\begin{align*}
 J(f)
 &=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}c(f_n,g(n))
   +\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_n^2\\
 &=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\left(f_n-\frac2n\right)^2+f_n^2\right]\\
 &=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(f_n-\frac1n\right)^2
   +2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2}.
\end{align*}
It follows immediately that the unique optimizer is
$\hat f_n=1/n$.  This sequence lies in $\ell^2$.

Suppose that a finite signed measure $\nu$ represented $\hat f$.  On the
discrete space $X$, put $a_n=\nu(\{n\})$.  Finiteness of total variation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:l1}
 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_n|=|\nu|(X)<\infty.
\end{equation}
For every $k$, the defining identity for the Pettis integral yields
\[
 \left\langle\int_Xe_n\,d\nu(n),e_k\right\rangle
 =\int_X\langle e_n,e_k\rangle\,d\nu(n)=\nu(\{k\})=a_k.
\]
Equality with $\hat f$ would force $a_k=1/k$ for all $k$, contradicting
\eqref{eq:l1}.  No representing measure exists.
\end{proof}

\section*{Sharp mechanism and scope}

For this normalized delta kernel, the kernel-mean map from finite signed
measures has range exactly $\ell^1$ inside $\ell^2$.  Its closure is all of
$\ell^2$, since finitely supported sequences are dense.  Thus the source's
Theorem 3.2 correctly supplies the truncation measures
\[
 \nu_N=\sum_{n=1}^N\frac1n\delta_n,
 \qquad \int_X\phi\,d\nu_N\longrightarrow\hat f\quad\hbox{in }\ell^2,
\]
but no single finite signed measure can replace the sequence.

The example disproves the conjecture under its stated general cost
hypotheses, even with a normalized kernel and a cost continuous in both
variables and strictly convex in the prediction variable.  It does not rule
out a strengthened representer theorem for narrower loss classes, such as a
fixed translation-invariant loss with an integrable pointwise subgradient.

\section*{Verification and novelty bounds}

Every hypothesis is checked explicitly above.  The completing-square identity
and the divergent total variation are exact; the included script checks their
finite truncations but is not used in the proof.  The source PDF and a
full-width crop of Conjecture 4.1 are included in this packet.

On 11 August 2026, bounded searches covered the exact arXiv id and title,
``probabilistic representer'' with RKHS and measure terms, and the broader
nonclosed-range/kernel-mean-embedding formulation, together with all cheap
indexes of this run.  No later paper answering Conjecture 4.1 and no instance
of this counterexample was found.  Novelty confidence is moderate, since the
$\ell^1\subsetneq\ell^2$ obstruction itself is elementary and may be folklore.

Human review should focus on the intended convention that an
$\mathbb F$-measure in the source is a finite signed/complex measure (as its
total-variation notation and examples indicate), and on confirming that
Theorem 2.6 indeed permits target-dependent weights of the form used here.
