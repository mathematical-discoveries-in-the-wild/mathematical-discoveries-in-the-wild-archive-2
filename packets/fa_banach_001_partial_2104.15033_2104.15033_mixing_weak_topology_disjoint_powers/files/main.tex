\section{Exact source question}

Cardeccia and Muro ask in Question 3.4, arXiv PDF page 7
of~\cite{source}:

\begin{sourcequestion}
Is every mixing linear operator on a separable Fr\'echet space necessarily
$\AP$-hypercyclic?  Equivalently, is every mixing operator multiply recurrent?
\end{sourcequestion}

Here multiple recurrence means that for every nonempty open set $U$ and every
$m\ge1$, there is $n\ge1$ such that
\[
 U\cap T^{-n}U\cap\cdots\cap T^{-mn}U\ne\varnothing.
\]

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/question_3_4_crop.png}
\end{center}

The source proves the assertion for mixing backward shifts and for operators
satisfying the strong Kitai criterion, but leaves the general implication
open.  We prove a different broad subcase.

\section{Idea of the proof}

On a weak-topology space, every basic open set imposes only finitely many
linear-functional constraints.  Shkarin's theorem says that mixing is exactly
the absence of nonzero finite-dimensional invariant subspaces for the dual
operator~\cite{shkarin}.  This turns the dual into a torsion-free module over
$\K[t]$.

Given the finite-dimensional space $L$ of functionals appearing in finitely
many open sets, the generated module $\K[t]L$ is free.  In free-module
coordinates, elements of $L$ have uniformly bounded polynomial degree.
Multiplication by $t^{jn}$ moves their supports into disjoint degree blocks
when $n$ is large.  Therefore
\[
 L,\ (T')^nL,\ldots,(T')^{mn}L
\]
form a direct sum.  The corresponding finite evaluation map on the original
space is onto, so all conditions on
$x,T^nx,\ldots,T^{mn}x$ can be met exactly.  This proves not only multiple
recurrence but disjoint mixing of every finite family of powers.

\section{The algebraic separation lemma}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:blocks}
Let $S$ be a linear operator on a vector space $Y$ over $\K$.  Suppose $S$ has
no nonzero finite-dimensional invariant subspace.  Given a finite-dimensional
subspace $L\subset Y$ and $m\ge1$, there is $N$ such that, for every $n\ge N$,
\[
 L\oplus S^nL\oplus\cdots\oplus S^{mn}L
\tag{1}\label{eq:direct}
\]
is an algebraic direct sum.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
No nonzero polynomial $p$ can have a nonzero vector in $\ker p(S)$: otherwise
the span of $y,Sy,\ldots,S^{d-1}y$, where $d=\deg p$, contains a nonzero
finite-dimensional invariant subspace.  Thus the $\K[t]$-module $Y$, with
$t\cdot y=Sy$, is torsion-free.

The submodule $M:=\K[t]L$ is finitely generated and torsion-free.  Since
$\K[t]$ is a principal ideal domain, $M$ is free of finite rank.  Choose a
module basis.  In these coordinates, every element of $L$ is a vector of
polynomials, and finite dimensionality gives a common degree bound $d$ for
all its coordinate polynomials.

Take $n>d$.  If $y_j\in L$ and
$\sum_{j=0}^m S^{jn}y_j=0$, then in each free coordinate we have
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^m t^{jn}p_j(t)=0,
 \qquad \deg p_j\le d.
\]
The polynomial supports lie in the pairwise disjoint intervals
$[jn,jn+d]$.  Hence every $p_j$ is zero, so every $y_j$ is zero.  This proves
\eqref{eq:direct} with $N=d+1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Weak-topology theorem}

Recall that $T_1,\ldots,T_m$ are \emph{disjoint mixing} if, for every
nonempty open $U_0,U_1,\ldots,U_m$, there is $N$ such that for every $n\ge N$
one can find $x\in U_0$ with $T_j^nx\in U_j$ for all $j$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $X$ be a Hausdorff topological vector space over $\K$ whose topology is
weak, and let $T:X\to X$ be continuous linear and mixing.  Then, for every
$m\ge1$, the operators $T,T^2,\ldots,T^m$ are disjoint mixing.  In
particular, $T$ is multiply recurrent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix nonempty open sets $U_0,\ldots,U_m$.  Because the topology is weak, for
each $j$ we may choose $u_j\in U_j$ and a basic weak neighborhood
\[
 V_j=\{x\in X: |f(x-u_j)|<\varepsilon_j\text{ for every }f\in F_j\}
 \subseteq U_j,
\]
where $F_j\subset X'$ is finite.  Let
$L=\Span(\bigcup_{j=0}^m F_j)$.

By Shkarin's Theorem 1.1~\cite{shkarin}, mixing of $T$ implies that the dual
operator $T'$ has no nonzero finite-dimensional invariant subspace.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:blocks} to $S=T'$ and $L$.  For all sufficiently large $n$,
the linear map
\[
 A_n:X\longrightarrow (L^*)^{m+1},\qquad
 (A_nx)_j(f)=f(T^{jn}x)
\tag{2}\label{eq:evaluation}
\]
is onto.  Indeed, its algebraic transpose sends $(g_0,\ldots,g_m)\in
L^{m+1}$ to
\[
 g_0+(T')^ng_1+\cdots+(T')^{mn}g_m,
\]
which is injective by \eqref{eq:direct}; since the target of $A_n$ is finite
dimensional, injectivity of the transpose is equivalent to surjectivity.

For each $j$, let $\alpha_j\in L^*$ be evaluation at $u_j$:
$\alpha_j(f)=f(u_j)$.  Surjectivity of \eqref{eq:evaluation} gives $x\in X$
such that
\[
 f(T^{jn}x)=f(u_j)\qquad(f\in L,\ 0\le j\le m).
\]
Thus $T^{jn}x\in V_j\subseteq U_j$ for every $j$.  This holds for every
sufficiently large $n$, proving disjoint mixing.

Taking all $U_j=U$ gives multiple recurrence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:omega}
Every hypercyclic continuous linear operator on
$\omega=\K^{\N}$ with its product topology is $\AP$-hypercyclic.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The space $\omega$ is separable, complete, metrizable, and carries a weak
topology.  Shkarin's Corollary 1.2~\cite{shkarin} says that a hypercyclic
operator on $\omega$ satisfies the same dual condition and hence is mixing.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} makes it multiply recurrent.  Proposition 2.1 of the
source~\cite{source} identifies hypercyclicity plus multiple recurrence with
$\AP$-hypercyclicity.
\end{proof}

\section{Eight focused upgrade attempts}

The following routes were checked after obtaining Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
They explain why the result is not promoted as a full answer.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{General dual separation.}  Separating the diagonal orbit range
from $U^{m+1}$ produces a relation
$\sum_{j=0}^m(T')^{jn}f_j=0$.  For a norm or seminorm ball, however, the
$f_j$ range through an infinite-dimensional equicontinuous polar; finite
module directness gives no uniform lower bound on these relations.

\item \textbf{Weak-topology module route.}  This is the successful route
above.  It works because a basic neighborhood has a finite-dimensional polar
span and yields the stronger disjoint-mixing conclusion.

\item \textbf{Full-sequence hypercyclicity criteria.}  The strong Kitai
criterion synchronizes all powers and is already handled by the source.
Mixing does not imply that criterion; known mixing counterexamples to Kitai
therefore prevent this from settling the general question.

\item \textbf{Quasi-rigidity.}  Weak mixing supplies dense one-time
recurrence along a common sequence, but $T^{n_k}x\to x$ does not imply
$T^{jn_k}x\to x$: the operators $T^{n_k}$ need not be uniformly bounded on
the moving intermediate vectors.

\item \textbf{Composition-operator gluing.}  Mixing runaway conditions
provide small forward and backward images for one time.  Pasting $m$ orbit
copies requires simultaneous control of overlaps and of pullback norms at
$n,2n,\ldots,mn$; without bounded distortion these errors are not controlled.

\item \textbf{Direct-sum counterexamples.}  Assigning a separate obstruction
to each time $n$ is compatible with mixing at the coordinate level, but an
open ball in a separable direct sum cannot impose a uniformly positive
obstruction on infinitely many tail coordinates.

\item \textbf{Scalar dual recurrences.}  A fixed functional relation could
forbid a three-term return near one vector, but any polynomially annihilated
dual vector creates a finite-dimensional invariant dual subspace and is
incompatible with mixing in the weak-topology model.  No Banach-space
replacement producing all-time obstructions was found.

\item \textbf{Known mixing non-Kitai constructions.}  Grivaux-type and recent
composition-operator and generalized-shift examples were inspected as counterexample
candidates.  Failure of Kitai's criterion does not imply failure of multiple
recurrence, and no open set excluding all arithmetic return patterns could be
extracted from their defining estimates.
\end{enumerate}

The surviving obstruction is therefore precise: extend the finite-dimensional
dual-block separation to the full equicontinuous polar of a Fr\'echet
neighborhood, or build a mixing operator for which such a polar supports
uniform near-relations that separate a diagonal orbit from $U^{m+1}$.

\section{Verification, novelty, and scope}

The proof is algebraic once Shkarin's characterization is accepted.  The key
checks are: torsion-freeness of the dual module; freeness of a finitely
generated torsion-free module over the PID $\K[t]$; disjointness of shifted
polynomial degree blocks; and the finite-dimensional transpose criterion for
surjectivity.  No compactness or Baire argument enters Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

A bounded search checked the run's four lightweight indexes, the parsed local
arXiv corpus, the exact source question, Shkarin's paper, and focused
arXiv/web queries combining mixing, multiple recurrence, $\AP$-hypercyclicity,
weak topology, and $\omega$.  Recent linear-recurrence papers still treat
multiple recurrence as a distinct notion; no explicit statement of the
weak-topology/disjoint-powers theorem was found.  The module argument may be
folklore, so novelty confidence is moderate rather than definitive.

Human review should prioritize Lemma~\ref{lem:blocks}, especially the passage
from torsion-free to free for $\K[t]L$, and the claim that injectivity of the
transpose in \eqref{eq:evaluation} makes the finite-dimensional target map
onto.  The general normable or arbitrary Fr\'echet case is not claimed.
