\fbox{\parbox{0.92\textwidth}{\raggedright\textbf{Status: candidate partial result.}
For an arbitrary bounded operator and invariant subspace, a canonical exact
defect pairing identifies every filled resolvent component with an open set of
eigenvalues of the quotient operator; the common defect is constant throughout
the component.  Conversely, one fixed nonnormal operator with finitely many
circular holes realizes every fill/no-fill pattern, and even every prescribed
finite-or-infinite defect-multiplicity profile, by varying its invariant
subspace.}}
\end{center}

\section{Source question and result}

Let $T\in\B(X)$ on a complex Banach space and let $M\subset X$ be a closed
$T$-invariant subspace.  Write
\[
 T_M=T|_M,\qquad \dot T(x+M)=Tx+M\quad(x\in X).
\]
Drivaliaris and Yannakakis~\cite{source} recall that a bounded component $D$
of $\rho(T)$ is either disjoint from $\sigma(T_M)$ or is entirely contained
in it.  Their final remark asks which holes can be filled.  The following
theorem gives a defect-theoretic answer for a fixed pair $(T,M)$ and records
the exact complementary phenomenon on the quotient.

For an operator with closed range, call
\[
 \operatorname{def}A=\dim(Y/\ran A)
\]
its defect, with the value $\infty$ when the quotient is infinite-dimensional.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical hole-defect pairing]\label{thm:pairing}
For every $\lambda\in\rho(T)$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T_M-\lambda$ is bounded below and $\dot T-\lambda$ is surjective;
\item there is a canonical Banach-space isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta}
 \delta_\lambda:\ker(\dot T-\lambda)
 \longrightarrow M/(T_M-\lambda)M,
 \qquad
 \delta_\lambda(x+M)=(T-\lambda)x+(T_M-\lambda)M;
\end{equation}
\item on each connected component $D$ of $\rho(T)$, the common extended
dimension
\[
 d_D=\dim\ker(\dot T-\lambda)
     =\operatorname{def}(T_M-\lambda)\in\N_0\cup\{\infty\}
\]
is independent of $\lambda\in D$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently,
\[
 D\subseteq\sigma(T_M)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_D>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D\subseteq\sigma_p(\dot T).
\]
When these conditions hold, $D$ lies in the residual spectrum of $T_M$.
\end{theorem}

Thus a filled hole is not merely extra spectrum of a restriction: point by
point it is an eigenvalue region of the quotient, and its quotient eigenspace
is canonically paired with the cokernel of the restriction.  The residual
spectrum conclusion itself is classical; see, for example,
Barnes~\cite[Results 1]{barnes}.  The explicit isomorphism
\eqref{eq:delta}, its componentwise defect consequence, and the realization
below are the useful combined formulation here.

\section{Proof intuition}

Outside $\sigma(T)$ the operator $T-\lambda$ is invertible.  Restricting an
invertible map to an invariant subspace cannot create a kernel, but it can
lose surjectivity.  Exactly the same loss reappears on the quotient in the
opposite form: the induced quotient map is always onto, but can acquire a
kernel.  Applying $(T-\lambda)^{-1}$ turns a missing range class in $M$ into
the corresponding quotient eigenvector, which gives \eqref{eq:delta}.

The restriction remains bounded below everywhere in $\rho(T)$, so it stays
in the upper semi-Fredholm set.  Its index is locally constant there and hence
constant on every resolvent component.  This upgrades the usual all-or-none
spectral statement to a constant multiplicity statement.

For realization, a bilateral shift supplies a circular spectrum and its
unilateral invariant half supplies the filled disk.  Countably many copies
allow an arbitrary defect multiplicity in each disk.  A $2\times2$ Jordan
extension makes the ambient operator nonnormal without changing either its
spectrum or the restrictions used in the construction.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:pairing}}

Fix $\lambda\in\rho(T)$.  For $m\in M$,
\[
 \|(T_M-\lambda)m\|=\|(T-\lambda)m\|
 \ge \frac{1}{\|(T-\lambda)^{-1}\|}\,\|m\|.
\]
Thus $T_M-\lambda$ is injective with closed range.  If $y+M\in X/M$ and
$x=(T-\lambda)^{-1}y$, then
\[
 (\dot T-\lambda)(x+M)=y+M,
\]
so $\dot T-\lambda$ is surjective.

If $x+M\in\ker(\dot T-\lambda)$, then $(T-\lambda)x\in M$, so
\eqref{eq:delta} makes sense.  Replacing $x$ by $x+m$ changes the displayed
representative by $(T_M-\lambda)m$, hence $\delta_\lambda$ is well defined.
Its inverse is the equally explicit map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse}
 y+(T_M-\lambda)M\longmapsto (T-\lambda)^{-1}y+M.
\end{equation}
Both maps are bounded, because they are induced by bounded operators between
quotient spaces.  Equations \eqref{eq:delta} and \eqref{eq:inverse} are inverse
to one another, proving the canonical isomorphism.

Every $T_M-\lambda$, $\lambda\in\rho(T)$, is upper semi-Fredholm: it has
closed range and zero-dimensional kernel.  The semi-Fredholm index is locally
constant on the upper semi-Fredholm set, with the extended value $-\infty$
when the defect is infinite.  Since
\[
 \ind(T_M-\lambda)=-\operatorname{def}(T_M-\lambda),
\]
connectedness gives the asserted constant $d_D$.  Finally,
$\lambda\in\sigma(T_M)$ is equivalent to failure of surjectivity, because the
restriction is already bounded below.  This is equivalent to $d_D>0$, and by
\eqref{eq:delta} to $\ker(\dot T-\lambda)\ne0$.  A proper closed range is not
dense, proving the residual-spectrum assertion. \qed

\section{One nonnormal operator realizing all finite hole profiles}

The preceding invariant has no universal interaction constraint across
different holes, even if the ambient operator is fixed and nonnormal.

\begin{theorem}[Independent realization with prescribed defects]\label{thm:realize}
Let
\[
 \overline D_j=\{z\in\C:|z-c_j|\le r_j\},\qquad 1\le j\le m,
\]
be pairwise disjoint closed disks, and put $\Gamma_j=\partial D_j$.  There is
a single nonnormal operator $J$ on a separable Hilbert space with
\[
 \sigma(J)=\bigcup_{j=1}^m\Gamma_j
\]
such that, for every $(d_1,\ldots,d_m)\in(\N_0\cup\{\infty\})^m$, there is a
closed $J$-invariant subspace $M_{\boldsymbol d}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectrumprofile}
 \sigma(J|_{M_{\boldsymbol d}})
 =\bigcup_{d_j=0}\Gamma_j\ \cup\
   \bigcup_{d_j>0}\overline D_j.
\end{equation}
Moreover, the constant defect from Theorem~\ref{thm:pairing} on $D_j$ is
exactly $d_j$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $U$ be the bilateral shift on $\ell^2(\mathbb Z)$ and let
$H_+=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{e_n:n\ge0\}$.  Thus $H_+$ is
$U$-invariant, $U|_{H_+}$ is the unilateral shift $S$, and
\[
 \sigma(c_jI+r_jU)=\Gamma_j,
 \qquad
 \sigma(c_jI+r_jS)=\overline D_j.
\]

On
\[
 G=\bigoplus_{j=1}^m\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\ell^2(\mathbb Z)
\]
define the normal operator $B$ to act as $c_jI+r_jU$ on the $(j,k)$ copy.
Then $\sigma(B)=\bigcup_j\Gamma_j$.  On $G\oplus G$, set
\[
 J=\begin{pmatrix}B&I\\0&B\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The nilpotent off-diagonal part commutes with the diagonal, so
$\sigma(J)=\sigma(B)$.  Also $J$ is not normal: the top-left blocks of
$J^*J$ and $JJ^*$ differ by $I$.

For the profile $\boldsymbol d$, define a closed $B$-invariant subspace
$N_{\boldsymbol d}\subset G$ as follows.  In every $j$-family include the
entire $k=0$ copy.  In precisely $d_j$ of the remaining copies include
$H_+$ (all remaining copies when $d_j=\infty$), and put zero in the other
copies.  Finally set
\[
 M_{\boldsymbol d}=N_{\boldsymbol d}\oplus\{0\}\subset G\oplus G.
\]
It is $J$-invariant and $J|_{M_{\boldsymbol d}}$ is exactly
$B|_{N_{\boldsymbol d}}$.  The full $k=0$ copy contributes $\Gamma_j$;
each included half-space contributes $\overline D_j$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:spectrumprofile}.

If $\lambda\in D_j$, then $c_jI+r_jU-\lambda$ is invertible, while
$c_jI+r_jS-\lambda$ is bounded below with range of codimension one.  All
blocks belonging to the other disks are invertible because the closed disks
are disjoint.  Direct sums therefore give defect $d_j$, as claimed.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and novelty caution}

Theorem~\ref{thm:pairing} gives an exact structural test once the invariant
subspace is fixed, and Theorem~\ref{thm:realize} shows that arbitrary finite
profiles occur without cross-hole restrictions for one nonnormal operator.
This does \emph{not} classify which profiles occur in the invariant-subspace
lattice of an arbitrary prescribed operator.  That remaining question can
encode difficult information about the lattice itself.

The individual ingredients are classical in flavor.  In particular, the
all-or-none theorem and the residual-spectrum statement predate the source,
Barnes~\cite{barnes} studies restriction/quotient Fredholm relations and gives
an invertible shift/backward-shift triangular example, and Conway--Olin solve
the normal-Hilbert case~\cite{conwayolin}.  Bounded exact-phrase and subject
searches through 11 August 2026 did not locate the precise canonical pairing
plus one-fixed-nonnormal-operator simultaneous multiplicity realization stated
above.  The appropriate novelty claim is therefore a candidate partial
synthesis, not a claimed solution of the full arbitrary-operator problem.
