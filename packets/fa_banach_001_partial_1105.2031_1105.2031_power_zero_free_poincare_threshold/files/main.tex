\section{Source question and normalization}

Michel Ledoux and Ionel Popescu ask in Remark 2 of
\emph{The One Dimensional Free Poincar\'e Inequality}
\cite{LP} (arXiv:1105.2031, PDF page 15):

\begin{quote}
Poincar\'e's inequality and the $C^2$ condition on the density $w$ imply
that $w$ must be positive. Also, if $w$ is positive everywhere and
continuous then $P(\rho)$ holds for some $\rho$. It is interesting to see
what happens if the $C^2$ condition on $w$ is dropped. Is it still true that
there is a Poincar\'e inequality satisfied for some $\rho>0$? And if so,
under what are the regularity conditions on $w$?
\end{quote}

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{Remark 2 in arXiv:1105.2031, PDF page 15.}
\end{figure}

We use the source normalization on $[-2,2]$. Set
\[
 \alpha(dx)=\frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}{2\pi}\,dx,
 \qquad
 \beta(dx)=\frac{dx}{\pi\sqrt{4-x^2}},
\]
and
\[
 \omega(dx\,dy)=
 \frac{4-xy}{4\pi^2\sqrt{(4-x^2)(4-y^2)}}\,dx\,dy.
\]
For a smooth real function $f$, write
\[
 \Qf(f)=\iint\left(\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}\right)^2
             \omega(dx\,dy),
 \qquad
 \E_w(f)=\int (f')^2w\,d\alpha.
\]
A probability measure $\mu=w\alpha$ satisfies $P(\rho)$ when
\[
 2\rho\,\Qf(f)\le \E_w(f)                         \tag{1}
\]
for every smooth $f$ on $[-2,2]$.

For $0<\theta<\pi$, put
\[
 x=2\cos\theta,\qquad W(\theta)=w(2\cos\theta).
\]
Thus $\beta$ becomes $d\theta/\pi$, while
$\alpha$ becomes $(2/\pi)\sin^2\theta\,d\theta$.

\section{Proof intuition}

The source diagonalizes the free energy with Chebyshev polynomials. If
$g=\mathcal U f$ and
\[
 k(\theta)=g(2\cos\theta)\sin\theta,
\]
then $k$ is a sine series and $\Qf(f)$ is exactly the weighted coefficient
sum $\sum n|b_n|^2$. This borderline one-dimensional $H^{1/2}$ quantity
controls every finite $L^p$ norm of $k$. Consequently, H\"older's inequality
controls $\int g^2/w\,d\alpha$ by $\Qf(f)$ whenever $W^{-1}$ has slightly
more than $L^1$ integrability. A weighted Cauchy--Schwarz inequality then
gives (1), with no differentiability assumption on $w$.

The reverse mechanism is weighted capacity. Near an interior zero of order
$\gamma>1$, a transition across a window of width $\varepsilon$ costs only
$O(\varepsilon^{\gamma-1})$ in $\E_w$. At the borderline $\gamma=1$, the
optimal logarithmic transition costs $O(1/\log(1/\varepsilon))$. These
functions converge to a nonconstant step. The source estimate
$\Qf(f)\ge \tfrac12\Var_\beta(f)$ keeps their free energy bounded away from
zero, so (1) cannot hold. The two mechanisms meet at the sharp exponent
$\gamma=1$.

\section{A rough-density sufficient condition}

Let $\phi_n(x)=T_n(x/2)$ and $\psi_n(x)=U_n(x/2)$ be the Chebyshev systems
used in \cite{LP}. The operator
\[
 (\mathcal U f)(x)=\int\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}\,\beta(dy)
\]
satisfies $\mathcal U\phi_n=\frac12\psi_{n-1}$ for $n\ge1$.

\begin{proposition}[Reciprocal-integrability criterion]\label{prop:suff}
Let $\mu=w\alpha$ be a probability measure, with $w>0$ almost everywhere.
If
\[
 W^{-1}\in L^r(0,\pi)\quad\text{for some }r>1,                 \tag{2}
\]
then $\mu$ satisfies $P(\rho)$ for some $\rho>0$.
No continuity or differentiability of $w$ is required.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
It is enough first to consider a polynomial $f$; the final inequality then
extends to smooth $f$ by its Chebyshev expansion. Write
\[
 g=\mathcal U f=\sum_{m\ge0}b_m\psi_m.
\]
The identities in \cite{LP} give
\[
 \Qf(f)=\langle(\mathcal M+I)g,g\rangle_{L^2(\alpha)}
         =\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)|b_m|^2,                           \tag{3}
\]
where $\mathcal M\psi_m=m\psi_m$. Since
\[
 \psi_m(2\cos\theta)=\frac{\sin((m+1)\theta)}{\sin\theta},
\]
we have
\[
 k(\theta):=g(2\cos\theta)\sin\theta
     =\sum_{m\ge0}b_m\sin((m+1)\theta).                      \tag{4}
\]

Fix $2\le p<\infty$ and set $q=p/(p-1)\in(1,2]$. The
Hausdorff--Young inequality for the odd extension of (4), followed by
H\"older's inequality for sequences, gives
\begin{align*}
 \|k\|_{L^p(0,\pi)}&\le C_p\|(b_m)\|_{\ell^q},\\
 \sum_{m\ge0}|b_m|^q
 &\le
 \left(\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)|b_m|^2\right)^{q/2}
 \left(\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)^{-q/(2-q)}\right)^{(2-q)/2}.
\end{align*}
The last series converges because $q>1$ (the case $q=2$ is immediate).
Therefore
\[
 \|k\|_{L^p(0,\pi)}^2\le C_p'\,\Qf(f)                       \tag{5}
\]
for every finite $p\ge2$.

Let $r'=r/(r-1)$ and take $p=2r'$. From the change of variables above,
(2), H\"older's inequality, and (5),
\begin{align*}
 J(f):=\int\frac{g^2}{w}\,d\alpha
 &=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi\frac{|k(\theta)|^2}{W(\theta)}\,d\theta\\
 &\le \frac{2}{\pi}\|W^{-1}\|_{L^r}
       \|k\|_{L^{2r'}}^2
 \le C\,\Qf(f).                                             \tag{6}
\end{align*}
Finally, the source identity
\[
 \Qf(f)=\int f'\,\mathcal U f\,d\alpha
\]
and weighted Cauchy--Schwarz yield
\[
 \Qf(f)^2
 \le \left(\int(f')^2w\,d\alpha\right)
      \left(\int\frac{(\mathcal U f)^2}{w}\,d\alpha\right)
 \le C\,\E_w(f)\Qf(f).
\]
After cancelling $\Qf(f)$ (the zero case is trivial), we obtain
$\E_w(f)\ge C^{-1}\Qf(f)$. Thus (1) holds with
$\rho=(2C)^{-1}>0$.
\end{proof}

\section{The sharp power-zero threshold}

\begin{theorem}[Finite interior power zeros]\label{thm:sharp}
Let $\mu=w\alpha$ be a probability measure. Assume there are finitely many
points $a_1,\dots,a_N\in(-2,2)$ and exponents $\gamma_j\ge0$ such that,
in pairwise disjoint neighborhoods of the $a_j$,
\[
 c_j|x-a_j|^{\gamma_j}\le w(x)le
 C_j|x-a_j|^{\gamma_j},                                     \tag{7}
\]
with $c_j,C_j>0$, and assume $w$ is bounded above and bounded away from zero
off those neighborhoods. Then
\[
 \mu\text{ satisfies }P(\rho)\text{ for some }\rho>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \max_j\gamma_j<1.                                         \tag{8}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $\gamma_*:=\max_j\gamma_j<1$. Choose $r>1$ with
$r\gamma_*<1$. Since $x=2\cos\theta$ is bi-Lipschitz near every interior
point $a_j$, (7) implies $W(\theta)^{-r}$ is locally integrable near each
corresponding $\theta_j$. Away from those points, $W^{-r}$ is bounded.
Thus (2) holds, and Proposition~\ref{prop:suff} proves $P(\rho)$.

Conversely, suppose that (7) holds at an $a_j$ with exponent
$\gamma:=\gamma_j\ge1$, and write $a_j=2\cos\theta_0$. For
$h(\theta)=f(2\cos\theta)$, a direct change of variables gives
\[
 \E_w(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\pi W(\theta)|h'(\theta)|^2\,d\theta,
 \qquad
 \Var_\beta(f)=\Var_{d\theta/\pi}(h).                       \tag{9}
\]

If $\gamma>1$, take a fixed smooth transition from $0$ to $1$ and compress
it into a window of width $O(\varepsilon)$ around $\theta_0$. The upper
bound in (7) and (9) give
\[
 \E_w(f_\varepsilon)=O(\varepsilon^{\gamma-1})\longrightarrow0. \tag{10}
\]
If $\gamma=1$, use on one side of $\theta_0$ the logarithmic profile
\[
 h_\varepsilon(\theta_0+t)=
 \frac{\log(t/\varepsilon)}{\log(\delta/\varepsilon)},
 \qquad \varepsilon\le t\le\delta,                          \tag{11}
\]
extended by $0$ before the transition and by $1$ after it. Smooth the two
corners inside comparable subintervals. Since
$|h_\varepsilon'|=(t\log(\delta/\varepsilon))^{-1}$ on the main
transition, (7) and (9) give
\[
 \E_w(f_\varepsilon)
 \le \frac{C}{\log^2(\delta/\varepsilon)}
       \int_\varepsilon^\delta\frac{dt}{t}
 =O\!\left(\frac1{\log(\delta/\varepsilon)}\right)
 \longrightarrow0.                                         \tag{12}
\]
The smoothing can be chosen with the same bound.

In both cases, $h_\varepsilon$ converges in $L^2(d\theta)$ to the
nonconstant step $\mathbf 1_{(\theta_0,\pi)}$ (up to reversing orientation).
Consequently
\[
 \Var_\beta(f_\varepsilon)\longrightarrow
 \frac{\theta_0}{\pi}\left(1-\frac{\theta_0}{\pi}\right)>0.
\]
Proposition 3.2(3) of \cite{LP} states
\[
 \Qf(f)\ge\frac12\Var_\beta(f).                             \tag{13}
\]
Thus $\Qf(f_\varepsilon)$ stays bounded away from zero while
$\E_w(f_\varepsilon)\to0$, contradicting (1) for every $\rho>0$.
This proves the reverse implication in (8).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact one-zero family]
Fix $a\in(-2,2)$ and $\gamma\ge0$, and normalize
\[
 w_{a,\gamma}(x)=
 \frac{|x-a|^\gamma}{\int_{-2}^2|t-a|^\gamma\,\alpha(dt)}.
\]
Then $w_{a,\gamma}\alpha$ satisfies a free Poincar\'e inequality if and
only if $\gamma<1$. In particular, dropping $C^2$ does not preserve the
inequality automatically: the continuous full-support density
$w_{a,1}$ is a counterexample. On the other hand, every
$0<\gamma<1$ gives a density which vanishes at $a$ but still satisfies
$P(\rho)$ for some $\rho>0$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty check}

The normalization, the identities for $\mathcal U$, $\mathcal M$, and
$\Qf$, and the lower bound (13) were checked directly against \cite{LP}.
The exponent computation in the sufficiency proof is sharp at the method's
endpoint: with $p=2r'$ one has
$q=p/(p-1)=2r/(r+1)$ and $q/(2-q)=r>1$. The necessity proof separately
checks the power and logarithmic transition costs.

This packet gives a complete characterization for finitely many interior
power-law zeros and a more general sufficient condition. It does not
characterize arbitrary measurable or oscillatory densities, endpoint
degeneracies, singular components, or measures with several support
intervals. For that reason it is a partial result rather than a claimed full
answer to the source's broad regularity question.

A bounded literature search checked arXiv:1105.2031; the exact wording of
Remark 2; combinations of ``free Poincar\'e'', ``density regularity'',
``power weight'', ``semicircle'', ``fractional Hardy'', and ``interior zero'';
and the later refinement paper of Houdr\'e and Popescu
\cite{HP} (arXiv:1311.4585). The latter develops higher-order and
Brascamp--Lieb refinements, not the rough-density or power-zero question.
No source stating the criterion or the exponent-one threshold above was
located. This is bounded evidence, not a guarantee of novelty.

Review as a likely valid substantial partial result. The highest-value checks
