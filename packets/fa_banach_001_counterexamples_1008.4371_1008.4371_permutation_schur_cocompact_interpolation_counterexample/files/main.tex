\begin{abstract}
Cwikel and Tintarev ask whether cocompactness persists under interpolation
for every choice of the acting group, without the additional mollifier
hypotheses in their theorem.  It does not.  Take both Banach couples to be
$(\ell_\infty,\ell_1)$ and let the group consist of all coordinate
permutations.  The endpoint identity on $\ell_1$ is cocompact because
$\ell_1$ has the Schur property.  For every $0<\theta<1$, however, both
complex interpolation and the real method with fine index $1/\theta$ yield
$\ell_{1/\theta}$.  Normalized indicators of growing finite sets have norm
one but are weakly null after every sequence of coordinate permutations.
Thus the interpolated identity is not cocompact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source question}

The source is Cwikel and Tintarev's \emph{On interpolation of cocompact
imbeddings} \cite{CT}.  We use arXiv:1008.4371v3.  For a group $\D$ of
continuous linear bijections of a Banach space $A$, the paper defines
\[
 u_k\stackrel{\D}{\rightharpoonup}u
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 g_k(u_k-u)\rightharpoonup0
 \text{ for every sequence }(g_k)\subset\D.
 \tag{1}\label{eq:dweak}
\]
An embedding $A\hookrightarrow B$ is $\D$-cocompact when every sequence
converging in the sense of \eqref{eq:dweak} converges in the norm of $B$.

Theorem 2.3 proves persistence of cocompactness under real and complex
interpolation assuming, among other baseline hypotheses, the existence of a
$\D$-covariant mollifier family.  Remark 1.7 asks whether the additional
conditions can be removed so that persistence holds for every group $\D$,
and explicitly anticipates that the answer may be negative.

\section{Proof intuition}

The two endpoints play opposite roles.  At the strong endpoint, the Schur
property of $\ell_1$ turns ordinary weak convergence into norm convergence.
Because the identity belongs to every group, this immediately makes the
identity on $\ell_1$ cocompact relative to any group of isometries.

Interpolation with $\ell_\infty$ destroys the Schur property.  In the
intermediate $\ell_p$ space, distribute unit mass evenly over $k$ coordinates.
The resulting vector still has norm one.  A permutation may move its support
to any $k$-element set, but it cannot concentrate the individual coefficient,
which is only $k^{-1/p}$.  Splitting any dual vector into a finite head and a
small tail proves weak convergence uniformly over all such moved supports.

\section{The construction}

For a permutation $\pi$ of $\N$, let
\[
 (P_\pi x)_j=x_{\pi^{-1}(j)},
 \qquad
 \D=\{P_\pi:\pi\in\operatorname{Sym}(\N)\}.
\]
Every $P_\pi$ is a linear bijective isometry on $\ell_r$ for
$1\le r\le\infty$.  Set
\[
 (A_0,A_1)=(B_0,B_1)=(\linf,\lone).
 \tag{2}\label{eq:couples}
\]
Thus $A_1\hookrightarrow A_0$, all endpoint inclusions $A_j\hookrightarrow
B_j$ are identities, and the same group acts isometrically on all four
endpoints.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:endpoint}
The identity embedding $\lone\hookrightarrow\lone$ is $\D$-cocompact.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $x_k\stackrel{\D}{\rightharpoonup}0$ in $\lone$.  Since the identity
operator belongs to $\D$, definition \eqref{eq:dweak} implies
$x_k\rightharpoonup0$ ordinarily.  The Schur property of $\ell_1$ says that
weakly convergent sequences converge in norm \cite{Diestel}.  Hence
$\|x_k\|_1\to0$, exactly as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:flat}
Let $1<p<\infty$ and define
\[
 u_k=k^{-1/p}\sum_{j=1}^k e_j\in\ell_p.
 \tag{3}\label{eq:uk}
\]
Then $\|u_k\|_p=1$ and
$u_k\stackrel{\D}{\rightharpoonup}0$ in $\ell_p$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The norm assertion is immediate.  Let $(P_{\pi_k})$ be an arbitrary sequence
in $\D$.  The vector $P_{\pi_k}u_k$ equals $k^{-1/p}$ on a $k$-element set
$S_k\subset\N$ and vanishes elsewhere.

Write $p'=p/(p-1)$.  Fix $f\in\ell_{p'}$ and a finite set $F\subset\N$.
By the triangle inequality and H\"older's inequality,
\begin{align*}
 \bigl|\langle P_{\pi_k}u_k,f\rangle\bigr|
 &\le k^{-1/p}\sum_{j\in S_k\cap F}|f_j|
     +k^{-1/p}\sum_{j\in S_k\setminus F}|f_j|\\
 &\le k^{-1/p}\|f\mathbf1_F\|_1
     +k^{-1/p}|S_k|^{1/p}\|f\mathbf1_{F^c}\|_{p'}\\
 &=k^{-1/p}\|f\mathbf1_F\|_1+\|f\mathbf1_{F^c}\|_{p'}.
 \tag{4}\label{eq:headtail}
\end{align*}
For fixed $F$, the first term tends to zero.  The second can be made
arbitrarily small by taking $F$ sufficiently large, independently of the
sets $S_k$.  Therefore $P_{\pi_k}u_k\rightharpoonup0$ for every permutation
sequence, proving the $\D$-weak convergence.
\end{proof}

\section{Failure of persistence}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For the couples and group in \eqref{eq:couples}, the endpoint embedding
$A_1\hookrightarrow B_1$ is $\D$-cocompact.  Nevertheless, for every
$\theta\in(0,1)$, if $p=1/\theta$, then neither
\[
 [A_0,A_1]_\theta\hookrightarrow[B_0,B_1]_\theta
 \tag{5}\label{eq:complex}
\]
nor
\[
 (A_0,A_1)_{\theta,p}\hookrightarrow(B_0,B_1)_{\theta,p}
 \tag{6}\label{eq:real}
\]
is $\D$-cocompact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Endpoint cocompactness is Lemma \ref{lem:endpoint}.  The standard
sequence-space interpolation identities are \cite{BL}
\[
 [\ell_\infty,\ell_1]_\theta=\ell_p,
 \qquad
 (\ell_\infty,\ell_1)_{\theta,q}=\ell_{p,q},
 \qquad \frac1p=\theta.
 \tag{7}\label{eq:interpolation}
\]
For $q=p$, $\ell_{p,p}=\ell_p$ with an equivalent norm.  Thus both
\eqref{eq:complex} and \eqref{eq:real} are identity embeddings of $\ell_p$
into itself, up to equivalent norms.  Lemma \ref{lem:flat} supplies a
$\D$-weakly null sequence whose norm is constantly one.  Neither embedding
is $\D$-cocompact.
\end{proof}

This is a negative answer to Remark 1.7.  The same example defeats the
complex assertion and, at an allowed fine index, the real assertion for every
interpolation parameter.

\section{Checks, comparison, and novelty status}

\paragraph{Quantifiers.}
The crucial estimate \eqref{eq:headtail} is uniform in the permutation
sequence.  It therefore proves the universal quantifier in the source's
definition of $\D$-weak convergence, not merely weak convergence along one
selected sequence of permutations.

\paragraph{Equivalent norms.}
Replacing the real-method norm by the equivalent standard $\ell_p$ norm does
not change the continuous dual, weak convergence, or which sequences converge
to zero in norm.  Hence it cannot change cocompactness of the identity.

\paragraph{Relation to the source hypotheses.}
All baseline endpoint and group assumptions hold.  The construction need not
admit the source's covariant mollifier family: that is an additional condition
whose proposed removal is exactly what the question asks about.  Indeed, the
source theorem and Theorem \ref{thm:main} together imply that no such family
can satisfy all its stated conditions for this example.

\paragraph{Bounded novelty audit.}
The run indexes contained no duplicate.  Exact and close searches found no
published answer.  OpenAlex listed twelve works citing the source; their titles
and available abstracts concern applications rather than this abstract
question.  Tintarev's 2013 survey \cite{Tintarev} still says one would expect a
general interpolation principle and refers only to the conditional source
theorem.  That survey uses the same flat vectors to illustrate remainders for
the shift group, but does not combine them with the full permutation group and
the Schur endpoint.  Novelty confidence is moderate-to-high under this bounded
audit, not an exhaustive priority claim.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Prioritize review.  The proof is short and answers both interpolation methods.
Check the exponent convention in \eqref{eq:interpolation}, the head--tail
estimate, and priority beyond the searched citation graph.
