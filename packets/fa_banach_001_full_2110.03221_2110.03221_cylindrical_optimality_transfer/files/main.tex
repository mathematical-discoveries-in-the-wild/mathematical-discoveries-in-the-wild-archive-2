\section{Conjecture and precise conclusion}

For $A>0$, the source defines $\E(A)\subset L^2(\R^4)$ from functions
\[
 f(x,t)=h_0(x)g_0(t)+h_1(x)\mathbf1_B(x)g_1(t),
 \qquad x\in\R^3, t\in\R,
\]
where the spatial boundary $\partial B$ is a uniformly $C^2$ two-manifold and
all amplitudes have bounded $C^2$ norm.  Its cylindrical shearlet theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper}
 \sup_{f\in\E(A)}\|f-f_N\|_2^2
 \le C N^{-1}(\log N)^2.
\end{equation}
The following remark conjectures that the exponent one is optimal.

\begin{figure}[h]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{figures/conjecture_crop.png}
 \caption{The optimality conjecture on page 10 of arXiv:2110.03221.}
\end{figure}

The word ``optimal'' requires a feasibility convention.  We use exactly the
standard one in the 3D cartoon/shearlet literature cited by the source:
normalized dictionaries with adaptive $N$-term selection subject to
polynomial-depth search.  Without an information budget of this kind, an
arbitrarily fine dense dictionary destroys every universal lower bound [2].

\begin{theorem}[optimality of the cylindrical exponent]\label{thm:main}
Under polynomial-depth search, no dictionary and selection scheme can satisfy,
for any $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 \sup_{f\in\E(A)}\|f-A_Nf\|_2^2
 \le C_\varepsilon N^{-1-\varepsilon}
 \qquad(N\ge1).
\]
Consequently \eqref{eq:upper} has the optimal power exponent, up to its
logarithmic factor.
\end{theorem}

\section{A 3D hard class inside the 4D class}

Let $\C_3(A_0)$ be the ordinary class of 3D $C^2$ cartoon functions
\[
 u(x)=a_0(x)+a_1(x)\mathbf1_B(x),\qquad x\in\R^3,
\]
with uniformly controlled amplitudes and $C^2$ surface boundary.  Its standard
information-theoretic optimality theorem states that no polynomial-depth
scheme has squared $L^2$ error exponent strictly larger than one [2,3].

Choose a nonzero $g\in C_c^2((-1,1))$ and normalize it in $L^2(\R)$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
 Ju(x,t)=u(x)g(t).
\end{equation}
Then $J:L^2(\R^3)\to L^2(\R^4)$ is an isometry.  After multiplying the whole
class $\C_3(A_0)$ by one fixed positive constant, chosen only from the $C^2$
bounds of $g$ and $A$, its image under $J$ is contained in $\E(A)$.  Such a
fixed rescaling does not change any approximation exponent.

The adjoint of $J$ is the time-profile projection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjoint}
 (J^*F)(x)=\int_{\R}F(x,t)\overline{g(t)}\,dt.
\end{equation}
It satisfies $J^*J=I$ and $\|J^*\|=1$.

\begin{lemma}[projection of schemes]\label{lem:projection}
Every polynomial-depth $N$-term scheme on $L^2(\R^4)$ induces a
polynomial-depth $N$-term scheme on $L^2(\R^3)$ whose error on $u$ is at most
the original error on $Ju$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let an approximant selected from a normalized 4D dictionary be
\[
 A_N(Ju)=\sum_{n=1}^N c_n\phi_{i_n}.
\]
Apply $J^*$ and use $J^*J=I$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contract}
 \left\|u-\sum_{n=1}^N c_nJ^*\phi_{i_n}\right\|_2
 \le \|Ju-A_N(Ju)\|_2.
\end{equation}
Each projected atom has norm at most one.  Delete zero atoms; for every
nonzero one, normalize $J^*\phi_i$ and absorb its norm into its coefficient.
The indices and their ordering have not changed, so the same polynomial search
bound applies.  Equation \eqref{eq:contract} proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof and scope}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Suppose a 4D scheme achieved squared error
$C_\varepsilon N^{-1-\varepsilon}$ uniformly on $\E(A)$.  Restrict it to the
fixed scaled copy $J\C_3(A_0)\subset\E(A)$ and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:projection}.  The result is a polynomial-depth 3D scheme with
the same squared-error exponent on $\C_3(A_0)$.  This contradicts the known
3D optimality theorem.  Hence no exponent larger than one is possible.
Together with \eqref{eq:upper}, this proves optimality of the power exponent.
\end{proof}

The result does not remove the logarithmic factor in \eqref{eq:upper}; it
settles exactly the power-law conjecture.  A later paper [4] proves a related
projection-based optimality statement for a different $L^2(\R^3)$ model with
two spatial variables and time, whose optimal exponent is two.  A bounded
exact-title and conjecture search on 13 August 2026 found no explicit later
answer for the present 3D-space-plus-time class.
