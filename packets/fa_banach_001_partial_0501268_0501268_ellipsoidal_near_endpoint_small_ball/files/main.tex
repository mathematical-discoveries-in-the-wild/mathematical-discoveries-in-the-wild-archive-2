\section{Source conjecture}

Lata\l a and Oleszkiewicz~\cite{LO} consider the standard Gaussian measure
$\gamma_n$ on $\R^n$ and the inradius
\[
 w(K)=\sup\{r>0:B_2^n(0,r)\subset K\}
\]
of a symmetric convex set.  Their Conjecture~1, on PDF page~2 of
arXiv:math/0501268, is the following.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Thus, for every $\kappa\in(0,1)$, the aim is to find constants
$C(\kappa),w_0(\kappa)>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conj}
 \gamma_n(tK)\le (C(\kappa)t)^{\kappa w(K)^2}\gamma_n(K),
 \qquad 0\le t\le1,
\end{equation}
whenever $\gamma_n(K)\le1/2$ and $w(K)\ge w_0(\kappa)$.  The source proves
the coefficient $1/4$ in place of every $\kappa<1$.

\section{Proof intuition}

For an ellipsoid, the Gaussian measure of $tK$ is the lower tail at $t^2$ of
a weighted chi-square variable $X=\sum_i\lambda_i g_i^2$.  The inradius
condition bounds every weight by $w^{-2}$.  After enlarging the ellipsoid so
that its Gaussian measure is exactly $1/2$, the number $1$ is a median of
$X$.  Since no single weight can dominate, a one-sided variance estimate
forces the total spectral mass $m=\sum_i\lambda_i$ to satisfy
$m\ge1-O(w^{-1})$.

The exact product Laplace transform of $X$ then produces a lower-tail power
$m w^2$.  As the inradius grows this power approaches the conjectured
endpoint $w^2$, so every fixed coefficient $\kappa<1$ follows.  The argument
also tolerates zero eigenvalues, hence unbounded ellipsoidal cylinders.

\section{Statement of the partial result}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Fix $\kappa\in(0,1)$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constants}
 \eta=\frac{1+\kappa}{2},\qquad
 C_\kappa=\exp(1/\eta),\qquad
 w_{0,\kappa}=\max\left\{
 \frac{2\sqrt2}{1-\kappa},\sqrt{2\log2}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cylinder}
 K=\left\{x\in\R^n:\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i x_i^2\le1\right\},
 \qquad 1\le r\le n,\quad \lambda_i>0,
\end{equation}
after an orthogonal change of coordinates.  If $\gamma_n(K)\le1/2$ and
$w(K)\ge w_{0,\kappa}$, then
\[
 \gamma_n(tK)\le(C_\kappa t)^{\kappa w(K)^2}\gamma_n(K)
 \qquad(0\le t\le1).
\]
In particular, Conjecture~1 holds for every centered ellipsoid and every
ellipsoidal cylinder.
\end{theorem}

\section{Half-measure reduction}

We use the B-inequality of Cordero-Erausquin, Fradelizi, and Maurey
\cite{CFM}: for a symmetric convex set $K$, the function
$a\mapsto\log\gamma_n(e^aK)$ is concave.  The following consequence is also
used explicitly in the source paper.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:normalize}
If $\gamma_n(K)\le1/2$, choose $q\ge1$ so that $\gamma_n(qK)=1/2$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
 \frac{\gamma_n(tK)}{\gamma_n(K)}
 \le\frac{\gamma_n(tqK)}{\gamma_n(qK)}
 \qquad(0<t\le1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $F(a)=\log\gamma_n(e^aK)$, $A=\log q\ge0$, and $h=\log t\le0$.
For concave $F$, the backward increment $F(x+h)-F(x)$ is nondecreasing in
$x$.  Hence $F(h)-F(0)\le F(A+h)-F(A)$, which is exactly
\eqref{eq:ratio} after exponentiation.
\end{proof}

It is therefore enough to prove the theorem when $\gamma_n(K)=1/2$.
Indeed, $qK$ has inradius $W=qw(K)\ge w(K)$.  When $C_\kappa t\le1$, the
power with $W^2$ is no larger than the power with $w(K)^2$; when
$C_\kappa t\ge1$, the conclusion follows directly from $tK\subset K$.

\section{The weighted chi-square estimate}

Assume now that $\gamma_n(K)=1/2$ and write $W=w(K)$.  If
$g_1,\dots,g_r$ are independent standard Gaussians, set
\[
 X=\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i g_i^2,\qquad
 m=\mathbb EX=\sum_i\lambda_i,\qquad
 v=\operatorname{Var}(X)=2\sum_i\lambda_i^2.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:translation}
 \gamma_n(tK)=\mathbb P\{X\le t^2\},\qquad
 \mathbb P\{X\le1\}=\mathbb P\{X\ge1\}=\frac12.
\end{equation}
The inradius identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights}
 \max_i\lambda_i=W^{-2},\qquad
 v\le2W^{-2}m.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Median-to-mean estimate]\label{lem:mass}
Under \eqref{eq:translation}--\eqref{eq:weights},
\[
 m\ge1-\frac{\sqrt2}{W}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The assertion is immediate if $m\ge1$.  Suppose $m<1$.  Cantelli's
one-sided variance inequality gives
\[
 \frac12=\mathbb P\{X-m\ge1-m\}
 \le\frac{v}{v+(1-m)^2}.
\]
Consequently $(1-m)^2\le v\le2m/W^2\le2/W^2$, proving the claim.
For completeness, Cantelli's inequality follows by applying Markov's
inequality to $(Y+v/a)^2$ when $\mathbb EY=0$,
$\mathbb EY^2=v$, and $a>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lower-tail estimate]\label{lem:tail}
For every $t>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 \mathbb P\{X\le t^2\}
 \le e^{W^2/2}t^{mW^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $s>0$, Markov's inequality and the Gaussian Laplace transform give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laplace}
 \mathbb P\{X\le t^2\}
 \le e^{st^2}\mathbb Ee^{-sX}
 =e^{st^2}\prod_i(1+2s\lambda_i)^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
Put $L=W^{-2}$.  The function
$f(x)=\log(1+2sx)$ is concave and vanishes at zero, so
$f(x)\ge(x/L)f(L)$ for $0\le x\le L$.  Therefore
\[
 \sum_i\log(1+2s\lambda_i)
 \ge mW^2\log(1+2s/W^2).
\]
Choosing $s=W^2/(2t^2)$ in \eqref{eq:laplace} yields
\[
 \mathbb P\{X\le t^2\}
 \le e^{W^2/2}(1+t^{-2})^{-mW^2/2}
 \le e^{W^2/2}t^{mW^2}.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:normalize}, assume $\gamma_n(K)=1/2$.  The first lower
bound in \eqref{eq:constants} and Lemma~\ref{lem:mass} imply
\[
 m\ge1-\frac{\sqrt2}{W}\ge\eta.
\]
For $0<t\le C_\kappa^{-1}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} gives
\begin{align*}
 \frac{\gamma_n(tK)}{(C_\kappa t)^{\kappa W^2}/2}
 &\le2e^{W^2/2}C_\kappa^{-\kappa W^2}
 t^{(m-\kappa)W^2}\\
 &\le2e^{W^2/2}C_\kappa^{-mW^2}
 \le2e^{-W^2/2}\le1.
\end{align*}
Here we used $\log C_\kappa=1/\eta$, $m\ge\eta$, and
$W\ge\sqrt{2\log2}$.  If $C_\kappa^{-1}\le t\le1$, then
\[
 \gamma_n(tK)\le\gamma_n(K)=\frac12
 \le(C_\kappa t)^{\kappa W^2}\frac12.
\]
The case $t=0$ is immediate.  The reduction following
Lemma~\ref{lem:normalize} completes the proof for the original $K$.

\section{Scope, sharpness, and novelty}

For Euclidean balls of Gaussian measure $1/2$, the squared radius is
asymptotic to $n$, while the probability at scale $t\downarrow0$ has power
$n$.  Thus coefficient $1$ is the natural endpoint and cannot be replaced by
a larger universal coefficient.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} reaches every strict
sub-endpoint for quadratic bodies.

The factorized transform in \eqref{eq:laplace} is the decisive special
structure.  For a general norm, the analogous random variable is a supremum
of correlated Gaussian linear forms; the inradius bounds individual
variances but does not supply the product estimate.  Optimizing the source
paper's isoperimetric anchor is likewise capped at coefficient $1/4$.  These
two upgrade attempts did not yield the general conjecture.

A bounded novelty search on 11 August 2026 covered the run indexes, the exact
arXiv id and title, exact-statement searches involving $\gamma_n(tK)$ and
$\kappa w^2$, and combinations of ``Gaussian small ball'', ``inradius'',
``ellipsoid'', and ``weighted chi-square''.  No explicit near-endpoint
ellipsoidal theorem or full resolution of Conjecture~1 was found.  This
supports but does not certify novelty.
