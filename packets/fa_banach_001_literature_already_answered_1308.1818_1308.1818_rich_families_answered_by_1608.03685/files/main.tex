\section*{The source questions}
Let $X_M=\overline{X\cap M}$.  The source says that \emph{suitable models generate nice rich families in $X$} if, for every prescribed finite list of formulas and countable parameter set, one can choose suitable models $M$ so that
\[
 \{X_M:M\in\mathcal M\}
\]
is a cofinal, $\sigma$-closed family of separable subspaces and
$M\subset N$ exactly when $X_M\subset X_N$.

Question 2 on source PDF page 7 asks whether this holds for every Banach space, specifically including $\ell_\infty^c(\Gamma)$. Question 3 on source PDF page 10 asks whether separable determination by suitable models and by rich families is equivalent without assuming a fundamental minimal system or density $\aleph_1$ \cite{CK}.

\section*{The explicit later answer}
The abstract of \cite{Cuth} announces equivalence in Banach spaces and positive answers to two questions of C\'uth--Kalenda.  The exact results are:

\begin{itemize}
\item Theorem 15 (supporting PDF page 5): if a topological space is homeomorphic to a Banach space, then suitable models generate nice rich families in it.  Taking the space to be any Banach space proves source Question 2, including the stated $\ell_\infty^c(\Gamma)$ case.
\item Theorem 4 (supporting PDF page 2): for a Banach space and a family $\mathcal F$ of closed separable subspaces, every rich $\mathcal F$ contains a suitable-model-large subfamily, and every suitable-model-large $\mathcal F$ contains a rich subfamily.  Applied to any family on which a statement is separably determined, these refinements preserve the statement and prove source Question 3.
\end{itemize}

The supporting paper itself explicitly identifies Theorem 15 as a positive answer to source Question 2 and Theorem 4 as yielding the positive answer to source Question 3.

\section*{Proof intuition}
Hilbert spaces already satisfy the desired model/subspace order correspondence.  Toru\'nczyk's topological classification supplies, for an arbitrary infinite-dimensional Banach space $X$, a homeomorphism $f:X\to H$ onto a Hilbert space of the same density.  Choose the models to contain $f$ and $f^{-1}$.  Elementarity then gives
\[
 f(X\cap M)=H\cap M,
 \qquad f(X_M)=H_M.
\]
Consequently cofinality, closure under increasing countable unions, and the equivalence
$M\subset N\Longleftrightarrow H_M\subset H_N$ transfer through $f$ to $X$.  This proves Theorem 15.  Theorem 4 then converts either large-family formulation into the other by passing to a subfamily, so every separable-determination equivalence survives unchanged.

\section*{Scope boundary}
The source also asks whether the particular spanning system used as a sufficient condition in its Lemma 2.6 is \emph{necessary} (Question 1).  The later theorem bypasses that condition by a topological conjugacy; it does not construct such a spanning system or prove its necessity.  Thus this packet classifies only Questions 2 and 3 as fully answered.
