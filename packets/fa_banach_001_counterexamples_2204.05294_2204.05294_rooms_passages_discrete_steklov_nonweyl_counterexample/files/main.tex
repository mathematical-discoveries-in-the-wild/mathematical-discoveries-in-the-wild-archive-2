\section{Source question}

Karpukhin, Lagac\'e, and Polterovich ask in Remark 4.3 of
\emph{Weyl's law for the Steklov problem on surfaces with rough boundary}
\cite{KLP} whether there exist domains for which the Steklov spectrum is
discrete but Weyl's law does not hold.  The remark appears on page 13 of the
arXiv PDF.  In exact transcription, the question is:

\begin{quote}
``It would be interesting to understand if there exist domains for which the
Steklov spectrum is discrete but the Weyl's law (1.2) does not hold.''
\end{quote}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

For a planar domain with finite boundary length, the asserted Weyl law is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weyl}
 N_{\Om}(\sigma)=\frac{|\partial\Om|}{\pi}\sigma+o(\sigma),
 \qquad \sigma\to\infty,
\end{equation}
where $N_{\Om}(\sigma)=\#\{k:\sigma_k(\Om)<\sigma\}$.

We use the natural variational setup of the source paper.  The boundary
Sobolev space $H^1_{\partial}(\Om)$ is the completion of the finite-norm
piecewise-smooth functions under
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bdy-norm}
 \|u\|_{H^1_{\partial}(\Om)}^2
 =\int_{\Om}|\nabla u|^2\dd x+\int_{\partial\Om}|u|^2\dd s.
\end{equation}
Its boundary map into $L^2(\partial\Om)$ is denoted by $\Tr$.  When this map
is compact, the associated Steklov form has compact resolvent, and its
eigenvalues have the min--max characterization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minmax}
 \sigma_k(\Om)=\inf_{\dim E=k+1}\ \sup_{0\ne u\in E}
 \frac{\int_{\Om}|\nabla u|^2\dd x}
 {\int_{\partial\Om}|\Tr u|^2\dd s}.
\end{equation}

\section{Proof intuition}

Attach infinitely many tiny rooms to a square through much thinner passages.
A function supported in one room pays only the passage conductance
$w_j/r_j$ in Dirichlet energy, while it receives boundary mass of order
$r_j$ from the room walls.  Thus its Steklov quotient is roughly
$w_j/r_j^2$.  We choose this to be merely $\log j$, producing far too many
low Steklov eigenvalues for a linear counting law.

The passages cannot be made arbitrarily thin without losing compactness of
the trace.  Two effects govern the exact threshold.  A neck of width $w_j$
leading to a room of size $r_j$ amplifies inlet boundary mass by $r_j/w_j$
and energy by $r_j^2/w_j$.  Meanwhile an interval of length $w_j$ on the
square carries at most $Cw_j\log(e/w_j)$ of the $L^2$ mass of a bounded
$H^{1/2}$ trace.  With
\[
 r_j=e^{-j^2},\qquad w_j=r_j^2\log(j+1),
\]
the inlet tail is summable and the energy amplification tends to zero.  This
is exactly enough for compactness, while the room test functions retain their
logarithmic quotients.

\section{The counterexample}

Let $Q=(0,1)^2$.  For $j\ge3$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
 x_j=2^{-j},\qquad r_j=e^{-j^2},\qquad
 w_j=r_j^2\log(j+1).
\end{equation}
Above the interval
\[
 B_j=(x_j-w_j/2,x_j+w_j/2)\times\{1\}
\]
attach the passage and room
\begin{align*}
 P_j&=(x_j-w_j/2,x_j+w_j/2)\times(1,1+r_j),\\
 R_j&=(x_j-r_j/2,x_j+r_j/2)\times(1+r_j,1+2r_j).
\end{align*}
Precisely, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:domain}
 \Om=\operatorname{int}\!\left(
 \overline Q\cup\bigcup_{j\ge3}(\overline P_j\cup\overline R_j)
 \right).
\end{equation}
The pieces are mutually disjoint away from their intended seams: indeed
$r_j=o(2^{-j})$.  They accumulate only at $(0,1)$, and their heights tend to
zero.  Following the top boundary of $Q$ and detouring around each rectangular
attachment therefore gives a simple closed curve.  Moreover,
\[
 |\partial\Om|\le 4+C\sum_{j\ge3}r_j<\infty.
\]
Thus $\Om$ is a bounded simply connected planar domain with rectifiable
Jordan boundary.

We next prove compactness of the trace.  Write $A_j=P_j\cup R_j$ and let
$\Gamma_j=\partial\Om\cap\partial A_j$ be the external boundary of the
$j$th attachment.

\begin{lemma}[Critical interval estimate]\label{lem:critical}
There is a constant $C$ such that, for every interval $I$ in one edge of
$\partial Q$ with length $0<\delta\le1/2$ and every
$v\in H^{1/2}(\partial Q)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical}
 \int_I|v|^2\dd s\le C\delta\log(e/\delta)
 \|v\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial Q)}^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The one-dimensional critical Sobolev estimate gives, for every $p\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp}
 \|v\|_{L^p(\partial Q)}\le C\sqrt p\,
 \|v\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial Q)}.
\end{equation}
For completeness, parameterize the whole polygonal curve $\partial Q$ by
arclength as a circle.  Hausdorff--Young and Cauchy--Schwarz then give
\eqref{eq:lp}: the relevant weight
sum is
$\sum_n(1+|n|)^{-p/(p-2)}=O(p)$, and its resulting exponent is at most
$1/2$.  H\"older's inequality now yields
\[
 \int_I|v|^2\dd s
 \le \delta^{1-2/p}\|v\|_{L^p}^2
 \le Cp\delta^{1-2/p}\|v\|_{H^{1/2}}^2.
\]
Take $p=2\log(e/\delta)$; then $\delta^{-2/p}$ is bounded by an absolute
constant.  This proves \eqref{eq:critical}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform neck trace estimate]\label{lem:neck}
Let a square room of side $r$ be joined to an inlet interval $B$ by a
rectangular passage of length $r$ and width $w$, where $0<w\le r/2$.
If $A$ is the room-passage union and $\Gamma$ is its external boundary, then
every $u\in H^1(A)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neck}
 \int_\Gamma|u|^2\dd s
 \le C\left(
 \frac rw\int_B|u|^2\dd s+
 \frac{r^2}{w}\int_A|\nabla u|^2\dd x
 \right),
\end{equation}
with an absolute constant $C$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Scale by $r$ and put $\eta=w/r$.  The passage becomes
$C_\eta=(0,\eta)\times(0,1)$, with bottom inlet $B$ and top opening $T$.
The one-dimensional fundamental theorem of calculus on each vertical line
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corridor-volume}
 \int_{C_\eta}|u|^2
 \le 2\int_B|u|^2+2\int_{C_\eta}|\partial_yu|^2.
\end{equation}
The one-dimensional trace inequality on each horizontal cross-section,
integrated in $y$, bounds the two vertical sides by
\[
 \frac C\eta\int_{C_\eta}|u|^2+C\eta
 \int_{C_\eta}|\partial_xu|^2.
\]
The same vertical fundamental-theorem estimate bounds $\int_T|u|^2$ by
$2\int_B|u|^2+2\int_{C_\eta}|\partial_yu|^2$.  Consequently the passage
boundary and its top opening are bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corridor}
 \frac C\eta\left(\int_B|u|^2+
 \int_{C_\eta}|\nabla u|^2\right).
\end{equation}

For the unit square room $S$, let $m$ be the mean of $u$ on $S$.  Standard
Poincar\'e and trace inequalities give
\[
 \|u-m\|_{L^2(\partial S)}^2\le C\int_S|\nabla u|^2.
\]
Comparing $m$ with the mean of $u$ on the opening $T$, whose length is
$\eta$, gives
\[
 |m|^2\le \frac C\eta\int_T|u|^2+
 \frac C\eta\int_S|\nabla u|^2.
\]
It follows that the room boundary is bounded by the right side of
\eqref{eq:corridor}, with the room energy added.  Hence, at unit scale,
\[
 \int_\Gamma|u|^2\le \frac C\eta
 \left(\int_B|u|^2+\int_A|\nabla u|^2\right).
\]
Boundary measure scales by $r$, whereas two-dimensional Dirichlet energy is
scale invariant.  Multiplying the last inequality by $r$ and using
$\eta=w/r$ gives \eqref{eq:neck}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Compact trace]\label{lem:compact}
The boundary embedding
\[
 \Tr:H^1_{\partial}(\Om)\longrightarrow L^2(\partial\Om)
\]
is compact.  In particular, the natural Steklov spectrum of $\Om$ is
discrete.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We first work on the dense piecewise-smooth core.  The bottom edge of $Q$ is
part of $\partial\Om$.  Poincar\'e's inequality anchored on that edge and the
ordinary trace theorem on $Q$ imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:base-control}
 \|u|_Q\|_{H^1(Q)}^2+
 \|u|_{\partial Q}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial Q)}^2
 \le C\|u\|_{H^1_{\partial}(\Om)}^2.
\end{equation}
Apply Lemmas \ref{lem:critical} and \ref{lem:neck} to every attachment with
$j\ge J$.  Since the $B_j$ are disjoint,
\begin{align}
 \sum_{j\ge J}\int_{\Gamma_j}|u|^2\dd s
 &\le C\sum_{j\ge J}\frac{r_j}{w_j}
       \int_{B_j}|u|^2\dd s
   +C\sum_{j\ge J}\frac{r_j^2}{w_j}
       \int_{A_j}|\nabla u|^2\dd x \notag\\
 &\le C\left(\sum_{j\ge J}r_j\log(e/w_j)
       +\sup_{j\ge J}\frac{r_j^2}{w_j}\right)
       \|u\|_{H^1_{\partial}(\Om)}^2.\label{eq:tail}
\end{align}
For the parameters \eqref{eq:parameters},
\[
 \sum_{j\ge J}r_j\log(e/w_j)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \sup_{j\ge J}\frac{r_j^2}{w_j}
 =\frac1{\log(J+1)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the $L^2$ trace outside any finite collection of attachments has
uniformly vanishing operator norm.

It remains to combine this with local compactness.  A sequence bounded in
$H^1_{\partial}(\Om)$ is bounded in $H^1(Q)$ by
\eqref{eq:base-control}.  It is also bounded in $H^1(A_j)$ for each fixed
$j$, by the same Poincar\'e argument anchored on any fixed external side of
$A_j$ (whose $L^2$ trace is part of \eqref{eq:bdy-norm}).
The ordinary compact trace theorem on these finitely many polygonal pieces
therefore gives a subsequence whose traces converge on the base boundary and
on $\Gamma_3,\ldots,\Gamma_{J-1}$.  Estimate \eqref{eq:tail} makes the
remaining trace uniformly small.  Hence this subsequence is Cauchy in
$L^2(\partial\Om)$.

All estimates are stable under the norm \eqref{eq:bdy-norm}, so they extend
from the dense core to $H^1_{\partial}(\Om)$.  This proves compactness.  The
standard closed-form construction, equivalently the variational setup
\eqref{eq:minmax} used in \cite{KLP}, then gives compact resolvent and a
discrete Steklov spectrum.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The domain $\Om$ in \eqref{eq:domain} has discrete Steklov spectrum, but its
counting function satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limsup}
 \limsup_{\sigma\to\infty}\frac{N_{\Om}(\sigma)}{\sigma}=\infty.
\end{equation}
In particular, the Weyl law \eqref{eq:weyl} fails.  This answers Remark 4.3
of \cite{KLP} affirmatively.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Discreteness follows from Lemma \ref{lem:compact}.  For $j\ge3$, define
$u_j$ to be zero on $Q$ and on every other attachment, to increase linearly
from $0$ to $1$ along the vertical coordinate of $P_j$, and to equal $1$ on
$R_j$.  These continuous piecewise-affine functions belong to the natural
boundary Sobolev space (or may be approximated there by smooth functions).
Their Dirichlet energies are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \int_\Om|\nabla u_j|^2\dd x
 =w_jr_j\frac1{r_j^2}
 =\frac{w_j}{r_j}=r_j\log(j+1).
\end{equation}
The external boundary of the room has length $4r_j-w_j$ and carries value
$1$.  The two passage sides contribute an additional
$2\int_0^{r_j}(t/r_j)^2\dd t=2r_j/3$.  In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass}
 \int_{\partial\Om}|u_j|^2\dd s\ge 3r_j,
 \qquad
 \frac{\int_\Om|\nabla u_j|^2}
 {\int_{\partial\Om}|u_j|^2}\le\log(j+1).
\end{equation}

The functions have disjoint supports, so both the energy and boundary
quadratic forms are diagonal on their span.  The $N$-dimensional space
$E_N=\operatorname{span}\{u_3,\ldots,u_{N+2}\}$ therefore satisfies
\[
 \sup_{0\ne u\in E_N}
 \frac{\int_\Om|\nabla u|^2}
 {\int_{\partial\Om}|u|^2}
 \le\log(N+3).
\]
The min--max formula \eqref{eq:minmax} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eigenbound}
 \sigma_{N-1}(\Om)\le\log(N+3).
\end{equation}
Consequently, for $\tau_N=2\log(N+3)$ and all sufficiently large $N$,
\[
 N_{\Om}(\tau_N)\ge N,
 \qquad
 \frac{N_{\Om}(\tau_N)}{\tau_N}
 \ge\frac{N}{2\log(N+3)}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:limsup}.  Since $0<|\partial\Om|<\infty$, it contradicts
the finite limit $|\partial\Om|/\pi$ required by \eqref{eq:weyl}.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification notes and limitations}

The construction satisfies every hypothesis in the source question:
$\Om$ is a genuine bounded planar domain, its boundary is a finite-length
Jordan curve, and its natural Steklov resolvent is compact by
Lemma \ref{lem:compact}.  The exact Weyl conclusion fails by the quantitative
lower bound \eqref{eq:limsup}; the failure is stronger than a wrong leading
constant.

No computational assertion or unproved lemma enters the proof.  The two
review-sensitive points are the uniform scaling in Lemma \ref{lem:neck} and
the extension of the tail estimate to the completed boundary Sobolev space.
Both are written out explicitly.  The packet does not claim Weyl failure for
ordinary $H^1(\Om)$ under an alternative trace convention; it uses precisely
the natural rough-domain variational space selected in the source paper.
