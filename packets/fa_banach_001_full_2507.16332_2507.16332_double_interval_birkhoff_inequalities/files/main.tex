\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.94\textwidth}{\textbf{Result.}
When both the integrand $F=[f_-,f_+]$ and set function
$M=[\nu_-,\nu_+]$ are interval-valued, their Birkhoff weak integral factors
exactly into the two scalar endpoint integrals.  Consequently Hölder
($p,q>1$), Minkowski ($p\ge1$), and reverse Minkowski ($0<p<1$) hold
componentwise in the weak interval order.  For reverse Hölder, the natural
interval $q$-gauge fails even on one atom: at $p=1/2$ the desired inequality
would require $[1,4]\succeq[1/4,16]$.  There is nevertheless an unconditional
sharp diagonal-hull form.  If $\nu_+\le C\nu_-$, the natural form is restored
with the optimal factor $C^{1/q}$, and no positive width-independent factor
exists.}}
\end{center}

\section{The source question}

Croitoru, Iosif, Sambucini, and Zampogni \cite{CISZ} prove scalar Birkhoff
weak Hölder and Minkowski inequalities for non-additive set functions, along
with their reverses.  They extend the results separately to interval-valued
integrands and to interval-valued set functions.  Their conclusion asks for
the remaining case where \emph{both} objects are interval-valued:

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.91\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\caption{The open problem in the conclusion of arXiv:2507.16332.}
\end{figure}

The same authors had previously treated an analogous doubly interval-valued
Riemann--Lebesgue setting \cite{CGIS}.  The question here is specifically for
the Birkhoff weak integral and the scalar theorems proved in \cite{CISZ}.

\section{Setup and proof intuition}

Let $\I$ denote the nonempty compact intervals in $\R_+$.  Use Minkowski
addition and multiplication,
\[
 [a,b]\oplus[c,d]=[a+c,b+d],\qquad
 [a,b]\otimes[c,d]=[ac,bd],
\]
and the weak order $[a,b]\preceq[c,d]$ iff $a\le c$ and $b\le d$.
Let
\[
 M(A)=[\nu_-(A),\nu_+(A)],\qquad \nu_-(A)\le\nu_+(A),
\tag{1}\label{eq:M}
\]
where each endpoint belongs to the source class
$\mathcal M_{cs}(\mathcal E)$: it is nonnegative, subadditive, continuous
from below, and $\mathcal E$-integrable in the source's sense.

For $F(t)=[f_-(t),f_+(t)]\in\I$, define its doubly interval-valued Birkhoff
weak integral by convergence in Hausdorff distance of the tagged sums
\[
 \bigoplus_{k=1}^n F(t_k)\otimes M(B_k).
\tag{2}\label{eq:sums}
\]
The proof idea is visible before taking a limit: every sum in
\eqref{eq:sums} is exactly
\[
 \left[
  \sum_{k=1}^n f_-(t_k)\nu_-(B_k),
  \sum_{k=1}^n f_+(t_k)\nu_+(B_k)
 \right].
\tag{3}\label{eq:finite-factor}
\]
Hausdorff convergence of intervals is coordinatewise convergence.  Thus the
whole problem splits into the two scalar channels $(f_-,\nu_-)$ and
$(f_+,\nu_+)$.  Positive powers preserve endpoints, so the standard Hölder
and both Minkowski regimes follow immediately.  Negative powers reverse
endpoints; that single reversal is exactly why natural reverse Hölder fails.

\section{Exact endpoint factorization}

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint theorem]\label{thm:endpoint}
The integral in \eqref{eq:sums} exists if and only if
$f_-\in\Bw(\nu_-,T)$ and $f_+\in\Bw(\nu_+,T)$.  In that case
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \int_T F\,dM=
 \left[\int_T f_-\,d\nu_-,\ \int_T f_+\,d\nu_+\right].}
\tag{4}\label{eq:endpoint}
\]
The same assertion holds for every endpoint expression below.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Identity \eqref{eq:finite-factor} follows from interval arithmetic.  If the
interval sums converge to $[a,b]$ in Hausdorff distance, their lower and upper
endpoints converge uniformly over all sufficiently fine tagged partitions to
$a$ and $b$, which is precisely scalar Birkhoff weak integrability.  Conversely,
choose the common refinement of scalar control partitions for the two
endpoints and the maximum of their tail indices.  Both endpoint errors are
then below $\varepsilon$, so their maximum, the Hausdorff error, is below
$\varepsilon$.  Passing to the limits in \eqref{eq:finite-factor} gives
\eqref{eq:endpoint}.
\end{proof}

For $r>0$ and all required endpoint powers integrable, put
\[
 \N_{r,M}(F):=
 \left[
  \left(\int f_-^r\,d\nu_-\right)^{1/r},
  \left(\int f_+^r\,d\nu_+\right)^{1/r}
 \right].
\tag{5}\label{eq:gauge}
\]
This is an interval: the corresponding tagged sums for the lower channel are
bounded by those of the upper channel.

\begin{theorem}[Complete positive-power theory]\label{thm:positive}
Let $F=[f_-,f_+]$ and $G=[g_-,g_+]$ be nonnegative and assume every scalar
endpoint expression displayed is Birkhoff weak integrable.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $p,q>1$ and $p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1$, then
\[
 \N_{1,M}(F\otimes G)
 \preceq \N_{p,M}(F)\otimes\N_{q,M}(G).
\tag{6}\label{eq:holder}
\]
\item If $p\ge1$, then
\[
 \N_{p,M}(F\oplus G)
 \preceq \N_{p,M}(F)\oplus\N_{p,M}(G).
\tag{7}\label{eq:minkowski}
\]
\item If $0<p<1$, then the direction reverses:
\[
 \N_{p,M}(F\oplus G)
 \succeq \N_{p,M}(F)\oplus\N_{p,M}(G).
\tag{8}\label{eq:reverse-minkowski}
\]
\end{enumerate}
Here the integrability assumptions are exactly the scalar hypotheses from
\cite{CISZ}, applied once to each endpoint channel.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem \ref{thm:endpoint} turns each side into an interval whose lower
endpoint is the scalar expression for $(f_-,g_-,\nu_-)$ and whose upper
endpoint is the corresponding expression for $(f_+,g_+,\nu_+)$.  Apply the
source's scalar Hölder, Minkowski, or reverse Minkowski theorem in the two
channels.  The two resulting inequalities are exactly the weak interval
orders \eqref{eq:holder}--\eqref{eq:reverse-minkowski}.
\end{proof}

\section{Reverse Hölder: failure, sharp repair, and width factor}

Fix $0<p<1$ and $q=p/(p-1)<0$, and suppose $g_->0$.  Natural interval
functional calculus gives
\[
 G^q=[g_+^q,g_-^q],\qquad
 \N^{\rm nat}_{q,M}(G):=\left(\int G^q\,dM\right)^{1/q}
 =\left[\|g_-\|_{q,\nu_+},\|g_+\|_{q,\nu_-}\right],
\tag{9}\label{eq:natural-q}
\]
where $\|h\|_{r,\nu}=(\int h^r\,d\nu)^{1/r}$.  The crossed measures in
\eqref{eq:natural-q} are forced by the two order reversals.

\begin{proposition}[The natural reverse fails maximally]\label{prop:failure}
In general,
\[
 \N_{1,M}(F\otimes G)\succeq
 \N_{p,M}(F)\otimes\N^{\rm nat}_{q,M}(G)
\tag{10}\label{eq:false}
\]
is false, even for additive endpoint measures concentrated at one atom and
constant singleton intervals $F=G=[1,1]$.  No positive universal constant can
be inserted before the right side of \eqref{eq:false}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On $T=\mathbb N$, concentrate $\nu_-$ and $\nu_+$ at $1$ with masses
$a$ and $b$, where $0<a<b$.  Constant integrands make every tagged sum exact.
The two sides of \eqref{eq:false} are
\[
 [a,b],\qquad
 [a^{1/p}b^{1/q},\ b^{1/p}a^{1/q}].
\tag{11}\label{eq:one-atom}
\]
For $p=1/2,q=-1,a=1,b=4$, these are $[1,4]$ and $[1/4,16]$, so the upper
endpoint inequality fails.  More generally, the ratio of the left upper
endpoint to the right upper endpoint is $(b/a)^{1/q}\to0$ as $b/a\to\infty$.
This excludes every positive width-independent factor.
\end{proof}

The failure has a sharp unconditional repair.  For two numbers let
$\Hull(x,y)=[\min(x,y),\max(x,y)]$, and define
\[
 \mathcal D_{p,q}(F,G;M)=\Hull\!\left(
 \|f_-\|_{p,\nu_-}\|g_-\|_{q,\nu_-},
 \|f_+\|_{p,\nu_+}\|g_+\|_{q,\nu_+}
 \right).
\tag{12}\label{eq:diagonal}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Sharp reverse Hölder replacements]\label{thm:reverse}
Under the scalar reverse-Hölder integrability hypotheses,
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \N_{1,M}(F\otimes G)\succeq\mathcal D_{p,q}(F,G;M).}
\tag{13}\label{eq:hull}
\]
This is exact on one-atom constant examples.  Moreover, if for some $C\ge1$
\[
                 \nu_-\le\nu_+\le C\nu_-
\tag{14}\label{eq:width}
\]
pointwise on $\mathcal E$, then the natural gauge satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \N_{1,M}(F\otimes G)\succeq
 C^{1/q}\bigl(\N_{p,M}(F)\otimes\N^{\rm nat}_{q,M}(G)\bigr).}
\tag{15}\label{eq:factor}
\]
The factor $C^{1/q}$ is optimal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Scalar reverse Hölder in the two matching channels gives
\[
 L:=\int f_-g_-\,d\nu_-\ge R_-,\qquad
 U:=\int f_+g_+\,d\nu_+\ge R_+,
\]
where $R_-$ and $R_+$ are the two numbers in \eqref{eq:diagonal}.  Since
$L\le U$, we have $L\ge\min(R_-,R_+)$ and
$U\ge\max(R_-,R_+)$, proving \eqref{eq:hull}.

For \eqref{eq:factor}, positive tagged sums and \eqref{eq:width} imply
$\int h\,d\nu_+\le C\int h\,d\nu_-$ for every relevant $h\ge0$.  As
$1/q<0$,
\[
 \|g_+\|_{q,\nu_+}\ge C^{1/q}\|g_+\|_{q,\nu_-},
 \qquad
 \|g_-\|_{q,\nu_-}\ge\|g_-\|_{q,\nu_+}.
\]
Insert the first estimate into the upper-channel scalar reverse Hölder
inequality.  The second estimate proves the lower endpoint inequality even
with factor $1$, hence also with $C^{1/q}\le1$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:factor}.  In \eqref{eq:one-atom}, take $b=Ca$; the upper endpoints
in \eqref{eq:factor} are equal, proving optimality.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, novelty, and scope}

The included script checked 2,000 random finite-atomic double-interval models
for \eqref{eq:holder}, both Minkowski directions, and the diagonal-hull reverse
Hölder inequality.  It also checks the exact $[1,4]$ versus $[1/4,16]$
obstruction and equality for the factor $C^{1/q}=1/4$.  These are sanity
checks; all proofs above are endpoint identities plus the source's scalar
theorems.

A bounded novelty search on 17 August 2026 covered the run's cheap indexes,
the exact source title and question, and double interval-valued Birkhoff,
Hölder, and Minkowski combinations.  The closest prior result is the
Riemann--Lebesgue theory in \cite{CGIS}, which treats the analogous endpoint
calculus for that different integral.  The 2026 published version of
\cite{CISZ} still states the Birkhoff weak problem as open.  No later direct
answer was found.  Novelty is plausible, not certified.

Equations \eqref{eq:holder} and \eqref{eq:minkowski} fully answer the named
positive-exponent question.  Equations \eqref{eq:reverse-minkowski},
\eqref{eq:hull}, and \eqref{eq:factor}, together with Proposition
\ref{prop:failure}, give the complete natural endpoint picture for the reverse
regime.  Human review should audit that the double Birkhoff definition in
\eqref{eq:sums} matches the intended established definition and that the
endpoint set functions satisfy the same $\mathcal M_{cs}$ hypotheses as the
source scalar theorems.
