\section{Question and conclusion}

Let $X$ be a Banach space and $\Omega=X\setminus\{0\}$.  The
quasihyperbolic length and distance are
\[
 \ell_k(\gamma)=\int \frac{\lVert d\gamma\rVert}{\lVert\gamma\rVert},
 \qquad
 k(x,y)=\inf_{\gamma:x\leadsto y}\ell_k(\gamma).
\]
Kl\'en--Rasila--Talponen ask whether there is, in the Banach-space setting, a
positive critical radius below which quasihyperbolic balls in punctured spaces
are convex \cite[PDF p.~16]{KRT}.  Rasila--Talponen later emphasize that their
arbitrarily-small counterexample concerns the distance-ratio metric and that
the analogous assertion for quasihyperbolic balls was only presumed
\cite[PDF p.~11]{RT}.

\section{Proof intuition}

At a fixed face point of the square unit sphere, the most obvious local
counterexample fails: the reachable-set boundary is a concave square-root
curve, hence the small quasihyperbolic ball is convex.  The obstruction instead
appears when the center approaches a corner.  Two short paths can exploit
different dominant coordinates.  Their endpoints remain at distance
$\frac32\delta+\frac98\delta^2+O(\delta^3)$, while every path to their midpoint
must cross the diagonal and pays the larger second-order cost
$\frac32\delta+\frac{81}{64}\delta^2+O(\delta^3)$.

The lower bound is global.  Coordinate sorting is nonexpansive for the infinity
norm, so any path can be folded into one which stays on one side of the
diagonal before a single crossing and on the other side afterwards.  On each
side the dominant coordinate controls both coordinate speeds.  A sharp
one-dimensional envelope estimate then bounds the two pieces of the path.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $X=(\R^2,\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty)$ and
$\Omega=X\setminus\{0\}$. For every $R>0$ there exist $c\in\Omega$ and
$0<r<R$ such that the quasihyperbolic ball
\[
 B_k(c,r)=\{z\in\Omega:k(c,z)<r\}
\]
is not convex.
\end{theorem}

\section{Two elementary path estimates}

We use the following sharp envelope bound.

\begin{lemma}[exponential tent bound]\label{lem:tent}
Suppose $u:[0,T]\to(0,\infty)$ is absolutely continuous,
$|u'|\leq u$ almost everywhere, $u(0)=a$, and $u(T)=b$. Then
\[
 u(t)\leq\min\{ae^t,be^{T-t}\}
\]
and consequently
\[
 \int_0^T u(t)\,dt
 \leq 2e^{T/2}\sqrt{ab}-a-b.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The function $\log u$ is $1$-Lipschitz, giving the two pointwise bounds.
Their graphs meet at
$t_0=(T+\log(b/a))/2$, which lies in $[0,T]$ because necessarily
$T\geq|\log(b/a)|$. Integrating the two exponentials on the corresponding
subintervals gives the displayed formula.
\end{proof}

We also use the standard radial lower bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial}
 k(x,y)\geq\left|\log\frac{\norm{y}}{\norm{x}}\right|.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the norm is absolutely continuous along a rectifiable path and the
absolute derivative of its logarithm is bounded by the quasihyperbolic speed.

\section{The explicit configuration}

Fix a sufficiently small $\delta>0$ and put
\begin{align*}
 c_\delta&=(1,1-\delta),
 &q_0&=1-\frac\delta2,\\
 A_\delta&=\left(1-\frac{3\delta}{2},1-2\delta\right),
 &B_\delta&=\left(1-\frac{3\delta}{2}-\frac{3\delta^2}{4},
                         1-\frac\delta2\right),\\
 M_\delta&=\frac{A_\delta+B_\delta}{2}
 =\left(1-\frac{3\delta}{2}-\frac{3\delta^2}{8},
          1-\frac{5\delta}{4}\right).
\end{align*}
Thus $M_\delta$ is the midpoint of the two proposed in-ball points. Define
\begin{align}
 L_A(\delta)
 &= -\log\left(1-\frac{3\delta}{2}\right),\label{eq:LA}\\
 g_B(\delta)
 &= (B_\delta)_2-(B_\delta)_1
 =\delta+\frac{3\delta^2}{4},\nonumber\\
 L_B(\delta)
 &= -\log q_0+2\log\left(1+\frac{g_B(\delta)}{2q_0}\right),\label{eq:LB}\\
 g_M(\delta)
 &= (M_\delta)_2-(M_\delta)_1
 =\frac\delta4+\frac{3\delta^2}{8},\nonumber\\
 L_M(\delta)
 &= -\log q_0
 +2\log\left(
 \frac{2q_0+g_M(\delta)}
 {2\sqrt{q_0(M_\delta)_2}}
 \right).\label{eq:LM}
\end{align}

\subsection{Upper bound for \texorpdfstring{$A_\delta$}{A-delta}}

Let $T=L_A(\delta)$ and, for $0\leq t\leq T$, set
\[
 x(t)=e^{-t},\qquad
 y(t)=1-\delta-\frac23(1-e^{-t}).
\]
This path joins $c_\delta$ to $A_\delta$.  Moreover $x(t)>y(t)>0$ for
small $\delta$, $\max\{|x'|,|y'|\}=x$, and
$\norm{(x,y)}=x$.  Its quasihyperbolic speed is therefore one, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Aupper}
 k(c_\delta,A_\delta)\leq L_A(\delta).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Upper bound for \texorpdfstring{$B_\delta$}{B-delta}}

First follow
\[
 x(t)=e^{-t},\qquad y(t)=2-\delta-e^{-t},
 \qquad 0\leq t\leq-\log q_0.
\]
This has unit quasihyperbolic speed, stays in $x\geq y$, and ends at
$(q_0,q_0)$.  Set
\[
 T_B=2\log\left(1+\frac{g_B(\delta)}{2q_0}\right)
\]
and define, for $0\leq t\leq T_B$,
\[
 Y(t)=q_0e^{\min\{t,T_B-t\}},
 \qquad
 X(t)=q_0-\int_0^tY(s)\,ds.
\]
Here $Y\geq q_0\geq X$, and
$\max\{|X'|,|Y'|\}=Y=\norm{(X,Y)}$ almost everywhere. Also
\[
 \int_0^{T_B}Y(s)\,ds
 =2q_0(e^{T_B/2}-1)=g_B(\delta),
\]
so the second path ends at $B_\delta$. Concatenation yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bupper}
 k(c_\delta,B_\delta)\leq L_B(\delta).
\end{equation}

\section{A path-independent lower bound for the midpoint}

We prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Mlower}
 k(c_\delta,M_\delta)\geq L_M(\delta).
\end{equation}
For small $\delta$, $L_M(\delta)<1/4$.  A path of quasihyperbolic length
less than $1/4$ starting at $c_\delta$ remains in the positive quadrant:
after quasihyperbolic arclength $t$, its norm is at most $e^t$, hence its
ordinary displacement is at most $te^t<1/2$.  Longer paths already satisfy
\eqref{eq:Mlower}.

Consider therefore a path in the positive quadrant from $c_\delta$, where
$x>y$, to $M_\delta$, where $y>x$. Choose a time at which it meets the
diagonal. Before that time apply the coordinate-sorting map
$(x,y)\mapsto(\max\{x,y\},\min\{x,y\})$; afterwards apply the reverse sorting
map. These maps fix the diagonal, preserve the infinity norm, and are
$1$-Lipschitz. Thus the folded path has no greater quasihyperbolic length,
stays in $x\geq y$ before one crossing $(q,q)$, and stays in $y\geq x$
afterwards.

Let its two lengths be $T_1,T_2$.  On the first piece the dominant coordinate
is $x$, so both coordinate speeds are bounded by $x$. Lemma~\ref{lem:tent}
and the net increase of the second coordinate give
\[
 q-(1-\delta)
 \leq 2e^{T_1/2}\sqrt q-1-q.
\]
Consequently, writing $q_0=1-\delta/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T1}
 T_1\geq
 \begin{cases}
  -\log q,&q\leq q_0,\\[2mm]
  2\log\dfrac{2q+\delta}{2\sqrt q},&q\geq q_0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The first line is \eqref{eq:radial}; the second is the preceding envelope
inequality.

Put $y_M=(M_\delta)_2$ and $g=g_M(\delta)=y_M-(M_\delta)_1$. On the second
piece the dominant coordinate is $y$.  The required decrease of the first
coordinate and Lemma~\ref{lem:tent} imply
\[
 q-(M_\delta)_1
 \leq 2e^{T_2/2}\sqrt{qy_M}-q-y_M,
\]
hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T2}
 T_2\geq2\log\frac{2q+g}{2\sqrt{qy_M}}.
\end{equation}

If $q\leq q_0$, adding \eqref{eq:T1} and \eqref{eq:T2} gives
\[
 T_1+T_2
 \geq -\log y_M+2\log\left(1+\frac{g}{2q}\right),
\]
which decreases as $q$ increases and is therefore minimized at $q_0$.
If $q\geq q_0$, the lower bound is
\[
 2\log\frac{(2q+\delta)(2q+g)}{4q\sqrt{y_M}}.
\]
Apart from constants independent of $q$, its argument is
\[
 \frac{(2q+\delta)(2q+g)}q
 =4q+2(\delta+g)+\frac{\delta g}{q}.
\]
Its derivative is $4-\delta g/q^2>0$ for all $q\geq q_0$ when $\delta$ is
small. This branch is likewise minimized at $q_0$. At $q=q_0$ the common
lower bound is exactly \eqref{eq:LM}, proving \eqref{eq:Mlower}.

\section{The second-order separation}

Taylor expansion at $\delta=0$ gives
\begin{align*}
 L_A(\delta)
 &=\frac32\delta+\frac98\delta^2+O(\delta^3),\\
 L_B(\delta)
 &=\frac32\delta+\frac98\delta^2+O(\delta^3),\\
 L_M(\delta)
 &=\frac32\delta+\frac{81}{64}\delta^2+O(\delta^3).
\end{align*}
Since $81/64-9/8=9/64>0$, for every sufficiently small positive $\delta$,
\[
 \max\{L_A(\delta),L_B(\delta)\}<L_M(\delta).
\]
Choose
\[
 r_\delta=\frac{max\{L_A(\delta),L_B(\delta)\}+L_M(\delta)}2.
\]
Equations \eqref{eq:Aupper}, \eqref{eq:Bupper}, and \eqref{eq:Mlower} show
that $A_\delta,B_\delta\in B_k(c_\delta,r_\delta)$ but their midpoint
$M_\delta$ is not in the ball. Finally $r_\delta\to0$, proving
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Scope and literature check}

The counterexample answers the unrestricted Banach-space question. It does
not contradict positive results under Hilbertian, uniformly convex, or
uniformly smooth hypotheses: the infinity norm is deliberately nonsmooth and
not strictly convex.  The related paper \cite{RT} proves an averaging estimate
under power-type-$2$ assumptions but explicitly says it does not obtain a
critical radius for punctured Banach spaces.

A bounded search through 11 August 2026 used the exact question wording,
``critical radius,'' ``punctured Banach space,'' and quasihyperbolic
convexity keywords, together with the citation neighborhood of
arXiv:1007.3197 and arXiv:1104.3745. It found Luiro's later solution of the
Euclidean finite-dimensional convexity conjecture \cite{Luiro}, but no later
answer to the Banach-space question treated here. The present result is
therefore recorded as an agent counterexample, subject to expert verification
and normal novelty review.
