\section*{Status and exact source question}

The status is \textbf{literature\_already\_answered}.  The resolving work is a
2023 preprint, and the exact implication needed by the source can also be checked
directly from the corrected version of Kloeckner's coupling theorem.

In the final paragraph of Example 4.2 of Cioletti--Melo--Ruviaro--Silva,
\emph{On the dimension of the space of harmonic functions on transitive shift
spaces}, arXiv:2009.07209 (published in \emph{Advances in Mathematics} 385
(2021), 107758), the authors consider
\[
 X=(\Sph^{N-1})^{\mathbb N},\qquad
 f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}J(n)\langle x_1,x_{n+1}\rangle,
 \qquad |J(n)|=O(n^{-2-\varepsilon}),\quad \varepsilon>2.
 \tag{2}
\]
They explain that their functional central limit theorem (FCLT) argument would
extend from scalar spins to this uncountable alphabet if one could prove, for
centered observables in the relevant logarithmic Hölder class,
\[
 \|\cL_{\bar f}^{\,n}\varphi\|_\infty\le D n^{1-\varepsilon}.
 \tag{3}
\]
They identify the missing ingredient as an uncountable-alphabet extension of the
optimal-transport method of Kloeckner.

\section*{Direct later answer}

Silva--Mesquita--Matias, \emph{Some Probabilistic Properties of General
Topological Markov Chains}, arXiv:2310.01697 (2023), explicitly cites the 2021
paper and revisits this task.  Its Section 5 first formulates the natural coupling
for general alphabets, then proves polynomial decay for compact metric alphabets,
and finally applies the result to a long-range \(O(N)\) model.  For the canonical
interaction
\[
 f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
      \frac{\langle x_1,x_{n+1}\rangle}{n^{2+\varepsilon}},
\]
the paper obtains (3), constructs a solution of the Poisson equation, and
concludes the FCLT.  Thus this is a separate later-paper answer, not a same-paper
resolution.

The later paper writes the canonical coefficients in its model subsection, while
the original question permits every sequence satisfying the bound in (2).  The
proof below verifies that the same argument covers that full coefficient class.
It also makes explicit two formula-level corrections: a coordinate projection is
not uniformly Lipschitz for the product metric, and the decay estimate is
proportional to an observable norm, not to a potential norm.

\section*{Audited form of the answer}

Endow \(X\) with
\[
 d(x,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}
        \min\{\|x_k-y_k\|,1\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
For \(\gamma>0\), write
\[
 \omega_\gamma(r)=\bigl(\log(r_0/r)\bigr)^{-\gamma}
\]
near zero, extended in the standard way to a modulus of continuity on the
diameter of \(X\).  Denote the corresponding logarithmic Hölder space by
\(C^{\gamma\log}(X)\).

Let \(p\) be the a priori probability on \(\Sph^{N-1}\) and let
\[
 \cL_f u(x)=\int_{\Sph^{N-1}}e^{f(ax)}u(ax)\,dp(a).
\]
The equilibrium measure and normalized potential below are those furnished by
the Ruelle--Perron--Frobenius normalization.

\begin{theorem}
Assume (2).  There are a positive eigenfunction \(h\), an eigenvalue \(\rho>0\),
and the normalized potential
\[
 \bar f=f+\log h-\log h\circ\sigma-\log\rho
\]
such that, for every \(\varphi\in C^{(\varepsilon-1)\log}(X)\) satisfying
\(\int_X\varphi\,d\mu_f=0\),
\[
 \|\cL_{\bar f}^{\,n}\varphi\|_\infty
 \le C n^{-(\varepsilon-1)}
       \|\varphi\|_{(\varepsilon-1)\log},\qquad n\ge1.
 \tag{5}
\]
In particular the Poisson series converges and the FCLT criterion used in
arXiv:2009.07209 applies.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(r=d(x,y)\).  From (4), and from the fact that two sphere points have
distance at most \(2\),
\[
 \|x_k-y_k\|\le 2\min\{1,2^k r\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The inner-product difference is bounded by
\[
 \big|\langle x_1,x_{n+1}\rangle-
          \langle y_1,y_{n+1}\rangle\big|
 \le \|x_1-y_1\|+\|x_{n+1}-y_{n+1}\|.
\]
Using (6), the summability of \((J(n))\), and
\(|J(n)|\le Cn^{-2-\varepsilon}\), we obtain
\[
 |f(x)-f(y)|
 \le C r+C\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2-\varepsilon}
                  \min\{1,2^{n+1}r\}.
 \tag{7}
\]
For \(0<r<1/4\), split the sum at
\(M=\lfloor\log_2(1/r)\rfloor\).  The part \(n\le M\) is at most
\[
 Cr\sum_{n\le M}2^n n^{-2-\varepsilon}
 \le C M^{-2-\varepsilon},
\]
whereas the tail is at most \(CM^{-1-\varepsilon}\).  Thus
\[
 |f(x)-f(y)|\le C\omega_{1+\varepsilon}(r).
 \tag{8}
\]
In particular \(f\in C^{\varepsilon\log}(X)\).  This argument works unchanged
for every coefficient sequence in (2).

The natural synchronous coupling prepends the same random point \(a\) to both
sequences.  It has the exact contraction
\[
 d(ax,ay)=\tfrac12d(x,y).
 \tag{9}
\]
Along a coupled inverse path, (8) and the elementary integral comparison give,
uniformly in its length,
\[
 \sum_{j\ge0}\omega_\varepsilon(2^{-j}r)
 \le C\int_0^\infty
       \bigl(\log(r_0/r)+t\log2\bigr)^{-\varepsilon}\,dt
 \le C'\omega_{\varepsilon-1}(r).
 \tag{10}
\]
Hence \(f\) is flat with respect to \(\omega_{\varepsilon-1}\) and the
synchronous coupling.

Kloeckner's corrected normalization theorem applies to backward transition
kernels on arbitrary compact metric spaces; it is not restricted to finite
fibers.  It gives the positive \(h\), \(\rho\), and a cohomologous normalized
potential \(\bar f\) which remains flat.  The synchronous coupling is Markovian,
non-dilating, and contracts with probability one by (9).  The corrected
stable-decay criterion therefore gives weighted-coupling decay of polynomial
degree \(\varepsilon-1\).  The core Wasserstein contraction theorem, followed by
the standard observable/measure duality, is precisely (5).  Notice that this
duality supplies \(\|\varphi\|_{(\varepsilon-1)\log}\) on the right side.

Finally \(\varepsilon>2\), so
\(\sum_n n^{-(\varepsilon-1)}<\infty\).  Therefore
\[
 v=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\cL_{\bar f}^{\,n}\varphi
\]
converges uniformly and hence in \(L^2(\mu_f)\); telescoping gives the Poisson
equation \((I-\cL_{\bar f})v=\varphi\).  The FCLT theorem invoked in the source
now applies.
\end{proof}

\section*{Scope and verification note}

This packet records a full literature answer for the precise compact-sphere,
long-range question.  It does not claim a new proof of Kloeckner's general
coupling theorem.  The consolidated arXiv version of that theorem must be used:
it incorporates the 2023 corrigendum by strengthening the stability hypothesis.
Condition (9) satisfies the corrected criterion, so the corrigendum creates no
gap here.

The 2023 answer preprint contains two harmless presentational slips.  First, its
proof of logarithmic regularity writes a coordinate estimate without the factor
depending on the coordinate; the split estimate (6)--(8) is the correct
replacement.  Second, one displayed uncountable-alphabet estimate has a
potential norm where the observable norm is required; (5) is the form yielded by
Wasserstein duality.  Neither affects the claimed \(O(N)\) FCLT.
