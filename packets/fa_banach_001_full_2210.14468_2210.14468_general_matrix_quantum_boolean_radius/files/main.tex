\section{Source problem}

Volberg and Zhang study the Pauli expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pauli}
 A=\sum_{\mathbf s\in\{0,1,2,3\}^n}\widehat A_{\mathbf s}
 \sigma_{\mathbf s},
 \qquad
 |\mathbf s|=\#\{j:s_j\ne0\},
\end{equation}
in $M_2(\C)^{\otimes n}$ and define the quantum Boolean radius of a
self-adjoint nonscalar matrix by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radius}
 \sum_{\mathbf s}|\widehat A_{\mathbf s}|\,
 \qBr_n(A)^{|\mathbf s|}=\|A\|.
\end{equation}
Their Theorem 5.5 compares the resulting radii for four classes---all
matrices, homogeneous matrices, exactly $d$-homogeneous matrices, and
degree-at-most-$d$ matrices---with the corresponding real Boolean radii
\cite{VZ}.  Remark 5.4 on page 16 says:
\begin{quote}
``We leave the problem for general $A$ to future study.''
\end{quote}

We answer the literal general-matrix version of those same four classes.
For clarity, scalar matrices are omitted when taking class infima: their
radius is not uniquely specified by \eqref{eq:radius}, and they cannot lower
any of the infima below.

\section{Definitions}

For a nonscalar arbitrary matrix $A$ with expansion \eqref{eq:pauli}, put
\[
 M_A(r)=\sum_{\mathbf s}|\widehat A_{\mathbf s}|r^{|\mathbf s|}.
\]
The normalized trace bound gives
$|\widehat A_{\mathbf0}|\leq\|A\|$, with strict inequality unless $A$ is
scalar.  Hence $M_A(r)=\|A\|$ has a unique positive solution; this is the
general-matrix value $\qBr_n^{\C}(A)$.  Write $\qBr_n^{\C}(\mathcal F)$ for
the infimum over arbitrary matrices in a class $\mathcal F$, and
$\qBr_n^{\sa}(\mathcal F)$ for the source's self-adjoint infimum.

Likewise, for a nonscalar complex-valued function
$f=\sum_{S\subset[n]}\widehat f(S)\chi_S$ on the Boolean cube, let
$\Br_n^{\C}(f)$ solve
\[
 \sum_{S\subset[n]}|\widehat f(S)|\Br_n^{\C}(f)^{|S|}
 =\|f\|_\infty.
\]
Class infima are denoted $\Br_n^{\C}(\mathcal F)$; the source's real-valued
counterparts are denoted $\Br_n^{\R}(\mathcal F)$.

Let $\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\C}$ be the space of matrices whose Pauli support is
exactly in degree $d$, and let $\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\sa}$ be its self-adjoint
part.  We interpret $\homc_+$ as the union of the positive homogeneous
sectors $d=1,\ldots,n$.  This is the nonconstant part of the source class of
homogeneous functions.

\section{Idea of the proof}

The obstruction is phase cancellation across degrees.  A scalar multiple of
the identity and an imaginary Pauli string can form a unitary matrix.  The
operator norm sees their orthogonal phases quadratically, while the Boolean
majorant in \eqref{eq:radius} adds their absolute coefficients.  Moving the
scalar coefficient toward one forces the radius to zero.  This immediately
settles the two source classes that permit a mixture of degrees zero and one.

That mechanism is unavailable inside a fixed positive degree.  There the
radius is simply the reciprocal $d$th root of an $\ell_1$ coefficient-to-norm
constant.  Rotating an arbitrary matrix by a scalar phase and taking its real
part gives a self-adjoint matrix without increasing norm.  Averaging over the
phase preserves exactly $2/\pi$ of every coefficient's modulus.  Thus the
arbitrary complex coefficient constant is at most $\pi/2$ times its
self-adjoint counterpart.  This proves that every homogeneous sector, and
therefore their union, retains the source's asymptotic order.

Finally, the source's matrix-to-Boolean reduction applies to arbitrary
matrices.  It produces a complex-valued function.  With the complex Boolean
radius, its coefficient identity proves the factor-three comparison verbatim.

\section{Main theorem}

\begin{theorem}[General-matrix quantum Boolean radii]\label{thm:main}
Let $n\geq1$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item The class of all arbitrary matrices satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\qBr_n^{\C}(\all)=0.}
\]
\item For every $1\leq d\leq n$, the arbitrary degree-at-most-$d$ class
satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\qBr_n^{\C}(\leq d)=0.}
\]
\item For every $1\leq d\leq n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixeddegree}
 \boxed{
 \left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{1/d}\qBr_n^{\sa}(=d)
 \ \leq\ \qBr_n^{\C}(=d)
 \ \leq\ \qBr_n^{\sa}(=d).}
\end{equation}
\item For the union of positive homogeneous degrees,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:homogeneous}
 \boxed{
 \frac{2}{\pi}\qBr_n^{\sa}(\homc_+)
 \ \leq\ \qBr_n^{\C}(\homc_+)
 \ \leq\ \qBr_n^{\sa}(\homc_+).}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $P=\sigma_1\otimes I^{\otimes(n-1)}$.  Thus $P=P^*$,
$P^2=I$, and $P$ has Pauli degree one.  For $0<a<1$, set
\[
 b=(1-a^2)^{1/2},\qquad A_a=aI+ibP.
\]
The two terms commute and
\[
 A_a^*A_a=(aI-ibP)(aI+ibP)=(a^2+b^2)I=I,
\]
so $A_a$ is unitary and $\|A_a\|=1$.  Its only nonzero Pauli
coefficients are $a$ in degree zero and $ib$ in degree one.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counterradius}
 \qBr_n^{\C}(A_a)=\frac{1-a}{b}
 =\sqrt{\frac{1-a}{1+a}}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(a\uparrow1).
\end{equation}
Every $A_a$ belongs both to the all-matrix class and to every
degree-at-most-$d$ class with $d\geq1$.  Since radii are nonnegative,
\eqref{eq:counterradius} proves (i) and (ii).

Fix a positive degree $d$.  For $A\in\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\C}$, write
\[
 L_d(A)=\sum_{|\mathbf s|=d}|\widehat A_{\mathbf s}|.
\]
Homogeneity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:homradius}
 \qBr_n^{\C}(A)=\left(\frac{\|A\|}{L_d(A)}\right)^{1/d}.
\end{equation}
Define the finite coefficient constants
\[
 K_{n,d}^{\C}=\sup_{0\ne A\in\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\C}}
 \frac{L_d(A)}{\|A\|},
 \qquad
 K_{n,d}^{\sa}=\sup_{0\ne A\in\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\sa}}
 \frac{L_d(A)}{\|A\|}.
\]
The inclusion of the self-adjoint space gives
$K_{n,d}^{\sa}\leq K_{n,d}^{\C}$.

For arbitrary $A\in\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\C}$ and $\theta\in[0,2\pi]$, let
\[
 H_\theta=\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}A)
 =\frac{e^{-i\theta}A+e^{i\theta}A^*}{2}.
\]
Each Pauli string is self-adjoint, so $H_\theta$ lies in
$\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\sa}$ and has coefficient
$\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}\widehat A_{\mathbf s})$ at $\mathbf s$.
Moreover, $\|H_\theta\|\leq\|A\|$.  The scalar identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phaseaverage}
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
 \left|\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}z)\right|\,d\theta
 =\frac2\pi|z|
\end{equation}
therefore yields
\begin{align*}
 \frac2\pi L_d(A)
 &=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}L_d(H_\theta)\,d\theta\\
 &\leq K_{n,d}^{\sa}\|A\|.
\end{align*}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Kcompare}
 K_{n,d}^{\sa}\leq K_{n,d}^{\C}
 \leq\frac\pi2K_{n,d}^{\sa}.
\end{equation}
Taking reciprocal $d$th roots in \eqref{eq:Kcompare}, using
\eqref{eq:homradius}, proves (iii).

Finally, the radius of the positive homogeneous union is the infimum of the
exact-degree radii over $1\leq d\leq n$.  Since
$(2/\pi)^{1/d}\geq2/\pi$, taking infima in (iii) proves (iv).
\end{proof}

\section{The complex Boolean cube and the full comparison}

The same phase obstruction and phase averaging describe the complex-valued
analogue on the classical Boolean cube.

\begin{theorem}[Complex Boolean radii]\label{thm:classicalcomplex}
For every $n\geq1$ and $1\leq d\leq n$,
\[
 \Br_n^{\C}(\all)=0,
 \qquad
 \Br_n^{\C}(\leq d)=0,
\]
while
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classicalexact}
 \left(\frac2\pi\right)^{1/d}\Br_n^{\R}(=d)
 \leq\Br_n^{\C}(=d)
 \leq\Br_n^{\R}(=d)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classicalhom}
 \frac2\pi\Br_n^{\R}(\homc_+)
 \leq\Br_n^{\C}(\homc_+)
 \leq\Br_n^{\R}(\homc_+).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $0<a<1$ and $b=(1-a^2)^{1/2}$, the function
$f_a(x)=a+ibx_1$ has $\|f_a\|_\infty=1$ and
\[
 \Br_n^{\C}(f_a)=\frac{1-a}{b}
 =\sqrt{\frac{1-a}{1+a}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
This proves both zero-radius statements.  In exact degree $d$, define the
$\ell_1$ Fourier coefficient-to-supremum-norm constants over complex- and
real-valued functions.  For complex $f$, the real-valued function
$g_\theta=\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}f)$ satisfies
$\|g_\theta\|_\infty\leq\|f\|_\infty$.  Applying
\eqref{eq:phaseaverage} coefficientwise gives the same comparison
$K_{n,d}^{\R}\leq K_{n,d}^{\C}\leq(\pi/2)K_{n,d}^{\R}$ as in
\eqref{eq:Kcompare}.  Reciprocal $d$th roots prove
\eqref{eq:classicalexact}; taking the infimum over positive degrees proves
\eqref{eq:classicalhom}.
\end{proof}

The next theorem is the exact arbitrary-matrix replacement for Theorem 5.5
of the source.

\begin{theorem}[Complex quantum-classical comparison]\label{thm:fullcomparison}
Let $\mathcal F$ be any of the four classes $\all$, $\homc_+$, $(=d)$, or
$(\leq d)$, with arbitrary matrices on the quantum side and complex-valued
functions on the classical side.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fullcomparison}
 \boxed{
 \qBr_n^{\C}(\mathcal F_n^q)
 \leq\Br_n^{\C}(\mathcal F_n),
 \qquad
 \Br_{3n}^{\C}(\mathcal F_{3n})
 \leq3\qBr_n^{\C}(\mathcal F_n^q).}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For complex-valued
$f(x)=\sum_{S\subset[n]}a_S\chi_S(x)$, the diagonal matrix
\[
 A_f=\sum_{S\subset[n]}a_S\prod_{j\in S}\sigma_3^{(j)}
\]
has the values $f(x)$ as its diagonal entries.  Hence
$\|A_f\|=\|f\|_\infty$, its Pauli coefficients are the $a_S$, and it
preserves support classes and radius.  Taking infima proves the first
inequality.

Conversely, Proposition 2.2 of \cite{VZ} applies to every arbitrary matrix
$A$ and produces a complex-valued function $f_A$ on $\{-1,1\}^{3n}$ with
$\|f_A\|_\infty\leq\|A\|$, the same degree type, and coefficients
\[
 \widehat f_A(q(\mathbf s))=3^{-|\mathbf s|}\widehat A_{\mathbf s},
 \qquad
 \widehat f_A(S)=0\quad(S\notin\operatorname{im}q).
\]
At $r=3\qBr_n^{\C}(A)$, the coefficient majorant of $f_A$ is exactly
$\|A\|$, which is at least $\|f_A\|_\infty$.  Monotonicity gives
$\Br_{3n}^{\C}(f_A)\leq3\qBr_n^{\C}(A)$.  Taking infima proves the second
inequality.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences for the source theorem}

Let $\Br_n^{\R}(=d)$ and $\Br_n^{\R}(\homc)$ denote the real Boolean radii
used by Volberg and Zhang.  Their Theorem 5.5 says, in the two homogeneous
classes,
\[
 \frac13\Br_{3n}^{\R}(\mathcal F_{3n})
 \leq\qBr_n^{\sa}(\mathcal F_n^q)
 \leq\Br_n^{\R}(\mathcal F_n).
\]

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:source}
For arbitrary matrices and $1\leq d\leq n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sourceexact}
 \frac13\left(\frac2\pi\right)^{1/d}\Br_{3n}^{\R}(=d)
 \leq\qBr_n^{\C}(=d)
 \leq\Br_n^{\R}(=d),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sourcehom}
 \frac2{3\pi}\Br_{3n}^{\R}(\homc)
 \leq\qBr_n^{\C}(\homc_+)
 \leq\Br_n^{\R}(\homc).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\[
 \qBr_n^{\C}(\homc_+)\asymp\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.
\]
For each fixed $d$, the exactly $d$-homogeneous arbitrary-matrix radius has
the same order in $n$ as its real and self-adjoint counterparts.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:classicalcomplex} and
\ref{thm:fullcomparison}.  The final orders follow from Theorem 5.2 of
\cite{VZ}.
\end{proof}

Thus there is no positive analogue of the source comparison for the literal
general-matrix versions of $\all$ and $(\leq d)$: the left-hand quantum
radii are exactly zero, while their real/self-adjoint counterparts are
positive.  Equations \eqref{eq:sourceexact} and \eqref{eq:sourcehom} give the
surviving general-matrix program.
