\section{Source problem}

The source is V.~Benci, S.~Nardulli, L.~E.~Osorio Acevedo and P.~Piccione,
\emph{Lusternik--Schnirelman and Morse Theory for the Van der
Waals--Cahn--Hilliard equation with volume constraint}, arXiv:2007.07024,
Nonlinear Analysis 220 (2022), Paper 112851 \cite{source}.  Its corrected
photography argument appears in the subsequent corrigendum
\cite{corrigendum}.

For a closed connected Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$, the constrained critical
point equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pde}
 -\varepsilon\Delta_g u+\frac1\varepsilon W'(u)=\lambda,
 \qquad \int_Mu\,dv_g=V.
\end{equation}
The source proves the full multiplicity theorem under a subcritical upper
growth condition on $W'$.  If that upper-growth condition is replaced by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tailconvex}
 W''(s)\ge c_0>0\qquad (|s|\ge t_1>1),
\end{equation}
Theorem 5.9 obtains only the low-energy counts $\cat(M)$ and $P_1(M)$.
Immediately before that theorem, on PDF page 28, the authors conjecture that
the result remains true for high-energy solutions.  The intended missing
conclusions are those of their main theorem: $\cat(M)+1$, and
$2P_1(M)-1$ in the nondegenerate case.

\section{Proof intuition}

The source truncates a general potential to a subcritical one, but transfers
only critical points whose energy is bounded: an energy estimate controls the
Lagrange multiplier, after which the maximum principle keeps the solution in
the unchanged part of the potential.  The multiplier can instead be removed
by comparing the global maximum and global minimum simultaneously.

Indeed, at a maximum and a minimum of a solution of \eqref{eq:pde},
\[
 W'(\max u)\le \varepsilon\lambda\le W'(\min u).
\]
The fixed mean $a=V/\vol_g(M)$ lies between these extrema.  Since the derivative
of the potential tends to $-\infty$ on the left and $+\infty$ on the right,
one can place two fixed tail barriers $L<a<U$.  A subcritical truncation is
chosen to equal $W$ on $[L,U]$, with derivative below every value attainable
on $[a,\infty)$ to the left of $L$, and above every value attainable on
$(-\infty,a]$ to the right of $U$.  The displayed extrema inequality then
forbids every truncated solution from leaving $[L,U]$, regardless of its
energy.  Thus the corrected subcritical multiplicity theorem transfers in its
entirety.

\section{Statement}

We use the source's structural double-well assumptions (its condition (2.2)
and the local barrier condition near the well at $1$).  Let
$P_1(M)=\sum_k\beta_k(M)$, with coefficients in a fixed field.

\begin{theorem}[High-energy extension without subcritical upper growth]
\label{thm:main}
Let $(M,g)$ be a closed connected Riemannian manifold and let
$W\in C^2(\mathbb R,[0,\infty))$ satisfy the source's double-well assumptions
and \eqref{eq:tailconvex}.  Then there is $V^*>0$ such that, for each
$V\in(0,V^*)$, there is $\varepsilon^*>0$ for which every
$\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^*)$ gives at least
\[
 \cat(M)+1
\]
distinct solutions of \eqref{eq:pde}.  If all solutions for the given
$(V,\varepsilon)$ are nondegenerate constrained critical points, then there
are at least
\[
 2P_1(M)-1
\]
distinct solutions.
\end{theorem}

This restores exactly the low- plus high-energy conclusions of the corrected
subcritical theorem under the general tail condition used in source
Theorem 5.9.

\section{Proof}

We isolate the one-dimensional truncation and the energy-free range estimate.

\begin{lemma}[A fixed admissible truncation]\label{lem:trunc}
Fix $a\in\mathbb R$.  Under \eqref{eq:tailconvex}, there exist
$L<a<U$ and a nonnegative $C^2$ double-well potential $\widehat W$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $L<0<1<U$ and $\widehat W=W$ on $[L,U]$;
 \item $\widehat W$ satisfies the subcritical upper-growth and tail-growth
 assumptions of the corrected main theorem in \cite{source,corrigendum};
 \item if
 \[
  A:=\sup_{s\le a}W'(s),\qquad B:=\inf_{s\ge a}W'(s),
 \]
 then
 \[
  \widehat W'(s)<B\quad(s<L),\qquad
  \widehat W'(s)>A\quad(s>U).
 \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Condition \eqref{eq:tailconvex} implies
$W'(s)\to-\infty$ as $s\to-\infty$ and
$W'(s)\to+\infty$ as $s\to+\infty$.  Hence $A<\infty$, $B>-\infty$, and
$B\le W'(a)\le A$.  Choose $L<\min\{0,a,-t_1\}$ and
$U>\max\{1,a,t_1\}$ so that
\[
 W'(L)<B,qquad W'(U)>A.
\]
On the left of $L$ and the right of $U$, continue the endpoint two-jets by
strictly convex $C^2$ tails.  The left derivative is chosen increasing and
strictly below $B$, while the right derivative is increasing and strictly
above $A$.  After a compact interpolation, the tails may be taken to have
power growth $|s|^q$, where $2<q<2^*=2\dim(M)/(\dim(M)-2)$ when
$\dim(M)\ge3$, and any finite $q>2$ in dimensions one and two.  The
continuation grows away from the positive values $W(L)$ and $W(U)$, so it
creates no new zero or critical well.  This is the standard convex tail
truncation used in Section 5 of \cite{source}, with the two strict derivative
barriers retained.  It has all the asserted properties.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[All truncated critical points stay in the common core]
\label{lem:range}
Put $a=V/\vol_g(M)$ and choose $L,U,\widehat W$ from Lemma
\ref{lem:trunc}.  Every solution $(u,\lambda)$ of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truncated}
 -\varepsilon\Delta_g u+\frac1\varepsilon\widehat W'(u)=\lambda,
 \qquad \int_Mu\,dv_g=V,
\end{equation}
satisfies $L\le u\le U$ on $M$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The subcritical growth of $\widehat W$ gives the standard elliptic regularity
needed to use the maximum principle (alternatively, the same inequalities
follow by testing with positive parts).  Let
\[
 m=\min_Mu,\qquad R=\max_Mu.
\]
At a maximum, $\Delta_g u\le0$, whereas at a minimum
$\Delta_g u\ge0$.  Equation \eqref{eq:truncated} therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extrema}
 \widehat W'(R)\le\varepsilon\lambda\le\widehat W'(m).
\end{equation}
The mean constraint gives $m\le a\le R$.

Suppose first that $R>U$.  By Lemma \ref{lem:trunc},
$\widehat W'(R)>A$.  Since $m\le a$, either $m\in[L,a]$, when
$\widehat W'(m)=W'(m)\le A$, or $m<L$, when
$\widehat W'(m)<B\le A$.  In both cases
$\widehat W'(R)>\widehat W'(m)$, contradicting \eqref{eq:extrema}.
Thus $R\le U$.

If $m<L$, then $\widehat W'(m)<B$.  Since $R\ge a$, either
$R\in[a,U]$, when $\widehat W'(R)=W'(R)\ge B$, or $R>U$, when
$\widehat W'(R)>A\ge B$.  Again
$\widehat W'(R)>\widehat W'(m)$, contradicting \eqref{eq:extrema}.
Thus $m\ge L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}]
Fix $V$ in the small-volume range and set $a=V/\vol_g(M)$.  Let
$\widehat W$ be the fixed subcritical potential supplied by Lemma
\ref{lem:trunc}.  Apply the corrected subcritical multiplicity theorem
\cite[Theorem 2.1]{corrigendum} to $\widehat W$.  For sufficiently small
$\varepsilon$, it produces at least $\cat(M)+1$ distinct solutions of
\eqref{eq:truncated}.

Lemma \ref{lem:range} places the range of every one of these solutions in
$[L,U]$, where $\widehat W=W$.  Hence each pair also solves the original
equation \eqref{eq:pde}.  This proves the first count.  Notice that no energy
bound has entered: the high-energy critical point transfers exactly as the
low-energy ones do.

For the second count, assume all solutions of the original problem for the
chosen $(V,\varepsilon)$ are nondegenerate.  Every solution of the truncated
problem is an original solution by Lemma \ref{lem:range}.  Moreover
$\widehat W''(u(x))=W''(u(x))$ at every point, including a possible endpoint
value because the extension is $C^2$.  Thus the constrained Hessians for the
two functionals coincide at each truncated solution, so all truncated
solutions are nondegenerate.  The Morse part of the corrected subcritical
theorem gives at least $2P_1(M)-1$ of them, and all transfer to the original
problem.

The small-volume constants in the corrected photography construction depend
on the geometry and on the transition data on $[0,1]$.  These data are
unchanged by the truncation; the fixed truncation may affect only the allowed
$\varepsilon^*$, which is permitted to depend on $V,M,g,W$.  This completes
the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and limitations}

The proof uses only two external inputs from the source: the corrected
subcritical multiplicity theorem and the standard existence of a subcritical
convex tail truncation.  The new step is Lemma \ref{lem:range}.  Its signs have
been checked directly at both extrema, and both possible locations of the
opposite extremum (inside or outside the common core) are included explicitly.
No numerical computation is involved.

The conclusion is limited to the source's tail-convex class
\eqref{eq:tailconvex}; it does not assert a result for a potential whose
derivative fails to tend to the corresponding infinities.  It also inherits
the small-volume and small-$\varepsilon$ regime of the corrected photography
theorem.

\section{Novelty check}

Before promotion, the four cheap run indexes were searched for the arXiv id,
title, and the core high-energy/subcritical-growth terms.  Bounded external
searches through 11 August 2026 used the exact conjecture sentence, the title,
the terms ``high energy solutions'', ``dropping the subcritical growth
condition'', and the extrema inequality.  They found the original article and
its corrigendum, but no later solution.  The 2026 survey by Andrade, Nardulli
and Ponciano \cite{survey} still presents the closed scalar theorem for a
subcritical potential and does not record this extension.  This is evidence,
not an exhaustive bibliographic guarantee.

\section{Human-review recommendation}

Review Lemma \ref{lem:range} first; it is the decisive and elementary step.
Then check that the one-dimensional continuation in Lemma \ref{lem:trunc} is
chosen within the exact admissible class used by the corrected theorem.  If
both checks pass, the packet gives a full resolution of the Section 5
high-energy conjecture, including its Morse-theoretic count.
