\section*{Source conjecture}

Let $\mathcal D_n$ be the set of real $n\times n$ matrices with entries in
$[0,1]$.  An S-matrix is a $\{0,1\}$-matrix $A$ satisfying
\[
 A^T A=\frac{n+1}{4}(I+\one\one^T).
\]
Drnov\v{s}ek \cite{Drnovsek} restates the Sloane--Harwit conjecture on PDF
page 2 as follows: every nonsingular $A\in\mathcal D_n$ satisfies
\[
 \|A^{-1}\|_F\ge \frac{2n}{n+1},
\]
with equality precisely for S-matrices.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The source proves the conjecture for odd $n$, proves the sharper bound
$\|A^{-1}\|_F\ge\sqrt2$ for $n=2$, and obtains a weaker bound for even
$n\ge4$.  Frankel and Urschel \cite{FrankelUrschel} later prove the conjecture
for every $n\ge1000$.  We use their structural Lemma 3.2 verbatim and replace
their fixed-parameter final step.

\section*{Result}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
If $A\in\mathcal D_n$ is nonsingular and $n\ge158$, then
\[
 \|A^{-1}\|_F\ge\frac{2n}{n+1}.
\]
For even $n$ the inequality is strict.  Consequently the S-matrix conjecture
holds in all odd dimensions, in dimension $2$, and in every dimension
$n\ge158$.
\end{theorem}

\section*{Proof intuition}

Frankel--Urschel show that a hypothetical even-dimensional counterexample is
almost binary.  From it one can select $t+1$ binary rows and a short vector
$y$.  The first $t$ row pairings with $y$ must lie very close to $1/4$, while
the last must lie close to $0$.  The selected rows have an exactly prescribed
Gram matrix, so after orthonormalizing them, Bessel's inequality says that the
pairings cannot fluctuate that much unless $y$ is longer than allowed.

Their proof fixes $t=50$ and replaces the first two moments of the pairings by
separate coarse bounds.  The key improvement is to keep $t$ free and write the
completed-square error in terms of the \emph{variance}.  The $t$ forced gaps
between a quarter and zero then contribute directly.  Optimizing the integer
$t$ makes the contradiction work from $n=158$ onward.

\section*{The upstream structural lemma}

Normalize a hypothetical counterexample $B$ by $\|B\|_{\max}=1$.  For even
$n$, integers $t\ge4$ with $n\ge4t$, Frankel--Urschel's Lemma 3.2 gives
$m=t+1$, a matrix $C\in\{0,1\}^{m\times n}$, and $y\in\mathbb R^n$ such that
\begin{align}
 CC^T&=qI+p\one\one^T,&
 q&=\left\lceil\frac n4\right\rceil,&
 p&=\left\lfloor\frac n4\right\rfloor,\label{eq:gram}\\
 \|y\|_2^2&\le\frac1{4(n-t)}.\label{eq:ynorm}
\end{align}
Writing $z=Cy$, their coordinate estimates are
\begin{align}
 |z_j-\frac14|&<\varepsilon_+(n,t) &&(1\le j\le t),\label{eq:zplus}\\
 |z_m|&<\varepsilon_0(n,t),\label{eq:zzero}
\end{align}
where, with $s=n-t$,
\begin{align*}
\varepsilon_+(n,t)
 &=\frac{n-1}{4(n-2)n}+\frac5{4s}
   +\frac1{(8s-4)\sqrt{s}},\\
\varepsilon_0(n,t)
 &=\frac{2n^2-4n+1}{4(n-2)(n-1)n}+\frac5{4s}
   +\frac1{(8s-4)\sqrt{s}}.
\end{align*}

\section*{A free-parameter projection contradiction}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:projection}
Set $m=t+1$, $\gamma=n/q$,
\[
 d=\frac14-\varepsilon_+-\varepsilon_0,
 \qquad
 \mu_- =\frac{t(\frac14-\varepsilon_+)-\varepsilon_0}{m},
\]
and
\[
 R=\frac{n}{4m(n-t)}
 -\gamma\frac{t d^2}{m^2}
 -\gamma\left(1-\frac\gamma4\right)\mu_-^2.
\]
Assume $d>0$, $\mu_->0$, $R\ge0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:certificate}
 \frac{\gamma\mu_-}{2}-\sqrt R-\varepsilon_0
 >\sqrt{\frac{n}{8(n-t)}}.
\end{equation}
Then no $C,y$ satisfying \eqref{eq:gram}--\eqref{eq:zzero} exist.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The row-orthonormalization used in the proof of
\cite[Lemma 3.3]{FrankelUrschel} applies to any number $m$ of selected rows.
Put $\alpha=\one^Ty$, and let
\[
 \mu=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m z_j,
 \qquad
 \nu=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m z_j^2.
\]
Bessel's inequality and \eqref{eq:ynorm} give, by the same direct Gram-matrix
calculation,
\[
 \alpha^2-\gamma\mu\alpha+\gamma\nu
 \le \frac{n}{4m(n-t)}.
\]
Completing the square yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square}
 \left(\alpha-\frac{\gamma\mu}{2}\right)^2
 \le \frac{n}{4m(n-t)}
 -\gamma\operatorname{Var}(z)
 -\gamma\left(1-\frac\gamma4\right)\mu^2.
\end{equation}
Here $\gamma\le4$.  The coordinate estimates imply $\mu\ge\mu_->0$.
Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(z)
 =\frac1{m^2}\sum_{i<j}(z_i-z_j)^2
 \ge \frac{t d^2}{m^2},
\]
because each of the first $t$ coordinates differs from $z_m$ by at least $d$.
Thus the right side of \eqref{eq:square} is at most $R$, and
\[
 \alpha\ge\frac{\gamma\mu_-}{2}-\sqrt R.
\]

The last row of $C$ contains $q+p=n/2$ ones by \eqref{eq:gram}.  Therefore
\[
 \alpha-z_m=\sum_{k:C_{m,k}=0}y_k,
 \qquad
 |\alpha-z_m|\le\sqrt{\frac n2}\,\|y\|_2
 \le\sqrt{\frac{n}{8(n-t)}}.
\]
The lower bound for $\alpha$, \eqref{eq:zzero}, and
\eqref{eq:certificate} give the strict reverse inequality, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\section*{Certified parameters and proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}}

The interval checker \texttt{code/verify\_cutoff.py} evaluates the hypotheses
of Lemma \ref{lem:projection} for every even $n$ from $158$ through $998$ and
searches the finite range $4\le t\le\lfloor n/4\rfloor$.  It uses directed
\texttt{mpmath.iv} arithmetic at 80 decimal digits and accepts a dimension only
when the lower endpoint of the margin in \eqref{eq:certificate} is positive.
All 421 dimensions are certified.  The weakest certificate occurs at
$(n,t)=(160,23)$ and has lower margin
\[
 0.0002579293062736265\ldots>0.
\]
The full transcript is in \texttt{verification.md}; the first choices include
$t=26$ for $n=158$, $t=23$ for $n=160$, and $t=26$ for $n=162$.

Hence the structural consequence of a counterexample is impossible for every
even $158\le n\le998$.  Frankel--Urschel's Theorem 1.2 handles all
$n\ge1000$, and the Drnov\v{s}ek--Cheng theorem handles odd dimensions (with
Drnov\v{s}ek also treating $n=2$).  This proves Theorem \ref{thm:main}.
For even $n$ equality is impossible because an S-matrix would have column
weight $(n+1)/2$, which is not an integer.

\section*{Verification, limitations, and novelty search}

The code was rerun from the packet directory and checked all 421 dimensions;
the final PDF and every rendered page were also inspected.  The proof depends
on the correctness of Frankel--Urschel Lemma 3.2, copied as a supporting PDF,
but the new projection lemma is proved in full above.

The remaining unresolved scope is the finite list of even dimensions
$4,6,\ldots,156$.  Seven focused upgrade routes were examined: free-parameter
optimization, variance retention, directed-interval certification, sharpening
the upstream constants, small-dimensional KKT/numerical analysis, dimension
lifting, and inverse sign-pattern bounds.  The last three do not supply a
responsible finite-case proof, and the sharper tentative cutoff was not
promoted because it modifies multiple upstream estimates.

A bounded search through 11 August 2026 used the exact conjecture name, the
source and supporting titles/authors, citation trails, and threshold phrases.
It found the published $n\ge1000$ theorem but no prior cutoff below $1000$.
This supports, but does not prove, novelty.  Human review should focus on the
general-$m$ Gram calculation in Lemma \ref{lem:projection} and rerun the
interval checker.
