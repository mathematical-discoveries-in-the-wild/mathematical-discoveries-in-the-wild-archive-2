\section{Source question and scope}
Faulhuber, Shafkulovska, and Zlotnikov ask whether, for an odd Hermite
function $h_n$, the Gabor system is always a frame when the lattice covolume
is less than one and differs from $N/(N+1)$ \cite[Introduction, p.~2]{FSZ}.
They later state explicitly that $h_1$ is the only Hermite case for which the
frame-set conjecture remains open \cite[p.~19]{FSZ}.  The two source excerpts
are reproduced below.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/frame_set_conjecture_crop.png}
  \caption{The frame-set questions in the source paper, PDF page 2.}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{The source paper's explicit open-status statement for $h_1$, PDF
  page 19.}
\end{figure}

For rectangular lattices $a\Z\times b\Z$, the conjecture specializes to
\[
  \G(h_1,a\Z\times b\Z)\text{ is a frame whenever }
  ab<1,\qquad ab\ne\frac{N}{N+1}.
\]
The classical safety region is $ab<1/2$.  Lyubarskii and Nes proved the
positive hyperbola $ab=3/5$ and the negative hyperbolas
$ab=N/(N+1)$ \cite{LN}.  The theorem below adds the entire positive
hyperbola $ab=4/7$.

\section{Proof intuition}
At rational density $p/q$, the frame question is equivalent to full row rank
of a finite Zak-transform matrix.  For $p/q=4/7$, two carefully chosen
$4\times4$ minors cover the two halves of the fundamental $x$-interval.
After centering every Zak series at its nearest Gaussian term, each minor has
one determinant monomial whose Gaussian exponent is uniformly smaller than
that of every competitor.  A rigorous interval calculation at the square
lattice shows that the total absolute mass of \emph{all} monomials is less
than $1.078$ times the dominant one.  Consequently the remainder has size
less than $0.078$ times the dominant monomial, so cancellation cannot make the
minor vanish.  Exact exponent gaps show that this ratio only decreases as the
time spacing increases; Fourier symmetry supplies the complementary aspect
ratios.

\section{The result}
We use the harmless normalization
\[
 h_1(t)=t e^{-\pi t^2};
\]
multiplying the window by a nonzero constant does not affect the frame
property.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $a,b>0$ with $ab=4/7$, the rectangular Gabor system
\[
 \G(h_1,a\Z\times b\Z)
 =\{e^{2\pi i b n(\cdot)}h_1(\cdot-am):m,n\in\Z\}
\]
is a frame for $L^2(\R)$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The rational rank criterion}
For $L>0$, write
\[
 Z_Lg(u,\omega)=\sum_{k\in\Z}g(u-Lk)e^{2\pi i kL\omega}.
\]
Lyubarskii and Nes prove that if $ab=p/q$ in lowest terms, then
$\G(g,a\Z\times b\Z)$ is a frame if and only if the $p\times q$ matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P}
 P_{s,t}(x,\omega)
 =Z_{aq}g\!\left(x+at+\frac{aq}{p}s,\omega\right),
 \quad 0\le s<p,\quad0\le t<q,
\end{equation}
has rank $p$ throughout a fundamental rectangle
\cite[Theorem 2]{LN}.  It is enough below to treat $0\le x<a/p$ and all
$\omega$, because the matrix is quasiperiodic.

\subsection{Two minors and their dominant terms}
Set $p=4$, $q=7$, $y=x/a$, and first assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a-large}
 a\ge a_0:=\sqrt{4/7}.
\end{equation}
Thus $0\le y\le1/4$.  On $0\le y\le1/8$, select columns
$(1,4,5,6)$ of \eqref{eq:P}; on $1/8\le y\le1/4$, select columns
$(0,1,4,6)$.  After shifting the arguments by integer multiples of
$7a$, the centered dimensionless arguments $a(y+B_{sj})$ are given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bminus}
B_-=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-3&-2&-1\\
11/4&-5/4&-1/4&3/4\\
-5/2&1/2&3/2&5/2\\
-3/4&9/4&13/4&-11/4
\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
on the first half, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bplus}
B_+=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&-3&-1\\
7/4&11/4&-5/4&3/4\\
-7/2&-5/2&1/2&5/2\\
-7/4&-3/4&9/4&-11/4
\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
on the second.  The determinant permutation is written as a tuple whose
$s$th entry is the chosen column index in these displayed matrices.  Choose
\[
 \sigma_-=(3,2,1,0),\qquad \sigma_+=(0,3,2,1).
\]
The monomial obtained from these permutations and the centered term of each
Zak series will be called the dominant monomial.  Its magnitude is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D}
 D_\pm(a,y)=a^4
 \prod_{s=0}^3|y+(B_\pm)_{s,\sigma_\pm(s)}|
 \exp\!\left(-\pi a^2
 \sum_{s=0}^3(y+(B_\pm)_{s,\sigma_\pm(s)})^2\right).
\end{equation}
Every factor in \eqref{eq:D} has absolute value at least $1/8$ on its
assigned half interval, so $D_\pm(a,y)>0$.

For a selected entry with centered coefficient $c=y+B_{sj}$, put
\[
 A_{sj}(a,y)=a\sum_{k\in\Z}|c-7k|e^{-\pi a^2(c-7k)^2},
 \qquad T_\pm(a,y)=\per(A(a,y)).
\]
Expanding both the determinant and every Zak series shows that
$T_\pm$ is the sum of the absolute values of all determinant monomials,
including the chosen monomial $D_\pm$.  Hence, for every $\omega$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reverse-triangle}
 |\det P_\pm(x,\omega)|
 \ge D_\pm(a,y)-\bigl(T_\pm(a,y)-D_\pm(a,y)\bigr)
 =2D_\pm(a,y)-T_\pm(a,y).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Certified dominance]\label{lem:certificate}
On the respective half intervals,
\[
 \frac{T_\pm(a_0,y)}{D_\pm(a_0,y)}<1.077024<2.
\]
Moreover, $T_\pm(a,y)/D_\pm(a,y)$ is nonincreasing for $a\ge a_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is a finite, outward-rounded interval certificate.  Each
half interval is divided into 512 equal rational boxes.  On each box the
script evaluates all $24$ permanent terms using 50-decimal interval
arithmetic.  Five translates, $-2\le k\le2$, are evaluated directly.  Since
$|c|\le27/8<7/2$, the omitted two-sided tail is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 2\left(Ae^{-\kappa A^2}
 +\frac{e^{-\kappa A^2}}{14\kappa}\right),\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{4\pi}{7},\quad A=\frac{35}{2},
\end{equation}
which is less than $6.015\cdot10^{-238}$ per entry.  The largest
interval upper endpoint for $T/D$ is
\[
 1.077023594634533
\]
(rounded upward), occurring in the box adjacent to $y=1/8$ on either side.

For the monotonicity assertion, remove the common $a^4$ from every ratio.
Exact rational arithmetic verifies that, among centered terms, every
non-dominant permutation has sum-of-squares exponent at least $7/2$ above
the dominant permutation.  Also, replacing any centered coefficient by a
different $7$-translate increases its square by at least
\[
 49-14\max|c|=49-14\cdot\frac{27}{8}=\frac74.
\]
Thus every non-dominant monomial divided by the dominant monomial has the
form $C e^{-\pi a^2\Delta}$ with $C\ge0$ independent of $a$ and
$\Delta\ge7/4$.  Their sum $(T-D)/D$ is therefore nonincreasing in $a$.
All rational gap checks, tail bounds, and interval evaluations are performed
and asserted by the supplied verifier.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Under \eqref{eq:a-large}, Lemma~\ref{lem:certificate} and
\eqref{eq:reverse-triangle} show that the selected minor is nonzero on each
half of $0\le y\le1/4$, uniformly in $\omega$.  Therefore the matrix
\eqref{eq:P} has rank four everywhere, and the Lyubarskii--Nes criterion
proves the frame property.

If instead $a<a_0$, then $b=(4/7)/a>a_0$.  The Fourier transform interchanges
the time and frequency lattice parameters, and $\widehat h_1=-ih_1$ (up to
the chosen normalization).  Hence the system with parameters $(a,b)$ is
unitarily equivalent to the already treated system with parameters $(b,a)$.
This proves the theorem for every aspect ratio.
\end{proof}

\section{Limitations and human-review recommendation}
The full frame-set conjecture remains open.  The present dominant-monomial
method certifies $4/7$ and appears numerically effective at several other
rational densities, but its absolute-value estimate fails at some high
densities (already $13/15$), where phase cancellation among multiple leading
monomials must be controlled instead.

A reviewer should check (i) the transcription of the rational rank criterion,
(ii) the two centered matrices and selected permutations, (iii) that the
reverse-triangle estimate counts every determinant/Zak monomial once, and
(iv) the interval script's outward rounding and tail formula.  These are the
only substantive dependencies.
