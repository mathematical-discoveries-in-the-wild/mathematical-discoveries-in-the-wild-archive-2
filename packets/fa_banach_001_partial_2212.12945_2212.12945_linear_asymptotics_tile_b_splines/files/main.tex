\section{Source question}

The source is T.~I.~Zaitseva, \emph{Multivariate tile B-splines},
arXiv:2212.12945, later published in \emph{Izvestiya: Mathematics}
87(2) (2023), 284--325 \cite{Zaitseva2023}.  If $G\subset\R^d$ is a tile,
the tile B-spline of order $\ell$ is
\[
 B_\ell^G=\underbrace{\chi_G*\cdots*\chi_G}_{\ell+1\text{ factors}}.
\]
After computing small-order regularities by growing transition matrices, the
paper asks in Remark~6 (PDF page 33):
\begin{quote}
``The question of the asymptotics of the regularity with the increase of order
of B-splines remains open.''
\end{quote}

The source considers both $C$-regularity and $L_2$-H\"older regularity.  The
result below addresses the latter.  It applies more generally to convolution
powers of any normalized bounded measurable set.

\section{Proof intuition}

The growing refinement matrices disappear after taking Fourier transforms:
if $F=\wh{\chi_G}$, then $\wh{B_\ell^G}=F^{\ell+1}$.  Regularity is therefore
the boundary of the set of pairs $(p,s)$ for which
\[
 \int_{|\xi|\ge1}|\xi|^{2s}|F(\xi)|^{2p}\dd\xi<\infty.
\]
H\"older's inequality makes this integrability set convex, so its upper
boundary as a function of $p$ is concave.  Its value at $p=0$ is exactly
$-d/2$.  A nonnegative concave function vanishing at the origin has decreasing
secant slopes; this yields a limiting regularity per convolution and exact
finite-order upper bounds on it.

The tile sum rules bound the limiting slope above by $1$.  In the other
direction, a $p$th difference of a $p$fold convolution is the convolution of
$p$ first differences.  Young's inequality converts the translation modulus
of the tile itself into the lower bound $L_G\ge2\alpha_{0,2}(G)$.  What remains
open is the computation of $L_G$ for particular fractal tiles and the analogous
$C$-regularity problem.

\section{Definitions and a threshold lemma}

Let $G\subset\R^d$ be bounded and measurable with $|G|=1$, put
$f=\chi_G$, and use the Fourier convention
\(
\wh f(\xi)=\int f(x)e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}\dd x.
\)
Then $F=\wh f$ satisfies $|F|\le1$.  For real $p\ge0$, define the extended
regularity threshold
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Adef}
 A_G(p)=\sup\left\{s\in\R:
 \int_{|\xi|\ge1}|\xi|^{2s}|F(\xi)|^{2p}\dd\xi<\infty\right\},
\end{equation}
where $|F|^0=1$.

For a compactly supported $g\in L_2(\R^d)$, let
\(\alpha_2(g)\) denote the supremal $L_2$-H\"older/Besov regularity used by
the source.  Only the supremal threshold matters here, so endpoint conventions
at integral exponents do not affect the result.

\begin{lemma}[Sobolev and $L_2$-H\"older thresholds]\label{lem:threshold}
For compactly supported $g\in L_2(\R^d)$,
\[
 \alpha_2(g)=\sup\{s:g\in H^s(\R^d)\}.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alphaA}
 \alpha_{\ell,2}(G):=\alpha_2(B_\ell^G)=A_G(\ell+1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 E_j=\int_{2^j\le|\xi|<2^{j+1}}|\wh g(\xi)|^2\dd\xi,
 \qquad j\ge0.
\]
Up to the harmless low-frequency term, membership in $H^s$ is equivalent to
\(\sum_j2^{2sj}E_j<\infty\), while the $B^t_{2,\infty}$ condition is
\(\sup_j2^{2tj}E_j<\infty\).  The former implies the latter at the same
exponent.  Conversely, the latter at exponent $t$ implies the former at every
$s<t$ by a geometric series.  Their supremal thresholds are equal.  Since
\(\wh{B_\ell^G}=F^{\ell+1}\), the Sobolev integral gives
\eqref{eq:alphaA}.
\end{proof}

\section{The linear-asymptotic theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Concavity and asymptotic slope]\label{thm:main}
Let $G\subset\R^d$ be bounded, measurable, and $|G|=1$.  Assume
$\alpha_{0,2}(G)<\infty$, as holds for every nontrivial tile indicator.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $A_G$ is nondecreasing and concave on $[0,\infty)$, and
      $A_G(0)=-d/2$.
\item The shifted normalized regularities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qmono}
 q_\ell(G)=\frac{\alpha_{\ell,2}(G)+d/2}{\ell+1}
\end{equation}
are nonincreasing.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ldef}
 L_G:=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}q_\ell(G)
     =\inf_{\ell\ge0}q_\ell(G)
\end{equation}
exists and is finite and nonnegative.
\item The regularity has the first-order asymptotic
\begin{equation}\label{eq:asymptotic}
 \alpha_{\ell,2}(G)=L_G(\ell+1)-\frac d2+o(\ell).
\end{equation}
Here the displayed $-d/2$ is the natural concavity normalization, not a claim
about a second-order constant.
\item The sequence $\alpha_{\ell,2}(G)$ is nondecreasing and discretely
concave.  Its marginal gains decrease and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:increments}
 \alpha_{\ell+1,2}(G)-\alpha_{\ell,2}(G)\ \downarrow\ L_G.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
If $G$ is a lattice tile and its tile B-splines are stable with the exact
sum-rule orders described in \cite{Zaitseva2023,ProtasovZaitseva2025}, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tileupper}
 0\le L_G\le1.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose the integral in \eqref{eq:Adef} is finite at $(p_0,s_0)$ and
$(p_1,s_1)$.  For $0<\theta<1$, set
\[
 p_\theta=\theta p_0+(1-\theta)p_1,
 \qquad s_\theta=\theta s_0+(1-\theta)s_1.
\]
The integrand at $(p_\theta,s_\theta)$ is the product of the two endpoint
integrands raised to the powers $\theta$ and $1-\theta$.  H\"older's
inequality proves its integrability.  Letting $s_i\uparrow A_G(p_i)$ shows
\[
 A_G(p_\theta)\ge\theta A_G(p_0)+(1-\theta)A_G(p_1),
\]
so $A_G$ is concave.  Since $|F|\le1$, increasing $p$ can only decrease the
integrand, hence $A_G$ is nondecreasing.  At $p=0$, radial integration gives
\[
 \int_{|\xi|\ge1}|\xi|^{2s}\dd\xi<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s<-d/2,
\]
and therefore $A_G(0)=-d/2$.

Put $C(p)=A_G(p)+d/2$.  Then $C$ is nonnegative and concave, with $C(0)=0$.
For $0<p<r$, concavity gives
\[
 C(p)\ge\frac p r C(r),
\]
so $C(p)/p$ is nonincreasing.  Restricting to $p=\ell+1$ and using
Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold} proves \eqref{eq:qmono}--\eqref{eq:asymptotic}.
The assumed finiteness at $p=1$ and the secant inequality make the limit
finite.  Monotonicity and concavity of $A_G$ restricted to the integers give
the corresponding properties of $\alpha_{\ell,2}$.  The decreasing discrete
increments have a limit, and the Ces\`aro mean identity forces that limit to
equal $L_G$, proving \eqref{eq:increments}.

Finally, the source proves stability of tile B-splines and identifies their
sum-rule order.  The standard stability/sum-rule obstruction recalled and
used in \cite{ProtasovZaitseva2025} gives
\(\alpha_{\ell,2}(G)\le\ell+1\).  Divide by $\ell+1$ and take the limit to
obtain $L_G\le1$; nonnegativity was already proved.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A geometric lower bound]\label{cor:lower}
For every normalized tile $G$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 2\alpha_{0,2}(G)\le L_G\le1.
\end{equation}
In particular, the square has $L_G=1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write $f=\chi_G$, $D(h)=|G\triangle(G+h)|$, and
$\Delta_h=\tau_h-I$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:indicatornorms}
 \|\Delta_hf\|_1=D(h),\qquad
 \|\Delta_hf\|_2=D(h)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
For an integer $p\ge1$, the convolution and translation algebras give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diffidentity}
 \Delta_h^p(f^{*p})=(\Delta_hf)^{*p}.
\end{equation}
Young's inequality and \eqref{eq:indicatornorms} imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:young}
 \|\Delta_h^p(f^{*p})\|_2
 \le \|\Delta_hf\|_2\|\Delta_hf\|_1^{p-1}
 =D(h)^{p-1/2}.
\end{equation}
Fix $0\le\eta<\alpha_{0,2}(G)$.  By the definition of the regularity of
$\chi_G$, $D(h)^{1/2}\le C_\eta|h|^\eta$ for small $h$.  Thus
\[
 \|\Delta_h^p(f^{*p})\|_2
 \le C_{\eta,p}|h|^{2\eta(p-1/2)}.
\]
A nonconstant compactly supported indicator has
$\alpha_{0,2}\le1/2$, so $2\eta(p-1/2)<p$.  The $p$th-difference
characterization of $B^s_{2,\infty}$ therefore yields
\[
 \alpha_{p-1,2}(G)\ge2\eta(p-1/2).
\]
Divide by $p$, let $p\to\infty$, and then let
$\eta\uparrow\alpha_{0,2}(G)$ to get $L_G\ge2\alpha_{0,2}(G)$.
The upper bound is Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  For the square,
$\alpha_{0,2}=1/2$, so equality follows.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences for the source examples}

In dimension two, every computed order gives the rigorous implication
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finitebound}
 L_G\le\frac{\alpha_{\ell,2}(G)+1}{\ell+1},
\end{equation}
and these upper bounds must decrease with $\ell$.  The tables in
\cite{ProtasovZaitseva2025} exhibit exactly this pattern.  Using their displayed
decimal values only as numerical evidence, not as interval-certified proof,
the order-$8$ Bear value $8.9199$ gives
$L_{\rm Bear}\lesssim1.10221$, while the order-$5$ Dragon value $3.6688$ gives
$L_{\rm Dragon}\lesssim0.77813$.  Corollary~\ref{cor:lower} and the source's
order-zero decimals give the indicative lower bounds
\[
 L_{\rm Bear}\gtrsim0.7892,
 \qquad L_{\rm Dragon}\gtrsim0.4764.
\]
The exact theorem is the monotonicity and limiting law; the decimal endpoints
inherit the precision limitations of the published tables.

\section{Verification, limitations, and novelty status}

\paragraph{Internal checks.}
The Fourier-power identity and the dyadic threshold equivalence were checked
independently, as were all exponents in H\"older's and Young's inequalities.
The theorem reproduces
the square sequence $\alpha_{\ell,2}=\ell+1/2$: its normalized quantities are
$1+1/(2(\ell+1))$, decreasing to $1$.  The published Dragon and Bear tables
also have decreasing marginal gains and decreasing normalized shifted values,
as the theorem requires.

\paragraph{Bounded literature audit.}
The search covered the source and its citations, exact question phrases,
arXiv/title/author searches, the run indexes, arXiv:2312.11182 and its 2025
journal version \cite{ProtasovZaitseva2025}, and Zaitseva's 2025
\emph{Supersmooth tile B-splines} \cite{Zaitseva2025}.  Those papers give new
finite-order formulas and tables and a supersmoothness classification
conjecture.  No theorem asserting concavity, existence of the large-order
slope, or the displayed geometric lower bound was found through 13 August
2026.  This bounded search does not certify novelty.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The result does not compute $L_G$ for Dragon, Bear, or a general tile.  It does
not show that the remainder in \eqref{eq:asymptotic} is bounded, and the
$-d/2$ normalization is not a second-order asymptotic.  It does not address
the joint-spectral-radius asymptotics governing $C$-regularity.  Hence this is
a substantial partial answer rather than a full resolution of every reading
of Remark~6.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
High priority.  The core proof is short and matrix-free.  A reviewer should
focus on Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold}, the endpoint-independent interpretation
of the source regularity, the exact sum-rule upper bound, and the use of the
$p$th-difference characterization in Corollary~\ref{cor:lower}.
