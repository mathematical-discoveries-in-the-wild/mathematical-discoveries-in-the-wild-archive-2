\section{Source and exact question}

The source is Saurabh Dwivedi, \emph{A study on coreflexive Banach spaces},
arXiv:2604.14068; published online in \emph{Proceedings -- Mathematical
Sciences}, DOI \texttt{10.1007/s12044-026-00868-3} \cite{Dwivedi}.
Question~3.13 on page~13 asks:

\begin{quote}
Is $L^p(\mu,X)$ weak-star sequentially dense in its bidual whenever $X$ is
weak-star sequentially dense in its bidual?  The source adds that this remains
open even when both $X^*$ and $X^{**}$ have the Radon--Nikodym property.
\end{quote}

Here $X$ is \emph{weak-star sequentially dense in $X^{**}$} if every
$x^{**}\in X^{**}$ is the $\sigma(X^{**},X^*)$-limit of a sequence from the
canonical copy of $X$.

\section{Result}

\begin{theorem}[RNP and atomic subcases of Question 3.13]\label{thm:main}
Let $1<p<\infty$, let $q$ be conjugate to $p$, and suppose that $X$ is
weak-star sequentially dense in $X^{**}$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textnormal{(\alph*)}]
\item If $X^*$ and $X^{**}$ have the Radon--Nikodym property, then for every
probability space $(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)$, the canonical copy of
$L^p(\mu,X)$ is weak-star sequentially dense in
$L^p(\mu,X)^{**}$.
\item If $(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)$ is purely atomic, then the same conclusion
holds with no Radon--Nikodym assumptions on $X^*$ or $X^{**}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Part (a) is exactly the subcase singled out as still open in the final sentence
of Question~3.13.  No coreflexivity assumption is needed.  Part (b) is also an
instance of the source's preceding $\ell_p$-sum proposition; it is included
because Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge} supplies the uniform boundedness needed for a
fully dominated coordinate proof.  The novelty claim in this packet concerns
part (a), not that same-paper atomic consequence.

\section{Proof intuition}

The definition gives a bounded approximating sequence for each individual
$x^{**}$, but apparently with a bound depending on the point.  The first key
observation is that the dependence is automatically uniform.  Put on $X^{**}$
the infimum of the sup norms of all weak-star convergent lifting sequences.
This ``sequential approximation norm'' is complete.  The open mapping theorem
therefore makes it equivalent to the ordinary bidual norm.

Under the two RNP assumptions, the standard Bochner duality identifications
are
\[
  L^p(\mu,X)^*=L^q(\mu,X^*),
  \qquad
  L^p(\mu,X)^{**}=L^p(\mu,X^{**}).
\]
Write an arbitrary $f\in L^p(\mu,X^{**})$ as a norm-convergent series of
simple increments whose $L^p$ norms are summable.  Lift every value of every
increment by its uniformly bounded weak-star approximating sequence.  At
stage $n$, add the $n$th lifts of the first $n$ increments.  Dominated
convergence first on each finite-range increment and then on the summable
increment index proves weak-star convergence against every
$L^q(\mu,X^*)$ test function.

On a purely atomic probability space, Bochner $L^p$ is a weighted countable
$\ell_p$-sum.  The same uniform constant and an ordinary dominated-series
argument work coordinatewise, without any RNP representation theorem.

\section{Uniform sequential lifting}

\begin{lemma}[Sequential approximation norm]\label{lem:gauge}
Suppose $X$ is weak-star sequentially dense in $X^{**}$.  There is a constant
$K_X<\infty$ such that, for every $x^{**}\in X^{**}$, one can choose a
sequence $(x_n)\subset X$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-lift}
 x_n\xrightarrow{\wstar}x^{**},
 \qquad
 \sup_n\|x_n\|\leq K_X\|x^{**}\|.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $z\in X^{**}$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge}
 |z|_s=\inf\left\{\sup_n\|z_n\|:
       (z_n)\subset X,\ z_n\xrightarrow{\wstar}z\right\}.
\end{equation}
The hypothesis makes this finite everywhere.  It is a norm: homogeneity and
the triangle inequality follow by taking scalar multiples and termwise sums
of lifting sequences, while weak-star lower semicontinuity of the norm gives
$\|z\|\leq|z|_s$.

We verify completeness.  Let $(z^m)$ be Cauchy in $|\cdot|_s$.  It is Cauchy
in the ordinary norm, so let $z$ be its norm limit.  Pass to a subsequence,
still denoted $(z^m)$, such that
\[
 |z^{m+1}-z^m|_s<2^{-m-2}.
\]
Put $u_m=z^{m+1}-z^m$.  Choose $u_{m,n}\in X$ with
$u_{m,n}\to u_m$ weak-star and
$\sup_n\|u_{m,n}\|\leq2^{-m-1}$.  Also choose a bounded weak-star lifting
$(a_n)\subset X$ of $z^1$.  Then
\[
 v_n=a_n+\sum_{m=1}^n u_{m,n}\in X
\]
is bounded.  For each $x^*\in X^*$, dominated convergence over the counting
measure on $m$ gives
\[
 x^*(v_n)\longrightarrow
 z^1(x^*)+\sum_{m=1}^\infty u_m(x^*)=z(x^*).
\]
Indeed, for fixed $N$ the triangular sums
\[
 w_{N,n}=\sum_{m=N}^{n}u_{m,n}\qquad(n\geq N)
\]
belong to $X$, have norms at most $\sum_{m=N}^\infty2^{-m-1}$, and
converge weak-star to $\sum_{m=N}^\infty u_m=z-z^N$ by the same dominated
convergence argument.  Consequently,
\[
 |z-z^N|_s\leq\sum_{m=N}^\infty 2^{-m-1},
\]
so the selected subsequence converges in $|\cdot|_s$; the original Cauchy
sequence does as well.  Thus $(X^{**},|\cdot|_s)$ is Banach.

The identity
\[
 I:(X^{**},|\cdot|_s)\longrightarrow(X^{**},\|\cdot\|)
\]
is a continuous linear bijection.  The open mapping theorem gives
$|z|_s\leq C_X\|z\|$.  Taking a sequence within $\|z\|$ of the infimum in
\eqref{eq:gauge} gives \eqref{eq:uniform-lift} with $K_X=C_X+1$ (and the zero
sequence when $z=0$).
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

\subsection*{The RNP case}

Assume $X^*$ and $X^{**}$ have the RNP.  Standard Bochner duality
\cite[Chapter IV]{DiestelUhl} gives the canonical isometric identifications
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rnp-id}
 Y:=L^p(\mu,X),\qquad
 Y^*=L^q(\mu,X^*),\qquad
 Y^{**}=L^p(\mu,X^{**}).
\end{equation}
Fix $f\in L^p(\mu,X^{**})$.  Choose simple functions $s_m$ such that
$s_0=0$ and
\[
 \|f-s_m\|_p\leq4^{-m}\qquad(m\geq1).
\]
Set $d_m=s_m-s_{m-1}$.  Then every $d_m$ is simple,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:series}
 f=\sum_{m=1}^\infty d_m\quad\hbox{in }L^p(\mu,X^{**}),
 \qquad
 \sum_{m=1}^\infty\|d_m\|_p<\infty.
\end{equation}

Write the finite range of $d_m$ as $\{v_{m,1},\dots,v_{m,r_m}\}$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge}, for every nonzero $v_{m,j}$ choose
$x_{m,j,n}\in X$ with
\[
 x_{m,j,n}\xrightarrow{\wstar}v_{m,j},
 \qquad
 \sup_n\|x_{m,j,n}\|\leq K_X\|v_{m,j}\|,
\]
and use zero for a zero value.  Replacing every value $v_{m,j}$ of $d_m$ by
$x_{m,j,n}$ gives an $X$-valued simple function $d_{m,n}$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pointwise-bound}
 d_{m,n}(t)\xrightarrow{\wstar}d_m(t),
 \qquad
 \|d_{m,n}(t)\|\leq K_X\|d_m(t)\|\qquad(t\in\Omega).
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gn}
 g_n=\sum_{m=1}^n d_{m,n}\in L^p(\mu,X).
\end{equation}

Let $h\in L^q(\mu,X^*)$.  For fixed $m$, pointwise weak-star convergence in
\eqref{eq:pointwise-bound}, followed by dominated convergence and H\"older's
inequality, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-m}
 \int_\Omega\langle d_{m,n}(t)-d_m(t),h(t)\rangle\,d\mu(t)
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The absolute value of the integral in \eqref{eq:fixed-m} is at most
$(K_X+1)\|d_m\|_p\|h\|_q$.  These majorants are summable by
\eqref{eq:series}.  Treating the omitted terms $m>n$ as
$-\int\langle d_m,h\rangle$ and applying dominated convergence once more,
now over the counting measure on $m$, yields
\[
 \int_\Omega\langle g_n(t)-f(t),h(t)\rangle\,d\mu(t)\longrightarrow0.
\]
By \eqref{eq:rnp-id}, this says exactly that $g_n\to f$ in
$\sigma(Y^{**},Y^*)$.  Part (a) follows.

\subsection*{The purely atomic case}

A purely atomic probability space gives, after rescaling the atoms, an
isometric identification
\[
 L^p(\mu,X)\cong\left(\bigoplus_{k\in I}X\right)_{\ell_p},
\]
where $I$ is finite or countable.  For $1<p<\infty$, without any RNP
assumption,
\[
 \left(\bigoplus_{k\in I}X\right)_{\ell_p}^{**}
 =\left(\bigoplus_{k\in I}X^{**}\right)_{\ell_p},
 \qquad
 \left(\bigoplus_{k\in I}X\right)_{\ell_p}^{*}
 =\left(\bigoplus_{k\in I}X^*\right)_{\ell_q}.
\]
Given $z=(z_k)\in\ell_p(I;X^{**})$, use Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge} to choose
$x_{k,n}\to z_k$ weak-star with
$\sup_n\|x_{k,n}\|\leq K_X\|z_k\|$, and put
$x^{(n)}=(x_{k,n})_{k\in I}$.  Then $x^{(n)}\in\ell_p(I;X)$ and
$\|x^{(n)}\|_p\leq K_X\|z\|_p$.  Against every
$x^*=(x_k^*)\in\ell_q(I;X^*)$, coordinatewise convergence and the summable
majorant $2K_X\|z_k\|\|x_k^*\|$ give
\[
 \sum_{k\in I}\langle x_{k,n},x_k^*\rangle
 \longrightarrow
 \sum_{k\in I}\langle z_k,x_k^*\rangle.
\]
This proves part (b).  \qed

\section{Upgrade attempts and remaining obstruction}

Eight materially distinct routes were checked on this target.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\textbf{Route \arabic*.}]
\item The sequential approximation gauge was introduced and proved complete;
the open mapping theorem supplied the uniform lifting constant.  This route
succeeded and is the reusable core of the result.

\item A direct lift of one simple approximation at a time fails because a
single diagonal choice need not control the nonmetrizable weak-star topology.
Replacing approximations by a summable series of simple increments fixes the
problem and proves the RNP subcase.

\item The coordinate version of the same uniform bound proves the purely
atomic case without RNP assumptions.

\item If $X^*$ has the RNP but $X^{**}$ does not, then
$L^p(\mu,X)^*=L^q(\mu,X^*)$, but the bidual can contain nonrepresentable
functionals beyond $L^p(\mu,X^{**})$.  The proof reaches the canonical
representable part only.

\item If $X^{**}$ has the RNP but $X^*$ does not, the dual itself contains
nonrepresentable vector-measure functionals.  Pointwise weak-star convergence
in $X^{**}$ does not control those extra tests.

\item A measurable-selection approach was tested.  It cannot address a
general element of $L^p(\mu,X)^{**}$ without the RNP identifications, because
such an element has no pointwise $X^{**}$ values from which to select lifts.

\item Finite partitions and martingale conditional expectations approximate
the representable Bochner subspace, but singular bidual components persist
under refinement.  No sequential lifting of those singular martingale
differences follows from the hypothesis on $X$.

\item Counterexample routes through $c_0(\Gamma)$, $\ell_1$, and complemented
copies of $\ell_1$ were checked.  The obvious nonseparable candidates already
fail weak-star sequential density, while that property passes to closed
subspaces and excludes $\ell_1$.  No viable counterexample survived.
\end{enumerate}

The exact remaining obstacle is therefore the singular part of the Bochner
bidual.  The hypothesis controls sequences in $X^{**}$, while without RNP a
general point of $L^p(\mu,X)^{**}$ need not be an $X^{**}$-valued function at
all.

\section{Verification, novelty bounds, and recommendation}

The proof was audited at three levels: completeness of the approximation
gauge, the two dominated-convergence passages in the RNP argument, and the
unconditional coordinate domination in the atomic argument.  No computation
is used.  The only external mathematical input beyond standard Banach-space
theorems is the classical RNP representation of Bochner duals.

The four lightweight run indexes were searched for arXiv:2604.14068,
``coreflexive,'' ``weak-star sequentially dense,'' ``Bochner $L^p$,'' and the
exact final-question phrasing.  A bounded arXiv search returned only the
source for ``coreflexive Banach,'' while Crossref/OpenAlex searches for the
weak-star/Bochner combination returned no matching result.  OpenAlex listed
zero citing works for the published source on 11 August 2026.  The 1970 paper
of McWilliams on weak-star sequential density was also checked at the metadata
level; no Bochner-space answer was located.  This is not an exhaustive
literature certification.

Human review should focus first on Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge}, especially the
counting-measure dominated convergence used to prove completeness, and then on
the double domination in \eqref{eq:fixed-m}.  If those steps are accepted,
part (a) directly closes the precise RNP subcase emphasized by the source and
should be recorded as a substantial partial answer.
