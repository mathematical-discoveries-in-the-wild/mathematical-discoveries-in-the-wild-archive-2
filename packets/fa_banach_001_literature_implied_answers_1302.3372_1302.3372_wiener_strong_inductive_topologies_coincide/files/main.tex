\textbf{Status.} \textcolor{statusblue}{\texttt{literature\_implied\_answer\_full\_scope}.}
The answer is affirmative under all hypotheses stated in the source remark.
This is not a new theorem: it follows from a 1999 result of Diaz and
Domanski after a direct identification.

\section*{Question in the source}
Alpay and Salomon assume that
\[
  \Phi_1\supseteq\Phi_2\supseteq\cdots,
  \qquad E:=\bigcap_{p\geq1}\Phi_p
\]
is a reflexive Frechet space, with each \(\Phi_p\) a reflexive Banach space.
They put
\[
 \Psi_p=c_0(\mathbb Z;\Phi_p),\qquad
 \Psi_p'=\ell_1(\mathbb Z;\Phi_p'),
\]
identify \(\bigcup_p\Psi_p'\) with the associated Wiener algebra, and ask in
Remark 5.2 when its inductive-limit topology coincides with its topology as
the strong dual of \(\bigcap_p\Psi_p\) \cite[Remark 5.2]{AS}.

\section*{Identification and answer}
Let \(K=\mathbb Z\cup\{\infty\}\) be the one-point compactification of the
discrete space \(\mathbb Z\). With the natural Frechet seminorms,
\[
 \bigcap_{p\geq1}c_0(\mathbb Z;\Phi_p)
 =c_0(\mathbb Z;E).
\]
Indeed, membership on either side says exactly that
\(\sup_n\lVert x_n\rVert_p<\infty\) and
\(\lVert x_n\rVert_p\to0\) as \(|n|\to\infty\), for every \(p\).

Moreover,
\[
 c_0(\mathbb Z;E)
 =\{f\in C(K,E):f(\infty)=0\}
\]
is complemented in \(C(K,E)\).  A continuous projection is
\[
 (Pf)(n)=f(n)-f(\infty),\qquad (Pf)(\infty)=0.
\]

Diaz and Domanski proved that for every reflexive Frechet space \(E\) and
every compact Hausdorff \(K\), the space \(C(K,E)\) is distinguished
\cite{DD}; the theorem is also recorded explicitly in the
Bierstedt--Bonet survey \cite[p.~183]{BB}. Distinguishedness passes to
complemented Frechet subspaces, so \(c_0(\mathbb Z;E)\) is distinguished.

For a Frechet space \(F\), distinguishedness is equivalent to the equality
of the strong-dual topology \(\beta(F',F)\) and the inductive topology on
\(F'\) obtained from the duals of its local Banach spaces
\cite[Sec.~3(a)]{BB}.  Here those local Banach spaces are the \(\Psi_p\),
whose duals are \(\ell_1(\mathbb Z;\Phi_p')\). Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 \beta\!\left(\left(\bigcap_p\Psi_p\right)',\bigcap_p\Psi_p\right)
 =\underrightarrow{\lim}_{p}\,\ell_1(\mathbb Z;\Phi_p') .}
\]
Thus the two topologies in Remark 5.2 always coincide under the assumptions
of that remark.

\section*{Why this is literature-implied rather than new}
The supporting result predates the 2013 source paper, so its authors could
not explicitly identify their theorem as an answer to Remark 5.2.  The
specialization through the one-point compactification and the complemented
zero-limit subspace is the present identification.  Accordingly this packet
records a full-scope literature-implied answer, not an original solution.

\section*{Search record and review recommendation}
The bounded search used the exact source sentence and the terms
\emph{distinguished Frechet}, \(C(K,E)\), \emph{injective tensor product},
and the source title.  It located the official abstract of \cite{DD} and the
survey statement \cite[p.~183]{BB}; no later paper explicitly naming the
source remark was found.  Human review should check only the standard
stability of distinguishedness under complemented subspaces and the local
Banach-space identification above.
