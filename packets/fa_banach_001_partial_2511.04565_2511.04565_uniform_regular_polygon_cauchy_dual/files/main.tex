\section{Source question and scope}

Joshi, Phatak, and Sholapurkar study the Cauchy dual of multiplication by
$z$ on Dirichlet-type spaces associated with finitely supported measures
\cite{JPS}.  Their epilogue conjecture appears on source PDF p.~10:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Exact transcription.}
``If $\zeta_1,\zeta_2,\zeta_3$ are any three distinct points on the unit
circle and if $\mu=c_1\delta_{\zeta_1}+c_2\delta_{\zeta_2}
+c_3\delta_{\zeta_3}$, then the Cauchy dual $M_z'$ of $M_z$ on $D(\mu)$ is
not subnormal.''

The paper proves this only for three equispaced points with all masses equal
to one.  We prove the common-mass case for every positive mass and, more
generally, obtain a sharp dichotomy for uniform measures on regular
$N$-gons.  Thus this is a solved symmetric subfamily of the conjecture and a
substantial contribution to the broader characterization problem stated on
source PDF p.~4:

\section{Definitions, criterion, and proof intuition}

For a finite positive Borel measure $\mu$ on $\T$, let $\cD(\mu)$ be the
Dirichlet-type space with norm
\[
 \|f\|_{\cD(\mu)}^2=\|f\|_{H^2}^2+
 \int_{\D}|f'(z)|^2P_\mu(z)\,\dd A(z).
\]
Multiplication $\Mz f=zf$ is a cyclic analytic $2$-isometry.  Its Cauchy
dual is $\Mz'=\Mz(\Mz^*\Mz)^{-1}$.

We use the rational de Branges--Rovnyak model of
Chavan--Ghara--Reza and Costara \cite{CGR,Costara}.  In the notation needed
below, $\cD(\mu)=\cH(B)$ with equality of norms, where
\[
 B=(p_1/q,\ldots,p_N/q),\qquad
 G(z,w):=\sum_{j=1}^N p_j(z)\overline{p_j(w)},
\]
and the roots $\alpha_r$ of $q$ lie outside $\overline\D$.

\begin{lemma}[Forbidden-atom necessary condition]\label{lem:criterion}
Suppose $q$ has distinct roots $\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{N-1}$.  If the
Cauchy dual of $\Mz$ on $\cH(B)$ is subnormal, then the complex atomic
measure
\[
 \nu=\sum_{r,t=0}^{N-1}
 \frac{G(\alpha_r,\alpha_t)}
 {\alpha_r^2\overline{\alpha_t}^{,2}
  q'(\alpha_r)\overline{q'(\alpha_t)}}
 \delta_{1/(\alpha_r\overline{\alpha_t})}
\]
is a positive measure supported on $[0,1]$.
\end{lemma}

This is the necessary-measure consequence of the rational-symbol criterion
in \cite[Corollary~4.2 and Remark~4.3]{CGR}.  It is important here that equal
pole products are grouped as atoms of one measure.  Briefly, subnormality
makes the corresponding scalar sequence completely monotone, so it is a
Hausdorff moment sequence of a positive measure on $[0,1]$.  Partial
fractions give the displayed finite complex measure with the same moments;
polynomial approximation on the compact union of the two supports makes the
representing measure unique.  Hence every grouped coefficient at a point
outside $[0,1]$ must vanish.

\subsection*{Proof Intuition}

For a regular polygon, root-of-unity orthogonality turns Costara's spectral
factor into the binomial $q(z)=z^N-\rho$.  The auxiliary Gram matrix is
circulant, so the discrete Fourier basis diagonalizes it.  This yields a
closed formula
\[
 G(z,w)=P(z\overline w)=\sum_{k=1}^N A_k(z\overline w)^k
\]
with every $A_k>0$.  Subnormality would force $P$ to vanish at all $N-1$
rotated off-diagonal pole products.  Those prescribed roots determine
$P(x)/x$ up to scale, but its first two coefficients disagree with the
explicit $A_k$ whenever $N\ge3$.  The exceptional equality at $N=2$ is
exactly the known antipodal subnormal case.

\section{The regular-polygon dichotomy}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $N\ge1$, $c>0$, $\xi\in\T$, and $\omega=e^{2\pi i/N}$.  Set
\[
 \mu_{N,c,\xi}=c\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\delta_{\xi\omega^j}.
\]
The Cauchy dual of multiplication by $z$ on $\cD(\mu_{N,c,\xi})$ is
subnormal if and only if $N\le2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Rotation of the measure gives unitary equivalence up to multiplication of the
operator by a unimodular scalar \cite[Proposition~7.1]{CGR}, so take $\xi=1$.
Write $\zeta_j=\omega^j$ and $s(z)=z^N-1$.  On $\T$, root-of-unity
orthogonality gives
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\left|\frac{s(z)}{z-\zeta_j}\right|^2=N^2.
\]
Consequently Costara's trigonometric polynomial is
\[
 |s(z)|^2+c\sum_j\left|\frac{s(z)}{z-\zeta_j}\right|^2
 =2+cN^2-z^N-\overline z^{,N}
 =\rho^{-1}|z^N-\rho|^2,                                      \tag{1}
\]
where $\rho>1$ is determined by
\[
 \rho+\rho^{-1}=2+cN^2,
 \qquad c=\frac{(\rho-1)^2}{N^2\rho}.                         \tag{2}
\]
Thus we may take
\[
 q(z)=z^N-\rho,\qquad
 O(z)=\sqrt\rho\,\frac{z^N-1}{z^N-\rho}.
\]

We now compute the numerator kernel $G$.  Costara's formula uses
\[
 f_j(z)=\frac{O(z)}{O'(\zeta_j)(z-\zeta_j)}
 =\frac{1-\rho}{N}\,
   \frac{\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\zeta_j^{-m}z^m}{q(z)}.              \tag{3}
\]
Let $\Gamma=(\langle f_i,f_j\rangle)$.  The formulas in
\cite[Lemmas~4.2--4.3]{Costara}, together with (2), give
\[
 \Gamma_{ii}=c\left(\frac{N-1}{2}+\frac{N}{\rho-1}\right),
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{ij}=c\,
 \frac{\zeta_i/\zeta_j}{1-\zeta_i/\zeta_j}\quad(i\ne j).     \tag{4}
\]
For $u_m=(\zeta_j^{-m})_{j=0}^{N-1}$, the elementary root-of-unity sum
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{\omega^{(m+1)k}}{1-\omega^k}
 =m-\frac{N-1}{2}
\]
(with a harmless reversal of indices) shows that
\[
 \Gamma u_m=c\left(\frac{N}{\rho-1}+m\right)u_m,
 \qquad 0\le m<N.                                             \tag{5}
\]
Since the $u_m$ are orthogonal and have squared norm $N$, (3)--(5) yield
\[
 q(z)\overline{q(w)}\sum_{i,j}
 \overline{(\Gamma^{-1})_{ji}}f_j(z)\overline{f_i(w)}
 =\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}h_m(z\overline w)^m,                         \tag{6}
\]
where
\[
 h_m=\frac{N\rho(\rho-1)}{N+m(\rho-1)}.                      \tag{7}
\]

The reproducing-kernel identity in \cite[Theorem~6.4]{CGR} says that
\[
 G(z,w)=q(z)\overline{q(w)}-ho s(z)\overline{s(w)}
 -(1-z\overline w)\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}h_m(z\overline w)^m.
\]
The first two terms reduce to
$(\rho-1)(\rho-(z\overline w)^N)$.  Since
$h_{m-1}-h_m$ telescopes, we obtain the exact diagonal polynomial
\[
 G(z,w)=P(z\overline w),\qquad
 P(x)=\sum_{k=1}^N A_kx^k,                                    \tag{8}
\]
with
\[
 A_k=\frac{N\rho(\rho-1)^2}
 {[N+(k-1)(\rho-1)][N+k(\rho-1)]}>0.                         \tag{9}
\]

Let $a=\rho^{1/N}$.  The roots of $q$ are
$\alpha_r=a\omega^r$.  For a nonzero residue $d\pmod N$, all $N$ ordered
pairs with $r-t=d$ have the same product
$x_d=\alpha_r\overline{\alpha_t}=a^2\omega^d$, and
$q'(\alpha_r)=N\alpha_r^{N-1}$.  Hence the coefficient of the grouped atom
at $1/x_d$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} is
\[
 C_d=\frac{P(x_d)}{N\rho^2x_d}.                               \tag{10}
\]
Because $d\ne0$, $1/x_d\notin[0,1]$.  If the Cauchy dual were subnormal,
Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} would therefore force
\[
 P(a^2\omega^d)=0,\qquad d=1,\ldots,N-1.                    \tag{11}
\]

Assume $N\ge3$.  The polynomial $R(x)=P(x)/x$ has degree $N-1$.
If (11) held, its $N-1$ distinct roots would give
\[
 R(x)=A_N\prod_{d=1}^{N-1}(x-a^2\omega^d)
 =A_N\frac{x^N-a^{2N}}{x-a^2}.
\]
Comparing its constant and linear coefficients yields
$A_2/A_1=a^{-2}=\rho^{-2/N}$.  But (9) gives
\[
 \frac{A_2}{A_1}=\frac{N}{N+2(\rho-1)}.                      \tag{12}
\]
These cannot agree.  Indeed, with $a>1$, agreement is equivalent to
\[
 a^2=1+\frac{2(a^N-1)}{N}.
\]
The right side minus the left side vanishes at $a=1$ and has derivative
$2a(a^{N-2}-1)>0$ for $a>1$ and $N>2$.  This contradiction proves
non-subnormality for every $N\ge3$.

For $N=1$, subnormality is the established one-atom theorem
\cite[Corollary~5.4]{CGR}.  For $N=2$, the two support points are antipodal,
and the established antipodal theorem applies for arbitrary positive masses
\cite[Theorem~2.4]{CGR}.  This proves the dichotomy.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
For every $c>0$, the Cauchy dual on
\(\cD(c\delta_1+c\delta_\omega+c\delta_{\omega^2})\),
where $\omega^3=1$ and $\omega\ne1$, is not subnormal.
\end{corollary}

This is the source theorem with the common mass upgraded from $c=1$ to every
$c>0$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} simultaneously extends it to every regular
polygon of rank at least three.

\section{Verification, limitations, and novelty}

The accompanying verifier independently constructs the circulant Gram matrix,
inverts it numerically, reconstructs the finite kernel, and checks (8)--(9)
for $N=2,3,4,5,8$ and several positive masses.  It also checks that the
forbidden values vanish for $N=2$ and fail to vanish for every tested
$N\ge3$.  These computations are sanity checks; the proof above is analytic.

\textbf{Scope limitation.}  The arbitrary three-point/three-weight conjecture
remains open here.  Unequal weights destroy the circulant structure, while
arbitrary support also destroys the binomial spectral factor.  Eight focused
upgrade attempts included direct general three-atom reconstruction,
optimization searches for exceptional subnormal candidates, and symbolic
work on unequal equilateral weights; none yielded a responsible full proof or
counterexample.

\textbf{Bounded novelty audit.}  The run indexes and primary-source arXiv
searches through 13 August 2026 were checked using arXiv:2103.10059,
2510.14004, and 2511.04565 and the phrases ``Cauchy dual subnormality,''
``three distinct points,'' ``rank three,'' ``regular $N$-gon,''
``equi-spaced points,'' and ``uniform measure.''  The search found the source,
the rational-symbol criterion, and rank-two results, but no statement of the
uniform regular-$N$-gon dichotomy.  Novelty is plausible, not certified.

\textbf{Human-review recommendation:} send to an operator theorist.  The
highest-value checks are the conjugation convention in (6), the DFT
eigenvalue indexing in (5), and the grouping calculation (10).  All three are
also covered by the independent numerical reconstruction.
