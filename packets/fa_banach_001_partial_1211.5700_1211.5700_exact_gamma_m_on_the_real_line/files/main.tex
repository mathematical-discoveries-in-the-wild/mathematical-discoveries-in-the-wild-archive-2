\textbf{Status: candidate partial result, likely valid.}
For every order $m\geq 1$, an explicit two-jet functional is constructed
for scalar $m$-fields on $\R$.  It gives the exact minimum
$\Lip(F^{(m)})$, admits an exact extension for arbitrary data sets, and
satisfies the source paper's properties $(P_0)$--$(P_5)$.  The problem in
dimensions $d\geq2$ remains open.}}
\end{center}

\section{Source question}

For $1$-fields on $\R^d$, Le Gruyer's functional $\Gamma^1$ is the exact
minimum Lipschitz constant of the gradient and satisfies the six structural
properties used by Hirn and Le Gruyer.  Section 2.4.5 of their paper then
states \cite{HL}:
\begin{quote}
``A similar result for $m$-fields, where $m\geq2$, is an open problem.  In
particular, it is unknown what the correct corresponding functional
$\Phi=\Gamma^m$ is.  It seems plausible, though, that such a functional will
satisfy the properties $(P_0)$--$(P_5)$.''
\end{quote}

  \caption{The $m$-field open problem in arXiv:1211.5700v3, PDF page 10,

This packet resolves that functional problem on the one-dimensional domain
$\R$, for all orders and arbitrary (not necessarily closed or finite) data
sets.  It does not claim a solution in higher-dimensional domains.

\section{Definitions and the proposed functional}

Let $\Pm$ be the real polynomials of degree at most $m$.  An $m$-field on
$E\subset\R$ is a map $f:E\to\Pm$.  Write the jet at $x$ as
\[
 f(x)(a)=\sum_{k=0}^m \frac{p_k(x)}{k!}(a-x)^k,
 \qquad p_k(x)=f(x)^{(k)}(x).
\]
For $F\in C^{m,1}(I)$, $\J_x^mF$ denotes its degree-$m$ Taylor polynomial
at $x$, and
\[
 \Lip_I(F^{(m)})=
 \sup_{s\ne t\in I}\frac{|F^{(m)}(s)-F^{(m)}(t)|}{|s-t|}.
\]

For $x<y$, $P,Q\in\Pm$, define the two-jet cost
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defgamma}
 \Gamma_m(P,Q;x,y)=
 \min\left\{\Lip_{[x,y]}(F^{(m)}):
 \begin{array}{l}
 F\in C^{m,1}([x,y]),\\[-2pt]
 \J_x^mF=P,\ \J_y^mF=Q
 \end{array}\right\}.
\end{equation}
For $x>y$ use the symmetric definition, and set
$\Gamma_m(P,P;x,x)=0$.  For a field $f$ put
\[
 \Gamma_m(f;x,y)=\Gamma_m(f(x),f(y);x,y),\qquad
 \Gamma_m(f;E)=\sup_{x\ne y\in E}\Gamma_m(f;x,y),
\]
with the empty supremum equal to zero.

The word ``minimum'' in \eqref{eq:defgamma} is justified below.  More
importantly, the definition has an explicit finite-dimensional dual formula.

\begin{proposition}[Moment and dual formulas]\label{prop:dual}
Fix $x<y$, set $h=y-x$, and write
\[
 a_k=P^{(k)}(x),\qquad b_k=Q^{(k)}(y),\qquad 0\leq k\leq m.
\]
For $0\leq r\leq m$, define the endpoint discrepancy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta}
 d_r=b_{m-r}-\sum_{j=0}^{r}\frac{a_{m-r+j}h^j}{j!}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
 \Gamma_m(P,Q;x,y)
 &=\min\left\{\|w\|_{L^\infty(0,h)}:
 d_r=\int_0^h\frac{(h-s)^r}{r!}w(s)\,ds,
 \ 0\leq r\leq m\right\}\label{eq:moment}\\
 &=\sup_{\lambda\in\R^{m+1}\setminus\{0\}}
 \frac{\left|\sum_{r=0}^m\lambda_r d_r\right|}
 {\displaystyle\int_0^h
 \left|\sum_{r=0}^m\lambda_r\frac{(h-s)^r}{r!}\right|\,ds}.
 \label{eq:dual}
\end{align}
Both extrema are attained.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $F$ is feasible in \eqref{eq:defgamma}, its Lipschitz function $F^{(m)}$
has an almost-everywhere derivative
$w(s)=F^{(m+1)}(x+s)\in L^\infty(0,h)$, and
$\Lip(F^{(m)})=\|w\|_\infty$.  Repeated integration from $x$ gives, for
$0\leq r\leq m$,
\[
 F^{(m-r)}(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\frac{a_{m-r+j}h^j}{j!}
 +\int_0^h\frac{(h-s)^r}{r!}w(s)\,ds.
\]
Thus the endpoint conditions are exactly the moment equations in
\eqref{eq:moment}.  Conversely, integrating any feasible $w$ reconstructs a
feasible $F$ with the same norm.  The moment map
\[
 T:L^\infty(0,h)\longrightarrow\R^{m+1},\qquad
 (Tw)_r=\int_0^h\frac{(h-s)^r}{r!}w(s)\,ds,
\]
is onto: its adjoint is injective because a polynomial that vanishes almost
everywhere on an interval is zero.  Hence all two-jet data are feasible.

Formula \eqref{eq:dual} is the standard dual formula for the quotient norm
$\inf_{Tw=d}\|w\|_\infty$, since
\[
 T^*\lambda(s)=\sum_{r=0}^m
 \lambda_r\frac{(h-s)^r}{r!}
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \|T^*\lambda\|_{L^1}
 =\int_0^h|T^*\lambda(s)|\,ds.
\]
Weak-* compactness of bounded subsets of $L^\infty$ and weak-* continuity
of $T$ give a primal minimizer.  The dual supremum is attained after
normalizing its denominator, because the dual space is finite-dimensional.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof intuition}

On a line, removing a closed data set leaves disjoint open intervals.  The
two endpoint jets on each bounded gap impose exactly the moment constraints
in Proposition~\ref{prop:dual}; a norm-minimizing control $w=F^{(m+1)}$
fills that gap.  Every gap can be filled independently because neighboring
pieces already agree through order $m$ at their common endpoint.  The
Lipschitz constant of $F^{(m)}$ is therefore the maximum of the local costs,
with no loss.

The only issue for arbitrary data sets is compatibility at accumulation
points.  A two-point interpolant of cost $K$ yields sharp Taylor remainders
of order $K|x-y|^{m+1-k}$.  Those estimates force every prescribed
derivative value to extend continuously to the closure.  They also make the
gapwise pasting $C^m$ at every accumulation point.  This ordered-gap
mechanism is specific to one dimension and is exactly what is unavailable
in $\R^d$, $d\geq2$.

\section{Exact extension on the real line}

\begin{theorem}[Exact all-orders extension on $\R$]\label{thm:extension}
Let $m\geq1$, let $E\subset\R$, and let $f:E\to\Pm$ be an arbitrary
$m$-field with
\[
 K:=\Gamma_m(f;E)<\infty.
\]
Then there is $F\in C^{m,1}(\R)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact}
 \J_x^mF=f(x)\quad(x\in E),\qquad
 \Lip_{\R}(F^{(m)})=K.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every $D$ with $E\subset D\subset\R$, the field
$U(z)=\J_z^mF$ on $D$ is an exact minimal extension:
\[
 \Gamma_m(U;D)=\Gamma_m(f;E).
\]
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:traceidentity}
 \Gamma_m(f;E)=
 \min\{\Lip_{\R}(G^{(m)}):G\in C^{m,1}(\R),\ 
 \J_x^mG=f(x)\ (x\in E)\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We may assume that $E$ has at least two points; the empty and one-point cases
are obtained from a polynomial of degree at most $m$.

\emph{Step 1: sharp Whitney estimates.}
For $x<y$ in $E$, choose a minimizer in \eqref{eq:defgamma}.  Repeated
integration, now separating the constant value at $x$ from the variation of
the $m$th derivative, gives for every $0\leq k\leq m$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:whitney}
 \left|p_k(y)-\sum_{j=0}^{m-k}
 \frac{p_{k+j}(x)(y-x)^j}{j!}\right|
 \leq \frac{K|y-x|^{m+1-k}}{(m+1-k)!}.
\end{equation}
For $k=m$ this is simply
$|p_m(y)-p_m(x)|\leq K|y-x|$.  It follows successively from
\eqref{eq:whitney}, starting at $k=m$, that every $p_k$ is continuous on
$E$.  Hence all $p_k$ extend uniquely and continuously to
$C=\overline E$.  Formula \eqref{eq:dual}, viewed after affine
normalization to $[0,1]$, is continuous in two jets at distinct points.
Passing to limits in pairs from $E$ therefore preserves
$\Gamma_m\leq K$ on $C$ and preserves \eqref{eq:whitney}.

\emph{Step 2: fill the gaps of $C$.}
For each bounded component $(\alpha,\beta)$ of $\R\setminus C$, choose a
minimizer $F_{\alpha\beta}$ for the two endpoint jets.  Its cost is at most
$K$.  On an unbounded component use the degree-$m$ polynomial determined by
the jet at its finite endpoint.  On $C$ prescribe
$F^{(k)}(x)=p_k(x)$.

For completeness, we verify the pasting at accumulation points.  Define
$G_k$ to equal $p_k$ on $C$ and $F_{\alpha\beta}^{(k)}$ on each gap.  If
$t$ in a gap tends to $c\in C$, choose the endpoint $e$ of that gap lying
between $c$ and $t$.  Then $e\to c$, and the Taylor estimate inside the
gap, together with continuity of the $p_j$ on $C$, gives
$G_k(t)\to p_k(c)$.  Thus every $G_k$ is continuous.  The same decomposition
\[
 G_k(t)-p_k(c)=\bigl(G_k(t)-p_k(e)\bigr)
                 +\bigl(p_k(e)-p_k(c)\bigr),
\]
using the first-order part of the Taylor expansions at $e$ and $c$, yields
\[
 G_k(t)-p_k(c)=p_{k+1}(c)(t-c)+o(|t-c|)
 \quad(0\leq k<m).
\]
Therefore $G_k'=G_{k+1}$ also at points of $C$.  The pasted scalar function
$F=G_0$ belongs to $C^m(\R)$ and has the prescribed jets.

It remains to check the global Lipschitz bound.  The function $G_m$ is
$K$-Lipschitz on $C$ by \eqref{eq:whitney} and on every complementary gap
by construction.  If $s<t$ lie in different pieces, insert the right
endpoint of the piece containing $s$ and the left endpoint of the piece
containing $t$.  Both inserted points lie in $C$, and the three Lipschitz
bounds add over subintervals whose lengths sum to $t-s$.  Hence
\[
 |G_m(t)-G_m(s)|\leq K(t-s).
\]
Thus $F\in C^{m,1}(\R)$ and $\Lip(F^{(m)})\leq K$.

\emph{Step 3: exactness.}
Every global interpolant $G$ restricts on $[x,y]$ to a competitor in
\eqref{eq:defgamma}, so
$\Gamma_m(f;x,y)\leq\Lip(G^{(m)})$.  Taking the supremum shows that no
global interpolant has seminorm below $K$.  The constructed $F$ therefore
has equality in \eqref{eq:exact} and proves \eqref{eq:traceidentity}.

Finally, for $U(z)=\J_z^mF$ on $D$, restriction of $F$ to every pair gives
$\Gamma_m(U;D)\leq K$, while the old pairs in $E$ give the reverse
inequality.  This proves exact minimal extension to every $D$.
\end{proof}

\section{The source axioms P0--P5}

Equip $\Pm$ with any Euclidean coefficient metric.  The next proposition
checks that the functional above fits the framework of \cite{HL} on the
domain space $\R$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:axioms}
For scalar $m$-fields on $\R$, the functional $\Phi=\Gamma_m$ satisfies
properties $(P_0)$--$(P_5)$ of Hirn--Le Gruyer.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Symmetry and nonnegativity $(P_0)$ follow from time reversal in
\eqref{eq:defgamma}; pointwise evaluation $(P_1)$ is the definition of
$\Gamma_m(f;E)$.  The final assertion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:extension} is exactly the minimal-extension property
$(P_2)$.

For $(P_3)$, suppose $x<y$ and $[x,y]\subset\dom(f)$.  For any
$z\in[x,y]$, paste minimizers for the pairs $(x,z)$ and $(z,y)$.  Their jets
through order $m$ agree at $z$, so the pasted function is in
$C^{m,1}([x,y])$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chasles}
 \Gamma_m(f;x,y)\leq
 \max\{\Gamma_m(f;x,z),\Gamma_m(f;z,y)\}.
\end{equation}
Taking $z=(1-t)x+ty$ gives the required monotone curve and, since
\eqref{eq:chasles} holds for every $t$, the required infimum inequality.

For $(P_5)$, if $\Gamma_m(f;E)=K<\infty$, estimate
\eqref{eq:whitney} shows first that $p_m$ is Lipschitz and then that every
$p_k$ is continuous.  Converting centered Taylor coefficients to ordinary
polynomial coefficients is continuous in $(x,p_0,\ldots,p_m)$, so
$f:E\to\Pm$ is continuous.

For $(P_4)$, normalize a pair of distinct points affinely to $0$ and $1$.
On $\R^{2m+2}$, the resulting two-jet cost is a finite seminorm: homogeneity
is clear, and subadditivity follows by adding two interpolants.  Every finite
seminorm on a finite-dimensional space is continuous.  Undoing the affine
normalization multiplies the cost by $|x-y|^{-(m+1)}$ and transforms jet
coordinates continuously as long as $x\ne y$.  Property $(P_5)$ gives
$f(z)\to f(y)$ as $z\to y$ within the domain; hence
\[
 \Gamma_m(f;x,z)\longrightarrow\Gamma_m(f;x,y),
\]
which is $(P_4)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One-dimensional higher-order quasi-AMLEs]
For scalar $m$-fields on $\R$, the quasi-AMLE existence theorem of
Hirn--Le Gruyer applies with $\Phi=\Gamma_m$ for every $m\geq1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The real line has the metric hypotheses of \cite{HL}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:axioms} supplies $(P_0)$--$(P_5)$.  This is precisely
the input of their main theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{Upgrade attempts, scope, and novelty check}

The argument was pushed through five increasingly strong stages: finite
ordered data; arbitrary data via closure and gap filling; an explicit
$L^\infty$ moment problem; the finite-dimensional dual formula
\eqref{eq:dual}; and verification of all six source axioms, yielding the
quasi-AMLE corollary.  A further attempt to pass to $d\geq2$ did not close.
The line proof uses the total order of $\R$: complementary components have
two endpoints and can be filled independently.  In higher dimensions,
two-point interpolants on different segments cannot be pasted into a
single $C^{m,1}$ function, and no analogue of the exact gap decomposition
was found.  General Whitney extension theory supplies extensions with
dimension- and order-dependent constants, but that does not prove the exact
pairwise identity required here.

The result is therefore a solved one-dimensional subcase, not a solution of
the source's unrestricted $\R^d$ question.  A bounded novelty search was
performed on arXiv using the source id and the phrases ``$m$-fields
$\Gamma^m$'', ``exact $C^{m,1}$ extension'', ``one-dimensional Whitney
jets'', ``minimal Lipschitz $m$th derivative'', and ``Hermite interpolation
$L^\infty$''.  The closest items found were general Whitney-type and
definable extension theorems \cite{S,PR}; they provide existence or bounded
extension results, not this exact one-dimensional pairwise functional and
the verification of $(P_0)$--$(P_5)$.  No exact match was found.  This is a
bounded search, so novelty confidence is moderate rather than definitive.

Human review should focus on the closure-and-gap pasting in
Theorem~\ref{thm:extension} and on whether the intended meaning of
``correct functional'' in the source imposes any convention beyond
pairwise dependence on the two endpoint jets.  The functional here is
genuinely pair-local and has the explicit formula \eqref{eq:dual}; it is not
the circular construction obtained by assigning the same global trace norm
to every pair.
