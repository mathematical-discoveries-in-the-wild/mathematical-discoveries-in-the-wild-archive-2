\section*{The source question}

Problem~5 of Repov\v{s}--Semenov, on printed and PDF page~32 of
arXiv:1401.2257, asks: if $A$ and $B$ are closed convex subsets of a Banach
space and $C=A+B$, must there be continuous maps
\[
 a:C\longrightarrow A,\qquad b:C\longrightarrow B,
 \qquad c=a(c)+b(c)\quad(c\in C)?
\]

\section*{Exact prior answer}

Balashov--Repov\v{s}, \emph{On the splitting problem for selections},
J. Math. Anal. Appl. \textbf{355} (2009), 277--287,
arXiv:\allowbreak0807.3104, quotes this question on PDF page~1 and immediately states
that the answer is negative in every dimension at least three.

Their Example~1.1, on PDF pages~1--2, gives compact convex sets $A,B\subset
\mathbb R^3$.  Here $B$ is the vertical segment joining $(0,0,0)$ and
$(0,0,1)$, while $A$ is assembled from the convex hulls of the upper and
lower semicircles in the planes $z=1$ and $z=0$, together with the opposite
endpoints in the other plane. Put $C=A+B$ and $c_0=(1,0,1)$.

The boundary of $C$ contains two arcs $\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2$ ending at $c_0$.
For every $c$ on either punctured arc the decomposition
$c=a(c)+b(c)$ with $a(c)\in A$ and $b(c)\in B$ is unique.  Along the first arc,
\[
 \lim_{c\to c_0,\ c\in\Gamma_1}a(c)=(1,0,0),
\]
whereas along the second,
\[
 \lim_{c\to c_0,\ c\in\Gamma_2}a(c)=(1,0,1).
\]
Thus no admissible $a$ can be continuous at $c_0$, and consequently no pair
of continuous splitting maps can exist.

\section*{Disposition}

This is the same universal question, with a stronger counterexample than is
needed: the ambient Banach space is finite-dimensional and both summands are
compact convex sets. The run should therefore record the target as
\texttt{literature\_already\_answered}, not as an original counterexample.
