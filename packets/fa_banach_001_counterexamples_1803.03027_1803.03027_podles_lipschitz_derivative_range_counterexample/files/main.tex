\section{Source question}

Aguilar and Kaad study the D\k{a}browski--Sitarz spectral triple on the
standard Podle\'s sphere in \cite{AguilarKaad}.  On the coordinate algebra,
the commutator has off-diagonal entries $\partial_1$ and $\partial_2$ taking
values in $\SU$.  They extend these maps to the maximal Lipschitz algebra as
bounded operators $H_+\to H_-$ and $H_-\to H_+$, respectively, and ask:

\begin{quote}
``We do not know whether the images of $\partial_1$ and $\partial_2$ are
contained in the $C^*$-algebra $SU_q(2)$ even though this holds for their
restrictions to the coordinate algebra $\mathcal O(S_q^2)$.''
\end{quote}

This is Section 3, official PDF page 7.  The complete source passage is shown
in Figure~\ref{fig:source}.

\section{Definitions and statement}

Fix $q\in(0,1)$.  Recall that $\SU$ is generated by $a,b$ with
\[
 ba=qab,\qquad b^*a=qab^*,\qquad bb^*=b^*b,
 \qquad a^*a+q^2bb^*=1=aa^*+bb^*.
\]
The standard sphere $\Sq\subseteq\SU$ is generated by
$A=b^*b$ and $B=ab^*$.  Put
\[
 p_k=\chi_{\{q^{2k}\}}(A),\qquad k\in\N_0.
\]
Thus $C^*(A)\cong C(\{0\}\cup\{q^{2k}:k\geq0\})$.  For
$f\in C(\operatorname{Sp}(A))$, write
\begin{equation}
 f_k=f(q^{2k}),\qquad f_\infty=f(0),\qquad
 w_k(f)=\frac{q^{-k}\sqrt{1-q^{2k+2}}}{1-q^2}
             (f_k-f_{k+1}).                                      \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
We use the source's identification of an element of $\SU$ with its left
multiplication operator between the homogeneous GNS subspaces.

\begin{theorem}[Diagonal domain and range characterization]\label{thm:main}
For every $f\in C(\operatorname{Sp}(A))$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $f(A)\in\Lip_{D_q}(\Sq)$ if and only if
      $w(f)=(w_k(f))_{k\geq0}\in\ell^\infty$.
\item When these conditions hold,
\[
 \partial_1(f(A))\in\SU
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w(f)\in c_0.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the source question has a negative answer for both
$\partial_1$ and $\partial_2$.
\end{theorem}

An explicit common witness for both maps is the selfadjoint element $x=f(A)$
defined by $f(0)=0$ and
\begin{equation}
 f(q^{2k})=
 \sum_{j=k}^{\infty}
 \frac{(1-q^2)q^j(-1)^j}{\sqrt{1-q^{2j+2}}}.                     \label{eq:witness}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $w_k(f)=(-1)^k$.

\section{Proof intuition}

On $C^*(A)$, the derivation is an ordinary finite-difference operator in
disguise.  The factor in \eqref{eq:weights} is exactly the norm normalization that turns
its mutually orthogonal columns into a weighted backward shift.  Bounded
weights therefore describe the \emph{maximal} commutator domain.  Membership
in the original $C^*$-algebra is stricter.

In the faithful Toeplitz representation of $\SU$, the derivative becomes
\[
 \sum_{k\geq0}w_k E_{k,k+1}\otimes U^*.
\]
A Toeplitz weighted shift must have convergent weights.  The quotient of
$\SU$ is independent of the second circle variable, while the displayed
operator retains $U^*$; hence the limit must actually be zero.  This is the
gap between bounded commutators and the graph closure of the coordinate
derivation.

\section{Proof}

We use two computations from \cite{AguilarKaad}.  First,
\begin{equation}
 \partial_1(p_k)=\frac{q^{-2k}p_k-q^{-2(k-1)}p_{k-1}}{1-q^2}\,b^*a^*,
                                                               \label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
with the second term absent for $k=0$ (Lemma 5.3 there).  Second, their
Proposition 6.7 gives the right-column formula for every element of the
maximal Lipschitz algebra.  Specializing it to $f(A)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 (1-q^2)\partial_1(f(A))p_{k+1}
   ={}&(f_k-f_{k+1})q^{-3k-2}\sqrt{1-q^{2k+2}}\\
     &\hspace{25mm}\cdot f_{k,k+1}(b^*)^2 ,
 \end{split}                                                    \label{eq:column}
\end{equation}
where $f_{r,s}$ are the source's matrix units.  Proposition 6.2 there says
\[
 \|f_{k,k+1}(b^*)^2\|=q^{2k+2}.
\]
It follows immediately from \eqref{eq:column} that
\begin{equation}
 \|\partial_1(f(A))p_{k+1}\|=|w_k(f)|.                         \label{eq:columnnorm}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{The maximal domain}

If $f(A)$ is Lipschitz, \eqref{eq:columnnorm} proves that $w(f)$ is bounded.
Conversely, suppose $\|w(f)\|_\infty=M<\infty$.  Telescoping
\eqref{eq:weights} yields
\begin{equation}
 f_k-f_\infty=(1-q^2)\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}
 \frac{q^j w_j(f)}{\sqrt{1-q^{2j+2}}}.                         \label{eq:telescope}
\end{equation}
In particular, $f_k-f_\infty=O(q^k)$.

Define the finite truncations
\[
 x_N=f_\infty1+\sum_{k=0}^N(f_k-f_\infty)p_k.
\]
Then $x_N\to f(A)$ in norm.  All interior normalized derivative weights of
$x_N$ are $w_0,\ldots,w_{N-1}$.  Its sole boundary weight is
\[
 \beta_N=\frac{q^{-N}\sqrt{1-q^{2N+2}}}{1-q^2}(f_N-f_\infty).
\]
By \eqref{eq:telescope}, monotonicity of $1-q^{2j+2}$ gives
\begin{equation}
 |\beta_N|\leq M\sum_{j=N}^{\infty}q^{j-N}=\frac{M}{1-q}.       \label{eq:boundary}
\end{equation}
The same bound holds for the other off-diagonal derivative because
$\partial_2(z)=-\partial_1(z^*)^*$.  Hence the commutator seminorms of
$x_N$ are uniformly bounded.  The commutator seminorm is lower
semicontinuous \cite[Lemma 2.3]{AguilarKaad}, so
$f(A)\in\Lip_{D_q}(\Sq)$.  This proves part 1.

\subsection*{The $C^*$-range}

We next record the concrete obstruction.  Let $U$ be multiplication by the
coordinate function on $L^2(\T)$, let $(e_k)$ be the standard basis of
$\ell^2(\N_0)$, and consider the standard faithful representation
$\rho$ of $\SU$ \cite[Chapter 4]{KlimykSchmudgen}:
\begin{align}
 \rho(a)(e_k\otimes\xi)
   &=\sqrt{1-q^{2k+2}}\,e_{k+1}\otimes\xi,\notag\\
 \rho(b)(e_k\otimes\xi)&=q^k e_k\otimes U\xi.                 \label{eq:rho}
\end{align}
Thus $\rho(p_k)=E_{k,k}\otimes1$, and a direct use of the source's
normalization of $f_{k,k+1}$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \rho\!\left(
 \frac{(f_k-f_{k+1})q^{-3k-2}\sqrt{1-q^{2k+2}}}{1-q^2}
 f_{k,k+1}(b^*)^2\right)
 =w_k(f)E_{k,k+1}\otimes U^*.                                  \label{eq:rhocolumn}
\end{equation}

Suppose $\partial_1(f(A))$ were left multiplication by some $y\in\SU$.
Equation \eqref{eq:column} then determines $yp_{k+1}$ as the element inside
\eqref{eq:rhocolumn}; the $k=0$ case of the source's full column formula also
gives $\partial_1(f(A))p_0=0$.
There is no faithfulness gap here.  If $z\in\SU$ acts as zero on $H_+$,
then in particular $za=zb=0$, because $a,b\in\mathcal A_1\subset H_+$;
faithfulness of the Haar representation and $aa^*+bb^*=1$ give
\begin{equation}
 z=z(aa^*+bb^*)=(za)a^*+(zb)b^*=0.                              \label{eq:faithful}
\end{equation}
Since $\sum p_k=1$ strongly in \eqref{eq:rho}, all columns together force
\begin{equation}
 \rho(y)=W_w\otimes U^*,\qquad
 W_w=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}w_k E_{k,k+1}.                          \label{eq:weightedshift}
\end{equation}

We now identify exactly when \eqref{eq:weightedshift} belongs to $\rho(\SU)$.  Let
$\mathcal T=C^*(S)$ be the Toeplitz algebra.  Since the weights of $\rho(a)$
tend to one, $\rho(a)-S\otimes1$ is compact in the first factor.  Spectral
projections of $\rho(A)$, powers of $\rho(b)$, and powers of $\rho(a)$
generate every $E_{ij}\otimes U^m$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \rho(\SU)=C^*(S\otimes1,\K\otimes C(\T))
 =\{X\in\mathcal T\otimes C(\T):
       (\sigma\otimes\mathrm{id})(X)\in C(\T)\otimes1\},       \label{eq:pullback}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma:\mathcal T\to C(\T)$ is the Toeplitz symbol map.
This also proves the needed pullback description directly from \eqref{eq:rho}.

If \eqref{eq:weightedshift} belongs to \eqref{eq:pullback}, evaluation in the
second circle variable shows
$W_w\in\mathcal T$.  Every $T\in\mathcal T$ has the form $T_g+K$ with
$g\in C(\T)$ and $K$ compact.  Hence
$\langle Te_{k+1},e_k\rangle$ converges to the corresponding Fourier
coefficient of $g$.  Applied to $W_w$, this proves $w_k\to L$ for some
$L\in\C$.  Then $W_w-LS^*$ is compact and
\begin{equation}
 (\sigma\otimes\mathrm{id})(W_w\otimes U^*)
       =L\,\overline\zeta\otimes\overline u.                   \label{eq:quotient}
\end{equation}
The pullback condition in \eqref{eq:pullback} says that this function is independent of
$u$, so $L=0$.  Thus $w\in c_0$ is necessary.

If $w\in c_0$, then $W_w$ is compact, so
$W_w\otimes U^*\in\K\otimes C(\T)\subseteq\rho(\SU)$.  Equivalently, the
orthogonal-column series in \eqref{eq:column} converges in norm, and its limit is the
maximal derivative.  Therefore $w\in c_0$ is also sufficient.  Part 2 is
proved.

\subsection*{The explicit witness and both derivatives}

The series in \eqref{eq:witness} converges absolutely and is $O(q^k)$, so it defines a
real continuous $f$ on $\operatorname{Sp}(A)$ with $f(0)=0$.  Its successive
difference is its first summand, and \eqref{eq:weights} gives
$w_k(f)=(-1)^k$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} now gives
\[
 x=f(A)\in\Lip_{D_q}(\Sq),\qquad \partial_1(x)\notin\SU.
\]
Since $x=x^*$ and $\partial_2(x)=-\partial_1(x)^*$, membership of
$\partial_2(x)$ in the $*$-algebra $\SU$ would imply membership of
$\partial_1(x)$.  Hence $\partial_2(x)\notin\SU$ as well.  This completes
the negative answer. \qed

\section{Verification and limitations}

The accompanying script checks \eqref{eq:weights}--\eqref{eq:witness}, the finite weighted-shift
matrix entries, and the uniform boundary bound \eqref{eq:boundary} for four deformation
parameters.  These checks audit indexing and normalization only; the proof is
the analytic argument above.

The result concerns the maximal Lipschitz algebra used in the source.  It is
compatible with the fact that the graph closure of the coordinate derivation
does take values in $\SU$: on the diagonal slice that smaller graph domain is
exactly the additional condition $w\in c_0$.

A bounded literature search on 13 August 2026 used local run indexes and
fresh searches for the exact quoted sentence, the title and authors,
``Podle\'s Lipschitz algebra'', ``maximal Lipschitz'', ``derivative range'',
and ``$SU_q(2)$''.  It found no later explicit answer to the inclusion
question.  The directly relevant later paper \cite{Approximation} distinguishes
the graph-closure algebra from the maximal Lipschitz algebra and explains that
maximal derivatives generally require a von Neumann-algebraic framework.  Its
Theorem B approximates in $C^*$-norm with a uniform Lip bound, not in graph
norm; it neither states the original $SU_q(2)$ question nor gives the explicit
counterexample or the $\ell^\infty/c_0$ characterization above.  It therefore
strongly anticipates the negative mechanism.  Novelty confidence is
moderate-low for the qualitative yes/no answer and moderate for the precise
diagonal theorem.

The separate question in the source introduction about $q\to1$ convergence
was answered in \cite{Convergence}; that does not overlap the result here.

The highest-priority human checks are \eqref{eq:rhocolumn}, including its phase, and the
faithfulness step \eqref{eq:faithful}.  The Toeplitz pullback argument after those points is
standard and elementary.
