\noindent\textbf{Status: literature already answered (negative).}

\section*{Original question}
Baillon, Combettes, and Cominetti \cite{BCC} discuss De Pierro's conjecture
in Section 3 (source PDF p.~10): as the relaxation parameter
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the limit cycles of under-relaxed cyclic
projections onto finitely many closed convex sets should shrink to a least
squares solution.  They state that the conjecture remained open for general
convex sets, and later prove it for $m=2$ closed convex sets in Euclidean
spaces (Corollary in Section 3.3, source PDF p.~13).

\section*{Exact later answer}
Cominetti, Roshchina, and Williamson \cite{CRW} explicitly identify the same
De Pierro conjecture and give a counterexample.  Their construction consists
of three compact convex sets in $\mathbb R^3$ for which the
$\varepsilon$-under-relaxed cycles do not converge as
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$.  Hence the unrestricted conjecture is false.

This is an explicit same-problem answer known to the supporting authors, not
an agent-derived implication.  It does not alter the original paper's
positive results under additional hypotheses or its complete two-set
Euclidean theorem.

\section*{Search evidence}
An exact-title and keyword search for ``De Pierro's conjecture'',
``under-relaxed cyclic projections'', and ``counterexample'' located
arXiv:1801.03216.  Its title and abstract explicitly state the disproof, so no
