\noindent\textbf{Status.} Candidate full counterexample, likely valid, to
Shimorin's question as quoted by Chavan and Trivedi.

\section{Question and answer}
For $T\in\mathcal B(\cH)$ put
\[
 \Delta_{r,T}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}(-1)^k\binom rk T^{*k}T^k.
\]
The source calls $T$ norm-increasing when $\Delta_{1,T}\leq0$, and
$r$-concave when $(-1)^r\Delta_{r,T}\leq0$.  It recalls Shimorin's
question: must every norm-increasing $r$-concave operator, $r\geq3$, have
Wold-type decomposition?

\begin{theorem}
There is a bounded analytic norm-increasing $4$-isometry $S$ which does not
have Wold-type decomposition.  Consequently Shimorin's question has a
negative answer.  The same $S$ is an $r$-isometry, hence $r$-concave, for
every $r\geq4$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof intuition}
Chavan and Trivedi construct norm-increasing shifts without Wold
decomposition on a rootless quasi-Brownian tree.  Quadratic moments make the
third defect vanish off the tree's spine.  Their constant-coefficient example
has a nonzero third defect on the spine and therefore is only a
$3$-expansion, with the wrong sign for Shimorin's problem.

The extra observation is to ask for the \emph{next} defect to vanish.  Since
\[
 \Delta_{4,S}=\Delta_{3,S}-S^*\Delta_{3,S}S,
\]
the spine values of the third defect need only be transported by the squared
spine weights.  Changing one linear coefficient, at level $m=2$, makes those
values exactly $-2$ below that level and $-2/m$ above it.  This is precisely
the transport law.  The coefficient change leaves intact the source's
convergent Cauchy-dual series, which is the obstruction to Wold
decomposition.

\section{The weighted shift}
Let $V=\N\times\Z$.  Give $V$ the rootless directed-tree structure
\[
 \operatorname{Chi}(0,m)=\{(0,m-1),(1,m)\},\qquad
 \operatorname{Chi}(n,m)=\{(n+1,m)\}\quad(n\geq1).
\]
Set
\[
 a_m=\begin{cases}1,&m=2,\\2,&m\ne2,\end{cases}
 \qquad p_m(j)=1+a_mj+j^2\quad(j\in\N).
\]
The weight indexed by a child $(n,m)$ is
\[
 \lambda_{(n,m)}=
 \begin{cases}
 \sqrt{p_m(n-1)/p_m(n-2)},&n\geq2,\\
 \sqrt{m/(m+1)},&n=0,\ m\geq1,\\
 1/\sqrt m,&n=1,\ m\geq1,\\
 1,&n\in\{0,1\},\ m\leq0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Define $Se_v=\sum_{w\in\operatorname{Chi}(v)}\lambda_we_w$ on
$\ell^2(V)$.

These are the weights of Proposition~5.1 of the source, with quadratic
coefficients bounded above and with quadratic coefficient uniformly equal to
one.  For completeness, the one-step norms are
\[
 \|Se_{(0,m)}\|^2=\begin{cases}1,&m\geq2,\\2,&m\leq1,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \|Se_{(n,m)}\|^2=\frac{p_m(n)}{p_m(n-1)}\geq1\quad(n\geq1).
\]
Moreover $p_m(n)/p_m(n-1)\leq4$.  Thus $S$ is bounded and
$S^*S\geq I$, so it is norm-increasing and left-invertible.

\section{Exact fourth-defect computation}
Descendant sets at a fixed depth are disjoint for distinct vertices.  Hence
$S^{*k}S^k$, and therefore every $\Delta_{r,S}$, is diagonal in the canonical
basis.

If $n\geq1$, direct telescoping gives
\[
 \|S^ke_{(n,m)}\|^2=\frac{p_m(n+k-1)}{p_m(n-1)}.
\]
This is quadratic in $k$, so both its third and fourth finite differences
vanish.  Thus $\Delta_{3,S}$ and $\Delta_{4,S}$ vanish off the spine.

Let
\[
 d_m=\langle\Delta_{3,S}e_{(0,m)},e_{(0,m)}\rangle.
\]
Substitution of $b_m=1$ and the above $a_m$ in the exact spine calculation
from the source gives
\[
 d_m=\begin{cases}-2,&m\leq1,\\-2/m,&m\geq2.
 \end{cases}
\]
The branch child $(1,m)$ has zero third-defect entry.  The squared weight of
the spine child $(0,m-1)$ is
\[
 \lambda_{(0,m-1)}^2=
 \begin{cases}1,&m\leq1,\\(m-1)/m,&m\geq2.
 \end{cases}
\]
Using $\Delta_{4,S}=\Delta_{3,S}-S^*\Delta_{3,S}S$, its spine entry is
therefore
\[
 d_m-\lambda_{(0,m-1)}^2d_{m-1}=0
\]
for every $m\in\Z$: for $m\leq1$ both $d$'s equal $-2$; for $m=2$ the
product is $\tfrac12(-2)=-1=d_2$; and for $m\geq3$ it is
$\frac{m-1}{m}\frac{-2}{m-1}=-2/m=d_m$.  Every diagonal entry of
$\Delta_{4,S}$ is zero, hence $\Delta_{4,S}=0$.

Finally, $\Delta_{r+1,S}=\Delta_{r,S}-S^*\Delta_{r,S}S$, so
$\Delta_{r,S}=0$ for all $r\geq4$.

\section{Analyticity and failure of Wold decomposition}
The source's proof of Proposition~5.1 applies verbatim.  Its lower bound for
$\alpha_\lambda((0,m))$ diverges because every $p_m(j)\geq1$; hence $S$ is
analytic.  For the Cauchy-dual weights it computes
\[
 \alpha_{\lambda'}((0,0))
   =\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{p_{n+1}(n-1)}
   =1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2}
   =\frac{\pi^2}{6}<\infty.
\]
Theorem~1.3 of the source says that an analytic shift in this setting has
Wold-type decomposition exactly when its Cauchy dual is analytic, equivalently
when these $\alpha_{\lambda'}$ series diverge.  Thus $S$ has no Wold-type
decomposition.  This proves the theorem. \qed

\section{Scope and verification}
The universal question is fully answered in the negative; no claim is made
about the still sharper order-$3$ case.  Exact rational code independently
propagates the tree weights, checks norm increase and $\Delta_4=0$ on a large
two-dimensional window, and verifies the displayed third-defect formula.

The earlier arXiv:2212.04446 claimed an order-$3$ counterexample, but its v3
metadata says that the paper was withdrawn because the main proof has a gap.
Bounded exact-phrase and structural searches found no prior order-$4$
counterexample.  Novelty is therefore plausible but provisional.
