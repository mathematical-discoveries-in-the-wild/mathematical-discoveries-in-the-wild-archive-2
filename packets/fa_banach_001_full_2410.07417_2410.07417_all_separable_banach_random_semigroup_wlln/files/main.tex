\section{Source problem}

Dzhenzher and Sakbaev study weakly measurable random generators on
$\ell_p$, $1\leq p\leq2$, whose operator norms are uniformly bounded.  Their
Theorem 2.4 proves convergence in the strong topology of $\ell_2$.  On page 4
they formulate the following conjecture \cite{DS24}:

\begin{quote}
Under the assumptions of Theorem 2.4,
$e^{A_1t/n}\cdots e^{A_nt/n}$ converges in probability in SOT of $\ell_p$ to
$e^{(\E A)t}$ uniformly for $t$ in any compact interval.
\end{quote}

Their precise meaning is that for every $x\in\ell_p$, $T>0$, and
$\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 \Pp\!\left\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}
 \left\|\left(e^{A_1t/n}\cdots e^{A_nt/n}-e^{(\E A)t}\right)x\right\|_p
 >\varepsilon\right\}\longrightarrow0.
\]

\section{Proof intuition}

At scale $t/n$, replacing each exponential by $I+tA_i/n$ costs only
$O(n^{-2})$ per factor and therefore $O(n^{-1})$ for the whole product.
The resulting Euler product has an exact expansion by ordered subsets:
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^n\left(I+\frac tn A_i\right)x
 =\sum_{k=0}^n t^k\,\frac1{n^k}
   \sum_{i_1<\cdots<i_k}A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_k}x.
\]
For each fixed $k$, the coefficient is a nonsymmetric Banach-valued
$U$-statistic.  A direct simple-function argument shows that it converges in
$L^1$ to $(\E A)^kx/k!$ on \emph{any} separable Banach space: disjoint index
tuples are independent, and only $O(n^{2k-1})$ pairs overlap.  Finally,
boundedness supplies the uniform factorial majorant
$\rho^k\|x\|/k!$.  Thus fixed-order convergence can be summed over every
$k$, uniformly for $t$ in a compact interval.  This bypasses all type,
smoothness, and Hilbert-space variance estimates.

\section{An ordered $U$-statistic lemma}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ustat}
Let $S$ be a measurable space, let $\xi_1,\xi_2,\ldots$ be i.i.d.
$S$-valued random elements with law $\mu$, and let $X$ be a separable Banach
space.  If $h:S^k\to X$ is Bochner integrable, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ustat}
 \frac1{n^k}\sum_{1\leq i_1<\cdots<i_k\leq n}
 h(\xi_{i_1},\ldots,\xi_{i_k})
 \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{L^1(X)}
 \frac1{k!}\int_{S^k}h\,d\mu^k.
\end{equation}
No symmetry of $h$ is required.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First take $h=\mathbf1_D$ for a measurable $D\subseteq S^k$.  The
expectation of the left side is
$n^{-k}\binom nk\mu^k(D)$, which tends to $\mu^k(D)/k!$.  Two summands
whose index sets are disjoint are independent.  Among the
$\binom nk^2=O(n^{2k})$ ordered pairs of increasing $k$-tuples, only
$O_k(n^{2k-1})$ pairs have an intersecting index set.  Since every covariance
has absolute value at most one, the variance of the normalized sum is
$O_k(n^{-1})$.  Hence \eqref{eq:ustat} holds in $L^2$, and therefore in
$L^1$, for indicator kernels.  By linearity and the triangle inequality it
holds for finite simple $X$-valued kernels.

For general $h$, choose finite simple $g$ with
$\int\|h-g\|\,d\mu^k<\delta$.  If $U_n(h)$ denotes the left side of
\eqref{eq:ustat}, then
\[
 \E\|U_n(h)-U_n(g)\|
 \leq \frac{\binom nk}{n^k}\int\|h-g\|\,d\mu^k
 \leq\frac{\delta}{k!}.
\]
The distance between the two proposed limits is also at most $\delta/k!$.
Let first $n\to\infty$ and then $\delta\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Full theorem}

We use the following standard measurable formulation.  On the norm ball
\[
 \mathcal B_\rho=\{T\in\Lcal(X):\|T\|\leq\rho\}
\]
put the strong operator topology and its Borel $\sigma$-field.  When $X$ is
separable this topology is metrizable on $\mathcal B_\rho$ by evaluating on
a countable dense subset of $X$.

\begin{theorem}[all-separable-space weak law]\label{thm:main}
Let $X$ be a separable real or complex Banach space.  Let
$A,A_1,A_2,\ldots$ be i.i.d. $\mathcal B_\rho$-valued random elements for
the strong-operator Borel $\sigma$-field.  Define $B\in\Lcal(X)$ by
\[
 Bx=\E(Ax),\qquad x\in X.
\]
Then for every $x\in X$ and $T>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L1}
 \E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}
 \left\|e^{A_1t/n}\cdots e^{A_nt/n}x-e^{Bt}x\right\|
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Consequently the convergence is uniform on $[0,T]$ in probability in SOT.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x$ and $T$.  Write $h=t/n$.  Taylor's formula and
$\|A_i\|\leq\rho$ give
\[
 \|e^{hA_i}-(I+hA_i)\|
 \leq \frac{\rho^2h^2}{2}e^{\rho h},
 \qquad
 \|e^{hA_i}\|,\ \|I+hA_i\|\leq e^{\rho h}.
\]
A telescoping product estimate therefore yields, deterministically and
uniformly for $0\leq t\leq T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euler-error}
 \left\|\prod_{i=1}^ne^{tA_i/n}x-
 \prod_{i=1}^n\left(I+\frac tnA_i\right)x\right\|
 \leq \frac{\rho^2T^2e^{\rho T}}{2n}\|x\|.
\end{equation}

Expand the Euler product without commuting its factors:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expand}
 \prod_{i=1}^n\left(I+\frac tnA_i\right)x
 =\sum_{k=0}^n t^k C_{n,k}(x),\qquad
 C_{n,k}(x)=\frac1{n^k}\sum_{i_1<\cdots<i_k}
 A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_k}x.
\end{equation}
For fixed $k$, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:ustat} to
$h_k(T_1,\ldots,T_k)=T_1\cdots T_kx$.  This kernel is strongly measurable:
on $\mathcal B_\rho^k$, multiplication followed by evaluation at $x$ is
continuous for the product SOT, because all operators have the common norm
bound $\rho$.  It is bounded by $\rho^k\|x\|$.  Iterated Bochner integration
gives
\[
 \int h_k\,d\mu^k=B^kx,
\]
so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coeff}
 C_{n,k}(x)\xrightarrow{L^1(X)}\frac1{k!}B^kx.
\end{equation}

Moreover, for every outcome,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maj}
 \|C_{n,k}(x)\|
 \leq\frac{\binom nk}{n^k}\rho^k\|x\|
 \leq\frac{\rho^k\|x\|}{k!},
 \qquad
 \left\|\frac{B^kx}{k!}\right\|
 \leq\frac{\rho^k\|x\|}{k!}.
\end{equation}
For an integer $K$, equations \eqref{eq:coeff}--\eqref{eq:maj} imply
\begin{align*}
 &\E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}
 \left\|\sum_{k=0}^nt^kC_{n,k}(x)-e^{tB}x\right\|\\
 &\quad\leq
 \sum_{k=0}^KT^k\E\left\|C_{n,k}(x)-\frac{B^kx}{k!}\right\|
 +2\|x\|\sum_{k>K}\frac{(\rho T)^k}{k!}.
\end{align*}
For fixed $K$ the first term tends to zero; the second tends to zero as
$K\to\infty$, uniformly in $n$.  This proves the Euler-product version of
\eqref{eq:L1}.  Combining it with \eqref{eq:euler-error} proves
\eqref{eq:L1}.  Markov's inequality gives the asserted convergence in
probability.  The displayed supremum is measurable because the paths are
continuous and the supremum may be taken over rational $t\in[0,T]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[source conjecture]\label{cor:source}
Let $1\leq p<\infty$.  If the weakly measurable, uniformly bounded
operators in Conjecture 2.5 are i.i.d. in the standard random-element sense
for the $\ell_p$ strong-operator Borel $\sigma$-field, then the conjecture
holds.  In fact, its convergence is uniform-in-time $L^1$ as in
\eqref{eq:L1}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Because $\ell_p$ is separable, weak measurability of $A$ implies strong
measurability of every $Ax$ by Pettis's measurability theorem.  On a fixed
operator-norm ball, a countable dense set of vectors metrizes SOT; hence $A$
is a Borel random element for that topology.  The Pettis mean used in
\cite{DS24} agrees pointwise with the Bochner integrals $\E(Ax)$.  Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

\section{Conventions, limitations, and verification}

\paragraph{Independence convention.}
The proof uses ordinary independence of the operator random elements, or
equivalently independence of the $\sigma$-fields generated by all SOT
evaluations.  This is the standard meaning of ``i.i.d. random generators''
and is the meaning adopted explicitly in the authors' later paper
\cite{DS25}.  The 2024 source also gives a bespoke sentence defining
independence by requiring one scalar rank-one observation
$\langle f_i,A_ix_i\rangle$ from each operator to be independent.  Taken
literally, that sentence does not visibly imply independence of the full
evaluation $\sigma$-fields, because the class of rank-one observations is not
closed under finite joint cylinders.  Thus a reviewer should either confirm
that the sentence was intended as shorthand for standard random-element
independence, or regard Corollary~\ref{cor:source} as a precise correction of
that convention.  No geometric assumption on $X$ is hidden in the proof.

\paragraph{Computational check.}
The accompanying script tests noncommuting finite-dimensional examples.  It
compares exact exponential products, Euler products, and $e^{t\E A}$ on a
time grid, and separately checks the first three ordered coefficients.
Observed mean errors decrease with $n$.  This is a sanity check only; the
proof above is analytic and dimension-free.

\section{Literature and novelty check}

A bounded search completed on 17 August 2026 used the run's registry,
solution, attempt, and proof-gap indexes; the local arXiv corpus; exact-title
and author queries for arXiv:2410.07417; and close variants of ``random
semigroup weak law'', ``Banach-valued ordered U-statistic'', and ``strong
operator law of large numbers''.  The principal later match is the same
authors' 2025 preprint \cite{DS25}.  It proves almost-sure SOT convergence on
uniformly smooth Banach spaces (hence $\ell_p$, $1<p<\infty$), proves an
all-Banach WOT theorem, and states an $\ell_1$ consequence via Schur's
theorem.  Its random operators, however, are Bochner measurable as elements
of the \emph{operator-norm} Banach space.  That is a stricter measurability
hypothesis than the weak/SOT-measurable hypothesis of \cite{DS24} and of
Corollary~\ref{cor:source}.  No source was located that states the
all-separable-space uniform $L^1$ theorem above or proves the original weakly
measurable formulation by this ordered-$U$-statistic argument.  Novelty
confidence is moderate pending a specialist search.

\section{Human review recommendation}

The decisive points to audit are: (i) the $L^1$ simple-kernel approximation
in Lemma~\ref{lem:ustat}; (ii) continuity and measurability of the ordered
composition kernel on a common SOT norm ball; and (iii) whether the source's
use of ``i.i.d.'' is intended in the standard random-element sense.  The
Euler estimate and factorial-tail resummation are deterministic.  If (iii)
is accepted, the packet is a full solution of Conjecture 2.5 and a strict
space-class strengthening.
