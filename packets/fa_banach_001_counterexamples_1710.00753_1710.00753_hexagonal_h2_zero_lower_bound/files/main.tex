The Gabor system generated by the second Hermite function and the hexagonal
lattice of density \(2\) does \emph{not} have a positive lower frame bound.
Its integer-density Janssen symbol vanishes exactly at a three-torsion point.
The cancellation follows from the cubic theta product and a weight-four

\section{The source question}

Faulhuber \cite{Faulhuber} asks whether the Gabor system generated by the
second Hermite function \(h_2\) and the hexagonal lattice of density \(2\)
has a positive lower frame bound.  The paper reports numerical evidence for
a frame with lower bound approximately \(0.29\).

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\caption{The source question and its numerical prediction (PDF page 2).}
\end{figure}

The source also proves the exact tool needed here.  At even integer density,
the sharp lower frame bound is the essential infimum of the absolutely
convergent Janssen symbol
\[
 \phi(z)=\operatorname{vol}(\Lambda)^{-1}
 \sum_{\lambda^\circ\in\Lambda^\circ}
 \cA g(\lambda^\circ)e^{2\pi i\sigma(\lambda^\circ,z)},  \tag{1}
\]
where \(\Lambda^\circ\) is the adjoint lattice and \(\cA g\) is the
ambiguity function.  Thus one exact zero of \(\phi\) rules out a frame.

\section{Proof intuition}

At density \(2\), the adjoint of a normalized hexagonal lattice is its
dilate by \(2\).  At a natural three-torsion point of the torus, the
characters in (1) become the cubic roots \(\omega^{k-l}\).  Since the
ambiguity function of \(h_2\) is a Laguerre polynomial times a Gaussian, the
symbol becomes a second differential expression in the cubic theta series
\[
 \sum_{k,l}\omega^{k-l}e^{-u(k^2+kl+l^2)}.
\]

This series is the eta quotient \(\eta(\tau)^3/\eta(3\tau)\).  The density
fixes \(\tau=i/\sqrt3\), the Fricke-symmetric point of level \(3\).  The
Ramanujan derivative equation reduces the differential expression to
\(5\mathcal E^2-E_4\), where \(\mathcal E\) is the normalized weight-two
Eisenstein series on \(\Gamma_0(3)\).  A one-coefficient Sturm-bound
identity gives \(E_4=5\mathcal E^2\) at that point, forcing the symbol to
vanish.

\section{The hexagonal Janssen symbol}

Put
\[
 H=\sqrt{\frac2{\sqrt3}}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\0&\sqrt3/2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Lambda=2^{-1/2}H\Z^2.                                 \tag{2}
\]
Then \(\det H=1\), so \(\operatorname{vol}(\Lambda)=1/2\), and in
dimension one \(H\) is symplectic.  Consequently
\[
 \Lambda^\circ=\sqrt2H\Z^2=2\Lambda.                    \tag{3}
\]
Every density-two hexagonal lattice is obtained from this representative by
a phase-space rotation or reflection; the Hermite ambiguity function is
radial, so the frame bounds are unchanged.

For the standard normalized second Hermite function, direct Gaussian
integration gives
\[
 \cA h_2(x,\xi)=e^{-\pi(x^2+\xi^2)/2}
 L_2\bigl(\pi(x^2+\xi^2)\bigr),
 \qquad L_2(t)=1-2t+\frac{t^2}{2}.                        \tag{4}
\]
Let
\[
 z_0=2^{-1/2}H\binom{1/3}{1/3},
 \qquad \omega=e^{2\pi i/3}.
\]
For \(\lambda^\circ=\sqrt2H(k,l)^T\), symplecticity and (2) give
\[
 \sigma(\lambda^\circ,z_0)=\frac{k-l}{3},
 \qquad
 |\lambda^\circ|^2=\frac4{\sqrt3}(k^2+kl+l^2).          \tag{5}
\]
Set \(Q(k,l)=k^2+kl+l^2\) and \(u_0=2\pi/\sqrt3\).  Substituting
(4)--(5) into (1) yields
\[
 \frac{\phi(z_0)}2=
 \sum_{k,l\in\Z}\omega^{k-l}e^{-u_0Q(k,l)}
 \left(1-4u_0Q(k,l)+2u_0^2Q(k,l)^2\right).              \tag{6}
\]

\section{Exact cubic-theta cancellation}

For \(u>0\), define
\[
 B(u)=\sum_{k,l\in\Z}\omega^{k-l}e^{-uQ(k,l)}.
\]
The series and all its derivatives converge absolutely.  Equation (6) is
\[
 \frac{\phi(z_0)}2=B(u_0)+4u_0B'(u_0)+2u_0^2B''(u_0).    \tag{7}
\]

Let \(q=e^{2\pi i\tau}=e^{-u}\).  The classical Borwein cubic theta
product \cite{Borwein} is
\[
 B(u)=b(\tau):=\sum_{k,l\in\Z}\omega^{k-l}q^{Q(k,l)}
 =\frac{\eta(\tau)^3}{\eta(3\tau)}.                     \tag{8}
\]

We need one elementary level-three modular identity.  Write \(E_2,E_4\)
for the normalized Eisenstein series and set
\[
 \mathcal E(\tau)=\frac{3E_2(3\tau)-E_2(\tau)}2.
\]
Then
\[
 \mathcal E(\tau)^2
 =\frac{E_4(\tau)+9E_4(3\tau)}{10}.                     \tag{9}
\]
Indeed, both sides are weight-four modular forms on \(\Gamma_0(3)\).
The Sturm bound is
\(\lfloor(4/12)[\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z):\Gamma_0(3)]\rfloor=1\), and their
expansions both begin \(1+24q+O(q^2)\).

Now take \(\tau_0=i/\sqrt3\), so \(u_0=2\pi/\sqrt3\) and
\(3\tau_0=-1/\tau_0\).  The modular transformations of \(E_2,E_4\)
give, with \(e=E_2(\tau_0)\) and \(f=E_4(\tau_0)\),
\[
 E_2(3\tau_0)=-\frac e3+\frac{2\sqrt3}{\pi},
 \qquad E_4(3\tau_0)=\frac f9.                           \tag{10}
\]
It follows immediately from (9) that
\[
 \mathcal E(\tau_0)^2=\frac f5.                          \tag{11}
\]

For completeness, let \(\delta=q\,d/dq=-d/du\) and
\[
 a(\tau)=\delta\log b(\tau)
 =\frac{E_2(\tau)-E_2(3\tau)}8.                         \tag{12}
\]
Hence \(B'=-aB\) and \(B''=(a^2+\delta a)B\).  Ramanujan's identity
\(\delta E_2=(E_2^2-E_4)/12\), remembering the factor \(3\) when
differentiating \(E_2(3\tau)\), gives at \(\tau_0\)
\[
 \delta a=\frac1{96}
 \left[e^2-f-3\left(E_2(3\tau_0)^2-\frac f9\right)\right]. \tag{13}
\]
Substitution of (10), (12), and (13) into (7) simplifies exactly to
\[
 \frac{B+4u_0B'+2u_0^2B''}{B}
 =\frac{\pi^2}{54}\left(5\mathcal E(\tau_0)^2-f\right)=0, \tag{14}
\]
where the last equality is (11).  Since \(\eta\) has no zeros in the upper
half-plane, \(B(u_0)\ne0\), so the division in (14) is legitimate.

\begin{theorem}
The Gabor system \(\mathcal G(h_2,\Lambda)\), with \(\Lambda\) the
hexagonal lattice of density \(2\), is not a frame.  Its sharp lower frame
bound is zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equations (7) and (14) give \(\phi(z_0)=0\).  The Janssen series is
continuous because \(h_2\) is Schwartz, and the source's sharp-bound formula
(1) says its essential infimum is the lower frame bound.  A continuous
symbol with a zero cannot have positive essential infimum.  Hence the lower
frame bound is zero and the system is not a frame.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The accompanying script evaluates the truncated sum in (6) at 100-digit
precision: square cutoffs \(2,3,4,5,6,8,10\) converge successively from
\(4.45\cdot10^{-8}\) to below \(10^{-99}\).  It also checks symbolically
the reduction in (14).  These computations audit signs and normalizations;
the proof is the exact modular calculation above.

The result completely answers the source's displayed question negatively
and contradicts only its numerical prediction, not its theorem about odd
windows.  On 12 August 2026, the four run indexes and bounded exact/current
searches for the source identifier, exact question, claimed \(0.29\) bound,
hexagonal/nonseparable \(h_2\) systems, and later citations found no explicit
resolution.  Later separable-lattice frame-set results do not cover this
question.  Novelty confidence is moderate-high.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Review as a full negative answer.  The most important checks are the lattice
normalization (2)--(5), the cubic theta product (8), and the factor-of-three
derivative in (13).
