\section{Source question}

Wirth \cite{Wirth} constructs a transport metric for tracially symmetric
quantum Markov semigroups.  At the end of Example 4.19 (source PDF page 26),
after recalling the finite-dimensional construction of Carlen and Maas
\cite{CM}, he asks:
\begin{quote}
How can the construction of the metric $\W$ be generalized to
infinite-dimensional quantum Markov semigroups satisfying detailed balance
for a nontracial state or weight?
\end{quote}

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering

  \caption{The finite-dimensional construction and open question in the
  original paper, source PDF page 26.}
\end{figure}

The result below answers the question for decomposable semigroups on a
countable matrix bundle.  It uses the Carlen--Maas theorem on each fibre, but
the infinite product metric, its exact distance formula, and the global
entropy-gradient-flow identification require a separate argument.

\section{Setup and statement}

For every $n\geq1$, let $\M_n=M_{d_n}(\mathbb C)$ with normalized trace
$\tr_n$, let $\sigma_n\in\M_n$ be a strictly positive density matrix, and let
$(P_t^{(n)})_{t\geq0}$ be a quantum Markov semigroup satisfying detailed
balance with respect to $\sigma_n$.  Write $g^{(n)}$ and $\W_n$ for its
Carlen--Maas Riemannian metric and induced transport distance on the strictly
positive density matrices $\D_n^+$.  Thus the predual evolution
$P_{t,*}^{(n)}$ is the $g^{(n)}$-gradient flow of
\[
  E_n(r)=\tr_n\bigl(r(\log r-\log\sigma_n)\bigr).
\]

Let
\[
  \M=\prod_{n\geq1}\M_n,
  \qquad
  \phi(a)=\sum_{n\geq1}w_n\tr_n(\sigma_n a_n),
\]
where $w_n>0$ and $\sum_nw_n=1$.  This is a faithful normal state.  It is
nontracial as soon as some $\sigma_n$ is nonscalar.  Define the decomposable
semigroup $P_t(a)=(P_t^{(n)}a_n)_n$.  It is a quantum Markov semigroup on
$\M$, is strongly continuous on the predual, and satisfies detailed balance
with respect to $\phi$, by summing the corresponding fibre identities.

A normal state on $\M$ is a sequence $\rho=(p_nr_n)_n$, where $p_n\geq0$,
$\sum_np_n=1$, and $r_n$ is a density matrix whenever $p_n>0$.  The numbers
$p_n$ are its central masses.  Since $P_t$ preserves each central projection,
the vector $p=(p_n)$ is invariant.  Fix $p_n>0$ and put
\[
  \D_p^+=\{(p_nr_n)_n:r_n\in\D_n^+\}.
\]

For $\rho=(p_nr_n)$ and a tangent vector
$h=(p_nh_n)$ with $h_n=h_n^*$ and $\tr_nh_n=0$, define the possibly infinite
quadratic form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:productmetric}
  g^p_\rho(h,h)=\sum_{n\geq1}p_n g^{(n)}_{r_n}(h_n,h_n).
\end{equation}
Also set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:productdistance}
  \W_p(\rho,\eta)^2
  =\sum_{n\geq1}p_n\W_n(r_n,s_n)^2,
  \qquad \eta=(p_ns_n).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Countable matrix-bundle extension]\label{thm:main}
The function $\W_p$ is an extended nondegenerate metric on $\D_p^+$, and it
is the length distance associated with the product Riemannian form
\eqref{eq:productmetric}.  On the finite-distance, finite-entropy domain, the
predual evolution
\[
 \rho_t=P_{t,*}\rho_0
       =(p_nP_{t,*}^{(n)}r_{n,0})_{n\geq1}
\]
is the gradient flow of the relative entropy
\[
 \Ent_\phi(\rho)
 =\sum_{n\geq1}p_nE_n(r_n)
  +\sum_{n\geq1}p_n\log\frac{p_n}{w_n}.
\]
More precisely, the Riemannian gradient identity holds for every finite-block
tangent vector; whenever the displayed metric-slope and entropy-dissipation
sums are finite, it extends by monotone convergence to the full product and
the usual energy-dissipation identity is the sum of its fibre identities.
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof intuition}

The center records which matrix block a state occupies.  A decomposable
semigroup cannot transport mass between blocks, so each fixed central-mass
fibre is invariant.  On that fibre the only possible dynamics are the known
finite-dimensional detailed-balance dynamics.  The correct kinetic energy is
therefore the weighted sum of the fibre kinetic energies, which produces the
weighted $\ell_2$ product of their distances.

Relative entropy splits in exactly the same way.  Its additional classical
term measures the discrepancy between the central masses $p_n$ and the
reference weights $w_n$, but this term is constant along the invariant fibre.
Consequently its metric gradient is simply the tuple of the fibre gradients.

\section{Proof}

\begin{lemma}[Weighted product length]\label{lem:length}
Let $(X_n,d_n)$ be length spaces and let $p_n>0$ with $\sum_np_n=1$.  On
$X=\prod_nX_n$, the formula
\[
 d(x,y)=\left(\sum_np_nd_n(x_n,y_n)^2\right)^{1/2}
\]
defines an extended metric.  Its restriction to every finite-distance
component is the length metric of the product speed
\[
 |\dot\gamma_t|^2=\sum_np_n|\dot\gamma_{n,t}|^2.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Separation and symmetry are immediate.  Minkowski's inequality in weighted
$\ell_2$ gives the triangle inequality.  For every rectifiable product curve,
$d_n(x_n,y_n)\leq\int_0^1|\dot\gamma_{n,t}|\,dt$; Minkowski's integral
inequality then gives
\[
 d(x,y)\leq\int_0^1
 \left(\sum_np_n|\dot\gamma_{n,t}|^2\right)^{1/2}dt.
\]
Conversely choose in each factor a path of length at most
$d_n(x_n,y_n)+\epsilon_n$, reparametrize it with constant speed, and choose
$\epsilon_n>0$ so that
$\sum_np_n\epsilon_n^2$ and
$\sum_np_nd_n(x_n,y_n)\epsilon_n$ are arbitrarily small.  Their product path
has length at most
$\bigl(\sum_np_n(d_n(x_n,y_n)+\epsilon_n)^2\bigr)^{1/2}$.
This proves the reverse inequality by letting the errors tend to zero.
\end{proof}

Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:length} to $(\D_n^+,\W_n)$ proves all metric and
length assertions in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; the infinitesimal product speed
is precisely \eqref{eq:productmetric}.

It remains to identify the gradient flow.  Since scalar central weights
commute with all matrices,
\begin{align*}
 \Ent_\phi(\rho)
 &=\sum_n\tr_n\!\left(p_nr_n
   \left(\log(p_nr_n)-\log(w_n\sigma_n)\right)\right)\\
 &=\sum_np_nE_n(r_n)+\sum_np_n\log\frac{p_n}{w_n}.
\end{align*}
On $\D_p^+$ the second sum is constant.  If $h=(p_nh_n)$ has only finitely
many nonzero coordinates, differentiation gives
\[
 D\Ent_\phi(\rho)[h]
 =\sum_np_nDE_n(r_n)[h_n].
\]
By the Carlen--Maas gradient-flow theorem in each block,
\[
 DE_n(r_n)[h_n]
 =g^{(n)}_{r_n}\bigl(\operatorname{grad}_{g^{(n)}}E_n,h_n\bigr),
 \qquad
 \dot r_{n,t}=-\operatorname{grad}_{g^{(n)}}E_n(r_{n,t}).
\]
Multiplying by $p_n$ and summing yields
\[
 D\Ent_\phi(\rho)[h]
 =g^p_\rho\bigl((\operatorname{grad}_{g^{(n)}}E_n)_n,h\bigr)
\]
and
\[
 \dot\rho_t
 =-\operatorname{grad}_{g^p}\Ent_\phi(\rho_t)
 =(p_n\dot r_{n,t})_n.
\]
This proves the classical Riemannian identity on the finite-block core.

For a general product trajectory for which the sums are finite, the fibre
energy-dissipation identities are nonnegative identities.  Summing their first
$N$ terms and sending $N\to\infty$ gives, by monotone convergence, the global
identity and hence the asserted gradient-flow statement on its natural
finite-energy domain.  Strong continuity of the predual product semigroup
follows independently from dominated convergence, since each predual map is
an $L^1$ contraction.  This completes the proof.

\section{Scope, full-upgrade obstruction, and verification}

The construction is not merely a finite direct sum: with infinitely many
blocks, $\M$ is infinite dimensional and generally nonseparable in operator
norm, while $\phi$ can be genuinely nontracial.  Equation
\eqref{eq:productdistance} may take the value $+\infty$, exactly as Wirth's
transport metric may.

Two upgrades were tested.  The same product argument extends formally to
measurable fields $L^\infty(X;M_d)$, but an exact length-space statement needs
a measurable choice of almost-minimizing fibre paths; this was not inserted as
an unproved selection lemma.  More importantly, the derivation theorem for
arbitrary KMS-symmetric semigroups \cite{VW} does not by itself supply the
operator-valued logarithmic-mean chain rule needed to identify the Onsager
operator on Haagerup $L^1$ densities.  In type III algebras such densities are
not bounded elements of $\M$, so the finite-matrix left/right functional
calculus used above cannot simply be repeated.  Thus no claim is made for the
full question.

The proof is purely analytic.  The verifier should focus on (i) the intended
notion of detailed balance in the source, (ii) the domain on which an infinite
sum of fibre energy-dissipation identities is finite, and (iii) whether a
standard reference already records this countable-product closure explicitly.
