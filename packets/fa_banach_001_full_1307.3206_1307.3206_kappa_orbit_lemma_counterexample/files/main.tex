\begin{abstract}
Dann and Zymonopoulou assert that the orbit of every vector in
$\R^{\kappa n}$ under simultaneous block rotation by $\SO(\kappa)$ is a
$(\kappa-1)$-sphere whose linear span has dimension $\kappa$.  We give a
minimal counterexample: for $\kappa=3$, $n=2$, the orbit of $(e_1,e_2)$
spans all of $\R^3\oplus\R^3$.  More generally, if the $n$ blocks form a
$\kappa\times n$ matrix $X$ of rank $r$, then for every $\kappa\geq3$ the
orbit span has dimension $\kappa r$.  Consequently the paper's subspace
$H_x^\perp$ has variable dimension and the ensuing fixed-codimension
``hyperplane'' and intersection-body construction does not work as stated.
The separately formulated $L_{-p}$ embedding question remains open; we do
not claim a resolution of it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source assertion}

The source is S.~Dann and M.~Zymonopoulou, \emph{Intersection Bodies with
Certain Symmetries}, arXiv:1307.3206v2, published under the title
\emph{Sections of Convex Bodies with Symmetries} \cite{DannZym}.  Write a
vector $x\in\R^{\kappa n}$ as $n$ consecutive blocks
$v_1,\ldots,v_n\in\R^\kappa$.  The paper defines
\[
 R_\sigma(x)=(\sigma v_1,\ldots,\sigma v_n),
 \qquad \sigma\in\SO(\kappa).
\]
Lemma~1 on PDF page~4 asserts that
$\{R_\sigma(x):\sigma\in\SO(\kappa)\}$ is always a
$(\kappa-1)$-sphere, that its span $H_x^\perp$ always has dimension
$\kappa$, and that $H_x=(H_x^\perp)^\perp$ always has dimension
$\kappa n-\kappa$.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/lemma1_crop.png}
  \caption{Lemma 1 and its proof in arXiv:1307.3206v2, PDF page 4.}
\end{figure}

The proof checks only the special rank-one vector $(0,\ldots,0,1)$.
Simultaneous left rotation is not transitive on the unit sphere of
$\R^{\kappa n}$: it preserves the Gram matrix of the blocks, and in
particular their linear rank.

\section{Idea of the counterexample}

Regard $x$ as the matrix $X=[v_1\ \cdots\ v_n]\in M_{\kappa,n}(\R)$.
Then $R_\sigma(x)$ is simply $\sigma X$.  When $X$ has two independent
columns, its orbit retains their mutual inner products and is an orbit of
orthonormal frames, not a sphere.  For $X=[e_1\ e_2]\in M_{3,2}(\R)$,
six rotations give orbit points whose sums and differences are the six
coordinate vectors of $\R^3\oplus\R^3$.

The general calculation is equally short.  For $\kappa\geq3$, the linear
span of the group $\SO(\kappa)$ is the full matrix algebra
$M_\kappa(\R)$.  Hence the orbit span is $\{AX:A\in M_\kappa(\R)\}$.
Each of its $\kappa$ rows can be chosen independently in the row space of
$X$, giving dimension $\kappa\rank X$.

\section{The concrete counterexample}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:counterexample}
Let $\kappa=3$, $n=2$, and $X=[e_1\ e_2]$.  Then
\[
 \Span\{\sigma X:\sigma\in\SO(3)\}=M_{3,2}(\R)\cong\R^6.
\]
In particular the orbit is not a $2$-sphere spanning a $3$-plane.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every ordered orthonormal pair $(u,v)$ in $\R^3$, the matrix with
columns $u,v,u\times v$ belongs to $\SO(3)$.  Thus the following six pairs
are orbit points:
\[
 (e_1,e_2),\ (e_1,-e_2),\quad
 (e_2,e_3),\ (e_2,-e_3),\quad
 (e_3,e_1),\ (e_3,-e_1).
\]
Taking half-sums and half-differences of the two points in each pair gives
\[
 (e_1,0),(0,e_2),\quad
 (e_2,0),(0,e_3),\quad
 (e_3,0),(0,e_1).
\]
These are the six coordinate vectors of $\R^3\oplus\R^3$, so the orbit
span is all of $\R^6$.  A $2$-sphere in a linear $3$-plane cannot have a
six-dimensional span.
\end{proof}

Indeed, the orbit is the Stiefel manifold of ordered orthonormal $2$-frames
in $\R^3$, which is diffeomorphic to $\SO(3)$ and has dimension $3$, rather
than a $2$-sphere.

\section{General corrected orbit-span formula}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:general}
Let $\kappa\geq3$, let $X\in M_{\kappa,n}(\R)$ have rank $r$, and let
$W\subseteq\R^n$ be its row space.  Then
\[
 \Span\{\sigma X:\sigma\in\SO(\kappa)\}
 =\{Y\in M_{\kappa,n}(\R):\text{every row of $Y$ belongs to $W$}\}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \dim\Span\{\sigma X:\sigma\in\SO(\kappa)\}=\kappa r,
\]
and the orthogonal complement has dimension $\kappa(n-r)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First we show that $\Span\SO(\kappa)=M_\kappa(\R)$.  Let
\[
 \mathcal D=\{\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_\kappa):
 \varepsilon_j\in\{-1,1\},\ \prod_j\varepsilon_j=1\}\subset\SO(\kappa).
\]
For $\kappa\geq3$, character cancellation in this finite sign group gives
\[
 E_{jj}=\frac1{|\mathcal D|}
 \sum_{D_\varepsilon\in\mathcal D}\varepsilon_jD_\varepsilon.
\]
For each $i,j$, choose $Q_{ij}\in\SO(\kappa)$ with
$Q_{ij}e_j=e_i$.  Since left multiplication by $Q_{ij}$ preserves the
linear span of the group,
\[
 E_{ij}=Q_{ij}E_{jj}\in\Span\SO(\kappa).
\]
All matrix units therefore belong to this span.

It follows that
\[
 \Span\{\sigma X:\sigma\in\SO(\kappa)\}
 =\{AX:A\in M_\kappa(\R)\}.
\]
Every row of $AX$ is a linear combination of the rows of $X$, hence belongs
to $W$.  Conversely, if every row of $Y$ belongs to $W$, choose the
coefficients expressing each row of $Y$ in terms of the rows of $X$ and
assemble them as the rows of a matrix $A$; then $Y=AX$.  The displayed
space consists of $\kappa$ independently chosen rows in the $r$-dimensional
space $W$, so its dimension is $\kappa r$.  Its orthogonal complement has
dimension $\kappa n-\kappa r=\kappa(n-r)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
For $\kappa\geq3$, the dimension asserted in source Lemma~1 holds only when
$X$ has rank one (apart from the zero vector).  For the rank-two matrix in
Proposition~\ref{prop:counterexample}, the source's notation gives
$H_x^\perp=\R^6$ and $H_x=\{0\}$, not two complementary $3$-planes.
\end{corollary}

\section{Consequences and scope}

Immediately after Lemma~1, the paper calls $H_\xi$ a hyperplane in
$\mathbb K^n$ for every unit vector $\xi$ and defines intersection bodies
using the volumes of $D\cap H_\xi^\perp$ and $L\cap H_\xi$.  The next
paragraph requires $D\cap H_\xi^\perp$ to be a $\kappa$-dimensional ball.
Theorem~\ref{thm:general} shows instead that the two dimensions are
$\kappa r$ and $\kappa(n-r)$, where $r$ is the rank of the block matrix of
$\xi$.  They vary with $\xi$, and for $r>1$ the first section need not be a
$\kappa$-ball.  Thus the defect is structural, not merely a faulty
parametrization of a correct sphere.

Later parallel-section arguments that take $H=H_\xi$ with fixed codimension
$\kappa$, or identify the orbit with the unit sphere of $H_\xi^\perp$, need
a new definition or an additional rank-one restriction.  In particular,
the printed proof of the universal embedding range for $\kappa\geq3$ cannot
be accepted from this route without repair.

The source also asks whether every origin-symmetric $\kappa$-balanced convex
body embeds in $L_{-p}$ for
$p\in(\kappa n-\kappa,\kappa n-3)$ when $\kappa\geq4$.  That statement is
well-defined independently of the subspaces $H_\xi$.  This packet neither
proves nor disproves it; the full result here is the counterexample to the
foundational orbit lemma and the exact replacement for its span formula.
