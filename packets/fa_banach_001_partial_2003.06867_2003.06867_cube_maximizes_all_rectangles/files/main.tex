\section{Source problem}

Let $B$ be standard Brownian motion in $\R^d$, let $\tau_D$ be its first exit
time from $D$, and let $\lambda_1(D)$ be the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
$-\Delta$.  For $a_k>0$, put
\[
 R_{\mathbf a}=\prod_{k=1}^d(-a_k,a_k),\qquad Q_d=(-1,1)^d.
\]
Remark~5.5 and equation~(5.22) of Bañuelos--Mariano--Wang ask whether, for
all $p>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
 \lambda_1(R_{\mathbf a})^p\E_0[\tau_{R_{\mathbf a}}^p]
 \le \lambda_1(Q_d)^p\E_0[\tau_{Q_d}^p].
\end{equation}
The source says this was not verified even for $d=2,p=1$.

\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Because the functional is invariant under common dilation, the unnormalized
equality class must consist of all homothetic cubes.  Under the source's later
normalization $a_1=1$, this reduces to $Q_d$.

\section{Idea of the proof}

The rectangle problem tensorizes.  If $T$ is the exit time from $(-1,1)$ and
$S(t)=\Pp(T>t)$, then Brownian scaling and coordinate independence give
\[
 \Pp_0(\tau_{R_{\mathbf a}}>t)=\prod_{k=1}^d S(a_k^{-2}t).
\]
Thus the desired balancing inequality follows immediately if $\log S$ is
concave.  The key observation is that $T$ is an infinite convolution of
exponential laws: its Laplace transform is $\operatorname{sech}\sqrt{2s}$,
and Euler's product for $\cosh$ factors this into exponential Laplace
transforms.  Log-concavity survives convolution and weak limits, and the tail
of a log-concave law is log-concave.  Jensen then gives not merely all moment
inequalities but a scale-free stochastic domination.

\section{The interval exit time is log-concave}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:logconcave}
Let $T=\inf\{t>0:|B_t|\ge1\}$ for one-dimensional standard Brownian motion
with $B_0=0$.  The law of $T$ is log-concave on $[0,\infty)$, and its survival
function $S(t)=\Pp(T>t)$ is log-concave on $[0,\infty)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $s\ge0$, the function $u_s(x)=\E_x e^{-sT}$ solves
\[
 \tfrac12u_s''=su_s\quad(-1<x<1),\qquad u_s(-1)=u_s(1)=1.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \E_0e^{-sT}=u_s(0)=\frac1{\cosh\sqrt{2s}}.
\]
Euler's product
\[
 \cosh z=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}
 \left(1+\frac{4z^2}{(2n+1)^2\pi^2}\right)
\]
therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product}
 \E_0e^{-sT}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_n}{\lambda_n+s},
 \qquad \lambda_n=\frac{(2n+1)^2\pi^2}{8}.
\end{equation}
Let $X_n$ be independent exponential variables of rates $\lambda_n$.  Since
\[
 \sum_{n\ge0}\E X_n=\frac8{\pi^2}
 \sum_{n\ge0}\frac1{(2n+1)^2}=1,
\]
$\sum_nX_n$ is finite almost surely.  Equation~\eqref{eq:product} and
uniqueness of Laplace transforms show
\[
 T\ \stackrel{d}=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}X_n.
\]
Every exponential density is log-concave.  The Prékopa--Leindler theorem
shows that convolution preserves log-concavity; log-concave probability
measures are closed under weak convergence.  Hence the law $\mu$ of $T$ is
log-concave.

Finally, for $x,y\ge0$ and $0<\theta<1$, apply log-concavity of $\mu$ to the
upper rays $A=(x,\infty)$ and $C=(y,\infty)$.  Since
\[
 \theta A+(1-\theta)C=(\theta x+(1-\theta)y,\infty),
\]
we obtain
\[
 S(\theta x+(1-\theta)y)
 \ge S(x)^\theta S(y)^{1-\theta}.
\]
Thus $S$ is log-concave.
\end{proof}

\section{Stochastic domination and the rectangle theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $d\ge2$ and every axis-aligned rectangle $R_{\mathbf a}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stoch}
 \lambda_1(R_{\mathbf a})\tau_{R_{\mathbf a}}
 \ \st\
 \lambda_1(Q_d)\tau_{Q_d}
 \qquad\text{under }\Pp_0.
\end{equation}
Consequently, \eqref{eq:source} holds for every $p>0$.  Equality in any one
of these moment inequalities holds if and only if
$a_1=\cdots=a_d$, that is, $R_{\mathbf a}$ is a homothetic cube.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $b_k=a_k^{-2}$ and $\bar b=d^{-1}\sum_kb_k$.  The coordinate exit times
are independent and Brownian scaling gives
\[
 \Pp_0(\tau_{R_{\mathbf a}}>t)=\prod_{k=1}^dS(b_kt).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:logconcave} and Jensen's inequality,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jensen}
 \prod_{k=1}^dS(b_kt)
 \le S(\bar bt)^d
 =\Pp_0(\tau_{Q_d}>\bar bt).
\end{equation}
Thus $\tau_{R_{\mathbf a}}\st \bar b^{-1}\tau_{Q_d}$.  On the other hand,
separation of variables gives
\[
 \lambda_1(R_{\mathbf a})=\frac{\pi^2}{4}\sum_{k=1}^db_k
 =\frac{\pi^2d}{4}\bar b,
 \qquad \lambda_1(Q_d)=\frac{\pi^2d}{4}.
\]
Multiplying the stochastic comparison by $\lambda_1(R_{\mathbf a})$ proves
\eqref{eq:stoch}.  Applying the increasing function $x\mapsto x^p$ proves
\eqref{eq:source} for every $p>0$.

It remains to identify equality.  If the $b_k$ are all equal, every step is
an equality.  Conversely, suppose $m=\min b_k<M=\max b_k$ and equality holds
for one moment.  The tail-moment formula
\[
 \E X^p=p\int_0^{\infty}t^{p-1}\Pp(X>t)\,dt
\]
and continuity force equality in \eqref{eq:jensen} for all $t>0$.  Equality
in Jensen for the concave function $\log S$ then makes $\log S$ affine on
every interval $[mt,Mt]$.  These intervals give overlapping neighborhoods of
every point of $(0,\infty)$, so $\log S$ would be globally affine.

This is impossible.  Indeed $T\ge X_0+X_1$, whence
\[
 1-S(t)=\Pp(T\le t)\le \Pp(X_0\le t,X_1\le t)=O(t^2),
\]
so $(\log S)'(0+)=0$, whereas $S(t)\to0$ as $t\to\infty$.  Therefore every
moment inequality is strict unless all $b_k$, equivalently all $a_k$, are
equal.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
Among rectangles with a fixed first Dirichlet eigenvalue, the balanced cube
maximizes the centered exit time in the usual stochastic order.  Hence it
maximizes $\E\Phi(\tau)$ for every increasing $\Phi$ for which the expectation
exists, not only the positive moments.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Maximizing starting point.}
For completeness, $x\mapsto\Pp_x(\tau_{(-a,a)}>t)$ is even and nonincreasing
on $[0,a)$.  This follows, for example, by applying the parabolic maximum
principle to the spatial derivative of the killed heat solution.  The
rectangular survival probability is the product of the coordinate survival
probabilities, so the origin maximizes it for every $t$, and hence maximizes
every positive exit-time moment.  The theorem therefore proves the source
functional with its supremum over starting points as well.

\section{Scope, verification, and novelty}

The proof settles the full rectangular clause of Remark~5.5 for all
$d\ge2$ and all $p>0$, and strengthens it to stochastic domination.  It does
not settle Conjecture~5.4 for arbitrary coordinate-symmetric convex domains.
For such domains the independent-coordinate factorization is lost; moreover,
domain inclusion or symmetrization moves $\lambda_1$ and the exit-time moment
in opposite directions, so the rectangle argument does not formally extend.

An adversarial audit checked the Brownian/eigenvalue normalization, scaling,
tail-moment identity, equality class, and the $0<p<1$ range.  A bounded search
through 11 August 2026 found the source and standard interval-exit formulas
but no later proof of (5.22) or of the stochastic strengthening.  Priority is
not asserted.  The accompanying numerical script checks constants and sample
Jensen gaps but is not used in the proof.
