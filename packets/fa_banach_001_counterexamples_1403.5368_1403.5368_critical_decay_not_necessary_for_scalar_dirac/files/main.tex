\title{The strict decay condition in Proposition 3.3 is not necessary\\
\large A critical-endpoint theorem for the scalar Dirac operator}
\author{Candidate solution packet for arXiv:1403.5368}
\date{August 11, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Stahlhut asked whether the strict condition
$\tau>n/p-n/q$ in his $L^p$--$L^q$ off-diagonal functional-calculus
theorem is necessary.  We give a negative answer in general.  For the
one-dimensional unperturbed scalar Dirac operator $D=-i\,d/dx$, the entire
conclusion holds at the critical exponent $\tau=1/p-1/q$, uniformly for
every admissible symbol $\psi$ and every bounded holomorphic multiplier $g$.
In fact the resulting off-diagonal order is stronger than the order asserted
in the source proposition.  An explicit symbol with exact critical decay
shows that this is not an artifact of nested symbol classes.
\end{abstract}

\section*{1. Exact source question}

The source defines $\Psiclass{\sigma}{\tau}(\dot S_\nu)$ by the estimate
\[
 |\psi(z)|\lesssim \frac{|z|^\sigma}{1+|z|^{\sigma+\tau}}
 \qquad(z\in\dot S_\nu),
\]
where $\dot S_\nu$ is the union of the two open sectors about the positive
and negative real axes.  Proposition 3.3 states that, for $p<q$ in the
admissible interval and $\tau>n/p-n/q$, the families
$\{g(T)\psi(tT)\}_{t>0}$ satisfy $L^p$--$L^q$ off-diagonal estimates of all
orders $0\le K<\sigma/c_{p,q}$.  The immediately following remark reads:

\begin{quote}
``We do not know if the condition $\tau>\frac np-\frac nq$ is necessary in
Proposition 3.3.''
\end{quote}

For clarity, a family $\{A_t\}_{t>0}$ has $L^p$--$L^q$ off-diagonal estimates
of order $K$ when
\[
 \|\1_E A_t(\1_F f)\|_q
 \le C t^{1/q-1/p}
 \left(1+\frac{\dist(E,F)}t\right)^{-K}\|\1_Ff\|_p
\]
for all Borel sets $E,F\subset\R$, all $t>0$, and all $f\in L^p$.

\section*{2. Result and proof intuition}

Fix $1<p<q<\infty$ and put
\[
 \delta=\frac1p-\frac1q\in(0,1).
\]
At high frequency, a critical symbol has order $-\delta$.  Its Fourier
kernel therefore has the Riesz-potential singularity $|x|^{\delta-1}$,
which is exactly the Hardy--Littlewood--Sobolev kernel mapping $L^p$ to
$L^q$.  At low frequency, the zero of order $\sigma$ gives the much faster
far-field decay $|x|^{-1-\sigma}$.  The first behavior gives endpoint
boundedness; the second gives off-diagonal decay.  Holomorphy supplies all
annular derivative bounds uniformly, even after inserting the unscaled
factor $g(D)$.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform critical endpoint]
Let $D=-i\,d/dx$ on scalar functions on $\R$, let $B=I$, and fix
$0<\nu<\pi/2$, $\sigma>0$, and $1<p<q<\infty$.  Set
$\delta=1/p-1/q$.  For every
\[
 \psi\in\Psiclass{\sigma}{\delta}(\dot S_\nu)
 \quad\text{and}\quad g\in\Hinfty(\dot S_\nu),
\]
the family $\{g(D)\psi(tD)\}_{t>0}$ satisfies $L^p$--$L^q$
off-diagonal estimates of every order
\[
 0\le K\le \sigma+\delta.
\]
The constants are uniform in $t$, depend on $\psi$ through its defining
$\Psiclass{\sigma}{\delta}$ bound, and depend on $g$ only through
$\|g\|_\infty$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We use the Fourier transform convention for which $D$ has symbol $\xi$.
The multiplier of $g(D)\psi(tD)$ is
\[
 g(\xi)\psi(t\xi)=h_t(t\xi),
 \qquad h_t(\eta):=g(\eta/t)\psi(\eta).
\]

We first prove a kernel estimate uniform in $t$.  On either real half-axis,
a disk of radius $c_\nu|\eta|$ about $\eta$ remains inside the corresponding
component of $\dot S_\nu$.  Cauchy's inequalities, the boundedness of $g$,
and the defining estimate for $\psi$ imply, for every integer $m\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cauchy}
 |h_t^{(m)}(\eta)|\le C_m\|g\|_\infty |\eta|^{-m}
 \begin{cases}
 |\eta|^\sigma,&0<|\eta|\le1,\\
 |\eta|^{-\delta},&|\eta|\ge1.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The constants do not depend on $t$: positive dilation preserves each sector,
so $z\mapsto g(z/t)$ has the same supremum bound on it.

Choose $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\{1/2\le|\eta|\le2\})$ such that
$\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\varphi(2^{-j}\eta)=1$ for $\eta\ne0$, and put
\[
 h_{t,j}(\eta)=\varphi(2^{-j}\eta)h_t(\eta),
 \qquad \kappa_{t,j}=\F^{-1}h_{t,j}.
\]
Scaling and $M$ integrations by parts, using \eqref{eq:cauchy}, give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:band}
 |\kappa_{t,j}(x)|\le C_M,2^j a_j(1+2^j|x|)^{-M},
 \qquad
 a_j=\begin{cases}2^{j\sigma},&j\le0,\\2^{-j\delta},&j>0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This standard estimate can also be read directly after rescaling the
annulus to unit size: all derivatives of the rescaled multiplier are
$O(a_j)$, and Fourier integration by parts supplies the last factor.

The series of $L^1$ norms is bounded by $C\sum_j a_j<\infty$.  Hence
$\kappa_t:=\sum_j\kappa_{t,j}$ is an $L^1$ kernel representing $h_t$.
Summing \eqref{eq:band}, and splitting at $2^j|x|\simeq1$, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 |\kappa_t(x)|\le C
 \begin{cases}
 |x|^{\delta-1},&0<|x|\le1,\\
 |x|^{-1-\sigma},&|x|\ge1.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For completeness, when $|x|\le1$, the low-frequency sum is bounded and the
high-frequency sum is $O(|x|^{\delta-1})$.  When $|x|\ge1$, the low-frequency
sum is $O(|x|^{-1-\sigma})$; choosing $M>1+\sigma$ makes the high-frequency
sum smaller.  All bounds remain uniform in $t$.

The kernel of $g(D)\psi(tD)$ is therefore
\[
 K_t(x)=t^{-1}\kappa_t(x/t).
\]
Since \eqref{eq:kernel} implies
$|K_t(x)|\le C t^{-\delta}|x|^{\delta-1}$, the one-dimensional
Hardy--Littlewood--Sobolev inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hls}
 \|K_t*f\|_q\le C t^{-\delta}\|f\|_p.
\end{equation}

It remains to retain decay between separated sets.  Let
$\rho=\dist(E,F)$ and let $r$ satisfy
\[
 1+\frac1q=\frac1p+\frac1r,
 \qquad\text{so that}\qquad \frac1r=1-\delta.
\]
If $\rho\ge t$, \eqref{eq:kernel} and scaling give
\begin{align*}
 \|\1_{\{|x|\ge\rho\}}K_t\|_r
 &=t^{1/r-1}
   \|\1_{\{|u|\ge\rho/t\}}\kappa_t\|_r\\
 &\le C t^{-\delta}(\rho/t)^{-\sigma-\delta}.
\end{align*}
Young's convolution inequality applies because $x\in E$, $y\in F$ implies
$|x-y|\ge\rho$.  It thus supplies the desired estimate for every
$K\le\sigma+\delta$ when $\rho\ge t$.  When $\rho<t$, the global bound
\eqref{eq:hls} supplies it, since $(1+\rho/t)^{-K}$ is bounded below by
$2^{-K}$.  This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\section*{3. Genuine critical decay and comparison with Proposition 3.3}

The critical class is genuinely reached.  On each component of $\dot S_\nu$
define
\[
 w(z)=\sgn(\Re z)z,
 \qquad
 \psi_0(z)=\frac{w(z)^\sigma}{(1+w(z))^{\sigma+\delta}},
\]
using the principal powers.  Since $w$ lies in the right sector,
$|1+w|\simeq1+|w|$, so
$\psi_0\in\Psiclass{\sigma}{\delta}(\dot S_\nu)$.  Along the positive axis,
\[
 \lim_{s\to\infty}s^\delta\psi_0(s)=1.
\]
Consequently $\psi_0$ belongs to no
$\Psiclass{\sigma}{\tau'}(\dot S_\nu)$ with $\tau'>\delta$.

For $B=I$, the source records $\mathcal I_{D,I}=(1,\infty)$.  Hence in
dimension one its printed constant becomes
\[
 c_{p,q}=\bigl(1-(1/p-1/q)\bigr)^{-1}=(1-\delta)^{-1},
\]
and Proposition 3.3 asks for every
$K<\sigma/c_{p,q}=\sigma(1-\delta)$.  Our endpoint theorem gives every
$K\le\sigma+\delta$, and
$\sigma(1-\delta)<\sigma+\delta$.  Thus the full conclusion of Proposition
3.3 holds in this admissible model at equality
$\tau=n/p-n/q$, even for a symbol having no extra decay.

This supplies a full negative answer to the necessity question as stated.
It does \emph{not} prove that the endpoint holds for every rough perturbation
$DB$ or $BD$; that stronger universal endpoint problem remains open.

\section*{4. Verification and novelty boundary}

The proof uses only: (i) Fourier diagonalization of $-i\,d/dx$;
(ii) Cauchy estimates on sectors; (iii) a smooth dyadic partition and Fourier
integration by parts; (iv) the one-dimensional Hardy--Littlewood--Sobolev
inequality; and (v) Young's convolution inequality.  No numerical evidence
is used.  The two exponents in \eqref{eq:kernel} have independent roles:
$\delta-1$ gives the exact $L^p$--$L^q$ endpoint, while $-1-\sigma$ gives
the tail power $\sigma+\delta$.

The run registry and bounded primary-source searches through August 11, 2026
found no later paper claiming this resolution.  Priority is not asserted;
specialist citation review is recommended.  Human review should focus on the
uniformity of \eqref{eq:cauchy} under $g(\eta/t)$, the dyadic summation in
\eqref{eq:kernel}, and the intended logical reading of ``necessary'' in the
source remark.
