\section{Source questions and scope}

For a Banach space $X$, $n\geq2$, and $a\in[0,n)$, Pongsriiam and
Termwuttipong define $\gamma(X,n,a)$ as the infimum of Lipschitz bounds for
fixed-point-free $(n,a)$-rotative maps on closed convex subsets of $X$. Their
Questions 3--6 ask for finiteness, exact values, the case $X=C[0,1]$ with
$n=2$ and $a<1$, and continuity in $a$ \cite[p.~4]{PT14}.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering

  \caption{Questions 3--6 in \cite[p.~4]{PT14}.}

This note completely classifies the affine subclass for all Banach spaces and all
parameters: its threshold is always infinity. It also gives a quantified nonlinear
extension. These results do not determine the unrestricted function $\gamma$.

\section{Definitions and proof intuition}

Let $C$ be a nonempty closed convex subset of a Banach space. A map $T:C\to C$
is $(n,a)$-\emph{rotative} if
\[
 \|T^n x-x\|\leq a\|Tx-x\|\qquad(x\in C),
 \quad n\geq2,\quad 0\leq a<n.
\]
Write $d_T(x)=\|Tx-x\|$ and form the length-$n$ orbit barycenter
\[
 B_nx=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T^j x\in C.
\]

\paragraph{Proof intuition.}
For an affine map, applying $T$ to this barycenter shifts every orbit term forward by
one. All intermediate terms cancel when $TB_nx-B_nx$ is formed, leaving exactly
$(T^nx-x)/n$. Rotativeness therefore reduces the displacement by the strict factor
$a/n$. The move from $x$ to $B_nx$ is bounded by a fixed multiple of the current
displacement, using only the Lipschitz constant. Repeating the barycenter operation gives
geometrically decreasing displacement and summable moves, hence a Cauchy sequence whose
limit is fixed.

The same mechanism survives for a nonlinear map if its failure to commute with its own
orbit barycenter is quantitatively smaller than the remaining contraction margin.

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[Affine rotative fixed-point theorem]\label{thm:affine}
Let $C$ be a nonempty closed convex subset of a Banach space and let $T:C\to C$
be affine and $k$-Lipschitz for some finite $k\geq0$. If $T$ is $(n,a)$-rotative
for $n\geq2$ and $0\leq a<n$, then $T$ has a fixed point.

More precisely, for every $x_0\in C$, the iteration
\[
 x_{m+1}=B_nx_m=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T^jx_m
\]
converges to a point of $\Fix T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Affineness and convexity give
\[
 TB_nx=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T^{j+1}x,
 \qquad
 TB_nx-B_nx=\frac{T^nx-x}{n}.
\]
Consequently, with $q=a/n<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decay}
 d_T(B_nx)\leq qd_T(x).
\end{equation}

Since $T^r$ is $k^r$-Lipschitz,
\[
 \|T^{r+1}x-T^rx\|\leq k^r d_T(x).
\]
Thus
\begin{align*}
 \|B_nx-x\|
 &\leq\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\|T^jx-x\|\\
 &\leq \underbrace{\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}
       \sum_{r=0}^{j-1}k^r}_{M_{n,k}}d_T(x).
\end{align*}
Applying \eqref{eq:decay} recursively yields
\[
 d_T(x_m)\leq q^m d_T(x_0),
 \qquad
 \|x_{m+1}-x_m\|\leq M_{n,k}q^m d_T(x_0).
\]
The second estimate is summable, so $(x_m)$ is Cauchy. Closedness of $C$ gives a
limit $p\in C$. As $T$ is Lipschitz and $d_T(x_m)\to0$, we obtain $Tp=p$.
\end{proof}

Define the affine-restricted analogue of the source invariant by
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \gamma_{\mathrm{aff}}(X,n,a)=\inf\{k:\;&\text{some affine }T\in
 \Phi(C,n,a,k)\text{ on a closed convex }C\subseteq X\\
 &\text{has }\Fix T=\varnothing\},
 \end{aligned}
\]
with $\inf\varnothing=\infty$.

\begin{corollary}[Complete affine classification]\label{cor:class}
For every Banach space $X$, every $n\geq2$, and every $a\in[0,n)$,
\[
 \gamma_{\mathrm{aff}}(X,n,a)=\infty.
\]
Hence every finite witness for $\gamma(X,n,a)$ must be genuinely nonlinear.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Robust orbit-barycentric criterion]\label{thm:robust}
Let $T:C\to C$ be $k$-Lipschitz and $(n,a)$-rotative. Suppose that some
$\eta\geq0$ satisfies, for every $x\in C$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect}
 \left\|T(B_nx)-\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^{n}T^jx\right\|
 \leq\eta d_T(x).
\end{equation}
If $\eta+a/n<1$, then $T$ has a fixed point, and the iteration
$x_{m+1}=B_nx_m$ converges to one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combining \eqref{eq:defect} with the telescoping identity for the formal shifted
barycenter gives
\[
 d_T(B_nx)\leq\left(\eta+\frac an\right)d_T(x).
\]
The bound $\|B_nx-x\|\leq M_{n,k}d_T(x)$ did not use affineness. With
$q=\eta+a/n<1$, the same geometric-decay and summable-step proof as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:affine} applies.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, failed full-upgrade routes, and verification}

The result eliminates every affine construction from Questions 3--6 and gives a stable
nonlinear neighborhood measured by \eqref{eq:defect}. It does not compute the unrestricted
$\gamma(X,n,a)$. In particular, at $a=0$ the source problem already contains the
fixed-point theory of Lipschitz periodic maps; a 2017 paper still asks whether a
fixed-point-free $2$-Lipschitz involution exists on a closed convex Banach subset
\cite[Question~4.2]{Gornicki17}.

Eight focused routes were audited: source-signal separation; arbitrary affine operators;
the robust defect extension; positive unilateral-shift constructions; an alternating
affine shift on $c_0$; direct contraction of orbit increments; periodic/involution
constructions at $a=0$; and deformation arguments for continuity of $\gamma$. The two
barycentric routes succeeded. Both shift families attain the forbidden ratio $2$, direct
increment estimates lose the contraction, and no fixed-point-free nonlinear involution or
parameter deformation was obtained.

The proof is symbolic. Verification reduces to the affine telescoping identity, the
finite constant $M_{n,k}$, and the geometric-series Cauchy estimate. The run's four cheap
indexes and a bounded web search through 17 August 2026 used the exact arXiv id and the
phrases ``affine rotative mapping'', ``orbit barycenter'', and
``$\gamma(X,n,a)$''. The source, the 1999 rotative results, affine fixed-point searches,
and the 2017 involution discussion were checked. No exact statement of
Theorems~\ref{thm:affine} or \ref{thm:robust} was found. This is bounded evidence, not a
priority claim.
