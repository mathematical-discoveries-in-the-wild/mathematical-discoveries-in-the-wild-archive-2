\section{Source question}

For a probability measure $\mu$ on $PSU(1,1)$, the source studies holomorphic
solutions of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
 \int f(\gamma^{-1}.z)(\gamma^{-1})'(z)\,d\mu(\gamma)-f(z)
 =\int \frac{d\mu(\gamma)}{z-\gamma.\infty}.
\end{equation}
Its first open question asks whether, for finitely supported $\mu$, every
solution has radius of convergence exactly $1$.  It also proposes that
rational solutions should occur only when $\mu$ is supported on a single
element~\cite{K}.

\section{Counterexample}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:counterexample}
There is a two-point probability measure $\mu$ on $PSU(1,1)$, both of whose
support elements move the origin, for which \eqref{eq:source} has the nonzero
rational solution
\[
 f(z)=\frac{1}{2-z}.
\]
Its Taylor series at the origin has radius of convergence $2$.  Consequently,
both literal assertions in the source question are false.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $a=\tfrac12$, let $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$, and define
\[
 h(z)=\frac{z+a}{1+az},\qquad r_j(z)=\omega^jz,\qquad
 \gamma_j=h\circ r_j\circ h^{-1}\quad(j=1,2).
\]
These are elements of $PSU(1,1)$.  Let
\[
 \mu=\tfrac12(\delta_{\gamma_1}+\delta_{\gamma_2}),
 \qquad \nu=h_*m,
\]
where $m$ is normalized Lebesgue measure on $\T$.  Rotation invariance gives
$(r_j)_*m=m$, and hence
\[
 (\gamma_j)_*\nu=h_*(r_j)_*m=h_*m=\nu.
\]
Thus $\nu$ is invariant under each support element and, in particular, is
$\mu$-stationary.

We compute its Cauchy transform.  For $k\geq1$, the mean-value property for
the holomorphic function $h^k$ gives
\[
 \int_{\T}\xi^k\,d\nu(\xi)
 =\int_{\T}h(\eta)^k\,dm(\eta)=h(0)^k=a^k.
\]
Since $a$ is real and $|\xi|=1$, conjugation yields
$\int\xi^{-k}\,d\nu(\xi)=a^k$.  Therefore, for $|z|<1$,
\[
 f_\nu(z)
 =\int_{\T}\frac{d\nu(\xi)}{\xi-z}
 =\sum_{n\geq0}z^n\int_{\T}\xi^{-(n+1)}\,d\nu(\xi)
 =\sum_{n\geq0}a^{n+1}z^n
 =\frac{a}{1-az}=\frac{1}{2-z}.
\]
The source's main theorem says that the Cauchy transform of every stationary
probability measure satisfies \eqref{eq:source}.  Hence $f_\nu$ is a solution.
It is nonzero and rational, and its unique pole is at $z=2$, so its Taylor
radius is $2$.

Finally, for $j=1,2$,
\[
 \gamma_j(0)=h(-a\omega^j)
 =\frac{a(1-\omega^j)}{1-a^2\omega^j}\neq0.
\]
The two maps are distinct, so $|\operatorname{supp}\mu|=2$.  This completes
all claimed properties.
\end{proof}

\section{\hspace{0.25em}Why this is the correct obstruction}

The example is not an accident at the level of a single formula.  If $K$ is
any finite rotation group, $h\in PSU(1,1)$, and $\mu$ is supported on at least
two elements of $hKh^{-1}$, then $h_*m$ is invariant under every support
element.  When $h(0)=a$, after a harmless rotation making $a>0$, its Cauchy
transform is $a/(1-az)$ and has radius $1/a>1$.  Thus an entire family of
finite elliptic examples defeats the proposed conclusion.

At the un-conjugated center the kernel is also transparent.  For
$\rho_\alpha(z)=\alpha z$,
\[
 f(z)=\sum_{n\geq0}c_nz^n
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 f(\rho_\alpha^{-1}z)(\rho_\alpha^{-1})'(z)
 =\sum_{n\geq0}c_n\alpha^{-(n+1)}z^n.
\]
For a probability measure on rotations, a coefficient $c_n$ may be nonzero
exactly when every supported rotation satisfies $\alpha^{n+1}=1$.  In a
cyclic group of order $q$, all functions $z^{q-1}g(z^q)$ solve the homogeneous
equation.  Choosing $g$ entire gives further nonzero entire solutions with
multi-point support.  Conjugation produces the stronger off-center rational
solution used in Theorem~\ref{thm:counterexample}.

\section{Verification and scope}

The accompanying SymPy script performs an independent exact check for
$a=1/2$ and $q=3$: it constructs $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$ and their inverses,
checks both maps move $0$, and simplifies the left side of
\eqref{eq:source} minus the right side to zero.  All arithmetic lies in
$\mathbb Q(i\sqrt3)(z)$; numerical approximation is not used.

This counterexample meets the source's earlier nontriviality condition that
some supported map not fix the origin.  It does generate only a finite
elliptic (hence elementary) subgroup.  It therefore does not settle a
strengthened question requiring the support to generate a non-elementary
group or a lattice, which is the setting relevant to the singularity
conjecture.  Such a hypothesis, or at least exclusion of compact elementary
subgroups, is necessary for the proposed rigidity statement.

\section{Conclusion}

The first open question has a negative answer as written.  A two-point measure
supported on an off-center finite elliptic group admits the stationary Cauchy
transform $1/(2-z)$, whose radius is $2$.  The same example refutes the
proposed one-point-support restriction for rational solutions.  Human review
should confirm the literal scope and, if desired, restate the surviving
problem with a non-elementary or lattice-generation assumption.
