\section*{Source assertion}
Daniel Lazarev, \emph{Stokes' theorem as an entropy-extremizing duality},
arXiv:2509.16386v2, closes by considering alternative derivative operators
$\mathfrak d$ satisfying the Stokes integral relation and reports preliminary
evidence that entropy is uniquely maximized at $\mathfrak d=\dd$.

\section*{Precise interpretation and verdict}
The source defines $S^*(\alpha,M)$ for a top form $\alpha$, but does not
separately define entropy of an operator.  The direct extension is therefore
to compare
\[
 S^*(\mathfrak d\omega,M).
\]
Under this natural formulation the assertion is false, even for smooth local
first-order linear operators satisfying Stokes' identity for \emph{every}
smooth input.  If a different operator entropy is intended, it must first be
defined.

\begin{theorem}[Smooth local counterexample]\label{thm:counterexample}
Let $M=[0,1]$ with its standard orientation.  For every
$0<\varepsilon<1$ there is a local first-order linear operator
\[
 \De:C^\infty([0,1])\longrightarrow\Omega^1([0,1])
\]
such that
\[
 \int_M\De g=g(1)-g(0)=\int_{\partial M}g
 \qquad\text{for every }g\in C^\infty([0,1]), \tag{1}
\]
but, for $\omega(x)=x$,
\[
 S^*(\De\omega,M)>S^*(\dd\omega,M)=0. \tag{2}
\]
Consequently the exterior derivative is not an entropy maximizer in the
announced competitor class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $x>0$ set
\[
 a_\varepsilon(x)=1+\varepsilon
 \frac{1-\cos(2\pi x)}{2\pi x},
 \qquad a_\varepsilon(0)=1,
\]
and define
\[
 \De g=\dd(a_\varepsilon g)
 =\bigl(a_\varepsilon g'+a_\varepsilon'g\bigr)\,\dd x. \tag{3}
\]
The Taylor series of $1-\cos(2\pi x)$ shows that $a_\varepsilon$ extends
smoothly at zero.  Moreover
$a_\varepsilon(0)=a_\varepsilon(1)=1$.  The fundamental theorem of calculus
now gives (1), so this is a globally Stokes-compatible operator, not merely
one tailored to a single $\omega$.

For $\omega(x)=x$, direct differentiation gives
\[
 \De\omega
 =\dd\!\left(x+\varepsilon\frac{1-\cos(2\pi x)}{2\pi}\right)
 =\bigl(1+\varepsilon\sin(2\pi x)\bigr)\,\dd x. \tag{4}
\]
Its density is
$\rho_\varepsilon(x)=1+\varepsilon\sin(2\pi x)>0$ and its source
normalization is $C=\|\rho_\varepsilon\|_\infty=1+\varepsilon$.  Hence
\[
 S^*(\De\omega,M)=
 \int_0^1\rho_\varepsilon(x)
 \log\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\rho_\varepsilon(x)}\,\dd x>0. \tag{5}
\]
Indeed the integrand is nonnegative everywhere and positive except on the
measure-zero set where $\sin(2\pi x)=1$.  On the other hand
$\dd\omega=\dd x$ has constant density one and $C=1$, so its entropy is
zero.  This proves (2).  Notice that nonnegativity of $S^*$ makes $\dd$ a
minimum, not a maximum, for this example.
\end{proof}

\section*{What extra axioms can rescue the claim?}
The construction (3) is already local and first order.  Thus locality alone
does not remove the obstruction.  The natural stronger axiom is the Leibniz
rule.

\begin{proposition}[Derivation rigidity]
Suppose a scalar local first-order operator on $[0,1]$ is a derivation and
satisfies (1) for every smooth input.  Then it equals $\dd$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every such derivation has the form $Dg=a(x)g'(x)\,\dd x$.  For a compactly
supported test function $g$, (1) gives $\int_0^1a g'=0$; distributional
integration by parts yields $a'=0$.  Thus $a$ is constant.  Taking $g(x)=x$
in (1) then gives $a=1$.
\end{proof}

This produces a sharp dichotomy.  In the broad linear or local first-order
class announced in the source, Theorem~\ref{thm:counterexample} disproves
entropy maximality.  In the derivation class, Stokes compatibility already
forces the sole competitor to be $\dd$, so uniqueness is algebraic and the
entropy variational principle is vacuous.
