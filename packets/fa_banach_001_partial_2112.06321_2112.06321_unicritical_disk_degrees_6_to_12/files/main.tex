\section{Source question and scope}

Bickel and Gorkin ask the following in Question~1 of Section~6, PDF page~28
of \cite{BG21}.

\begin{quote}
If $\Theta$ is unicritical with $\deg\Theta=n$, does $W(S_\Theta)$
necessarily contain a pseudohyperbolic disk with pseudohyperbolic radius
$(1/2)^{1/(n-1)}$?
\end{quote}

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{The complete source question and its immediate context, rendered
  from PDF page~28 of \cite{BG21}.}
\end{figure}

The source proves the requested assertion through degree four.  Bickel,
Corbett, Glenning, Guan, and Vollmayr-Lee later proved the degree-five case,
in fact with radius $\sqrt3/2$, in Theorem~2.1 of \cite{BCGGV23}.  Their
Remark following Corollary~2.2 reports numerical evidence in degrees
$6,7,8,$ and $11$, but not a proof.  The result below rigorously covers every
degree from six through twelve.

\section{Idea of the proof}

For a unicritical product with repeated zero $t\in[0,1)$, the compressed
shift is represented by a KMS matrix
\[
 M_{n,t}=tI+(1-t^2)A_{n,t},\qquad
 (A_{n,t})_{ij}=\begin{cases}(-t)^{j-i-1},&i<j,\\0,&i\geq j.\end{cases}
\]
Bickel and Gorkin give an explicit trigonometric curve $f_{n,t}(s)$ inside
$W(A_{n,t})$.  Its values at $s=0$ and $s=\pi$ have midpoint $b_{n,t}$ and
half-separation $L_{n,t}>0$.

The key new point is an exact lower bound
\[
 |f_{n,t}(s)-b_{n,t}|\geq \kappa_n L_{n,t}
 \quad (0\leq t\leq1,\;s\in\mathbb R),
\]
for explicit rational $\kappa_n<1$.  Since the centered curve has winding
number one, this puts the whole disk
$D(b_{n,t},\kappa_nL_{n,t})$ in its convex hull.  Affine rescaling puts a
corresponding Euclidean disk in $W(M_{n,t})$.  A second exact inequality
shows that this Euclidean disk has pseudohyperbolic radius at least
$2^{-1/(n-1)}$.

Both inequalities reduce to polynomial positivity on a unit box.  The
included checker proves positivity using exact rational Bernstein
coefficients, with exact isolating intervals and error bounds for the two
algebraic constants.  Thus the computation is a finite proof certificate,
not sampled numerical evidence.

\section{The partial theorem}

Recall that a degree-$n$ unicritical Blaschke product has the form
\[
 \Theta(z)=\lambda\left(\frac{z-z_0}{1-\overline{z_0}z}\right)^n,
 \qquad |\lambda|=1,\quad z_0\in\D.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Degrees six through twelve]\label{thm:main}
Let $6\leq n\leq12$, and let $\Theta$ be a degree-$n$ unicritical finite
Blaschke product.  Then $W(S_\Theta)$ contains a pseudohyperbolic disk of
radius
\[
 q_n=2^{-1/(n-1)}.
\]
Consequently, together with \cite{BG21,BCGGV23}, the source Question~1 has
an affirmative answer for every degree $2\leq n\leq12$.
\end{theorem}

\section{The KMS curve}

Multiplying $\Theta$ by a unimodular constant does not change the model
operator.  The rotation identity for the numerical range in \cite{BG21}
reduces the repeated zero to $t=|z_0|\in[0,1)$.  In the
Takenaka--Malmquist--Walsh basis, the matrix of $S_\Theta$ is $M_{n,t}$ as
displayed above.

Put $\vartheta_n=\pi/(n+1)$ and
\[
 a_{n,k}=\frac{(n-k)\cos(k\vartheta_n)\sin\vartheta_n
 +\sin((n-k)\vartheta_n)}{(n+1)\sin\vartheta_n}
 \qquad(1\leq k\leq n-1).
\]
Proposition~5.1 of \cite{BG21} gives the curve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve}
 f_{n,t}(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{n,k}(-t)^{k-1}e^{iks}
 \in W(A_{n,t}).
\end{equation}
For completeness, this follows by testing $A_{n,t}$ on the unit vector with
coordinates
\[
 x_\ell(s)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n+1}}\sin(\ell\vartheta_n)e^{i(\ell-1)s}.
\]

Define
\begin{align}
 b_{n,t}&=\sum_{\substack{1\leq k\leq n-1\\ k\text{ even}}}
             a_{n,k}(-t)^{k-1},\label{eq:centerA}\\
 L_{n,t}&=\sum_{\substack{1\leq k\leq n-1\\ k\text{ odd}}}
             a_{n,k}t^{k-1}.\label{eq:LA}
\end{align}
All $a_{n,k}$ are positive, so $L_{n,t}>0$.  Directly from
\eqref{eq:curve},
\[
 f_{n,t}(0)=b_{n,t}+L_{n,t},\qquad
 f_{n,t}(\pi)=b_{n,t}-L_{n,t}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Certified curve separation]\label{lem:curve}
For every $t\in[0,1]$ and $s\in\mathbb R$,
\[
 |f_{n,t}(s)-b_{n,t}|\geq \kappa_nL_{n,t},
\]
where the constants are as follows.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c@{\qquad}c|c@{\qquad}c}
\toprule
$n$&$\kappa_n$&$n$&$\kappa_n$\\
\midrule
6&$124/125$&10&$499/500$\\
7&$997/1000$&11&$999/1000$\\
8&$249/250$&12&$999/1000$\\
9&$499/500$&&\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The inequality is strict after squaring and moving the right side to the
left.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $c_n=\cos\vartheta_n$ and $x=\cos s$.  The identity
\[
 \frac{\sin((j+1)\vartheta_n)}{\sin\vartheta_n}=U_j(c_n)
\]
expresses every $a_{n,k}$ as a polynomial in $c_n$ with rational
coefficients.  Because all coefficients of $f_{n,t}$ are real,
\[
 P_n(t,x,c_n)=|f_{n,t}(s)-b_{n,t}|^2-\kappa_n^2L_{n,t}^2
\]
is a polynomial in $t$, $x$, and $c_n$.  Substitute $x=2y-1$.

The script \texttt{code/verify\_degrees\_6\_to\_12.py} constructs this
polynomial exactly.  It isolates $c_n$ by its exact minimal polynomial in a
rational interval of width $10^{-32}$.  At the rational midpoint it
subdivides $[0,1]^2$ into rational boxes.  On every box all tensor-product
Bernstein coefficients of the transformed polynomial are nonnegative.
The polynomial is therefore nonnegative there, since it is a convex
combination of those coefficients in the Bernstein basis.  Finally, an
exact coefficient $\ell^1$ bound for $\partial P_n/\partial c$ dominates the
whole isolating-interval error.  The resulting lower bounds remain strictly
positive.  Table~\ref{tab:certificate} records the certificate sizes and
decimal displays of the exact rational lower bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Disk from the winding curve]\label{lem:winding}
For $6\leq n\leq12$ and $0\leq t<1$,
\[
 D(b_{n,t},\kappa_nL_{n,t})\subseteq W(A_{n,t}).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The curve \eqref{eq:curve} is closed and lies in the convex set
$W(A_{n,t})$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:curve}, its translate
$f_{n,t}(s)-b_{n,t}$ never vanishes.  Its winding number about zero is
constant under the homotopy $t\in[0,1]$.  At $t=0$ the curve is
$a_{n,1}e^{is}$, so the winding number is one.

If $|w-b_{n,t}|<\kappa_nL_{n,t}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:curve} allows $w$ to be
moved to $b_{n,t}$ without crossing the curve.  Hence the curve has winding
number one about $w$.  A point outside the convex hull of a closed curve can
be strictly separated from it by a line, and the curve then has winding
number zero about that point.  Thus every such $w$ lies in the convex hull
of the curve, which is contained in $W(A_{n,t})$.  Closure gives the claimed
closed disk.
\end{proof}

\section{Pseudohyperbolic radius and proof of the theorem}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:winding} and the affine relation between $M_{n,t}$ and
$A_{n,t}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-disk}
 D(C_{n,t},R_{n,t})\subseteq W(M_{n,t}),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CR}
 C_{n,t}=t+(1-t^2)b_{n,t},\qquad
 R_{n,t}=(1-t^2)\kappa_nL_{n,t}.
\end{equation}
Every Euclidean disk contained in $\D$ is a pseudohyperbolic disk.  If its
pseudohyperbolic radius is $r\in(0,1)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conversion}
 r+\frac1r=\frac{R^2-|C|^2+1}{R};
\end{equation}
see Equation~(13) of \cite{BCGGV23}.

\begin{lemma}[Certified radius comparison]\label{lem:radius}
For $6\leq n\leq12$, $0\leq t<1$, and $q_n=2^{-1/(n-1)}$, the quantities
in \eqref{eq:CR} satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radius-ineq}
 q_n\bigl(R_{n,t}^2-C_{n,t}^2+1\bigr)
 -(q_n^2+1)R_{n,t}\leq0.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The left side of \eqref{eq:radius-ineq} factors exactly as
\[
 (t^2-1)Q_n(t,c_n,q_n).
\]
The verifier isolates both algebraic constants by their exact minimal
polynomials.  At the rational interval midpoints, every univariate
Bernstein coefficient of $Q_n$ on $[0,1]$ is positive, without subdivision.
Exact coefficient $\ell^1$ bounds for the derivatives with respect to
$c_n$ and $q_n$ dominate the isolating-interval errors.  The final exact
rational lower bounds for $Q_n$ are the last column of
Table~\ref{tab:certificate}.  Hence $Q_n>0$, while $t^2-1<0$ for $t<1$,
which proves \eqref{eq:radius-ineq}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Let $r_{n,t}$ be the pseudohyperbolic radius of the disk in
\eqref{eq:euclidean-disk}.  Dividing \eqref{eq:radius-ineq} by the positive
quantity $q_nR_{n,t}$ gives
\[
 \frac{R_{n,t}^2-C_{n,t}^2+1}{R_{n,t}}
 \leq q_n+\frac1{q_n}.
\]
The function $u\mapsto u+u^{-1}$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,1]$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:conversion} therefore implies $r_{n,t}\geq q_n$.
This proves the theorem for a repeated zero $t\in[0,1)$.  The rotation
reduction at the start of Section~4 handles every $z_0\in\D$.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, novelty check, and limitations}

The proof certificate is reproducible with
\begin{verbatim}
conda run --no-capture-output -n sandbox python \
  code/verify_degrees_6_to_12.py
\end{verbatim}
It prints one \texttt{PASS} line for each $n=6,\ldots,12$.
The complete recorded output and an explanation of why the checks are exact
appear in \path{verification_report.txt}.  The smaller
\path{code/verify_degree6.py} independently specializes the construction to
the first previously unresolved degree.

A bounded primary-source search was performed on 11--12 August 2026.  It
included arXiv searches for the exact phrases ``pseudohyperbolic disk
criterion'', ``level set Crouzeix'', ``unicritical Blaschke numerical
range'', and the radius $\cos(\pi/(n+1))$, together with citation tracing from
arXiv:2112.06321.  The search found arXiv:2312.04537, which explicitly proves
degree five and reports only numerical evidence in several higher degrees.
No primary arXiv source proving degrees six through twelve or all degrees was
located.  This is a bounded search, not a definitive priority claim.

The limitation is essential: the certificate is finite and degree-specific.
The computations strongly suggest a uniform inequality, but no proof of that
uniform inequality was obtained.  In particular, this packet does not answer
the source question for $n\geq13$.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
The result is suitable for expert review as a candidate partial theorem.
The two most important manual checks are Lemma~\ref{lem:winding} and the
direction of the monotonicity argument following
Equation~\eqref{eq:conversion}.  The exact verifier should then be rerun in a
clean environment.
