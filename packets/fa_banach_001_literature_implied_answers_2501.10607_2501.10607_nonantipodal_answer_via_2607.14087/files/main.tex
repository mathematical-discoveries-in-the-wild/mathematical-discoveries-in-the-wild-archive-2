\section{Source question and exact scope}

Let $C_d(x)$ be the geodesic cap in $\Sph^{d-1}$ centered at
$x\in\Sph^{d-1}$ and having normalized surface measure $1/N$.  Define the
unrestricted optimum
\[
 U_N(d):=\sup_{x_1,\ldots,x_N\in\Sph^{d-1}}
 \sigma_{d-1}\!\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^N C_d(x_i)\right).
\]
Hoehner--Kur prove the limit $1-e^{-1}$ when the centers occur in antipodal
pairs.  On page~2 they explicitly write that they ``do not know how to remove
the antipodality assumption.''  The surrounding paragraph is reproduced
below for location verification.

The present packet settles precisely that first direction in the same regime.
It does not settle the paper's exponential-regime, monotonicity, or
Laguerre--Dirichlet--Voronoi questions.

\section{Result}

\begin{theorem}[Full non-antipodal extension]\label{thm:main}
Let $N=N(d)$ be any integer sequence such that $N(d)\to\infty$ and
$\log N(d)=o(\sqrt d)$.  Then
\[
 \lim_{d\to\infty}U_N(d)=1-e^{-1}.
\]
Thus arbitrary arrangements of $N$ equal caps are asymptotically no better
than random arrangements; no antipodal hypothesis is needed.
\end{theorem}

The proof imports one later theorem and otherwise uses estimates already
proved by the source paper.  We state the imported result in exactly the form
needed here.

\begin{theorem}[Mulgund, Theorem 2.1]\label{thm:mulgund}
Let $R$ be an $m\times m$ correlation matrix and let $J=\mathbf1\mathbf1^T$.
If $R-J/m\succeq0$ and $X\sim N(0,R)$, then for every $c\in\R$,
\[
 \PP\{X_i\le c\text{ for all }i\}\ge \Phi(c)^m.
\]
Singular correlation matrices are allowed.
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof intuition}

The source paper replaces each spherical cap by its Gaussian cone and then
shows that, at a slightly trimmed level $u$, each cone differs from the
one-sided Gaussian halfspace with the same axis by only $o(1/N)$ in measure.
This estimate is rotationally uniform and does not use antipodality.
Consequently, the unrestricted problem becomes a statement about the maximum
of an arbitrary centered Gaussian vector with unit coordinate variances.

Mulgund's theorem does not apply directly to its covariance $G$.  The useful
normalization is
\[
 R=\frac{N-1}{N}G+\frac1N J.
\]
It is always admissible because $R-J/N=((N-1)/N)G\succeq0$.  Probabilistically,
this merely rescales the original Gaussian vector and adds the same small
independent normal variable to every coordinate.  At an extreme-value level
$u\sim\sqrt{2\log N}$, that common perturbation is negligible.  Mulgund's
independent lower-orthant bound therefore yields a universal no-hit
probability of at least $e^{-1}-o(1)$.

\section{Proof}

Write $\phibar(t)=1-\Phi(t)$.  We use the following consequence of
Theorem~\ref{thm:mulgund}.

\begin{lemma}[Universal rare-exceedance bound]\label{lem:gaussian}
For each $N$, let $G_N$ be an arbitrary $N\times N$ correlation matrix and
let $\xi^{(N)}\sim N(0,G_N)$.  If $u_N\to\infty$ and
$N\phibar(u_N)\to1$, then, uniformly over $G_N$,
\[
 \PP\!\left\{\max_{1\le i\le N}\xi_i^{(N)}\le u_N\right\}
 \ge e^{-1}-o(1).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $a_N=(N-1)/N$ and
\[
 R_N=a_NG_N+\frac1N J.
\]
This is a correlation matrix and $R_N-J/N=a_NG_N\succeq0$.  If $W$ is an
independent standard normal random variable, then
\[
 X_i:=\sqrt{a_N}\,\xi_i^{(N)}+\frac{W}{\sqrt N}
\]
has covariance $R_N$.  Let $L_N=N^{1/4}$ and
\[
 c_N:=\sqrt{a_N}\,u_N-\frac{L_N}{\sqrt N}
      =\sqrt{a_N}\,u_N-N^{-1/4}.
\]
Because the same $W$ is added to all coordinates,
\[
 \max_iX_i=\sqrt{a_N}\max_i\xi_i^{(N)}+\frac{W}{\sqrt N}.
\]
Hence the event $\{\max_iX_i\le c_N\}\cap\{W\ge-L_N\}$ is contained in
$\{\max_i\xi_i^{(N)}\le u_N\}$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:mulgund} gives
\begin{align*}
 \PP\{\max_i\xi_i^{(N)}\le u_N\}
 &\ge \PP\{\max_iX_i\le c_N\}-\PP\{W<-L_N\}\\
 &\ge \Phi(c_N)^N-o(1).
\end{align*}

It remains to identify the limit.  From $N\phibar(u_N)\to1$ and Mills'
ratio, $u_N\sim\sqrt{2\log N}$.  Moreover,
\[
 0\le u_N-c_N=(1-\sqrt{a_N})u_N+N^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 u_N(u_N-c_N)=o(1).
\]
The standard Mills-ratio comparison therefore gives
$\phibar(c_N)/\phibar(u_N)\to1$.  Thus
$N\phibar(c_N)\to1$, and
$\Phi(c_N)^N\to e^{-1}$.  This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Fix arbitrary centers $x_1,\ldots,x_N\in\Sph^{d-1}$ and let $A_i$ be the
positive cone over $C_d(x_i)$.  For $Y\sim N(0,I_d)$, radial invariance gives
\[
 \sigma_{d-1}\!\left(\bigcup_iC_d(x_i)\right)
 =\gauss\!\left(\bigcup_iA_i\right),
 \qquad \gauss(A_i)=\frac1N.
\]

Let $t=t_{d,N}$ satisfy $\phibar(t)=1/N$.  In the proof of its Theorem~1,
the source paper constructs a positive trim $s_d$ and $u=t+s_d$ satisfying
\[
 \sqrt{\log N}\,s_d\to0,
 \qquad
 \phibar(u)=\frac{1+o(1)}N,
\]
and proves, uniformly in the cap axis $x$, that for
$H(x)=\{y:\langle y,x\rangle\ge u\}$,
\[
 \gauss\bigl(A(x)\cap H(x)\bigr)=\frac{1+o(1)}N.
\]
Since $\gauss(A(x))=1/N$ and
$\gauss(H(x))=\phibar(u)=(1+o(1))/N$, inclusion--exclusion yields the
stronger reformulation
\[
 \gauss\bigl(A(x)\mathbin\triangle H(x)\bigr)=o(1/N),
\]
again uniformly in $x$.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unionapprox}
 \left|\gauss\!\left(\bigcup_iA_i\right)
 -\gauss\!\left(\bigcup_iH(x_i)\right)\right|
 \le\sum_{i=1}^N\gauss(A_i\mathbin\triangle H(x_i))=o(1).
\end{equation}

Now put $G=(\langle x_i,x_j\rangle)_{i,j}$ and
$\xi_i=\langle Y,x_i\rangle$.  Then $G$ is a possibly singular correlation
matrix and
\[
 \gauss\!\left(\bigcup_iH(x_i)\right)
 =\PP\{\max_i\xi_i\ge u\}.
\]
Because $N\phibar(u)\to1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian} implies
\[
 \PP\{\max_i\xi_i<u\}\ge e^{-1}-o(1).
\]
(The strict and non-strict inequalities have the same probability because
each Gaussian coordinate has a continuous marginal.)
Together with \eqref{eq:unionapprox}, this proves, uniformly over all centers,
\[
 U_N(d)\le1-e^{-1}+o(1).
\]

For the reverse inequality, choose $x_1,\ldots,x_N$ independently and
uniformly on the sphere.  For every fixed point $z\in\Sph^{d-1}$, the events
$z\in C_d(x_i)$ are independent and each has probability $1/N$.  Fubini's
theorem gives
\[
 \EE\,\sigma_{d-1}\!\left(\bigcup_iC_d(x_i)\right)
 =1-\left(1-\frac1N\right)^N.
\]
Some deterministic configuration attains at least this expectation, so
$U_N(d)\ge1-(1-1/N)^N\to1-e^{-1}$.  The two bounds prove the theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and dependency audit}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Imported theorem.} Theorem~2.1 of arXiv:2607.14087 states the
lower-orthant inequality for every correlation matrix $R$ with
$R-J/m\succeq0$, including singular matrices.
\item \textbf{Normalization.} $R_N=((N-1)/N)G_N+J/N$ has diagonal one and
$R_N-J/N=((N-1)/N)G_N\succeq0$.
\item \textbf{Event inclusion.} The common shift is exact, so no independence
between the two events is used; only
$\PP(A\cap B)\ge\PP(A)-\PP(B^c)$ is invoked.
\item \textbf{Tail stability.} The perturbation obeys
$u_N(u_N-c_N)=O(u_N^2/N+u_NN^{-1/4})=o(1)$, precisely the scale needed for
the normal-tail ratio to tend to one.
\item \textbf{Geometric uniformity.} The source paper proves its cone estimate
for one axis and invokes rotational invariance.  All caps have the same area,
so the $o(1/N)$ error is uniform and survives summation over $N$ caps.
\item \textbf{No rank assumption.} The Gram matrix may have rank smaller than
$N$; both the Gaussian construction and Mulgund's theorem permit this.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Literature search, novelty, and limitations}

Targeted searches for the two arXiv identifiers together, for the source
paper's antipodality phrase together with Mulgund's title, and for partial
sphere coverings together with stochastic domination of Gaussian maxima did
not reveal an explicit statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  The result should
therefore be read as an agent-identified corollary of a later primary theorem,
not as a claim that the Gaussian comparison itself is new.

The proof does \emph{not} extend the dimensional regime.  Its spherical
input is the source estimate $\log N=o(\sqrt d)$.  It also gives no monotonicity
of the antipodal quantity $V_N(d)$ and no value of the
Laguerre--Dirichlet--Voronoi tiling constant.  Those questions remain open on
the evidence reviewed here.
