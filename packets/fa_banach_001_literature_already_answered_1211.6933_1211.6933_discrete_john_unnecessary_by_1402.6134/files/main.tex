\fbox{\parbox{0.9\textwidth}{
\textbf{Status: literature already answered (fully).}
The question in arXiv:1211.6933 asks whether a discrete John/accessibility
condition is really needed to deduce a Hardy inequality and quasiadditivity
of variational capacity when the complement has Aikawa codimension greater
than $p$.  Theorem~1.1 and Corollary~1.4 of arXiv:1402.6134 remove that
condition in the same metric-space setting.
}}
\end{center}

\section{The source question}

J. Lehrb\"ack and N. Shanmugalingam, \emph{Quasiadditivity of variational
capacity}, arXiv:1211.6933, work in a complete doubling metric measure
space $X$ supporting a $p$-Poincar\'e inequality, with $1<p<\infty$.
For an open set $\Omega\subset X$, their Proposition~4.1 proves that
\[
  \operatorname{codim}_{A}(X\setminus\Omega)>p
\]
implies both a $p$-Hardy inequality and quasiadditivity of the relative
variational $p$-capacity, provided that $\Omega$ satisfies an additional
discrete John condition for its Whitney cover.

Immediately after Proposition~4.1, on PDF page~13, they ask:

\begin{question}
Is the discrete John condition really necessary in Proposition~4.1?
\end{question}

They knew no counterexample and observed that the condition was already
unnecessary in Euclidean space.

\section{The later resolution}

J. Lehrb\"ack, \emph{Hardy inequalities and Assouad dimensions},
arXiv:1402.6134, uses the notation
$\underline{\operatorname{codim}}_{A}$.  Remark~2.1 identifies this lower
Assouad codimension with the integral notion called the \emph{Aikawa
codimension} in the source paper.

Specializing Theorem~1.1 of the later paper to $\beta=0$ gives:

\begin{laterthm}
Let $1<p<\infty$.  Assume that $X$ is an unbounded complete doubling
metric measure space supporting a $p$-Poincar\'e inequality.  If
$\Omega\subset X$ is open and
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{codim}}_{A}(\Omega^c)>p,
\]
then $\Omega$ admits a $p$-Hardy inequality.  Consequently, the
variational $p$-capacity $\operatorname{cap}_p(\cdot,\Omega)$ is
quasiadditive with respect to Whitney covers $\mathcal W_c(\Omega)$ for
sufficiently small $c>0$.
\end{laterthm}

The consequence is stated verbatim as Corollary~1.4 of the later paper.
The paragraph immediately preceding that corollary explicitly explains
that Theorem~1.1 makes the earlier ``additional accessibility condition''
unnecessary.

\section{Why this answers the exact question}

There is no gap in terminology or hypotheses:

\begin{itemize}
  \item the source's $\operatorname{codim}_{A}$ is precisely the later
  paper's lower Assouad codimension;
  \item setting $\beta=0$ in the later theorem gives the same unweighted
  $p$-Hardy inequality;
  \item the ambient assumptions are the same complete, unbounded,
  doubling, $p$-Poincar\'e setting; and
  \item Corollary~1.4 states the desired Whitney-cover quasiadditivity
  directly, with no discrete John hypothesis.
\end{itemize}

Thus the answer is: \emph{no, the discrete John condition is not
necessary}.  The codimension assumption alone suffices under the standing
metric-space hypotheses.  No new mathematical result is claimed here.
