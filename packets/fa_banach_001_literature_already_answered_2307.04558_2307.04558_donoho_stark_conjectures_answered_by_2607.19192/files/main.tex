\section{Original questions}

O. Baeza Guasch, \emph{On an uncertainty result by Donoho and Stark},
arXiv:2307.04558~\cite{source}, restates the continuous conjecture as
Conjecture~1 on arXiv PDF page~1.  With $P_{\mathcal T}$ denoting
multiplication by the indicator of a measurable time set and
$P_{\mathcal W}$ the projection onto frequencies in an interval
$\mathcal W$, it asks:

\begin{sourceconjecture}[continuous version]
Among measurable $\mathcal T\subset\mathbb R$ of fixed finite measure, the
supremum of $\lVert P_{\mathcal W}P_{\mathcal T}\rVert$ is attained when
$\mathcal T$ is an interval.
\end{sourceconjecture}

The same paper formulates Conjecture~2 on arXiv PDF page~10.  For analytic
polynomials $p(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_kz^k$ on the unit circle and measurable
$E\subset\mathbb T$ of prescribed angular measure, it asks:

\begin{sourceconjecture}[circle-polynomial version]
The maximum of
\[
  \sup_{0\ne p\in\mathcal A_n}
  \frac{\int_E |p(e^{it})|^2\,dt}
       {\int_{\mathbb T}|p(e^{it})|^2\,dt}
\]
over all such $E$ is attained when $E$ is a circle interval.
\end{sourceconjecture}

Baeza Guasch proves the continuous statement only when
$|\mathcal W||\mathcal T|\leq1$ and the circle statement only when
$n\delta\leq\pi$ for $|E|=2\delta$.

\section{Separate later answer}

The separate later paper of L. D. Abreu and M. Speckbacher,
arXiv:2607.19192~\cite{answer}, proves both unrestricted statements.

Their concentration constant for a bounded interval $\Omega$ and a measurable
finite-measure set $E$ is
\[
 \mathfrak C_\Omega(E)
 =\sup_{0\ne f\in PW(\Omega)}
   \frac{\int_E|f|^2}{\int_{\mathbb R}|f|^2}
 =\lVert P_\Omega M_E\rVert^2.
\]
Theorem~1.1 on arXiv PDF page~1 states:

\begin{laterresult}[Theorem 1.1 of arXiv:2607.19192]
If $\Omega\subset\mathbb R$ is a bounded interval of positive length,
$0<|E|<\infty$, and $I$ is any interval with $|I|=|E|$, then
\[
  \mathfrak C_\Omega(E)\leq\mathfrak C_\Omega(I),
  \qquad
  \lVert P_\Omega M_E\rVert\leq\lVert P_\Omega M_I\rVert.
\]
\end{laterresult}

This is exactly Conjecture~1 after identifying
$M_E=P_{\mathcal T}$ and $P_\Omega=P_{\mathcal W}$.  On PDF page~2 the
supporting authors explicitly state that this confirms the Donoho--Stark
conjecture, cite Baeza--Guasch's product-$1$ result, and say that their theorem
removes all restrictions on $|\Omega||E|$.

For the circle let
$\mathcal A_N=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{1,e^{it},\ldots,e^{iNt}\}$
and $T_E^{(N)}=P_NM_EP_N|_{\mathcal A_N}$.  Equation~(5) of the later paper
identifies
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(T_E^{(N)})
 =\sup_{0\ne p\in\mathcal A_N}
   \frac{\int_E|p(t)|^2\,dt}{\int_{\mathbb T}|p(t)|^2\,dt}.
\]
Theorem~2.1 on arXiv PDF page~3 states:

\begin{laterresult}[Theorem 2.1 of arXiv:2607.19192]
For every $N\geq0$, every measurable $E\subset\mathbb T$, and every circle
interval $I$ with $|I|=|E|$,
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(T_E^{(N)})
 \leq \lambda_{\max}(T_I^{(N)}).
\]
\end{laterresult}

This Rayleigh quotient and polynomial space are precisely those of source
Conjecture~2.  The later theorem has no $N\delta\leq\pi$ hypothesis, so it is
the full circle-polynomial answer.

\section{Scope and provenance}

Both source conjectures are completely answered.  The later results prove
that intervals are maximizers; neither theorem asserts that all maximizers are
intervals, but the source conjectures did not request uniqueness.

The continuous identification is explicit in the supporting paper.  The
circle identification is an exact equation-by-equation match; the supporting
paper cites Baeza--Guasch, although it does not separately label Theorem~2.1
as the answer to Baeza--Guasch's Conjecture~2.  This packet records a later
literature answer and claims no new mathematics.

and conjecture phrases, official arXiv metadata, and the full source and PDF of
