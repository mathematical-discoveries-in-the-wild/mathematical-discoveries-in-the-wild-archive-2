\fbox{\parbox{0.9\textwidth}{
\textbf{Status: literature already answered, negatively.}
Problem~II.2 of Neeb asks whether every Lie group modelled on a
Mackey-complete locally convex space is regular.  Han and Liu construct a
complex analytic BCH--Lie group modelled on a complete Silva space that is
not even $C^0$-semiregular.  Completeness implies Mackey completeness, so
their example is a counterexample to the stated problem.
}}
\end{center}

\section{Original question}

Karl-Hermann Neeb, \emph{Towards a Lie theory of locally convex groups},
arXiv:1501.06269~\cite{source}, asks on arXiv PDF page~40:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Problem II.2.} Show that every Lie group $G$ modeled on a Mackey
complete locally convex space is regular, or find a counterexample.
\end{quote}
Regularity requires, in particular, existence of the group-valued evolution
for every smooth Lie-algebra curve.

\section{Separate later counterexample}

Zongjian Han and Fungo Liu, \emph{Completeness of the Model Space Does Not
Force Regularity for Infinite-Dimensional Lie Groups},
arXiv:2607.28557~\cite{answerpaper}, prove in Theorem~1.1 (arXiv PDF page~2)
that there is a contractible complex analytic BCH--Lie group $H$, modelled on
the complete Silva space $\varphi_{\mathbb C}$, whose exponential map is a
homeomorphism but which is not $C^0$-semiregular.  More concretely, a fixed
finite-dimensional subspace $W\subseteq L(H)$ contains smooth controls
\[
  \gamma_\lambda\in C^\infty([0,1],W)
\]
for which the evolution equation has no $H$-valued $C^1$ solution.  Their
Theorem~1.2 gives the construction as the principal unit group of the
unitization of a positively graded infinite-dimensional nil algebra; the
failure is caused by products escaping to arbitrarily high homogeneous
degrees.

This matches the source hypotheses exactly.  A complete locally convex space
is Mackey complete, and a failure of evolution existence for even one smooth
control rules out Milnor regularity.  Hence $H$ is the counterexample demanded
by Problem~II.2.  In fact, the example is stronger than requested: the model
space is complete (not merely Mackey complete), and $H$ is BCH with a globally
well-behaved exponential map.

\section{Scope and verification}

This is a direct literature identification, not a new theorem of this run.
The source question was checked in the original PDF, and the later theorem
and its construction were checked in the separate supporting manuscript.
The result answers Problem~II.2 only.  As the answering paper itself notes,
the weaker Problem~II.1---whether Mackey completeness forces the existence of
a smooth exponential map---remains open, because its counterexample does have
an exponential map.
