\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{Affirmative in two large later-literature subcases.}
Every finite-index regular inclusion of $II_1$ factors has a two-sided
Pimsner--Popa basis [2].  Every finite-index irreducible depth-two inclusion
has, more strongly, a two-sided orthonormal basis [3].  These results answer
the source question for regular subfactors and for all irreducible depth-two
subfactors, including nonregular Kac-algebra examples.  They do not answer the
question for an arbitrary finite-index irreducible (equivalently here,
extremal) subfactor.

\section*{Source question}
For $N\subset M$, the source calls a finite set
\(\{\lambda_i\}\subset M\) a right Pimsner--Popa basis when
\[
 x=\sum_i\lambda_iE_N(\lambda_i^*x)
   =\sum_iE_N(x\lambda_i)\lambda_i^*\qquad(x\in M).
\]
It is a left basis when \(\{\lambda_i^*\}\) is a right basis, and it is
two-sided when both conditions hold.  Remark~3.14 asks whether every
finite-index (irreducible) subfactor admits such a two-sided basis [1, p.~8].

\section*{Later affirmative subcases}

\begin{theorem}[Bakshi--Gupta, regular case \cite{regular}]
If $N\subset M$ is a regular subfactor of type $II_1$ with finite Jones
index, then $M/N$ admits a two-sided Pimsner--Popa basis.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Bakshi--Gupta, depth-two case \cite{depthtwo}]
Let $N\subset M$ be a finite-index depth-two inclusion of $II_1$ factors
with simple relative commutant $N'\cap M$.  Then $M/N$ admits a two-sided
orthonormal basis.  In particular, this holds for every finite-index
irreducible depth-two inclusion.
\end{theorem}

The second theorem is not subsumed by regularity.  The answering paper points
out that an outer action of a finite-dimensional Kac algebra that is not a
group algebra gives a nonregular irreducible depth-two example covered by the
theorem.

\section*{Proof intuition}
The regular proof factors the inclusion through
\[
 R=N\vee(N'\cap M).
\]
The finite-dimensional algebra $N'\cap M$ supplies a two-sided basis for
\(R/N\).  The generalized Weyl-group normalizers supply a two-sided basis for
\(M/R\).  Because those normalizers preserve both $N$ and $R$, the two
bases can be patched: if \(\{\lambda_i\}\) is the first and
\(\{\mu_j\}\) the second, then \(\{\mu_j\lambda_i\}_{i,j}\) is
two-sided for $M/N$.

For depth two, the Jones tower provides a nondegenerate commuting square
\[
\begin{matrix}
 M_1&\subset&M_2\\[-2pt]
 \cup&&\cup\\[-2pt]
 M'\cap M_1&\subset&M'\cap M_2.
\end{matrix}
\]
Consequently, a left or right basis for the bottom finite-dimensional
inclusion lifts to the top inclusion.  Simplicity of $N'\cap M$ and the
depth-two basic-construction identities identify the bottom inclusion with
\[
 M_n(\C)\subset M_n(\C)\otimes Q
\]
for a finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebra $Q$.  A two-sided orthonormal basis
of $Q/\C$ then gives one for the bottom row and hence for $M_2/M_1$.
A two-step downward basic construction transfers the conclusion back to
$M/N$.

\section*{Exact scope and obstruction to the obvious upgrade}
The depth-two proof uses two facts that are absent at higher depth: equality of
the inclusion-matrix norm with the Jones index makes the commuting square
nondegenerate, and the relevant relative-commutant inclusion splits as a
tensor factor $M_n(\C)\subset M_n(\C)\otimes Q$.  At higher depth neither
statement follows from irreducibility or extremality alone.  Thus iterating the
Jones tower does not upgrade [3] to arbitrary finite depth.

The source's separate conjecture that the number of minimal intermediate
subfactors grows polynomially in $[M:N]$ is not addressed by either basis
paper.  This packet makes no claim about that conjecture.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
This is a literature-implied answer to explicit proper subcases, not a new
proof from the run.  The 2019 regular theorem includes one source author; the
2021 paper explicitly presents its depth-two theorem as partial progress on
Jones' general two-sided-basis question.  Exact arXiv-id, title, author, and
core-keyword searches through 13 August 2026 found no later arXiv theorem
settling the unrestricted question.
