Davidson and Passer give a positive answer to uniqueness of fully compressed

\section{Source questions}

For a tuple $T\in\B(H)^d$, its matrix range is
\[
  \cW_n(T)=\{\phi(T):\phi:\B(H)\to M_n\text{ is unital completely positive}\},
  \qquad \cW(T)=\bigcup_{n\geq1}\cW_n(T).
\]
The tuple is \emph{fully compressed} when compression to every proper closed
subspace strictly decreases its matrix range.

On page 15 of the official arXiv PDF of arXiv:1809.05154, Questions 4.8 and
4.9 ask:

\begin{quote}
For tuples $A,B\in\B(H)^d$ which are not necessarily compact, if $A$ and $B$
are fully compressed and $\cW(A)=\cW(B)$, does it follow that $A$ is unitarily
equivalent to $B$?
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
Let $\cC$ be a closed and bounded matrix convex set. When can $\cC$ be written
as the matrix range of a fully compressed tuple $T\in\B(H)^d$?
\end{quote}

\section{Exact later answers}

Davidson--Passer, arXiv:2005.11582, proves both results and cites the source
paper directly.

\subsection*{Uniqueness}

Corollary 2.13 on page 11 states that if $S$ and $T$ are fully compressed
$d$-tuples and $\cW(S)=\cW(T)$, then $S$ and $T$ are unitarily equivalent.
Immediately before it, the authors write that this gives a positive answer to
Passer--Shalit Question 4.8.  The proof uses equality of matrix ranges to
obtain a complete order isomorphism of the associated separable operator
systems, then Corollary 2.12 upgrades that isomorphism to a unitary
equivalence.

Thus the answer to the first source question is \textbf{yes}, with no
compactness hypothesis.

\subsection*{Existence}

Associate to a closed bounded matrix convex set $\cC$ its separable abstract
operator system $\cS_{\cC}$ (equivalently, use any tuple $T$ with
$\cW(T)=\cC$; every such $\cC$ has a tuple realization).  Corollary 3.6 on
page 15 proves the equivalence
\[
\begin{split}
&\cS_{\cC}\text{ has a fully compressed concrete presentation}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\\
&\text{the isolated points of }\widehat{C_e^*(\cS_{\cC})}\text{ are dense}.
\end{split}
\]
Equivalently, $\cS_{\cC}$ must admit a concrete presentation completely normed
by irreducible representations that are strongly peaking.  This is the
necessary-and-sufficient answer to the second source question.  The later
paper explicitly says it is answering the Passer--Shalit question, though it
cites it as Question 4.19 rather than Question 4.9 in the arXiv PDF.

The same paper also supplies intrinsic specializations.  For example, if
$\cC$ is the closed matrix convex hull of its absolute extreme points, then
Corollary 4.7 says that such a fully compressed tuple exists exactly when
$\cC$ is the closed matrix convex hull of its crucial matrix extreme points.

\section{Status, scope, and audit}

The match is explicit and complete: uniqueness is Corollary 2.13, and the
general existence criterion is Corollary 3.6.  These are later-literature
answers, not new results of this run.  No part of either source question is
left open within the separable setting relevant here; Davidson--Passer also
show that every fully compressed tuple acts on a finite-dimensional or
separable Hilbert space.

\paragraph{Search audit.}
The run's cheap indexes had no prior record for arXiv:1809.05154.  Exact-phrase
and title searches of arXiv found arXiv:2005.11582.  The official source and
PDF of both papers were inspected; the decisive passages are source page 15
and supporting pages 11 and 15.

\paragraph{Human review.}
Recommended as a high-confidence literature match.  Verify only the harmless
4.9/4.19 numbering discrepancy and the standard passage from a closed bounded
matrix convex set to its abstract operator system.
