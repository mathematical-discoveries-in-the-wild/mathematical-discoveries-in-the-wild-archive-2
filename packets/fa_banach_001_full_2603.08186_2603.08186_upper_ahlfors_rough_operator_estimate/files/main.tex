\section*{Source problem and answer}

Chamorro, Marcoci, and Marcoci ask whether their estimate
\[
  T_K^*f(x)\le C\mathcal R_{1,\mu}g(x)
\]
for rough kernels can be proved assuming only the upper Ahlfors bound
\(\mu(B(x,r))\le c_2r^\nu\).  Their proof assumes two-sided Ahlfors
regularity and the extra restriction
\(2^{1-\nu}c_2/c_1<1\).  The answer is \emph{yes}: neither the lower
Ahlfors bound nor the ratio restriction is needed.  The proof below retains
the ball-measure factor through the Poincare--Sobolev step, after which the
scale sum is geometric because \(\nu>1\).

\section*{The theorem}

Let \((X,d,\mu)\) have \(0<\mu(B)<\infty\) for every ball and null metric
spheres, as in the source.  Write
\[
 \mathcal R_{1,\mu}g(x)
 =\int_X\frac{d(x,y)}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}g(y)\,d\mu(y).
\]
Assume that for some \(q\ge1\), \(C_P>0\), and \(\sigma\ge1\), every
admissible \(f\) and upper gradient \(g\) satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \left(\avgint_{B(x,r)}|f-f_{B(x,r)}|^q\,d\mu\right)^{1/q}
 \le C_Pr\,\avgint_{B(x,\sigma r)}g\,d\mu .
 \tag{PS}
\end{equation}
For \(q=1\), this has its literal usual meaning.

\begin{theorem}[Upper-Ahlfors resolution]
Suppose \(\nu>1\) and
\begin{equation}
  \mu(B(x,r))\le c_2r^\nu \qquad(x\in X,\ r>0).
  \tag{UA}
\end{equation}
Let \(K:X\times X\setminus\{x=y\}\to\mathbb R\) satisfy
\begin{align}
 |K(x,y)|&\le C_Kd(x,y)^{-\nu},                                      \tag{K1}\\
 \int_{\{a<d(x,y)<b\}}K(x,y)\,d\mu(y)&=0
       \qquad(0<a<b<\infty).                                        \tag{K2}
\end{align}
Then, wherever the source truncations are defined,
\[
  T_K^*f(x)
  :=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left|
       \int_{\{\varepsilon<d(x,y)\}}K(x,y)f(y)\,d\mu(y)
     \right|
  \le C\mathcal R_{1,\mu}g(x),
\]
where \(C\) depends only on \(C_K,C_P,c_2,\nu,\sigma\).  In particular,
the source estimate (1.12) holds under the upper Ahlfors condition alone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(x\), an inner truncation \(\varepsilon>0\), and first impose a finite
outer truncation.  Choose \(k_0\) with
\(2^{k_0-1}<\varepsilon\le2^{k_0}\), and decompose into the first partial
annulus \(\{\varepsilon<d(x,y)\le2^{k_0}\}\) and the dyadic annuli
\[
 A_k=\{2^{k-1}<d(x,y)\le2^k\},\qquad k>k_0.
\]
Put \(B_k=B(x,2^k)\) and \(B_k^\sigma=B(x,\sigma2^k)\).  Condition (K2)
allows the subtraction of the separate constant \(f_{B_k}\) on every
annulus, including the first partial annulus.  Therefore (with harmless
changes in constants)
\begin{equation}
 |T_{K}^{\varepsilon}f(x)|
 \le C\sum_{k\ge k_0}2^{-k\nu}
       \int_{A_k}|f(y)-f_{B_k}|\,d\mu(y).                              \tag{1}
\end{equation}
The displayed estimate can be proved first for finitely many annuli; all
subsequent bounds are independent of the outer cutoff.

Set \(\alpha=1-1/q\) when \(q>1\).  Holder's inequality, the inclusions
\(A_k\subset B_k\subset B_k^\sigma\), and (PS) give
\begin{align*}
 \int_{A_k}|f-f_{B_k}|\,d\mu
 &\le \mu(B_k)^\alpha
       \left(\int_{B_k}|f-f_{B_k}|^q\,d\mu\right)^{1/q}\\
 &\le C_P2^k\,
       \frac{\mu(B_k)}{\mu(B_k^\sigma)}
       \int_{B_k^\sigma}g\,d\mu\\
 &\le C_P2^k\int_{B_k^\sigma}g\,d\mu .                              \tag{2}
\end{align*}
For \(q=1\), (2) follows directly from (PS).  This is the decisive point:
the two powers of \(\mu(B_k)\) add to one, and the resulting volume ratio is
at most one.  Substitution into (1) yields
\begin{equation}
 |T_{K}^{\varepsilon}f(x)|
 \le C\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}2^{k(1-\nu)}
                 \int_{B(x,\sigma2^k)}g(y)\,d\mu(y).                  \tag{3}
\end{equation}
Extending the nonnegative sum to all integers only enlarges it.

Tonelli's theorem and \(t=d(x,y)\) transform the right side of (3) into
\[
 C\int_X g(y)
   \sum_{\{k:\,\sigma2^k\ge t\}}2^{k(1-\nu)}\,d\mu(y).
\]
Since \(\nu>1\), the inner series is geometric and
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{\{k:\,\sigma2^k\ge t\}}2^{k(1-\nu)}
  \le C_{\nu,\sigma}t^{1-\nu}.                                      \tag{4}
\end{equation}
Finally, (UA) gives
\[
 t^{1-\nu}\le c_2\frac{t}{\mu(B(x,t))}.
\]
Combining this with (3)--(4) proves the desired bound, uniformly in both
truncations.  Passing to the source truncation and taking the supremum over
\(\varepsilon\) completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\section*{Why the source needed a lower bound, and why this proof does not}

The source first replaces each annular integral by a normalized whole-ball
term.  It then has to control
\(\sum_k2^k\mu(B(x,\sigma2^k))^{-1}\int_{B(x,\sigma2^k)}g\), whose inner
ball contribution is absorbed using lower Ahlfors growth and condition
(1.11); this is the step on pp.~9--10 of the source reproduced in the packet's
audit files.

Estimate (2) avoids that larger series.  Its coefficient is instead
\(2^{k(1-\nu)}\), already summable at large scales.  Thus the proof also
shows that the source's numerical restriction is not merely improvable by
choosing a larger geometric base: it is completely unnecessary.

The hypothesis \(\nu>1\) is exactly what makes (4) converge and is already
implicit in the source's use of \(\mathcal R_{1,\mu}\), defined for
\(0<1<\nu\).  No relation such as \(q<\nu\) is used here.  This packet does
not alter the source's separate Morrey estimate for \(\mathcal R_{s,\mu}\),
whose proof may still require lower volume information.

\section*{Verification and literature boundary}

The proof was checked annulus by annulus, with a finite outer cutoff before
Tonelli.  Its only volume comparison before the final line is
\(\mu(B_k)/\mu(B_k^\sigma)\le1\); the final conversion uses (UA) in the
correct direction.  Exact-id and exact-phrase searches through August 11,
2026 found the source and no later paper resolving its stated open problem.
