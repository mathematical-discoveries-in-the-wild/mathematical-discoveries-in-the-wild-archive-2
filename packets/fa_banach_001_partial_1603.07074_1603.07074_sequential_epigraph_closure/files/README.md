## Source question

T. Guo, E. Zhang, M. Wu, B. Yang, G. Yuan, and X. Zeng,
*On random convex analysis*, arXiv:1603.07074, Remark 2.14 (PDF page 10).
For a proper local map from a random locally convex module to extended
random variables, the authors ask whether closedness of every random sublevel
set in the epsilon-lambda topology implies closedness of the epigraph in the
product epsilon-lambda topology.

## Result

The implication is proved in two substantial regimes:

1. On every random locally convex module, closed random sublevels force the
   epigraph to be **sequentially closed**.
2. If the module `E` has the countable concatenation property, the source
   implication `(1)' => (2)'` holds with **no countable-concatenation
   assumption on the seminorm family `P`**. This strictly improves the
   hypothesis of source Theorem 2.13 for this implication.
3. Independently, `(1)' => (2)'` holds whenever the
   epsilon-lambda topology of the module is first countable. This includes
   every random normed module and every random locally convex module whose
   epsilon-lambda topology is generated by a countable family of random
   seminorms.

The universal sequential theorem needs neither properness nor locality. The
countable-concatenation theorem uses properness only to localize outside a
positive-measure gap set, and locality to evaluate a countable paste.

## Proof mechanism

Suppose `(x_n,r_n)` lies in the epigraph and converges to `(x,r)`. From
convergence in probability, pass to a subsequence on which `r_n -> r` almost
everywhere. If `f(x)>r` on a positive-measure set, retain a positive subset on
which the gap is uniform. After discarding finitely many terms, the random
variable

`q = sup_j r_{n_j}`

is finite, dominates every retained threshold globally, and is still strictly
below `f(x)` on a positive-measure subset. Thus every retained `x_{n_j}` lies
in the single closed sublevel `{f<=q}`. Its limit `x` must lie there too, a
contradiction.

For the module-CCP theorem, assume a putative epigraph closure point violates
the inequality on a positive-measure set and choose a fixed level `q` halfway
inside the gap. Closedness of `{f<=q}` gives one separating neighborhood,
which involves only finitely many seminorms. Select a sequence of epigraph
points increasingly accurate for exactly those seminorms and for the random
threshold. Their good sets cover the base modulo null sets. The CCP of `E`
allows the corresponding countable paste. Locality puts that paste in
`{f<=q}`, while summable probability errors put it inside the separating
neighborhood, a contradiction. The seminorm family itself is never pasted.

## Scope and obstruction

The unrestricted problem remains open only when `E` lacks the countable
concatenation property and its topology is genuinely nonsequential. A closure
witness may then be a net from which no convergent sequence in the module can
be extracted, while the countable paste used above may not exist in `E`.
Convergence in probability of a net of thresholds also need not provide a
single finite random upper envelope for a whole tail: arbitrarily high spikes
can move through sets of shrinking probability.
