\noindent\textbf{Status.} \texttt{candidate\_partial\_likely\_valid}.

\begin{abstract}
Amrutam and Jiang ask whether a finite Pimsner--Popa-index subalgebra of a
confined von Neumann subalgebra of a group factor must remain confined. We
prove this when the conjugacy orbit closure of the larger algebra is compact.
More sharply, any sequence which sends the smaller algebra to the scalars and
for which the larger algebras have an Effros--Mar\'echal cluster point forces
that cluster point to be finite-dimensional. In an i.c.c. group factor such a
cluster point can itself be conjugated to the scalars. Thus every hypothetical
counterexample to the unrestricted question must exhibit noncompact escape.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source question}

Let \(\Gamma\) be a countable i.c.c. group and put \(A=L(\Gamma)\), with its
canonical trace \(\tau\). For \(\mathcal B\subseteq A\), write
\(E_{\mathcal B}:A\to\mathcal B\) for the trace-preserving conditional
expectation and \(\mathcal B^g=\lambda(g)\mathcal B\lambda(g)^*\).

Question 3.9 of Amrutam--Jiang \cite{source} asks whether, for
\[
 \mathcal N\subseteq\mathcal M\subseteq A,
 \qquad [\mathcal M:\mathcal N]_{\rm PP}<\infty,
\]
confinement of \(\mathcal M\) implies confinement of \(\mathcal N\). Here
\(\mathcal B\) is confined when \(\C1\) is not in the Effros--Mar\'echal (EM)
closure of \(\{\mathcal B^g:g\in\Gamma\}\). The source page is reproduced in
Figure~\ref{fig:source}.

\section{The cluster-point theorem}

Set
\[
 \lambda=[\mathcal M:\mathcal N]_{\rm PP}^{-1}>0.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite-index cluster obstruction]\label{thm:cluster}
Suppose \(g_i\in\Gamma\) satisfies
\(\mathcal N^{g_i}\to\C1\) in the EM topology. If, along a subnet,
\(\mathcal M^{g_i}\to\mathcal P\), then \(\mathcal P\) is finite-dimensional
and every nonzero projection \(e\in\mathcal P\) satisfies
\(\tau(e)\geq\lambda\). Moreover, \(\mathcal M\) is not confined.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For nested tracial subalgebras,
\(E_{\mathcal N}E_{\mathcal M}=E_{\mathcal N}\). The Pimsner--Popa
inequality therefore gives, for every \(x\in A_+\),
\[
 E_{\mathcal N}(x)
 =E_{\mathcal N}(E_{\mathcal M}(x))
 \geq\lambda E_{\mathcal M}(x).
\]
Conjugating yields
\[
 E_{\mathcal N^{g_i}}(x)\geq
 \lambda E_{\mathcal M^{g_i}}(x),\qquad x\in A_+.
\tag{1}\label{eq:domination}
\]
EM convergence is pointwise \(L^2\)-convergence of conditional expectations.
Since the positive cone is \(L^2\)-closed, the limit of
\eqref{eq:domination} is
\[
 \tau(x)1\geq\lambda E_{\mathcal P}(x),\qquad x\in A_+.
\tag{2}\label{eq:limit}
\]
For \(y\in\mathcal P_+\), take \(x=y\) in \eqref{eq:limit}; then
\(\tau(y)1\geq\lambda y\). In particular, a nonzero projection
\(e\in\mathcal P\) satisfies \(\tau(e)\geq\lambda\). A diffuse von Neumann
algebra, or an infinite atomic one, has arbitrarily small nonzero projections.
Thus \(\mathcal P\) is finite-dimensional.

Lemma~\ref{lem:finite} below gives
\(\C1\in\overline{\{\mathcal P^g:g\in\Gamma\}}^{\rm EM}\). On the other hand,
\(\mathcal P\) belongs to the closed invariant orbit closure of \(\mathcal M\),
so the same is true of the entire orbit closure of \(\mathcal P\). Hence
\(\C1\) belongs to the orbit closure of \(\mathcal M\), and \(\mathcal M\)
is not confined.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite-dimensional algebras escape]\label{lem:finite}
If \(\Gamma\) is i.c.c. and \(\mathcal P\subseteq L(\Gamma)\) is
finite-dimensional, then \(\mathcal P\) is not confined.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose an \(L^2\)-orthonormal basis
\(1,a_1,\ldots,a_d\) of \(\mathcal P\), with \(\tau(a_j)=0\). Enumerate
\(\Gamma\setminus\{e\}=\{h_1,h_2,\ldots\}\), and approximate every \(a_j\)
in \(L^2\) by finite Fourier sums.

For finite \(F\subseteq\Gamma\setminus\{e\}\) and finite
\(S\subseteq\Gamma\), there is \(g\in\Gamma\) such that
\(g^{-1}Fg\cap S=\varnothing\). Indeed, each forbidden equality
\(g^{-1}hg=s\) places \(g\) in a coset of \(C_\Gamma(h)\); these centralizers
have infinite index because \(\Gamma\) is i.c.c., and finitely many cosets of
infinite-index subgroups cannot cover a group.

Applying this avoidance successively to the first \(n\) group elements and
Fourier approximants of error \(1/n\), choose \(g_n\) so that
\[
 \tau(a_j^*\lambda(g_n^{-1}h_kg_n))\longrightarrow0
 \quad(j=1,\ldots,d;\ k\geq1).
\]
The orthonormal-basis formula for conditional expectation then gives
\[
 \|E_{\mathcal P}(\lambda(g_n^{-1}h_kg_n))\|_2^2
 =\sum_{j=1}^d
   |\tau(a_j^*\lambda(g_n^{-1}h_kg_n))|^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Equivalently,
\(E_{\mathcal P^{g_n}}(\lambda(h_k))\to0\) for every \(h_k\ne e\), while
the expectations fix \(1\). Density of the group algebra and contractivity of
conditional expectations imply \(\mathcal P^{g_n}\to\C1\) in EM.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Compact orbit closure]\label{cor:compact}
If the EM orbit closure of \(\mathcal M\) is compact and \(\mathcal M\) is
confined, then \(\mathcal N\) is confined.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If \(\mathcal N\) were not confined, choose
\(\mathcal N^{g_i}\to\C1\). Compactness supplies an EM-convergent subnet of
\((\mathcal M^{g_i})\), contradicting Theorem~\ref{thm:cluster}.
\end{proof}

In particular, confinement descends through finite index whenever
\(\mathcal M\) belongs to a compact uniformly recurrent subalgebra system.
The theorem also gives a sharp necessary feature of any counterexample to the
source question: every sequence sending \(\mathcal N\) to the scalars must send
\(\mathcal M\) out of every EM-compact set.

\section{Why the unrestricted upgrade stalls}

The tempting inference from \eqref{eq:domination} is false without a cluster
point. For the normalized trace on \(M_d(\C)\), scalar expectation satisfies
\[
 E_{\C1}(x)=\tau(x)1\geq d^{-1}x\qquad(x\geq0),
\]
but it annihilates every traceless matrix. Thus a finite-index quotient can
hide precisely the non-scalar directions witnessing confinement.

The June 2026 follow-up \cite{ura} proves that subalgebra spaces of diffuse
finite von Neumann algebras can contain closed copies of arbitrary Polish
spaces and constructs noncompact uniformly recurrent systems. Consequently,
there is no ambient compactness principle with which to manufacture the
cluster point in Theorem~\ref{thm:cluster}. Finite module bases lead to moving
coefficients after conjugation, and the positive-definite-function
compactification does not preserve representability by subalgebras. Six
focused upgrade attempts are documented in the accompanying attempt log.

\paragraph{Novelty and review.}
Searches using the exact question, ``compact orbit closure'', ``finite index'',
``confined subalgebra'', and the June follow-up found no statement of
Corollary~\ref{cor:compact} or Theorem~\ref{thm:cluster}. Novelty confidence is
moderate because the argument is elementary and may be unindexed folklore.
Expert review is recommended, especially for the EM limit passage and
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite}.

\paragraph{Limitation.}
This packet does not answer Question 3.9 in the genuinely noncompact case and
does not claim a counterexample.
