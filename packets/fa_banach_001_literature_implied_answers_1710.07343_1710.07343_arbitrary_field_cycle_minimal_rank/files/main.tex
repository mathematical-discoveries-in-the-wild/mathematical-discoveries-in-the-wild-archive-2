\begin{abstract}
Grossmann--Woerdeman ask whether the equality case in their block-cycle
Theorem 3.2 remains true over an arbitrary field when the block size is
$n-1$ and the cycle holonomy has no fixed vector.  A theorem of
Cohen--Pereira, as explicitly summarized in Grossmann's 2019 thesis, already
supplies the required arbitrary-field upper bound; combined with the lower
bound in the source, it answers the question.  We also give a short independent
construction.  It proves the stronger formula that the normalized cycle has
minimal rank $n-1$ when its holonomy is the identity and $n$ for every other
invertible holonomy, over every field.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Status.}
\texttt{literature\_implied\_answer}, with an independent constructive proof.
No novelty is claimed for the minimal-rank value itself.

\section{Source question}

Let $n\geq2$, let $k=n-1$, and consider the normalized partial block matrix
over a field $\F$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycle}
 \mathcal A_H=
 \begin{bmatrix}
 I&I&?&\
 ?&I&I&\
 &\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
 &&I&I\\
 H&?&\cdots&I
 \end{bmatrix},\qquad H\in GL_k(\F).
\end{equation}
Thus the diagonal blocks and the blocks immediately above the diagonal are
$I_k$, and the lower-left block is $H$.

Theorem 3.2 of Grossmann--Woerdeman \cite{GW} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 \mr(\mathcal A_H)\geq \frac{nk-d}{n-1},
 \qquad d=\dim\ker(H-I),
\end{equation}
and obtains equality $\mr(\mathcal A_H)=n$ when $k=n-1$, $d=0$, and all
eigenvalues of $H$ lie in $\F$.  Section 5.2, Problem 1, asks whether that
conclusion holds over every field, without the eigenvalue-splitting hypothesis.

\section{Why the question is already answered in the literature}

Cohen--Pereira \cite{CP} study precisely a bipartite cycle with invertible
specified blocks over an arbitrary base field and obtain a tight upper bound.
The direct bridge is recorded by Grossmann himself in his 2019 thesis
\cite[pp.~64--65]{Gthesis}: for the partial matrix in the source theorem,
Cohen--Pereira give a completion whose rank is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CPbound}
 \frac{nk-d}{n-1}.
\end{equation}
In the open-problem regime $k=n-1$ and $d=0$, this is the integer $n$.
Together with \eqref{eq:lower}, it gives
\[
 \mr(\mathcal A_H)=n
\]
over every field.  Cohen--Pereira do not appear to frame their theorem as an
answer to the later numbered question, so this packet records an implication
rather than an explicit answer claim.

\section{Idea of the independent proof}

A rank-$n$ completion follows from factoring every completed block as
$P_iQ_j$, where the $Q_j$ are $(k+1)\times k$ frames and each $P_i$ is a
common left inverse of two consecutive frames.  The cycle condition turns
this into a path from $[I;0]$ to $[H^{-1};0]$ made of $k+1$ transverse
rank-one steps.

The path is constructed using one extra coordinate.  First conjugate
$H^{-1}$ so that all its leading principal minors are nonzero.  Lift the
standard frame by a bottom row $r$.  Then replace its columns one at a time
by the target columns.  Choosing the entries of $r$ backwards makes each
replacement transverse: at stage $j$ the relevant determinant is affine in
$r_j$, with nonzero coefficient equal to the $j$th leading principal minor.

\section{The constructive frame lemmas}

\begin{lemma}[Strong leading-minor basis]\label{lem:strong}
For every $T\in GL_k(\F)$ there is an ordered basis in which every leading
principal minor of the matrix of $T$ is nonzero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We induct on $k$.  Choose $v\ne0$.  There is a functional $f$ with
$f(v)=1$ and $f(Tv)\ne0$: if $v,Tv$ are independent, prescribe the two
values to be $1$; if $Tv=\lambda v$, any $f(v)=1$ works because
$\lambda\ne0$.  Put $W=\ker f$.  In a basis beginning with $v$ and followed
by a basis of $W$, write
\[
 T=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&D\end{bmatrix},\qquad a=f(Tv)\ne0.
\]
The Schur complement $S=D-ca^{-1}b$ is invertible because
$\det T=a\det S$.  By induction, choose a basis of $W$ in which all leading
principal minors of $S$ are nonzero.  In the resulting basis of the whole
space, the leading minor of order $j\ge2$ of $T$ is $a$ times the leading
minor of order $j-1$ of $S$; the first one is $a$.  All are nonzero.
\end{proof}

Call full-column-rank $(k+1)\times k$ matrices $X,Y$ \emph{adjacent} when
$\rank(Y-X)=1$ and $\rank[X\ Y]=k+1$.

\begin{lemma}[Lift-and-replace path]\label{lem:path}
For every $M\in GL_k(\F)$ there exist full-column-rank frames
\[
 X_0=[I_k;0],X_1,\ldots,X_{k+1}=[M;0]
\]
such that every consecutive pair is adjacent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Adjacency is preserved by left and right multiplication by invertible
matrices.  Lemma \ref{lem:strong} therefore lets us assume that every leading
principal minor $\Delta_j$ of $M$ is nonzero.  Let $e_1,\ldots,e_k$ be the
standard basis.  We shall choose a row $r=(r_1,\ldots,r_k)$ and use
\[
 X_0=\begin{bmatrix}I\\0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 X_1=\begin{bmatrix}I\\r\end{bmatrix}.
\]
For $1\le j\le k$, obtain $X_{j+1}$ from $X_j$ by replacing column $j$
of $X_j$ by $[Me_j;0]$.  Before that replacement the columns are
\[
 [Me_1;0],\ldots,[Me_{j-1};0],
 [e_j;r_j],\ldots,[e_k;r_k].
\]
The replacement is transverse precisely when the determinant of these $k$
columns together with $[Me_j;0]$ is nonzero.  After reordering columns and
expanding in the bottom row, this determinant is a linear expression in
$r_j,\ldots,r_k$ whose coefficient of $r_j$, up to sign, is
\[
 \det[Me_1\ \cdots\ Me_j\ e_{j+1}\ \cdots\ e_k]=\Delta_j\ne0.
\]
Choose $r_k,r_{k-1},\ldots,r_1$ in that order.  At each stage there is exactly
one forbidden value for the new coordinate, so another value is available
over any field.  At $j=k$ this also forces $r_k\ne0$, hence $r\ne0$ and the
first lift is a rank-one transverse step.  Every later difference changes
one column and has rank one; the nonzero determinant proves transversality
and preserves full column rank.  The final frame is $[M;0]$.

If the preliminary similarity was $C=S^{-1}MS$, apply the construction to
$C$ and send every frame $X$ to $\operatorname{diag}(S,1)XS^{-1}$.  This fixes
$[I;0]$ and sends $[C;0]$ to $[M;0]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Common left inverse]\label{lem:left}
Adjacent frames $X,Y$ have a common left inverse: there is a
$k\times(k+1)$ matrix $P$ such that $PX=PY=I_k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $Y-X=w\varphi$ with $w\ne0$ and $\varphi\ne0$.  Adjacency says
$w\notin\operatorname{im}X$, so $[X\ w]$ is invertible.  Define
$P=[I_k\ 0][X\ w]^{-1}$.  Then $PX=I_k$, $Pw=0$, and hence $PY=I_k$.
\end{proof}

\section{Stronger arbitrary-field formula}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For the normalized block cycle \eqref{eq:cycle}, over an arbitrary field,
\[
 \mr(\mathcal A_H)=
 \begin{cases}
 n-1,&H=I_{n-1},\\
 n,&H\ne I_{n-1}.
 \end{cases}
\]
In particular, the source question has an affirmative answer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $k=n-1$ and apply Lemma \ref{lem:path} to $M=H^{-1}$.  Thus
\[
 X_0=[I;0],X_1,\ldots,X_n=[H^{-1};0].
\]
Set $Q_j=X_{j-1}$ for $1\le j\le n$.  For $i<n$, Lemma \ref{lem:left}
gives $P_i$ with $P_iQ_i=P_iQ_{i+1}=I$.  For the final edge, it gives $P_n$
with
\[
 P_nQ_n=I,\qquad P_n(Q_1H^{-1})=I,
\]
so $P_nQ_1=H$.  Therefore the block matrix
\[
 C=(P_iQ_j)_{i,j=1}^n
 =\begin{bmatrix}P_1\\ \vdots\\P_n\end{bmatrix}
   \begin{bmatrix}Q_1&\cdots&Q_n\end{bmatrix}
\]
is a completion of $\mathcal A_H$ and has rank at most $k+1=n$.

If $H\ne I$, then $d=\dim\ker(H-I)\le k-1=n-2$.  The source lower bound
\eqref{eq:lower} is strictly larger than $n-1$, so the integer rank of every
completion is at least $n$.  Hence it equals $n$.  If $H=I$, filling every
block by $I_k$ gives rank $k=n-1$, and the specified identity block gives the
matching lower bound.
\end{proof}

The source normalizes an arbitrary cycle with invertible specified blocks by
invertible block row and column operations.  Hence Theorem \ref{thm:main}
also applies to the original formulation, with $H$ equal to the source's
ordered product of specified blocks.

\section{Verification, scope, and review recommendation}

The accompanying script performs two checks.  First, a breadth-first search
over the entire frame graph finds the optimal $k+1$ distance for every
fixed-point-free endpoint for $(k,|\F|)=(1,3),(2,2),(2,3),(3,2)$.  Second,
it implements Lemmas \ref{lem:strong} and \ref{lem:path} and verifies the
constructed path for every invertible $2\times2$ matrix over $\F_2$ and
$\F_3$, and every invertible $3\times3$ matrix over $\F_2$ (168 endpoints).
These computations are checks, not dependencies of the proof.

The literature classification rests on the exact upper-bound attribution in
Grossmann's thesis and the matching scope of Cohen--Pereira.  The original
Cohen--Pereira article is closed access in the sources checked; a reviewer
should compare its theorem statement directly before final archival.  The
independent proof does not depend on that article, but it uses the lower bound
from Theorem 3.2 of the source.  Recommended status:
\texttt{literature\_implied\_answer; constructive proof likely valid}.
