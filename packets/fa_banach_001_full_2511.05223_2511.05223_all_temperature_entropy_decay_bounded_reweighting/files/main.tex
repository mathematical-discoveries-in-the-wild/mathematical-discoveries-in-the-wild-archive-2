\section{Source question}

The source asks whether exponential decay to equilibrium continues to hold
for \emph{every} symmetric interaction matrix $J$, allowing a rate that may
deteriorate exponentially in the dimension.  It proposes, as one route, a
uniform-in-tilt covariance estimate for the associated canonical particle
system.  The full source passage is reproduced below.

\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}

The proof below bypasses the proposed particle-system covariance conjecture.
It transports the already-proved $J=0$ nonlinear modified logarithmic Sobolev
inequality (NMLSI) through a bounded Gibbs reweighting.

\section{Definitions and statement}

Let $\Omega=\{-1,+1\}^n$, let $J$ be an arbitrary real symmetric
$n\times n$ matrix, and set
\[
 E_J(\sigma)=\frac12\sigma^{\mathsf T}J\sigma,
 \qquad w(\sigma)=e^{E_J(\sigma)},
 \qquad
 \rho_J=\frac{\min_{\sigma\in\Omega}w(\sigma)}
                  {\max_{\sigma\in\Omega}w(\sigma)}
       =e^{-\osc_{\sigma\in\Omega}E_J(\sigma)}.
\]
Fix a partition $\cA$ of $[n]$ and the multi-component mean-field transport
kernel $\cK_{\cA}$ of the source.  Write
\[
 S_A(\sigma)=\sum_{i\in A}\sigma_i,\qquad A\in\cA,
\]
and let $\Gamma_{\cA}$ be the space of fields constant on each $A\in\cA$.
The equilibrium family is
\[
 \mu^J_h(\sigma)=\frac{1}{Z_J(h)}
 e^{E_J(\sigma)+\langle h,\sigma\rangle},
 \qquad h\in\Gamma_{\cA}.
\]
For an $\cA$-regular law $p$, let $h_J(p)$ be the unique field for which
$p$ and $\mu^J_{h_J(p)}$ have the same $\cA$-magnetization profile, and put
\[
 I_J(p)=\KL\bigl(p\,\|\,\mu^J_{h_J(p)}\bigr).
\]
The source defines a nonlinear entropy production $\cD_J(p)$ by writing
$p=f\mu^J_{h_J(p)}$ in its H-theorem.  We use the standard boundary
convention for
\[
 \Phi(x,y)=(x-y)\log(x/y),
\]
so $\Phi(x,x)=0$ and $\Phi(x,0)=+\infty$ for $x>0$.

\begin{theorem}[All-temperature exponential ergodicity]\label{thm:main}
For every real symmetric interaction matrix $J$, every partition $\cA$ of
$[n]$, and the multi-component mean-field kernel $\cK_{\cA}$, the nonlinear
spin-exchange dynamics of \cite{source} satisfies
\[
 I_J(p_t)\le e^{-\alpha_Jt}I_J(p_0),\qquad t\ge0,
\]
for every $\cA$-regular initial law $p_0$, where the equilibrium entering
$I_J$ is fixed along the evolution by the conserved magnetization and
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \alpha_J=\frac{\rho_J^3}{4n}
 =\frac1{4n}\exp\!\left[-3\,
       \osc_{\sigma\in\Omega}\!\left(\frac12\sigma^{\mathsf T}J\sigma\right)
                 \right]>0.}
\]
Thus the answer to the exponential-ergodicity question in Section~4.5 is
affirmative.  No definiteness or spectral-radius assumption on $J$ is needed.
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof intuition}

Multiplication by the bounded weight $w=e^{E_J}$ maps the noninteracting
exponential family exactly onto the interacting Ising family.  This same
change of density also has a particularly favorable effect on the Barker
acceptance rule used by the collision kernel.  For a proposed exchange, the
two Gibbs pair weights appear through their harmonic mean.  After the change
of density, the nonlinear entropy-production integrand becomes the $J=0$
integrand times a coefficient bounded below by $\rho_J^2$.  Separately, a
bounded likelihood update can increase KL divergence by at most
$\rho_J^{-1}$; an accept/reject channel proves this in one line.  The source's
$J=0$ NMLSI then transfers with total loss $\rho_J^3$.

The information-projection step is important: the transformed law need not
have the same magnetization as the naively transformed target.  Instead, one
projects the transformed law onto the whole $J=0$ equilibrium family and maps
that competitor back.  This avoids any unjustified matching of fields.

\section{Proof}

For a probability $q$ on $\Omega$, define the bounded likelihood transform
\[
 T_wq(\sigma)=\frac{w(\sigma)q(\sigma)}{q[w]},
 \qquad q[w]=\sum_{\sigma}w(\sigma)q(\sigma).
\]
This is a bijection of the probability simplex, preserves supports, and
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family-map}
 T_w\mu^0_h=\mu^J_h,\qquad h\in\Gamma_{\cA}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{KL comparison}

\begin{lemma}[Bounded likelihood updates]\label{lem:kl}
If $a=\min w$ and $b=\max w$, then for all probabilities $q,r$ on
$\Omega$,
\[
 \KL(T_wq\,\|\,T_wr)\le\frac ba\KL(q\,\|\,r).
\]
Consequently, if $p=T_wq$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:information-comparison}
 I_0(q)\ge\rho_J I_J(p).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply to both $q$ and $r$ the same channel which, conditionally on
$X=\sigma$, sets an auxiliary bit $B=1$ with probability $w(\sigma)/b$.
The accepted conditional laws are $T_wq$ and $T_wr$.  The chain rule for KL
gives
\[
 \KL(q\|r)\ge \frac{q[w]}{b}\KL(T_wq\|T_wr)
 \ge\frac ab\KL(T_wq\|T_wr),
\]
because the KL terms from the bit marginal and rejected branch are
nonnegative.  This proves the first assertion.

For the second, recall that the matching-magnetization member of either
exponential family is its KL information projection.  This follows directly
by differentiating $\KL(p\|\mu^J_h)$ in $h$; its gradient is the difference
between the sufficient-statistic means.  Let $r=\mu^0_g$ minimize
$\KL(q\|\mu^0_g)$ over $g\in\Gamma_{\cA}$.  By
\eqref{eq:family-map}, $T_wr=\mu^J_g$ is an admissible competitor in the
interacting family.  Hence
\[
 I_J(p)\le\KL(T_wq\|T_wr)
 \le\rho_J^{-1}\KL(q\|r)=\rho_J^{-1}I_0(q),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:information-comparison}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact entropy-production comparison}

Fix one ordered proposed exchange
\[
 x=(\sigma,\sigma')\longmapsto y=(\tau,\tau').
\]
The proposal is an involution and conserves the vector
$S(\sigma)+S(\sigma')$.  Set
\[
 A=w(\sigma)w(\sigma'),\qquad B=w(\tau)w(\tau'),
 \qquad Z=q[w].
\]
All external-field factors therefore cancel between the two pair weights.
The Barker acceptance probability from $x$ to $y$ is exactly
$B/(A+B)$.

\begin{lemma}[Collision comparison]\label{lem:diss}
If $p=T_wq$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diss-comparison}
 \cD_J(p)\ge\rho_J^2\cD_0(q).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mu=\mu^J_h$ for any $h\in\Gamma_{\cA}$ and put $f=p/\mu$.
In the source's entropy-production formula, the contribution of the accepted
proposal $x\to y$, before the common proposal rate and common factor $1/4$
are applied, is
\[
 \mu(\sigma)\mu(\sigma')\frac{B}{A+B}
 \Phi\bigl(f(\sigma)f(\sigma'),f(\tau)f(\tau')\bigr).
\]
Using pair-statistic conservation, the ratio of the two equilibrium pair
weights is $A/B$.  Substituting
$p(\eta)=w(\eta)q(\eta)/Z$ and simplifying gives exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-summand}
 \frac{AB}{Z^2(A+B)}
 \Phi\bigl(q(\sigma)q(\sigma'),q(\tau)q(\tau')\bigr).
\end{equation}
The rejected proposal contributes zero.  For $J=0$, the acceptance
probability is $1/2$, so the corresponding coefficient of $\Phi$ is $1/2$.
The ratio of \eqref{eq:exact-summand}'s coefficient to $1/2$ is
\[
 \frac{2AB}{Z^2(A+B)}
 \ge \frac{\min(A,B)}{Z^2}
 \ge \frac{a^2}{b^2}=\rho_J^2.
\]
The first inequality is the elementary harmonic-mean bound.  Multiplying by
the identical proposal rates and the identical factor $1/4$, then summing
over all ordered input pairs and site proposals, proves
\eqref{eq:diss-comparison}.  The calculation remains valid on the boundary
of the simplex by the lower-semicontinuous convention for $\Phi$, or by
positive regularization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Completion}

The source proves Theorem~1.7 and its equivalent NMLSI for every partition
whenever the interaction is nonnegative definite with spectral radius below
$1/2$ \cite{source}.  At $J=0$ its stated constant is exactly $1/(4n)$.
Therefore, for every $\cA$-regular $q$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:base}
 \cD_0(q)\ge\frac1{4n}I_0(q).
\end{equation}
If $p$ is $\cA$-regular then $q=T_w^{-1}p$ is also $\cA$-regular, because
the positive transform preserves its support.  Combining
Lemmas~\ref{lem:kl}--\ref{lem:diss} with \eqref{eq:base} yields
\[
 \cD_J(p)ge\rho_J^2\cD_0(q)
 \ge\frac{\rho_J^2}{4n}I_0(q)
 \ge\frac{\rho_J^3}{4n}I_J(p).
\]
This is the all-temperature NMLSI.  The source H-theorem says that along the
nonlinear evolution,
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}I_J(p_t)=-\cD_J(p_t),
\]
while the matching field is fixed by conservation of the $\cA$-magnetization.
Gr\"onwall's inequality proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\qed

\section{Verification and limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item The reusable script \texttt{code/verify\_reweighting.py} exhaustively
  sums every ordered collision and site proposal on small Boolean cubes.  On
  400 randomized positive-law cases ($n=2,3$), it matched
  \eqref{eq:exact-summand} to relative error at most
  $1.30\times10^{-15}$; every dissipation, KL, information-projection, and
  final comparison had positive numerical slack.
\item The theorem answers the all-temperature exponential-ergodicity question
  in the exact quantitative setting of Theorem~1.7: arbitrary partitions and
  their multi-component mean-field kernels.  It does not claim the result for
  an arbitrary symmetric transport kernel.
\item The source's separate uniform covariance conjecture for the Kac particle
  system is not proved.  The result shows that this conjecture is unnecessary
  for the nonlinear exponential-decay conclusion.
\item The rate is deliberately crude.  In particular, it does not recover the
  expected order-$1/n$ rate throughout the subcritical Curie--Weiss regime.
  It does establish the source's requested positive $c(J)$, with exponential
  deterioration in $n$ allowed.
\item Diagonal modifications of $J$ add a constant to $E_J$ on the Boolean
  cube, so $\osc E_J$ and the stated rate are invariant under them.
\end{itemize}

Lemma~\ref{lem:kl}.  The included exact finite-state checker targets (i)--(ii).
