\section{Source problem}

The source is Q.~Xu, \emph{Operator-space Grothendieck inequalities for
noncommutative $L_p$-spaces}, arXiv:math/0505306, later published in
\emph{Duke Mathematical Journal} \textbf{131} (2006), 525--574
\cite{Xu}.  For a scalar matrix $\varphi=(\varphi_{ij})$, its Schur
multiplier is
\[
 M_\varphi(x)=(\varphi_{ij}x_{ij}).
\]
The Schatten classes carry their canonical operator-space structures.  Xu
characterizes bounded multipliers $S_p\to S_q$ throughout
$1\le q\le2\le p\le\infty$, and completely bounded multipliers on the dual
line $S_p\to S_{p'}$.  Problem 7.7 asks for the completely bounded
characterization away from that line.

\section{Statement and proof idea}

Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rdef}
 \frac1r=\frac1q-\frac1p,
 \qquad 1\le q\le2\le p\le\infty,
\end{equation}
with the usual convention $r=\infty$ if $p=q=2$.  For $1\le s\le\infty$,
define the positive domination norm
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor-norm}
 \F_s(\varphi)=\inf\left\{\|a\|_s\|b\|_s:
 |\varphi_{ij}|\le a_i b_j,\ a,b\ge0\right\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Mixed completely bounded Schur multipliers]
\label{thm:main}
Let $1\le q\le2\le p\le\infty$, and let $r$ be given by
\eqref{eq:rdef}.  A scalar matrix $\varphi$ defines a completely bounded
Schur multiplier
\[
 M_\varphi:S_p\longrightarrow S_q
\]
if and only if $\F_{2r}(\varphi)<\infty$.  Quantitatively, there is a
constant $C_{p,q}<\infty$, depending only on $p,q$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm-equivalence}
 C_{p,q}^{-1}\F_{2r}(\varphi)
 \le \|M_\varphi:S_p\to S_q\|_{\cb}
 \le \F_{2r}(\varphi).
\end{equation}
For $p=q=2$, this says simply that $\varphi$ is bounded.
\end{theorem}

The central space is $S_2$, the homogeneous operator Hilbert space.  The easy
direction splits an $\ell_{2r}$ weight into the portions needed for
$S_p\to S_2$ and $S_2\to S_q$.  For necessity, operator-space Maurey
factorization first extracts the $S_p\to S_2$ weights.  Schur symmetry lets us
choose them diagonal.  The residual is a cb Schur map $S_2\to S_q$; the
operator-space little Grothendieck theorem extracts its endpoint weights.
H\"older's identity combines the two pairs at exactly the exponent $2r$.

\section{Three ingredients}

Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deltas}
 \delta_1=\frac12-\frac1p,\qquad
 \delta_2=\frac1q-\frac12,\qquad
 \delta=\delta_1+\delta_2=\frac1r.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Diagonal multiplication and the Hilbert midpoint]
\label{lem:sufficiency}
Assume $\delta>0$.  If $a,b\in\ell_{2r}$ are nonnegative and
$|\varphi_{ij}|\le a_i b_j$, then
\[
 \|M_\varphi:S_p\to S_q\|_{\cb}
 \le \|a\|_{2r}\|b\|_{2r}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\theta_k=\delta_k/\delta$, so $\theta_1+\theta_2=1$, and put
$\psi_{ij}=\varphi_{ij}/(a_i b_j)$ when $a_i b_j\ne0$, and $\psi_{ij}=0$
otherwise.  Then $\|\psi\|_\infty\le1$.  The diagonal multiplication maps
\[
 J_1(x)=\diag(a^{\theta_1})x\diag(b^{\theta_1}):S_p\to S_2,
\]
and
\[
 J_2(y)=\diag(a^{\theta_2})y\diag(b^{\theta_2}):S_2\to S_q
\]
are completely bounded by noncommutative H\"older at every matrix level.
Indeed, when $\delta_k>0$, the corresponding weight exponent is
\[
 \frac{2r}{\theta_k}=\frac2{\delta_k};
\]
when $\delta_k=0$, the associated map is omitted.  Moreover,
\[
 \|J_k\|_{\cb}\le
 \|a\|_{2r}^{\theta_k}\|b\|_{2r}^{\theta_k}.
\]
Since $S_2$ is a homogeneous operator Hilbert space,
$\|M_\psi:S_2\to S_2\|_{\cb}=\|\psi\|_\infty\le1$.  Finally
\[
 M_\varphi=J_2M_\psi J_1,
\]
which gives the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Little Grothendieck endpoint]
\label{lem:endpoint}
Let $2<s\le\infty$.  If $M_\eta:S_s\to S_2$ is completely bounded, then
there are nonnegative $c,d\in\ell_{4s/(s-2)}$ such that
\[
 |\eta_{ij}|\le c_i d_j,
 \qquad
 \|c\|_{4s/(s-2)}\|d\|_{4s/(s-2)}
 \le K_s\|M_\eta\|_{\cb}.
\]
The transposed dual statement holds for $M_\eta:S_2\to S_{s'}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply Xu's operator-space little Grothendieck theorem
\cite[Corollary 7.2]{Xu} to the map $S_s\to S_2=OH$.  It supplies positive
unit functionals $f,g$ on $S_{s/2}$ such that
\[
 \|M_\eta(x)\|_2
 \le K_s\|M_\eta\|_{\cb}
       f(x^*x)^{1/4}g(xx^*)^{1/4}.
\]
Testing $x=e_{ij}$ yields
\[
 |\eta_{ij}|\le K_s\|M_\eta\|_{\cb}
 f(e_{jj})^{1/4}g(e_{ii})^{1/4}.
\]
The diagonal of a positive unit element of
$(S_{s/2})^*=S_{s/(s-2)}$ has norm at most one in
$\ell_{s/(s-2)}$.  Its fourth root therefore belongs to
$\ell_{4s/(s-2)}$.  For $s=\infty$, a state may have a singular part, but
the sequence of its values on the minimal diagonal projections still lies in
the unit ball of $\ell_1$.  This proves the first assertion.  The second
follows by taking adjoints.
\end{proof}

The next lemma is the only place where mixed exponents enter.

\begin{lemma}[Equivariant Maurey factorization]
\label{lem:equivariant}
Let $1\le q<2<p\le\infty$, initially in $n$ dimensions.  If
$T=M_\varphi:S_p^n\to S_q^n$ is completely bounded, then there are
nonnegative diagonal weights $a,b\in\ell_{2/\delta_1}^n$ and a completely
bounded Schur multiplier $M_\psi:S_2^n\to S_q^n$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maurey-factor}
 \varphi_{ij}=a_i\psi_{ij}b_j
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maurey-bound}
 \|a\|_{2/\delta_1}\|M_\psi:S_2^n\to S_q^n\|_{\cb}
 \|b\|_{2/\delta_1}
 \le A_{p,q}\|T\|_{\cb},
\end{equation}
where $A_{p,q}$ is independent of $n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Junge--Parcet's Lemma 4.1(iii) says that a cb map into $S_q$ is completely
$(q,1)$-summing with summing norm at most its cb norm.  Apply their
operator-space Maurey theorem \cite[Theorem A(iii)]{JungeParcet} with domain
exponent $p$, summing exponent $q$, and intermediate exponent $2$.  It gives
a factorization
\[
 T(x)=V(d_1xd_2),\qquad V:S_2^n\to S_q^n,
\]
where $d_1,d_2\in S_{2/\delta_1}^n$ are positive, with the product of the
three norms bounded as in \eqref{eq:maurey-bound}.  If $p=\infty$, use
Theorem A(i) instead; it gives $d_1,d_2\in S_4^n$, again
$4=2/\delta_1$.

Let $E_\Delta:M_n\to\ell_\infty^n$ be the diagonal conditional expectation
and set
\[
 a=E_\Delta(d_1^2)^{1/2},\qquad
 b=E_\Delta(d_2^2)^{1/2}.
\]
These are diagonal, and conditional-expectation contractivity on
$S_{1/\delta_1}^n$ gives
\[
 \|a\|_{2/\delta_1}\le\|d_1\|_{2/\delta_1},\qquad
 \|b\|_{2/\delta_1}\le\|d_2\|_{2/\delta_1}.
\]
We claim that $T$ factors completely boundedly through $x\mapsto axb$.
Let $m\ge1$ and $x\in S_2^m[S_p^n]$.  For diagonal unitaries $U,W$, Schur
equivariance and unitary invariance give
\[
 \|(I_{S_2^m}\otimes T)x\|_{S_2^m[S_q^n]}
 =\|(I_{S_2^m}\otimes T)(UxW)\|_{S_2^m[S_q^n]}.
\]
Use $T(z)=V(d_1zd_2)$, square, and integrate over the two independent
diagonal tori.  Since
$S_2^m[S_2^n]=S_2^{mn}$ is Hilbertian, Fubini and orthogonality of the
characters give the exact identity
\begin{align*}
 \|(I_{S_2^m}\otimes T)x\|_{S_2^m[S_q^n]}^2
 &\le \|V\|_{\cb}^2
 \int\!\int\|d_1UxWd_2\|_{S_2^{mn}}^2\,dU\,dW\\
 &=\|V\|_{\cb}^2\|axb\|_{S_2^{mn}}^2.
\end{align*}
Thus $V_0(axb)=T(x)$ is well defined on the range of
$x\mapsto axb$, and Xu's $S_2$-amplification criterion
\cite[Lemma 1.1]{Xu} gives $\|V_0\|_{\cb}\le\|V\|_{\cb}$.  The range is
the span of those matrix units $e_{ij}$ for which $a_i b_j>0$.  Its
orthogonal coordinate projection in $S_2^n=OH_{n^2}$ is a complete
contraction, by homogeneity of $OH$.  Extending $V_0$ by zero on the
orthogonal complement therefore gives a cb map on all of $S_2^n$.

It remains to make this residual map Schur.  Average it by
\[
 \widetilde V(y)=\int_{\mathbb T^n\times\mathbb T^n}
 U^*V(UyW)W^*\,dU\,dW.
\]
This is a cb Schur multiplier and $\|\widetilde V\|_{\cb}\le\|V\|_{\cb}$.
Since $a,b$ are diagonal,
\[
 \widetilde V(axb)=T(x)
\]
for every $x$.  Writing $\widetilde V=M_\psi$ gives
\eqref{eq:maurey-factor}--\eqref{eq:maurey-bound}.  If $a_i b_j=0$, the
factorization forces $\varphi_{ij}=0$, so we set $\psi_{ij}=0$ there.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

The upper bound in \eqref{eq:norm-equivalence} is
Lemma~\ref{lem:sufficiency}.  We prove necessity.

First suppose $1\le q<2<p\le\infty$ and work in $n$ dimensions.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:equivariant}.  Thus
\[
 \varphi_{ij}=a_i\psi_{ij}b_j,
 \qquad a,b\in\ell_{s_p}^n,
 \qquad s_p=\frac2{\delta_1}.
\]
The adjoint of $M_\psi:S_2^n\to S_q^n$ is a Schur multiplier
$S_{q'}^n\to S_2^n$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint}, with $s=q'$, gives
nonnegative $c,d\in\ell_{s_q}^n$ such that
\[
 |\psi_{ij}|\le c_i d_j,
 \qquad s_q=\frac{4q}{2-q}=\frac2{\delta_2}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 |\varphi_{ij}|\le (a_ic_i)(b_jd_j).
\]
By H\"older and \eqref{eq:deltas},
\[
 \frac1{2r}=\frac\delta2
 =\frac{\delta_1}{2}+\frac{\delta_2}{2}
 =\frac1{s_p}+\frac1{s_q},
\]
so $ac,bd\in\ell_{2r}^n$.  The norm bounds in the two lemmas give
\[
 \F_{2r}(\varphi|_{[n]^2})
 \le C_{p,q}\|M_\varphi:S_p^n\to S_q^n\|_{\cb},
\]
with $C_{p,q}$ independent of $n$.

If $q=2<p$, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint} directly to
$M_\varphi:S_p\to S_2$; its exponent is
\[
 \frac{4p}{p-2}=\frac2{\delta_1}=2r.
\]
If $p=2>q$, apply the adjoint version.  If $p=q=2$, homogeneity of $S_2$
gives the claim with $a_i=b_j=\|\varphi\|_\infty^{1/2}$.  These arguments
also include $p=\infty$ and $q=1$.

Finally let $\varphi$ be infinite.  Every finite northwest compression has
cb norm at most $\|M_\varphi\|_{\cb}$ and hence admits factors
$A^{(n)},B^{(n)}\in\ell_{2r}^n$ with uniformly bounded product.  Balance the
two norms by scalar rescaling and extend the sequences by zero.  Since
$2r\ge2$, bounded sets in $\ell_{2r}$ are weakly sequentially compact.
Pass to a common weakly convergent subsequence.  Coordinates converge, so
the pointwise domination passes to the limit, while weak lower
semicontinuity preserves the norm bound.  Thus
$\F_{2r}(\varphi)\le C_{p,q}\|M_\varphi\|_{\cb}$, completing the proof.

\section{Checks, sharpness, and limitations}

On the dual line $q=p'$, one has
\[
 2r=\frac{2p}{p-2},
\]
so Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is exactly Xu's Corollary 7.6(ii).  At the two
off-dual Hilbert corners it gives $\ell_4\times\ell_4$ for
$S_\infty\to S_2$ and $S_2\to S_1$.  At the extreme corner
$S_\infty\to S_1$ it gives $\ell_2\times\ell_2$.

For rank-one symbols $\varphi_{ij}=\alpha_i\beta_j$, the multiplier is
$x\mapsto\diag(\alpha)x\diag(\beta)$.  Oikhberg proved the exact formula
\cite[Theorem 2.1]{Oikhberg}
\[
 \|x\mapsto AxB:S_p\to S_q\|_{\cb}
 =\|A\|_{2r}\|B\|_{2r}.
\]
Thus the exponent $2r$ is forced already by rank-one symbols.  Oikhberg also
records the $S_\infty\to S_2$ Schur endpoint in his Theorems 5.1--5.2.

No numerical calculation enters the proof.  The accompanying script checks
the exponent identities exactly on representative rational cases.

The result is a characterization of Schur multipliers, not a solution to the
broader mixed j.c.b. Problem 7.8.  The Schur symmetry is used decisively to
diagonalize the Maurey densities and the residual map.

\section{Novelty check and review recommendation}

The bounded search performed on 11 August 2026 covered the run registry and
source indexes, exact web/arXiv phrases for mixed cb Schur multipliers and
factorization through $S_2/OH$, and the 33 works indexed by OpenAlex as citing
Xu's paper.  It found the two ingredients used here---Junge--Parcet and
Oikhberg---and later same-exponent work, but no paper stating the full mixed
characterization.  Because the proof combines known machinery in a short new
way, novelty confidence is moderate rather than definitive.

Human review should first inspect Lemma~\ref{lem:equivariant}, specifically
the equality obtained by averaging the squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm and the
subsequent use of the $S_2$-amplification criterion.  Once this diagonal
factorization is accepted, the remaining argument is the cited endpoint
theorem plus H\"older arithmetic.
