\section{The source problem}

For a Tychonoff space $X$, let $L(X)$ denote its free locally convex space.
Gabriyelyan and Ka\c{k}ol ask in Problem 1.1 on PDF page 2 of
arXiv:1604.02555v2 \cite{GK}:
\begin{quote}
For which spaces $X$ does the free locally convex space $L(X)$ have a
$\mathfrak G$-base?
\end{quote}
Their terminology means a neighborhood base at zero indexed monotonically by
$\NN^{\NN}$.  The problem is reproduced from Question 4.15 of an earlier paper
by Gabriyelyan, Ka\c{k}ol, and Leiderman.

The source proves a useful Ascoli-space reformulation: if $X$ is Dieudonn\'e
complete and Ascoli, then $L(X)$ has a $\mathfrak G$-base exactly when
$C_k(X)$ admits a compact resolution swallowing compact subsets
\cite[Theorem~1.2]{GK}.

\section{The explicit complete characterization}

Banakh and Leiderman state in the introduction of arXiv:1606.01967v2 that
their Theorem 8.1 ``resolves completely'' Question 4.15 and repeat the question
in the same sentence \cite[PDF pp.~2--3]{BL}.  Theorem 8.1(1), on PDF page 19,
gives the promised characterization for every Tychonoff space $X$:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&L(X)\text{ has a local }\mathfrak G\text{-base}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\\
&\mathcal U(X)\text{ has a }\mathfrak G\text{-base and }C(X)
  \text{ is }\NN^{\NN}\text{-dominated}.
\end{aligned}}
\]
Here $\mathcal U(X)$ is the finest compatible uniformity, $C(X)$ is ordered
pointwise, and $\NN^{\NN}$-dominated means that there is a monotone family
$(f_\alpha)_{\alpha\in\NN^{\NN}}\subseteq C(X)$ cofinal for the pointwise
order.  The theorem also gives the equivalent condition that $C(X)$ is
$\NN^{\NN}$-dominated as a subposet of $\RR^X$, and equivalence with the free
topological vector space $V(X)$ having a local $\mathfrak G$-base.

Since a $\mathfrak G$-base for a topological vector space is, by definition,
such a local base at zero, this is a necessary-and-sufficient answer to the
source's broad Problem 1.1, with no Ascoli, separability, or metrizability
hypothesis.

\section{Scope that remains open}

This identification must not be overread.  On PDF page 8, the source paper
explicitly says that it does not know whether the Ascoli assumption in its
Theorem 1.2 is essential.  The later Theorem 8.1 does not remove that
assumption from the particular equivalence involving a compact resolution in
$C_k(X)$: its item (7), relating $L(X)$ to a local $\mathfrak G$-base of
$C_k(C_k(X))$, still assumes $X$ is Ascoli.  Thus the broad intrinsic
characterization is complete, while this sharper reformulation question is
not claimed here.

Likewise, Problem 1.3 of the source asks more broadly which spaces $X$ make
$C_k(X)$ admit a compact resolution swallowing compact subsets.  The cited
theorem is not a characterization of that class, so this packet does not
dispose of Problem 1.3.
