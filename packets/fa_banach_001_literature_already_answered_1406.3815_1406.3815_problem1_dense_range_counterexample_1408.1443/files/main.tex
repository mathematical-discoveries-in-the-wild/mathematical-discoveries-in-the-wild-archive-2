\section*{Original question}

In Section 4, Problem 1, page 18 of the arXiv PDF, Nasseri
\cite{Nasseri2014} asks:
\begin{quote}
Is it true that an operator $T:C(K)\to C(K)$ with dense range, where
$C(K)$ has the Grothendieck property, is actually surjective?
\end{quote}

\section*{Answer}

Johnson, Nasseri, Schechtman, and Tkocz \cite{JNST2015} answer this
negatively.  Corollary 1 on page 5 of arXiv:1408.1443 states that there is an
injective, dense-range, nonsurjective operator on $\ellinf$.  Since
\[
 \ellinf=C(\beta\mathbb N)
\]
and $\ellinf$ has the Grothendieck property, their operator satisfies every
hypothesis of Problem 1 and violates its proposed conclusion.

The construction starts with an injective, dense-range, nonsurjective
Tauberian operator $S$ on $L_1(0,1)$.  Tauberianity makes $S^{**}$ injective,
so $S^*$ has dense proper range; density of the range of $S$ makes $S^*$
injective.  The classical isomorphism $L_1(0,1)^*\cong\ellinf$ transfers
$S^*$ to the required operator on $\ellinf$.  This paragraph is only an
identification summary; the full construction is in the supporting paper.

The answering paper cites arXiv:1406.3815 and notes, using its spectral
theorem, that zero is an interior point of the spectrum of any dense-range,
nonsurjective operator on $\ellinf$.

\section*{Scope and search status}

This is an exact answer only to Problem 1.  Problems 2 (the converse
Grothendieck/Dunford--Pettis characterization) and 3 (the $H^\infty$ analogue
of the commuting-product corollary) are not answered by arXiv:1408.1443 and
are not claimed solved here.

The identification was checked against the original PDFs: Problem 1 occurs
on original PDF page 18, and the counterexample is Corollary 1 on supporting
PDF page 5.  Exact-title, dense-range, Grothendieck, and $H^\infty$ searches
through 11 August 2026 found the direct answering paper; no new mathematical
result is claimed by this packet.
