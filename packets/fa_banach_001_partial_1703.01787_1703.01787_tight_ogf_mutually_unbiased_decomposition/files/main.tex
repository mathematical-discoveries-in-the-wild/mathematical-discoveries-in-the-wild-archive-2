\section{Question and statement}

\textbf{Source question (transcribed).} ``Given the existence of an OGF
$\Phi\in\Omega_{n,m}(\F)$, under what conditions does
$\mu_{n,m}(\F)=\overline\mu_{n,m}(\F)$?''  In the source notation the
unbarred constant is minimized over unit-norm tight frames and the barred
constant over all unit-norm frames.

Let $g_{n,m}(\F)$ denote the minimum coherence among all $n$-element
unit-norm frames for $\F^m$, and let $t_{n,m}(\F)$ be the corresponding
minimum under the additional tightness constraint.  These are respectively
the Grassmannian and $1$-Grassmannian constants of Haas and
Casazza~\cite{HC}.  Put
\[
 d_{\F}(m)=
 \begin{cases}
  m(m+1)/2,&\F=\R,\\
  m^2,&\F=\C.
 \end{cases}
\]
In the orthoplex regime $n>d_{\F}(m)$, existence of an orthoplectic
Grassmannian frame (OGF) means
$g_{n,m}(\F)=1/\sqrt m$.  Question~2 of~\cite{HC} asks when
$t_{n,m}(\F)=g_{n,m}(\F)$.

Two frames are called \emph{mutually unbiased} here if every cross inner
product has modulus $1/\sqrt m$.

\subsection*{Proof Intuition}

The normalized traceless projector of each frame vector converts the
orthoplex coherence inequality into a nonpositive-inner-product condition.
Tightness becomes the assertion that these projector vectors sum to zero.
Join two projector vectors when their inner product is strictly negative.
Different connected components are orthogonal, so the global zero sum splits
into a separate zero sum on every component.  Translating back says that each
component is itself a unit-norm tight frame, while orthogonality between
components says precisely that the blocks are mutually unbiased.  Inside a
connected component the projector Gram matrix is a weighted graph Laplacian;
its rank is therefore the block size minus one.  The finite dimension of the
traceless-matrix space then gives the component and cardinality bounds.  The
argument also reverses directly.  What remains open is the design-theoretic
existence of the required blocks for individual parameters, including the
complete-MUB obstruction at the complex upper endpoint.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical decomposition criterion]\label{thm:main}
Assume $n>d_{\F}(m)$ and that an OGF exists.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality}
 t_{n,m}(\F)=g_{n,m}(\F)=\frac1{\sqrt m}
\end{equation}
if and only if there is a partition
$\Phi=\bigsqcup_{a=1}^r\Phi_a$ of $n$ unit vectors such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item each $\Phi_a$ is a unit-norm tight frame for $\F^m$;
\item distinct $\Phi_a,\Phi_b$ are mutually unbiased; and
\item every inner product within each $\Phi_a$ has modulus at most
      $1/\sqrt m$.
\end{enumerate}
The partition may be chosen canonically so that the graph on each $\Phi_a$
whose edges are the pairs with modulus \emph{strictly less} than
$1/\sqrt m$ is connected.  For this canonical partition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counts}
 |\Phi_a|\geq m,
 \qquad
 r\geq n-d_{\F}(m)+1,
 \qquad
 n-r\leq d_{\F}(m)-1.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Thus equality of the two constants is not merely the presence of some tight
minimizer: beyond the Gerzon threshold, that minimizer must break into at
least two mutually unbiased tight designs.

\section{Proof}

For a unit vector $\phi\in\F^m$, put $P_\phi=\phi\phi^*$ and use the
normalized traceless embedding
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q}
 Q_\phi=\sqrt{\frac{m}{m-1}}
 \left(P_\phi-\frac1mI_m\right).
\end{equation}
The vectors $Q_\phi$ lie on the unit sphere of the real Hilbert space of
traceless symmetric or Hermitian matrices, whose dimension is
$D=d_{\F}(m)-1$.  Direct calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inner}
 \langle Q_\phi,Q_\psi\rangle
 =\frac{m|\langle\phi,\psi\rangle|^2-1}{m-1}.
\end{equation}
Moreover a unit-norm family $\Phi$ is tight precisely when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zerosum}
 \sum_{\phi\in\Phi}Q_\phi=0.
\end{equation}
This zero-sum observation is recorded in~\cite{CH}; we exploit the sign in
\eqref{eq:inner} as well.

Suppose first that \eqref{eq:equality} holds.  Compactness of the unit-norm-tight-frame
manifold gives a tight minimizer $\Phi$ with coherence $1/\sqrt m$.  Write
$Q_i=Q_{\phi_i}$.  Equations \eqref{eq:inner} and \eqref{eq:zerosum} say
\begin{equation}\label{eq:code}
 \langle Q_i,Q_j\rangle\leq0\quad(i\ne j),
 \qquad \sum_iQ_i=0.
\end{equation}

Make a graph on the indices by joining $i$ and $j$ exactly when
$\langle Q_i,Q_j\rangle<0$, and let $C_1,\ldots,C_r$ be its connected
components.  Vectors belonging to different components are orthogonal.
Consequently their spans are mutually orthogonal.  If
$S_a=\sum_{i\in C_a}Q_i$, then $S_a\perp S_b$ for $a\ne b$, while
$\sum_aS_a=0$ by \eqref{eq:code}.  Hence
\[
 0=\left\|\sum_aS_a\right\|^2=\sum_a\|S_a\|^2,
\]
so every $S_a=0$.  Undoing \eqref{eq:Q} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blocktight}
 \sum_{i\in C_a}P_{\phi_i}=\frac{|C_a|}{m}I_m.
\end{equation}
Thus each block $\Phi_a=\{\phi_i:i\in C_a\}$ is a unit-norm tight frame,
and in particular $|C_a|\geq m$.  Cross-component orthogonality in
\eqref{eq:inner} is exactly mutual unbiasedness.  This proves the structural
assertions.

It remains to obtain the count.  Let $G_a$ be the Gram matrix of the
$Q_i$ with $i\in C_a$.  It has diagonal entries one, nonpositive
off-diagonal entries, and $G_a\mathbf1=0$ because $S_a=0$.  With weights
$w_{ij}=-\langle Q_i,Q_j\rangle$, the matrix $G_a$ is therefore the weighted
graph Laplacian of the connected graph on $C_a$.  Its kernel is
$\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}$, so
\[
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{Q_i:i\in C_a\}=\operatorname{rank}G_a=|C_a|-1.
\]
The component spans are mutually orthogonal inside a $D$-dimensional space;
hence
\[
 n-r=\sum_a(|C_a|-1)\leq D=d_{\F}(m)-1,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:counts}.

Conversely, suppose a partition satisfying (1)--(3) of the theorem is
given.  The union is unit norm and tight because the block frame operators
in \eqref{eq:blocktight} add to $(n/m)I_m$.  Its coherence is at most
$1/\sqrt m$.  Since $n>d_{\F}(m)$, the orthoplex bound says that no
unit-norm frame can have smaller coherence.  The union is therefore both a
tight OGF and a tight-constrained minimizer, proving \eqref{eq:equality}. \qed

\section{Consequences}

\begin{corollary}[Cardinality obstruction]\label{cor:card}
If the two constants agree in the orthoplex regime, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:card}
 n\leq
 \begin{cases}
  \displaystyle\frac{m(m+2)}2,&\F=\R,\\[4pt]
  m(m+1),&\F=\C.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
At the complex upper endpoint $n=m(m+1)$, every tight OGF is a union of
$m+1$ mutually unbiased orthonormal bases.  At the real upper endpoint, when
$m$ is even, every tight OGF is a union of $m/2+1$ mutually unbiased
orthonormal bases.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since every block has at least $m$ elements, $r\leq n/m$.  Combine this with
$r\geq n-d_{\F}(m)+1$ to obtain
\[
 n\leq\frac{m(d_{\F}(m)-1)}{m-1},
\]
which simplifies to \eqref{eq:card}.  At either stated integral endpoint, equality
forces equality in all counting inequalities, so every block has exactly
$m$ unit vectors.  A unit-norm tight frame of $m$ vectors in $\F^m$ is an
orthonormal basis.
\end{proof}

For example, in $\C^2$ a tight five-vector OGF must have exactly two
canonical blocks of sizes $2$ and $3$: an orthonormal basis and a
three-vector unit-norm tight frame, mutually unbiased to one another.  A
tight six-vector OGF must be the union of three mutually unbiased bases.
This recovers and structurally explains the tight five-vector example in
the papers rather than merely verifying its matrix entries.

\section{Verification notes and review recommendation}

The projector identity \eqref{eq:inner} follows by expanding
$\operatorname{tr}((P_\phi-I/m)(P_\psi-I/m))$, and
\eqref{eq:zerosum} is exactly the tight-frame-operator identity after the
same affine change of variables.  Compactness of the unit-norm tight-frame
manifold ensures that the constrained minimum used in the forward direction
is attained.  The key splitting step uses only Pythagoras: mutually
orthogonal component sums whose total is zero must each vanish.  For a
connected component, $G_a$ has diagonal one, entries $-w_{ij}$ off the
diagonal, and zero row sums, hence is the weighted Laplacian of the strict-edge
graph and has one-dimensional kernel.  Summing the mutually orthogonal
component ranks gives \eqref{eq:counts}; the converse uses only the block
frame-operator sum and the orthoplex lower bound.  No computational or
numerical assertion is used.

\textbf{Human-review recommendation.} Review as a likely-valid substantial
partial answer.  The highest-value checks are the Laplacian identification
and the claim that no equivalent decomposition/cardinality theorem already
appears in the OGF or mutually-unbiased-frame literature.

\section{Scope, upgrade barrier, and novelty check}

The theorem is an exact structural criterion, but not a parameter-only
classification: existence of the required mutually unbiased tight
subframes remains a separate design problem.  At maximal complex cardinality
it is precisely the complete-MUB existence problem; for example, a universal
parameter theorem in dimension six would decide the famous question of seven
complex MUBs.  This is why the packet is classified as a strong partial
answer rather than a complete numerical table for all $(n,m)$.

Cheap run indexes contained no prior result for arXiv:1703.01787.  Bounded
searches through 11 August 2026 for tight OGF decompositions, the cardinality
bounds in \eqref{eq:card}, negative-inner-product graphs, and mutually unbiased tight
frames found the source papers and later work introducing mutually unbiased
frames~\cite{MUF}, but no statement of the converse decomposition theorem
above.  This is not an exhaustive novelty claim.
