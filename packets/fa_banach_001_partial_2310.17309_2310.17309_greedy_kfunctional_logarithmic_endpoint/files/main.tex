\section*{Source question and setup}

Gulgowski, Kamont, and Passenbrunner~\cite{GKP} study a $p$-normalized local
orthonormal system $\Phi=(\phi_j)$ associated with a $\nu$-ary filtration.
For $1<p<\infty$, it is a greedy (hence unconditional) basis of
$X=L^p(S)$ and has the $p$-Temlyakov property.  Given $0<\sigma<p$, put
\[
 \alpha=\frac1\sigma-\frac1p,
 \qquad q=\min(\sigma,2).
\]
Their ring-variation space is equipped with
\[
 |f|_{\V}=\sup_{\Pi}
 \left(\sum_{R\in\Pi}E_p(f,R)^\sigma\right)^{1/\sigma},
 \qquad \norm f_{\V}=\norm f_p+|f|_{\V},
\]
where $\Pi$ ranges over finite disjoint families of atoms or rings and
$E_p(f,R)$ is the local best approximation error by $S$.

The open question is whether, under the paper's Bernstein/geometric
hypothesis, one has uniformly in $f$ and $n$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-question}
 K(f,2^{-n\alpha};X,\V)
 \asymp \norm{f-\G_{2^n}f}_p
       +2^{-n\alpha}\norm{\G_{2^n}f}_{\V}.
\end{equation}
(The source reverses the subscripts once on the left of its display (4.3);
$V_{\sigma,p}$ is plainly intended.)  Here
$K(f,t;X,\V)=\inf_{g\in\V}(\norm{f-g}_p+t\norm g_{\V})$.

\section*{Proof Intuition}

Fix the set of the $2^n$ largest coefficients of $f$ and decompose
$f=g+(f-g)$ for an arbitrary competitor in the $K$-functional.  The error
part is harmless: unconditionality controls its coordinate projection in
$L^p$, and the variation Bernstein inequality pays exactly $2^{n\alpha}$,
which is cancelled by the outside factor $2^{-n\alpha}$.  The structured
part $g$ lies at the weak endpoint $\A_\infty^\alpha$.  Truncating any
$\A_\infty^\alpha$ coefficient sequence to $2^n$ entries puts it in the
strong endpoint $\A_q^\alpha$, at the cost of only $(n+1)^{1/q}$.  The
embedding $\A_q^\alpha\hookrightarrow V_{\sigma,p}$ then completes the
estimate.  Thus the missing endpoint boundedness is localized to a
logarithmic-in-cardinality loss.

\section*{Partial theorem}

Assume $0<\tau<p$, set $\beta=1/\tau-1/p$, and suppose the source's
condition $\operatorname{BI}(\mathscr A,S,p,\tau)$ holds.  Choose
$0<\alpha<\beta$ and define $\sigma$ by
$\alpha=1/\sigma-1/p$; put $q=\min(\sigma,2)$.

\begin{theorem}[Pointwise near-attainment]\label{thm:main}
There is $C\ge1$ such that for every $f\in L^p(S)$ and every integer
$n\ge0$, if
\[
 A_n(f):=\norm{f-\G_{2^n}f}_p
  +2^{-n\alpha}\norm{\G_{2^n}f}_{\V},
 \qquad
 K_n(f):=K(f,2^{-n\alpha};L^p(S),\V),
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 K_n(f)\le A_n(f)\le C(n+1)^{1/q}K_n(f).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the loss is
$O((1+\log N)^{1/q})$ for an $N$-term greedy approximant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $n$, write $N=2^n$, $t=N^{-\alpha}$, and let
$P_\Lambda f=\G_Nf$, where $\Lambda$ is the greedy index set selected from
$f$.  We use four results established in the source:
\begin{align}
 \norm{u-\G_Nu}_p&\lesssim N^{-\alpha}\norm u_{\V}
       &&(u\in\V),                                      \label{eq:jackson}\tag{J}\\
 \norm v_{\V}&\lesssim N^\alpha\norm v_p
       &&(v\in\Sigma_N^\Phi),                           \label{eq:bernstein}\tag{B}\\
 \A_q^\alpha&\hookrightarrow\V,
       &\V&\hookrightarrow\A_\infty^\alpha.            \label{eq:embeddings}\tag{E}
\end{align}
For \eqref{eq:bernstein}, the source states the estimate first for its local
dictionary $\mathscr C$; since every element of $\Phi$ is a sum of at most
$\nu$ elements of $\mathscr C$, the displayed form follows after changing
the constant.  Estimate \eqref{eq:jackson} is (4.9), and
\eqref{eq:embeddings} comprises Theorems 4.4 and 4.5(B) of~\cite{GKP}.

We also need the following direct consequence of the coefficient
characterization of approximation spaces (Fact 2.2 there).  For every finite
$\Lambda$ with $|\Lambda|\le2^n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truncation}
 \norm{P_\Lambda u}_{\A_q^\alpha}
 \lesssim (n+1)^{1/q}\norm u_{\A_\infty^\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, let $(a_m)$ and $(b_m)$ be the decreasing rearrangements of the
$\Phi$-coefficients of $u$ and $P_\Lambda u$.  Then $b_m\le a_m$ and
$b_m=0$ for $m>2^n$.  The weak endpoint gives
$2^{j(\alpha+1/p)}a_{2^j}\lesssim\norm u_{\A_\infty^\alpha}$.
The $\ell^q$ coefficient formula, summed only over $0\le j\le n$, proves
\eqref{eq:truncation}.

Take arbitrary $g\in\V$ and set $h=f-g$.  Since $\Phi$ is greedy,
best-approximation subadditivity and \eqref{eq:jackson} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error}
 \norm{f-P_\Lambda f}_p
 \lesssim \sigma_N(f;\Phi)
 \le \norm h_p+\sigma_N(g;\Phi)
 \lesssim \norm h_p+t\norm g_{\V}.
\end{equation}
Coordinate projections are uniformly bounded on $L^p(S)$ because $\Phi$ is
unconditional.  Hence \eqref{eq:bernstein} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hpart}
 t\norm{P_\Lambda h}_{\V}
 \lesssim tN^\alpha\norm{P_\Lambda h}_p
 \lesssim \norm h_p.
\end{equation}
For the structured part, \eqref{eq:embeddings} and
\eqref{eq:truncation} yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gpart}
 t\norm{P_\Lambda g}_{\V}
 \lesssim t\norm{P_\Lambda g}_{\A_q^\alpha}
 \lesssim t(n+1)^{1/q}\norm g_{\A_\infty^\alpha}
 \lesssim t(n+1)^{1/q}\norm g_{\V}.
\end{equation}
The (quasi-)triangle inequality in $\V$, followed by
\eqref{eq:error}--\eqref{eq:gpart}, gives
\[
 A_n(f)\lesssim(n+1)^{1/q}
    \bigl(\norm{f-g}_p+t\norm g_{\V}\bigr).
\]
Taking the infimum over $g\in\V$ proves the upper estimate in
\eqref{eq:main}.  Conversely, $P_\Lambda f$ is a finite linear combination
of local basis elements and therefore belongs to $\V$; using it as a
$K$-functional competitor proves $K_n(f)\le A_n(f)$.
\end{proof}

\section*{Verification, scope, and novelty status}

The exponent check is exact: $tN^\alpha=1$, while the coefficient block
$0\le j\le n$ has $n+1$ entries and hence costs $(n+1)^{1/q}$.  No
linearity of the greedy map is used: $P_\Lambda$ is fixed only after the
greedy set of $f$ is chosen, and the same linear coordinate projection is
then applied to $g$ and $h$.  The proof works for quasi-Banach
$V_{\sigma,p}$ because only a two-term quasi-triangle inequality is needed.

The theorem does \emph{not} prove the uniform upper estimate in
\eqref{eq:source-question}; removing the scale factor is exactly the endpoint
cancellation problem.  Eight upgrade attempts were made.  In particular, an
exact finite-tree mixed-integer search found no growing instability on
balanced dyadic trees of depths four and five; this computation is exploratory
and is not used in the proof.  Searches through the run indexes and bounded
web searches by exact arXiv id/title and the phrases ``greedy approximation'',
``ring variation'', and ``K-functional'' found no later explicit resolution as
of 12 August 2026.  This is a bounded audit, not a priority claim.

\noindent\textbf{Human-review recommendation.} Check chiefly the endpoint
coefficient estimate \eqref{eq:truncation} with the source's normalization,
and the passage from the $\mathscr C$-Bernstein estimate to an $N$-term
$\Phi$ projection.  These are the only nonliteral combinations of source
results.
