{\Large Literature Status and Explicit Counterexample:\\Projective Tensors Do Not Preserve Property-$U$\par}
\vspace{0.45em}
{fa\_banach\_001, lane 8 \quad August 9, 2026\par}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.35em}

\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{literature\_already\_answered}. The later paper gives a negative answer; below is a direct two-dimensional witness.

\paragraph{Question.}
Daptari--Paul--Rao, arXiv:2009.09581, Theorem 3.7 proves under suitable hypotheses that property-$U$ and property-$SU$ pass back and forth between $Y\subset Z$ and
$X\widehat\otimes_\varepsilon Y\subset X\widehat\otimes_\varepsilon Z$.
Immediately afterward they ask whether the analogous conclusion holds for $\widehat\otimes_\pi$.

\paragraph{Later answer.}
Daptari--Paul, arXiv:2202.00947, states in its tensor-product subsection that property-$U$ need not be stable under projective tensor products, citing Basu--Rao, Example 2.3. It then proves only the restricted positive result that, when $Y$ has property-$SU$ in $Z$, every \emph{isometry} in $\mathcal L(X,Y^*)$ has a unique norm-preserving extension. Property-$U$ would require this for every bounded operator.

\paragraph{Explicit finite-dimensional witness.}
Work over $\R$, as in the source, and take
\[
 X=\ell_\infty^2,\qquad Z=\ell_2^2,\qquad Y=\operatorname{span}\{e_1\}.
\]
Here $X^*=\ell_1^2$, so $X$ is an $L_1$-predual; $Z^*$ has the RNP; and $Y$ has property-$SU$ in the Hilbert space $Z$. The orthogonal projection $Z\to Y$ is contractive, hence the canonical map $X\widehat\otimes_\pi Y\to X\widehat\otimes_\pi Z$ is an isometry.

Using $(X\widehat\otimes_\pi Z)^*=\mathcal B(X,Z)$, for $x=(a,b)$ and $z=(s,t)$ define
\[
 G_0(x,z)=\frac{s(a+b)}2,\qquad
 G_1(x,z)=\frac{s(a+b)}2+\frac{t(a-b)}2.
\]
They have the same restriction
$F(x,se_1)=s(a+b)/2$ to $X\times Y$. Since $Y$ is one dimensional,
\[
 \|F\|=\|(1/2,1/2)\|_{\ell_1^2}=1,
 \qquad \|G_0\|=1.
\]
For the other extension, optimize first over $z\in B_{\ell_2^2}$:
\[
 \|G_1\|
 =\sup_{|a|,|b|\le1}
 \sqrt{\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)^2+\left(\frac{a-b}{2}\right)^2}
 =\sup_{|a|,|b|\le1}\sqrt{\frac{a^2+b^2}{2}}=1.
\]
But $G_0\ne G_1$ (evaluate at $x=(1,-1)$ and $z=e_2$). Thus $F$ has two distinct norm-preserving extensions. Therefore
\[
 X\widehat\otimes_\pi Y\ \text{does not have property-$U$ in}\
 X\widehat\otimes_\pi Z.
\]
Since property-$SU$ implies property-$U$, this single example disproves both proposed projective-tensor equivalences, despite satisfying every hypothesis of the injective theorem.
