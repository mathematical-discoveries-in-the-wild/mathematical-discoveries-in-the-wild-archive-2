\section*{Status and source problem}

This is a \texttt{candidate\_partial\_likely\_valid} result for Karl-Mikael
Perfekt and Alexander Pushnitski, \emph{On the spectrum of the multiplicative
Hilbert matrix}, arXiv:1705.01959 \cite{PP}.

For $a>0$, the source considers the positive Helson matrix on
$\ell^2(\N)$
\[
 M(g_a)_{m,n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{mn}\,(a+\log(mn))},\qquad m,n\geq1.
\]
Its Theorem 1.2 produces a critical number $a_*>0$: if $a\geq a_*$,
$M(g_a)$ has no eigenvalue above $\pi$, whereas if $0<a<a_*$ it has exactly
one simple eigenvalue above $\pi$.  On PDF page 3 the authors say that they
cannot explicitly compute $a_*$ and give only
\[
 \frac1\pi\leq a_*\leq2.
\]
The same passage asks for an explicit diagonalization of $M(g_a)$ and of the
multiplicative Hilbert matrix $M_2(g_0)$.

\section*{Main result}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $a_*$ be the critical parameter in \cite[Theorem 1.2]{PP}, and let
$\gamma$ denote the Euler--Mascheroni constant.  Then
\[
 \boxed{\quad \frac13<a_*\leq2\gamma\quad}.
\]
Numerically, $2\gamma=1.1544313298\ldots$.  Both inequalities strictly
improve the source estimates.
\end{theorem}

\section*{Proof intuition}

The two bounds use different representations.  At the matrix level, a
two-dimensional compression already detects an eigenvalue above $\pi$ at
$a=1/3$; deliberately coarse rational bounds for $\pi$ and $\log 2$ suffice
to certify the sign of its characteristic polynomial.

For the upper bound, the source factors $M(g_a)$ through an integral Hankel
operator with kernel
\[
 h_a(t)=e^{-at/2}\zeta(1+t).
\]
The logarithmic derivative of $t\zeta(1+t)$ is a decreasing function whose
limit at zero is $\gamma$.  Thus $t\zeta(1+t)\leq e^{\gamma t}$.  When
$a\geq2\gamma$, this cancels the exponential in $h_a$ and leaves the
Carleman majorant $1/t$, whose Hankel operator has norm $\pi$.

\section*{Upper bound}

We use a recent real-axis zeta inequality.

\begin{lemma}[Garcia--Greni\'e--Molteni \cite{GGM}]\label{lem:zeta}
The function
\[
 s\longmapsto \frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}+\frac{1}{s-1}
\]
is decreasing on $(1,\infty)$.
\end{lemma}

This is Theorem 5 of arXiv:2607.08342.  Define
\[
 \Phi(t)=\log\bigl(t\zeta(1+t)\bigr),\qquad t>0.
\]
The Laurent expansion $\zeta(1+t)=t^{-1}+\gamma+O(t)$ gives
$\Phi(0+)=0$ and $\Phi'(0+)=\gamma$.  Moreover,
\[
 \Phi'(t)=\frac1t+\frac{\zeta'(1+t)}{\zeta(1+t)}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:zeta} says that $\Phi'$ is decreasing.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zetaexp}
 \Phi(t)\leq\gamma t,
 \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
 t\zeta(1+t)\leq e^{\gamma t}quad(t>0).
\end{equation}

The source's Theorem 2.2 identifies the nonzero part of $M(g_a)$ unitarily
with the integral Hankel operator $H(h_a)$ on $L^2(\R_+)$, where
$h_a(t)=e^{-at/2}\zeta(1+t)$.  If $a\geq2\gamma$, then
\[
 0<h_a(t)\leq e^{-\gamma t}\frac{e^{\gamma t}}t=\frac1t.
\]
The source's Carleman estimate (Lemma 2.1) consequently gives
$\lVert H(h_a)\rVert\leq\pi$, hence
$\lVert M(g_a)\rVert\leq\pi$.  There can be no eigenvalue above $\pi$, so
the defining dichotomy for $a_*$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper}
 a_*\leq2\gamma.
\end{equation}

This use of pointwise Carleman domination cannot improve the constant:
\[
 e^{-at/2}t\zeta(1+t)
 =1+(\gamma-a/2)t+O(t^2),
\]
so the needed pointwise inequality fails near zero for every $a<2\gamma$.

\section*{Lower bound}

Set $a=1/3$ and compress $M(g_a)$ to
$\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_2\}$.  Its matrix is
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}
 3 & \dfrac{1}{\sqrt2(1/3+\log2)}\\[6pt]
 \dfrac{1}{\sqrt2(1/3+\log2)}
   & \dfrac{1}{2(1/3+\log4)}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
We show that $\det(\pi I-A)<0$.  Since $3<\pi<22/7$ and the lower-right
entry of $A$ is positive,
\[
 (\pi-3)\left(\pi-\frac{1}{2(1/3+\log4)}\right)
 <\pi(\pi-3)<\frac{22}{49}.
\]
Also $\log2<7/10$.  For completeness, the latter follows from
\[
 e^{7/10}>1+\frac7{10}+\frac{(7/10)^2}{2}
                 +\frac{(7/10)^3}{6}>2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 A_{12}^2
 =\frac{1}{2(1/3+\log2)^2}
 >\frac{1}{2(31/30)^2}
 =\frac{450}{961}
 >\frac{22}{49}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \det(\pi I-A)
 =(\pi-A_{11})(\pi-A_{22})-A_{12}^2<0.
\]
For a real symmetric $2\times2$ matrix this says that its two eigenvalues
straddle $\pi$; in particular $\lambda_{\max}(A)>\pi$.  The maximal Rayleigh
quotient of the full operator $M(g_{1/3})$ is therefore greater than $\pi$.
By the spectral dichotomy in \cite[Theorem 1.2]{PP}, $M(g_{1/3})$ has its
simple eigenvalue above $\pi$, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 \frac13<a_*.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:upper} and \eqref{eq:lower} proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section*{Verification, upgrade attempts, and limitations}

The lower estimate is a finite exact certificate; no floating-point spectral
calculation enters the proof.  The only external input beyond the source
operator theory is Lemma~\ref{lem:zeta}.  Its variable substitution is exact:
$s=1+t$ makes its left-hand side equal to $\Phi'(t)$.

Before locating Lemma~\ref{lem:zeta}, an elementary midpoint estimate for the
convex function $x^{-1-t}$ gave
\[
 t\zeta(1+t)\leq t+(3/2)^{-t}
 \leq e^{(1-\log(3/2))t},
\]
and hence the weaker new upper bound
$a_*\leq2(1-\log(3/2))$.  The monotonicity theorem upgrades this to the
method-sharp constant $2\gamma$.

The packet does not determine $a_*$, compute the eigenvalue $\lambda(a)$,
or explicitly diagonalize either matrix.  Higher finite compressions can
numerically strengthen the lower endpoint, but no similarly transparent
closed certificate was retained here.  The bounded-symbol endpoint mentioned
on the same source page is a distinct question already covered by the run
attempt
\path{2005.11951_multiplicative_hilbert_h1_endpoint_attempt.md}.

A bounded novelty search used the exact phrases \texttt{critical value
multiplicative Hilbert matrix}, \texttt{M(g\_a) critical value}, the source
arXiv id, and the constants in the preliminary bound.  It found the source
estimate but no later improvement.  The July 2026 zeta paper was found through
a separate search for real-axis bounds on $(s-1)\zeta(s)$; no application of
its Theorem 5 to this Helson family was located.  Novelty confidence is
moderate.  Human review should focus on the use of the source's critical
dichotomy at $a=1/3$ and on the bibliographic novelty check.
