\section{Source conjecture}

For a continuous three-input kernel $K:(\Sph)^3\to\R$ and a probability
measure $\mu$, write
\[
 I_K(\mu)=\iiint K(x,y,z)\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y)d\mu(z),
 \qquad
 U_K^\sigma(x,y)=\int K(x,y,z)\,d\sigma(z),
\]
where $\sigma$ is normalized surface measure.  Bilyk--Ferizovi\'c--Glazyrin--
Matzke--Park--Vlasiuk conjecture, more generally for $n$ inputs, that $\sigma$
globally minimizes $I_K$ if and only if the averaged two-input kernel
$U_K^{\sigma^{n-2}}$ is conditionally positive definite
\cite{BilykEtAl}.  Their Theorem~5.3 proves the necessary direction and proves
only local minimality in the converse direction when the lower energy has
$\sigma$ as its unique minimizer.

The construction below disproves the global converse while satisfying the
stronger uniqueness hypothesis in Theorem~5.3(ii).

\section{Proof intuition}

The averaged kernel $U_K^\sigma$ records the quadratic variation of the
three-input energy at $\sigma$.  It cannot see a symmetric cubic interaction
whose integral in one variable vanishes.  Such an interaction can be locally
flat at $\sigma$ yet have a negative value at a concentrated measure.

We first build an explicit rotationally invariant cubic $H_d$ with
$U_{H_d}^\sigma=0$ and negative Dirac energy.  To rule out degeneracy as the
explanation, we add a small symmetric lift of a centered exponential
dot-product kernel.  Its averaged kernel is strictly conditionally positive
definite and uniquely minimized by $\sigma$.  Taking the coefficient small
preserves the negative Dirac gap.  The result is a strict directional local
minimum at $\sigma$ which is not a global minimum.

\section{Counterexample}

Fix $d\ge3$.  For $x,y,z\in\Sph$, set
\[
 u=\langle x,y\rangle,\qquad
 v=\langle y,z\rangle,\qquad
 t=\langle z,x\rangle.
\]
Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 H_d(x,y,z)=-uvt+\frac1d(u^2+v^2+t^2)-\frac{2}{d^2}.
\end{equation}
Define the dimension-dependent constant
\[
 c_d=\int_{\Sph}e^{\langle p,z\rangle}\,d\sigma(z),
\]
which is independent of $p\in\Sph$, and the centered pair kernel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G}
 G_d(x,y)=e^{\langle x,y\rangle}-c_d.
\end{equation}
Since $\langle p,z\rangle<1$ for $\sigma$-almost every $z$, one has $c_d<e$.
Put
\[
 L_d(x,y,z)=G_d(x,y)+G_d(y,z)+G_d(z,x),
\]
and choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta}
 \eta_d=\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{6d^2(e-c_d)}>0,
 \qquad K_d=H_d+\eta_dL_d.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The kernel $K_d:(\Sph)^3\to\R$ is continuous, symmetric, and rotationally
invariant.  Its averaged pair kernel is
\[
 U_{K_d}^\sigma=\eta_dG_d,
\]
which is strictly conditionally positive definite; in particular, $\sigma$
is the unique minimizer of its pair energy.  The measure $\sigma$ is a strict
local minimizer of $I_{K_d}$ in every direction, but it is not a global
minimizer.  More precisely, for every $p\in\Sph$,
\[
 I_{K_d}(\sigma)=0,
 \qquad
 I_{K_d}(\delta_p)=-\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2d^2}<0.
\]
Consequently, the source conjecture and the converse question following
Theorem~5.3 have negative answers.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Symmetry, continuity, and rotational invariance are immediate because a
permutation of $x,y,z$ merely permutes $u,v,t$, while simultaneous rotations
preserve them.

Normalized surface measure satisfies
\[
 \int_{\Sph}zz^{\mathsf T}\,d\sigma(z)=\frac1dI_d.
\]
For fixed $x,y$, this gives
\[
 \int vt\,d\sigma(z)=\frac ud,
 \qquad
 \int v^2\,d\sigma(z)=\int t^2\,d\sigma(z)=\frac1d.
\]
Substitution in \eqref{eq:H} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hzero}
 U_{H_d}^\sigma(x,y)
 =-\frac{u^2}{d}+\frac1d\left(u^2+\frac2d\right)
  -\frac2{d^2}=0.
\end{equation}
By the definition of $c_d$, the $\sigma$-potential of $G_d$ is zero.  Hence
\[
 U_{L_d}^\sigma(x,y)=G_d(x,y),
 \qquad U_{K_d}^\sigma=\eta_dG_d.
\]

We next prove strict conditional positive definiteness.  If $\nu$ is a finite
signed measure on $\Sph$ with $\nu(\Sph)=0$, uniform convergence of the
exponential series gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:tensors}
 \iint G_d(x,y)\,d\nu(x)d\nu(y)
 &=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}
   \left\|\int_{\Sph}x^{\otimes k}\,d\nu(x)\right\|^2\ge0.
\end{align}
If equality holds, every tensor moment of $\nu$ vanishes.  Thus $\nu$
annihilates every polynomial restricted to $\Sph$.  Those polynomials are
dense in $C(\Sph)$, so $\nu=0$.  This proves strictness.

For any $\mu\in\Pmeas(\Sph)$, put $\nu=\mu-\sigma$.  Since the
$\sigma$-potential and $\sigma$-energy of $G_d$ vanish,
\[
 I_{G_d}(\mu)=I_{G_d}(\nu)\ge0,
\]
with equality only for $\mu=\sigma$.  Therefore $\sigma$ is the unique
minimizer of the pair energy of $U_{K_d}^\sigma=\eta_dG_d$.

Equations \eqref{eq:Hzero} and the zero potential of $G_d$ imply
$I_{K_d}(\sigma)=0$.  At a Dirac mass all three inner products are one, so
\begin{align*}
 I_{H_d}(\delta_p)
 &=-1+\frac3d-\frac2{d^2}
   =-\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{d^2},\\
 I_{L_d}(\delta_p)&=3(e-c_d).
\end{align*}
The choice \eqref{eq:eta} consequently gives
\[
 I_{K_d}(\delta_p)
 =-\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{d^2}
   +\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2d^2}
 =-\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2d^2}<0.
\]
Thus $\sigma$ is not a global minimizer.

For completeness, fix $\mu\ne\sigma$ and
$\mu_s=(1-s)\sigma+s\mu$.  Trilinearity, the vanishing one-point variation
at $\sigma$, and $U_{K_d}^\sigma=\eta_dG_d$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local}
 I_{K_d}(\mu_s)
 =3\eta_d s^2(1-s)I_{G_d}(\mu)+s^3 I_{K_d}(\mu).
\end{equation}
The quadratic coefficient is strictly positive, so the right side is positive
for all sufficiently small $s>0$.  Hence $\sigma$ is a strict local minimizer
in every direction, exactly in the source's sense.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences and limitations}

The example isolates the missing implication: even a strictly positive second
variation at $\sigma$ cannot control the cubic energy globally.  It also shows
that the word ``unique'' in Theorem~5.3(ii) is not enough to upgrade that
theorem's local conclusion to a global one.

The source's necessary direction remains untouched: if $\sigma$ globally
minimizes the full energy, its averaged pair kernel is conditionally positive
definite.  Likewise, this example does not contradict the source's sufficient
results assuming conditional three-positive-definiteness of the full kernel.

\section{Verification and novelty status}

The construction was independently checked through the spherical covariance
identity, the tensor-series certificate \eqref{eq:tensors}, the exact Dirac
energy, and the directional expansion \eqref{eq:local}.  No numerical or
external theorem is needed beyond density of polynomial restrictions on the
sphere.

On August 11, 2026, the local run indexes were searched by arXiv id, exact
title, the averaged-potential conjecture, and its local/global terminology.
Exact-phrase and citation-oriented web searches found the source, its 2022
journal version, and related talks, but no later answer or matching
counterexample.  The search was bounded, so novelty confidence is moderate
pending a specialist citation review.
