\section{Source problem}

Cardona, Chatzakou, Ruzhansky, and Toft ask whether a non-elliptic operator
\[
 A\in\Psi^m_{\rho,\delta}(G),\qquad
 0\le\delta<\rho\le1,
\]
that belongs to \(\Schatten_r(L^2(G))\) must have strict order
\(m<-n/r\), where \(n=\dim G\) \cite{CCRT}. Their own exotic example at
\((\rho,\delta)=(0,0)\) is outside the strict parameter range of the problem.

\begin{figure}[H]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=0.97\textwidth]{figures/open_problems_crop.png}
 \caption{Open Problems 1.4 and 1.5 on PDF page 6 of the source.}
\end{figure}

We use the standard toroidal quantization
\[
 \Op(a)f(x)=\sum_{k\in\Z^n}e^{2\pi i k\cdot x}a(x,k)\widehat f(k).
\]
The toroidal class \(S^m_{\rho,\delta}\) is characterized by
\[
 |\partial_x^\beta\Delta_k^\alpha a(x,k)|
 \le C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle k\rangle^{m-\rho|\alpha|+\delta|\beta|};
\]
this agrees with the usual periodic H\"ormander calculus
\cite{RuzhanskyTurunen}.

\section{Proof intuition}

A symbol of order \(m\) need only reach size \(R^m\) somewhere at frequency
scale \(R\). When \(\rho<1\), the finite-difference estimates allow such a
peak to occupy a ball of radius only \(R^\rho\). Placing these balls at widely
separated scales gives exact order while the number of active Fourier modes
is only \(R^{\rho n}\), not \(R^n\). At the critical order \(m=-n/r\), each
packet then has Schatten mass \(R^{-n(1-\rho)}\), which is summable.

At \(\rho=1\), a frequency packet has the full \(R^n\) modes. If \(\delta>0\),
however, the symbol may be spatially concentrated on a set of volume
\(R^{-\delta n}\). The exact Hilbert--Schmidt formula sees this phase-space
volume and again makes the critical-order series summable.

\section{The counterexamples}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let \(n\ge1\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(r>0\), \(0<\rho<1\), and \(0\le\delta<\rho\), there is a
non-elliptic operator
\[
 A\in\Psi^{-n/r}_{\rho,\delta}(\T^n)
 \setminus\bigcup_{\varepsilon>0}
 \Psi^{-n/r-\varepsilon}_{\rho,\delta}(\T^n)
\]
such that \(A\in\Schatten_r(L^2(\T^n))\).
\item If \(0<\delta<1\), there is a non-elliptic operator
\[
 A\in\Psi^{-n/2}_{1,\delta}(\T^n)
 \setminus\bigcup_{\varepsilon>0}
 \Psi^{-n/2-\varepsilon}_{1,\delta}(\T^n)
\]
such that \(A\in\Schatten_2(L^2(\T^n))\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently Open Problem 1.4 is false for every allowed
\((\rho,\delta)\ne(1,0)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a nonnegative \(\chi\in C_c^\infty(\R^n)\), supported in a sufficiently
small ball about zero, with \(\chi(0)=1\). Choose a large integer \(Q\) and
put \(R_j=Q^j\), starting at a sufficiently large index. All frequency
packets below are then disjoint.

For the first assertion, put \(m=-n/r\) and define a smooth function of the
continuous frequency variable by
\[
 \widetilde a(\xi)=
 \sum_{j} R_j^m
 \chi\!\left(\frac{\xi-R_je_1}{R_j^\rho}\right),
 \qquad \xi\in\R^n,
\]
and let \(a(k)=\widetilde a(k)\) for \(k\in\Z^n\). On the \(j\)-th packet,
\(\langle\xi\rangle\asymp R_j\) and
\[
 |\partial_\xi^\alpha\widetilde a(\xi)|
 \le C_\alpha R_j^{m-\rho|\alpha|}
 \lesssim \langle\xi\rangle^{m-\rho|\alpha|}.
\]
Coordinate finite differences of \(a\) are iterated integrals of the
corresponding derivatives of \(\widetilde a\). The same estimates therefore
hold for \(\Delta_k^\alpha a(k)\), including at packet boundaries. Since the
symbol is independent of \(x\), all positive-order spatial derivatives
vanish. Thus \(a\in S^m_{\rho,\delta}(\T^n\times\Z^n)\).

At the integer center \(k_j=R_je_1\),
\[
 a(k_j)=R_j^m.
\]
If \(a\) had order \(m-\varepsilon\), the zeroth symbol estimate at these
centers would give \(R_j^m\le C R_j^{m-\varepsilon}\), or
\(R_j^\varepsilon\le C\), a contradiction. Hence the displayed order is
exact. The symbol also vanishes at arbitrarily large frequencies outside the
packet union, so the operator is not elliptic.

The operator is a Fourier multiplier and is diagonal in the standard Fourier
basis. Its singular values are the numbers \(|a(k)|\). A packet contains at
most \(C R_j^{\rho n}\) lattice points, so
\[
 \|A\|_{\Schatten_r}^r
 =\sum_{k\in\Z^n}|a(k)|^r
 \le C\sum_j R_j^{mr+\rho n}
 =C\sum_jR_j^{-n(1-\rho)}<\infty.
\]
This proves the first assertion.

For the second assertion, choose a coordinate chart \(\kappa\) about a point
\(x_0\in\T^n\), with \(\kappa(x_0)=0\), and a function
\(\phi\in C_c^\infty(\R^n)\) supported strictly inside the chart, with
\(\phi(0)=1\). Define \(\phi_j\) on the torus by
\[
 \phi_j(x)=\phi(R_j^\delta\kappa(x))
\]
inside the chart and zero outside it. After increasing the starting index,
this is smooth and
\[
 |\partial_x^\beta\phi_j(x)|\le C_\beta R_j^{\delta|\beta|},
 \qquad
 \int_{\T^n}|\phi_j(x)|^2\,dx\le C R_j^{-\delta n}.
\]
With \(m=-n/2\), set
\[
 a(x,k)=\sum_j R_j^m\phi_j(x)
 \chi\!\left(\frac{k-R_je_1}{R_j}\right).
\]
The same derivative-to-difference argument gives
\[
 |\partial_x^\beta\Delta_k^\alpha a(x,k)|
 \le C_{\alpha,\beta}
 \langle k\rangle^{m-|\alpha|+\delta|\beta|}.
\]
Thus \(a\in S^m_{1,\delta}\). The identities
\(a(x_0,R_je_1)=R_j^m\) prove exact order, and the large zero set proves
non-ellipticity.

Finally, if \(e_k(x)=e^{2\pi i k\cdot x}\), then
\(\Op(a)e_k=e_k a(\cdot,k)\). Therefore
\[
 \|\Op(a)\|_{\Schatten_2}^2
 =\sum_{k\in\Z^n}\|\Op(a)e_k\|_2^2
 =\int_{\T^n}\sum_{k\in\Z^n}|a(x,k)|^2\,dx.
\]
Disjointness of the frequency packets, their \(O(R_j^n)\) lattice points,
and the spatial-volume estimate give
\[
 \|\Op(a)\|_{\Schatten_2}^2
 \le C\sum_j R_j^{2m}R_j^nR_j^{-\delta n}
 =C\sum_jR_j^{-\delta n}<\infty.
\]
This proves the second assertion and the stated consequence.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, limitations, and novelty}

The proof is exact and uses no numerical computation. The two decisive norm
identities are elementary: diagonal multipliers have singular values
\(|a(k)|\), and the Hilbert--Schmidt norm is the squared \(L^2\) norm of the
symbol over \(\T^n\times\Z^n\). The symbol-order checks use all finite
differences and all spatial derivatives, not merely zeroth-order size.

The construction deliberately does not cover \((\rho,\delta)=(1,0)\). At that
endpoint, a critical frequency packet occupies \(R^n\) modes and no shrinking
spatial volume is permitted. This is consistent with, but does not prove, the
strict estimate in the source's separate classical Open Problem 1.5.

A bounded primary-source search through 12 August 2026 used the source title
and arXiv id, the exact strict-order phrase, non-elliptic Schatten
pseudodifferential operators, and sparse toroidal H\"ormander symbols. It found
the source, the foundational toroidal quantization paper, and a later analogous
Fourier-integral-operator problem, but no resolution or these constructions.
Novelty confidence is moderate; specialist review is recommended before any
priority claim.
