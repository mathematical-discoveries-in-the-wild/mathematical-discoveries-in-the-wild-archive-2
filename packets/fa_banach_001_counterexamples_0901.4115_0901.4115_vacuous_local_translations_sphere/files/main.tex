\section*{Target and answer}

Dutkay--Han--Jorgensen ask whether a probability measure $\mu$ on
$\mathbb R^n$ must be spectral if $L^2(\mu)$ carries a strongly continuous
unitary group $U(t)$ such that
\[
 U(t)\chi_{O+t}=\chi_O
\]
whenever $O,O+t\subset\operatorname{supp}\mu$ \cite{DHJ}.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=.9\textwidth]{figures/local_translation_question_crop.png}
\end{center}

The answer is no.  In fact, the hypothesis can be vacuous away from $t=0$.

\begin{theorem}
For every $d\ge2$, let $\mu$ be normalized surface measure on the unit sphere
$\sphere^{d-1}\subset\mathbb R^d$.  The trivial representation
$U(t)=I_{L^2(\mu)}$ satisfies the displayed local-translation condition, but
$\mu$ is not a spectral measure.
\end{theorem}

\section*{Proof}

The maps $U(t)=I$ form a strongly continuous unitary representation of
$(\mathbb R^d,+)$.  Fix a measurable $O$ and $t\in\mathbb R^d$ for which
\[
 O\subset\sphere^{d-1},\qquad O+t\subset\sphere^{d-1}.
\]
If $t=0$, then $U(0)\chi_O=\chi_O$.

Suppose $t\ne0$.  Every $x\in O$ obeys $|x|=|x+t|=1$, and subtraction of the
squared equations gives
\[
 2x\mathbin{\cdot}t+|t|^2=0.                                    \tag{1}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 O\subset \sphere^{d-1}\cap
 \{x:x\mathbin{\cdot}t=-|t|^2/2\}.
\]
This intersection is empty, a point, or a $(d-2)$-sphere.  In every case it
has $(d-1)$-dimensional surface measure zero.  Thus $\mu(O)=0$.  Applying the
same argument to $O+t$ and the translation $-t$ gives $\mu(O+t)=0$.  Hence
$\chi_O=\chi_{O+t}=0$ as elements of $L^2(\mu)$, and
\[
 U(t)\chi_{O+t}=0=\chi_O.
\]
The required condition holds for every $O$ and $t$.

It remains to rule out spectrality.  Iosevich--Lai--Liu--Wyman prove that
surface measure on the smooth boundary of a convex body with everywhere
positive Gaussian curvature admits no Fourier frame \cite[Theorem~1.1]{ILLW}.
The unit sphere is such a boundary.  Multiplying a measure by a positive
constant does not affect whether an exponential family is a frame.  If
$\mu$ had an orthonormal basis of exponentials, that basis would in particular
be a Fourier frame (with frame bounds $1,1$), contradicting their theorem.
Therefore $\mu$ is not spectral. \qed

\section*{A general obstruction}

The same argument isolates the defect in the proposed condition.  If a
probability measure $\nu$ with support $K$ satisfies
\[
 \nu\bigl(K\cap(K+t)\bigr)=0\qquad(t\ne0),                       \tag{2}
\]
then the trivial unitary group satisfies the question's hypothesis.  Thus
that hypothesis cannot characterize spectrality on any class containing a
non-spectral measure with property (2).  Curved hypersurface measure gives a
particularly concrete family: intersections with nontrivial translates have
codimension at least one.

This counterexample even survives the stronger, pointwise-looking
formulation $U(t)f(x)=f(x+t)$ for $\mu$-almost every $x$ for which both points
lie in the support: for $t\ne0$, that region is still $\mu$-null.

\section*{Proof intuition}

The local-translation identity only tests portions of the support that remain
inside the support after translation.  A curved surface has essentially no
such portions: it meets a distinct translate only in lower dimension.  The
identity therefore contains no information, and even the identity
representation passes it.  Positive curvature simultaneously supplies a
known obstruction to any Fourier frame, turning this vacuity into a full
counterexample.
