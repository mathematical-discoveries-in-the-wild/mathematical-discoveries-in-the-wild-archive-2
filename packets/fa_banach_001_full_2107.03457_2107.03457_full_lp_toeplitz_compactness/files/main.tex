For every $1<p<\infty$ and every B\'ekoll\`e--Bonami weight
$\sigma\in B_p$, the criterion below characterizes all bounded symbols
$u$ for which $T_u=P M_u$ is compact on the \emph{full} space
$L^p_\sigma$.
}}
\end{center}

\section{Source question}

Stockdale and Wagner ask the following on PDF page~8 of
\cite{SW}:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Open Question 1.13.}
Given $p\in(1,\infty)$ and $\sigma\in B_p$, characterize the symbols
$u\in L^\infty$ for which $T_u$ is compact on $L^p_\sigma$.
\end{quote}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.97\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\caption{The source question and its surrounding list on PDF page~8 of
arXiv:2107.03457.}
\end{figure}

Let $V$ be normalized Lebesgue measure on the unit ball $\B\subset
\mathbb C^n$.  The unweighted Bergman projection is denoted by $P$, and
$T_u=P M_u$.  For a weight $\rho$, write
\[
 \|f\|_{L^s_\rho}^s=\int_{\B}|f|^s\rho\dd,
 \qquad
 \A^s_\rho=L^s_\rho\cap\operatorname{Hol}(\B).
\]
For a fixed $r>0$, let $D(a,r)$ denote the Bergman-metric ball of radius
$r$ centered at $a$.

\section{Answer}

\begin{theorem}[Full symbol characterization]\label{thm:main}
Let $1<p<\infty$, let $q=p/(p-1)$, and let $\sigma\in B_p$.  Put
\[
 \tau=\sigma^{1-q}=\sigma^{-1/(p-1)}\in B_q.
\]
For $u\in L^\infty(\B)$ and any fixed $r>0$, the following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $T_u=P M_u$ is compact on $L^p_\sigma$.
\item Multiplication by $\overline u$ is compact from
$\A^q_\tau$ to $L^q_\tau$.
\item The dual-weight local means vanish at the boundary:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:criterion}
 \lim_{|a|\to1^-}
 \frac{1}{\tau(D(a,r))}
 \int_{D(a,r)} |u(z)|^q\tau(z)\dd(z)=0.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The truth of \eqref{eq:criterion} is independent of the chosen fixed
radius $r>0$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{Idea of the proof}

The full $L^p_\sigma$ domain lets us eliminate all cancellation in the
symbol.  Pair $L^p_\sigma$ with its natural dual $L^q_\tau$ using
unweighted integration.  The Hermitian Bergman kernel then gives the exact
adjoint formula
\[
                    T_u^*=M_{\overline u}P.
\]
Because $P$ is a bounded projection onto $\A^q_\tau$, compactness of this
adjoint is exactly compactness of multiplication by $\overline u$ on the
dual analytic space.  That multiplication map is the canonical embedding
of $\A^q_\tau$ into the measure
$|u|^q\tau\,dV$.  A weighted compact Carleson lemma turns compactness of
this embedding into the local-density condition \eqref{eq:criterion}.

The appearance of the \emph{dual} weight $\tau$, rather than $\sigma$, is
forced by the Banach adjoint.  It is also what makes phase cancellation
irrelevant: only $|u|^q$ remains.

\section{A compact Carleson lemma for B\'ekoll\`e weights}

We give the embedding argument to keep the result independent of any
unstated regularity of the weight.  We use only standard geometry of fixed
Bergman balls and the $B_s$ condition.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted compact embedding]\label{lem:carleson}
Let $1<s<\infty$, let $\rho\in B_s$, and let $\mu$ be a finite positive
Borel measure on $\B$ satisfying $\mu\le C_0\rho\,dV$.  For any fixed
$r>0$, the inclusion
\[
              J_\mu:\A^s_\rho\longrightarrow L^s(\mu)
\]
is compact if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
       \frac{\mu(D(a,r))}{\rho(D(a,r))}\longrightarrow0
       \qquad (|a|\to1^-).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We first record three consequences of $\rho\in B_s$.  The tent definition
of $B_s$, together with the fact that a fixed-radius Bergman ball is
contained in a Carleson tent of comparable volume, gives the ball estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ballBs}
 \left\langle\rho\right\rangle_D
 \left\langle\rho^{-1/(s-1)}\right\rangle_D^{s-1}
 \le C_{s,r,\rho}
\end{equation}
for every ball $D=D(a,r)$ and for every fixed enlargement of such a ball.
H\"older's inequality and \eqref{eq:ballBs} imply the subset estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subset}
 \frac{\rho(E)}{\rho(D)}
 \ge c_{s,r,\rho}\left(\frac{V(E)}{V(D)}\right)^s,
 \qquad E\subseteq D.
\end{equation}
In particular, $\rho$ is doubling along fixed-factor enlargements of
Bergman balls and comparable Carleson tents.

The holomorphic submean inequality followed by H\"older and
\eqref{eq:ballBs} yields, for fixed $0<r<R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weightedsubmean}
 \sup_{z\in D(a,r)}|f(z)|^s
 \le \frac{C}{\rho(D(a,R))}
        \int_{D(a,R)}|f|^s\rho\dd
 \qquad(f\in\A^s_\rho).
\end{equation}

Assume \eqref{eq:density}.  Choose an $r$-lattice
$\{a_j\}$ for the Bergman metric.  The balls $D(a_j,r)$ cover $\B$, while
fixed enlargements $D(a_j,R)$ have bounded overlap.  Applying
\eqref{eq:weightedsubmean} on the balls meeting $\{|z|>t\}$ gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:tail}
 \int_{|z|>t}|f|^s\,d\mu
 &\le C
 \sup_{|a_j|>t'}
 \frac{\mu(D(a_j,r))}{\rho(D(a_j,r))}
 \sum_j\int_{D(a_j,R)}|f|^s\rho\dd \\
 &\le C
 \sup_{|a|>t'}
 \frac{\mu(D(a,r))}{\rho(D(a,r))}
 \|f\|_{\A^s_\rho}^s,
\end{align}
where $t'\to1$ as $t\to1$.  Thus the norm of the outer restriction tends
to zero.  On a fixed compact subball, bounded subsets of $\A^s_\rho$ are
normal by \eqref{eq:weightedsubmean}; Montel's theorem and
$\mu\le C_0\rho\,dV$ show that restriction there is compact.  Hence
$J_\mu$ is a norm limit of compact restrictions and is compact.

Conversely, suppose $J_\mu$ is compact.  Fix an integer $N>n+1$ and set
\[
 F_a(z)=\left(\frac{1-|a|^2}{1-\langle z,a\rangle}\right)^N,
 \qquad
 f_a=\frac{F_a}{\rho(D(a,r))^{1/s}}.
\]
On $D(a,r)$, the modulus of $F_a$ is bounded above and below by positive
constants depending only on $N,r$.  We claim
\begin{equation}\label{eq:peaknorm}
             \int_{\B}|F_a|^s\rho\dd
             \le C\rho(D(a,r)).
\end{equation}
Indeed, decompose $\B$ into the nonisotropic annuli on which
$|1-\langle z,a\rangle|$ is comparable to
$2^k(1-|a|^2)$.  The $k$th annulus lies in a Carleson tent whose volume is
at most $C2^{k(n+1)}V(D(a,r))$.  Applying \eqref{eq:subset} inside the
larger tent shows that its $\rho$-mass is at most
$C2^{ks(n+1)}\rho(D(a,r))$.  On that annulus,
$|F_a|^s\le C2^{-kNs}$.  Summation gives
\[
 \int_{\B}|F_a|^s\rho\dd
 \le C\rho(D(a,r))
       \sum_{k\ge0}2^{-ks(N-n-1)},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:peaknorm}.

Applying the subset argument in the whole unit ball also gives
\[
 \rho(D(a,r))^{1/s}\ge c\,V(D(a,r))
 \asymp (1-|a|^2)^{n+1}.
\]
Since $N>n+1$, it follows that $f_a\to0$ uniformly on compact subsets as
$|a|\to1$.  The family $(f_a)$ is bounded in the reflexive space
$\A^s_\rho$ by \eqref{eq:peaknorm}; bounded point evaluations and the
compact convergence show that $f_a\to0$ weakly.  Compactness of $J_\mu$
therefore gives $\|f_a\|_{L^s(\mu)}\to0$.  The lower bound for $|F_a|$ on
$D(a,r)$ yields
\[
 \frac{\mu(D(a,r))}{\rho(D(a,r))}
 \le C\|f_a\|_{L^s(\mu)}^s\longrightarrow0.
\]
This proves necessity.  Since compactness of $J_\mu$ does not refer to a
radius, the same equivalence also proves radius independence.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the characterization}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
The natural Banach dual of $L^p_\sigma$, under the unweighted pairing
\[
                  \langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\B}f\overline g\dd,
\]
is $L^q_\tau$, where $\tau=\sigma^{1-q}$.  The elementary duality of
B\'ekoll\`e classes gives $\tau\in B_q$.  Hence $P$ is bounded both on
$L^p_\sigma$ and on $L^q_\tau$, and its range in the latter space is
$\A^q_\tau$.

First take bounded functions of compact support, so that Fubini's theorem
applies directly.  Hermitian symmetry of the Bergman kernel gives
\[
 \langle T_uf,g\rangle
 =\langle P(uf),g\rangle
 =\langle uf,Pg\rangle
 =\langle f,\overline u\,Pg\rangle.
\]
Density and boundedness extend this identity to all
$f\in L^p_\sigma$ and $g\in L^q_\tau$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjoint}
                   T_u^*=M_{\overline u}P
                   \quad\hbox{on }L^q_\tau.
\end{equation}

If $M_{\overline u}:\A^q_\tau\to L^q_\tau$ is compact, then
\eqref{eq:adjoint} factors $T_u^*$ as that compact map after the bounded
projection $P$, so $T_u^*$ is compact.  Conversely, if $T_u^*$ is compact,
its restriction to $\A^q_\tau$ is compact, and $Pg=g$ on this subspace;
the restriction is exactly $M_{\overline u}$.  Schauder's theorem now gives
\[
 T_u\text{ compact}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 M_{\overline u}:\A^q_\tau\to L^q_\tau\text{ compact}.
\]

Finally,
\[
 \|\overline u f\|_{L^q_\tau}^q
 =\int_{\B}|f|^q\,d\mu_u,
 \qquad d\mu_u=|u|^q\tau\,dV.
\]
Since $u\in L^\infty$, $\mu_u\le\|u\|_\infty^q\tau\,dV$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:carleson}, with $s=q$, $\rho=\tau$, and
$\mu=\mu_u$, says that this multiplication map is compact exactly when
\eqref{eq:criterion} holds.  This proves all equivalences.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences and stress tests}

\begin{corollary}[Unweighted form]
For $\sigma\equiv1$ and $q=p'$, $T_u$ is compact on $L^p(\B)$ if and only if
\[
 \lim_{|a|\to1^-}\frac{1}{V(D(a,r))}
 \int_{D(a,r)}|u|^q\dd=0.
\]
For $p=2$, this is equivalently the vanishing of the Berezin transform of
the positive symbol $|u|^2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Why pointwise decay is not necessary]
If $u=\mathbf1_E$, then $T_u$ is compact precisely when the dual weight of
$E$ has vanishing relative density:
\[
 \frac{\tau(E\cap D(a,r))}{\tau(D(a,r))}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus $E$ may meet every boundary collar, so $u$ need not tend pointwise to
zero.  This explains why the source's boundary-decay condition is
sufficient but does not capture all compact symbols.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Cancellation audit]
The criterion contains $|u|^q$, not the Berezin transform of $u$.  This is
forced by the full $L^p_\sigma$ domain: inputs can absorb the phase of $u$.
There is no contradiction with the subtler analytic-domain problem, where
the input is required to be holomorphic and phase cancellation cannot be
removed in this way.
\end{remark}

\section{Verification and novelty bounds}

The proof was stress-tested in six successive upgrades: the Hilbert-space
$p=2$ factorization, unweighted Banach duality for all $p$, weighted duality
for every $B_p$ weight, the explicit compact-Carleson lemma, sparse-support
tests, and an adjacent-literature audit.  The key exponent in
\eqref{eq:peaknorm} is strict because $N>n+1$.

The bounded novelty search covered the run indexes and parsed arXiv corpus,
the exact arXiv id and quoted question, the paper's DOI citation graph in
OpenAlex, Crossref searches for compact weighted Bergman embeddings, and
formula/keyword searches for compact $P M_u$ operators.  Chen
\cite{Chen} explicitly answers the \emph{different} analytic-space Open
Question~1.14 for all $B_p$ weights.  Tong and Li \cite{TL} give general
Carleson-measure theory for B\'ekoll\`e-weighted Bergman spaces, consistent
with Lemma~\ref{lem:carleson}.  No source found in this bounded search states
the full-$L^p_\sigma$ dual-weight characterization above.  Novelty confidence
is therefore moderate, while the validity confidence is high.  Human review
should focus on the ball/tent passage in \eqref{eq:ballBs} and the annular
peak estimate \eqref{eq:peaknorm}.
