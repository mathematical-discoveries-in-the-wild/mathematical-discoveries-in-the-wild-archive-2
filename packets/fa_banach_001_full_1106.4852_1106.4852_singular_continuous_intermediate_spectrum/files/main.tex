\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Candidate full answer to the explicit alternative in item~(c), likely valid,
pending human review.  Under the one-dimensional spectral conclusions used by
the source, the singular-continuous spectrum of its two-dimensional separable
operator is not merely nonempty in the intermediate band: it contains that
entire band.  The other open problems in the source are not addressed.

\section{Source question}

Marchetti and Wreszinski consider two independent copies $J_1,J_2$ of a
random sparse Jacobi operator and the Kronecker sum
\[
 K_\theta=J_1\otimes I+\theta I\otimes J_2,
 \qquad 0<\theta\leq1.
\]
Write $L=\lambda^+=-\lambda^-$, where $0<L<2$.  Item~(c) of their
Theorem~2.6 leaves open whether
\[
 \sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}(K_\theta)
 \cap\bigl(-L(1+\theta),L(1+\theta)\bigr)
\]
is empty~\cite{MarchettiWreszinski}.

The conclusion explicitly asks for clarification of this alternative.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The one-dimensional theorem on which the source relies supplies, for the
generic parameters and configurations in question,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oned}
 [-L,L]\subseteq\sigma(J_i|\Hsc^{(i)}),\qquad
 [-2,-L]\cup[L,2]\subseteq\sigma(J_i|\Hpp^{(i)}),
 \quad i=1,2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the exceptional set in the singular-continuous region has Lebesgue
measure zero and hence dense complement, while the pure-point spectrum is
dense in the two outer intervals.  Taking closures gives~\eqref{eq:oned}.

\section{Proof intuition}

The mixed tensor sectors were not exploited in item~(c).  If $u$ is an
eigenvector of $J_1$ with eigenvalue $e$, then
$\mathbb C u\otimes\Hsc^{(2)}$ is a reducing subspace for $K_\theta$, and
the restriction is unitarily equivalent to $e+\theta J_2|\Hsc^{(2)}$.
It is therefore singular continuous.  Summing over the countably many
eigenvectors preserves singular continuity: every scalar spectral measure is
a countable sum of translated singular continuous measures.

Consequently the singular-continuous spectrum contains both mixed Minkowski
sums
\[
 \sigma(J_1|\Hpp^{(1)})+\theta\sigma(J_2|\Hsc^{(2)})
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \sigma(J_1|\Hsc^{(1)})+\theta\sigma(J_2|\Hpp^{(2)}).
\]
For the inner interval $[-L,L]$ and outer intervals
$[-2,-L]\cup[L,2]$, these two sums overlap and fill one large interval.

\section{The mixed-sector lemma}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:mixed}
Let $A$ and $B$ be bounded self-adjoint operators on separable Hilbert
spaces, and let $\theta>0$.  For
\[
 T=A\otimes I+\theta I\otimes B,
\]
the reducing subspaces
\[
 \Hpp(A)\otimes\Hsc(B),\qquad
 \Hsc(A)\otimes\Hpp(B)
\]
are purely singular continuous.  In particular,
\begin{align*}
 \sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}(T)\supseteq{}&
 \bigl(\sigma(A|\Hpp(A))+\theta\sigma(B|\Hsc(B))\bigr)\\
 &{}\cup
 \bigl(\sigma(A|\Hsc(A))+\theta\sigma(B|\Hpp(B))\bigr).
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose an orthonormal eigenbasis $(u_n)$ for $\Hpp(A)$, repeating
eigenvalues according to multiplicity, and write $Au_n=a_nu_n$.  Under the
canonical identification
\[
 \Hpp(A)\otimes\Hsc(B)
 \simeq \bigoplus_n\Hsc(B),
\]
the restriction of $T$ is
\[
 \bigoplus_n\bigl(a_n+\theta B|\Hsc(B)\bigr).
\]
For a vector $(x_n)$, its scalar spectral measure is the countable sum of
the translated and rescaled singular-continuous measures of the $x_n$.
This sum has no atoms.  It is singular because each summand is carried by a
Lebesgue-null set and a countable union of null sets is null.  Hence the
restriction is purely singular continuous.

Its spectrum is the closure of
$\bigcup_n(a_n+\theta\sigma(B|\Hsc(B)))$.  Since the eigenvalues $a_n$ are
dense in the spectrum of the pure-point restriction and all spectra here are
compact, this closure equals
\[
 \sigma(A|\Hpp(A))+\theta\sigma(B|\Hsc(B)).
\]
The second mixed sector follows by interchanging $A$ and $B$.
\end{proof}

\section{Resolution of item (c)}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Under the hypotheses and almost-everywhere one-dimensional spectral
conclusions used in Theorem~2.6 of the source, for every $0<\theta\leq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:large}
 [-2-\theta L,\,2+\theta L]
 \subseteq \sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}(K_\theta).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:answer}
 \bigl(-L(1+\theta),L(1+\theta)\bigr)
 \subseteq \sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}(K_\theta),
\end{equation}
so the set in item~(c) is never empty in this regime.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 I=[-L,L],\qquad P=[-2,-L]\cup[L,2].
\]
Equations~\eqref{eq:oned} and Lemma~\ref{lem:mixed} give
\[
 \sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}(K_\theta)
 \supseteq (P+\theta I)\cup(I+\theta P).
\]
The four component intervals are
\begin{align*}
 P+\theta I
   &=[-2-\theta L,-L(1-\theta)]
     \cup[L(1-\theta),2+\theta L],\\
 I+\theta P
   &=[-L-2\theta,L(1-\theta)]
     \cup[-L(1-\theta),L+2\theta].
\end{align*}
Because $0<\theta\leq1$ and $0<L<2$, consecutive intervals overlap, the
leftmost endpoint is $-2-\theta L$, and the rightmost endpoint is
$2+\theta L$.  Their union is exactly the interval in~\eqref{eq:large}.

Finally $L\leq2$ implies
$L(1+\theta)\leq2+\theta L$.  The symmetric inequality at the left endpoint
proves~\eqref{eq:answer}.
\end{proof}

\section{Strength, extensions, and novelty}

The first attack used one pure-point eigenvalue close to $L$ and one
singular-continuous spectral point close to $-L$, which already proves
nonemptiness.  The deeper upgrade above uses both mixed reducing sectors and
their full spectra, yielding the interval inclusion~\eqref{eq:large}.  This
also shows that the source's pure-point and singular-continuous spectral sets
can overlap topologically; no contradiction arises because spectral-type
subspaces, rather than their spectra as closed sets, are mutually orthogonal.

The same argument extends to the source's separable $d$-dimensional variants:
any tensor sector with exactly one singular-continuous factor and all
remaining factors pure point is purely singular continuous, and its spectrum
is the corresponding Minkowski sum.  This observation does not prove a
mobility edge or pointwise Fourier--Stieltjes decay, nor does it remove the
one-dimensional exceptional null set; those separate source questions remain
open.

A bounded primary-source novelty search covered the exact title and authors,
the phrases ``singular continuous'' and ``Kronecker sum'', the stated item~(c)
alternative, and the authors' related arXiv papers on sparse Jacobi matrices.
It found the source and its one-dimensional predecessors, but no later primary
paper giving this mixed-sector answer.  Novelty confidence is moderate because
the argument is elementary; correctness confidence is high.  Human review
should verify that the source's one-dimensional type statements indeed give
the closed spectral inclusions~\eqref{eq:oned} in precisely the boundary-phase
regime intended by Theorem~2.6.
