\section*{Source Question and Status}

Ball, Bolotnikov, and ter Horst associate to admissible abstract
interpolation data $(P,T,E,N)$ a unitary colligation with characteristic
function $\Sigma=(\Sigma_{ij})$.  After proving sufficient conditions for
the multiplication operator
\[
 M_{\Sigma_{12}}:\Hol_{\widetilde\Delta_*}(\D)
       \longrightarrow \Hol_{\mathcal Y}(\D)
\]
to be injective, they write: ``The question of finding a condition that is
both necessary and sufficient for injectivity of $M_{\Sigma_{12}}$ remains
open'' \cite[p.~16]{BBH}.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

This packet gives such a condition directly in terms of the original
operators $P$ and $T$.  It applies without a nondegeneracy assumption on
$P$.  Status: \emph{candidate full solution, likely valid}, subject to human
review.

\section*{Setup and the Source Criterion}

Let $\mathcal X$ be the state Hilbert space, let $P\geq0$, and put
\[
 \mathcal X_0=\overline{\Ran P^{1/2}}.
\]
The restriction of $P^{1/2}$ to $\mathcal X_0$ is injective.  The source's
unitary colligation has state and input blocks $A$ and $B_2$; in particular,
$\|A\|\leq1$ and $\|B_2\|\leq1$.

Lemma 4.7 of \cite{BBH} says that $M_{\Sigma_{12}}$ is noninjective if and
only if there are nonzero $g_n\in\mathcal X_0$ such that
\begin{align}
 T^*P^{1/2}g_1&=0,\label{eq:first}\\
 T^*P^{1/2}g_{n+1}&=P^{1/2}g_n\qquad(n\geq1),\label{eq:chain}\\
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\|B_2^*g_{n+1}\|^{1/n}&\leq1.\label{eq:B-growth}
\end{align}
The point of the result below is to remove the colligation-dependent block
$B_2$ and package the entire chain as one holomorphic operator-pencil
equation.

\section*{Proof Intuition}

The chain in \eqref{eq:first}--\eqref{eq:chain} is already the Taylor
coefficient recursion for a generalized eigenvector field
$G(z)=\sum_{n\geq0}g_{n+1}z^n$.  The only apparent mismatch is that the
source controls $B_2^*g_{n+1}$ rather than $g_{n+1}$.  In the converse proof
of Lemma 4.7, however, the same coefficients satisfy
\[
 g_{n+1}=Ag_n+B_2B_2^*g_{n+1}.
\]
Because $A$ is a contraction, unit-disk Taylor growth of $B_2^*g_{n+1}$ is
equivalent to unit-disk Taylor growth of $g_{n+1}$.  This turns the source's
sequence condition into a single intrinsic equation involving only $P,T$.

\section*{Necessary-and-Sufficient Condition}

\begin{theorem}[analytic-eigenfield criterion]\label{thm:main}
For any admissible data set considered in \cite{BBH}, the following are
equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M_{\Sigma_{12}}:\Hol_{\widetilde\Delta_*}(\D)
\to\Hol_{\mathcal Y}(\D)$ is injective.
\item The only norm-holomorphic function $G:\D\to\mathcal X_0$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pencil}
 T^*P^{1/2}G(z)=zP^{1/2}G(z)\qquad(z\in\D)
\end{equation}
is $G\equiv0$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus \eqref{eq:pencil} is a necessary-and-sufficient condition expressed
solely in the original $P,T$ data, including when $P$ is degenerate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first isolate the growth equivalence missing from the source statement.
Suppose $g_n$ satisfies the algebraic relations
\eqref{eq:first}--\eqref{eq:chain}.  In the converse half of the proof of
Lemma 4.7, set
\[
 x_0=g_1,\quad x_n=g_{n+1}-Ag_n,\quad
 y_0=0,\quad y_n=-C_1g_n\quad(n\geq1),
\]
and $h_n=\mathbf i_*[x_n\;y_n]^{\mathsf T}$.  The algebraic computation in
that proof, before any growth assumption is used, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:h-identities}
 h_n=B_2^*g_{n+1},\qquad x_n=B_2h_n.
\end{equation}
The second equality follows as well from the colligation identity
$[x_n\;y_n]^{\mathsf T}=[B_2\;D_{12}]^{\mathsf T}h_n$.  Hence, for $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recursion}
 g_{n+1}=Ag_n+B_2h_n.
\end{equation}

If \eqref{eq:B-growth} holds, then for every $r>1$ there is $C_r$ such that
$\|h_n\|\leq C_rr^n$.  Iterating \eqref{eq:recursion} and using
$\|A\|,\|B_2\|\leq1$ gives
\[
 \|g_{n+1}\|\leq\|g_1\|+C_r\sum_{j=1}^n r^j,
\]
and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g-growth}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\|g_{n+1}\|^{1/n}\leq1.
\end{equation}
Conversely, \eqref{eq:g-growth} implies \eqref{eq:B-growth} immediately
from $h_n=B_2^*g_{n+1}$.  Thus the two growth conditions are equivalent.

Assume first that $M_{\Sigma_{12}}$ is not injective.  Lemma 4.7 supplies
a chain satisfying \eqref{eq:first}--\eqref{eq:B-growth}; by the preceding
paragraph it also satisfies \eqref{eq:g-growth}.  Consequently
\[
 G(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{n+1}z^n
\]
defines a nonzero norm-holomorphic $\mathcal X_0$-valued function on $\D$.
Termwise application of the bounded operators $P^{1/2}$ and $T^*$, followed
by \eqref{eq:first}--\eqref{eq:chain}, gives
\[
 T^*P^{1/2}G(z)
 =\sum_{n\geq1}P^{1/2}g_nz^n
 =zP^{1/2}G(z).
\]

Conversely, suppose a nonzero holomorphic $G$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:pencil}.  Divide by its largest common initial power of $z$ so
that, without loss of generality, $G(0)\neq0$; equation
\eqref{eq:pencil} is preserved.  Write
$G(z)=\sum_{n\geq0}g_{n+1}z^n$.  The Banach-valued Cauchy--Hadamard formula
gives \eqref{eq:g-growth}, while coefficient comparison in
\eqref{eq:pencil} gives \eqref{eq:first}--\eqref{eq:chain}.  The growth
equivalence gives \eqref{eq:B-growth}.  Lemma 4.7 now says that
$M_{\Sigma_{12}}$ is not injective.  This proves the equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
If $P$ is positive definite, define the bounded similar pencil operator
$L_P=P^{-1/2}T^*P^{1/2}$.  The criterion says that $L_P$ has no nonzero
holomorphic eigenvector field $G$ on the whole unit disk with
$L_PG(z)=zG(z)$.  For degenerate $P$, formula \eqref{eq:pencil} avoids the
unbounded inverse of $P^{1/2}$ entirely.
\end{remark}

\section*{Weighted Shifts: Sharpness of the Criterion}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:shift}
Let $\mathcal X=\ell_2(\mathbb N_0)$, $P=I$, and
\[
 Te_n=w_ne_{n+1},\qquad 0<w_n\leq1.
\]
Take $N=0$ and $E=(I-T^*T)^{1/2}$, so the Stein identity holds.  Then
$M_{\Sigma_{12}}$ is noninjective if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}(w_0w_1\cdots w_{n-1})^{1/n}=1.
\end{equation}
In particular, for $w_n=1/(n+1)$ the paper's sufficient condition (4.32)
fails, but $M_{\Sigma_{12}}$ is injective.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Here $T^*e_0=0$ and $T^*e_{n+1}=w_ne_n$.  If a nonzero field satisfies
$T^*G(z)=zG(z)$, division by its initial zero makes $G(0)=ce_0$ with
$c\neq0$.  Comparing Taylor coefficients shows that the coefficient of
$e_n$ in the $n$th Taylor coefficient is forced to equal
\[
 \frac{c}{w_0w_1\cdots w_{n-1}}.
\]
Conversely, choosing precisely these forced vectors gives a formal solution.
It is holomorphic on $\D$ exactly when their root growth is at most one,
which, since every $w_n\leq1$, is equivalent to \eqref{eq:weights}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} proves the first assertion.

For $w_n=1/(n+1)$, one has
$e_0=(T^*)^n(n!e_n)$ for every $n$, so
$e_0\in\Ker T^*\cap\bigcap_{n\geq1}\Ran(T^*)^n$ and (4.32) fails.  On the
other hand, every prospective eigenfield has a coefficient of norm at least
$|c|n!$, whose root growth is unbounded.  No nonzero field is holomorphic on
$\D$, so the multiplier is injective by Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

\section*{Scope, Novelty Check, and Verification}

The result answers the source's injectivity question exactly.  It is a
structural criterion, not a claim of a finite algorithm for arbitrary
infinite-dimensional data.  Its content beyond Lemma 4.7 is the elimination
of $B_2$ and the equivalent holomorphic pencil formulation; the weighted
shift example shows that this retains cases missed by condition (4.32).

The run's cheap indexes contained no answer.  A bounded search through
17 August 2026 used the exact open sentence, the title and authors, and the
phrases \emph{Redheffer injectivity $\Sigma_{12}$}, \emph{analytic core
Redheffer transform}, and \emph{de Branges--Rovnyak analytic core}.  It found
the source, surveys, and applications, but no later answer or this criterion.

The companion script checks the weighted-product formulas, the hyperrange
witnesses, and the factorial growth for finite indices.  These computations
are consistency checks only; the proof above is analytic.

\section*{Human Review Recommendation}

Check that identities \eqref{eq:h-identities} follow from the source's
converse construction before its growth hypothesis is used, and then verify
the contraction estimate leading to \eqref{eq:g-growth}.  Those are the only
new proof steps on which the full criterion depends.
