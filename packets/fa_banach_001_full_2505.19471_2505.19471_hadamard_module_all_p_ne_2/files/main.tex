\section{Source question}

Calin, Cartwright, Coffman, Delf\'in, Girard, Goldrick, Nerella and Wu,
\emph{C*--like modules and matrix \(p\)-operator norms}, arXiv:2505.19471,
Example 5.2 and the paragraph following it (source PDF, p.~18), ask whether
their simultaneously Hadamard-diagonalizable unital algebra gives a
non-C*--like row--column module for
\(p\in(1,\infty)\setminus\{2\}\) \cite{source}.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{The exact open-question paragraph and its immediate conclusion in
  the source paper, p.~18.}
\end{figure}

The answer is yes for every such \(p\).  More precisely, the source column is
a witness below \(2\), and its adjoint row is a witness above \(2\).

\section{Definitions and statement}

For \(1<p<\infty\), let
\[
 H=2^{-1/2}\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 A_p=\{H\diag(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)H:\lambda_1,\lambda_2\in\C\}
 \subseteq \mathcal L(\ell_p^2),
\]
with its inherited \(p\)-operator norm.  Put
\[
 a_1=\frac12\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\1&3\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 a_2=\frac12\begin{bmatrix}3&-1\\-1&3\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 T=\begin{bmatrix}a_1\\a_2\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Thus \(T\in X_p:=M^p_{2,1}(A_p)\), while
\(T^*=[a_1\ a_2]\in Y_p:=M^p_{1,2}(A_p)\).  The pairing is matrix
multiplication, \((B\mid C)_{A_p}=BC\).

\begin{theorem}[Full classification of Example 5.2]\label{thm:main}
For \(1<p<\infty\), the row--column module \((Y_p,X_p)\) over \(A_p\) is
C*--like if and only if \(p=2\).  More explicitly:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if \(1<p<2\), then
\[
 \max_{B\in\Ba(Y_p)}\|BT\|_{A_p}<\|T\|_{X_p};
\]
\item if \(2<p<\infty\), then
\[
 \max_{C\in\Ba(X_p)}\|T^*C\|_{A_p}<\|T^*\|_{Y_p}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the first norm-recovery axiom fails below \(2\), and the second
one fails above \(2\).
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof intuition}

Below \(2\), the source column has only two norming input directions: the
coordinate axes.  If a structured row contraction recovered its norm, dual
smoothness would force the adjoint column contraction to carry a unit vector
to the unique support functional of \(Te_1\).  Simultaneous Hadamard
diagonalization makes that adjoint column unique once the unit vector is
chosen.  Its norm-attainment equations leave only four projective choices:
\(e_1,e_2,(1,i),(1,-i)\).  Strict Clarkson inequalities exclude the coordinate
directions, and a positive second variation excludes the two complex phase
directions.  Compactness then makes the recovery gap strict.

Above \(2\), no new optimization is needed.  Taking Banach adjoints identifies
columns at \(p\) with rows at the conjugate exponent \(p'\), preserves all
operator norms, and reverses products.  Thus the strict failure below \(2\)
becomes strict failure of the other C*--likeness axiom above \(2\).

\section{Scalar lemmas}

For a unit vector \(z\in\ell_r^m\), write
\(\jj_r(z)=(|z_k|^{r-2}z_k)_k\in\ell_{r'}^m\).  We use the Hermitian dual
pairing, linear in the first variable.

\begin{lemma}[Scalar Clarkson inequalities]\label{lem:clarkson}
For \(z,w\in\C\),
\[
 |z+w|^r+|z-w|^r
 \begin{cases}
 \le 2(|z|^r+|w|^r),&1<r<2,\\
 \ge 2(|z|^r+|w|^r),&2<r<\infty.
 \end{cases}
\]
In either non-Hilbertian range the inequality is strict when \(zw\ne0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(a=|z|\), \(b=|w|\), and write
\(|z\pm w|^2=a^2+b^2\pm 2ab\cos\theta\).  If \(1<r<2\), concavity of
\(t^{r/2}\) shows that the sum of the two \(r/2\)-powers is at most
\(2(a^2+b^2)^{r/2}\), which is at most \(2(a^r+b^r)\).  Both inequalities
are strict when \(ab>0\).  For \(r>2\), convexity reverses both comparisons.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The exceptional-phase inequality]\label{lem:scalar}
Let \(s>2\) and \(q=3^{1/(s-1)}\).  Then
\[
 (s-1)(q^{s-2}+q^2)>q^s+1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set \(L=\log 3\), so \(1<q<3\), \(s-1=L/\log q\), and \(q^{s-1}=3\).
After multiplying by \(\log q\), the desired inequality is
\[
 h(q):=L(3/q+q^2)-(3q+1)\log q>0.
\]
We have \(h(3)=0\).  Moreover \(-h'(q)=R(q)\), where
\[
 R(q)=3\log q+3+q^{-1}-L(2q-3q^{-2}).
\]
For \(1\le q\le3\),
\[
 R'(q)=3q^{-1}-q^{-2}-2L-6Lq^{-3}<2-2L<0.
\]
Here \(3q^{-1}-q^{-2}\le2\) and \(L>1\).  Hence
\[
 R(q)\ge R(3)=\frac{10-8\log3}{3}>0;
\]
the last inequality follows, for example, from \(\log3<5/4\).
Thus \(h'<0\) on \((1,3)\), and \(h(q)>h(3)=0\).
\end{proof}

\section{Failure for \(1<p<2\)}

Fix \(1<p<2\) and put \(s=p'=p/(p-1)>2\).  For \(x,y\in\C\), Lemma
\ref{lem:clarkson} gives
\begin{align*}
 \left\|T\binom{x}{y}\right\|_p^p
 &=2^{-p}\bigl(|3x+y|^p+|3x-y|^p
       +|x+3y|^p+|-x+3y|^p\bigr)\\
 &\le 2^{1-p}(3^p+1)(|x|^p+|y|^p).
\end{align*}
Equality holds at the coordinate axes, and is strict if \(xy\ne0\).  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Tnorm}
 \|T\|=N:=2^{1/p-1}(3^p+1)^{1/p},
\end{equation}
and the only projective norming inputs are \(e_1,e_2\).

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
\(\max_{B\in\Ba(Y_p)}\|BT\|=N\).  Finite-dimensional compactness provides
a contraction \(B\in Y_p\) and a unit vector at which equality holds.  The
preceding paragraph makes that vector a coordinate axis.  The two cases are
equivalent.  Indeed, if
\(S=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}\in A_p\) and
\(P=\diag(S,S)\), then \(PTe_1=Te_2\), while \(P\) is an isometry and
\(BP\in Y_p\) whenever \(B\in Y_p\).  We may therefore use \(e_1\).

Let
\[
 \eta=N^{-1}Te_1=\frac1{2N}(3,1,3,-1)^T,
 \qquad q=3^{p-1},
\]
and choose the unit norming functional \(\phi=(u,v)^T\in\ell_s^2\) of
\(B\eta\).  With \(C=B^*:\ell_s^2\to\ell_s^4\), equality in the contraction
chain yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support}
 C\phi=g:=\jj_p(\eta)=D^{-1}(q,1,q,-1)^T,
 \qquad C^*\eta=\jj_s(\phi),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D}
 D^s=2(q^s+1).
\end{equation}
Indeed, the first equality is the unique-support-functional equality, while
the second follows by applying the same equality to the contraction \(C\),
which maps the unit vector \(\phi\) to the unit vector \(g\).

Write the two blocks of \(C\) as
\[
 C_1=H\diag(\mu_1,\mu_2)H,\qquad
 C_2=H\diag(\mu_3,\mu_4)H,
\]
and put \(\alpha=(u+v)/\sqrt2\), \(\beta=(u-v)/\sqrt2\).  The first equality
in \eqref{eq:support} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mus}
 \mu_1\alpha=\frac{q+1}{\sqrt2D},\quad
 \mu_2\beta=\frac{q-1}{\sqrt2D},\quad
 \mu_3\alpha=\frac{q-1}{\sqrt2D},\quad
 \mu_4\beta=\frac{q+1}{\sqrt2D}.
\end{equation}
In particular, \(\alpha\beta\ne0\), and \(C\) is uniquely determined by
\(\phi\).  Insert \eqref{eq:mus} into the second equality in
\eqref{eq:support}.  After cancelling one common positive constant, the two
Hadamard coordinates say
\[
 \frac1{\bar u+\bar v}=\kappa\bigl(|u|^{s-2}u+|v|^{s-2}v\bigr),\qquad
 \frac1{\bar u-\bar v}=\kappa\bigl(|u|^{s-2}u-|v|^{s-2}v\bigr)
\]
for a nonzero common scalar \(\kappa\).  Adding and subtracting these equations
shows that, when \(uv\ne0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase}
 -\frac{\bar v}{\bar u}
 =\frac{|v|^{s-2}v}{|u|^{s-2}u}.
\end{equation}
Taking moduli in \eqref{eq:phase} gives \(|u|=|v|\); taking phases then gives
\(v/u=\pm i\).  If one coordinate vanishes, \(\phi\) is a coordinate vector.
Up to a unimodular scalar, only
\begin{equation}\label{eq:four}
 e_1,\quad e_2,\quad 2^{-1/s}(1,i)^T,\quad 2^{-1/s}(1,-i)^T
\end{equation}
remain.

We now show that the unique \(C\) in each case has norm strictly greater than
one, contradicting \(\|C\|=\|B\|\le1\).

If \(\phi=e_1\), equations \eqref{eq:support} and the block symmetry give
\[
 C=D^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}
 q&1\\1&q\\q&-1\\-1&q
 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Applying this matrix to \((1,1)^T\), using \eqref{eq:D}, and invoking the
strict \(s>2\) case of Lemma \ref{lem:clarkson}, gives
\[
 \frac{\|C(1,1)^T\|_s^s}{\|(1,1)^T\|_s^s}
 =\frac{(q+1)^s+(q-1)^s}{2(q^s+1)}>1.
\]
The case \(e_2\) is the same after swapping coordinates.

It remains to exclude \(2^{-1/s}(1,i)^T\); complex conjugation handles the
minus sign.  Let \(\phi_+=(1,i)^T\), \(\phi_-=(1,-i)^T\), and let \(C_0\) be
the unique structured column satisfying \(C_0\phi_+=g\).  Directly from
\eqref{eq:mus},
\[
 C_0=\frac1{2D}\begin{bmatrix}
 q-i&1-iq\\1-iq&q-i\\
 q+i&-1-iq\\-1-iq&q+i
 \end{bmatrix},
 \qquad C=2^{1/s}C_0.
\]
For real \(t\) near zero set \(\xi_t=\phi_++it\phi_-\).  A direct calculation
gives
\begin{align*}
 F(t)&:=\|C\xi_t\|_s^s-\|\xi_t\|_s^s\\
 &=\frac{(q+t)^s+(q-t)^s+(1+qt)^s+(1-qt)^s}{q^s+1}
   -2(1+t^2)^{s/2}.
\end{align*}
Thus \(F(0)=F'(0)=0\), while
\[
 F''(0)=2s\left(\frac{(s-1)(q^{s-2}+q^2)}{q^s+1}-1\right)>0
\]
by Lemma \ref{lem:scalar}.  Hence \(F(t)>0\) for all sufficiently small
nonzero \(t\), so again \(\|C\|>1\).  This exhausts \eqref{eq:four} and proves
the first assertion of Theorem \ref{thm:main}.

\section{Adjoint duality and the range \(2<p<\infty\)}

Let now \(2<p<\infty\), and put \(r=p'\in(1,2)\).  Banach adjoints give
isometric bijections
\[
 X_r\longleftrightarrow Y_p,\qquad Y_r\longleftrightarrow X_p,
\]
because every matrix in the Hadamard-diagonal algebra has its adjoint in the
same algebra, and \(\|S\|_{\ell_r\to\ell_r}=\|S^*\|_{\ell_p\to\ell_p}\).
They also reverse products.  Therefore the already proved strict inequality at
\(r\) implies
\begin{align*}
 \max_{C\in\Ba(X_p)}\|T^*C\|_{A_p}
 &=\max_{B\in\Ba(Y_r)}\|(BT)^*\|_{A_p}\\
 &=\max_{B\in\Ba(Y_r)}\|BT\|_{A_r}
 <\|T\|_{X_r}=\|T^*\|_{Y_p}.
\end{align*}
This proves the second assertion.  At \(p=2\), \(H\) is unitary and \(A_2\)
is a C*--algebra, so the row--column module is C*--like by the standard
C*--module norm recovery (also Lemma 2.13 of the source paper
\cite{source}).  Theorem \ref{thm:main} follows.

\section{Limitations and reviewer focus}

The theorem concerns exactly the two-summand row--column module and algebra of
Example 5.2.  It does not classify arbitrary simultaneously diagonalizable
subalgebras or higher-dimensional Hadamard changes of basis.  The most useful
human checks are: (i) the equality-case passage from \eqref{eq:support} to
\eqref{eq:phase}; (ii) the normalization \(D^s=2(q^s+1)\); and (iii) the
adjoint identification of the two module norm-recovery axioms.
