\section{Source question}

Let $I=[0,1]$, let
\begin{equation}
 0<p<q\leq1,\qquad s=d(1/p-1),
 \label{eq:range}
\end{equation}
and put $X=B^s_{p,q,1}(I^d)$, the Besov space defined using first-order
$L_p$ moduli of smoothness.  The source asks on PDF p.~2 whether the spaces
in \eqref{eq:range} possess Schauder bases, or even unconditional Schauder bases, at all
\cite{Oswald}:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.97\linewidth]{figures/open_question_crop.png}
\end{center}

The condition $s<1/p$ implicit in the source's setup forces
$p>(d-1)/d$.  The source proves that the Haar system is not a Schauder basis
in this range, but also that $X\hookrightarrow L_1(I^d)$ and that dyadic step
functions are dense in $X$.  We resolve the parenthesized, unconditional
part of the question negatively.

\section{Result and idea of the proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $d\geq1$ and every choice of parameters satisfying
\eqref{eq:range} and
$s<1/p$, the quasi-Banach space $B^s_{p,q,1}(I^d)$ has no unconditional
Schauder basis.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{Idea of the Proof}

At the critical exponent, the Besov cost of a constant on a dyadic cube is
at most its $L_1$ mass.  Splitting a dyadic step function into its individual
cells therefore shows that its convexified Besov norm is at most its
$L_1$ norm.  The known embedding into $L_1$ gives the reverse estimate.
Consequently the Banach envelope of $X$ is exactly $L_1(I^d)$, up to
equivalent norms.

If $(x_n)$ were an unconditional basis of $X$, all finite coordinate
projections would be uniformly bounded.  Bounded operators extend to the
Banach envelope, so these projections would extend uniformly to $L_1$.
Their ordinary partial sums converge on the dense subspace $X$ and hence on
all of $L_1$.  Thus $(x_n)$ would be an unconditional Schauder basis of
$L_1(I^d)$, contradicting the classical theorem that nonatomic $L_1$ has no
unconditional Schauder basis.

\section{The Banach-envelope computation}

Let $\mathcal S_k$ be the functions constant on the dyadic cubes of side
$2^{-k}$, and set
\[
 E_k(f)_p=\inf_{g\in\mathcal S_k}\|f-g\|_p.
\]
The source's Lemma~1 gives the equivalent quasi-norm
\begin{equation}
 \|f\|_X\asymp
 \left(\|f\|_p^q+
 \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigl(2^{ks}E_k(f)_p\bigr)^q\right)^{1/q}.
 \label{eq:approx}
\end{equation}

For a quasi-Banach space $Z$, write
\begin{equation}
 \|z\|_{c}:=
 \inf\left\{\sum_{j=1}^N\|z_j\|_Z:
 z=\sum_{j=1}^N z_j,\ N<\infty\right\}.
 \label{eq:convexified}
\end{equation}
Its Hausdorff completion is the Banach envelope whenever
\eqref{eq:convexified} separates
points.

\begin{lemma}[Critical dyadic atoms]\label{lem:atoms}
If $\Delta$ is a dyadic cube of level $m$ and $a$ is a scalar, then
\[
 \|a\one_\Delta\|_X\lesssim |a|\,|\Delta|.
 \tag{4}
\]
The implicit constant is independent of $a,m,$ and $\Delta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $k\geq m$, the atom belongs to $\mathcal S_k$, so its best-approximation
error is zero.  For $k<m$,
\[
 E_k(a\one_\Delta)_p\leq\|a\one_\Delta\|_p
 =|a|\,|\Delta|^{1/p}.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:approx}, $s>0$, and the geometric sum,
\begin{align*}
 \|a\one_\Delta\|_X
 &\lesssim |a|\,|\Delta|^{1/p}
 \left(1+\sum_{k<m}2^{ksq}\right)^{1/q} \\
 &\lesssim |a|\,|\Delta|^{1/p}2^{ms}.
\end{align*}
Since $|\Delta|=2^{-md}$ and $s=d(1/p-1)$, the last product is
$|a|2^{-md/p}2^{md(1/p-1)}=|a|2^{-md}=|a||\Delta|$.
The unweighted $L_p$ term is smaller because
$|\Delta|^{1/p}\leq|\Delta|$ for $p<1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Envelope identification]\label{lem:envelope}
The canonical inclusion of $X$ into $L_1(I^d)$ extends to an isomorphism
from the Banach envelope $\widehat X$ onto $L_1(I^d)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The source proves $\|f\|_1\lesssim\|f\|_X$ in the range
\eqref{eq:range}.  Hence every finite decomposition in
\eqref{eq:convexified} gives
\[
 \|f\|_1\leq\sum_j\|f_j\|_1\lesssim\sum_j\|f_j\|_X,
\]
and therefore $\|f\|_1\lesssim\|f\|_c$.  In particular,
\eqref{eq:convexified} separates
points.

If $f=\sum_{\Delta\in\mathcal T_m}a_\Delta\one_\Delta$ is a dyadic step
function on one level, Lemma~\ref{lem:atoms} and this very decomposition give
\[
 \|f\|_c\leq\sum_{\Delta\in\mathcal T_m}
 \|a_\Delta\one_\Delta\|_X
 \lesssim\sum_\Delta |a_\Delta||\Delta|=\|f\|_1.
\]
Thus the envelope norm and $L_1$ norm are equivalent on all dyadic step
functions.  They are dense in $X$ by the source and dense in $L_1(I^d)$.
Completing proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Unconditional bases pass to the envelope}

We record the required transfer without invoking a black-box envelope
theorem.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:transfer}
Let a quasi-Banach space $Z$ inject continuously and densely into its Banach
envelope $\widehat Z$.  If $(z_n)$ is an unconditional Schauder basis of
$Z$, then $(z_n)$ is an unconditional Schauder basis of $\widehat Z$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every bounded operator $T:Z\to Z$ is bounded for the convexified norm:
for every finite decomposition $z=\sum_jz_j$,
\[
 \|Tz\|_c\leq\sum_j\|Tz_j\|_Z
 \leq\|T\|\sum_j\|z_j\|_Z.
\]
It consequently extends to $\widehat Z$.

Let $P_A$ be the coordinate projection onto a finite set
$A\subset\mathbb N$.  Unconditionality gives
$\sup_A\|P_A\|<\infty$, so all $P_A$ extend to $\widehat Z$ with a common
bound.  In particular, the ordinary partial sums $P_{[1,N]}z$ converge to
$z$ for $z\in Z$.  Uniform boundedness and density then imply convergence
for every $z\in\widehat Z$: approximate $z$ by $y\in Z$ and estimate
\[
 \|P_{[1,N]}z-z\|
 \leq \|P_{[1,N]}(z-y)\|+
 \|P_{[1,N]}y-y\|+\|y-z\|.
\]
Each extended $P_{[1,N]}$ still has range
$\operatorname{span}\{z_1,\ldots,z_N\}$, since that finite-dimensional
range is closed.  The projection identities persist by continuity, so these
partial sums give a Schauder basis of $\widehat Z$.  The uniform bounds for
all finite $P_A$ give unconditionality there.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:envelope}, $\widehat X\simeq L_1(I^d)$.  An unconditional
basis of $X$ would, by Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}, induce an unconditional
basis of $L_1(I^d)$.  This contradicts the classical nonexistence theorem
for unconditional Schauder bases in nonatomic $L_1$; the source itself
recalls this theorem and cites Kashin--Saakyan for it.  Therefore $X$ has no
unconditional Schauder basis.
\end{proof}

\section{Why the conditional question remains}

For an ordinary Schauder basis, only the initial coordinate projections are
uniformly bounded.  The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer} still shows that
such a basis of $X$ would pass to a conditional basis of $L_1$.  This is no
contradiction: $L_1(I^d)$ does have conditional Schauder bases, including a
suitable ordering of the Haar system.  Thus the envelope method separates
the two words in the source's ``(unconditional)'' question sharply.

Standard smooth-wavelet results do not fill the gap: at the endpoint
\eqref{eq:range},
the first-difference space $B^s_{p,q,1}$ is not covered by the strict
norm-equivalence range with the distributional Besov space.  Likewise,
higher-order spline results concern spaces defined by higher-order moduli.
Finally, the source's exponential lower bound for the norms of the dyadic
conditional expectations rules out the natural Haar filtration, but it
does not rule out unrelated finite-rank approximation schemes.  A resolution
of the conditional-basis question therefore requires a new construction or
a genuinely stronger invariant than the Banach envelope.
