\section{The question}

Let $H$ be a real Hilbert space and let $\D\subset H$ be a symmetric
dictionary of unit vectors with dense linear span.  For $f\in H$, the Pure
Greedy Algorithm (PGA) starts with $r_0=f$ and iterates
\[
 d_m=\sup_{g\in\D}|\langle r_m,g\rangle|,
 \qquad r_{m+1}=r_m-d_m g_{m+1},
\]
where the sign and $g_{m+1}\in\D$ are chosen so that
$\langle r_m,g_{m+1}\rangle=d_m$.  Write $\|f\|_{\Aone}$ for the gauge of
the closed convex hull of $\D$, and define
\[
 \gamma_m(\alpha,H)=
 \sup_{\D,f,\mathrm{PGA}}
 \frac{\|r_m\|}{\|f\|^{1-\alpha}\|f\|_{\Aone}^{\alpha}},
 \qquad 0<\alpha\leq1.
\]
The source, V.~N. Temlyakov, \emph{On the rate of convergence of greedy
algorithms}, arXiv:2304.06423v1 \cite{Temlyakov2023}, proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
 \tfrac12m^{-\alpha/2}\leq\gamma_m(\alpha,H)
 \leq m^{-\alpha/2},\qquad 0<\alpha\leq\tfrac13,
\end{equation}
and asks on PDF page 5 whether the upper rate continues to hold for
$\alpha>1/3$.  It notes, using the lower exponent known at the time, that it
cannot continue beyond $0.3796$.

We determine the exact transition point.  Let $\Gamma\in[1,3/2]$ be the
unique root of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root}
 (1+x)^{1/(2+x)}\left(1+\frac1{1+x}\right)-1-\frac1x=0.
\end{equation}
Numerically,
\[
 \Gamma=1.1523436882652335\ldots,
 \qquad
 \alpha_*:=\frac{\Gamma}{2+\Gamma}
 =0.3655514126060854\ldots.
\]

\section{Proof intuition}

The familiar PGA proof tracks two quantities: residual energy
$a_m=\|r_m\|^2$ and accumulated atomic mass
$b_m=\|f\|_{\Aone}+\sum_{j<m}d_j$.  One elementary recurrence gives
$a_mb_m^{-2}\lesssim m^{-1}$.  Sil'nichenko's sharp standard-rate proof
contains a second invariant, customarily normalized with
$\|f\|_{\Aone}=1$.  Its scalar differential inequality is actually
invariant under \emph{independent} rescaling of the horizontal mass variable
by $\|f\|_{\Aone}$ and the vertical energy variable by $\|f\|^2$.  Restoring
both scales gives
\[
 a_mb_m^\Gamma\lesssim
 \|f\|^2\|f\|_{\Aone}^{\Gamma}.
\]
Taking the right powers of these two invariants cancels $b_m$ and gives the
desired mixed estimate at $\alpha_*$.  Interpolation with the trivial bound
$\|r_m\|\leq\|f\|$ covers all smaller $\alpha$.  The sharp matching-pursuit
lower construction of Klusowski--Siegel then forbids the rate for every
larger $\alpha$.

\section{The two-scale envelope lemma}

We record the part of Sil'nichenko's argument that carries the new scaling.

\begin{lemma}[two-scale Sil'nichenko invariant]\label{lem:envelope}
Let $r_m$ be a PGA residual sequence, put
\[
 h=\|f\|,\quad A=\|f\|_{\Aone},\quad
 a_m=\|r_m\|^2,\quad b_0=A,\quad b_{m+1}=b_m+d_m.
\]
With $\Gamma$ defined by \eqref{eq:root}, there is an absolute constant
$C_\Gamma$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:secondinv}
 a_m b_m^\Gamma\leq C_\Gamma h^2A^\Gamma
 \qquad(m\geq0).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We expose the scaling in the scalar proof of \cite{Silnichenko2004}.  The PGA
identities and the atomic norm give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:basic}
 a_{m+1}=a_m-d_m^2,\qquad
 \|r_m\|_{\Aone}\leq b_m.
\end{equation}
For $\ell\geq k$, the definition of $d_\ell$ and
$\|r_k\|_{\Aone}\leq b_k$ imply
\[
 d_\ell b_k\geq |\langle r_\ell,r_k\rangle|.
\]
Since $r_\ell=r_k-\sum_{j=k}^{\ell-1}d_jg_{j+1}$ and
$|\langle r_k,g_{j+1}\rangle|\leq d_k$, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross}
 d_\ell\geq
 \frac{a_k-d_k(b_\ell-b_k)}{b_k}
\end{equation}
whenever the numerator is positive; if it is nonpositive the inequality is
automatic.

Interpolate $a_m$ and $b_m$ linearly in the iteration variable.  On an
interval corresponding to step $m$, \eqref{eq:basic} gives
$a'=-d_m^2$ and $b'=d_m$.  Regarding energy as a function $F$ of accumulated
mass, so $F(b(t))=a(t)$, gives $F'=-d_m$.  Inequality
\eqref{eq:cross}, first at knots and then by linear interpolation, becomes
the scalar envelope inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
 F'(x_2)\leq
 \frac{-F(x_1)-F'(x_1)(x_2-x_1)}{x_1},\qquad A\leq x_1\leq x_2.
\end{equation}

The barrier lemma proved in \cite{Silnichenko2004} states that a nonnegative
decreasing piecewise-linear function $\Phi$ on $[1,\infty)$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:envelope} and $\Phi(1)\leq1$ obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barrier}
 \Phi(x)x^\Gamma\leq C_\Gamma,\qquad x\geq1,
\end{equation}
where the largest admissible exponent $\Gamma$ is characterized by
\eqref{eq:root}.  For completeness, its proof sets
$x=e^t$ and $G(t)=e^{\Gamma t}\Phi(e^t)$; applying
\eqref{eq:envelope} at the optimizing delay
$t_0=\log(1+\Gamma)/(2+\Gamma)$ gives a delayed linear inequality for $G$.
The equality case for the barrier is exactly \eqref{eq:root}, and integration
over consecutive intervals of length $t_0$ bounds $G$ uniformly.
The same conclusion up to the right endpoint holds when the domain is a
finite interval, by applying the argument only to intervals that precede
that endpoint.  Thus no assumption that $b_m\to\infty$ is needed.

The key point is that no relation between the two initial scales was used in
this scalar lemma.  Define
\[
 \Phi(z)=\frac{F(Az)}{h^2},\qquad z\geq1.
\]
Then $\Phi(1)=1$, and direct substitution shows that \eqref{eq:envelope} is
unchanged: both sides acquire the same factor $A/h^2$.  Applying
\eqref{eq:barrier} at $z=b_m/A$ gives
\[
 \frac{a_m}{h^2}\left(\frac{b_m}{A}\right)^\Gamma
 \leq C_\Gamma,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:secondinv}.
\end{proof}

\section{Sharp upper range}

The other invariant is elementary.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:first}
For every $m\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:firstinv}
 a_mb_m^{-2}\leq (m+1)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The atomic duality estimate gives
$d_m\geq a_m/\|r_m\|_{\Aone}\geq a_m/b_m$.  Hence, with
$x_m=a_mb_m^{-2}$ and using $b_{m+1}\geq b_m$,
\[
 x_{m+1}\leq x_m(1-x_m).
\]
Also $x_0=h^2/A^2\leq1$.  The standard reciprocal induction yields
$x_m\leq(m+1)^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[sharp upper endpoint]\label{thm:upper}
There is an absolute constant $C$ such that, for every Hilbert space,
dictionary, target, realization of the PGA, and $m\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint}
 \|r_m\|\leq C
 \|f\|^{1-\alpha_*}\|f\|_{\Aone}^{\alpha_*}
 m^{-\alpha_*/2}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every $0<\alpha\leq\alpha_*$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:allupper}
 \gamma_m(\alpha,H)\leq C_\alpha m^{-\alpha/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Raise \eqref{eq:secondinv} to the power $2/(2+\Gamma)$ and
\eqref{eq:firstinv} to the power $\Gamma/(2+\Gamma)$, and multiply.  The
powers of $b_m$ cancel and give
\[
 a_m\leq C_\Gamma^{2/(2+\Gamma)}
 h^{4/(2+\Gamma)}A^{2\Gamma/(2+\Gamma)}
 (m+1)^{-\Gamma/(2+\Gamma)}.
\]
Taking square roots and using
$\alpha_*=\Gamma/(2+\Gamma)$ proves \eqref{eq:endpoint}.

For $0<\alpha<\alpha_*$, put $\lambda=\alpha/\alpha_*$.  Energy decreases,
so $\|r_m\|\leq h$.  The elementary inequality
$\min\{u,v\}\leq u^{1-\lambda}v^\lambda$ applied to this trivial bound and
\eqref{eq:endpoint} proves \eqref{eq:allupper}.
\end{proof}

The orthonormal example already used in the source shows that this rate is
optimal throughout the upper range.  Namely, take
$f=\sum_{j=1}^{2m}e_j$ and the signed orthonormal dictionary.  After $m$
steps, $\|r_m\|=m^{1/2}$, while $\|f\|=(2m)^{1/2}$ and
$\|f\|_{\Aone}=2m$.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ortholower}
 \gamma_m(\alpha,H)\geq
 2^{-(1+\alpha)/2}m^{-\alpha/2}
 \geq\tfrac12m^{-\alpha/2}.
\end{equation}

\section{Failure above the threshold}

Klusowski and Siegel \cite{KlusowskiSiegel2025} proved the sharp lower
exponent for the standard PGA problem.  In the present notation, if
\[
 p_*:=\frac{\Gamma}{2(2+\Gamma)}=\frac{\alpha_*}{2},
\]
then for every $p>p_*$ there are a fixed Hilbert space, a fixed dictionary,
and a fixed nonzero $f\in A_1(\D)$ whose PGA residuals satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KS}
 \|r_m\|\geq c_p\|f\|_{\Aone}m^{-p}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large $m$.

\begin{theorem}[sharp failure]\label{thm:failure}
If $\alpha>\alpha_*$, there is no constant $C_\alpha$ for which
$\gamma_m(\alpha,H)\leq C_\alpha m^{-\alpha/2}$ holds uniformly in $m$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose $p$ with $p_*<p<\alpha/2$ and take the fixed example in
\eqref{eq:KS}.  Since $h=\|f\|$ and $A=\|f\|_{\Aone}$ are fixed positive
numbers,
\[
 \gamma_m(\alpha,H)
 \geq \frac{c_pA}{h^{1-\alpha}A^\alpha}m^{-p}
 =c'_{p,\alpha}m^{-p}.
\]
Multiplying by $m^{\alpha/2}$ tends to infinity, which contradicts any
uniform target bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[complete answer to the source question]
The upper rate in \eqref{eq:source} extends beyond $1/3$ exactly through
\[
 \alpha_*=\frac{\Gamma}{2+\Gamma}
       =0.365551412606085\ldots .
\]
For $0<\alpha\leq\alpha_*$ its order is exactly $m^{-\alpha/2}$; for every
$\alpha>\alpha_*$ that order is impossible uniformly over dictionaries and
targets.
\end{corollary}

\section{Scope, novelty, and review}

The result concerns the order of the uniform rate; constants may depend on
$\alpha$.  It does not assert that the unit constant in the source's display
remains optimal beyond $1/3$.  The threshold comes from combining a new
two-norm reading of Sil'nichenko's upper proof with the later sharp lower
construction.  Neither supporting paper states the mixed characteristic
$\gamma_m(\alpha,H)$ at this threshold.

The run indexes, parsed corpus, exact formula, source question, and bounded
primary-source web searches through 11 August 2026 found no explicit prior
statement of this characterization.  Temlyakov's 2025 survey records the
sharp standard exponent but not the two-scale invariant or mixed threshold.
Novelty confidence is moderate because the rescaling is short and may be
implicit folklore.

The proof is analytic.  The only numerical value is an evaluation of the
uniquely characterized root and is not used as evidence.  Expert review
should focus on the passage from the discrete cross-iteration inequality to
the scalar envelope inequality, the independent $(A,h^2)$ rescaling in
Lemma~\ref{lem:envelope}, and the quantifiers in the Klusowski--Siegel lower
theorem.
