\section{The source question and its scope ambiguity}

For operators $A_1,\dots,A_n$ and $k\le n$, Albar, Junge, and Zhao~\cite{AJZ}
write
\begin{align*}
 E_{\mathrm{wo},k}(\mathcal A)
 &=\frac{(n-k)!}{n!}
   \sum_{j_1,\dots,j_k\ \mathrm{all\ distinct}}
   A_{j_1}^*\cdots A_{j_k}^*A_{j_k}\cdots A_{j_1},\\
 E_{\mathrm{wr},k}(\mathcal A)
 &=\frac1{n^k}\sum_{j_1,\dots,j_k=1}^n
   A_{j_1}^*\cdots A_{j_k}^*A_{j_k}\cdots A_{j_1}.
\end{align*}
They ask whether
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conjecture}
 \|E_{\mathrm{wo},k}(\mathcal A)\|
 \le \|E_{\mathrm{wr},k}(\mathcal A)\|
\end{equation}
always holds, and state that this norm conjecture remains open.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/open_conjecture_crop.png}
\caption{The constant-one norm conjecture in arXiv:1803.02435, page 3.}
\end{figure}

There are two natural readings.  Equations (1.5)--(1.7) in~\cite{AJZ} use
adjoints and operator variables, and the subsequent results speak of families
of operators.  Read literally in that generality, \eqref{eq:conjecture} is
false by the theorem below.  But the preceding motivation in the same paper
starts with positive definite matrices, as the following source excerpt shows.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/positive_context_crop.png}
\caption{The positive-definite motivating context in arXiv:1803.02435, page 2.}
\end{figure}

More decisively, Recht and R\'e's original weak-variance conjecture explicitly
assumes positive semidefinite matrices~\cite[Conjecture 1]{RR}.  Our result
therefore resolves the literal operator version and isolates positivity as an
indispensable hypothesis; it does not claim to settle the historical PSD
version.

\section{The counterexample}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The norm comparison \eqref{eq:conjecture} fails for $n=k=3$ on the real
two-dimensional Hilbert space, even when $A_1,A_2,A_3$ are pairwise
nonproportional invertible self-adjoint operators.  One may take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mats}
 A_1=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-2\\-2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A_2=\begin{pmatrix}-2&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A_3=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For this triple,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:averages}
 E_{\mathrm{wo},3}
 =\begin{pmatrix}521/6&27\\27&641/6\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 E_{\mathrm{wr},3}
 =\frac1{27}\begin{pmatrix}3081&302\\302&3083\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norms}
 \|E_{\mathrm{wo},3}\|=\frac{581}{6}+\sqrt{829}
 >\frac{3082+\sqrt{91205}}{27}
 =\|E_{\mathrm{wr},3}\|.
\end{equation}
The norm ratio is approximately $1.00233216996281$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All three matrices in \eqref{eq:mats} are real symmetric.  Their determinants
are respectively $-4,-6,-3$, so they are invertible and indefinite; inspection
also shows that no two are proportional.

For a word $\mathbf j=(j_1,j_2,j_3)$ put
$P_{\mathbf j}=A_{j_3}A_{j_2}A_{j_1}$.  Its summand in either expectation is
$P_{\mathbf j}^*P_{\mathbf j}$.  Direct integer multiplication over the six
permutations and over all twenty-seven words gives
\[
 \sum_{\mathbf j\ \mathrm{distinct}}P_{\mathbf j}^*P_{\mathbf j}
 =\begin{pmatrix}521&162\\162&641\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \sum_{\mathbf j\in\{1,2,3\}^3}P_{\mathbf j}^*P_{\mathbf j}
 =\begin{pmatrix}3081&302\\302&3083\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Division by $3!=6$ and $3^3=27$ proves \eqref{eq:averages}.  The larger
eigenvalues of these two positive matrices are exactly the quantities displayed
in \eqref{eq:norms}.

The strict comparison needs no decimal approximation.  Since
$(1439/50)^2<829$ and $303^2>91205$,
\begin{align*}
 \|E_{\mathrm{wo},3}\|-\|E_{\mathrm{wr},3}\|
 &>\frac{581}{6}+\frac{1439}{50}-\frac{3082+303}{27}
 =\frac{164}{675}>0.
\end{align*}
This proves the claimed failure.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[an even shorter nonself-adjoint witness]
Let $N=e_{12}$ and take $A_1=N$, $A_2=2N$, $A_3=N^*$.  Nilpotence leaves
only alternating words, giving
\[
 E_{\mathrm{wo},3}=\tfrac43e_{22},\qquad
 E_{\mathrm{wr},3}=\tfrac5{27}e_{11}+\tfrac{25}{27}e_{22}.
\]
Thus the arbitrary-operator norm ratio can already be $36/25$.  Theorem
\ref{thm:main} is stronger in regularity: its matrices are self-adjoint,
invertible, and pairwise nonproportional.
\end{remark}

\section{What remains open}

Each matrix in \eqref{eq:mats} has one positive and one negative eigenvalue.
The witness therefore cannot answer the PSD problem by reinterpretation.  This
boundary is substantive: searches parameterizing $A_i=X_i^*X_i$ over real and
complex matrices, through $n=k=6$ and dimensions two and three, found no ratio
above one; local optimization repeatedly converged to equality families.
Positive block dilations and scalar shifts of the indefinite witnesses also
lost the violation before reaching the positive cone.  These computations are
only stress tests, not evidence sufficient for an affirmative theorem.

The 2020 Lai--Lim counterexample~\cite{LL} concerns the other Recht--R\'e
conjecture, the unsymmetrized first-moment/bias comparison for PSD matrices.  It
does not supply the palindromic second-moment comparison considered here.
Accordingly, the exact remaining question is:
\begin{quote}
Does \eqref{eq:conjecture} hold when every $A_i$ is positive semidefinite?
\end{quote}
The present theorem shows that any affirmative result must use positivity, not
merely self-adjointness, invertibility, small dimension, or the shortest
nontrivial sampling length.

\section{Verification and review focus}

The companion script \texttt{code/exact\_verifier.py} enumerates all words in
exact SymPy arithmetic, checks \eqref{eq:averages}, checks both eigenvalue
formulas, and verifies the rational gap certificate.  It is an independent
reproducibility aid; the displayed proof itself is finite and exact.

A reviewer should prioritize: (i) that the word order matches equations
(1.5)--(1.7) of~\cite{AJZ}; (ii) the two integer sums in the proof; and (iii)
the scope distinction between the literal operator formulation and the
positive-semidefinite hypothesis in~\cite{RR}.
