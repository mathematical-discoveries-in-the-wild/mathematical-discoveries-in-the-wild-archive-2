\section{Source questions and resolution}

For a finite Borel measure $\eta$ on a metric space and
$x\in\supp\eta$, write
\[
 \ldim\eta(x)=\liminf_{r\downarrow0}
 \frac{\log\eta(B(x,r))}{\log r},
 \qquad
 \udim\eta(x)=\limsup_{r\downarrow0}
 \frac{\log\eta(B(x,r))}{\log r}.
\]
When they agree, their common value is $\dim_{\rm loc}\eta(x)$.

Hare and Prandi~\cite{HP} prove lower-local-dimension inheritance for
convolution under a common-scale almost-everywhere hypothesis.  In Section~5,
PDF page 12, they ask:
\begin{quote}
``Is there a weaker or different condition for Theorem 2.7. If we know
$\ldim\mu(x)\geq\lambda$, under what conditions is it true that
$\ldim(\mu*\nu)(z)\geq\lambda$.''
\end{quote}
They then distinguish finite representation fibres from fibres containing an
open family and ask:
\begin{quote}
``What happens if there are countably many $x\in\supp\mu$ and
$y\in\supp\nu$ such that $x+y=z$?''
\end{quote}

Theorem~\ref{thm:universal} gives a complete negative resolution of the
countable-fibre question in the lower-local-dimension setting: every finite
nonnegative value is possible, even under the pointwise hypothesis and a
pairwise product-dimension lower bound.  Proposition~\ref{prop:rescue} answers
the first question constructively by replacing the common-scale condition
with decay of the mass of bad centres; Proposition~\ref{prop:strict} proves
the replacement is strictly weaker.

\section{Proof intuition}

Countability does not provide quantitative uniformity.  A countable fibre can
be assembled from tiny packets at geometrically separated centres.  The
packet weights determine the mass delivered to the target, while the packet
widths determine the scale at which it arrives.  These two parameters can be
tuned independently without changing the eventual pointwise dimension at
any fixed packet.

For target dimensions $0\leq\beta\leq1$, one measure consists of symmetric
uniform packets and the other of aligned atoms.  The $n$th aligned
contribution has mass $p_n^2$ and width $\ell_n$.  Choosing
$p_n^2\asymp\ell_n^\beta$ makes the aligned convolution mass scale like
$r^\beta$.  A different super-exponential choice of widths yields dimension
zero at every sufficiently small scale, not merely along a subsequence.

For $\beta>1$, atoms would force a linear contribution, so both factors are
made one-sided near each representation pair.  Power-law endpoint densities
with exponents $a$ and $b$ convolve to order $r^{a+b}$; setting
$a+b=\beta$ gives the target.  Geometric separation forces every
off-diagonal interaction into a small tail.  Choosing the tail exponent
$D>\beta/2$ makes its total mass $O(r^{2D})=o(r^\beta)$.

The positive criterion uses a complementary idea.  At each scale, centres
where the desired Frostman estimate fails are allowed, but their $\nu$-mass
must decay with the same power as the target ball mass.  Splitting the
convolution integral into good and bad centres then gives the desired bound.

\section{A common separation estimate}

The following elementary observation will control every unintended
interaction.  All constants below may depend on the fixed construction
parameters, never on $r$.

\begin{lemma}[Off-diagonal tail bound]\label{lem:tail}
Let $x_{n+1}\leq x_n/4$ and let $J_n$ be an interval contained in
$[x_n-x_n/16,x_n+x_n/16]$.  Give $J_n$ mass $p_n$, where the $p_n$ form a
geometric sequence.  Pair these packets with packets or atoms lying within
$x_n/16$ of $-x_n$ and also having mass $p_n$.

If an off-diagonal pair of indices $k\neq n$ contributes to a ball
$B(0,r)$ under addition, then
\[
 x_{\min\{k,n\}}\leq2r.
\]
Consequently its total contribution is at most
\[
 \left(\sum_{x_j\leq2r}p_j\right)^2.
\]
If $p_j=x_j^D$, up to a fixed normalization, this is $O(r^{2D})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $j=\min\{k,n\}$, so $x_j$ is the larger centre.  The centre separation is
at least $3x_j/4$.  The relevant packet radii have sum at most $x_j/8$.
Thus meeting $B(0,r)$ forces $r\geq x_j/2$.  Both indices therefore belong
to the displayed tail.  Summing all products $p_kp_n$ over that tail gives
the square bound.  For geometric $p_j$ the tail is comparable to its first
term; if $p_j\asymp x_j^D$, it is $O(r^D)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Complete universality}

\begin{theorem}[Countable-fibre universality]\label{thm:universal}
Fix $0<\lambda\leq1$ and a finite $\beta\geq0$.  There are compactly
supported Borel probability measures $\mu,\nu$ on $\mathbb R$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\ldim\mu(x)\geq\lambda$ for every $x\in\supp\mu$;
\item the representation fibre at zero is exactly
\[
 \mathcal F_0=
 \{(x,y)\in\supp\mu\times\supp\nu:x+y=0\}
 =\{(0,0)\}\cup\{(x_n,-x_n):n\geq1\};
\]
\item every point of $\mathcal F_0$ has product-measure lower local dimension
at least $\lambda$, for any standard product metric;
\item the local dimension of the convolution exists and satisfies
\[
 \dim_{\rm loc}(\mu*\nu)(0)=\beta.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The range \texorpdfstring{$0<\beta\leq1$}{0 < beta <= 1}}

Choose $0<q<1/16$ and let
\[
 p_n=(1-q)q^{n-1},\qquad x_n=p_n \qquad(n\geq1).
\]
Thus $\sum p_n=1$.  Choose $\tau>0$ small and set
\[
 \ell_n=\tau p_n^{2/\beta},\qquad
 I_n=[x_n-\ell_n,x_n+\ell_n],
\]
with $\ell_n\leq x_n/16$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric}
 \mu=\sum_{n=1}^\infty
 \frac{p_n}{2\ell_n}\mathbf1_{I_n}\,dx,
 \qquad
 \nu=\sum_{n=1}^\infty p_n\delta_{-x_n}.
\end{equation}

The intervals are disjoint and accumulate only at zero.  The measure $\mu$
has local dimension one at every point of every interval, including its
endpoints.  Since packet locations and their masses are both comparable to
$p_n$, geometric-tail comparison gives
\[
 \mu(B(0,r))\asymp r,
 \qquad
 \nu(B(0,r))\asymp r.
\]
Hence both measures have local dimension one at zero.  Their supports show
that the fibre is exactly the set in (ii).  Its nonzero points have product
dimension $1+0=1$, and $(0,0)$ has product dimension two.  Thus (i)--(iii)
hold for every $0<\lambda\leq1$.

Write $\eta=\mu*\nu$.  The aligned pairs contribute exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aligned}
 A(r)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty p_n^2
 \min\left\{1,\frac r{\ell_n}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, translating the $n$th uniform packet by $-x_n$ centres it at zero.
Since
\[
 p_n^2=\tau^{-\beta}\ell_n^\beta
\]
and the $\ell_n$ form a geometric sequence, choose $N=N(r)$ so that
$\ell_N\leq r<\ell_{N-1}$.  The full aligned tail gives
\[
 A(r)\geq\sum_{n\geq N}p_n^2\geq c r^\beta.
\]
For $0<\beta<1$, the partial-packet geometric sum and the full tail give
\[
 A(r)\leq C r^\beta.
\]
For $\beta=1$, each of the first $N-1$ partial packets contributes a fixed
multiple of $r$, and hence
\[
 A(r)\leq C N(r)r,
 \qquad N(r)=O(|\log r|).
\]

By Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}, all off-diagonal pairs contribute $O(r^2)$.  It
follows that, for $0<\beta<1$,
\[
 c r^\beta\leq\eta(B(0,r))\leq C r^\beta,
\]
while for $\beta=1$,
\[
 c r\leq\eta(B(0,r))\leq C(1+|\log r|)r.
\]
In both cases, division by $\log r$ proves
$\dim_{\rm loc}\eta(0)=\beta$.

\subsection{The value \texorpdfstring{$\beta=0$}{beta = 0}}

Keep $p_n,x_n$ and the measures in \eqref{eq:symmetric}, but now choose
\[
 \ell_n=\tau e^{-n^2},
\]
with $\tau$ small enough that the packets remain disjoint and
$\ell_n\leq x_n/16$.  If
$\ell_N\leq r<\ell_{N-1}$, the fully captured aligned tail gives
\[
 \eta(B(0,r))\geq\sum_{n\geq N}p_n^2\geq c q^{2N}.
\]
At the same time $|\log r|\asymp N^2$.  Since every small-scale exponent is
nonnegative,
\[
 0\leq\frac{\log\eta(B(0,r))}{\log r}
 \leq\frac{O(N)}{N^2+O(1)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the local dimension exists and equals zero.  The support and factor
local-dimension arguments above are unchanged.

\subsection{The range \texorpdfstring{$\beta>1$}{beta > 1}}

Choose
\[
 D>\max\{\lambda,\beta/2\},
 \qquad a=\lambda,
 \qquad b=\beta-\lambda>0.
\]
Take $q>0$ so small that $q^{1/D}<1/16$, set
\[
 p_n=(1-q)q^{n-1},
 \qquad x_n=p_n^{1/D},
 \qquad \ell_n=\tau p_n^{2/\beta},
\]
and choose $\tau>0$ so that $\ell_n\leq x_n/16$ for all $n$.  This is
possible because $2/\beta-1/D>0$.

The $n$th component of $\mu$ is supported on
$[x_n,x_n+\ell_n]$, has total mass $p_n$, and in the coordinate
$s=x-x_n$ has normalized density
\[
 \frac{a s^{a-1}}{\ell_n^a}\mathbf1_{(0,\ell_n)}(s).
\]
The $n$th component of $\nu$ is supported on
$[-x_n,-x_n+\ell_n]$, has total mass $p_n$, and in the coordinate
$t=y+x_n$ has normalized density
\[
 \frac{b t^{b-1}}{\ell_n^b}\mathbf1_{(0,\ell_n)}(t).
\]

At the left endpoint $x_n$, the local dimension of $\mu$ is $a=\lambda$;
at every other point of its $n$th packet it is one.  Geometric-tail comparison
at zero gives
\[
 \dim_{\rm loc}\mu(0)=D.
\]
Thus (i) holds.  The analogous dimension of $\nu$ at zero is also $D$.

For equal indices, a sum has the form $s+t\geq0$ and equals zero only when
$s=t=0$.  For unequal indices, the centre gap is larger than the sum of the
packet lengths, so zero is not represented.  This proves (ii).  The product
dimension at $(x_n,-x_n)$ is
\[
 a+b=\beta\geq\lambda,
\]
and the product dimension at $(0,0)$ is $2D>\beta$, proving (iii).

For an aligned pair and $0<r\leq\ell_n$, direct integration gives
\begin{align*}
 &p_n^2\int_0^r\frac{a s^{a-1}}{\ell_n^a}
 \left(\int_0^{r-s}\frac{b t^{b-1}}{\ell_n^b}\,dt\right)ds\\
 &\hspace{2cm}=
 p_n^2 a\,\mathrm B(a,b+1)
 \left(\frac r{\ell_n}\right)^{a+b}.
\end{align*}
For all $r>0$, the same contribution is bounded above by
\[
 C p_n^2\min\left\{1,(r/\ell_n)^\beta\right\}.
\]
Since $p_n^2=\tau^{-\beta}\ell_n^\beta$ and the lengths form a geometric
sequence, the total aligned contribution satisfies
\[
 c r^\beta\leq A(r)\leq C(1+|\log r|)r^\beta.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} bounds the off-diagonal contribution by
\[
 C\left(\sum_{x_n\leq2r}p_n\right)^2
 \leq C r^{2D}=o(r^\beta).
\]
Therefore
\[
 c r^\beta\leq(\mu*\nu)(B(0,r))
 \leq C(1+|\log r|)r^\beta,
\]
and $\dim_{\rm loc}(\mu*\nu)(0)=\beta$.  This completes the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal}.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:none}
For $0<\lambda\leq1$, neither the countability of the representation fibre,
the pointwise bound $\ldim\mu\geq\lambda$, nor even the conjunction of these
facts with product-dimension lower bounds on every fibre point imposes any
nontrivial numerical restriction on $\dim_{\rm loc}(\mu*\nu)(z)$.
\end{corollary}

\section{A weaker averaged condition that restores inheritance}

\begin{proposition}[Bad-centre decay criterion]\label{prop:rescue}
Let $\mu,\nu$ be compactly supported probability measures on $\mathbb R$, fix
$z\in\supp(\mu*\nu)$, and let $\alpha>0$.  For
$0<\varepsilon<\alpha$ put
\[
 E_\varepsilon(r)=
 \{y\in\supp\nu:\mu(B(z-y,r))>r^{\alpha-\varepsilon}\}.
\]
Suppose that for every $\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha)$ there are
$C_\varepsilon<\infty$ and $r_\varepsilon>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:baddecay}
 \nu(E_\varepsilon(r))
 \leq C_\varepsilon r^{\alpha-\varepsilon}
 \qquad(0<r<r_\varepsilon).
\end{equation}
Then $\ldim(\mu*\nu)(z)\geq\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Split the convolution integral into good and bad centres.  Since both
measures are probabilities,
\begin{align*}
 (\mu*\nu)(B(z,r))
 &=\int\mu(B(z-y,r))\,d\nu(y)\\
 &\leq r^{\alpha-\varepsilon}
      \nu(\mathbb R\setminus E_\varepsilon(r))
      +\nu(E_\varepsilon(r))\\
 &\leq(1+C_\varepsilon)r^{\alpha-\varepsilon}.
\end{align*}
Taking logarithms and dividing by the negative number $\log r$ gives
$\ldim(\mu*\nu)(z)\geq\alpha-\varepsilon$.  Let
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

The source hypothesis makes $\nu(E_\varepsilon(r))=0$ below a common scale,
so it implies \eqref{eq:baddecay}.  The converse fails.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:strict}
There are compactly supported probability measures $\mu,\widehat\nu$ for
which Proposition~\ref{prop:rescue} applies at $z=0$ and $\alpha=1$, but the
source paper's common-scale almost-everywhere condition fails.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $n\geq2$, set
\[
 x_n=2^{-n},\quad \ell_n=2^{-n^3},\quad
 I_n=[x_n-\ell_n,x_n+\ell_n],\quad m_n=12\,2^{-2n},
\]
and let
\[
 \mu=\sum_{n=2}^\infty
 \frac{m_n}{2\ell_n}\mathbf1_{I_n}\,dx,
 \qquad
 \widehat\nu=Z^{-1}\sum_{n=2}^\infty2^{-n^4}\delta_{-x_n}.
\]
Write $r=2^{-L}$.  If $n^3\leq\varepsilon L/2$ and $L$ is sufficiently
large, then
\[
 \mu(B(x_n,r))\leq12\,2^{\varepsilon L/2}r
 \leq r^{1-\varepsilon}.
\]
Thus every bad atom has
$n>(\varepsilon L/2)^{1/3}$, and its total mass is at most
\[
 C\sum_{n>(\varepsilon L/2)^{1/3}}2^{-n^4}
 \leq C'2^{-c_\varepsilon L^{4/3}}
 \leq C'r^{1-\varepsilon}.
\]
The averaged criterion applies.

At the packet scale $r=\ell_n$, however,
\[
 \frac{\log\mu(B(x_n,\ell_n))}{\log\ell_n}
 =\frac{2n\log2-\log12}{n^3\log2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Every proposed common cutoff therefore leaves a positive-mass atom that
violates the exponent-$1/2$ estimate.  The source condition fails.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The proof is exact.  The accompanying checker evaluates truncated versions
of both packet models, confirms the fibre-separation inequalities, and
recovers the target logarithmic slopes for representative values
$\beta=0,1/3,1,3/2,2,4$.  These numerical checks are sanity tests only; the
geometric-series and beta-integral estimates above are the proof.

Theorem~\ref{thm:universal} is full for the source's countable-fibre question
in the lower-local-dimension setting: every finite nonnegative value occurs,
so no finer answer can follow from the stated numerical data and countability
alone.  It does not classify upper local dimensions on arbitrary groups or
settle the source's separate higher-dimensional boundary question.

The run indexes were searched for arXiv:2502.18337, countable representation
fibres, lower local dimension under convolution, and scale-wise bad-centre
conditions.  Bounded web searches on August 9, 2026 used the exact source
sentence, title, arXiv id, and combinations of ``local dimension,''
``convolution,'' ``countable fibre,'' and ``universality.''  They found the
source and Prandi's 2026 thesis~\cite{Prandi}, but no later resolution or
matching construction.  The thesis's subsequent ``addition of measures''
chapter concerns the ordinary sum $\mu+\nu$; its convolution chapter retains
the source theorem and does not answer the countable-fibre question.  Novelty
confidence is positive but remains subject to expert bibliographic review.

Human review should focus on the off-diagonal tail lemma, the every-scale
lower bound in the $\beta=0$ construction, and the endpoint beta-integral for
$\beta>1$.  The recommended verdict is \emph{candidate full, likely valid}.
