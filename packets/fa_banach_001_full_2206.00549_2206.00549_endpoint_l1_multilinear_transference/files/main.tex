\section{Source and exact question}

The source is M. Caspers, A. Krishnaswamy-Usha, and G. Vos,
\emph{Multilinear transference of Fourier and Schur multipliers acting on
non-commutative \(L_p\)-spaces}, arXiv:2206.00549v3, published in
\emph{Canadian Journal of Mathematics} 75 (2023), 1986--2006
\cite{CKV}.  The question occurs in Remark 3.3 on page 11 of the locally
compiled arXiv source release.

\paragraph{Transcription.}
Fix \(1\le i\le n\).  If \(p_i=p=1\) and \(p_j=\infty\) for every
\(j\ne i\), does the Fourier-to-Schur transference theorem (Theorem 3.1 of
\cite{CKV}) still hold?  The authors identify three unavailable endpoint uses:
the limit in their equation (3.8) at index \(i\), and two further uses of
Proposition 3.9 inside the proof of CJKM Lemma 4.6 \cite{CJKM}.

\section{Definitions and statement}

Let \(G\) be a locally compact, second-countable, unimodular group, let
\(\LG\) be its group von Neumann algebra, and let \(L_q(\LG)\) be the
noncommutative \(L_q\)-space for the Plancherel trace.  Given
\(\phi\in C_b(G^n)\), put
\[
 \widetilde\phi(s_0,\ldots,s_n)
 =\phi(s_0s_1^{-1},s_1s_2^{-1},\ldots,s_{n-1}s_n^{-1}).
\]
The associated multilinear Fourier and Schur multipliers are denoted by
\(T_\phi\) and \(M_{\widetilde\phi}\), respectively.  We use exactly the
multiplicative amplifications and the
\((p_1,\ldots,p_n)\)-multiplicatively bounded norms of \cite[Section 2]{CKV}.

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint Fourier-to-Schur transference]\label{thm:main}
Let \(1\le i\le n\), set
\[
 p=p_i=1,\qquad p_j=\infty\quad(j\ne i),
\]
and suppose that \(\phi\in C_b(G^n)\) is the symbol of a
\((p_1,\ldots,p_n)\)-multiplicatively bounded Fourier multiplier.  Then
\(\widetilde\phi\) is the symbol of a
\((p_1,\ldots,p_n)\)-multiplicatively bounded Schur multiplier, and
\[
 \|M_{\widetilde\phi}\|_{(p_1,\ldots,p_n)-\mb}
 \le
 \|T_\phi\|_{(p_1,\ldots,p_n)-\mb}.
\]
Thus the question in Remark 3.3 of \cite{CKV} has an affirmative answer.
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof intuition}

The interior-exponent proof in \cite{CKV} concentrates the Plancherel mass
near the identity by using
\(h_U^2=k_U^*k_U\), where
\(k_U=\mu(U)^{-1/2}\lambda(1_U)\).  Its only endpoint obstruction is an
\(L_1\) asymptotic-intertwining limit for Fourier-algebra multipliers.

At \(p=1\), taking a fractional power of \(h_U\) is unnecessary: the element
\(h_U^2\) already factors as the product of two normalized \(L_2\) elements.
Write a Fourier-algebra symbol as a coefficient
\(m(s)=\langle\pi(s)\xi,\eta\rangle\).  Lift the two \(L_2\) factors to
Hilbert-space-valued columns.  Their product is exactly the multiplier image.
Because their Fourier supports lie in \(Ua^{-1}\) and \(Ub\), strong
continuity lets us freeze the representation vectors at \(a^{-1}\) and
\(b\).  Noncommutative H\"older then makes the trace-norm error tend to zero.
The frozen coefficient is \(m(ab)\).

This one lemma replaces all three unavailable uses named by the source.  In
the two nested uses, every other exponent is infinity, so the intervening
terms are scalars and all commutators vanish.

\section{The endpoint coefficient lemma}

We take Hilbert-space inner products linear in the first variable.  We use the
standard column space \(L_2(\LG;H_c)\): vector-valued Plancherel identifies
\(F\in L_2(G;H)\) with a column \(\Lambda(F)\), with
\[
 \|\Lambda(F)\|_2^2=\int_G\|F(s)\|_H^2\,ds,
 \qquad
 \|X^*Y\|_1\le \|X\|_2\|Y\|_2.                 \tag{4.1}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint coefficient freezing]\label{lem:freeze}
Let \(U\) run through a neighborhood basis of the identity consisting of
relatively compact measurable sets of positive measure.  Set
\[
 f_U=\mu(U)^{-1/2}1_U,\qquad k_U=\lambda(f_U)=u_Uh_U.
\]
For every \(a,b\in G\) and every \(m\in A(G)\),
\[
 \big\|T_m(\lambda_a h_U^2\lambda_b)
      -m(ab)\lambda_a h_U^2\lambda_b\big\|_1\longrightarrow0.       \tag{4.2}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \big\|(T_m-\id)(\lambda_a h_U^2\lambda_b)\big\|_1
 \longrightarrow |m(ab)-1|.                                      \tag{4.3}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a strongly continuous unitary representation \(\pi:G\to\mathcal
U(H)\) and \(\xi,\eta\in H\) such that
\(m(s)=\langle\pi(s)\xi,\eta\rangle\).  Put
\[
 A_U=k_U\lambda_{a^{-1}},\qquad B_U=k_U\lambda_b.
\]
Then \(A_U^*B_U=\lambda_a h_U^2\lambda_b\) and
\(\|A_U\|_2=\|B_U\|_2=1\).

Let \(\alpha_U,\beta_U\in L_2(G)\) be the Fourier densities of \(A_U,B_U\).
Thus \(\alpha_U\) is supported in \(Ua^{-1}\) and \(\beta_U\) in \(Ub\).
Define four vector-valued densities
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_U(s)&=\alpha_U(s)\pi(s)\eta,&
 P_U^0(s)&=\alpha_U(s)\pi(a^{-1})\eta,\\
 Q_U(t)&=\beta_U(t)\pi(t)\xi,&
 Q_U^0(t)&=\beta_U(t)\pi(b)\xi.
\end{aligned}                                                       \tag{4.4}
\]
For compactly supported scalar densities \(\alpha,\beta\), the Fourier
density at \(r\) of
\(\Lambda(\alpha(\cdot)\pi(\cdot)\eta)^*
  \Lambda(\beta(\cdot)\pi(\cdot)\xi)\)
is
\[
 \int_G \overline{\alpha(s)}\,\beta(sr)
 \langle\pi(sr)\xi,\pi(s)\eta\rangle\,ds
 =m(r)(\alpha^**\beta)(r).                                        \tag{4.5}
\]
By \(L_2\) approximation and (4.1), (4.5) remains valid here.  Hence
\[
 \Lambda(P_U)^*\Lambda(Q_U)=T_m(A_U^*B_U).                          \tag{4.6}
\]
The frozen product is
\[
 \Lambda(P_U^0)^*\Lambda(Q_U^0)
 =\langle\pi(b)\xi,\pi(a^{-1})\eta\rangle A_U^*B_U
 =m(ab)A_U^*B_U.                                                    \tag{4.7}
\]

Vector-valued Plancherel and the supports give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\Lambda(P_U)-\Lambda(P_U^0)\|_2
 &\le \sup_{u\in U}
    \|\pi(ua^{-1})\eta-\pi(a^{-1})\eta\|,\\
 \|\Lambda(Q_U)-\Lambda(Q_U^0)\|_2
 &\le \sup_{v\in U}
    \|\pi(vb)\xi-\pi(b)\xi\|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.8}
\]
Both quantities tend to zero by strong continuity.  Applying (4.1) to the
difference of (4.6) and (4.7) proves (4.2).  Finally,
\(\|\lambda_a h_U^2\lambda_b\|_1=\Tr(h_U^2)=\|k_U\|_2^2=1\), and the
reverse triangle inequality applied to (4.2) proves (4.3).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Quantitative form]
The proof bounds the left-hand side of (4.2) by
\[
 \|\xi\|\sup_{u\in U}\|\pi(ua^{-1})\eta-\pi(a^{-1})\eta\|
 +\|\eta\|\sup_{v\in U}\|\pi(vb)\xi-\pi(b)\xi\|.
\]
This makes the endpoint convergence independent of any \(L_1\) operator norm
for a symbol truncated to a small Fourier support.
\end{remark}

\section{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}}

We follow the proof of \cite[Theorem 3.1]{CKV}; all references to equations in
this section are to that paper.  The authors already establish the argument
when \(1<p_j\le\infty\), and Remark 3.3 identifies exactly why their proof
does not include the present vertex of the exponent simplex.  We verify that
Lemma \ref{lem:freeze} supplies every missing limit.

Fix a finite set \(F\subset G\), a finite matrix amplification, and input
matrices in the appropriate Schatten classes.  The source introduces
coordinatewise convolutions \(\phi_k\) and the normalized elements
\(h_U^{2/q}\).  The estimate through equations (3.4)--(3.7) uses only unitary
invariance, H\"older's inequality, and the fact that coordinate convolution
is norm decreasing.  These statements remain valid at \(q=1,\infty\).  The
only issue is convergence of the error term.

\paragraph{The limits (3.8).}
The variables in (3.8) have the form
\[
 y_{j,U}=\lambda_{a_j}h_U^{2/p_j}\lambda_{b_j}.
\]
When \(j\ne i\), \(p_j=\infty\), so \(h_U^{2/p_j}=1\) and the required
identity is exact.  At \(j=i\), the symbol is a left-right translate of the
compactly supported positive-definite function \(\zeta\in A(G)\).  It is
therefore still in \(A(G)\), and (4.3) is exactly the asserted limit (3.8).
Dominated convergence then treats the source's \(C\)-term without change.

\paragraph{The two nested uses.}
For the \(B\)-term, \cite{CKV} specializes the proof of
\cite[Lemma 4.6]{CJKM} to discrete inputs \(x_j=1\).  As observed immediately
before Remark 3.2 of \cite{CKV}, every commutator in CJKM equation (4.13)
vanishes, and the later nested factor \(\widetilde S_j\) in equation (4.14) is
a scalar.  The first unavailable application of CJKM Proposition 3.9 is then
\[
 \|T_m(h_U^{2/p_n})-m(e)h_U^{2/p_n}\|_{p_n}\longrightarrow0.       \tag{5.1}
\]
It is exact if \(p_n=\infty\), while if \(i=n\) it is (4.2) with
\(a=b=e\).

In the telescoping part, the other unavailable expressions have the same
form with a cumulative exponent \(q_j\):
\[
 \|T_m(h_U^{2/q_j})-m(e)h_U^{2/q_j}\|_{q_j}\longrightarrow0.       \tag{5.2}
\]
Since precisely one reciprocal input exponent is nonzero, each \(q_j\) is
either \(1\) or \(\infty\).  The \(q_j=\infty\) cases are exact and the
\(q_j=1\) cases again follow from (4.2).  Thus the nested \(B\)-term tends to
zero.  No fractional-power estimate or SAIN commutator estimate remains.

It follows that the source's finite-set estimate holds at the endpoint:
\[
 \|M_{\widetilde\phi|_{F^{n+1}}}
       (a_1,\ldots,a_n)\|_{S_1}
 \le \|T_\phi\|_{(p_1,\ldots,p_n)-\mb}
       \prod_{j=1}^n\|a_j\|_{S_{p_j}}.                             \tag{5.3}
\]
The same proof at every finite matrix amplification is unchanged: Lemma
\ref{lem:freeze} tensors with matrix algebras, or equivalently is applied to
the finitely many matrix coefficients.  Finally the finite-truncation theorem
\cite[Theorem 2.2]{CKV} turns (5.3) and its amplifications into the asserted
global Schur-multiplier bound.  This proves Theorem \ref{thm:main}.

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

\begin{itemize}
\item The factorization was checked at the Fourier-density level in (4.5), so
there is no appeal to the generally false principle that uniform smallness of
a symbol controls its \(L_1\) multiplier norm.
\item Every nontrivial endpoint term in the source audit has exponent exactly
one.  Every other term has exponent infinity and is exact.  This prevents an
unproved interpolation or fractional-power step from entering the argument.
\item The column proof is stable under finite matrix amplification, which is
essential because the theorem concerns multiplicatively bounded norms.
\item The result is limited to the locally compact, second-countable,
unimodular setting of the source theorem.  It does not claim the corresponding
endpoint theorem for the non-unimodular framework of \cite{Vos}.
\item Bounded novelty checks were performed on 11 August 2026: the run's four
lightweight indexes; exact-id, title, and endpoint-phrase searches across the
locally ingested arXiv source corpus; and exact-id/endpoint searches on arXiv.
The non-unimodular follow-up arXiv:2308.16595 explicitly repeats this endpoint
as unknown in 2023 \cite{Vos}.  No later source found in these bounded searches
claims the unimodular endpoint theorem.  This is evidence, not an exhaustive
literature guarantee.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Specialist review is recommended.  The highest-priority checks are (i) the
column-product identity (4.5), including inner-product orientation, (ii) the
claim that after the \(x_j=1\) specialization the two occurrences inside CJKM
Lemma 4.6 are exactly (5.1)--(5.2), and (iii) the passage to all multiplicative
amplifications.
