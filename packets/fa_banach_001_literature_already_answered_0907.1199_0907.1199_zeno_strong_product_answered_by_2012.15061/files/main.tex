\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.91\textwidth}{\textbf{Status: literature already
answered.}  The later Theorem~1.1 proves the exact strong-operator Zeno
limit under the source hypothesis, and uniformly on bounded time intervals.}}
\end{center}

\section{The source question}

Let $B\ge0$ be self-adjoint on a separable Hilbert space $\mathcal H$, let
$P$ be the orthogonal projection onto a closed subspace $\mathfrak h$, and
assume
\[
 \mathop{\rm dom}(B^{1/2})\cap\mathfrak h
 \quad\hbox{is dense in }\mathfrak h.
\]
Let $C$ be the self-adjoint operator on $\mathfrak h$ associated with
$\mathfrak k(h,k)=(B^{1/2}h,B^{1/2}k)$.  In its introductory survey,
arXiv:0907.1199 proves only time-averaged convergence and states on PDF
page~5 that the convergence question in the strong topology remains open:
\[
 \left(Pe^{-itB/n}P\right)^n h\stackrel{?}{\longrightarrow}e^{-itC}h
 \qquad(h\in\mathfrak h).
\]
The particular admissible function at issue is $\phi(x)=e^{-ix}$
\cite{EN09}.

\section{The later answer and notation match}

Theorem~1.1 of arXiv:2012.15061 (statement beginning on PDF page~1) says:
if $H\ge0$ is self-adjoint, $P$ is orthogonal, and $H^{1/2}P$ is densely
defined, then
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\left(Pe^{-\varepsilon itH/n}P\right)^n f
 =e^{-\varepsilon itH_P}Pf,
 \qquad
 H_P=(H^{1/2}P)^*(H^{1/2}P),\quad \varepsilon=\pm1,
\]
in Hilbert-space norm for every $f\in\mathcal H$, uniformly for $t$ in
every bounded real interval \cite{EI20}.

Set $H=B$.  Since
\[
 \mathop{\rm dom}(H^{1/2}P)
 =\mathfrak h^\perp\oplus
   \bigl(\mathop{\rm dom}(B^{1/2})\cap\mathfrak h\bigr),
\]
the later density hypothesis is exactly the source hypothesis.  The closed
form of $H_P$ is $f\mapsto\|B^{1/2}Pf\|^2$, so under
$\mathcal H=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak h^\perp$ one has
$H_P=C\oplus0$.  Taking $f=h\in\mathfrak h$ and $\varepsilon=1$ therefore
gives exactly
\[
 \boxed{\left(Pe^{-itB/n}P\right)^n h\longrightarrow e^{-itC}h}
\]
strongly, with the stronger uniformity in bounded $t$-intervals.

The later authors explicitly describe strong-operator convergence as the
question left after their earlier $L^2_{\rm loc}$ result and state that
answering it is the aim of the paper.  This is therefore a known later
resolution, not a new proof produced by this run.
