\section{Source problem and result}

Fix $1<p<\infty$ and $p\ne2$.  Arhancet and Le Merdy~\cite{ALM} ask whether
every $p$-completely polynomially bounded operator
$T:\Lp(\Omega)\to\Lp(\Omega)$ admits a \emph{loose dilation}: do there exist
an $L^p$-space $Y$, an isomorphism $U:Y\to Y$ with
\[
 \sup_{n\in\mathbb Z}\|U^n\|<\infty,
\]
and bounded maps $J:\Lp(\Omega)\to Y$, $Q:Y\to\Lp(\Omega)$ such that
\[
 T^n=QU^nJ\qquad(n\ge0)?
\]
Such an operator is automatically power bounded.  We prove the desired
conclusion under quasi-compactness, with $U$ an onto isometry.  Recall that
$T$ is quasi-compact when its essential spectral radius is less than one.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $1<p<\infty$ and let $T:\Lp(\Omega)\to\Lp(\Omega)$ be quasi-compact and
power bounded.  Then $T$ admits a loose dilation for which the dilating
operator is an onto isometry.  Consequently, the open problem in
\cite{ALM} has an affirmative answer for every quasi-compact
$p$-completely polynomially bounded operator.
\end{theorem}

The proof separates the exponentially stable part from the finite-dimensional
peripheral part.  We first record the stable construction.

\section{An explicit shift dilation of a summable stable part}

Let $X=\Lp(\Omega)$, let $P\in B(X)$ be a projection commuting with $T$, and
put $X_s=PX$.  Write $p'=p/(p-1)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:stable}
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:summable}
 \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|T^kP\|^{p'}<\infty,
\end{equation}
then the restriction of $T$ to $X_s$ has an isometric loose dilation inside
$\ell^p(\mathbb Z;X)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The space $Y_s=\ell^p(\mathbb Z;X)$ is itself an $L^p$-space.  Let $V$ be the
bilateral right shift,
\[
 (Vy)_k=y_{k-1}\qquad(k\in\mathbb Z).
\]
It is an onto isometry.  Define
\[
 J_sx=(\ldots,0,Px,0,\ldots),
\]
with $Px$ in coordinate zero, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qstable}
 Q_s(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}T^kP y_k.
\end{equation}
The series converges absolutely in norm after scalar H\"older inequality:
\[
 \sum_{k\ge0}\|T^kP y_k\|
 \le
 \left(\sum_{k\ge0}\|T^kP\|^{p'}\right)^{1/p'}
 \left(\sum_{k\ge0}\|y_k\|^p\right)^{1/p}.
\]
Thus $Q_s$ is bounded.  Since $V^nJ_sx$ has $Px$ in coordinate $n$,
\[
 Q_sV^nJ_sx=T^nPx\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Peripheral spectral part}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:peripheral}
Suppose $T$ is quasi-compact and power bounded.  There is a bounded spectral
projection $P_u$ commuting with $T$ such that $X_u=P_uX$ is finite-dimensional,
$T|_{X_u}$ is similar to a diagonal onto isometry on some $\ell^p_r$, and
the complementary restriction $T|_{(I-P_u)X}$ has spectral radius strictly
less than one.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The quasi-compact spectral decomposition says that the spectrum outside any
circle strictly larger than the essential spectral radius consists of
finitely many isolated eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicity.  Hence
the peripheral spectrum $\sigma(T)\cap\mathbb T$ is finite and its Riesz
spectral subspace $X_u$ is finite-dimensional.  Power boundedness rules out
nontrivial Jordan blocks at eigenvalues of modulus one.  Choosing an
eigenbasis identifies $T|_{X_u}$ with
\[
 D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)
 \quad\text{on }\ell^p_r,
 \qquad |\lambda_j|=1,
\]
which is an onto isometry.  The complementary spectrum is a compact subset
of the open unit disc, so the complementary spectral radius is less than
one.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Take $P_u$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:peripheral}, put $P_s=I-P_u$, and choose an
isomorphism $S:X_u\to\ell^p_r$ satisfying
\[
 ST|_{X_u}=DS.
\]
Since the spectral radius of $T|_{P_sX}$ is less than one, there are
$C\ge1$ and $0<\rho<1$ such that
\[
 \|T^kP_s\|\le C\rho^k\qquad(k\ge0).
\]
In particular, condition~\eqref{eq:summable} holds.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:stable} to obtain $Y_s$, $V$, $J_s$, and $Q_s$.

Let
\[
 Y=\ell^p_r\oplus_pY_s,
 \qquad U=D\oplus V.
\]
This is an $L^p$-space and $U$ is an onto isometry.  Define
\[
 Jx=(SP_ux,J_sx),
 \qquad
 Q(z,y)=S^{-1}z+Q_sy.
\]
Both maps are bounded.  For every $n\ge0$,
\begin{align*}
 QU^nJx
 &=S^{-1}D^nSP_ux+Q_sV^nJ_sx\\
 &=T^nP_ux+T^nP_sx=T^nx.
\end{align*}
Thus $(Y,U,J,Q)$ is the required loose dilation.
\end{proof}

In particular, every power-bounded operator on a finite-dimensional
$L^p$-space admits such a dilation, whether or not it is Ritt.

\section{Boundary of the argument}

The stable lemma applies more generally whenever the powers on a complemented
invariant part have $\ell^{p'}$-summable operator norms, and the peripheral
part can be dilated separately.  Quasi-compactness supplies exactly this
spectral gap.

For an arbitrary power-bounded $T$, every Abel regularization $rT$ with
$0<r<1$ satisfies the stable summability condition.  However, the norm of
the map in \eqref{eq:Qstable} is controlled by
\[
 \left(\sum_{k\ge0}r^{kp'}\|T^k\|^{p'}\right)^{1/p'},
\]
which generally diverges as $r\uparrow1$.  Thus the construction does not
give uniformly bounded dilations from which one could take an ultraproduct
limit.  Removing this blow-up would require genuinely using
$p$-complete polynomial boundedness, not power boundedness alone.

The natural Hilbert-space proof would extend and factor the completely
bounded polynomial calculus.  For $p\ne2$, a general
Arveson--Wittstock--Hahn--Banach extension theorem for $p$-operator spaces is
false~\cite{Lee}.  No special extension result sufficient for the analytic
shift algebra was located in the bounded literature search.  This is why the
present theorem is a scoped answer rather than a claimed general solution.
