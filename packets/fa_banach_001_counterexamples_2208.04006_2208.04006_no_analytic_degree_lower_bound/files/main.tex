\section{Source question}

The source is Armin Rainer, \emph{Quantitative tame properties of
differentiable functions with controlled derivatives}, arXiv:2208.04006v2
and Nonlinear Analysis 237 (2023), 113372 \cite{source}.

For $\varepsilon>0$, the analytic degree $d_f(2\varepsilon)$ of a function
holomorphic on $D_{1+2\varepsilon}$ is the least $d\geq0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:analytic-degree}
 \|f\|_I\leq
 \left(\frac{4|I|}{|E|}\right)^d\|f\|_E
\end{equation}
for every interval $I$ obtained by intersecting a real affine line in
$\mathbb C$ with $D_1$ and every measurable $E\subseteq I$ of positive
length.

The source applies Cauchy's estimates and takes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight}
 M_0=\frac{\|f\|_{D_{1+\varepsilon}}}{\varepsilon},
 \qquad \mu_j=\frac{Cj}{\varepsilon}\quad(j\geq1),
\end{equation}
where $C>0$ is a fixed conversion constant.  It proves
\[
 d_f(2\varepsilon)\leq10\fd_{2\mu^{|\infty}}(\varepsilon)
\]
and then states on printed page 25:
\begin{quote}
``We do not know if there is a lower bound for $d_f(2\varepsilon)$ in terms
of $\fd_{2\mu^{|\infty}}(\varepsilon)$.''
\end{quote}

As stated, the answer is no: the weight degree records the Cauchy envelope,
but it does not record the distance of $f$ from a nonzero constant.

\section{Counterexample}

Let
\[
 j_0(\varepsilon)=\left\lceil\log\varepsilon^{-1}\right\rceil_{\mathbb N},
 \qquad
 D(\varepsilon)=\fd_{2\mu^{|\infty}}(\varepsilon)
\]
for the weight in \eqref{eq:weight}.  Directly from the source definition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dformula}
 D(\varepsilon)=\sup\left\{N\in\mathbb N:
 \frac{\varepsilon}{2C}
 \sum_{j=j_0(\varepsilon)+1}^{N}\frac1j<e\right\}.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dlower}
 D(\varepsilon)\geq j_0(\varepsilon).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:no-lower}
There is no positive lower bound for $d_f(2\varepsilon)$ depending only on
$D(\varepsilon)$, even if $f$ is required to be nonconstant and entire.
More precisely, for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$ with $\varepsilon<1$ there
are nonconstant entire functions $f_\delta$ satisfying the Cauchy weight
\eqref{eq:weight} such that
\[
 d_{f_\delta}(2\varepsilon)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{as }\delta\downarrow0,
\]
while $D(\varepsilon)$ is unchanged.  Moreover, these fixed degree values
can be chosen arbitrarily large.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $0<\varepsilon<1$ and, for $0<\delta<1$, set
\[
 f_\delta(z)=1+\delta z.
\]
This function is nonconstant and entire.  On the closed unit disk,
\[
 1-\delta\leq |f_\delta(z)|\leq1+\delta.
\]
Consequently, for every admissible $I$ and $E$ in
\eqref{eq:analytic-degree},
\[
 \frac{\|f_\delta\|_I}{\|f_\delta\|_E}
 \leq\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}.
\]
Since $E\subseteq I$ implies $4|I|/|E|\geq4$, the exponent
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deltabound}
 d_\delta=\frac{\log((1+\delta)/(1-\delta))}{\log4}
\end{equation}
makes \eqref{eq:analytic-degree} valid for every $I,E$.  Hence
\[
 0\leq d_{f_\delta}(2\varepsilon)\leq d_\delta
 \longrightarrow0.
\]

For each $\delta$, take $M_0$ and $\mu_j$ exactly as in
\eqref{eq:weight}.  The number $M_0$ changes with $f_\delta$, but the
sequence $\mu_j=Cj/\varepsilon$ does not.  The degree
$D(\varepsilon)$ depends only on this sequence and on $\varepsilon$, so it
is independent of $\delta$.  Thus any proposed lower bound
\[
 d_f(2\varepsilon)\geq L(D(\varepsilon))
\]
valid for all such nonconstant functions must satisfy
$L(D(\varepsilon))=0$.

Finally choose $\varepsilon_n=e^{-n}$.  Then
$j_0(\varepsilon_n)=n$, so \eqref{eq:Dlower} gives
$D(\varepsilon_n)\geq n\to\infty$.  The preceding fixed-$\varepsilon$
argument forces $L(D(\varepsilon_n))=0$ on this unbounded set of degree
values.  No positive lower bound depending only on the Cauchy-weight degree
can exist.
\end{proof}

If constant functions are allowed, the obstruction is even more immediate:
$f\equiv1$ has analytic degree zero for every $\varepsilon$, while the same
weight $\mu_j=Cj/\varepsilon$ gives the finite degree
\eqref{eq:Dformula}, which becomes unbounded as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
The family $1+\delta z$ shows that excluding constants does not repair the
question.

\section{Interpretation, checks, and novelty}

The counterexample does not contradict the source's upper bound.  That bound
uses an envelope of all derivatives and is intentionally uniform over every
function controlled by the chosen weight.  Such an envelope can be much
larger than the actual analytic complexity of a particular function.

A meaningful reverse comparison would need additional normalization, for
example a quantitative lower bound on the oscillation of $f$ relative to its
supremum, or a minimal function-adapted weight rather than the universal
Cauchy weight.  No such condition appears in the question as printed.

The bundled checker evaluates \eqref{eq:deltabound} along
$\varepsilon_n=e^{-n}$ and confirms $D(\varepsilon_n)\geq n$ from the exact
definition.  The proof itself is elementary and has no computational
dependency.

On 11 August 2026, the run registry and all cheap indexes were searched for
arXiv:2208.04006 and for the exact analytic-degree lower-bound phrase.
Bounded primary-source searches found the arXiv paper, its open-access 2023
publication, and no later answer or normalization of the question.  The
published version retains the same statement.

Human review should check that ``in terms of'' is intended to mean a bound
uniform over functions for the Cauchy-derived weight \eqref{eq:weight}.  Under
that literal and natural reading, the nonconstant counterexample is decisive.
