\section*{Status and exact source question}

This is a \texttt{literature\_implied\_answer (partial subcase)} packet.
Fan--Fan--Liao--Shi end Section~6.4 of \cite{FFLS}, on printed and PDF
page~23, with the statement
\begin{quote}
``Fuglede's conjecture in $\mathbb Q_p^2$ is open, even under the compact
open assumption.''
\end{quote}
The one-dimensional assertion in the paper's title is proved in that same
paper and is not the target here.

Zhou's later Theorem~1 \cite{Zhou}, on PDF page~26, proves that tiling and
spectral sets coincide in
$\mathbb Z_{p^2}\times\mathbb Z_{p^2}$.  The supporting paper does not
identify this as a partial answer to the quoted $p$-adic question.  The
identification is made below through an exact finite-quotient reduction.

\section*{Finite-quotient identification}

Normalize Haar measure by $|\Zp^2|=1$, and use the standard additive
character $\chi$ of $\Qp$ whose kernel is $\Zp$.  Put
\[
 K=\Zp^2,\qquad H=p^mK,\qquad
 \Gm=K/H\simeq(\mathbb Z/p^m\mathbb Z)^2.
\]
For $A\subset\Gm$, choose lifts in $K$ and write
\[
 \Omega_A=A+H.
\]

\begin{proposition}[exact compact-open reduction]\label{prop:reduction}
The set $\Omega_A$ tiles $\Qp^2$ by translations if and only if $A$ tiles
$\Gm$.  It is spectral in $\Qp^2$ if and only if $A$ is spectral in
$\Gm$ for the pairing
\[
 (a,s)\longmapsto \chi\!\left(p^{-m}\langle a,s\rangle\right).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $L$ be a set of representatives for $\Qp^2/K$.  If
$A\oplus B=\Gm$, then
\[
 \Omega_A+(B+L)=\Qp^2
\]
is a disjoint tiling: first choose the unique $K$-coset, then the unique
decomposition modulo $H$.  Conversely, suppose $\Omega_A+T$ tiles
$\Qp^2$.  Every translate $\Omega_A+t$ is contained in $t+K$.
Restrict the tiling to one coset $\ell+K$ and reduce the relevant
translations $t-\ell$ modulo $H$.  Two of them cannot have the same residue,
because the corresponding translates would coincide and overlap on a set of
positive measure.  The resulting residue set $B$ therefore satisfies
$A\oplus B=\Gm$.

For spectrality, note that
\[
 H^\perp=\{\xi:\chi(\langle\xi,h\rangle)=1\text{ for all }h\in H\}
          =p^{-m}K.
\]
Let $\Gamma$ represent $\Qp^2/H^\perp$.  If $S$ is a finite spectrum of
$A$, then
\[
 \Lambda=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
          \bigl(\gamma+p^{-m}S\bigr)                                  \tag{1}
\]
is a spectrum of $\Omega_A$.  Indeed, frequencies belonging to different
$H^\perp$-cosets are orthogonal on every $H$-ball.  Within a fixed coset,
their inner products are
\[
 |H|\sum_{a\in A}
 \chi\!\left(p^{-m}\langle a,s-s'\rangle\right),
\]
which are precisely the finite Fourier inner products on $A$.  Completeness
follows by the Fourier decomposition on the compact group $H$: each
$\gamma$-sector is a copy of the $|A|$-dimensional space $\ell^2(A)$.

Conversely, partition any spectrum $\Lambda$ of $\Omega_A$ into its cosets
modulo $H^\perp$.  The same orthogonal sector decomposition shows that each
coset must contain exactly $|A|$ frequencies.  Fixing one frequency in a
coset, the remaining differences lie in
$H^\perp/K\simeq\Gm$; after removing one common diagonal phase from the
cell vectors, those $|A|$ residues form an orthogonal basis of characters on
$A$.  Thus $A$ is spectral in $\Gm$.
\end{proof}

Every compact-open subset of $\Qp^2$ can, after a translation and a
$p$-adic dilation, be put in the form $\Omega_A$.  Its \emph{depth} is the
least such $m$.  Translation and dilation preserve both sides of Fuglede's
equivalence.

\section*{Partial answer}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:partial}
Let $\Omega\subset\Qp^2$ be compact and open.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\Omega$ has depth at most two, then $\Omega$ is spectral if and
only if it tiles $\Qp^2$.
\item At arbitrary depth $m$, after normalization $\Omega=\Omega_A\subset K$,
tiling implies spectrality when $|A|=p$ or $|A|=p^{2m-1}$.  At the latter
cardinality the converse also holds.
\item At arbitrary depth, the equivalence holds whenever $A$ has a subgroup
tiling complement.  Equivalently for the reverse formulation, it holds when
$A$ has a subgroup spectrum.  This includes all residue sets that are graphs
over either coordinate.
\end{enumerate}
The unrestricted compact-open problem, equivalently the assertion for every
subset of $\Gm$ and every $m\ge3$, remains open.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For (1), represent a depth-at-most-two set at level two and combine
Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} with Zhou's Theorem~1.  For (2), use
Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} and Zhou's Lemmas~11, 13, and 14
\cite[PDF pp.~11--12]{Zhou}.  Lemma~14 says in particular that a spectral set
with $p^{2m}>|A|\ge p^{2m-1}$ must have size $p^{2m-1}$ and tile.

For (3), if $A\oplus H=\Gm$ with $H\le\Gm$, then $A$ is a transversal for
$H$ and the annihilator $H^\perp$, of cardinality $|A|$, is a spectrum of
$A$: its character table is the Fourier table of $\Gm/H$.  Conversely, if a
subgroup $S$ is a spectrum of $A$, orthogonality and $|S|=|A|$ force $A$ to
be a transversal for $S^\perp$, so $A\oplus S^\perp=\Gm$.  A graph over one
coordinate is a transversal for the subgroup in the other coordinate.
\end{proof}

\section*{Why the evident induction does not finish the problem}

Projection $\Gm\to G_{m-1}$ does not preserve tiling as a property of the
set-valued image.  For example, in $(\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z)^2$ take
\[
 A=\{(x,f(x)):x\in\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\},\qquad
 f(4)=1,\quad f(x)=0\ (x\ne4).
\]
This is a tile with vertical subgroup complement, but its image modulo four
has the five points
\[
 (0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(2,0),(3,0),
\]
and cannot tile the $16$-element group $(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2$.
An induction must retain nonconstant fiber multiplicities and their Fourier
phases; the level-$m-1$ set alone loses them.  The general-$m$ lemmas in
\cite{Zhou} control only extremal sizes and do not bridge these intermediate
fiber patterns.

\section*{Provenance, search bounds, and review recommendation}

The run's cheap indexes were searched for arXiv:1512.08904, its title, the
quoted sentence, and the core finite-group terms.  A bounded current
arXiv/web search used ``$\Qp^2$ Fuglede,''
``$\mathbb Z_{p^m}\times\mathbb Z_{p^m}$ spectral tile,'' rank-two
$p$-groups, arXiv:2412.18132, and its companion arXiv:2412.16865.  No full
all-$m$ theorem or counterexample was found.  The strongest direct result was
the 22 March 2026 revision of \cite{Zhou}, whose introduction itself describes
rank-two results as limited and states only the $m=2$ theorem plus the cited
general-$m$ lemmas.

Three upgrade routes were audited: quotient induction (obstructed by the
explicit graph example), subgroup-complement reduction (complete only for
that structural class), and cardinality bootstrapping (leaving intermediate
sizes).  Thus this packet does not claim a full solution.  Human review is
recommended, with primary attention to the converse spectral reduction in
Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}; confidence in the stated partial result is
high.
