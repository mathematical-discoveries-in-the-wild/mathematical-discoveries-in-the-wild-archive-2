\section{Source problem}

Erdos, Kruger, and Nemish prove an optimal local law for a self-adjoint
polynomial once a nilpotent linearization satisfies two uniform conditions in
every $\kappa$-bulk~\cite{EKN}. In the notation of their Assumption~2.12,
\begin{align*}
 \text{(M1)}&\quad \sup\|M(z)\|<\infty,\\
 \text{(M2)}&\quad \sup\|\Lop(z)^{-1}\|<\infty,
\end{align*}
where $M$ solves the Dyson equation for the linearization and
\[
 \Lop[R]=R-M\Sop[R]M.
\]
They verify these conditions for general homogeneous quadratic polynomials and
for symmetrized products, then ask whether an appropriate linearization works
for every self-adjoint polynomial.

\section{Proof intuition}

For $1+x^k$, the source's standard symmetric linearization is a companion
pencil whose coefficient of $x$ is the reversal permutation $A_k$. Multiplying
the pencil by $A_k$ turns it into $xI+C_z$, where the eigenvalues of $-C_z$ are
the $k$ roots of $z-1$. Thus the matrix Dyson solution, after the same reversal,
is obtained by applying the scalar semicircle Stieltjes transform to those
roots.

The vectorized stability operator has eigenvalues
$1-m_{\rm sc}(r_i)m_{\rm sc}(r_j)$. The semicircle identity
$m^2+rm+1=0$ is rigid: a product $m_{\rm sc}(r_i)m_{\rm sc}(r_j)=1$ forces
$r_i=r_j$. Distinctness of the roots then reduces failure to
$m_{\rm sc}(r_i)=\pm1$, hence $r_i=\mp2$, which is a spectral edge. The only
place where roots collide is $z=1$, where the pushforward density is infinite.
The source's $\kappa$-bulk excludes both mechanisms.

\section{Partial theorem}

Let $A_n$ denote the $n\times n$ reversal permutation matrix, let
$J=e_1e_1^T$, and embed $A_{k-1}$ in the lower-right corner by
\[
 D_k=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&A_{k-1}\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[All one-variable monomial degrees]
For every integer $k\ge2$, the standard symmetric linearization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pencil}
 L_k(x)=J-D_k+xA_k
\end{equation}
of $p_k(x)=1+x^k$ satisfies assumptions \textup{(M1)} and \textup{(M2)} of
arXiv:1804.11340 uniformly on every $\kappa$-bulk. Consequently Theorem~2.14
of the source yields the optimal bulk local law for
$p_k(X^{(N)})=I+(X^{(N)})^k$ under its Wigner moment hypotheses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The matrix in~\eqref{eq:pencil} is exactly the basic monomial linearization in
Appendix~A.1 of the source, with constant term added in the $(1,1)$ entry.
Its Schur complement is $1+x^k$, and the source proves that standard
linearizations of this form are nilpotent.

Fix $z\in\C_+$, set $w=z-1$, and define
\[
 C_z=((1-z)J-D_k)A_k.
\]
Since $A_k^2=I$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:companion}
 (L_k(x)-zJ)A_k=xI+C_z,
 \qquad \det(xI+C_z)=x^k-w.
\end{equation}
For $w\ne0$, let $r_1,\ldots,r_k$ be the distinct roots of $r^k=w$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:companion} says that $C_z$ is diagonalizable with
eigenvalues $-r_j$.

Let $s$ be a standard semicircular element with law $\mu_{\rm sc}$. The source's
construction of the Dyson solution gives
\[
 M(z)=\int_{-2}^2 (L_k(x)-zJ)^{-1}\,d\mu_{\rm sc}(x).
\]
Writing
\[
 m_{\rm sc}(r):=\int_{-2}^2\frac{d\mu_{\rm sc}(x)}{x-r},
\]
functional calculus in~\eqref{eq:companion} shows that the eigenvalues of
$M(z)A_k$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eigenvalues}
 m_j=m_{\rm sc}(r_j),\qquad j=1,\ldots,k.
\end{equation}
The chosen semicircle branch satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scidentity}
 m_{\rm sc}(r)^2+r\,m_{\rm sc}(r)+1=0,
 \qquad r=-\left(m_{\rm sc}(r)+m_{\rm sc}(r)^{-1}\right).
\end{equation}

We first establish (M2). In the present one-variable pencil the self-energy is
$\Sop[R]=A_kRA_k$. Both $A_k$ and $M$ are symmetric. With column
vectorization,
\[
 \operatorname{vec}(M A_k R A_k M)
 =\bigl((A_kM)^T\otimes M A_k\bigr)\operatorname{vec}R
 =\bigl(MA_k\otimes MA_k\bigr)\operatorname{vec}R.
\]
Therefore $\Lop$ is singular precisely when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product}
 m_i m_j=1
\end{equation}
for some $i,j$. If~\eqref{eq:product} holds, then
\eqref{eq:scidentity} gives
\[
 r_j=-\left(m_j+m_j^{-1}\right)
     =-\left(m_i^{-1}+m_i\right)=r_i.
\]
When $w\ne0$ the roots are distinct, so $i=j$. Then $m_i^2=1$, and
\eqref{eq:scidentity} yields $r_i=\mp2$. Hence
$w=r_i^k=(\mp2)^k$, a spectral-edge value. For $\Im z>0$ this is impossible,
and for a real boundary value it is outside every $\kappa$-bulk.

It remains to justify uniformity and (M1). The limiting density of
$p_k(s)=1+s^k$ is the elementary pushforward
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
 \rho_k(E)=
 \sum_{\substack{x\in(-2,2)\\1+x^k=E}}
 \frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}{2\pi k|x|^{k-1}}.
\end{equation}
Thus a fixed $\kappa$-bulk stays a positive distance from the edges and from
$E=1$, where the density diverges. In particular, $|w|$ is bounded below and
the roots $r_j$ remain uniformly separated when $0\le\Im z\le1$ and
$\Re z$ lies in that bulk. Diagonalizing $C_z$ in~\eqref{eq:companion}, the
boundary values in~\eqref{eq:eigenvalues} are uniformly bounded, and so is
$M(z)$. This proves (M1) for $0<\Im z\le1$.

On the same compact parameter set, the preceding product argument shows that
$\Lop(z)$ is never singular, including at the boundary. Continuity and compactness
therefore give a uniform bound for $\|\Lop(z)^{-1}\|$. For $\Im z\ge1$, the
source's general nilpotent-resolvent estimate bounds $M$. Moreover, direct
adjugate expansion of~\eqref{eq:companion} shows that $M(z)A_k$ has a finite
limit as $\Im z\to\infty$: the denominator is $x^k-w$, while every cofactor is
affine in the rank-one parameter $w$. All its eigenvalues
$m_{\rm sc}(r_j)$ tend to zero, so this limiting matrix is nilpotent. Hence
$I-MA_k\otimes MA_k$ has an invertible limit. Enlarging the constant over the
remaining compact strip completes both uniform bounds. Thus (M1)--(M2) hold
exactly in the sense of Assumption~2.12.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification report}

The proof was checked in four independent ways.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Direct symbolic inversion gives
$(L_k(x)-zJ)^{-1}_{11}=(1+x^k-z)^{-1}$.
\item For $2\le k\le12$, the included SymPy check confirms
$\det((L_k(x)-zJ)A_k)=x^k-z+1$ exactly.
\item The vectorization identity reduces the $k^2$-dimensional stability
determinant to the pairwise products in~\eqref{eq:product}.
\item Numerical root checks for degrees $3,4,7,10$ at three nonreal spectral
parameters confirm the semicircle quadratic identity and find no unit product.
\end{enumerate}
The computational checks do not prove the all-degree theorem; the companion
factorization and identity~\eqref{eq:scidentity} do.

\section{Scope and limitations}

This proves the source's desired stability for the infinite family of
one-variable monomials, but not for arbitrary sums or genuinely
noncommutative multivariable polynomials. The remaining difficulty is that a
general linearization need not reduce to a single companion matrix whose
stability eigenvalues are scalar semicircle transforms of distinct roots.

For a single Wigner matrix, the local law for $1+X^k$ can also be transferred
from the usual Wigner local law by spectral mapping away from critical values.
The contribution here is therefore the exact verification of the source's
linearization conditions (M1)--(M2), not a previously inaccessible
one-variable universality phenomenon.

\section{Bounded novelty check}

The search through 11 August 2026 covered the run registry and packet indexes;
the exact open sentence; arXiv:1804.11340 and its title combined with
\emph{general polynomial}, \emph{monomial}, \emph{minimal linearization},
\emph{stability operator}, and \emph{bulk local law}; and later arXiv work on
polynomials of Wigner matrices. The search found later edge results for
multivariate quadratic polynomials, but no exact all-degree monomial
verification of (M1)--(M2). Novelty confidence is moderate-to-low because the
companion-matrix mechanism may be known folklore; mathematical validity does
not depend on novelty.

\section{Conclusion and review recommendation}

The full arbitrary-polynomial question remains open, but every monomial degree
admits the desired stable linearization. Promotion as a likely valid partial
result is recommended after checking the compactness argument and the source's
linearization sign convention.
