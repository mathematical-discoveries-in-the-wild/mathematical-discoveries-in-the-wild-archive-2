\section*{Original question}

On page 4, immediately after Theorem 2 of Glazer--Mikulincer, \emph{Anti-concentration of polynomials: dimension-free covariance bounds and decay of Fourier coefficients}, arXiv:2108.04268, the authors ask whether the same conclusion holds for general isotropic log-concave measures, perhaps with suitable symmetries \cite{GM22}.  The conclusion in question is a dimension-free estimate
\[
  \int_{\mathbb R^n} f^2\,d\mu \ge C_d\,\operatorname{coeff}_d(f)^2
\]
for every degree-$d$ homogeneous polynomial.

\section*{Decisive later counterexample}

Page 38, Section 5.1 and Proposition 5.1 of the same authors' later paper, \emph{Anti-concentration of polynomials: $L^p$ balls and symmetric measures}, arXiv:2603.22664, explicitly show that lack of symmetry produces pathological polynomials \cite{GM26}.  Let $\nu_n$ be the uniform probability measure on the isotropic Euclidean ball in $\mathbb R^n$, put $\mu_n=\nu_n\otimes\nu_n$, and define on $\mathbb R^{2n}$
\[
 f_n(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}
 \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i^2-\sum_{i=n+1}^{2n}x_i^2\right).
\]
The product measure $\mu_n$ is isotropic and log-concave, while $f_n$ is homogeneous quadratic and
\[
 \operatorname{coeff}_2(f_n)^2
 =2n\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}\right)^2=1.
\]
The two radial summands have opposite equal means and are independent.  Proposition 5.1 computes
\[
 \int f_n^2\,d\mu_n=\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_n}(f_n)
 =\frac{4}{n+4}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently there is no positive dimension-free $C_2$, and therefore the unrestricted general-isotropic question has a negative answer.

\section*{Scope and provenance}

The counterexample does not resolve every possible ``suitable symmetries'' refinement.  The 2026 paper makes one such refinement precise by imposing signed-permutation ($H_n$) invariance and states its all-degree $L^2$ version as Conjecture 1.4.  This packet records only the full negative answer for arbitrary isotropic log-concave measures.  The supporting authors cite the earlier paper and deliberately present the construction as showing that the symmetry assumption rules out pathological behavior, so this is an explicit later-literature resolution rather than a new result of the run.
