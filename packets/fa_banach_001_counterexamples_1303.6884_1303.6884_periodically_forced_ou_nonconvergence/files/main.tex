\section{Question and counterexample}

For a time-inhomogeneous diffusion
\[
 dX_t=\sigma(t,X_t)\,dB_t+b(t,X_t)\,dt,
\]
Cattiaux and Guillin assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HCK}
 |\sigma(t,x)-\sigma(t,y)|_{\rm HS}^2
 +2\langle b(t,x)-b(t,y),x-y\rangle
 \le -K'(t)|x-y|^2.
\end{equation}
In Remark 4.5 they observe that if $K(t)\to+\infty$ and
$\int_0^\infty e^{-K(s)}ds<\infty$, the transition laws $P(t,x,\cdot)$ are
tight, but state that they do not know whether this family converges weakly
\cite[p.~29]{CG2013}:

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_question_crop.png}
\caption{The convergence question in Remark 4.5 of \cite[p.~29]{CG2013}.}
\end{figure}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:counterexample}
The stated hypotheses do not imply weak convergence.  In dimension one, the
smooth diffusion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sde}
             dX_t=dB_t-\frac12X_t\,dt+\sin(t)\,dt,
             \qquad X_0=x,
\end{equation}
satisfies \eqref{eq:HCK} with $K(t)=t$ and has a tight, nonconvergent family
of laws.  More precisely,
\[
 \mathcal L(X_{2\pi n})\Longrightarrow\N(-4/5,1),
 \qquad
 \mathcal L(X_{(2n+1)\pi})\Longrightarrow\N(4/5,1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\section{Verification}

Here $\sigma\equiv1$ and $b(t,u)=-u/2+\sin t$.  Therefore, for every
$u,v\in\R$,
\[
 |\sigma(t,u)-\sigma(t,v)|^2
 +2(b(t,u)-b(t,v))(u-v)=-|u-v|^2.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:HCK} holds with equality for $K'(t)=1$, so one may take
$K(t)=t$.  Both remaining assumptions are immediate:
\[
 K(t)\longrightarrow+\infty,
 \qquad
 \int_0^\infty e^{-K(s)}\,ds=\int_0^\infty e^{-s}\,ds=1.
\]
The coefficients are globally Lipschitz and smooth, so all standard
regularity and nonexplosion requirements in the source are satisfied.

Solving \eqref{eq:sde} by variation of constants gives
\[
 X_t=e^{-t/2}x+\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)/2}\sin s\,ds
                 +\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)/2}\,dB_s.
\]
Consequently $X_t$ is Gaussian with variance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance}
 v(t)=\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)}ds=1-e^{-t}\longrightarrow1
\end{equation}
and mean
\begin{align}\label{eq:mean}
 m_x(t)
 &=e^{-t/2}x+\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)/2}\sin s\,ds\notag\\
 &=\frac25\sin t-\frac45\cos t+\left(x+\frac45\right)e^{-t/2}.
\end{align}
The means are uniformly bounded and the variances are at most one; hence the
family $\{\mathcal L(X_t):t\ge0\}$ is tight.  But
\[
 m_x(2\pi n)\longrightarrow-\frac45,
 \qquad
 m_x((2n+1)\pi)\longrightarrow\frac45.
\]
Combining these limits with \eqref{eq:variance} gives the two distinct
Gaussian subsequential limits in Theorem~\ref{thm:counterexample}.  The full
family therefore cannot converge weakly. \qed

\section{Mechanism and scope}

The obstruction is structural: the curvature condition \eqref{eq:HCK}
depends only on drift differences and is completely insensitive to an
additive forcing $a(t)$.  Strict contraction erases the initial condition but
does not erase a persistent periodic motion of the common center.  Any claim
of convergence under this framework therefore needs an additional condition
controlling the absolute drift, for example convergence of the forcing or of
an instantaneous equilibrium center.

The counterexample answers exactly the universal question in Remark 4.5; it
does not contradict the source's functional inequalities, all of which hold
uniformly for the translated Gaussian laws above.  The calculation is exact
and has no computational dependency.  The run indexes and exact arXiv id and
question wording were checked; no pre-existing packet or duplicate result was
found.  Because the mechanism is elementary, an uncatalogued prior example is
possible even though no direct later answer was located in the bounded audit.
