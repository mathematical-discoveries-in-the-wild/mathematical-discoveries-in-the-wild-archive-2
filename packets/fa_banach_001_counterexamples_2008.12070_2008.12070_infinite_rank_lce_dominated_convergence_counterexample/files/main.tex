\noindent
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\textwidth}
\textbf{Status: candidate full counterexample, likely valid.}
There exist a probability space, a centered random variable
\(V\in L^2(\Omega;\ellTwo)\) whose covariance has infinite rank, scalar
\(U_k\in L^2(\Omega)\), and \(Y\in L^2(\Omega)\) such that
\[
 U_k\longrightarrow0\quad\text{almost surely},
 \qquad |U_k|\le Y\quad(k\ge1),
\]
but
\[
 \mathbb E^{\cA}[U_k\mid V]\not\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Thus the infinite-rank extension asked for in Remark 4.6 of Klebanov,
Sprungk, and Sullivan is false. The construction realizes a graph-subspace
orthogonal projection as an LCE and feeds it the tails of a classical
Menshov orthogonal series that converges in \(L^2\) but diverges pointwise.
\end{minipage}}

\section{The exact open problem}

For scalar \(U\in L^2(\Omega)\) and Hilbert-valued
\(V\in L^2(\Omega;\mathcal H)\), the linear conditional expectation (LCE)
is the \(L^2\)-orthogonal projection
\[
 \E^{\cA}[U\mid V]
 =P_{\overline{\{b+\langle h,V\rangle:b\in\R,\ h\in\mathcal H\}}}U.
 \tag{1}
\]
The source proves an \(L^2\)-dominated convergence theorem when the
covariance \(C_V\) has finite rank. It asks whether the result remains true
at infinite rank under the stronger hypothesis
\[
 U_k\to U\ \text{a.s.},\qquad |U_k|\le Y\ \text{for one }Y\in L^2.
 \tag{2}
\]
The complete source page containing the question is reproduced below
\cite{KSS}.

\section{The only external ingredient}

The pointwise pathology needed below is classical.

\begin{lemma}[Menshov]\label{lem:menshov}
There are a real orthonormal system \((\varphi_n)_{n\ge1}\) in
\(L^2([0,1])\) and real numbers \((c_n)_{n\ge1}\) such that
\[
 \sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n^2<\infty,
 \qquad
 \sum_{n=1}^N c_n\varphi_n(x)
 \quad\text{fails to converge as }N\to\infty
\]
for every \(x\in[0,1]\), after changes on a null set if necessary.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Menshov proved that for every positive
\(W(n)=o((\log n)^2)\), there are an orthonormal system and coefficients
whose orthogonal series diverges everywhere although
\(\sum_n c_n^2W(n)<\infty\) \cite{Menshov}. Take \(W\equiv1\).
\end{proof}

We will also need mean-zero functions. Let
\[
 X_1=[0,1]\times\{-1,1\},
 \qquad m_1=\lambda\otimes\tfrac12(\delta_{-1}+\delta_1),
\]
and set
\[
 \psi_n(x,\varepsilon)=\varepsilon\varphi_n(x).
 \tag{3}
\]
Then \((\psi_n)\) is orthonormal in \(L^2(X_1,m_1)\), every \(\psi_n\)
has mean zero, and the series \(\sum c_n\psi_n\) diverges at every point
where the Menshov series does.

Nevertheless, Riesz--Fischer gives an \(L^2\) sum \(F\) of this series. If
\[
 r_k=F-\sum_{n<k}c_n\psi_n,
 \tag{4}
\]
then \(r_k\) is the \(L^2\) tail, but \(r_k\not\to0\) almost everywhere;
otherwise the finite partial sums would converge pointwise to \(F\).

\section{A graph projection that destroys dominated convergence}

Let \(X_0=[0,1]\) with Lebesgue measure. Choose pairwise disjoint intervals
\(I_n\subset X_0\), split each into equal halves
\(I_n=I_n^+\mathbin{\dot\cup}I_n^-\), and define
\[
 \delta_n=|I_n|^{-1/2}
 \bigl(\mathbf1_{I_n^+}-\mathbf1_{I_n^-}\bigr).
 \tag{5}
\]
The functions \(\delta_n\) are orthonormal, mean zero, and have disjoint
supports. Define the isometry
\[
 T:\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\delta_n\}
 \longrightarrow
 \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\psi_n\},
 \qquad T\delta_n=\psi_n.
 \tag{6}
\]

Form the disjoint-union probability space
\[
 (\Omega,\mathbb P)=\tfrac12(X_0,m_0)\ \dot\cup\
                    \tfrac12(X_1,m_1).
\]
Thus \(L^2(\Omega)\) is the half-weighted direct sum
\(L^2(X_0)\oplus L^2(X_1)\). Put
\[
 s_n=(\delta_n,\psi_n),
 \qquad
 \cS=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{s_n:n\ge1\}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Because of the half weights, \((s_n)\) is an orthonormal system. The
subspace \(\cS\) is the graph of \(T\) with this normalization, and direct
minimization gives
\begin{equation}
 P_{\cS}(f,0)=\tfrac12(f,Tf)
 \quad\text{for }f\in\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\delta_n\}.
 \tag{8}
\end{equation}
Indeed, if \(f=\sum a_n\delta_n\), then
\(\langle(f,0),s_n\rangle_{L^2(\Omega)}=a_n/2\), which proves (8).

Now define the disjoint-support tails
\[
 f_k=\sum_{n\ge k}c_n\delta_n,
 \qquad U_k=(f_k,0).
 \tag{9}
\]
These are genuine pointwise sums: at each \(x\in X_0\), at most one term is
nonzero. In particular, \(f_k(x)\to0\) for every \(x\) outside the interval
endpoints. Moreover,
\[
 Y=\left(\sum_{n\ge1}|c_n|\,|\delta_n|,0\right)
 \tag{10}
\]
dominates every \(U_k\), and disjointness gives
\[
 \|Y\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2
 =\tfrac12\sum_{n\ge1}c_n^2<\infty.
 \tag{11}
\]
Thus \(U_k\to0\) almost surely under one square-integrable dominator.

On the other hand, \(Tf_k=r_k\) as \(L^2\) equivalence classes: both are the
tail of the same orthogonal series. Hence (8) and (4) give
\[
 P_{\cS}U_k=\tfrac12(f_k,r_k),
 \tag{12}
\]
which does not converge to zero almost surely on the second component of
\(\Omega\).

\section{Realizing the graph projection as an infinite-rank LCE}

It remains to realize \(\mathbb R\mathbf1\oplus\cS\) as the affine
coordinate space of one Hilbert-valued random variable. Let
\((e_n)\) be the standard basis of \(\ellTwo\), choose
\(\tau_n=2^{-n}\), and define
\begin{equation}
 V(\omega)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty \tau_ns_n(\omega)e_n.
 \tag{13}
\end{equation}
Tonelli's theorem and orthonormality show that
\[
 \E\|V\|_{\ellTwo}^2=\sum_n\tau_n^2<\infty,
\]
so (13) defines an \(\ellTwo\)-valued square-integrable random variable
almost surely. Since both \(\delta_n\) and \(\psi_n\) have mean zero,
\(\E V=0\). Also
\[
 \langle C_Ve_m,e_n\rangle
 =\E[\tau_ms_m\tau_ns_n]
 =\tau_n^2\mathbf1_{m=n}.
 \tag{14}
\]
Thus \(C_V=\operatorname{diag}(4^{-n})\) has infinite rank.

For \(h=(h_n)\in\ellTwo\), a bounded scalar linear functional of \(V\) is
\[
 \langle h,V\rangle=\sum_nh_n\tau_ns_n.
\]
Every finite linear combination of the \(s_n\) occurs in this way because
all \(\tau_n\) are nonzero. Consequently,
\[
 \overline{\{b+\langle h,V\rangle:b\in\R, h\in\ellTwo\}}
 =\mathbb R\mathbf1\oplus\cS.
 \tag{15}
\]
The sum is orthogonal because every \(s_n\) has mean zero. Each \(U_k\) also
has mean zero, so (1), (12), and (15) imply
\[
 \E^{\cA}[U_k\mid V]=P_{\cS}U_k
 =\tfrac12(f_k,r_k)\not\longrightarrow0\quad\text{a.s.}
\]
This proves the announced counterexample.

\section{Conclusion and scope}

The source's finite-rank hypothesis cannot be removed from its
\(L^2\)-dominated convergence theorem, even when the predicted variables are
real-valued and the conditioning variable is centered and separable. The
mechanism is not the unbounded covariance pseudoinverse: it is the fact that,
at infinite rank, the LCE subspace can encode an arbitrary separable
orthogonal projection, including one that transports harmless disjoint tails
to the pointwise-divergent tails of an orthogonal series.

The finite-dimensional graph projection and covariance algebra were checked
by the accompanying script. The only nonconstructive input is the classical,
sharp Menshov divergence theorem; all probabilistic and LCE steps are explicit.
