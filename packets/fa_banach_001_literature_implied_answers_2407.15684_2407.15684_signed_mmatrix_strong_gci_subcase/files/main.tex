\section{Source question}

Assouline, Chor, and Sadovsky \cite{ACS} formulate Tehranchi's strong
Gaussian correlation conjecture in two equivalent forms.  For
origin-symmetric convex sets $K,T\subseteq\R^d$, the geometric form asks for
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geometric}
 \g_d(K+T)\g_d(K\cap T)\geq \g_d(K)\g_d(T).
\end{equation}
The Gaussian-box form says that, for any centered jointly Gaussian vector
$X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ and threshold vectors $s,t\in[0,\infty]^n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:box}
 \Pp(|X|\leq s+t)\Pp(|X|\leq s\wedge t)
 \geq \Pp(|X|\leq s)\Pp(|X|\leq t).
\end{equation}
Here all vector operations and inequalities are coordinatewise.  The source
proves special cases, including unconditional convex sets, but leaves the
general assertion conjectural.

\section{Literature input and exact implication}

Recall that a positive function $F$ on a product lattice is MTP$_2$ if
\[
 F(a\wedge b)F(a\vee b)\geq F(a)F(b)
 \qquad(a,b\geq0).
\]
A symmetric positive definite matrix is an $M$-matrix here if its
off-diagonal entries are nonpositive.

\begin{theorem}[Karlin--Rinott consequence]\label{thm:main}
Let $X\sim N(0,\Sigma)$ be a nonsingular centered Gaussian vector.  Suppose
there is a diagonal sign matrix $S=\operatorname{diag}(\pm1,\ldots,\pm1)$
such that $S\Sigma^{-1}S$ is an $M$-matrix.  Then for all
$s,t\in[0,\infty]^n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stronger}
 \Pp(|X|\leq s\wedge t)\Pp(|X|\leq s\vee t)
 \geq \Pp(|X|\leq s)\Pp(|X|\leq t).
\end{equation}
Consequently, the strong Gaussian-box inequality \eqref{eq:box} holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Karlin and Rinott \cite{KR} proved that the stated signed-$M$-matrix
condition is precisely the condition under which the absolute-value vector
$|X|$ is MTP$_2$.  Royen \cite{Royen} restates this criterion explicitly on
page 2 of the included supporting paper.

The lower-orthant distribution function
\[
 F(a)=\Pp(|X_i|\leq a_i\text{ for every }i)
\]
is therefore MTP$_2$.  One may see the standard integration closure directly:
the order kernel ${\bf1}_{\{x\leq a\}}$ is MTP$_2$ jointly in $(x,a)$;
products preserve MTP$_2$; and integration over $x$ preserves MTP$_2$.
Thus
\[
 F(s\wedge t)F(s\vee t)\geq F(s)F(t),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:stronger}.  Finally $s+t\geq s\vee t$ coordinatewise and
$F$ is increasing, so $F(s+t)\geq F(s\vee t)$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:box}.  Infinite coordinates follow by monotone convergence.
\end{proof}

\section{Geometric corollary}

For an invertible matrix $A\in\R^{d\times d}$ and $a\geq0$, put
\[
 P_A(a)=\{x\in\R^d: |(Ax)_i|\leq a_i\text{ for every }i\}.
\]

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:poly}
Suppose $(AA^{\mathsf T})^{-1}$ is sign-similar to an $M$-matrix.  Then every
pair $K=P_A(s)$ and $T=P_A(t)$ satisfies \eqref{eq:geometric}.
In dimension two the matrix condition is automatic, so this includes every
pair of centrally symmetric parallelograms having the same two facet
directions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a standard Gaussian $Y$, the vector $AY$ has covariance
$\Sigma=AA^{\mathsf T}$.  Since $A$ is bijective,
\[
 P_A(s)\cap P_A(t)=P_A(s\wedge t),\qquad
 P_A(s)+P_A(t)=P_A(s+t).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} now gives \eqref{eq:geometric}.  In dimension two a
single coordinate sign flip can always make the sole off-diagonal entry of a
positive definite precision matrix nonpositive.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and full-upgrade audit}

This does not settle the source conjecture.  In three or more coordinates a
precision matrix may contain a sign-frustrated cycle and fail to be
sign-similar to an $M$-matrix.  Indeed, numerical tests found that the stronger
max/min inequality \eqref{eq:stronger} can fail outside the MTP$_2$ class,
even though the conjectured sum/min inequality continued to hold in the same
tests.

Eight materially distinct full-upgrade routes were pursued: an orthogonal
product-space section argument; MTP$_2$ and an elementary bivariate proof;
the failed global max/min shortcut; deterministic two-dimensional polygon
searches; Sobol searches over non-lattice three-dimensional polytopes;
Ehrhard/Pr\'ekopa and radial reductions; and inverse Brascamp--Lieb plus
tensorization.  None closed the sign-frustrated general case or produced a
counterexample.  The detailed derivations, numerical scopes, and reusable
scripts are recorded in the run attempt associated with this packet.

The supporting authors did not claim to answer Tehranchi's conjecture.  This
packet records a direct implication of their MTP$_2$ theorem and is therefore
classified as a literature-implied partial answer, with no novelty claim.
