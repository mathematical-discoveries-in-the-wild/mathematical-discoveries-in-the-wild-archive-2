\section{Source problem}

Ahmed, Das, and Sahu study the multiplier variety associated with a complete
Nevanlinna--Pick Dirichlet-series kernel \cite{AhmedDasSahu}.  For positive
weights $\mathbf b=(b_j)_{j=1}^d$ and distinct integers
$\mathbf n=(n_j)_{j=1}^d$, where
$d\in\N_{\ge2}\cup\{\infty\}$, put
\[
 f_{\mathbf b,\mathbf n}(s)
   =\bigl(\sqrt{b_j}\,n_j^{-s}\bigr)_{j=1}^d.
\]
Their Corollary 4.11 states that if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-one}
 \{\log n_1,\log n_i\}\text{ is linearly dependent over }\Q
 \quad(i\ge2),
\end{equation}
then the associated multiplier variety $V$ is exactly the image of the
relevant half-plane, together with the origin.  Its two alternatives are
shown below.

\end{center}

Immediately after the proof, on source PDF page 24, the authors write:

\begin{center}
 \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Thus the open problem is to prove the converse: does either exact curve
description force \eqref{eq:rank-one}?  We prove that it does, simultaneously
for both alternatives and for finite or countably infinite data.

\section{Definitions and a closedness observation}

Write $\Hh_\gamma=\{s\in\C:\operatorname{Re}s>\gamma\}$ and let
$\B_d$ be the open unit ball of $\C^d$ when $d<\infty$, or of $\ell^2$ when
$d=\infty$.  If $f=f_{\mathbf b,\mathbf n}$ maps $\Hh_\gamma$ into $\B_d$,
the generated multiplier variety is
\[
 V=\{z\in\B_d:g(z)=0\text{ for every }g\in\mathcal M_d
       \text{ with }g|_{f(\Hh_\gamma)}=0\}.
\]
Every multiplier is holomorphic, hence norm-continuous, on $\B_d$.
Consequently, $V$ is relatively norm closed in $\B_d$ and
$f(\Hh_\gamma)\subseteq V$.

\section{Proof intuition}

If $\log n_i/\log n_1$ is irrational, move vertically along the curve in
steps of $2\pi/\log n_1$.  Coordinate 1 then returns exactly to its starting
phase, whereas coordinate $i$ follows a dense irrational rotation.  Choose a
limit phase for coordinate $i$ that is not among the countably many phases
compatible with the fixed first coordinate.  Compactness of finite phase
tori---or a diagonal argument in countably many coordinates---makes all
coordinates converge.  In the infinite case the fixed amplitudes are square
summable, so small tails turn coordinatewise convergence into norm
convergence.  Closedness places the limit in $V$, but the incompatible pair
of phases proves that it is not on the Dirichlet curve.  Hence exact curve
equality is possible only in rational rank one.

\section{The converse theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Norm-closure obstruction]\label{thm:obstruction}
Let $d\in\N_{\ge2}\cup\{\infty\}$, let $b_j>0$ and $n_j\ge2$, and suppose
\[
 f(s)=\bigl(\sqrt{b_j}\,n_j^{-s}\bigr)_{j=1}^d
 \quad(s\in\Hh_\gamma)
\]
takes values in $\B_d$.  Let $W\subseteq\B_d$ be relatively norm closed and
contain $f(\Hh_\gamma)$.  If
\[
 \alpha_i:=\frac{\log n_i}{\log n_1}\notin\Q
\]
for some $i\ge2$, then $W$ contains a nonzero point which is not $f(s)$ for
any complex $s$ for which $f(s)$ is defined.  In particular,
\[
 W\ne f(\Hh_\gamma)\cup\{0\},
 \qquad
 W\ne f(\overline{\Hh_\gamma})\cup\{0\}
\]
whenever the second displayed image is defined in $\B_d$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a real $x>\gamma$ and set
\[
 a_j=\sqrt{b_j}\,n_j^{-x}>0.
\]
Then $a=(a_j)\in\B_d$.  The subgroup
\[
 G_i=\{e^{-2\pi i m\alpha_i}:m\in\mathbb Z\}
\]
is countable and dense in $\T$: density is the elementary irrational-rotation
theorem (equivalently, the closure of an infinite cyclic subgroup of $\T$ is
all of $\T$).  Choose $\zeta\in\T\setminus G_i$, and choose distinct integers
$k_r$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeta}
 e^{-2\pi i k_r\alpha_i}\longrightarrow\zeta.
\end{equation}
For
\[
 s_r=x+\frac{2\pi i k_r}{\log n_1}
\]
we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit}
 f(s_r)_j=a_j e^{-2\pi i k_r\alpha_j},
 \qquad \alpha_j=\frac{\log n_j}{\log n_1}.
\end{equation}
In particular, the first phase is always $1$, while the $i$-th phase tends to
$\zeta$.

If $d<\infty$, compactness of $\T^d$ gives a subsequence on which every phase
in \eqref{eq:orbit} converges.  If $d=\infty$, successive extraction followed
by the diagonal subsequence gives the same conclusion coordinate by
coordinate.  Write the limiting phases as $\eta_j\in\T$.  We retain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:special-phases}
 \eta_1=1,\qquad \eta_i=\zeta,
\end{equation}
and define $w=(a_j\eta_j)_{j=1}^d$.

For completeness, the convergence is in norm even when $d=\infty$.  Since
$f(x)\in\B_\infty$,
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^\infty a_j^2
   =\sum_{j=1}^\infty b_j n_j^{-2x}<1.
\]
Given $\varepsilon>0$, first choose $J$ so that
$4\sum_{j>J}a_j^2<\varepsilon^2/2$.  Coordinatewise convergence makes
\[
 \sum_{j\le J}a_j^2
 \left|e^{-2\pi i k_r\alpha_j}-\eta_j\right|^2
 <\varepsilon^2/2
\]
for all sufficiently large $r$, while the remaining sum is at most
$4\sum_{j>J}a_j^2$.  Hence $f(s_r)\to w$ in $\ell^2$.  The finite-dimensional
case is immediate.  Moreover,
\[
 \|w\|^2=\sum_j a_j^2=\|f(x)\|^2<1,
\]
so $w\in\B_d$.  Since every $f(s_r)$ belongs to $W$ and $W$ is relatively
norm closed, $w\in W$.  Also $w\ne0$ because $w_1=a_1>0$.

It remains to exclude $w$ from the curve.  Suppose $w=f(s)$, and write
$s=u+iv$.  Comparing the modulus of coordinate 1 gives
\[
 \sqrt{b_1}n_1^{-u}=|w_1|=a_1=\sqrt{b_1}n_1^{-x},
\]
so $u=x$.  Comparing its phase and using $w_1=a_1>0$ gives
$e^{-iv\log n_1}=1$, hence
\[
 v=\frac{2\pi m}{\log n_1}
 \quad\text{for some }m\in\mathbb Z.
\]
The phase of coordinate $i$ would therefore be
$e^{-iv\log n_i}=e^{-2\pi i m\alpha_i}\in G_i$.  But
\eqref{eq:special-phases} says that this phase is $\zeta\notin G_i$, a
contradiction.  Thus $w$ has all the asserted properties.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Full converse to Corollary 4.11]\label{cor:characterization}
Under the hypotheses and notation of Corollary 4.11 of
\cite{AhmedDasSahu}, each equality in that corollary is an if-and-only-if
statement.  Explicitly, in either of its alternatives the displayed formula
for $V$ holds exactly when
\[
 \frac{\log n_i}{\log n_1}\in\Q\qquad(i=2,\ldots,d).
\]
Equivalently, the $\Q$-linear span of $\{\log n_j:1\le j\le d\}$ has
dimension one.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The forward direction from rational rank one to the appropriate equality is
Corollary 4.11 of \cite{AhmedDasSahu}.  Conversely, apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction} with $W=V$.  The multiplier variety is relatively
norm closed and contains the relevant open-half-plane image.  If any displayed
ratio were irrational, the theorem would produce a nonzero point of $V$
outside even the entire Dirichlet curve, contradicting either the open-image
equality in alternative (i) or the closed-image equality in alternative (ii).
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The proof uses no polynomial presentation of $V$ and no compactness claim for
the infinite-dimensional ball.  The only compactness is coordinatewise phase
compactness, followed by the displayed square-summable-tail estimate.  The
constructed limit stays strictly inside the ball, which is essential because
$V$ is only relatively closed there.

The result answers precisely the conjecture after Corollary 4.11; it does not
address the paper's broader classification questions.  A bounded literature
check through 12 August 2026 searched the current arXiv record and v3 full
text, the exact title, arXiv id, exact conjecture sentence, author publication
pages, citation listings, and close multiplier-variety/Dirichlet-curve
keywords.  No later solution or claim of this converse was found.  This is a
bounded novelty screen, not an exhaustive bibliographic proof of novelty.
