\section*{Status and source question}

This is a \texttt{candidate\_partial\_likely\_valid} result for the final
problem in Andrzej Wi\'snicki, \emph{H\"older continuous retractions and
amenable semigroups of uniformly Lipschitzian mappings in Hilbert spaces},
arXiv:1204.6464 \cite{Wisnicki}.  The source recalls the Goebel--Kirk theorem:
if $X$ is uniformly convex, $C\subset X$ is bounded, closed, and convex,
$T:C\to C$ is uniformly $k$-Lipschitzian, and
\[
 k\bigl(1-\delta_X(1/k)\bigr)<1,
\]
then $T$ has a fixed point.  On PDF page~6 it asks whether the fixed-point
set is a Lipschitz or H\"older retract.  The displayed problem writes
$\Fix\mathcal S$ although the referenced theorem concerns one map; the
surrounding discussion makes $\Fix T$ the natural single-map reading.

The arbitrary uniformly convex case is not settled here.  We prove a
quantitative strong-convexity criterion and obtain a genuine non-Hilbert
subcase in $L^p$, $3/2<p<2$.

\section*{Strong convexity and proof intuition}

Assume that $X$ is reflexive and that for some $c\in(0,1]$ and every $v\in X$
there is $j(v)\in X^*$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sc}
 \|u\|^2\ge \|v\|^2+2\langle j(v),u-v\rangle+c\|u-v\|^2
 \qquad(u\in X).
\end{equation}
This says that the squared norm has Bregman strong-convexity constant $c$.
In a Hilbert space $c=1$ and \eqref{eq:sc} is the variance identity with a
nonnegative remainder.  In $L^p$, $1<p\le2$, one may take $c=p-1$ by the
sharp Ball--Carlen--Lieb inequality \cite{BCL}.

For a left amenable semigroup, average an orbit using an invariant mean and
call its vector barycenter $Lx$.  Unlike an asymptotic-center map, $L$ is
automatically $k$-Lipschitz.  Inequality \eqref{eq:sc} replaces the exact
Hilbert variance identity and proves that the variance about the next
barycenter contracts by
\[
 \rho=\frac{k^2-c}{c}.
\]
When $k^2<2c$, the iterates of $L$ have geometrically decaying increments.
Their uniform limit is H\"older because $L^n$ is $k^n$-Lipschitz.

\section*{Main partial theorem}

\begin{theorem}[strongly convex amenable actions]\label{thm:main}
Let $X$ be a reflexive Banach space satisfying \eqref{eq:sc}, let $C$ be a
nonempty bounded closed convex subset of $X$, and let $S$ be a discrete left
amenable semigroup.  Suppose
$\mathcal S=\{T_s:s\in S\}$ acts on $C$, with
$T_{st}=T_sT_t$, and is uniformly $k$-Lipschitzian:
\[
 \|T_sx-T_sy\|\le k\|x-y\|
 \qquad(s\in S,\ x,y\in C).
\]
If $k^2<2c$, then the common fixed-point set
\[
 \Fix\mathcal S=\{x\in C:T_sx=x\text{ for every }s\in S\}
\]
is nonempty and is a H\"older continuous retract of $C$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mu$ be a left invariant mean on $\ell^\infty(S)$.  Reflexivity and the
Riesz-style barycenter construction used in \cite{Wisnicki} define $Lx\in C$
by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L}
 \langle Lx,x^*\rangle
 =\mu_t\langle T_tx,x^*\rangle\qquad(x^*\in X^*).
\end{equation}
Indeed $Lx$ lies in the weakly closed convex hull of the orbit.  Moreover,
duality immediately gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Llip}
 \|Lx-Ly\|\le k\|x-y\|.
\end{equation}

Set
\[
 \Q(x)=\mu_t\|T_tx-Lx\|^2,
 \qquad
 \E(x)=\mu_t\|T_tx-x\|^2.
\]
Fix $s\in S$.  Apply \eqref{eq:sc} with
\[
 u=T_{st}x-T_sLx,
 \qquad v=Lx-T_sLx,
\]
and then take the mean in $t$.  Left invariance makes the barycenter of
$(T_{st}x)_t$ equal to $Lx$, so the linear term has mean zero and
$\mu_t\|T_{st}x-Lx\|^2=\Q(x)$.  The Lipschitz bound for $T_s$ therefore gives
\begin{align}
 \|T_sLx-Lx\|^2+c\Q(x)
 &\le \mu_t\|T_{st}x-T_sLx\|^2 \\
 &\le k^2\mu_t\|T_tx-Lx\|^2=k^2\Q(x).
\end{align}
Taking the mean in $s$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ebound}
 \E(Lx)\le(k^2-c)\Q(x).
\end{equation}

Apply \eqref{eq:sc} once more to the orbit $(T_sLx)_s$, whose barycenter is
$L^2x$, now with reference point $Lx$.  Again the linear term vanishes:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:secondvariance}
 \E(Lx)\ge \|L^2x-Lx\|^2+c\Q(Lx).
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:Ebound} and \eqref{eq:secondvariance} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qcontract}
 \Q(Lx)\le\rho\Q(x),
 \qquad \rho=\frac{k^2-c}{c}<1.
\end{equation}
The case $\rho=0$ makes $Lx$ fixed after one step and is immediate, so assume
$0<\rho<1$.

Write $x_m=L^mx$ and $D=\operatorname{diam}C$.  Jensen's inequality for the
mean, \eqref{eq:Ebound}, and \eqref{eq:Qcontract} show, for $m\ge1$, that
\begin{align}
 \|x_{m+1}-x_m\|^2
 &\le \E(x_m)=\E(Lx_{m-1}) \\
 &\le(k^2-c)\Q(x_{m-1})
 \le(k^2-c)\rho^{m-1}D^2.\label{eq:increments}
\end{align}
Thus $L^mx$ converges uniformly in $x$ to a map $Rx\in C$.

Put $\gamma=\sqrt\rho$.  From \eqref{eq:increments}, the tail after the
$n$-th iterate is $O(\gamma^n)$, while \eqref{eq:Llip} gives
$\|L^nx-L^ny\|\le k^n\|x-y\|$.  Balancing these two bounds proves that $R$
is H\"older.  Explicitly, for $k>1$ one may take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha}
 \alpha=\frac{\log(1/\gamma)}{\log k+\log(1/\gamma)}\in(0,1),
\end{equation}
and when $k=1$, the uniform limit is nonexpansive.

It remains to identify the range.  From \eqref{eq:Ebound} and
\eqref{eq:Qcontract}, $\E(x_m)\to0$.  For every $t$,
\[
 \|T_tRx-Rx\|^2
 \le3k^2\|Rx-x_m\|^2+3\|T_tx_m-x_m\|^2
       +3\|x_m-Rx\|^2.
\]
Taking the mean and then letting $m\to\infty$ gives $\E(Rx)=0$.  For fixed
$s\in S$, the triangle inequality and left invariance give
\begin{align*}
 \|T_sRx-Rx\|^2
 &\le2\mu_t\|T_sRx-T_tRx\|^2+2\E(Rx)\\
 &=2\mu_t\|T_sRx-T_{st}Rx\|^2+2\E(Rx)\\
 &\le2(k^2+1)\E(Rx)=0.
\end{align*}
Hence $Rx\in\Fix\mathcal S$.  If $x\in\Fix\mathcal S$, then \eqref{eq:L}
gives $Lx=x$, and consequently $Rx=x$.  Thus $R$ is the asserted retraction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[$L^p$ subcase]\label{cor:lp}
Let $3/2<p\le2$, let $C$ be a nonempty bounded closed convex subset of a real
$L^p$ space, and let a discrete left amenable semigroup act on $C$ by
uniformly $k$-Lipschitzian maps.  If
\[
 k<\sqrt{2(p-1)},
\]
then its common fixed-point set is a H\"older retract of $C$.  In particular,
the same conclusion holds for the fixed-point set of one uniformly
$k$-Lipschitzian map, by applying the result to its power semigroup.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $1<p\le2$, the Ball--Carlen--Lieb inequality says
\[
 \frac{\|a+b\|_p^2+\|a-b\|_p^2}{2}
 \ge \|a\|_p^2+(p-1)\|b\|_p^2.
\]
Equivalently, $x\mapsto\|x\|_p^2$ is strongly convex.  Passing from the
midpoint inequality to the standard two-point inequality and differentiating
at one endpoint gives \eqref{eq:sc} with $c=p-1$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
applies.  The strict range is nonempty for $k\ge1$ precisely when $p>3/2$.
\end{proof}

\section*{Scope, verification, and remaining obstruction}

Corollary~\ref{cor:lp} gives a non-Hilbert family of affirmative cases.  For
the source's exact formulation, one may additionally impose its Goebel--Kirk
inequality; Theorem~\ref{thm:main} itself does not need that separate fixed
point criterion.

The full problem for arbitrary uniformly convex spaces remains open in this
packet.  The published asymptotic-center construction gives geometric convergence, but its
continuity is governed by $\delta_X^{-1}$ near zero.  A uniformly convex norm
need not have a power-type modulus, so that argument does not automatically
give a H\"older exponent.  The invariant-mean method above instead stops at
$k^2=2c$.  No claim is made that this threshold is sharp.

The included regression script checks the Ball--Carlen--Lieb midpoint
inequality on randomized finite-dimensional $\ell^p$ vectors and audits the
sign of $\rho$ throughout sampled admissible ranges.  It is not used as proof.

Eight focused attempts are recorded in the accompanying attempt note.
Bounded run-index and web/arXiv searches on 17 August 2026 used the exact
source problem, the paper title and author, and the phrases \emph{uniformly
Lipschitzian fixed point H\"older retract}, \emph{$L^p$ H\"older retract},
and \emph{invariant mean strong convexity uniformly Lipschitzian}.  They found
continuous-retract results, the Hilbert theorem, and asymptotically regular
variants, but no explicit statement of this criterion.  Novelty confidence is
moderate pending specialist review.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Check the passage from the Ball--Carlen--Lieb midpoint inequality to
\eqref{eq:sc}, and the use of a finitely additive invariant mean in the two
strong-convexity averages.  Also search the nonlinear ergodic literature for
an equivalent variance-contraction criterion.
