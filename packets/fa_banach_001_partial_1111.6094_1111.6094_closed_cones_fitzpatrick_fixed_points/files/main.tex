\section{Source problem and scope}
Garc\'ia Ramos, Mart\'inez-Legaz, and Simons ask for a characterization of
the sets $A$ satisfying $G_{\Phi_A}=A$ in a symmetrically self-dual (SSD)
space, explicitly presenting this as a generalized version of Fitzpatrick's
open monotone-operator problem \cite{GRMS,Fitzpatrick}. Their exact formulation
appears in Section 3.1 on PDF page 5:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.93\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

This packet solves the problem completely for the subclass of cones in the
positive-definite Hilbert SSD model. It does not solve the general problem in
an indefinite SSD space or the classical monotone-operator problem on
$X\times X^*$.

\section{Definitions}
Let $H$ be a real Hilbert space, paired with itself by its inner product, and
put
\[
 q(x)=\frac12\|x\|^2.
\]
For a nonempty set $A\subset H$, write $d_A(x)=\inf_{a\in A}\|x-a\|$ and
define
\[
 \Phi_A(x)=q(x)-\inf_{a\in A}q(x-a)
           =\sup_{a\in A}\bigl\{\langle x,a\rangle-q(a)\bigr\}.
\]
For a proper convex function $f$, its intrinsic conjugate is the ordinary
Fenchel conjugate
\[
 f^*(y)=\sup_{z\in H}\{\langle z,y\rangle-f(z)\}.
\]
The $q$-subdifferential and its diagonal fixed-point set are
\[
 \partial_q f(x)=\{y:f(x)+f^*(y)=\langle x,y\rangle\},
 \qquad G_f=\{x:x\in\partial_q f(x)\}.
\]
A cone means a nonempty set $A$ such that $tA\subset A$ for every $t\geq0$;
no convexity is assumed.

\section{Idea of the proof}
The fixed-point condition has an exact metric form involving the squared
distance to $A$. A cone makes this distance homogeneous along every ray.
At a point $x\notin A$, moving a little farther out on the same ray changes
$d_A^2$ to first order, while the penalty $\|b-x\|^2$ changes only to second
order. Consequently the metric equality required for $x\in G_{\Phi_A}$
fails. Closedness is exactly what makes $d_A(x)>0$ outside $A$. Conversely,
every point in $\cl A$ automatically satisfies the fixed-point equality, so
a conic fixed point cannot be nonclosed.

\section{Metric fixed-point criterion}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:criterion}
For every nonempty $A\subset H$ and $x\in H$,
\[
 x\in G_{\Phi_A}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sup_{b\in H}\{d_A(b)^2-\|b-x\|^2\}=d_A(x)^2.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Completing the square gives
\[
 \Phi_A(x)=\frac12\|x\|^2-\frac12d_A(x)^2.
\]
Therefore
\begin{align*}
 \Phi_A^*(x)
 &=\sup_{b\in H}
   \left\{\langle b,x\rangle-\frac12\|b\|^2+\frac12d_A(b)^2\right\}\\
 &=\frac12\|x\|^2+\frac12
   \sup_{b\in H}\{d_A(b)^2-\|b-x\|^2\}.
\end{align*}
By definition, $x\in G_{\Phi_A}$ exactly when
$\Phi_A(x)+\Phi_A^*(x)=\|x\|^2$. Substitution yields the stated equality.
Notice that the supremum is always at least $d_A(x)^2$, by taking $b=x$.
\end{proof}

\section{Closed-cone classification}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $A$ be a nonempty cone in a real Hilbert space $H$. Then
\[
 A=G_{\Phi_A}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A\text{ is norm closed}.
\]
In particular, every nonempty closed cone, whether convex or not, is a
generalized Fitzpatrick fixed point.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First suppose $A=G_{\Phi_A}$. Let $x\in\cl A$. Then $d_A(x)=0$, and for
every $b\in H$,
\[
 d_A(b)=d_{\cl A}(b)\leq\|b-x\|.
\]
Thus the supremum in Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} is at most zero; taking
$b=x$ shows that it equals zero. Hence $x\in G_{\Phi_A}=A$. We have
$\cl A\subset A$, so $A$ is closed. This direction in fact does not use the
cone assumption.

Conversely, suppose $A$ is closed. Since $A$ is a nonempty cone, $0\in A$.
If $x\in A$, then $d_A(x)=0$ and
$d_A(b)\leq\|b-x\|$ for every $b$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} gives
$x\in G_{\Phi_A}$.

Now let $x\notin A$ and set $\delta=d_A(x)>0$. For every $\lambda>0$,
the cone identity $\lambda A=A$ implies
\[
 d_A(\lambda x)=\lambda d_A(x)=\lambda\delta.
\]
Choose $\lambda>1$ sufficiently close to $1$ that
\[
 (\lambda+1)\delta^2>(\lambda-1)\|x\|^2;
\]
such a choice exists because the left side tends to $2\delta^2>0$ and the
right side tends to zero. Evaluating the supremum in
Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} at $b=\lambda x$ gives
\begin{align*}
 \sup_{b\in H}\{d_A(b)^2-\|b-x\|^2\}
 &\geq \lambda^2\delta^2-(\lambda-1)^2\|x\|^2\\
 &=\delta^2+(\lambda-1)
   \bigl((\lambda+1)\delta^2-(\lambda-1)\|x\|^2\bigr)\\
 &>\delta^2=d_A(x)^2.
\end{align*}
The criterion therefore gives $x\notin G_{\Phi_A}$. We have proved
$G_{\Phi_A}=A$.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences and relation to the source}
The source proves that every closed convex set is a fixed point, and then
checks directly that
\[
 \{(x_1,x_2)\in\mathbb R^2:x_1x_2=0\}
\]
is a nonconvex fixed point. That set is a closed cone, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} both recovers the example and extends it to arbitrary
closed cones in arbitrary real Hilbert spaces. It also supplies the converse
within the conic class: no nonclosed cone can be a fixed point.

The theorem does not characterize arbitrary closed nonconic sets, and the
general Fitzpatrick problem remains open outside the stated subclass.

\section{Verification and novelty check}
The proof uses only the displayed definitions, completion of the square,
distance homogeneity for cones, and the elementary fact that a point outside
a closed set has positive distance. Edge cases $A=\{0\}$ and $A=H$ are
included. No finite-dimensionality, convexity, or projection theorem is used.
