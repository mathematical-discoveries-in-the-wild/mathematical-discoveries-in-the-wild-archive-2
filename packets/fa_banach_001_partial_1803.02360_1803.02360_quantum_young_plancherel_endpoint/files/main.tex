\section{Source conjecture and result}

De Palma, Trevisan, Giovannetti, and Ambrosio formulate a quantum sharp Young
inequality for the $n$-mode beam-splitter and two-mode squeezer
\cite{DPTGA2018}.  Their Conjecture V.16 asserts that the bilinear Schatten
constant
\[
 C_n(p,q,r,\lambda)=
 \sup_{X,Y\ne0}
 \frac{\|\Bcal_\lambda^{\otimes n}(X\otimes Y)\|_r}
      {\|X\|_p\|Y\|_q}
\]
is obtained by restricting to thermal Gaussian inputs and is multiplicative
in $n$.  The exact source statement and its finiteness prediction appear on
PDF page 28:

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\caption{Conjecture V.16 and Remark V.17 in \cite{DPTGA2018}.}
\end{figure}

We prove the conjecture on the Plancherel endpoint
$(p,q,r)=(2,2,\infty)$, including both kinds of Gaussian mixing and every
mode number.

\section{Proof intuition}

A Gaussian mixing unitary acts linearly on phase-space displacements.  The
Weyl characteristic function of the output is therefore the pointwise product
of two rescaled input characteristic functions.  At Schatten exponent two,
the Weyl transform is exactly Plancherel.  Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the $L^1$
norm of that product, and Weyl inversion bounds the output operator norm.  The
rescaling Jacobians produce the exact constant.  High-energy thermal states,
with their two energies chosen in the inverse ratio of the mixing weights,
meet the constant asymptotically.

\section{The exact endpoint}

\begin{theorem}[Plancherel endpoint of Conjecture V.16]\label{thm:endpoint}
Let $n\ge1$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item If $0<\lambda<1$ (beam-splitter), then
\[
 C_n(2,2,\infty,\lambda)
 =\bigl[\lambda(1-\lambda)\bigr]^{-n/2}.
\]
\item If $\lambda>1$ (two-mode squeezer), then
\[
 C_n(2,2,\infty,\lambda)
 =\bigl[\lambda(\lambda-1)\bigr]^{-n/2}.
\]
\item At $\lambda=0$ or $\lambda=1$, the constant is infinite.
\end{enumerate}
For every nondegenerate $\lambda$, the sharp value is approached by thermal
Gaussian inputs.  In particular,
\[
 C_n(2,2,\infty,\lambda)=C_1(2,2,\infty,\lambda)^n,
\]
so Conjecture V.16 and the critical-case prediction of Remark V.17 hold on
this exponent slice.
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof}

For $z\in\C^n$, let $D(z)$ be the $n$-mode Weyl displacement and set
\[
 \chi_X(z)=\Tr[X D(z)],
 \qquad d\nu(z)=\pi^{-n}\,d^{2n}z.
\]
With this normalization, Weyl inversion and Plancherel are
\begin{align}
 X&=\int_{\C^n}\chi_X(z)D(-z)\,d\nu(z),\label{eq:inv}\\
 \int_{\C^n}|\chi_X(z)|^2\,d\nu(z)&=\|X\|_2^2.\label{eq:planch}
\end{align}
These standard identities are initially read on a convenient dense class and
extend to Hilbert--Schmidt operators; see, for example, Folland
\cite{Folland1989}.

Let $Z=\Bcal_\lambda^{\otimes n}(X\otimes Y)$.  The action of the Gaussian
mixing unitary on displacement operators gives, when $0<\lambda<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beam-char}
 \chi_Z(z)=\chi_X(\sqrt\lambda\,z)
           \chi_Y(\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,z).
\end{equation}
For the squeezer $\lambda>1$, the corresponding identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:squeeze-char}
 \chi_Z(z)=\chi_X(\sqrt\lambda\,z)
           \chi_Y(-\sqrt{\lambda-1}\,\overline z).
\end{equation}
The reflection and conjugation in the second factor preserve Lebesgue measure.

Write $a=\lambda$ and $b=|1-\lambda|$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:beam-char}--\eqref{eq:squeeze-char}, Weyl inversion, unitarity of
$D(z)$, Cauchy--Schwarz, and \eqref{eq:planch} yield
\begin{align*}
 \|Z\|_\infty
 &\le \int |\chi_X(\sqrt a\,z)\chi_Y(\sqrt b\,z)|\,d\nu(z)\\
 &\le
 \left(\int|\chi_X(\sqrt a\,z)|^2d\nu(z)\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\int|\chi_Y(\sqrt b\,z)|^2d\nu(z)\right)^{1/2}\\
 &=a^{-n/2}b^{-n/2}\|X\|_2\|Y\|_2.
\end{align*}
For finite-rank $X,Y$ this is a direct estimate of the physical partial-trace
formula.  Since finite-rank operators are dense in the Hilbert--Schmidt class,
the bound also defines and uniquely extends the bilinear map on
$S_2\times S_2$ with values in bounded operators.  This proves the required
upper bound.

It remains to prove sharpness.  The one-mode thermal state of mean photon
number $E$ satisfies
\[
 \|\widehat\omega(E)\|_2=(2E+1)^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \|\widehat\omega(E)\|_\infty=(E+1)^{-1}.
\]
Products of thermal states remain thermal under each modewise mixing.  For a
beam-splitter their output energy is
\[
 E_{\rm out}=\lambda E_A+(1-\lambda)E_B,
\]
whereas for a squeezer it is
\[
 E_{\rm out}=\lambda E_A+(\lambda-1)(E_B+1).
\]
Consequently the thermal quotient for $n$ modes equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thermalratio}
 \frac{[(2E_A+1)(2E_B+1)]^{n/2}}{(E_{\rm out}+1)^n}.
\end{equation}
Set $E_A=bt$ and $E_B=at$.  In both cases, as $t\to\infty$,
the expression in \eqref{eq:thermalratio} converges to
$(ab)^{-n/2}$.  This matches the upper bound, proves sharpness, and proves
multiplicativity.

At $\lambda=1$, the mixing is degenerate: the retained output is the first
input multiplied by the trace of the second.  Taking the first thermal input
to be vacuum and sending the second energy to infinity makes the quotient
diverge because its Hilbert--Schmidt norm tends to zero.  The symmetric
argument applies at $\lambda=0$.  This completes the proof.

In particular, thermal inputs still give the correct value $+\infty$ at the
two degenerate parameters, so the restriction equality in Conjecture V.16 is
not contradicted.  However, the finite-critical-case statement in Remark V.17
is literally false at $\lambda=0,1$ and should be read with nondegenerate
mixing assumed.

\section{Verification, scope, and upgrade obstruction}

The script \texttt{code/check\_endpoint.py} constructs the exact beam-splitter
isometry on a four-level input Fock sector without truncating its possible
output photon number.  It checks 300 deterministic random matrix pairs at each
of three transmissivities and checks three beam-splitter and three squeezer
thermal asymptotics.  It reports
\begin{verbatim}
PASS: Plancherel-endpoint sanity checks completed
\end{verbatim}
This is supplementary verification, not the proof.

The result does not settle Conjecture V.16 at finite critical exponents, the
$p\to q$ Gaussian-maximizer conjecture, the Entropy Photon-number Inequality,
or multimode constrained minimum output entropy.  The same characteristic
function route at finite exponents requires sharp Hausdorff--Young estimates
for the Weyl transform.  Ordinary interpolation proves nonsharp boundedness
but produces a constant strictly larger than the thermal value, so it cannot
upgrade the endpoint proof to the full conjecture.

\section{Novelty check and review recommendation}

Bounded searches on 11 August 2026 covered the run indexes and open web/arXiv
results using arXiv:1803.02360, the exact conjecture name, the authors, the
phrases ``$C_n(2,2,\infty)$,'' ``Hilbert--Schmidt to operator norm,'' ``Weyl
Plancherel,'' and close beam-splitter/squeezer variants.  They found known
one-mode, $p=1$, $p=q$, Gaussian-input, and related partial results, but no
explicit statement of this endpoint.  Novelty confidence is moderate rather
than high because the proof is short and may be folklore.

Human review is recommended, focusing on phase-space normalization, the
finite-rank density extension, and the squeezer characteristic-function
identity.
