# Inconsistent proximal splitting: later sufficient-condition answer

Status: `literature_already_answered (open problem 1, quantitative sufficient-condition resolution)`.

Source question: Florian Lauster and D. Russell Luke, *Convergence of
Proximal Splitting Algorithms in CAT(kappa) Spaces and Beyond*,
arXiv:2105.05691, Section 6, physical PDF page 17, first open direction.

Supporting answer: D. Russell Luke and Mahshid Mirhashemi,
*Quantitative Convergence of Proximal Splitting Iterations in Uniformly Convex
Metric Spaces*, arXiv:2605.03484, abstract and Introduction pages 1--2,
Theorem 3.3 on physical page 9, and Corollary 3.4 beginning on physical page
10.

The source asks for requirements ensuring quantitative convergence of
proximal splitting methods when the constituent proximal maps have no common
fixed point (the inconsistent case). The supporting paper explicitly removes
the common-minima assumption. Its Theorem 3.3 gives quantitative and linear
convergence criteria for cyclic and relaxed compositions of proximal maps in
general p-uniformly convex spaces with curvature bounded above. Corollary 3.4
specializes the result to Hadamard spaces and drops the general composition
and relaxation assumptions in the stated range.

The supporting authors knew the provenance. Luke coauthored both papers; the
supporting paper cites the source as reference [29], identifies its
CAT(kappa) convergence-rate result as a consistent-case theorem, states that
quantitative results for inconsistent instances were previously unknown, and
presents its main contribution as removing common minima.

Scope: this is a sufficient-condition answer, not a complete
necessary-and-sufficient characterization. The supporting theorem still
assumes existence/invariance and a stability (metric-subregularity-type)
gauge, and in positive curvature it retains composition/relaxation regularity
assumptions. Its conclusion explicitly leaves removal of some of these
assumptions open. The source's second direction--structural settings in which
metric subregularity holds automatically--also remains open and is reiterated
in the supporting paper.
