\section{Source question and scope}

Bartz, Bauschke, and Wang study costs
\[
 c(x_1,\ldots,x_N)=\sum_{1\le i<j\le N}c_{ij}(x_i,x_j)
\]
and ask on p.~12 of \cite{BBW}:
\begin{quote}
For which cost functions of this form does $c$-cyclical monotonicity of a
set $\Gamma$ imply $c_{ij}$-cyclical monotonicity of its two-marginal
projections?  Given a cost, for which sets $\Gamma$ does this implication
hold?
\end{quote}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\caption{The two questions in the source paper \cite[p.~12]{BBW}.}
\end{figure}

There are three levels to the answer.  First, for an arbitrary fixed family
of pair costs, we prove that the desired implication is equivalent to the
existence, for every finitely supported family of one-marginals, of one joint
coupling which is simultaneously optimal on every interacting pair.  This is
an exact criterion, but not yet an intrinsic classification of arbitrary
cost functions.  Second, when every $X_i$ has two points, we turn the
criterion into a complete and checkable answer: the signed graph of nonzero
$2\times2$ cross differences must be balanced.  Third, uniformly over all
sets and all pair costs supported on a graph, the answer is exactly that the
graph is a forest.

\section{Definitions}

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple graph, with $V=\{1,\ldots,N\}$.
For each $i\in V$, let $X_i$ be a nonempty set.  For every edge
$e=\{i,j\}\in E$, let $c_e:X_i\times X_j\to\mathbb R$ be arbitrary, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost}
 c(x_1,\ldots,x_N)=\sum_{e=\{i,j\}\in E}c_e(x_i,x_j).
\end{equation}
For $\Gamma\subseteq\prod_iX_i$, write
$\Gamma_e=\{(x_i,x_j):x\in\Gamma\}$.

The set $\Gamma$ is \emph{$c$-cyclically monotone} if, for every $n\ge2$,
points $x^1,\ldots,x^n\in\Gamma$, and permutations
$\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_N\in S_n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:globalcm}
 \sum_{r=1}^n c\bigl(x_1^{\sigma_1(r)},\ldots,
 x_N^{\sigma_N(r)}\bigr)
 \le \sum_{r=1}^n c(x^r).
\end{equation}
The analogous two-coordinate definition is used for $\Gamma_e$ and $c_e$.

Say that $G$ has the \emph{universal projection property} if, for every
choice above,
\[
 \Gamma\text{ is $c$-cyclically monotone}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Gamma_e\text{ is $c_e$-cyclically monotone for every }e\in E.
\]

\section{An exact fixed-cost compatibility criterion}

We first keep the sets $X_i$ and the numerical pair costs $c_e$ fixed.
For finitely supported probability measures $\mu_i$ on $X_i$, write
$\Pi(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_N)$ for their joint couplings.  We say that the
family $(c_e)_{e\in E}$ has the \emph{edgewise optimal compatibility
property} if, for every such $(\mu_i)_i$, there is a
$\pi\in\Pi(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_N)$ for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simopt}
 \pi_e\in\mathop{\rm argmax}_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu_i,\mu_j)}
       \int c_e\,d\gamma
 \qquad\text{for every }e=\{i,j\}\in E.
\end{equation}
Only finite transportation polytopes occur in this definition, so no
topological or integrability hypotheses are needed.

\begin{lemma}[Finite optimality bridge]\label{lem:bridge}
Let $d$ be any real-valued cost on a finite product of arbitrary sets.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item If $\Lambda$ is $d$-cyclically monotone, every finitely supported
probability measure carried by $\Lambda$ maximizes $\int d$ among couplings
of its one-coordinate marginals.
\item The support of every finitely supported maximizing coupling is
$d$-cyclically monotone.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For (a), suppose that a finitely supported $\pi$ carried by $\Lambda$ has a
strictly better competitor $\rho$.  Restrict to the finite product of the
supports of the marginals.  Pairs $(p,q)$ of nonnegative arrays such that
$p$ is carried by $\Lambda$, $p$ and $q$ have the same one-marginals, and
both have mass one form a polytope defined by integer equalities and
inequalities.  Rational points are dense in every nonempty face of such a
rational polytope.  Since the cost gap is strict, there is a rational pair
$(p,q)$ with the same positive gap.

Clear denominators.  List the atoms of $p$, with their integer
multiplicities, as $x^1,\ldots,x^m\in\Lambda$, and list the atoms of $q$ in
the same way as $y^1,\ldots,y^m$.  Equality of each one-marginal says that,
for every coordinate $i$, the list $(y_i^r)_r$ is a permutation of
$(x_i^r)_r$.  Thus $q$ is one of the coordinatewise rearrangements forbidden
by $d$-cyclical monotonicity, a contradiction.

For (b), if points $x^1,\ldots,x^m$ in the support and coordinate
permutations produced a strict gain, subtract a sufficiently small equal
mass from the selected support points (counting repetitions) and add it to
the permuted points.  This preserves all one-marginals and strictly raises
the objective, contradicting optimality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-cost equivalence]\label{thm:compatibility}
For fixed $X_i$ and fixed pair costs $(c_e)_{e\in E}$, the following are
equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item For every $\Gamma\subseteq\prod_iX_i$, $c$-cyclical monotonicity of
$\Gamma$ implies $c_e$-cyclical monotonicity of $\Gamma_e$ for every edge.
\item The family $(c_e)_{e\in E}$ has the edgewise optimal compatibility
property \eqref{eq:simopt}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume (i), fix finitely supported marginals, and let $\pi$ maximize the
total cost $c=\sum_e c_e$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge}(b),
$\Gamma=\operatorname{supp}\pi$ is $c$-cyclically monotone.  Hence every
$\Gamma_e$ is $c_e$-cyclically monotone, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge}(a), now in two coordinates, shows that every
$\pi_e$ is pairwise optimal.  Thus $\pi$ satisfies \eqref{eq:simopt}.

Conversely, assume (ii).  Let $\Gamma$ be $c$-cyclically monotone, choose
finitely many lifts $x^1,\ldots,x^m\in\Gamma$, and let
\[
 \pi^0=\frac1m\sum_{r=1}^m\delta_{x^r}.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge}(a), $\pi^0$ is globally optimal for its
one-marginals.  By (ii), those marginals admit a coupling $\widehat\pi$ all
of whose edge marginals are pairwise optimal.  If $M_e$ denotes the
pairwise optimal value, then
\[
 \sum_e\int c_e\,d\pi^0_e
 \ \geq\ \sum_e\int c_e\,d\widehat\pi_e
 =\sum_eM_e
 \ \geq\ \sum_e\int c_e\,d\pi^0_e.
\]
Every inequality is therefore equality, and each $\pi^0_e$ is pairwise
optimal.  Comparing it with any coordinatewise rearrangement of the chosen
edge pairs gives the $c_e$-cyclical inequality.  The edge, the lifts, and
the rearrangement were arbitrary, proving (i).
\end{proof}

This theorem is a full equivalence for a fixed cost family.  Its value is
that it replaces a quantifier over all subsets $\Gamma$ and all cycle
lengths by a standard marginal-compatibility question.  For general
finite alphabets, however, condition \eqref{eq:simopt} is still a substantial
compatibility problem rather than an intrinsic normal form for the matrices
$c_e$.

\section{Complete classification for binary spaces}

Now take $X_i=\{0,1\}$ for every vertex.  Define the cross difference
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crossdifference}
 \Delta_e=c_e(0,0)+c_e(1,1)-c_e(0,1)-c_e(1,0).
\end{equation}
Modulo one-coordinate terms, $c_e(s,t)=\Delta_e st$.  Let $H$ be the graph
of edges with $\Delta_e\ne0$, and give such an edge the sign
$\varepsilon_e=\operatorname{sgn}(\Delta_e)$.  A signed graph is
\emph{balanced} if the product of the edge signs around every cycle is
$+1$.

\begin{theorem}[Binary iff-balance theorem]\label{thm:binary}
For binary marginal spaces and arbitrary real pair costs, the following are
equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Global $c$-cyclical monotonicity forces $c_e$-cyclical monotonicity of
every edge projection, for every set $\Gamma\subseteq\{0,1\}^V$.
\item The signed graph $(H,\varepsilon)$ is balanced.
\item There are vertex signs $\eta_i\in\{-1,1\}$ such that
$\varepsilon_{\{i,j\}}=\eta_i\eta_j$ on every edge of $H$.
\end{enumerate}
If the signed graph is unbalanced, failure in (i) already has a two-point
witness of the form $\Gamma=\{a,\mathbf1-a\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The equivalence of (ii) and (iii) is the elementary switching
characterization of balanced signed graphs: define $\eta_i$ as the product
of edge signs along a path from a chosen root; this is path-independent
exactly when every cycle has positive sign product.

Assume (iii).  At every vertex with $\eta_i=-1$, relabel the binary
coordinate by $s\mapsto1-s$.  Relabeling one endpoint reverses the sign of
the cross difference, so all transformed nonzero cross differences are
positive.  Given arbitrary Bernoulli one-marginals in the transformed
labels, take one uniform random variable $U$ and set
\[
 Y_i=\1_{\{U\leq p_i\}}.
\]
For every edge, this comonotone coupling maximizes
$\mathbb E[Y_iY_j]$, and hence maximizes the transformed pair cost; edges
with zero cross difference are indifferent.  Undoing the relabelings gives
one joint coupling simultaneously optimal on every edge.  Theorem
\ref{thm:compatibility} proves (i).

Now suppose that the signed graph is unbalanced.  Give every coordinate the
Bernoulli$(1/2)$ marginal.  On a positive edge the unique pairwise optimum
has equal bits almost surely; on a negative edge it has opposite bits almost
surely.  These requirements are inconsistent around an unbalanced cycle,
so edgewise optimal compatibility fails and Theorem~\ref{thm:compatibility}
already disproves (i).

For the promised explicit form, choose $a\in\{0,1\}^V$ maximizing the total
cost of
\[
 \pi_a=\tfrac12(\delta_a+\delta_{\mathbf1-a})
\]
among all such complementary pairs.  Any joint law with Bernoulli$(1/2)$
marginals may be symmetrized under $x\mapsto\mathbf1-x$ without changing
any pair-cost expectation.  The symmetrized law is a mixture of the
$\pi_a$'s.  Hence $\pi_a$ is in fact a globally optimal joint coupling.
Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge}(b) makes its support
$\Gamma=\{a,\mathbf1-a\}$ globally $c$-cyclically monotone.  No assignment
$a$ satisfies all signed edge relations on an unbalanced cycle, so some
edge has the wrong relation; its two-point projection violates the
corresponding $c_e$-cyclical inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Consistently supermodular costs]\label{cor:supermodular}
Suppose each $X_i$ is totally ordered and, with respect to these vertex
orders, every $c_e$ is supermodular:
\[
 c_e(x,y)+c_e(x',y')\geq c_e(x,y')+c_e(x',y)
 \quad(x\leq x',\ y\leq y').
\]
Then the fixed family $(c_e)_e$ has the projection property in
Theorem~\ref{thm:compatibility}(i).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For finitely supported marginals, use a common uniform random variable and
the quantile function of every marginal.  The resulting joint coupling is
comonotone on every edge.  The elementary adjacent-swap rearrangement
argument shows that each such pair coupling maximizes every supermodular
edge cost.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:compatibility}.
\end{proof}

The binary theorem says that the common-order mechanism is also necessary
on two-point spaces: vertexwise label reversals make every edge
supermodular exactly when the cross-difference signed graph is balanced.

\section{The costs-independent graph boundary}

An edge of a forest is a bridge.  Delete it and use one permutation on all
coordinates on one side and a second permutation on all coordinates on the
other side.  The sum associated with every internal edge is unchanged by a
common reindexing.  The deleted edge is the only term left in the global
cyclical inequality, so its two-marginal inequality follows.

A cycle is precisely the obstruction.  On binary coordinates, compare the
two aligned points $0^V$ and $1^V$.  Under arbitrary coordinate
permutations, each row determines a graph cut.  Choose one cycle edge with
weight $-1$ and all other cycle edges with weight $+1$.  Every cut meets a
cycle in an even number of edges, so every cut has nonnegative total weight;
this proves all global cyclical inequalities at once.  The negative edge
itself violates its two-marginal inequality.

\subsection{Exact characterization}

\begin{theorem}[Universal iff-forest theorem]\label{thm:main}
For a finite simple graph $G$, the following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $G$ is a forest.
\item $G$ has the universal projection property.
\end{enumerate}
If $G$ contains a cycle, failure in \textup{(ii)} already occurs with
$X_i=\{0,1\}$ and a two-point set $\Gamma$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume first that $G$ is a forest.  Fix an edge $e=\{a,b\}$.
Deleting $e$ splits its tree component into vertex sets $A$ and $B$, with
$a\in A$ and $b\in B$; all other connected components of $G$ are irrelevant.

Take $n$ points $x^1,\ldots,x^n\in\Gamma$ and arbitrary
$\alpha,\beta\in S_n$.  In \eqref{eq:globalcm}, set
\[
 \sigma_i=\alpha\quad(i\in A),\qquad
 \sigma_i=\beta\quad(i\in B),
\]
and use the identity permutation on every other component.  If an edge has
both endpoints in $A$ (or both in $B$), its total contribution is unchanged,
because a common permutation merely reindexes the sum over $r$.  The same is
true in every other component.  Since $e$ is the unique edge between $A$ and
$B$, cancellation in \eqref{eq:globalcm} leaves exactly
\[
 \sum_{r=1}^n c_e\bigl(x_a^{\alpha(r)},x_b^{\beta(r)}\bigr)
 \le
 \sum_{r=1}^n c_e(x_a^r,x_b^r).
\]
This is the required $c_e$-cyclical inequality.  Since $e,n,\alpha,\beta$
were arbitrary, every $\Gamma_e$ is $c_e$-cyclically monotone.

Conversely, suppose that $G$ contains a simple cycle $C$, and fix an edge
$e_0\in C$.  Let $X_i=\{0,1\}$ for all $i$ and
\[
 \Gamma=\{0^V,1^V\}.
\]
Assign edge weights
\[
 w_e=
 \begin{cases}
 -1,&e=e_0,\\
 1,&e\in C\setminus\{e_0\},\\
 0,&e\notin C,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 c_e(s,t)=w_e\1_{\{s=t\}}.
\]

We first prove that $\Gamma$ is $c$-cyclically monotone.  Choose any
$n$ points from $\Gamma$, allowing repetitions, and encode them by bits
$b_1,\ldots,b_n$: the $r$th point is $b_r^V$.  For arbitrary coordinate
permutations $\sigma_i$, define for each row
\[
 S_r=\{i\in V:b_{\sigma_i(r)}=1\}.
\]
The original coordinates agree across every edge.  Hence the difference
between the original and permuted total costs is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutsum}
 \sum_{r=1}^n c(b_r^V)
 -\sum_{r=1}^n c\bigl(b_{\sigma_1(r)},\ldots,b_{\sigma_N(r)}\bigr)
 =\sum_{r=1}^n\sum_{e\in\delta(S_r)}w_e,
\end{equation}
where $\delta(S)$ is the cut consisting of edges with exactly one endpoint
in $S$.

Every cut intersects a cycle in an even number of edges.  If $e_0$ is not
in $\delta(S)$, its cut weight is nonnegative.  If $e_0$ is in the cut,
at least one other edge of $C$ is also in the cut, and therefore
\[
 \sum_{e\in\delta(S)}w_e
 =-1+\#\bigl((C\setminus\{e_0\})\cap\delta(S)\bigr)\ge0.
\]
Thus the right side of \eqref{eq:cutsum} is nonnegative for all choices,
which is exactly \eqref{eq:globalcm}.

On the other hand,
$\Gamma_{e_0}=\{(0,0),(1,1)\}$ is not $c_{e_0}$-cyclically monotone.
For the two points and the permutation that swaps the second coordinate,
the original total is $-2$ while the permuted total is $0$, contradicting
the required inequality $0\le-2$.  Hence a graph containing a cycle does
not have the universal projection property.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Removing inessential pair terms]
Suppose a pair term has the form
$c_{ij}(x_i,x_j)=a_i(x_i)+a_j(x_j)$.  It may be removed without changing
any cyclical inequality.  Consequently, if the graph of nonseparable pair
terms is a forest, global cyclical monotonicity forces cyclical monotonicity
of every remaining two-marginal projection.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Under coordinate permutations, the total of a one-coordinate function is
unchanged.  Remove all such terms and apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification notes, limitations, and novelty}

Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge} explicitly handles the point at which
multi-marginal arguments can go wrong: a finite multi-stochastic array need
not itself be a permutation array.  Repetition and rationalization are what
turn an arbitrary strict transport improvement into one forbidden
coordinatewise rearrangement.  Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:compatibility} does not
assume a false multi-marginal Birkhoff theorem.

The binary classification was also checked computationally on the triangle.
The verification script enumerates all $256$ subsets
of $\{0,1\}^3$ for each of the eight nonzero sign patterns.  Finite LP
splitting tests found the universal projection property for exactly the four
balanced patterns and produced two-point witnesses for all four unbalanced
patterns.  This is only a sanity check; the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:binary} is symbolic and works on every finite graph with
arbitrary edge magnitudes and zero cross differences.

Theorem~\ref{thm:compatibility} is an exact answer in terms of simultaneous
pairwise optimizer compatibility, and Theorem~\ref{thm:binary} is a complete
intrinsic answer for two-point spaces.  What remains open here is an
intrinsic classification of arbitrary pair-cost matrices or functions
satisfying \eqref{eq:simopt}.  We also do not classify the individual sets
$\Gamma$ requested in the source paper's second question.

A bounded search through 9 August 2026 used the exact source question and
the phrases \emph{simultaneously optimal pairwise couplings},
\emph{marginal compatibility}, \emph{balanced signed graph},
\emph{binary}, and \emph{multi-marginal cyclical monotonicity}.  No exact
statement of Theorems~\ref{thm:compatibility} or \ref{thm:binary} was found.
There is important adjacent work.  Haasler, Ringh, Chen, and Karlsson
\cite{HRCK} formulate discrete tree-structured multi-marginal transport
through coupled pairwise problems and exhibit a triangle obstruction for
cyclic graphs.  The extremal-dependence literature reviews simultaneous
comonotone and pairwise countermonotone couplings and their compatibility
restrictions \cite{PW,LLW}.  Harary's classical balance theorem
\cite{Harary} supplies the signed-graph terminology and the switching
equivalence used in the binary proof.  None of these sources found in the
bounded search states the present cyclical-monotonicity characterizations.
Novelty confidence remains \emph{moderate}, not high.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Verify the sign convention in \eqref{eq:globalcm}, the cancellation after
deleting a forest edge, the rational-polytope step in
Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge}, and the complement symmetrization in
Theorem~\ref{thm:binary}.  Then compare the fixed-cost equivalence against
the marginal-polytope and extremal-dependence literature, and the forest
statement against Remark~3 of \cite{HRCK}, before any novelty claim.
