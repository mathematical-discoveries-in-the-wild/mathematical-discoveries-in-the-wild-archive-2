\section*{The source question}

Let $A$ be a maximal subdiagonal algebra in a finite von Neumann algebra
$M$ with faithful normal tracial state, and let
$D=A\cap A^*$ be its diagonal.  Under the assumption $\dim D<\infty$, the
source proves the following results:
\begin{itemize}
 \item Corollary~3.5: a functional annihilating $A+A^*$ has zero normal part;
 \item Theorem~4.1: normal functionals on $A$ have both relevant
 Gleason--Whitney conclusions;
 \item Theorem~4.2: each $\omega\in M_*$ is the unique Hahn--Banach extension
 of $\omega|_{A+A^*}$;
 \item Corollary~4.3: the unit ball of $A+A^*$ is weak* dense in the unit
 ball of $M$.
\end{itemize}
Immediately after Corollary~4.3, the source says that it does not know whether
``the last few results'' hold without finite-dimensionality of $D$.
This appears on source PDF page~12~\cite{source}.

\section*{Ueda's exact answer}

Ueda proves a peak-set theorem for every noncommutative $H^\infty$-algebra
$A=H^\infty(M,\tau)$, with no finite-dimensional restriction on $D$.
Theorem~4.1 of the supporting paper says that whenever a bounded functional
$\varphi$ annihilates $A$, its normal and singular parts separately annihilate
$A$.  Ueda then observes that the original proofs need only this weaker
F.~and M.~Riesz consequence, rather than the stronger finite-dimensional
theorem used in the source.

The paragraph immediately before Ueda's next theorem states that the proofs
of the source's Corollary~3.5, Theorems~4.1--4.2, and Corollary~4.3 therefore
all work without any dimensional assumption.  Ueda's theorem on supporting
PDF pages~6--7 records the resulting four conclusions explicitly:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item a functional annihilating $A+A^*$ is singular;
 \item every Hahn--Banach extension to $M$ of a normal functional on $A$ is
 normal;
 \item every $\varphi\in M_*$ is the unique Hahn--Banach extension of
 $\varphi|_{A+A^*}$, with preservation of norm;
 \item every element of $M$ is weak* approximable by a norm-bounded net from
 $A+A^*$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the finite-dimensional-diagonal question in the source has an
unqualified affirmative answer~\cite{ueda}.

\section*{Independent author confirmation}

The source authors later make the provenance completely explicit.  In the
introduction of arXiv:1605.08932 they state that Ueda removed the dimensional
restriction from ``four or five'' of their theorems.  Their Theorem~3.2 on
supporting PDF page~8 again proves, for every maximal subdiagonal algebra in a
$\sigma$-finite von Neumann algebra,
\[
 \operatorname{Ball}(A+A^*)
 \ \text{is weak* dense in}\ 
 \operatorname{Ball}(M),
\]
and explicitly identifies this as the removal of the restriction in the
source's Corollary~4.3.  Corollary~5.9 on supporting PDF page~17 gives the
corresponding Gleason--Whitney equivalences and says that they all hold for
maximal subdiagonal algebras~\cite{confirmation}.

The original source setting already assumes a faithful normal tracial state,
so $M$ is $\sigma$-finite.  Consequently, the later confirmation applies
directly and also extends beyond the tracial setting.

\section*{Scope and remaining question}

This packet settles only the source remark about removing $\dim D<\infty$.
It does not settle the separate Corollary~2.6 question asking whether the
unique normal state extension property alone forces a tracial algebra to be
maximal subdiagonal.  A separate attempt note records a focused investigation
of that surviving problem.

The source remark was checked on source PDF page~12.  Ueda's exact list was
