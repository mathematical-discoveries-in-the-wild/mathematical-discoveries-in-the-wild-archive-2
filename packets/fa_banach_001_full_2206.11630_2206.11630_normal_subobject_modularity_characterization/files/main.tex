\fbox{\parbox{0.91\linewidth}{\textbf{Candidate full result.}
For every real seminormed space and every $0<\varepsilon<1$, the
normal-subobject lattice considered by Fritz is modular if and only if the
Hausdorff quotient has dimension at most one.  The proof works for arbitrary
norms and in arbitrary (possibly incomplete and infinite) dimension.}}
\end{center}

\section{Source conjecture and transcription}

Fix $0<\varepsilon<1$.  Corollary 4.9 of the source identifies the normal
subobjects of a seminormed space $(V,p)$ with
\[
 \mathcal N_\varepsilon(V,p)
 =\{q:q\text{ is a seminorm on }V, p\le q\le\varepsilon^{-1}p\},
\]
ordered by reverse pointwise order.  The meet and join are
\[
 (q_1\wedge q_2)(x)=\max\{q_1(x),q_2(x)\},\qquad
 (q_1\vee q_2)(x)=\inf_{x_1+x_2=x}\bigl(q_1(x_1)+q_2(x_2)\bigr).
\]
Footnote 12 on printed page 23 conjectures:
\begin{quote}
$\mathcal N_\varepsilon(V,p)$ is modular if and only if
$\dim(V/V_0)\le1$, where $V_0=\{x:p(x)=0\}$.
\end{quote}

\section{Idea and geometric translation}

Every admissible $q$ has the same null space $V_0$ as $p$, so the problem
passes exactly to the normed quotient $X=V/V_0$.  Write $U=\{x:p(x)\le1\}$.
The unit ball $K_q=\{x:q(x)\le1\}$ is a closed, convex, centrally symmetric
set satisfying
\[
 \varepsilon U\subseteq K_q\subseteq U.
\]
Conversely every such set is the unit ball of an admissible norm.  Under this
correspondence the lattice order is ordinary inclusion and
\[
 K\wedge L=K\cap L,
 \qquad K\vee L=\clconv(K\cup L).
\]
Indeed, the pointwise maximum has intersection as its unit ball.  For the
infimal-convolution join, every decomposition of cost at most one gives a
convex combination of points of the two balls.  Conversely, decompositions
of cost at most $1+\delta$ put $x/(1+\delta)$ in their convex hull; letting
$\delta\downarrow0$ gives the closure.  The join is still bounded between
$p$ and $\varepsilon^{-1}p$, so its unit ball is closed in the $p$-topology.
Thus the conjecture becomes a local statement about the lattice of symmetric
convex bodies in an arbitrarily thin annulus.

The key observation is that every norm ball in dimension at least two has a
thin exposed cap containing a short line segment.  Three subsets of that
segment reproduce the elementary nonmodular configuration
\[
 \{0\}\subset[0,2],\qquad \{3\}\cap[0,2]=\varnothing,
 \qquad \operatorname{conv}(\{0,3\})\cap[0,2]=[0,2].
\]
Adding a common inner ball and reflecting through the origin makes all three
sets absorbing and centrally symmetric without changing the exposed cap.

\section{An exposed-cap lemma}

Let $(X,p)$ be a real normed space, let $\varphi\in X^*$ satisfy
$\|\varphi\|=1$, and choose numbers $0<r<c<1$.  Put
$H_c=\{x:\varphi(x)=c\}$.  For a nonempty compact convex set
$S\subset U\cap H_c$, define
\[
 K(S)=\clconv\bigl(rU\cup S\cup(-S)\bigr).
\]

\begin{lemma}[cap localization]
For every $z\in K(S)$,
\[
 \dist_p(z,S)\le \frac{2\,[c-\varphi(z)]}{c-r}.
\]
In particular, $K(S)\cap H_c=S$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First take $z$ in the nonclosed convex hull.  After collecting coefficients
inside each convex set, write
\[
 z=\alpha s-\beta t+\gamma b,
 \quad s,t\in S,quad b\in rU,quad
 \alpha,\beta,\gamma\ge0,quad \alpha+\beta+\gamma=1.
\]
Since $\varphi(s)=\varphi(t)=c$ and $\varphi(b)\le r$,
\[
 c-\varphi(z)
 \ge 2c\beta+(c-r)\gamma
 \ge(c-r)(\beta+\gamma)=(c-r)(1-\alpha).
\]
Also $S\subset U$ and $b\in rU\subset U$, so
\[
 p(z-s)\le(1-\alpha)p(s)+\beta p(t)+\gamma p(b)
 \le2(1-\alpha).
\]
Combining the inequalities proves the estimate.  Approximation by convex
combinations and compactness of $S$ extend it to the closed convex hull.  If
$\varphi(z)=c$, the right side is zero, so $z\in S$; the reverse inclusion is
immediate.
\end{proof}

\section{The characterization}

\begin{theorem}
Let $(V,p)$ be a real seminormed space, let
$V_0=\{x:p(x)=0\}$, and fix $0<\varepsilon<1$.  Then
$\mathcal N_\varepsilon(V,p)$ is modular if and only if
$\dim(V/V_0)\le1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All admissible seminorms vanish exactly on $V_0$, so quotienting by $V_0$
gives a lattice isomorphism with the corresponding norm lattice on
$X=V/V_0$.  We may therefore assume that $p$ is a norm.

If $\dim X=0$, the lattice has one element.  If $\dim X=1$, every norm on
$X$ is a scalar multiple of $p$.  Hence the admissible norms form a chain
(the scalar interval $[1,\varepsilon^{-1}]$, with reversed order), and every
chain is modular.

Assume now that $\dim X\ge2$.  Choose
\[
 \varepsilon<r<c<1,qquad x\in X,quad p(x)=1,
 \qquad \varphi\in X^*,quad \|\varphi\|=1,quad\varphi(x)=1.
\]
Hahn--Banach provides $\varphi$.  Choose $y\in\ker\varphi$ with $p(y)=1$
and $h>0$ so small that $3h<1-c$.  Then the four points
\[
 z_j=cx+jhy\qquad(j=0,1,2,3)
\]
belong to $U\cap H_c$.  In that hyperplane set
\[
 S_A=\{z_0\},\qquad S_C=[z_0,z_2],\qquad S_D=\{z_3\},
\]
and define $A=K(S_A)$, $C=K(S_C)$, and $D=K(S_D)$.  These are closed,
convex, symmetric bodies with
\[
 rU\subseteq A,C,D\subseteq U.
\]
Their gauges therefore satisfy
$p\le q\le r^{-1}p<\varepsilon^{-1}p$, so they are elements of the required
lattice.  Moreover $A\subseteq C$.

We compare the two sides of the modular identity at the exposed hyperplane
$H_c$.  First,
\[
 \dist_p(S_D,S_C)=h.
\]
Set $\eta=(c-r)h/5$.  If a point $z$ belonged to $D\cap C$ with
$\varphi(z)>c-\eta$, the cap lemma applied once to $D$ and once to $C$ would
give
\[
 \dist_p(z,S_D)<2h/5,qquad \dist_p(z,S_C)<2h/5,
\]
contradicting the distance $h$ between the two cap sets.  Thus
\[
 \sup_{z\in D\cap C}\varphi(z)\le c-\eta.
\]
It follows that adjoining $D\cap C$ to $A$ cannot create a new point on the
top exposed hyperplane: using the cap lemma for $A$, or simply taking
convex combinations and passing to limits, gives
\[
 \bigl(A\vee(D\wedge C)\bigr)\cap H_c=\{z_0\}.
\]

On the other hand,
\[
 (A\vee D)\cap H_c=[z_0,z_3],
 \qquad C\cap H_c=[z_0,z_2].
\]
Therefore
\[
 \bigl((A\vee D)\wedge C\bigr)\cap H_c=[z_0,z_2].
\]
In particular $z_1$ belongs to the right side but not the left side.  Hence
\[
 A\vee(D\wedge C)\ne(A\vee D)\wedge C
 \quad\text{despite }A\subseteq C,
\]
so the lattice is not modular.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The proof was checked against the source's reverse seminorm order and its
explicit meet/join formulas.  The inner radius $r$ may be chosen strictly
between $\varepsilon$ and $1$, so the construction works even when
$\varepsilon$ is arbitrarily close to $1$; no ``sufficiently small'' caveat
remains.  The cap estimate supplies the uniform gap needed when closed convex
hulls are used, so neither completeness nor finite dimensionality is hidden in
the proof.  Quotienting by $V_0$ is exact because every admissible seminorm has
the same null space.

\paragraph{Scope.}
This resolves only the real-scalar conjecture stated in the source.  It does
not develop the paper's broader proposed theory of $\varepsilon$-curved group
cohomology.  The same geometric idea should adapt to complex spaces after
realification, but that variant is not claimed here.

\paragraph{Novelty status.}
Bounded searches by the exact conjectural phrase, title, normal-subobject
lattice terminology, and seminorm-lattice modularity found the source paper
and general seminorm-lattice literature, but no later resolution or matching
exposed-cap proof.  Novelty is therefore plausible, not certified; expert and
author review are recommended before dissemination.
