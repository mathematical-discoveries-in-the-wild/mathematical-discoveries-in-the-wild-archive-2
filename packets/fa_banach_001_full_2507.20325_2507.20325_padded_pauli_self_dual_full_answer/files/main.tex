\section*{Source and exact question}

Eric Evert and Benjamin Passer, \emph{Matrix convex sets over the Euclidean
ball and polar duals of real free spectrahedra}, arXiv:2507.20325v2
(15 January 2026), Remark~3.20, PDF p.~23 \cite{EP}.  The paper was published
in \emph{Linear Algebra and its Applications} 736 (2026), 54--83.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.97\linewidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Thus the question as written is whether there are integers $d\geq3$ and
$g<d^2-d+2$, and a tuple $A\in\SM_d(\C)^g$, for which the free
spectrahedron $\D_A$ is self-dual.

\section*{Definitions}

For a self-adjoint tuple $B=(B_1,\ldots,B_g)\in\SM_r(\C)^g$, its monic
pencil at a self-adjoint matrix tuple $X=(X_1,\ldots,X_g)\in\SM_n(\C)^g$ is
\[
 L_B(X)=I_{rn}-\sum_{j=1}^g B_j\otimes X_j,
 \qquad
 \D_B(n)=\{X:L_B(X)\succeq0\}.
\]
The free polar dual $K^\circ$ depends only on the matrix convex set $K$;
hence equal free spectrahedra have equal polar duals.

Let
\[
 P_1=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 P_2=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 P_3=\begin{bmatrix}0&i\\-i&0\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Evert--Passer prove in their Theorem~2.2 that the Pauli free spectrahedron is
self-dual:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pauli}
 \D_P=\D_P^\circ.
\end{equation}
Their proof identifies $\D_P$ with the matrix range $\W(P)$ using Choi's
criterion and then invokes the standard free-polar relation
$\D_P^\circ=\W(P)$ \cite{EP}.

\section*{Proof intuition}

The size $d$ of a coefficient tuple is not intrinsic unless a minimality
condition is imposed.  Appending a zero reducing summand to every coefficient
does not change any positivity inequality: it merely appends a fixed identity
block to the monic pencil.  We therefore lift the paper's $2\times2$
self-dual Pauli example to every coefficient size $d\geq3$ without changing
the free spectrahedron.

\begin{lemma}[Zero-padding invariance]\label{lem:padding}
Let $B\in\SM_r(\C)^g$ and $d\geq r$.  Define
$A_j=B_j\oplus0_{d-r}$.  Then $\D_A=\D_B$ at every matrix level.  In
particular, boundedness and self-duality pass from $\D_B$ to $\D_A$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every $n$ and every $X\in\SM_n(\C)^g$, Kronecker products respect direct
sums, so
\begin{align*}
 L_A(X)
 &=I_{dn}-\sum_{j=1}^g(B_j\oplus0_{d-r})\otimes X_j\\
 &=\left(I_{rn}-\sum_{j=1}^gB_j\otimes X_j\right)
   \oplus I_{(d-r)n}\\
 &=L_B(X)\oplus I_{(d-r)n}.
\end{align*}
The last matrix is positive semidefinite exactly when $L_B(X)$ is.  Hence
$\D_A(n)=\D_B(n)$ for every $n$.  Equality of the sets preserves boundedness,
and $\D_A^\circ=\D_B^\circ$ by the definition of free polarity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Affirmative answer to Remark~3.20]\label{thm:answer}
For every integer $d\geq3$ there exists
$A\in\SM_d(\C)^3$ such that $\D_A$ is bounded and self-dual.  Consequently
$g=3<d^2-d+2$, and the existence question in Remark~3.20 has an affirmative
answer as stated.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $j=1,2,3$, set
\[
 A_j=P_j\oplus0_{d-2}\in\SM_d(\C).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:padding} and \eqref{eq:pauli} give
\[
 \D_A=\D_P=\D_P^\circ=\D_A^\circ.
\]
The set is bounded because $\D_P=\W(P)$ in Theorem~2.2 of \cite{EP}, and the
matrix range of a finite matrix tuple is bounded.  Finally, for $d\geq3$,
\[
 d^2-d+2\geq8>3=g.
\]
All stated requirements are therefore satisfied (already with $d=3$).
\end{proof}

\section*{Verification and limitations}

The proof is an identity at every free level, not a scalar-level argument.
A seeded numerical regression check reproduced the block identity for
$d=3,4,5$, levels $n=1,2,3$, and ten random self-adjoint triples per pair;
the algebraic computation in Lemma~\ref{lem:padding} is the proof.

The construction has a zero reducing summand.  It is therefore not a minimal
defining pencil and does not span $M_d$ as an operator system.  Remark~3.20
does not impose irreducibility, size-minimality, or a spanning condition, so
this is a full answer to its literal statement.  The strengthened question
under any such nondegeneracy hypothesis remains open here.
