\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Candidate full counterexample and corrected result, likely valid, for the
statements surrounding Theorem~1.3 of the source paper.

\section{Source statement and definition}
Koskela and Nguyen define a weak polar coordinate system
$(\Sph,\sigma,\Gamma^O,h)$ by requiring a probability measure $\sigma$, a
family of infinite curves $\gamma^O_\xi$ starting from $O$, a weight
$h:X\to[0,\infty)$, and a constant $C>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{polar}
 \int_{\Sph}\int_{\gamma^O_\xi}|f|h\,ds\,d\sigma(\xi)
 \le C\int_X|f|\,d\mu
\end{equation}
for every integrable $f$~\cite{KN}.  They explicitly note that $h$ is not
assumed strictly positive.

For $A_{2^j}(O)=B(O,2^{j+1})\setminus B(O,2^j)$, they set
\begin{align*}
 R_p(h,O)&=\int_{X\setminus B(O,1)}h^{-p/(p-1)}\,d\mu,
 &&p>1,\\
 \mathcal R_p(O)&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb N}
 (2^j)^{p/(p-1)}\mu(A_{2^j}(O))^{-1/(p-1)},
 &&p>1,
\end{align*}
with the corresponding $p=1$ definitions
\[
 R_1(h,O)=\|h^{-1}\|_{L^\infty_\mu(X\setminus B(O,1))},
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_1(O)=\sup_j\frac{2^j}{\mu(A_{2^j}(O))}.
\]

Theorem~1.3(I) states that $R_p(h,O)<\infty$ suffices for five radial-limit
and modulus properties.  Immediately afterward the authors infer
$R_p(h,O)<\infty\Rightarrow\mathcal R_p(O)<\infty$ and ask for a simple
direct proof~\cite[p.~3]{KN}.

\section{Proof intuition}
The inequality~\eqref{polar} only sees the weighted line measure $h\,ds$.
If $h$ vanishes on every selected radial curve, the left side is identically
zero and the condition places no geometric restriction on the family.  The
negative-power integral $R_p$, however, is computed using $\mu$ and ignores
an infinite value on a $\mu$-null ray.  This mismatch gives a counterexample.

The natural repair is exactly the positivity mentioned after the source
definition.  Once $h>0$ along almost every radial point, test~\eqref{polar}
with a truncated version of $\mathbf1_A/h$.  It recovers unweighted curve
length.  Every radial curve must spend length at least $2^j$ crossing the
$j$th dyadic annulus, and one application of H\"older gives the corresponding
summand of $\mathcal R_p$.

\section{Counterexample}
\begin{theorem}\label{counterexample}
Under the printed definition of weak polar coordinates,
\[
 R_p(h,O)<\infty\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathcal R_p(O)<\infty
\]
is false.  Moreover, Theorem~1.3(I) of the source is false as printed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take
\[
 X=\R^2,\qquad d=d_E,\qquad d\mu=dx,
 \qquad O=0,\qquad p=2.
\]
This is a doubling metric measure space supporting a $2$-Poincar\'e
inequality and the annular chain property.  Let $\Sph=\{\ast\}$ with its
unit point mass and choose the single radial curve
\[
 \gamma_\ast^O(t)=(t,0),\qquad t\ge0.
\]
Writing $L=\{(t,0):t\ge0\}$, define the Borel coordinate weight
\[
 h(x)=\begin{cases}
  0,&x\in L,\\
  e^{|x|},&x\notin L.
 \end{cases}
\]
For every integrable $f$, the left side of~\eqref{polar} is
\[
 \int_0^\infty |f(t,0)|h(t,0)\,dt=0.
\]
Thus this is a weak polar coordinate system, even with $C=1$.

The ray $L$ is Lebesgue-null, so $h^{-2}=e^{-2|x|}$ almost everywhere.
Consequently
\[
 R_2(h,0)=\int_{\R^2\setminus B(0,1)}h^{-2}\,dx
 =2\pi\int_1^\infty r e^{-2r}\,dr
 =\frac{3\pi}{2}e^{-2}<\infty.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
 \mu(A_{2^j}(0))
 =\pi\bigl((2^{j+1})^2-(2^j)^2\bigr)
 =3\pi4^j,
\]
and hence
\[
 \mathcal R_2(0)
 =\sum_{j\in\mathbb N}\frac{(2^j)^2}{\mu(A_{2^j}(0))}
 =\sum_{j\in\mathbb N}\frac1{3\pi}=\infty.
\]
This disproves the asserted implication.

It also directly contradicts Theorem~1.3(I).  The tail family
$\widehat\Gamma^O(\Sph)$ consists of one shifted ray.  The indicator of its
first unit segment is admissible for its $2$-modulus, has line integral one,
and has zero planar $L^2$ norm.  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Mod}_2(\widehat\Gamma^O(\Sph))=0,
\]
whereas property~4 in the source theorem asserts strict positivity.
\end{proof}

\section{Corrected direct theorem}
The counterexample exploits only the omitted positivity condition.  The
following statement supplies both a repair and the requested elementary
proof.

\begin{proposition}\label{repair}
Assume the source's weak polar-coordinate inequality~\eqref{polar}, and in
addition assume
\[
 h(\gamma^O_\xi(t))>0
 \quad\text{for }ds\,d\sigma(\xi)\text{-almost every radial point.}
\]
Then, whenever the right side is finite,
\[
 \mathcal R_p(O)\le C^{p/(p-1)}R_p(h,O)\quad(p>1),
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_1(O)\le C R_1(h,O).
\]
In particular, $R_p(h,O)<\infty$ directly implies
$\mathcal R_p(O)<\infty$.  No doubling, Poincar\'e, or annular-chain
hypothesis is used in this numerical implication beyond the standing
finiteness of the annular measures.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $j$ and abbreviate $A_j=A_{2^j}(O)$.  Every infinite continuous curve
from $O$ reaches radii $2^j$ and $2^{j+1}$.  Taking the last visit to the
inner sphere before a first subsequent visit to the outer sphere shows that
the intervening subcurve lies in $A_j$ apart from its endpoints and has
length at least $2^j$.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{crossing}
 \int_{\gamma^O_\xi}\mathbf1_{A_j}\,ds\ge2^j
 \qquad(\xi\in\Sph).
\end{equation}

Apply~\eqref{polar} to the bounded integrable functions
\[
 f_N=\mathbf1_{A_j}\min\{N,h^{-1}\}.
\]
Radial almost-everywhere positivity and monotone convergence give
\[
 2^j
 \le\int_{\Sph}\int_{\gamma^O_\xi}\mathbf1_{A_j}\,ds\,d\sigma
 \le C\int_{A_j}h^{-1}\,d\mu.
\]
For $p>1$, write $q=p/(p-1)$.  H\"older's inequality yields
\[
 \int_{A_j}h^{-1}\,d\mu
 \le\mu(A_j)^{1/p}
     \left(\int_{A_j}h^{-q}\,d\mu\right)^{1/q}.
\]
Raising to the power $q$ and rearranging,
\[
 (2^j)^q\mu(A_j)^{-1/(p-1)}
 \le C^q\int_{A_j}h^{-q}\,d\mu.
\]
The annuli are disjoint, so summation over $j$ gives
$\mathcal R_p(O)\le C^qR_p(h,O)$.

For $p=1$, the same crossing argument gives
\[
 2^j\le C\int_{A_j}h^{-1}\,d\mu
 \le C R_1(h,O)\mu(A_j).
\]
Taking the supremum over $j$ proves the endpoint estimate.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, novelty, and verification}
Theorem~\ref{counterexample} concerns the literal published definition and
statement.  Proposition~\ref{repair} does not assert that radial positivity
is the weakest possible repair, but it is natural, is already flagged as
important immediately after the source definition, and exactly removes the
zero-weight mechanism.

A bounded search on 2026-08-17 covered arXiv:2203.01020, the 2023 accepted
and journal versions, the exact phrase ``do not know a simple direct proof'',
the theorem title and authors together with ``erratum'' and ``correction'',
and weak-polar/coordinate-weight keywords.  The accepted version retains the
same definition and request; no erratum, later explicit direct proof, or
record of this zero-weight counterexample was found.  This is not a priority
claim.  Novelty confidence is moderate and mathematical-validity confidence
is high.

The exact integral and annular-term calculations are checked by the
accompanying verification script; it is only a sanity check.  The independent
audit is in \texttt{VERIFICATION.md}.
Human review should focus on the literal absence of radial positivity; the
source itself explicitly emphasizes that strict positivity was not assumed.
