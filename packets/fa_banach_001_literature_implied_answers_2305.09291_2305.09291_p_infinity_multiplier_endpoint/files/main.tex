\paragraph{Status.}
\textbf{Literature-implied answer (endpoint subcase).}
This note completely answers the question at $p=\infty$.  It does not settle
the finite-$p$ cases.

\section*{Source question}
Let $(G,K)$ be a compact symmetric Gelfand pair of rank one and let
$d=\dim(G/K)$.  Dumas proves regularity for every $K$-bi-invariant
$S_p(L^2(G))$-multiplier by testing it against an $S_p$-valued operator path
$T$.  Remark~3.18 of \cite{Dumas}, on PDF page~28, asks whether this
regularity is optimal, observing that the paper does not construct
multipliers attaining the threshold.

Set
\[
 a=\frac{d-1}{2},\qquad
 \mathcal R(a)=\bigl(\lfloor a\rfloor,a-\lfloor a\rfloor\bigr).
\]
Regularity pairs are ordered lexicographically, as in \cite{Dumas}.

\section*{Identification and result}
The missing endpoint observation is that compactness forces every
$S_\infty$ Herz--Schur multiplier to be a unitary matrix coefficient.
Indeed, Spronk's Corollary~5.4 identifies invariant measurable Schur
multipliers on an amenable locally compact group with its
Fourier--Stieltjes algebra $B(G)$ \cite{Spronk}.  Compact groups are amenable,
and elements of $B(G)$ are precisely coefficients of unitary
representations.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp $p=\infty$ endpoint]
Let $(G,K)$ be a compact symmetric Gelfand pair of rank one.  Every
$K$-bi-invariant $S_\infty(L^2(G))$-multiplier $\varphi$ satisfies
\[
 \varphi\circ\exp\in C^{\mathcal R(a)}(Q).
\]
Moreover, for every regularity pair $(r,\delta)>\mathcal R(a)$ there is a
$K$-bi-invariant $S_\infty$-multiplier $\varphi_{r,\delta}$ such that
\[
 \varphi_{r,\delta}\circ\exp\notin C^{(r,\delta)}(Q).
\]
Thus $\mathcal R(a)$ is the optimal endpoint regularity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $\varphi$ is an $S_\infty$-multiplier, its Toeplitz kernel
\[
 u(s,t)=\varphi(st^{-1})
\]
defines an invariant measurable Schur multiplier on $B(L^2(G))$.  Since $G$
is compact and hence amenable, Spronk's Corollary~5.4 gives
$\varphi\in B(G)$.  Therefore there are a unitary representation $\pi$ of
$G$ and vectors $\xi,\eta$ such that
$\varphi(g)=\langle\pi(g)\xi,\eta\rangle$.  The function is already assumed
$K$-bi-invariant, so Dumas's Theorem~A applies and yields
$\varphi\circ\exp\in C^{\mathcal R(a)}(Q)$.

For sharpness, Dumas's Corollary~3.17(5) states that for every
$(r,\delta)>\mathcal R(a)$ there is a $K$-bi-invariant unitary matrix
coefficient $\psi$ which does not belong to $C^{(r,\delta)}(Q)$.  Such a
coefficient is an $S_\infty$-multiplier: if
$\psi(g)=\langle\pi(g)\xi,\eta\rangle$, then its Toeplitz kernel factors as
\[
 \psi(st^{-1})
 =\big\langle\pi(t^{-1})\xi,\pi(s^{-1})\eta\big\rangle.
\]
The two vector-valued factors are bounded, so the standard Schur
factorization gives
$\|S_\psi\|\leq\|\xi\|\,\|\eta\|$.  Hence $\psi$ is the required
$S_\infty$-multiplier.
\end{proof}

\section*{Integer endpoint consequence}
If $a\notin\Z$, the theorem gives exactly the regularity of the operator path
$T$ used in Corollary~3.13 of \cite{Dumas}.  If $a=m\in\Z$, that path is
proved only to lie in $C^{(m-1,1)}$, whereas the theorem shows that every
$S_\infty$-multiplier lies in $C^{(m,0)}=C^m$, and that $C^m$ is sharp.
Thus in the integer case the path regularity is not the true multiplier
threshold; no endpoint multiplier can have only the weaker regularity of
$T$.

\section*{Provenance, search, and limitations}
The implication above is not presented as new mathematics.  It combines
Dumas's sharp rank-one matrix-coefficient theorem and optimality corollary
with Spronk's standard amenable-group identification.  Spronk predates the
source question and does not claim to answer it; the connection is the
identification made in this packet.  Exact-phrase and title searches of arXiv
and the published source located no separate paper explicitly resolving
Remark~3.18.  Nothing here addresses optimality for finite $p$.
