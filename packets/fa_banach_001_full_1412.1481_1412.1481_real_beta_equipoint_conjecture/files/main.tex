\section{Source problem}

For $a,b>0$, write $X_{a,b}$ for a beta random variable, with density
\[
  \frac{x^{a-1}(1-x)^{b-1}}{\B(a,b)},\qquad 0<x<1.
\]
The source defines an equipoint $e_{a,b}$ and states that its inequality
$e_{a,b}\leq a/(a+b)$ is equivalent to the following comparison of beta
tails. Conjecture~1.13 on printed and PDF page~13 asks whether the comparison
holds for all real $a\geq b>0$~\cite{source}.

\begin{center}
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Transcription.}
For $a,b\in\mathbb R_{>0}$ with $a\geq b$, prove
\begin{equation}
 \Prob\!\left(X_{a,b+1}\geq \frac{a}{a+b}\right)
 \leq
 \Prob\!\left(X_{a+1,b}\leq \frac{a}{a+b}\right).
 \label{eq:C}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, prove $e_{a,b}\leq a/(a+b)$.

\section{Proof intuition}

The two sides of \eqref{eq:C} initially look like unrelated incomplete-beta
tails. Passing to odds, $z=x/(1-x)$, places both tails on the beta-prime
line, separated at $q=a/b$. Scaling the lower half by $z=qu$ and inverting
the upper half by $z=q/u$ puts both integrals on $(0,1)$.

The resulting integrand difference has one sign change: it is negative near
zero and positive afterward. There is also an exact weighted cancellation
with weight $(1-u)/u$. That cancellation is the zero first centered moment of
a beta-prime density whose mean is $q$. Because the weight is strictly
decreasing, cancellation under that weight can occur only if the unweighted
positive area is larger than the unweighted negative area. This is exactly
the desired inequality.

\section{The theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $a\geq b>0$, inequality \eqref{eq:C} holds. Equality holds when
$a=b$, and the inequality is strict when $a>b$. Consequently,
\[
  e_{a,b}\leq \frac{a}{a+b}
  \qquad(a\geq b>0),
\]
with equality exactly in the symmetric case $a=b$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof}

Put
\[
 d=a+b,\qquad m=\frac ad,
 \qquad q=\frac ab=\frac{m}{1-m}.
\]
The beta recurrences
\[
 \B(a+1,b)=\frac ad\B(a,b),\qquad
 \B(a,b+1)=\frac bd\B(a,b)
\]
show that the right side of \eqref{eq:C} minus the left side has the sign of
\begin{equation}
 \frac1a\int_0^m x^a(1-x)^{b-1}\,dx
 -\frac1b\int_m^1x^{a-1}(1-x)^b\,dx.
 \label{eq:first}
\end{equation}
With the odds substitution $z=x/(1-x)$, expression \eqref{eq:first} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \frac1a\int_0^q\frac{z^a}{(1+z)^{d+1}}\,dz
 -\frac1b\int_q^\infty\frac{z^{a-1}}{(1+z)^{d+1}}\,dz.
 \label{eq:odds}
\end{equation}

In the first integral set $z=qu$; in the second set $z=q/u$. Since
$q/a=1/b$, the two resulting positive prefactors agree. Therefore
\eqref{eq:odds} has the sign of
\begin{equation}
 H:=\int_0^1\bigl(P(u)-Q(u)\bigr)\,du,
 \label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 P(u)=\frac{u^a}{(1+qu)^{d+1}},
 \qquad
 Q(u)=\frac{u^b}{(q+u)^{d+1}}.
 \label{eq:PQ}
\end{equation}

We first prove an exact weighted cancellation. Let
$w(u)=(1-u)/u$. Undoing the two substitutions above gives
\begin{align*}
 \frac{q^a}{b}\int_0^1w(u)P(u)\,du
 &=\frac1a\int_0^q(q-z)\frac{z^{a-1}}{(1+z)^{d+1}}\,dz,\\
 \frac{q^a}{b}\int_0^1w(u)Q(u)\,du
 &=\frac1a\int_q^\infty(z-q)\frac{z^{a-1}}{(1+z)^{d+1}}\,dz.
\end{align*}
The density kernel
\[
 \varphi(z)=\frac{z^{a-1}}{(1+z)^{d+1}}
\]
has mean $q$ after normalization, because
\[
 \frac{\int_0^\infty z\varphi(z)\,dz}
      {\int_0^\infty \varphi(z)\,dz}
 =\frac{\B(a+1,b)}{\B(a,b+1)}=\frac ab=q.
\]
Its lower and upper first deviations about $q$ are consequently equal.
Thus
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^1 w(u)\bigl(P(u)-Q(u)\bigr)\,du=0.
 \label{eq:weightedzero}
\end{equation}
All integrals here are finite for $a,b>0$; near zero the only potentially
singular weighted term is of order $u^{b-1}$.

It remains to determine the sign pattern of $P-Q$. If $a=b$, then $q=1$ and
$P=Q$, so equality follows. Suppose $a>b$, hence $q>1$. For $0<u<1$, set
\[
 R(u)=\frac{P(u)}{Q(u)}
 =u^{a-b}\left(\frac{q+u}{1+qu}\right)^{d+1},
 \qquad g(u)=\log R(u).
\]
We have $g(u)\to-\infty$ as $u\downarrow0$ and $g(1)=0$. Moreover,
\begin{align}
 g'(u)
 &=(a-b)\frac1u
   -(d+1)\frac{q^2-1}{(q+u)(1+qu)} \notag\\
 &=(q-1)\left[
   \frac bu-\frac{(q+1)(d+1)}{(q+u)(1+qu)}
   \right].
 \label{eq:gprime}
\end{align}
The sign of the bracket is the sign of
\begin{equation}
 N(u)=bq u^2-(2bq+q+1)u+bq.
 \label{eq:N}
\end{equation}
The two roots of $N$ have product one and sum
\[
 2+\frac{q+1}{bq}>2.
\]
Hence exactly one root lies in $(0,1)$ and the other lies in $(1,\infty)$.
It follows that $g$ first increases and then decreases on $(0,1)$. Since
$g(1)=0$ and $g'(1)<0$, $g$ is positive immediately to the left of one.
Consequently there is a unique $c\in(0,1)$ such that
\begin{equation}
 P(u)-Q(u)<0\quad(0<u<c),
 \qquad
 P(u)-Q(u)>0\quad(c<u<1).
 \label{eq:crossing}
\end{equation}

Finally, $w(u)=(1-u)/u$ is strictly decreasing. Using
\eqref{eq:weightedzero} and the crossing point $c$, we obtain
\[
 0=\int_0^1\bigl(w(u)-w(c)\bigr)(P(u)-Q(u))\,du+w(c)H.
\]
By \eqref{eq:crossing}, the first integral is strictly negative: on each side
of $c$, its two factors have opposite signs. Since $w(c)>0$, this forces
$H>0$. Equations \eqref{eq:first}--\eqref{eq:H} now give the strict form of
\eqref{eq:C} when $a>b$. The source proves the equivalence with the equipoint
formulation, completing the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. \qed

equipoints, Simmons' theorem, and the incomplete-beta inequality. A bounded
external search used the exact label and wording, source title and authors,
equipoint notation, and close incomplete-beta phrases. It found the source
arXiv, AMS, and author-hosted versions, plus adjacent incomplete-beta work,
but no later proof, counterexample, or claimed resolution of Conjecture~1.13.

This packet resolves Conjecture~1.13 only. The distinct monotonicity
Conjecture~1.16 on the next page remains outside its scope. Human review should
focus on the common prefactor in \eqref{eq:H} and the direction of the final
decreasing-weight comparison.
