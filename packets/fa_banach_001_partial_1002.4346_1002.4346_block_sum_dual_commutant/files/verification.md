# Verification notes

Status: `partial_result_likely_valid`

1. **Topological fullness of the sum.** For (x\ge0), the closure of
   (Z(E)x) is exactly the (c_0)- or \(\ell_p\)-sum of the component ideals
   \(\overline{Z(E_\gamma)x_\gamma}\). The reverse inclusion is obtained by
   first truncating to finitely many coordinates and then choosing one
   component-center operator on each retained coordinate. This is an ideal.

2. **Center of the primal sum.** Central operators preserve every coordinate
   band and restrict to central operators on it. Conversely, uniformly
   bounded component-center operators define a global central operator.

3. **Block diagonalization on the dual.** If (Q_\gamma) is the coordinate
   projection, commutation with (Q_\gamma') yields
   \((Tx')_\gamma=T_\gamma x'_\gamma\) for every (x'\in E'). This remains
   valid for (p=1), even though (E') is an \(\ell_\infty\)-product.
   Therefore there is no residual operator with zero coordinate maps.

4. **Component theorem.** Extending (S\in Z(E_\gamma)) by zero on the other
   coordinates shows that (T_\gamma) commutes with the adjoint copy of the
   full component center. The quoted Abramovich--Arenson--Kitover theorem for
   a Banach lattice with quasi-interior point gives
   (T_\gamma\in Z(E_\gamma')).

5. **Reassembly.** The restrictions satisfy
   \(\|T_\gamma\|\le\|T\|\). Hence the componentwise family is a bounded
   central operator on the \(\ell_q\)- or \(\ell_\infty\)-product dual, and
   equals (T) coordinatewise.
