\noindent\textbf{Status.}
\texttt{literature\_already\_answered} (dimension constancy and the resulting
one-stratum perimeter formula).

\section*{Original open direction}

Let $(X,d,\mathfrak m)$ be an $\operatorname{RCD}(K,N)$ space of essential
dimension $n$, and let $E\subset X$ have finite perimeter.  The source
decomposes the reduced boundary into strata $\mathcal F_kE$, whose blow-ups
are $k$-dimensional Euclidean half-spaces, and proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:old}
 |D\chi_E|=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\omega_{k-1}}{\omega_k}
  \mathcal H^h\!\restriction\mathcal F_kE.
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal H^h$ is the codimension-one Hausdorff-type measure with gauge
$h(B_r(x))=\mathfrak m(B_r(x))/r$.  On source PDF page 4, the authors ask
whether tangent dimension is constant for finite-perimeter boundaries and
whether \eqref{eq:old} can be sharpened using the top-dimensional case
\cite{BPS19}.

\section*{Later answer}

The same authors answered this in arXiv:2109.12585.  Its introduction
explicitly identifies the possibility that
$|D\chi_E|(\mathcal F_kE)>0$ for $k<n$ as the question left open in the
source, and calls its first main result a negative answer.

Theorem 1.3 on answering PDF page 3 (equivalently Corollary 3.2 on page 16)
states that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant}
 |D\chi_E|(\mathcal F_kE)=0\qquad(k\ne n).
\end{equation}
It follows that the perimeter measure is concentrated on the ambient
$n$-regular stratum and \eqref{eq:old} becomes the single-term formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:new}
 |D\chi_E|=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{\omega_n}
  \mathcal H^h\!\restriction\mathcal F_nE.
\end{equation}
This is exactly the requested constancy and the corresponding
top-dimensional sharpening.  The later Theorem 3.1 is stronger: the total
variation $|Df|$ of every $BV$ function is concentrated on the $n$-regular
set.  Theorem 3.4 also proves that the tangent module over every
finite-perimeter boundary has constant dimension $n$ \cite{BPS21}.

\section*{Scope}

The resolution is complete for dimension constancy and the reduction of the
source's stratified formula to \eqref{eq:new}.  In a general collapsed space,
the measure in \eqref{eq:new} remains the reference-measure-dependent
$\mathcal H^h$.  This note therefore does not claim a general conversion to
ordinary $(n-1)$-dimensional Hausdorff measure.  In the non-collapsed case the
later paper records that the expected conversion is already available.
