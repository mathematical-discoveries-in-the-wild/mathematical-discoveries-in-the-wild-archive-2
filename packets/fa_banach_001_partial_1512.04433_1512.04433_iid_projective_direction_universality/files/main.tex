\section{Source question and scope}

Oymak and Recht study sign/Hamming embeddings of subsets of the unit sphere
and ask which matrix ensembles can exhibit Gaussian behavior
\cite{OymakRecht}.  In the numerical-experiments discussion on PDF page 16,
they write:

\begin{quote}
``Following from this example, it would be interesting to study the class of
matrices that has the same empirical behavior as a Gaussian.  For linear
embedding this question has been studied extensively [9,19] and it remains as
an open problem whether results of similar flavor would hold for binary
embedding.''
\end{quote}

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{The source passage, rendered from page 16 of arXiv:1512.04433.}
\end{figure}

The phrase ``same empirical behavior'' is intentionally broad.  This packet
isolates the exact iid, unshifted formulation: rows are independent copies of
one random vector, the quantizer is the sign at threshold zero, and ``same''
means equality in law of the complete array of pairwise empirical Hamming
distances on every finite set.  In this formulation there is a sharp answer.

\section{Definitions and statement}

Fix $n\geq 1$.  A random vector $A\in\R^n$ is called
\emph{hyperplane-nondegenerate} if
\[
  \Pp\{\langle A,x\rangle=0\}=0
  \qquad(x\in\Sph^{n-1}).
\]
This condition in particular implies $A\neq0$ almost surely.  Its
\emph{projective direction} is the unoriented line $[A]\in\R P^{n-1}$.
Let $\overline\mu$ denote the law of $[A]$, and let $\mu$ be its even lift to
$\Sph^{n-1}$: conditionally on a line, choose either unit representative with
probability $1/2$.  Write $\sigma$ for Haar probability on the sphere.

For iid rows $A_1,\dots,A_m$ and $x,y\in\Sph^{n-1}$ set
\[
  h_m(x,y)=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m
  \mathbf1\!\left\{
  \sgn\langle A_j,x\rangle\neq\sgn\langle A_j,y\rangle
  \right\},
  \qquad
  d_{\rm ang}(x,y)=\frac{\arccos\langle x,y\rangle}{\pi}.
\]
The population separation metric is
\[
  d_\mu(x,y)=\Pp\{\sgn\langle A,x\rangle
                     \neq\sgn\langle A,y\rangle\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact iid projective universality]\label{thm:main}
Let $A\in\R^n$ be hyperplane-nondegenerate.  The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The projective direction $[A]$ is Haar-uniform on $\R P^{n-1}$.
\item For every finite $K\subset\Sph^{n-1}$ and every $m\geq1$, the joint
array $(h_m(x,y))_{x,y\in K}$ has exactly the same law as for $m$ iid standard
Gaussian rows.
\item For every $x,y\in\Sph^{n-1}$,
\[
  d_\mu(x,y)=d_{\rm ang}(x,y).
\]
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, projective direction is the complete invariant for exact iid,
unshifted Gaussian universality of pairwise binary distances.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Any non-Haar law has a two-point obstruction]
If $[A]$ is not projective Haar, there exist $x,y\in\Sph^{n-1}$ and $b>0$
such that
\[
 |d_\mu(x,y)-d_{\rm ang}(x,y)|=b.
\]
For iid copies of $A$ and every $0<\delta<b$,
\[
 \Pp\{|h_m(x,y)-d_{\rm ang}(x,y)|\leq\delta\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{corollary}

\section{Proof intuition}

Pairwise sign disagreements ignore both the row length and a simultaneous
flip of all signs, so the row's projective direction is the only possible
invariant.  Haar projective direction is plainly sufficient.  The substantive
point is necessity.

Subtract Haar measure from the even spherical lift of the row law.  Equality
of all separation probabilities says that this signed measure annihilates
every product of two hemisphere-sign functions.  The hemisphere-sign
transform has a nonzero Funk--Hecke eigenvalue in every odd harmonic degree,
so every odd spherical harmonic can be recovered from such sign functions.
The signed measure therefore annihilates every product of two odd harmonics.
Those products span the even spherical polynomials, which determine an even
measure.  Hence the measure difference vanishes.

This gives exact classification in the iid model, but not the source paper's
dependent fast-transform problem.  It also reveals a sharp obstruction:
ordinary isotropy and subgaussian tails do not control projective direction.

\section{Proof of the characterization}

For $x\in\Sph^{n-1}$ define the hemisphere-sign function
\[
  s_x(u)=\sgn\langle u,x\rangle,
  \qquad u\in\Sph^{n-1}.
\]
Values on the equator are immaterial under the nondegeneracy assumption and
under Haar measure.

\begin{lemma}[Odd hemisphere transform is invertible by degree]
\label{lem:fh}
For every odd spherical harmonic $Y$ on $\Sph^{n-1}$ there is a nonzero
constant $\lambda_Y$ such that
\[
  \int_{\Sph^{n-1}} s_x(u)Y(x)\,\dd\sigma(x)
  =\lambda_Y Y(u).
\]
The constant depends only on the harmonic degree (and on $n$).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Funk--Hecke formula diagonalizes every zonal convolution on spherical
harmonics; see, for example, \cite[Chapter 1]{DaiXu}.  Applied to the kernel
$t\mapsto\sgn t$, it gives the asserted identity.  It remains to verify that
the eigenvalue is nonzero in every odd degree.

Suppose first that $n\geq3$, put $\alpha=(n-2)/2$, and let the odd degree be
$\ell=2k+1$.  Up to nonzero normalization factors, its eigenvalue is
\begin{align*}
 I_\ell
 &=\int_{-1}^{1}\sgn(t)C_\ell^\alpha(t)
       (1-t^2)^{\alpha-1/2}\,\dd t\\
 &=2\int_0^1 C_\ell^\alpha(t)
       (1-t^2)^{\alpha-1/2}\,\dd t,
\end{align*}
where $C_\ell^\alpha$ is the Gegenbauer polynomial.  Rodrigues' formula says
that the integrand is a nonzero constant times
\[
 \frac{\dd^\ell}{\dd t^\ell}
 (1-t^2)^{\ell+\alpha-1/2}.
\]
After integration, the derivative of order $\ell-1$ vanishes at $t=1$,
because the remaining order of vanishing is $\alpha+1/2>0$.  At $t=0$ it is
\[
 (2k)!(-1)^k
 \binom{\ell+\alpha-1/2}{k},
\]
which is nonzero.  Thus $I_\ell\neq0$.

For $n=2$, write the circle variable as an angle.  The Fourier series of
$\sgn(\cos\theta)$ has a nonzero coefficient at every odd frequency, so the
same conclusion holds for every odd circular harmonic.  When $n=1$ the
projective space is a singleton and no inversion is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Separation metric determines an even measure]
\label{lem:inject}
Let $\mu$ be an even probability measure on $\Sph^{n-1}$ that gives every
great subsphere measure zero.  If
\[
 \int \mathbf1_{\{s_x(u)\neq s_y(u)\}}\,\dd\mu(u)
 =d_{\rm ang}(x,y)
 \qquad(x,y\in\Sph^{n-1}),
\]
then $\mu=\sigma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $n=1$, then $\R P^0$ is a singleton and the conclusion is immediate.
Assume henceforth that $n\geq2$.

Let $\nu=\mu-\sigma$.  It is an even finite signed measure of total mass zero.
Rotational invariance in the plane spanned by $x$ and $y$ gives the classical
random-hyperplane identity
\[
 \int\mathbf1_{\{s_x\neq s_y\}}\,\dd\sigma
 =\frac{\arccos\langle x,y\rangle}{\pi}.
\]
Since $\mathbf1_{\{s_x\neq s_y\}}=(1-s_xs_y)/2$ away from the two equators,
the hypothesis is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annihilate-signs}
  \int_{\Sph^{n-1}}s_x(u)s_y(u)\,\dd\nu(u)=0
  \qquad(x,y\in\Sph^{n-1}).
\end{equation}

Let $Y$ and $Z$ be arbitrary odd spherical harmonics.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:fh}, each is a nonzero scalar multiple of its hemisphere-sign
integral representation.  Fubini's theorem and
\eqref{eq:annihilate-signs} therefore give
\[
 \int_{\Sph^{n-1}}Y(u)Z(u)\,\dd\nu(u)=0.
\]

These products span all even spherical polynomials.  Indeed, if $P$ is the
restriction to the sphere of an even polynomial, then on the sphere
\[
 P(u)=\sum_{i=1}^n u_i\bigl(u_iP(u)\bigr).
\]
Each $u_i$ is an odd degree-one harmonic, while the odd spherical polynomial
$u_iP$ is a finite sum of odd spherical harmonics by the standard harmonic
decomposition of polynomials.  Hence $\nu$ annihilates $P$.

Spherical polynomials are uniformly dense in continuous functions on the
sphere, and symmetrization makes the even ones dense in the continuous even
functions.  Thus $\nu$ annihilates every continuous even function.  Since
$\nu$ itself is even, it also annihilates the odd part of every continuous
function.  The Riesz representation theorem yields $\nu=0$, proving
$\mu=\sigma$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Suppose (1) holds.  A pairwise disagreement pattern induced by one row is
unchanged when that row is rescaled or negated, hence it is a measurable
function only of its projective direction.  A standard Gaussian row also has
projective Haar direction.  The per-row joint cut patterns on any finite $K$
therefore have the same law; taking $m$ iid products and averaging proves (2).

Condition (2), applied to $K=\{x,y\}$ (already with $m=1$), gives equality of
the expected disagreement indicators.  The Gaussian random-hyperplane
identity gives (3).

Finally, if (3) holds, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:inject} to the even lift $\mu$.
It follows that $\mu=\sigma$, which is equivalent to projective Haar
uniformity.  Thus (3) implies (1).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the corollary]
The contrapositive of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives $x,y$ with nonzero bias
$b$.  For this fixed pair the summands defining $h_m(x,y)$ are iid Bernoulli
variables of mean $d_\mu(x,y)$.  The strong law gives
$h_m(x,y)\to d_\mu(x,y)$ almost surely.  Therefore the displayed probability
tends to zero whenever $\delta<b$.
\end{proof}

\section{A sharp obstruction to linear-style universality}

\begin{corollary}[Isotropic subgaussian rows need not be binary-universal]
\label{cor:rademacher}
Let $n\geq2$ and let $A=(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_n)$ have independent
Rademacher coordinates.  The row is isotropic and bounded subgaussian.  Yet
for every $0<t<1$, with
\[
 x=e_1,
 \qquad
 y=\frac{e_1+t e_2}{\sqrt{1+t^2}},
\]
one has, for every row realization,
\[
 \sgn\langle A,x\rangle=\sgn\langle A,y\rangle.
\]
Consequently $h_m(x,y)=0$ deterministically for every $m$, while
\[
 d_{\rm ang}(x,y)=\frac{\arctan t}{\pi}>0.
\]
Thus no distortion smaller than $\arctan(t)/\pi$ is possible for this
two-point set, regardless of the number of rows.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Isotropy is $\E AA^T=I_n$, and the standard Rademacher moment-generating
function bound gives a dimension-free subgaussian constant.  Moreover
\[
 \sgn\langle A,x\rangle=\varepsilon_1,
 \qquad
 \sgn\langle A,y\rangle
 =\sgn(\varepsilon_1+t\varepsilon_2)=\varepsilon_1,
\]
because $0<t<1$.  The angle identity follows from
$\langle x,y\rangle=(1+t^2)^{-1/2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The Rademacher law has atoms on some great subspheres, so it is intentionally
presented as a direct obstruction to broad isotropic/subgaussian universality,
not as an application of the nondegenerate equivalence theorem.  The displayed
pair itself never encounters a zero inner product.
\end{remark}

\section{Verification, novelty boundary, and limitations}

The proof was audited separately in \path{verification.md}.  The reusable
script
\begin{center}
\path{code/check_gegenbauer_coefficients.py}
\end{center}
symbolically checks the odd coefficient integral for dimensions $3$ through
$8$ and odd degrees $1$ through $9$, and exhaustively checks the
two-dimensional Rademacher sign
calculation for three rational values of $t$.  These finite checks passed, but
they are not proof; Lemma~\ref{lem:fh} and Corollary~\ref{cor:rademacher} give
the general arguments.

A bounded novelty search checked the run's registry, solution, attempt, and
proof-gap indexes, as well as arXiv/web searches around binary embedding,
projective direction, random hyperplanes, universality, and Funk--Hecke.
Later work includes fast Gaussian circulant embeddings
\cite{DirksenStollenwerk}, structured quantized embeddings
\cite{HuynhSaab}, and non-Gaussian tessellations using affine random
hyperplanes (random shifts/dithering) \cite{DirksenMendelson}.  No exact version
of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} was located.  This is bounded-search evidence only,
not a guarantee of novelty.

The result is partial relative to the source question in three ways.
\begin{itemize}
\item It assumes iid rows and therefore does not analyze dependent fast maps
such as the structured transform highlighted by Oymak and Recht.
\item It characterizes exact equality in law, not equality of sample
complexity or distortion rates up to constants.
\item It concerns the unshifted sign quantizer.  Dithering can restore useful
non-Gaussian results by changing the geometry of the hyperplanes.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Human-review recommendation.}  Review as a substantial partial result.
The critical checks are the nonzero odd Funk--Hecke coefficient and the span of
even spherical polynomials by products of odd harmonics.
