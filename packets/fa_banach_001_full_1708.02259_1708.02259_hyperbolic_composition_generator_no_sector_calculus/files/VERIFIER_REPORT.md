# Verification Report

Candidate: arXiv:1708.02259, hyperbolic composition generator and
proper-sector H-infinity calculus

## Claims checked

For every positive weight sequence satisfying the source hypotheses, if
`T(t)=C_{alpha_{tanh t}}` is a C0-semigroup on `H^2(beta)`, then:

1. its generator `A=(1-z^2)d/dz` is unbounded; and
2. `A` has no bounded H-infinity calculus on a shifted sector of angle
   strictly below `pi/2`.

Consequently the literal source question has answer no. Under the natural
reading in which the semigroup, rather than its generator, is required to
consist of bounded operators, every admissible weight sequence gives the
proper-sector failure.

## Computational verification

`code/verify_hyperbolic_generator.py` checks:

- the Möbius group law and reflection identity on representative complex
  points and times;
- the exact adjacent-coefficient product through `n=500`; and
- the lower bound for constant, alternating, polynomial, geometric, and
  pseudorandom positive weights spanning many orders of magnitude.

All checks passed. The numerical suite is only a sanity check; the proof is
algebraic apart from the standard sectorial-generation theorem.

## Counterexample and loophole search

- Extreme adjacent weight ratios do not evade the bound: reducing the forward
  coefficient enlarges the next backward coefficient by exactly the reciprocal
  factor.
- The proof does not infer boundedness of the generator from pointwise
  boundedness of the semigroup. It proves the opposite directly on its domain.
- Uniform boundedness in time is never assumed.
- A C0 semigroup of noninvertible composition maps need not extend to a group;
  the packet does not generalize the group obstruction to that setting.
- A half-plane H-infinity calculus is not ruled out and is consistent with the
  source's quasicontractive result.

## External dependency

The only non-elementary dependency is the standard sectorial generation
principle: a bounded H-infinity calculus on a sector of angle below `pi/2`
implies an analytic semigroup. The remaining proof is self-contained, using
the source's generator-domain characterization.
