\begin{abstract}
Heilman left open the Gaussian-perimeter instability of the noncircular
convex $\lambda$-shrinker curves with rotational orders $m=2$ and $m=4$.
We settle the $m=4$ case.  In fact, every Chang curve of even rotational
order $m\geq4$, and every Euclidean cylinder over it, is unstable among
centrally symmetric competitors of fixed Gaussian volume.  The proof is a
nodal-count correction: the normal component of infinitesimal rotation has
two nodal domains per curvature period, hence $2m$ on the closed curve.  For
$m=4$ there are eight, so Heilman's existing ``more than four nodal domains''
criterion applies.  The same argument reaches exactly four domains for
$m=2$ and therefore does not settle that remaining case.
\end{abstract}

\section{The source question}

The source is S.~Heilman, \emph{Convex Cylinders and the Symmetric Gaussian
Isoperimetric Problem}, arXiv:2204.12003 \cite{Heilman22}.  Its page 4 recalls
the embedded convex curves $\Gamma_m$ constructed by Chang \cite{Chang},
which after normalization satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shrinker}
  H=\langle x,N\rangle+\lambda,\qquad \lambda<0,
\end{equation}
and have minimal rotational symmetry $2\pi/m$.  The source invokes a nodal
criterion for even $m\geq6$, then says that instability is unclear for
$m=2$ and $m=4$.

Write
\[
 \gauss_j(x)=(2\pi)^{-j/2}e^{-|x|^2/2}.
\]
Instability below means that there is a centrally symmetric,
Gaussian-volume-preserving normal variation with negative second variation
of Gaussian perimeter.

\section{Proof intuition}

There are two ingredients, both already visible in the cited papers.  First,
the normal speed generated by rotating a critical surface is a zero mode of
the Gaussian Jacobi operator.  Heilman's nodal argument says that a centrally
symmetric minimizer cannot have such a rotation mode with more than four
nodal domains.  Second, Chang's phase variable
$\tau=\langle x,T\rangle$ is, up to sign, exactly that rotation mode, and
$k'=k\tau$.  A nonconstant curvature oscillation has one increasing and one
decreasing half, hence two nodal domains of $\tau$.  Chang's $m$-fold closing
condition repeats the full oscillation $m$ times.  The total is therefore
$2m$, giving eight domains when $m=4$.  The source's existing nodal criterion
then finishes the proof without a new spectral estimate.

\section{A self-contained nodal criterion}

We first recall the mechanism behind Corollary 11.9 of \cite{Heilman17}.
Let $\Sigma=\partial\Omega\subset\R^q$ be a smooth critical point of Gaussian
perimeter under a Gaussian-volume constraint, and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L}
 L=\Delta_\Sigma-\langle x,\nabla_\Sigma\rangle+|A|^2+1
\end{equation}
be its Gaussian Jacobi operator.  Put
\[
 \mathcal B(u,v)=\int_\Sigma uLv\,\gauss_{q-1}.
\]
With this convention, a symmetric volume-preserving normal speed $u$ has
second variation $-\mathcal B(u,u)$.  Thus stability requires
$\mathcal B(u,u)\leq0$ whenever $u(-x)=u(x)$ and
$\int_\Sigma u\gauss_{q-1}=0$.

\begin{lemma}[rotation nodal criterion]\label{lem:nodal}
Assume $\Omega=-\Omega$.  Let $Q$ be a nonzero antisymmetric matrix and set
\[
 f(x)=\langle Qx,N(x)\rangle.
\]
If $f$ has more than four nodal domains, then $\Omega$ is unstable for the
symmetric Gaussian isoperimetric problem.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Rotating \eqref{eq:shrinker} and differentiating at the identity gives
$Lf=0$.  Moreover $f(-x)=f(x)$, and the weighted divergence theorem gives
\[
 \int_\Sigma f\gauss_{q-1}
 =\int_\Omega \operatorname{div}(\gauss_q Qx)
 =\int_\Omega\bigl(\operatorname{tr}Q-\langle x,Qx\rangle\bigr)\gauss_q=0.
\]

Suppose for contradiction that $\Omega$ is stable.  Antipodal symmetry pairs
the nodal domains of $f$.  More than four domains allow us to choose two
different antipodal pairs $D_1\cup(-D_1)$ and $D_2\cup(-D_2)$ while leaving
an unused nodal domain.  Define $g$ to equal $f$ on the first pair,
$\alpha f$ on the second pair, and zero elsewhere.  Because $f$ has one
strict sign on each nodal domain, one can choose $\alpha\in\R$ so that
$\int g\gauss_{q-1}=0$.  Nodal-domain integration by parts and $Lf=0$ give
$\mathcal B(g,g)=0$.

For every smooth symmetric mean-zero $h$, stability applied to $g+th$ for
all $t\in\R$ now forces $\mathcal B(g,h)=0$.  Equivalently, $Lg$ is a
constant distribution in the symmetric sector.  On the unused open nodal
domain, $g$ vanishes identically, so that constant is zero.  Elliptic
regularity and unique continuation then imply $g=0$ everywhere, contradicting
its definition.  The nodal truncations used here belong to the Gaussian
$W^{1,2}$ space and are obtained as limits of smooth speeds; this is the
standard justification given in \cite[Corollary 11.9]{Heilman17}.
\end{proof}

\section{The missing nodal count}

Let $\Gamma_m$ be one of Chang's noncircular embedded convex solutions,
parametrized by arclength $s$.  Choose the orientation so that its curvature
$k=H$ is positive, let $T$ be the unit tangent, and put
\[
 \tau=\langle x,T\rangle.
\]
Differentiating \eqref{eq:shrinker} and the Frenet equations gives the
phase-plane system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase}
 k'=k\tau,
 \qquad
 \tau'=1+\lambda k-k^2.
\end{equation}
These are Chang's equations after the normalization used in
arXiv:2204.12003.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:count}
The normal component of infinitesimal rotation on $\Gamma_m$ has exactly
$2m$ nodal domains.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $Q$ be rotation through $\pi/2$.  Depending only on the orientation,
\[
 \langle Qx,N\rangle=\pm\langle x,T\rangle=\pm\tau.
\]
It therefore suffices to count the sign intervals of $\tau$.

Chang writes $B=2\log k$ and reduces \eqref{eq:phase} to the energy equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \frac12(B')^2+2V(B)=2\eta,
 \qquad
 V(B)=e^B-2\lambda e^{B/2}-B.
\end{equation}
For $\lambda<0$, $V$ is strictly convex.  Every nonconstant periodic orbit
therefore has exactly two turning values $B_-<B_+$, and $B'$ has one strict
sign between successive turning points.  Since $B'=2k'/k=2\tau$, the
function $\tau$ has exactly two nodal domains during one full phase period.
The two zeros are simple: if $\tau=\tau'=0$, then \eqref{eq:phase} is at its
equilibrium, and uniqueness would make the curve circular.

In Chang's closing construction, one full phase period changes the tangent
angle by $2\pi/m$; repeating it $m$ times closes $\Gamma_m$ and gives its
minimal $m$-fold rotational symmetry.  Consequently the two sign intervals
per phase period produce exactly $2m$ nodal domains on the closed curve.
\end{proof}

For comparison, if $\theta$ denotes normal angle, then $d\theta/ds=k$ and
\eqref{eq:phase} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalangle}
 k_{\theta\theta}+k=\lambda+\frac1k.
\end{equation}
On functions of the curve, \eqref{eq:L} becomes
\[
 Lf=f+k^2(f_{\theta\theta}+f).
\]
Differentiating \eqref{eq:normalangle} verifies directly that
$L(k_\theta)=0$, while $k_\theta=k'/k=\tau$.  This independently checks both
the Jacobi field and its two zeros per curvature period.

\section{Result}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $\Gamma_m\subset\R^2$ be a noncircular embedded convex Chang curve with
rotational order $m$, normalized by \eqref{eq:shrinker}.  If $m$ is even and
$m\geq4$, the centrally symmetric region bounded by $\Gamma_m$ is unstable
for Gaussian perimeter under centrally symmetric, Gaussian-volume-preserving
variations.  More generally, for every $d\geq0$, the cylinder
\[
 \Gamma_m\times\R^d\subset\R^{d+2}
\]
is unstable in the same sense.  In particular, this settles the $m=4$ case
identified as open in the source paper.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Even $m$ makes the bounded region centrally symmetric.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:count}, the infinitesimal-rotation Jacobi field has $2m$ nodal
domains.  For $m\geq4$, this number is at least eight and hence is strictly
larger than four.  Lemma~\ref{lem:nodal} proves instability.

On $\Gamma_m\times\R^d$, rotate only the first two coordinates.  The same
Jacobi field is independent of the $\R^d$ coordinate, and each nodal domain
is a nodal arc times the connected space $\R^d$.  Thus its number of nodal
domains is still $2m$.  Gaussian integrals factor, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:nodal} applies without change.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
For $m=2$, Lemma~\ref{lem:count} gives exactly four nodal domains.  The strict
``more than four'' hypothesis in Lemma~\ref{lem:nodal} is then unavailable.
The present proof therefore resolves the source's $m=4$ case but does not
claim a solution of the $m=2$ case.
\end{remark}

\section{Novelty check}

The run's cheap indexes contained no entry for arXiv:2204.12003 or the
low-symmetry $\lambda$-shrinker question.  Bounded arXiv searches on 9 August
2026 used the source title and identifier together with ``$m=4$'',
``$\Gamma_4$'', ``four nodal domains'', ``infinitesimal rotation'', and
``Gaussian perimeter instability''.  They found the three sources below but
no later paper recording the $2m$ count or settling the $m=4$ case.  This is
evidence of possible novelty, not an exhaustive bibliographic guarantee.
