\section{Question and answer}

\textbf{Source question (transcribed).} ``Finding the exact value of
$\alpha_1^\#(X)$ is, in general, an open question; in particular it is open
for Sierpinski carpets.''

Let $K\subset[0,1]^2$ be the classical carpet, let $\mu$ be its normalized
$d_H$-Hausdorff measure, and let $d_W$ be the walk dimension of its canonical
diffusion.  Thus
\[
 d_H=\frac{\log 8}{\log3}.
\]
For $p\geq1$ and $\lambda>0$, use the normalized metric Besov seminorm
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KS}
 [f]_{\KS^{\lambda,p}}^p
 =\sup_{0<r\leq1}r^{-p\lambda}
 \int_K\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}
 |f(x)-f(y)|^p\,d\mu(y)\,d\mu(x).
\end{equation}
Write $\lambda_p^\#$ for the supremum of the $\lambda$ for which this space
contains a nonconstant function, and $\lambda_p^*$ for the corresponding
density threshold.  The heat-semigroup exponent in the source is
$\alpha=\lambda/d_W$; the equivalence is proved in~\cite{ABCRST3}.

\subsection*{Proof Intuition}

For $p>1$, Murugan and Shimizu identify the critical metric smoothness with
a renormalized discrete $p$-capacity on the cell graphs of the carpet.  A
bounded $L^1$ Besov function is automatically a $p$-Besov function at
smoothness divided by $p$, so the desired $L^1$ upper bound follows if the
$p$-capacity exponent has the correct limit as $p\downarrow1$.

At $p=1$, capacity is exactly a minimum cut by the coarea formula.  On a
level-$n$ carpet graph there are $2^n$ disjoint straight corridors indexed by
base-three words using only $0$ and $2$.  Conversely, a nested middle-third
wall crosses precisely those same $2^n$ transverse addresses.  Hence the
annular $1$-capacity is comparable to $2^n$.  H\"older's inequality compares
$p$-capacity with $1$-capacity on the $O(8^n)$ edges in the annulus and
squeezes its exponential scaling constant to $1/2$ as $p\downarrow1$.
This makes the limiting critical metric exponent $\log4/\log3$.  The known
indicator construction supplies the matching density lower bound.

\begin{theorem}[Exact classical-carpet endpoint]\label{thm:main}
For the classical planar Sierpi\'nski carpet,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 \lambda_1^*=\lambda_1^\#=\frac{\log4}{\log3}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for the heat-semigroup Besov spaces of~\cite{ABCRST1},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \alpha_1^*=\alpha_1^\#=\frac{\log4}{d_W\log3}.}
\end{equation}
The same equalities hold for the standard unbounded blow-up
$K^\infty=\bigcup_{j\geq0}3^jK$.
\end{theorem}

\section{The endpoint graph calculation}

Let $\mathbb G_n$ be the level-$n$ cell graph used in~\cite{MS}: its
vertices are the $8^n$ retained squares and two vertices are adjacent when
the corresponding carpet cells meet.  Murugan--Shimizu define an annular
capacity $\mathcal C_p(n)$ by taking the supremum, over a coarse cell $w$,
of the discrete $p$-capacity between all level-$n$ descendants of $w$ and
the descendants outside the coarse graph two-neighborhood of $w$; see
\cite[Eq.~(8.3)]{MS}.  Their quasi-multiplicativity theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
 \rho(p)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal C_p(n)^{-1/n},\qquad
 d_w(p)=\frac{\log(8\rho(p))}{\log3}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[The endpoint capacity]\label{lem:cap1}
There are constants $0<c<C<\infty$, independent of $n$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C1}
 c,2^n\leq\mathcal C_1(n)\leq C,2^n.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\rho(1)=1/2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First use the face-adjacency graph.  For $p=1$, truncation to $[0,1]$ and
the discrete coarea identity
\[
 \sum_{uv}|f(u)-f(v)|
 =\int_0^1|\partial\{f>t\}|\,dt
\]
identify capacity with the minimum cardinality of an edge cut.

For the lower bound, choose a coarse cell in a straight row with at least
three further coarse cells on one side.  At refinement depth $n$, every
word $\eta\in\{0,2\}^n$ specifies a complete horizontal row through these
copies: no square on that row is deleted, because a deletion requires the
same ternary digit to be $1$ in both coordinates.  The resulting $2^n$
paths are edge-disjoint, start in the descendants of the chosen cell, and
end outside its two-neighborhood.  Every separating cut therefore has at
least $2^n$ edges.  Since $\mathcal C_1(n)$ is a supremum, this proves its
lower bound.

For the upper bound, put
\[
 a_n=\frac{3^n-1}{2}.
\]
The vertical middle-third wall consists of the face edges between columns
$a_n-1$ and $a_n$.  A transverse row crosses this wall if and only if every
ternary digit of its row address lies in $\{0,2\}$, so the wall contains
exactly $2^n$ edges.  The same holds after rotation.  At the coarse scale,
the two-neighborhood of any cell has universally bounded size.  A planar
dual curve (or, at the outer boundary, a boundary-to-boundary dual arc)
separating the cell from the complement of that two-neighborhood uses only
a universal number of horizontal and vertical unit-square crossings.
Replace each crossing by the corresponding middle-third wall inside that
coarse cell.  At turns use the union of the two rotated walls.  This yields
a separating cut of at most $C_0 2^n$ edges, uniformly in the coarse level
and cell.  This is the usual nested-wall proof of the standard carpet's
$L^1$ isoperimetric scaling; compare~\cite{GP,GLC}.

Finally, the graph in~\cite{MS} also joins cells meeting only at a corner.
Every such diagonal edge has a two-edge route in the face graph, and each
face edge participates in only boundedly many such routes.  Enlarging a
face cut by the diagonal edges routed across it therefore changes its size
by at most a universal factor; conversely the face graph is a subgraph.
Thus \eqref{eq:C1} holds for the Murugan--Shimizu graph as stated.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$p\downarrow1$ continuity of the capacity exponent]
\label{lem:continuity}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rholimit}
 \lim_{p\downarrow1}\rho(p)=\frac12,\qquad
 \lim_{p\downarrow1}d_w(p)=\frac{\log4}{\log3}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
An admissible capacity function may be truncated to $[0,1]$ and made
constant off the relevant coarse two-neighborhood.  Only $M_n\leq C_1 8^n$
fine edges can then carry a nonzero gradient.  On these edges,
\[
 \mathcal E_p(f)\leq\mathcal E_1(f),\qquad
 \mathcal E_1(f)\leq M_n^{1-1/p}\mathcal E_p(f)^{1/p}.
\]
Taking infima in each annular problem and then the supremum defining
$\mathcal C_p(n)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capcompare}
 \mathcal C_p(n)\leq\mathcal C_1(n),\qquad
 \mathcal C_p(n)\geq
 \frac{\mathcal C_1(n)^p}{(C_1 8^n)^{p-1}}.
\end{equation}
Constants disappear after taking inverse $n$th roots.  Lemma~\ref{lem:cap1}
therefore yields
\[
 \frac12\leq\rho(p)\leq\frac{8^{p-1}}{2^p}.
\]
Both endpoints tend to $1/2$ as $p\downarrow1$.  Substitution into
\eqref{eq:rho} proves the second limit.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

Murugan and Shimizu prove for every $p>1$ that the critical Besov
smoothness on $K$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MScrit}
 s_p=\frac{d_w(p)}p;
\end{equation}
indeed their self-similar Sobolev space is exactly
$B^{d_w(p)/p}_{p,\infty}$~\cite[Theorem~1.4]{MS}.

Suppose that $f\in\KS^{\lambda,1}(K)$ is nonconstant.  Some two-sided
truncation $g=(-M)\vee(f\wedge M)$ remains nonconstant, is bounded, and does
not increase the $L^1$ seminorm.  For every $p>1$,
\[
 |g(x)-g(y)|^p\leq(2M)^{p-1}|g(x)-g(y)|.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:KS} now shows that
$g\in B^{\lambda/p}_{p,\infty}(K)$.  Since $g$ is nonconstant,
\eqref{eq:MScrit} implies
\[
 \frac\lambda p\leq\frac{d_w(p)}p,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad \lambda\leq d_w(p)\quad(p>1).
\]
Letting $p\downarrow1$ and applying Lemma~\ref{lem:continuity} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper}
 \lambda_1^\#(K)\leq\frac{\log4}{\log3}.
\end{equation}

The matching lower bound is known.  Alonso Ruiz et al. state for the
unbounded generalized-carpet setting that
\[
 \lambda_1^*\geq d_H-d_{tH}+1
\]
by constructing a topology-generating family of indicator functions with
controlled boundary neighborhoods~\cite[Section~5.4]{ABCRST3}.  The same
local cell-basis construction applies verbatim to compact $K$.  For the
classical carpet,
\[
 d_{tH}=1+\frac{\log2}{\log3},
 \qquad d_H-d_{tH}+1=\frac{\log4}{\log3}.
\]
Together with $\lambda_1^*\leq\lambda_1^\#$ and \eqref{eq:upper}, this proves
\eqref{eq:main}.  The heat-kernel/metric characterization in~\cite{ABCRST3}
then gives \eqref{eq:alpha}.

For completeness, the compact upper bound passes to $K^\infty$ locally.
Restriction of a global $\KS^{\lambda,1}$ function to any unit carpet cell
has bounded compact seminorm, since the cell and ambient ball measures are
uniformly comparable at radii at most one.  If $\lambda>\log4/\log3$, the
compact result makes every such restriction constant.  Constants on two
side-adjacent cells must agree: at $r=3^{-n}$, the $3^n$ opposing pairs of
boundary cells contribute at least
\[
 c|c_i-c_j|\,3^n8^{-n}=c|c_i-c_j|r^{d_H-1}
\]
to the normalized $L^1$ oscillation.  Multiplication by $r^{-\lambda}$
diverges because $\lambda>\log4/\log3>d_H-1$.  The unit-cell adjacency graph
is connected, so the global function is constant.  The indicator lower-bound
construction of~\cite{ABCRST3} was stated for the unbounded carpet, completing
the blow-up case. \qed

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The only new geometric input is Lemma~\ref{lem:cap1}; its two inequalities
are independently checkable as max-flow/min-cut statements.  The included
script constructs both the face and corner-adjacency graphs and verifies the
predicted doubling on small refinements.  This computation is a sanity check,
not part of the proof.  Lemma~\ref{lem:continuity} is then a direct two-line
H\"older comparison.  The truncation step is directionally important: it
turns a hypothetical $L^1$ function above the endpoint into a nonconstant
$p>1$ function, where the exact critical theorem is available.

\textbf{Scope.} This completely answers the classical standard carpet, in
both compact and standard unbounded forms.  It is classified as a partial
answer only because the source asks about Sierpi\'nski carpets broadly.  For a
generalized carpet one would need a uniform identification of the endpoint
annular min-cut exponent with the boundary exponent occurring in
$d_H-d_{tH}+1$, together with the full $p>1$ critical theorem in that
generality.  Those identifications are not asserted here.  The packet also
does not prove the sharper weak Bakry--\'Emery conjecture; it determines the
Besov critical exponent without it.

\textbf{Bounded novelty check.} The run indexes contained no answer for
arXiv:1811.04267.  Searches through 11 August 2026 for the exact symbols
$\alpha_1^\#$ and $\lambda_1^\#$, the formula $\log4/\log3$, carpet BV/Besov
criticality, $p$-walk dimension as $p\downarrow1$, and carpet min-cut scaling
found the 2022 lower-bound/conjecture paper~\cite{ABCRST3}, the 2025 $p>1$
critical theorem~\cite{MS}, and a January 2026 source that still calls the
relevant carpet H\"older endpoint conjecturally non-sharp~\cite{BC}.  No source
found states \eqref{eq:main} or the endpoint-continuity argument.  This is a
bounded search, not an exhaustive novelty claim.

\textbf{Human-review recommendation.} Review as a likely-valid full theorem
for the classical carpet and a substantial partial answer to the broad source
question.  The highest-value check is the uniform nested-wall upper bound in
Lemma~\ref{lem:cap1}; after that lemma, the analytic argument is formal.
