For every dimension and every missing parameter $0<a\leq 1/2$, an explicit
double-sum condition makes the random Gramian a Hilbert--Schmidt perturbation
of the identity.  In particular, the conjectured conclusion holds whenever

\section{Problem and result}

Fix $d\geq1$ and deterministic radii
$r_n=(r_n^1,\ldots,r_n^d)\in[0,1)^d$.  Let
\[
 \lambda_n=(r_n^1e^{i\theta_n^1},\ldots,r_n^de^{i\theta_n^d}),
\]
where the $\theta_n$ are independent and uniform on $\T^d$.  For
$m=(m_1,\ldots,m_d)\in\N^d$, put
\[
 A_m=\{z\in\D^d:2^{-(m_i+1)}\leq1-\abs{z_i}<2^{-m_i}
       \text{ for every }i\},
 \qquad N_m=\#\{n:r_n\in A_m\},
\]
and write $\abs m=\sum_i m_i$ and
$\abs{m\wedge k}=\sum_i\min(m_i,k_i)$.

For $0<a<1$, set $\beta=1-a$ and let $D_d^a$ be the reproducing-kernel
Hilbert space with
\[
 k_w^{(a)}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^d(1-\overline{w_i}z_i)^{-\beta},
 \qquad s_w^{(a)}=k_w^{(a)}/\|k_w^{(a)}\|.
\]
The associated discrete measure is
\[
 \mu_\Lambda=\sum_n\|k_{\lambda_n}^{(a)}\|^{-2}\delta_{\lambda_n}.
\]
It is Carleson exactly when $(s_{\lambda_n}^{(a)})_n$ is a Bessel sequence,
or equivalently when its Gramian is bounded on $\ell^2$.

Chalmoukis, Dayan, and Lamberti prove that
\[
 \sum_m N_m2^{-\beta\abs m}<\infty                                      \tag{1}
\]
is necessary and sufficient almost surely when $1/2<a\leq1$, and conjecture
the same equivalence for $0<a\leq1/2$ \cite{CDL}.

The following theorem extracts the strongest direct Hilbert--Schmidt
consequence of the source's second-moment computation.

\begin{theorem}[second-moment criterion]\label{thm:criterion}
Let $\Lambda$ be as above.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $0<a<1/2$ and
\[
 \sum_{m,k\in\N^d}N_mN_k
  2^{-\beta(\abs m+\abs k)}
  2^{(1-2a)\abs{m\wedge k}}<\infty,                                    \tag{2}
\]
then $\mu_\Lambda$ is almost surely a Carleson measure for $D_d^a$.
\item If $a=1/2$ and
\[
 \sum_{m,k\in\N^d}N_mN_k2^{-(\abs m+\abs k)/2}
       \prod_{i=1}^d\bigl(1+\min(m_i,k_i)\bigr)<\infty,                 \tag{3}
\]
then the same conclusion holds.
\end{enumerate}
In both cases the random Gramian is almost surely a Hilbert--Schmidt
perturbation of the identity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[square-root-sparse regime]\label{cor:sparse}
For every $d\geq1$ and $0<a\leq1/2$, suppose that for some $C<\infty$ and
$\varepsilon>0$,
\[
                  N_m\leq C2^{(1/2-\varepsilon)\abs m}
                  \qquad(m\in\N^d).                                    \tag{4}
\]
Then (1) holds and $\mu_\Lambda$ is almost surely Carleson for $D_d^a$.
\end{corollary}

Thus the conjecture holds throughout a parameter-independent exponential
subregion of its missing range.  More generally, (2) and (3) retain
anisotropic information through $m\wedge k$ and can apply beyond the
pointwise hypothesis (4).

\section{Proof}

We include the short second-moment argument to make the result independently
checkable.  In one variable write
\[
       (1-\overline wz)^{-\beta}=\sum_{\ell\geq0}c_\ell
       (\overline wz)^\ell,
       \qquad c_\ell\asymp(1+\ell)^{-a}.
\]
If $n\ne j$, independence and uniformity of the angles annihilate all
unequal Fourier frequencies.  After normalization this gives
\[
 \E\abs{\ip{s_{\lambda_n}^{(a)}}{s_{\lambda_j}^{(a)}}}^2
 \asymp
 \prod_{i=1}^d\left[(1-r_n^i)^\beta(1-r_j^i)^\beta
 \sum_{\ell\geq0}c_\ell^2(r_n^ir_j^i)^{2\ell}\right].                 \tag{5}
\]
The expectation factors because the coordinates are independent.  The
standard power-series estimates are
\[
 \sum_{\ell\geq0}(1+\ell)^{-2a}t^{2\ell}
 \asymp
 \begin{cases}
 (1-t)^{-(1-2a)},&0<a<1/2,\\
 1+\log\dfrac1{1-t},&a=1/2.
 \end{cases}                                                            \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) are precisely Lemma 4.4 of the source.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/source_lemma_4_4.png}
  \caption{The source's second-moment estimate, PDF page 16.}
\end{figure}

If $r_n\in A_m$ and $r_j\in A_k$, then coordinatewise
\[
 1-r_n^i\asymp2^{-m_i},\qquad
 1-r_j^i\asymp2^{-k_i},\qquad
 1-r_n^ir_j^i\asymp2^{-\min(m_i,k_i)}.                                 \tag{7}
\]
Consequently (5)--(7) imply, for $0<a<1/2$,
\[
 \E\abs{\ip{s_{\lambda_n}^{(a)}}{s_{\lambda_j}^{(a)}}}^2
 \lesssim
 2^{-\beta(\abs m+\abs k)}2^{(1-2a)\abs{m\wedge k}},                  \tag{8}
\]
and at the endpoint,
\[
 \E\abs{\ip{s_{\lambda_n}^{(1/2)}}{s_{\lambda_j}^{(1/2)}}}^2
 \lesssim2^{-(\abs m+\abs k)/2}
 \prod_{i=1}^d\bigl(1+\min(m_i,k_i)\bigr).                            \tag{9}
\]

Let $G=(\ip{s_{\lambda_n}^{(a)}}{s_{\lambda_j}^{(a)}})_{n,j}$ and
$H=G-I$.  Tonelli's theorem, grouping ordered pairs by dyadic cells, gives
from (8) or (9)
\[
 \E\|H\|_{\HS}^2
 =\sum_{n\ne j}\E\abs{\ip{s_{\lambda_n}^{(a)}}
                              {s_{\lambda_j}^{(a)}}}^2<\infty.          \tag{10}
\]
Hence the nonnegative random variable $\|H\|_{\HS}^2$ is finite almost
surely.  Therefore $G=I+H$ is bounded on $\ell^2$ almost surely.  For every
$f\in D_d^a$,
\[
 \int\abs f^2\,d\mu_\Lambda
 =\sum_n\abs{\ip f{s_{\lambda_n}^{(a)}}}^2
 \leq\|G\|\,\|f\|^2,
\]
first on finite subsystems and then by monotone convergence.  This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}.

For Corollary~\ref{cor:sparse}, first suppose $0<a<1/2$ and put
$\alpha=1-2a$.  Since
\[
 \abs{m\wedge k}\leq\frac{\abs m+\abs k}{2},\qquad
 \beta-\frac\alpha2=\frac12,
\]
each summand in (2), under (4), is at most
$C^2 2^{-\varepsilon(\abs m+\abs k)}$.  Its sum over
$\N^d\times\N^d$ converges.  At $a=1/2$, a summand in (3) is at most
\[
 C^2 2^{-\varepsilon(\abs m+\abs k)}
      (1+\abs m+\abs k)^d,
\]
which is again summable.  Finally,
\[
 N_m2^{-(1-a)\abs m}
 \leq C2^{-(1/2-a+\varepsilon)\abs m},
\]
so (1) also holds for all $0<a\leq1/2$.

\section{Why this does not settle the full conjecture}

Finite mass alone permits $N_m$ almost as large as
$2^{(1-a)\abs m}$ on a sparse set of cells, far above the square-root scale.
For $a\leq1/2$, the factor $2^{(1-2a)\abs{m\wedge k}}$ (or its endpoint
logarithm) can then make (10) diverge.  Divergence of the Hilbert--Schmidt
expectation is only failure of this proof, not evidence that the Gramian is
unbounded.

Eight focused upgrade routes were checked.  Schur-product reduction cannot
assign arbitrary product mass to finitely many coordinatewise summable
classes.  Finite mass does imply an almost-sure finite separated coloring,
but no available product-Dirichlet theorem turns that fact plus finite mass
into the desired embedding.  Iterating the one-variable random theorem
would require an operator-valued version with non-scalar weights.  The known
bi-tree and tri-tree criteria reduce the question to uniform quadratic
energy estimates over all rectangles, which finite total mass does not by
itself supply; the cited theory also has a dimension-four obstruction
\cite{MPVZ}.  Direct same-shell simulations revealed no growing Gram norm
and therefore no counterexample.  Finally, a beta-mixture representation of
the kernel does not yield Hardy estimates uniform enough to integrate.

These obstructions leave the finite-mass conjecture credible but unresolved.
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} is a rigorous intermediate result because it
isolates the exact overlap energy that the direct second-moment method can
control.
