\begin{abstract}
Baudier asks whether an $\mathcal A$-collection on a connected metric tree can
induce an embedding with Hilbert compression $1/2$.  We prove a general upper
obstruction for the gluing map used immediately before that question.  If
$\varphi_n$ are unit-ball-valued, their variation at scale $n$ is at most
$b_n$, and $\sum b_n^p<\infty$, then the unweighted $\ell_p$-sum map
$\Phi=(\varphi_n-\varphi_n(o))_n$ satisfies
\[
 \|\Phi(\gamma(t))-\Phi(\gamma(0))\|
 =o\bigl(t^{1/(p+1)}\bigr)
\]
on every geodesic ray.  For an adapted fast $\mathcal A$-collection,
Lemma 3.3 gives $b_n^p\le2\epsilon_n$ and
$\sum\epsilon_n<\infty$.  Thus the induced Hilbert map has compression at
most $1/3$, giving a negative answer for the paper's construction.
\end{abstract}

The exact question is whether it is possible ``to construct an
A-collection inducing an embedding with compression $1/2$.''  In context,
``inducing'' refers to the normalized-characteristic, unweighted
$\ell_p$-sum construction of Lemma 3.3 and Proposition 3.4: this is the map
the remark calls ``our embedding.''  We answer that constructional question
negatively.  We do not assert an upper bound on arbitrary embeddings of the
tree, whose Hilbert compression is indeed $1$.

\section{Proof intuition}

Fastness forces the local variations of the coordinate maps to be
$p$-summable.  Consider two points distance $t$ apart on a geodesic ray and
split the glued coordinates at $m$.  In the first $m$ coordinates, the
difference of two unit vectors costs at most $2$ per coordinate.  In every
later coordinate $n>m$, subdivide the ray segment into at most
$\lceil t/n\rceil$ pieces of length at most $n$; summing the local variation
along that chain controls the coordinate difference.  The head therefore
costs $O(m)$ in the $p$-th power, while the tail costs at most
$O((t/m)^p)$ times a summable tail.  Balancing at
$m\asymp t^{p/(p+1)}$ gives exponent $1/(p+1)$.  Since the summable tail
actually tends to zero, a two-limit argument improves the estimate to
little-$o$.

\section{The gluing obstruction}

For Banach spaces $(X_n)_{n\ge1}$, write
$\bigl(\bigoplus_n X_n\bigr)_p$ for their $\ell_p$-sum.  The compression
exponent of a particular map is the supremum of exponents $\alpha$ for which
its norm difference is bounded below by a positive multiple of
$d(x,y)^\alpha$ at all sufficiently large distances.

\begin{theorem}[Ray-chain obstruction]\label{thm:obstruction}
Let $1\le p<\infty$, and let a metric space $(M,d)$ contain an isometric
geodesic ray $\gamma:[0,\infty)\to M$.  Suppose that
$\varphi_n:M\to X_n$ take values in the closed unit balls and that numbers
$b_n\ge0$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local}
 \|\varphi_n(x)-\varphi_n(y)\|\le b_n
 \quad\text{whenever }d(x,y)\le n,
 \qquad \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_n^p<\infty.
\end{equation}
For a basepoint $o\in M$, define
\[
 \Phi(x)=\bigl(\varphi_n(x)-\varphi_n(o)\bigr)_{n\ge1}.
\]
Then $\Phi$ is well defined and, uniformly in $s\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:little-o}
 \|\Phi(\gamma(s+t))-\Phi(\gamma(s))\|
 =o\bigl(t^{1/(p+1)}\bigr)\qquad(t\to\infty).
\end{equation}
In particular, the compression exponent of $\Phi$ is at most $1/(p+1)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $x\in M$, condition \eqref{eq:local} applies to the pair $(x,o)$
for every integer $n\ge d(x,o)$.  The tail of
$(\varphi_n(x)-\varphi_n(o))_n$ is therefore dominated in $p$-th power by
$\sum b_n^p$, and its head is finite.  Thus $\Phi(x)$ belongs to the
$\ell_p$-sum.

Fix $s\ge0$, $t\ge1$, and put $x=\gamma(s)$,
$y=\gamma(s+t)$.  Let $m\ge1$ be an integer and set
$E_m=\sum_{n>m}b_n^p$.  Since the coordinate maps take values in unit balls,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:head}
 \sum_{n\le m}\|\varphi_n(x)-\varphi_n(y)\|^p\le2^pm.
\end{equation}
For $n>m$, partition the ray segment from $x$ to $y$ into
$L=\lceil t/n\rceil$ consecutive subsegments, each of length at most $n$.
Applying \eqref{eq:local} to every adjacent pair and using the triangle
inequality gives
\[
 \|\varphi_n(x)-\varphi_n(y)\|
 \le \left\lceil\frac tn\right\rceil b_n
 \le \left(1+\frac tm\right)b_n.
\]
The basepoint terms cancel in $\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)$, so \eqref{eq:head} and the
last estimate imply
\begin{align}
 \|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\|^p
 &\le 2^pm+\left(1+\frac tm\right)^pE_m \label{eq:master}\\
 &\le 2^pm+2^{p-1}\left(1+\left(\frac tm\right)^p\right)E_m.\nonumber
\end{align}
Taking $m=\lceil t^{p/(p+1)}\rceil$ already gives the big-$O$ version of
\eqref{eq:little-o}.

For the sharper statement, fix $0<\delta<1$ and take
$m=\lceil\delta t^{p/(p+1)}\rceil$.  Divide \eqref{eq:master} by
$t^{p/(p+1)}$.  As $t\to\infty$, one has $m\to\infty$ and hence
$E_m\to0$.  The tail terms tend to zero, while the limsup of the head term is
at most $2^p\delta$.  Thus
\[
 \limsup_{t\to\infty}
 \frac{\|\Phi(\gamma(s+t))-\Phi(\gamma(s))\|^p}
      {t^{p/(p+1)}}
 \le2^p\delta.
\]
The estimate is independent of $s$.  Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ proves
\eqref{eq:little-o}.  Since the ray contains pairs at every arbitrarily large
distance, no coarse lower power with exponent greater than $1/(p+1)$ can
hold for $\Phi$.
\end{proof}

\section{Negative answer for the source construction}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:answer}
Let $\mathcal T$ be a connected metric tree with an infinite geodesic.  No
adapted fast $\mathcal A$-collection can, through the normalized-characteristic
gluing map of Lemma 3.3 and Proposition 3.4 of the source, induce a
Hilbert-space embedding with compression $1/2$.  Every such induced map has
compression exponent at most $1/3$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
An infinite geodesic contains a geodesic ray.  For an adapted fast
$\mathcal A$-collection, the source defines
\[
 \varphi_n(x)=\frac{\chi_{A_n(x)}}{|A_n(x)|^{1/p}},
 \qquad \|\varphi_n(x)\|_p=1,
\]
and proves
\[
 \|\varphi_n(x)-\varphi_n(y)\|_p^p\le2\epsilon_n
 \quad\text{whenever }d(x,y)\le r_n=n.
\]
Set $b_n=(2\epsilon_n)^{1/p}$.  Fastness says
$\sum_n\epsilon_n<\infty$, so
$\sum_n b_n^p=2\sum_n\epsilon_n<\infty$.  Theorem
\ref{thm:obstruction} applies.  At $p=2$ it gives compression exponent at
most $1/(2+1)=1/3$, which rules out $1/2$.
\end{proof}

The obstruction is independent of radial dilation and of the particular
ray-segment collection in Example 3.5.  It therefore shows that optimizing
the support radii alone cannot reach the value suggested in Remark 3.6: the
summable local-variation requirement and the unweighted gluing operation
already impose a stricter ceiling.

\section{Verification, limitations, and novelty}

The proof uses only three ingredients: the unit-ball bound, a chain inside an
actual geodesic ray, and the vanishing tails of a summable sequence.  The
basepoint cancels from differences.  Compact supports and radial dilation,
which establish the source's coarse lower bound, are irrelevant to the new
upper bound.

The scope qualification is important.  The theorem concerns exactly the map
$\Phi=(\varphi_n-\varphi_n(o))_n$ appearing in the source.  It does not rule
out a materially different weighted or nonlinear map which merely uses an
$\mathcal A$-collection as auxiliary information, and it does not bound the
tree's intrinsic Hilbert compression.

Bounded searches on 11 August 2026 covered the run's four cheap indexes; the
exact arXiv id, title, and question wording; title/citation variants; and
combinations of adapted fast $\mathcal A$-collections, radial dilation,
normalized characteristic maps, trees, compression $1/2$, and compression
$1/3$.  No later paper explicitly answering Remark 3.6 and no matching
universal gluing obstruction were located.  Novelty is provisional.

Human review should first confirm the contextual reading of ``inducing.''
The mathematical audit should then check the ray-chain estimate and the
two-limit little-$o$ optimization.  If a broader meaning of ``inducing'' was
intended, the theorem should be classified as a sharp obstruction for the
paper's explicit method rather than as a claim about all conceivable
$\mathcal A$-collection-based constructions.
