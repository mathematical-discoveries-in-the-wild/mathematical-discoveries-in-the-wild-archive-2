\section{Source question}

Ambrosio, Ikonen, Lu\v{c}i\'c, and Pasqualetto define, for a metric measure
space $(\X,d,\m)$ and $q\in(1,\infty]$, the dual dynamic cost
\[
 \mathsf D_q(\mu)=\inf\bigl\{\|b\|_{\Der^q(\X)}:
 b\in\Der^q_{\mathfrak M}(\X),\ \diver b=\mu\bigr\}.
\]
For finite $\m$ and finite signed $\mu$, their Proposition 5.12 identifies
this with a cost $\mathsf B_q(\mu)$ over compact rectifiable curves.  After
explaining the obstruction when $\m(\X)=\infty$, Remark 5.13 says:

\begin{quote}
When $\m$ has infinite measure, in light of the superposition principle in
[13], the natural extension is to plans which are concentrated on locally
rectifiable curves and which have a barycenter on $L^q(\m)$.  We leave the
details for future work.
\end{quote}

The passage occurs on PDF page 59 of arXiv:2510.12424; see
Figure~\ref{fig:question}.

\section{Definitions}

Throughout, $(\X,d,\m)$ is a complete separable metric space equipped with a
boundedly-finite Borel measure, as in the source.  Let
$q\in(1,\infty]$ and let $p\in[1,\infty)$ be its conjugate exponent.
Write $\mathfrak M(\X)$ for the boundedly-finite signed Borel measures.

Let $\Gamma(\X)$ be the open-ended curve space of
Ambrosio--Renzi--Vitillaro~\cite{ARV}: its elements are increasing-
reparametrization classes of maps
\[
 \gamma\in\Lip_{\mathrm{loc}}((0,1);\X)
\]
whose length inside every bounded open subset of $\X$ is finite.  At either
end, such a curve has a limit in $\X$ or escapes every bounded set.  The curve
defines a locally normal one-current $T_\gamma$.  Its boundary is
\[
 \partial T_\gamma=\delta_{e_1(\gamma)}-\delta_{e_0(\gamma)},
 \tag{2.1}\label{eq:curve-boundary}
\]
where a Dirac mass is omitted when the corresponding end escapes to infinity.

\begin{definition}[Local plan and barycenter]
A \emph{local plan} is a positive Borel measure $\eta$ on the nonconstant
curves in $\Gamma(\X)$ for
which the following two locally finite objects exist.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Its arclength occupation measure is
\[
 \nu_\eta(A)=\int_{\Gamma(\X)}\int_\gamma {\bf1}_A\,ds\,d\eta(\gamma).
\]
\item Its finite-endpoint boundary is the signed measure
\[
 \beta_\eta=\int_{\Gamma(\X)}\partial T_\gamma\,d\eta(\gamma),
 \tag{2.2}\label{eq:plan-boundary}
\]
where local absolute integrability is required:
$\int\|\partial T_\gamma\|(A)\,d\eta(\gamma)<\infty$ for every bounded
Borel set $A$.
\end{enumerate}
We say that $\eta$ has $L^q$ barycenter if
$\nu_\eta=h\m$ for some $h\in L^q(\m)^+$, and then write
$\PlanBar(\eta)=h$.
\end{definition}

The measurability requirement in this definition is intrinsic: equivalently,
the displayed occupation and endpoint integrals are required to define Borel
measures.  On the injective curves furnished by~\cite{ARV}, arclength measure
equals $\|T_\gamma\|$, whose Borel measurability is part of their construction.

For $\mu\in\mathfrak M(\X)$ define
\[
 \mathsf B_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mu)
 :=\inf\left\{\|\PlanBar(\eta)\|_{L^q(\m)}:
 \eta\text{ is a local plan},\ \beta_\eta=-\mu,\
 \PlanBar(\eta)\in L^q(\m)\right\}.
 \tag{2.3}\label{eq:Bloc}
\]
The sign in \eqref{eq:Bloc} agrees with the source: an ordinary plan starting
at $\mu^+$ and ending at $\mu^-$ has current boundary
$\mu^--\mu^+=-\mu$.

\section{Proof intuition}

The missing object is already present in the two cited papers.  A derivation
$b$ produces a locally normal current by integrating $b(g)$ against the
coefficient of the one-form.  Bounded cutoffs reduce this correspondence to
the finite-mass correspondence proved in the source, showing that the current
has mass exactly $|b|\m$ and boundary $-\diver b$.

The source also proves that a norm-minimizing derivation exists and is
acyclic.  Therefore its local current is acyclic.  The local superposition
theorem~\cite{ARV} then decomposes it into injective, possibly unbounded
curves, with no cancellation of either mass or boundary.  Thus the curve
occupation density is exactly $|b|$, and finite curve ends give exactly
$-\mu$.  This constructs an optimal local plan.  In the other direction,
integrating any admissible local plan produces a current, hence a derivation,
whose norm is bounded by the barycenter.  These are the two cost inequalities.

\section{The local derivation--current correspondence}

Denote by $\mathbf N^q_{1,b}(\X;\m)$ the locally normal one-currents $T$ for
which
\[
 \|T\|=h\m\quad\text{for some }h\in L^q(\m)^+.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Localized isometric correspondence]\label{lem:local-t}
The formula
\[
 \mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(b)(f,dg)=\int f\,b(g)\,d\m,
 \qquad f\in\Lip_{bs}(\X),
 \tag{4.1}\label{eq:tloc}
\]
defines a linear bijection
\[
 \mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}:\Der^q_{\mathfrak M}(\X)
 \longrightarrow \mathbf N^q_{1,b}(\X;\m)
\]
satisfying
\[
 \|\mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(b)\|=|b|\m,
 \qquad
 \partial\mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(b)=-\diver b.
 \tag{4.2}\label{eq:isometry}
\]
Moreover, subderivations correspond to subcurrents, cycles correspond to
cycles, and hence $b$ is acyclic if and only if
$\mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(b)$ is acyclic.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The upper mass bound and boundary identity follow directly from weak locality
and integration by parts:
\[
 |\mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(b)(f,dg)|
 \leq\int |f|\,|b|\,\operatorname{lip}_a(g)\,d\m,
 \qquad
 \partial\mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(b)(g)
 =\int b(g)\,d\m=-\int g\,d\diver b.
\]
Here one localizes $g$ by a cutoff equal to one near $\supp f$, so
\eqref{eq:tloc} is meaningful with the local-current test class.

It remains to see that the mass bound is exact and that the map is onto.  Let
$U$ be bounded and choose $\chi\in\Lip_{bs}(\X)$ with $\chi=1$ on a
neighborhood of $U$.  Then $\chi b\in\Der^1(\X)\cap\Der^q(\X)$, while
\[
 \diver(\chi b)=\chi\diver b+b(\chi)\m
\]
is finite.  The finite-mass isometric correspondence in Theorem 4.21 of the
source~\cite{AIKP} gives
$\|\mathfrak t(\chi b)\|=|\chi b|\m$.  On $U$ this current agrees with
$\mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(b)$, and locality of current mass gives
\[
 \|\mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(b)\|\mathbin{\vrule height 1.4ex depth -0.2ex
 width .08ex\vrule height .08ex depth 0ex width .7ex}U
 =|b|\m\mathbin{\vrule height 1.4ex depth -0.2ex width .08ex
 \vrule height .08ex depth 0ex width .7ex}U.
\]
An exhaustion by bounded sets proves the first identity in
\eqref{eq:isometry}.

Conversely, for $T\in\mathbf N^q_{1,b}(\X;\m)$, multiply $T$ by bounded-
support cutoffs.  Each resulting finite normal current belongs to the class in
Theorem 4.21 of~\cite{AIKP}, hence comes from a unique integrable derivation.
On regions where two cutoffs equal one, injectivity of the finite
correspondence makes these derivations agree.  They therefore glue to a unique
$b\in\Der^q_{\mathfrak M}(\X)$ with $T=\mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(b)$.
The already proved mass identity yields $|b|\m=\|T\|$, and the boundary
identity yields $\diver b=-\partial T$.

Finally, the mass identity turns
$|b-c|+|c|=|b|$ into
\[
 \|\mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(b-c)\|
 +\|\mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(c)\|
 =\|\mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(b)\|,
\]
which is exactly the subcurrent relation.  The boundary identity identifies
zero-divergence derivations with boundaryless currents.  This proves the last
claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Full local-plan duality}

\begin{theorem}[Local-plan completion of Remark 5.13]\label{thm:main}
Let $(\X,d,\m)$ be any metric measure space in the sense of~\cite{AIKP}, let
$q\in(1,\infty]$, and let $p$ be conjugate to $q$.  Then, for every
$\mu\in\mathfrak M(\X)$,
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathsf B_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mu)
 =\mathsf D_q(\mu)
 =\mathsf F_p(\mu).
 \quad}
 \tag{5.1}\label{eq:main}
\]
Whenever this value is finite, the infimum defining
$\mathsf B_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mu)$ is attained.  It has a minimizer $\eta$
concentrated on injective curves and satisfying the no-cancellation identities
\[
 \nu_\eta=|b_\mu|\m,
 \qquad
 \int_{\Gamma(\X)}\|\partial T_\gamma\|\,d\eta(\gamma)=|\mu|,
 \tag{5.2}\label{eq:exact}
\]
as identities of boundedly-finite measures, where $b_\mu$ is a
$\mathsf D_q(\mu)$-minimizer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First let $\eta$ be admissible in \eqref{eq:Bloc}, and put
$h=\PlanBar(\eta)$.  Integrating the curve currents gives
\[
 T_\eta:=\int_{\Gamma(\X)}T_\gamma\,d\eta(\gamma).
\]
The occupation identity and the elementary mass estimate for a curve current
give
\[
 \|T_\eta\|\leq\nu_\eta=h\m.
 \tag{5.3}\label{eq:massbound}
\]
The local absolute integrability in \eqref{eq:plan-boundary} permits Fubini's
theorem for the boundary, so
\[
 \partial T_\eta=\beta_\eta=-\mu.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-t} supplies $b_\eta\in\Der^q_{\mathfrak M}(\X)$ with
$T_\eta=\mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(b_\eta)$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:isometry} and \eqref{eq:massbound} imply
\[
 \diver b_\eta=\mu,
 \qquad |b_\eta|\leq h\quad\m\text{-a.e.}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \mathsf D_q(\mu)\leq\|b_\eta\|_{\Der^q}
 \leq\|h\|_{L^q(\m)}.
\]
Taking the infimum over $\eta$ proves
$\mathsf D_q(\mu)\leq\mathsf B_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mu)$.

Conversely, suppose $\mathsf D_q(\mu)<\infty$.  Theorem 5.9 of~\cite{AIKP}
provides a minimizer $b_\mu$ and proves that every such minimizer is acyclic.
Set $T=\mathfrak t_{\mathrm{loc}}(b_\mu)$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-t},
\[
 \|T\|=|b_\mu|\m,
 \qquad \partial T=-\mu,
 \qquad T\text{ is acyclic}.
 \tag{5.4}\label{eq:Tdata}
\]
The metric space $\X$ is Polish, so Theorem 1.4 of
Ambrosio--Renzi--Vitillaro~\cite{ARV} applies to $T$.  It yields a positive
Borel measure $\eta$ on $\Gamma(\X)$ such that
\[
 T=\int T_\gamma\,d\eta(\gamma),
 \qquad
 \|T\|=\int\|T_\gamma\|\,d\eta(\gamma).
 \tag{5.5}\label{eq:ARVmass}
\]
Because $T$ is acyclic, their theorem also says that $\eta$-almost every
curve is injective and that
\[
 \|\partial T\|=int\|\partial T_\gamma\|\,d\eta(\gamma).
 \tag{5.6}\label{eq:ARVboundary}
\]
For an injective locally rectifiable curve, current mass is arclength.
Combining \eqref{eq:Tdata} and \eqref{eq:ARVmass} therefore gives
$\nu_\eta=|b_\mu|\m$.  Equation \eqref{eq:ARVboundary} gives the required
local absolute integrability of the endpoint boundary, and weak integration
in \eqref{eq:ARVmass} gives
$\beta_\eta=\partial T=-\mu$.  Hence $\eta$ is admissible and
\[
 \mathsf B_q^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mu)
 \leq\|\PlanBar(\eta)\|_{L^q}
 =\|b_\mu\|_{\Der^q}
 =\mathsf D_q(\mu).
\]
This proves equality and attainment.  If $\mathsf D_q(\mu)=\infty$, the first
inequality shows that no finite-cost local plan exists, so equality holds in
the extended sense.  Finally,
$\mathsf D_q=\mathsf F_p$ is Theorem 5.9 of~\cite{AIKP}.
\end{proof}

\section{Endpoint characterization and compatibility}

For the optimal plan in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, let $\eta_B$ be its restriction
to curves with two finite endpoints, let $\eta_{BL}$ be the restriction to
curves with only a finite left endpoint, and let $\eta_{BR}$ be the restriction
to curves with only a finite right endpoint.  Acyclicity rules out a
bi-infinite component.  Remark 4.19 of~\cite{ARV}, together with
$\partial T=-\mu$, gives
\[
 (e_0)_\#\eta_B+(e_0)_\#\eta_{BL}=\mu^+,
 \qquad
 (e_1)_\#\eta_B+(e_1)_\#\eta_{BR}=\mu^-.
 \tag{6.1}\label{eq:endpoints}
\]
Thus the theorem is not merely a cost identity: it characterizes the positive
and negative parts of every divergence measure as finite endpoints of a
no-cancellation family of local curves.  When $\mu$ is finite,
\[
 \mu^+(\X)-\mu^-(\X)
 =\eta_{BL}(\Gamma(\X))-\eta_{BR}(\Gamma(\X)),
 \tag{6.2}\label{eq:infinity-balance}
\]
so the total-mass imbalance is exactly net transport to infinity.

If $\m(\X)<\infty$ and $\mu$ is finite, then
$L^q(\m)\subseteq L^1(\m)$.  The optimal plan has finite total arclength,
hence almost every curve has finite length and extends, by completeness, to a
compact rectifiable curve.  Boundary noncancellation and finiteness of $\mu$
also make the plan itself finite.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} then reduces to
Proposition 5.12 of~\cite{AIKP}.  Thus the local definition is a genuine
extension rather than a competing normalization.

\section{Sanity checks and edge cases}

\paragraph{Why rays are essential.}
Take $\X=[0,\infty)$ with Lebesgue measure, $q=\infty$, and
$b(f)=f'$ almost everywhere.  Then $|b|=1$ and
\[
 \int_0^\infty f'(x)\,dx=-f(0),
\]
so $\diver b=\delta_0$.  The increasing ray from $0$ to infinity has
barycenter $1$ and current boundary $-\delta_0$.  Compact curves cannot have a
single finite endpoint.  The local curve space is therefore indispensable.

\paragraph{The source's non-$L^1$ example.}
In Remark 5.10 of~\cite{AIKP}, an interval of length $n^2$ carries curve weight
$n^{-2}$.  The barycenter has squared $L^2$ norm
$\sum_n n^2n^{-4}=\sum_n n^{-2}<\infty$, while its $L^1$ norm is
$\sum_n n^2n^{-2}=\infty$.  This local plan consists of compact curves but is
excluded from the original dynamic-plan class, exactly as expected.

\paragraph{The endpoint $q=\infty$.}
No global integrability is used.  An $L^\infty$ barycenter is integrable on
bounded sets because $\m$ is boundedly finite, which is precisely what is
needed to define the local current and all cutoff arguments.
