\section*{Original question signal}

In the first paragraph of its introduction (PDF page 1), Xiong's
\emph{Boundedness of metaplectic Toeplitz operators and Weyl symbols}
\cite{Xiong} describes the Berger--Coburn conjecture as a long-standing open
problem.  In the standard Bargmann--Fock normalization, it asks whether a
Toeplitz operator $T_g$ with a measurable symbol and the natural kernel-domain
condition extends boundedly if and only if the borderline heat transform
$g^{(1/4)}$---equivalently, the Weyl symbol---is bounded.

The source paper proves this equivalence when $g=e^q$ and $q$ is a complex
inhomogeneous quadratic polynomial subject to its stated nondegeneracy and
integrability assumptions.  Its abstract and introduction explicitly qualify
the conclusion as completing the conjecture ``in this case.''

\section*{Later answer}

Looi's \emph{A counterexample to the Berger--Coburn conjecture}
\cite{Looi} restates the measurable-symbol assertion as Conjecture 1.1 on PDF
pages 2--3 and then proves the following as Theorem 1.2 on PDF page 3:

\begin{quote}
For every complex dimension $n\geq1$, there is a measurable symbol
$g\in L^2(\C^n,d\mu)$ such that $gk_a\in L^2(d\mu)$ for every normalized
kernel $k_a$, the Toeplitz form $T_g$ extends to a bounded operator, but
$g^{(1/4)}$ is unbounded.
\end{quote}

This violates the necessity implication in the conjecture while satisfying
its explicit hypotheses.  The supporting paper also cites Xiong's work among
the metaplectic/quadratic settings in which the endpoint principle had been
verified.  The authors therefore explicitly know that they are disproving the
general problem surrounding the source's special-case theorem.

\section*{Scope and provenance}

The later result is a full negative answer for general measurable symbols in
every $n\geq1$.  It leaves Xiong's positive characterization for
quadratic-exponential symbols untouched.

The relation was found by searching the run indexes and current literature for
the exact conjecture name, the source arXiv id, ``Weyl symbol'', and the
critical heat time.  The supporting paper's title, Conjecture 1.1, Theorem
1.2, and citation to Xiong make this an explicit literature answer rather than
an agent-created implication.
