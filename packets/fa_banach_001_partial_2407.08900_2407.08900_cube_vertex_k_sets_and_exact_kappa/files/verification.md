## Analytic proof checks

The upgraded packet separates the argument into independently reviewable
claims.

1. For any boundary point `x`, its norming set is the simplex generated by
   the signed active-coordinate functionals.  James orthogonality is exactly
   the condition that their values straddle zero.
2. With `A=F_x` and `B=||Tx||^{-1}F_{Tx}T`, local preservation and a segment
   from `x` prove `Bz>0 => Az>0`; perturbation by `epsilon*x` gives the closed
   cone implication.
3. Polyhedral Farkas duality then gives `A=CB` with `C>=0`; evaluation at `x`
   gives `C1=1`.  This proves the exact norming-simplex inclusion and its
   converse.
4. At a vertex, the source norming simplex spans the dual space.  The
   inclusion therefore forces invertibility and a vertex image, recovering
   the positive-inverse law.
5. After normalizing a contained vertex, the inverse is row-stochastic.
   Equality in a stochastic convex combination proves the exact criterion
   `T preserves at x iff x in R(B_infinity)`.
6. The near-vertex determinant is nonzero for every parameter vector in
   `(-1,1)^n`, `n>=3`.  If a preserver is singular, the ranges forced by two
   nonzero images would be distinct coordinate hyperplanes; the remaining
   rank alternatives contradict `n>=3` or the basis property.
7. In the invertible near-vertex case, norming-functional uniqueness gives
   `c_i ||row_k(R)||_1=1` for `k!=i`.  Overlaps normalize all image norms and
   row norms.  Equality in Holder's inequality, followed by the
   shared-coordinate/private-coordinate count, forces `R` to be monomial.
8. In dimension two, a vertex plus both smooth active-coordinate types makes
   both rows of the normalized stochastic inverse deterministic.  The
   diagonal and triangular constructions defeat every omitted finite type,
   proving exhaustiveness and minimality.
9. The earlier row-sign proof classifies every vertex-supported set, and the
   rank-one annihilator plus a vertex basis proves the unrestricted value
   `kappa(ell_infinity^n)=n`.
10. Direct multiplication verifies `T=J-2I`, `T^{-1}=(J-I)/2` in dimension
    three.  Its explicit pulled-back norming functionals prove that
    `{1,e_1,e_2,e_3}` is not a K-set.
