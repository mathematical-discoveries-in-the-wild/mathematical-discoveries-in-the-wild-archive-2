\section{Source question}

Creutz and Evseev study Sobolev maps into a dual Banach space
$V^*$ \cite{CE}.  They define the \emph{weak* Lebesgue density property}
and prove that it is equivalent to almost-everywhere weak* differentiability
of every absolutely continuous curve into $V^*$.  At the end of page 12 they
ask whether this one-dimensional property implies that every
$f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;V^*)$ has a representative which is weak* differentiable
almost everywhere in the coordinate directions.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{The open question in arXiv:2303.17303v1, page 12.}
\end{figure}

Their two known positive cases are: the predual $V$ is separable, or the dual
$V^*$ has the Radon--Nikod\'ym property (RNP).  The theorem below closes the
question for every countable direct sum made by mixing these two kinds of
summands.

\section{Statement and proof intuition}

Let $(V_k)_{k\in\N}$ be Banach spaces and put $X_k=V_k^*$.  For
$1\le s\le\infty$ set
\[
 X_s=\Bigl(\bigoplus_{k\in\N}X_k\Bigr)_{\ell^s}.
\]
We always use the canonical predual
\[
 E_s=
 \begin{cases}
 (\bigoplus_k V_k)_{c_0},&s=1,\\
 (\bigoplus_k V_k)_{\ell^{s'}},&1<s\le\infty,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad X_s=E_s^*,
\]
where $s'$ is conjugate to $s$ and $s'=1$ when $s=\infty$.

\paragraph{Proof intuition.}
The source obstruction is measurability: weak* differentiability tests all
elements of a potentially nonseparable predual, so linewise full-measure sets
need not combine into a measurable full-measure subset of $\Omega$.  A
countable mixed sum has an extra coordinate structure.  In a
separable-predual coordinate, countably many scalar tests suffice.  In an RNP
coordinate, norm differentiability is itself a measurable property.  There
are only countably many coordinates, so their good sets have a measurable
full-measure intersection.  At such a point, the coordinate derivatives are
assembled by finite-coordinate weak* lower semicontinuity.  Approximation by
finite-support predual vectors then gives weak* convergence in the whole sum.
This proves a substantial new class but does not resolve the arbitrary-space
measurability obstruction.

\begin{theorem}[countable mixed-sum theorem]\label{thm:main}
Assume that for every $k\in\N$ at least one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $V_k$ is separable;
 \item $X_k=V_k^*$ has the Radon--Nikod\'ym property.
\end{enumerate}
Then, for every $1\le s\le\infty$, the canonical dual $X_s=E_s^*$ has the
weak* Lebesgue density property.  Moreover, if $\Omega\subset\R^n$ is a
bounded domain, $1\le p<\infty$, and
$f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;X_s)$, then $f$ has a representative which is weak*
differentiable almost everywhere in each coordinate direction.
\end{theorem}

We separate the assembly argument used twice in the proof.

\begin{lemma}[coordinate assembly]\label{lem:assembly}
Let $q_h\in X_s$ for $0<|h|<h_0$, assume
$\sup_{0<|h|<h_0}\|q_h\|\le M$, and suppose that for every $k$ there is
$d_k\in X_k$ such that $\pi_kq_h\weakstar d_k$ as $h\to0$.  Then
$d=(d_k)_k\in X_s$, $\|d\|\le M$, and $q_h\weakstar d$ in the duality
$X_s=E_s^*$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a finite set $F\subset\N$, coordinatewise convergence is weak* convergence
in the finite dual sum.  Weak* lower semicontinuity of its norm gives
\[
 \|(d_k)_{k\in F}\|_{\ell^s}
 \le \liminf_{h\to0}\|(\pi_kq_h)_{k\in F}\|_{\ell^s}\le M.
\]
Taking the supremum over finite $F$ shows $d\in X_s$ and $\|d\|\le M$;
this includes $s=\infty$.

Let $v\in E_s$ and let $v^F$ be its truncation to a finite initial coordinate
set $F$.  Finite-coordinate convergence gives
$\langle v^F,q_h-d\rangle\to0$, while
\[
 |\langle v-v^F,q_h-d\rangle|
 \le \|v-v^F\|_{E_s}(\|q_h\|+\|d\|).
\]
Finite sequences are dense in $E_s$: this is the usual fact for
$\ell^{s'}$ when $1<s\le\infty$, and for the $c_0$-sum when $s=1$.
Letting $F\uparrow\N$ proves the full weak* convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
We first prove the weak* Lebesgue density property.  By the source theorem
\cite[final theorem of Section 3.5]{CE}, it suffices to show that every
absolutely continuous curve $\gamma:(a,b)\to X_s$ is weak* differentiable
almost everywhere.  Each coordinate curve $\gamma_k=\pi_k\gamma$ is
absolutely continuous.  If $V_k$ is separable, it is weak* differentiable
almost everywhere by a countable dense family of tests in $V_k$.  If $X_k$
has the RNP, it is norm differentiable almost everywhere.  Intersect these
full-measure sets over the countably many $k$, and also intersect with the set
where the metric derivative of $\gamma$ exists.  At a resulting point $t$ the
difference quotients
\[
 q_h=\frac{\gamma(t+h)-\gamma(t)}{h}
\]
are bounded and converge weak* in every coordinate.  Lemma~\ref{lem:assembly}
gives a weak* derivative in $X_s$.  The cited equivalence now yields the weak*
Lebesgue density property.

We turn to Sobolev maps.  Choose the standard measurable ACL representative
$\widetilde f$ supplied by the metric-valued Sobolev ACL theorem used in
\cite[Proposition 2.4]{CE}.  Fix a coordinate direction $e_j$.  The metric
partial derivative
\[
 \operatorname{m}\partial_j\widetilde f(x)
 =\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\|\widetilde f(x+he_j)-\widetilde f(x)\|}{|h|}
\]
exists at almost every $x$ and is measurable.

We construct a measurable full-measure set for each target coordinate $k$.
If $V_k$ is separable, choose a countable dense rational linear subspace
$D_k\subset V_k$.  For
every $v\in D_k$, the scalar ACL function
$x\mapsto\langle v,\pi_k\widetilde f(x)\rangle$ has its $j$-th partial
derivative almost everywhere.  Intersecting these good sets over $D_k$ and
with the metric-partial-derivative set gives a measurable full-measure set.
At one of its points the $X_k$-valued difference quotients are bounded, and
their scalar limits on $D_k$ define a bounded functional on $D_k$ which
extends uniquely to $V_k$.  Density then gives weak* convergence in $X_k$.

If $X_k$ has the RNP, the coordinate map $\pi_k\widetilde f$ is absolutely
continuous on almost every $e_j$-parallel line and is norm differentiable
almost everywhere on each such line.  The corresponding set in $\Omega$ is
measurable: on an ACL line, existence of the norm derivative is the norm-Cauchy
condition for difference quotients with rational increments, a countable
measurable condition.  Fubini therefore produces a measurable full-measure
set on which the $k$-th coordinate difference quotient converges in norm.

Intersect these measurable sets over all $k$ and with the metric-partial-
derivative set.  This is still a measurable full-measure subset of $\Omega$.
At any point $x$ in it, put
\[
 q_h(x)=\frac{\widetilde f(x+he_j)-\widetilde f(x)}{h}.
\]
The quotients are bounded for small $h$ and converge weak* in every target
coordinate.  Lemma~\ref{lem:assembly} gives a weak* $j$-th partial derivative
of $\widetilde f$ at $x$.  Finally intersect over the finitely many domain
directions $j=1,\dots,n$.
\end{proof}

\section{A space beyond both source hypotheses}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:example}
Let $\Gamma$ have cardinality strictly larger than the continuum and set
\[
 X=\ell^\infty(\N)\oplus_2\ell^1(\Gamma)
   =\bigl(\ell^1(\N)\oplus_2c_0(\Gamma)\bigr)^*.
\]
Then $X$ has the weak* Lebesgue density property and satisfies the Sobolev
weak* differentiability conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, but $X$ has no
RNP and is not a dual of any separable Banach space.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first summand is the dual of the separable space $\ell^1(\N)$, while the
second has the RNP.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} applies.  The complemented
subspace $\ell^\infty(\N)$ prevents $X$ from having the RNP.  Finally, a dual
of a separable Banach space has cardinality at most the continuum, whereas
$X$ contains the family $(e_\gamma)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}$.  Hence $X$ admits no
separable predual.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, verification, and novelty}

The theorem does not prove that an arbitrary weak* Lebesgue-density dual has
the desired Sobolev differentiability property.  It also does not treat
uncountable direct sums.  Its contribution is a countable stability mechanism
which yields explicit spaces outside both sufficient classes listed in the
source.

The proof is exact and uses no numerical computation.  The endpoint preduals
$c_0$ and $\ell^1$ are handled explicitly in Lemma~\ref{lem:assembly}.  The
source question and the later related preprint \cite{Evseev} were searched by
title, arXiv id, exact question phrase, and combinations of ``weak* Lebesgue
density'', ``direct sum'', and ``Sobolev weak* differentiability''.  The local
run indexes were searched by the same terms.  No later resolution or prior
statement of this mixed-sum theorem was found.  This is a bounded novelty
check, not exhaustive bibliographic certification.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Verify especially the measurability of the RNP-coordinate norm-differentiability
set for the common ACL representative, and the finite-support tail argument at
$s=1$ and $s=\infty$.  These are isolated in the proof so that they can be
audited without reconstructing the discovery process.
