\section{Source question}

Giryes, Nam, Elad, Gribonval, and Davies study greedy-like algorithms for the
cosparse analysis model \cite{GiryesEtAl}. Their convergence theorems assume an
optimal or near-optimal cosupport-selection oracle. In the conclusion, PDF
page 26, they ask for which structured operators such an efficient oracle
exists and single out tight-frame operators for special attention.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{The source paper's open projection question and tight-frame
  direction, arXiv:1207.2456, PDF page 26.}
\end{figure}

The source defines an $\ell$-cosparse vector by requiring at least $\ell$
zero coefficients in $\Omega x$. A cosupport selector has constant $C_\ell$
if its squared projection error is at most $C_\ell$ times the optimal squared
error. Exact projection means $C_\ell=1$.

\section{Candidate theorem}

Let $B\in\R^{\binom n2\times n}$ be an oriented edge--vertex incidence
matrix of the complete graph $K_n$: the row indexed by $i<j$ is
$e_i^T-e_j^T$. Set
\[
 Z_B(x):=\#\{(i,j):1\le i<j\le n,\ x_i=x_j\}
\]
and, for $0\le r\le\binom n2$,
\[
 F_r(z):=\min\{\|z-x\|_2^2: Z_B(x)\ge r\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Complete-graph incidence projection]\label{thm:incidence}
For every $z\in\R^n$ and every $r$, the value $F_r(z)$ and a minimizing
vector can be computed exactly using $O(n^2\binom n2)=O(n^4)$ arithmetic
operations and $O(n\binom n2)=O(n^3)$ storage. The algorithm also returns a
cosupport $\Lambda$ such that the minimizer is $Q_\Lambda z$. Hence its
near-optimality constant is $C_r=1$.
\end{theorem}

Now append one row:
\[
 \Omega=\begin{bmatrix}B\\ \one^T\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Since $B^TB=nI-\one\one^T$, this is a full-rank redundant tight-frame
analysis operator:
\[
 \Omega^T\Omega=nI.
\]
Equivalently, $n^{-1/2}\Omega$ is Parseval; this common nonzero row scaling
does not change any zero coefficient or cosupport subspace.
Let
\[
 G_r(z):=\min\{\|z-x\|_2^2:\#\{k:(\Omega x)_k=0\}\ge r\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Tight completion]\label{thm:tight}
Write $\bar z=n^{-1}\one^Tz$, put $F_{-1}(z)=0$, and regard
$F_r(z)=+\infty$ when $r>\binom n2$. Then $G_0(z)=0$ and, for
$1\le r\le\binom n2+1$,
\[
 \boxed{\quad G_r(z)=\min\big\{F_r(z),\ F_{r-1}(z)+n\bar z^2\big\}.\quad}
\]
Both branches, a minimizing vector, and an exact cosupport are computed by
the dynamic program of Theorem~\ref{thm:incidence}. Thus the tight-frame
operator has an efficient exact projection oracle with $C_r=1$ for every
target cosparsity.
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof intuition}

For $K_n$, every zero analysis coefficient says that two coordinates agree.
Thus a candidate vector is described by a partition of the coordinates into
equality blocks, and a block of size $s$ contributes $\binom{s}{2}$ zeros.
For a fixed partition, the closest vector replaces the data on each block by
their mean. The key one-dimensional observation is that after sorting the
data, an optimal partition with any prescribed multiset of block sizes can be
taken to consist of consecutive intervals. The projection problem therefore
becomes a finite segmentation dynamic program with an extra state recording
the accumulated number of equal pairs.

The appended all-ones row creates only two cases. If it is nonzero, at least
$r$ pair-difference rows must vanish. If it is zero, only $r-1$ pair rows are
needed, and the projected vector must have mean zero. Enforcing mean zero
shifts every block mean by the same global mean and adds exactly
$n\bar z^2$ to the squared error.

\section{Proofs}

\begin{lemma}[Contiguous blocks]\label{lem:contiguous}
Let $y_1\le\cdots\le y_n$ and fix positive block sizes
$s_1,\ldots,s_m$ summing to $n$. Among all partitions of the data into
blocks with this multiset of sizes, one minimizing the within-block sum of
squares can be chosen so that every block is a consecutive interval in the
sorted order.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take an optimal partition, let its block means be
$\mu_1\le\cdots\le\mu_m$, and relabel the corresponding sizes in that
order. Temporarily keep these means fixed. Assigning the sorted data to means
with capacities $s_j$ has cost matrix
$c_{ij}=(y_i-\mu_j)^2$. For $i<i'$ and $j<j'$,
\[
 c_{ij}+c_{i'j'}\le c_{ij'}+c_{i'j},
\]
because the right side minus the left side is
$2(y_{i'}-y_i)(\mu_{j'}-\mu_j)\ge0$. Hence crossing assignments may be
uncrossed without increasing cost. Repeated uncrossing gives the consecutive
capacity-respecting assignment. Replacing each fixed mean by the mean of its
new block can only decrease the cost. The original partition was optimal, so
the resulting consecutive partition is also optimal and has the same block
sizes.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:incidence}]
Any $x$ determines equality blocks. Replacing its value on each block by the
mean of the corresponding entries of $z$ cannot increase the error and cannot
decrease the number of zero pair differences; coincident new means merely
merge blocks. By Lemma~\ref{lem:contiguous}, an optimum can therefore be
chosen as consecutive blocks after sorting $z$.

Let $y_1\le\cdots\le y_n$ be the sorted data and define the interval cost
\[
 E(a,b)=\sum_{i=a}^b(y_i-\bar y_{a:b})^2.
\]
Prefix sums of $y_i$ and $y_i^2$ evaluate every $E(a,b)$ in constant time.
Let $D(i,q)$ be the minimum cost of partitioning $y_1,\ldots,y_i$ into
consecutive blocks contributing exactly $q$ equal pairs. Initialize
$D(0,0)=0$ and all other states to $+\infty$. The recurrence is
\[
 D(i,q)=\min_{0\le j<i}\left\{
 D\!\left(j,q-\binom{i-j}{2}\right)+E(j+1,i)
 \right\},
\]
where states with an invalid second index are omitted. Therefore
\[
 F_r(z)=\min_{q\ge r}D(n,q).
\]
There are $O(n\binom n2)$ states and at most $n$ transitions per state.
Backpointers recover the intervals and their means. Taking every within-block
edge as $\Lambda$ gives precisely the subspace of block-constant vectors, so
the recovered minimizer equals $Q_\Lambda z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:tight}]
The Gram identity follows from the complete-graph Laplacian:
$B^TB=nI-\one\one^T$. Moreover,
\[
 \#\{k:(\Omega x)_k=0\}=Z_B(x)+\mathbf 1_{\{\one^Tx=0\}}.
\]
Thus the feasible set at level $r\ge1$ is the union of the level-$r$
incidence set and the intersection of the level-$(r-1)$ incidence set with
$\one^\perp$.

For a fixed equality partition, its block-constant subspace contains
$\one$. The unconstrained projection uses block means and preserves the
global sum of $z$. Its projection onto the additional hyperplane
$\one^\perp$ subtracts $\bar z\one$. Orthogonality gives the extra squared
cost $\|\bar z\one\|_2^2=n\bar z^2$, independent of the partition. Minimizing
the two branches gives the displayed formula. The second branch is recovered
from an optimal level-$(r-1)$ incidence partition followed by subtraction of
$\bar z\one$; the equality pattern is unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Clique unions and the source algorithms]
For an incidence operator of a disjoint union of cliques, compute a local
table on each clique and convolve the tables over the total cosparsity budget.
Appending the component-sum row on each clique gives the analogous tight
completion locally; nonzero componentwise row scaling normalizes the block
frame bounds without changing any cosupport. These projections remain
polynomial-time and exact.

In particular, for the single-clique tight operator in
Theorem~\ref{thm:tight}, every projection constant assumed in the source
paper's AIHT, AHTP, ACoSaMP, and ASP theorems is algorithmically available
with value one. Their recovery conclusions therefore apply under their
original $\Omega$-RIP and noise hypotheses without an unavailable projection
oracle.
\end{corollary}

\section{Exact verification}

The included script
\texttt{code/verify\_complete\_graph\_projection.py} implements both
dynamic programs using rational arithmetic and compares them with exhaustive
enumeration of all set partitions. The default run checks every vector in
$\{-1,0,1\}^n$ for $1\le n\le6$ and 80 deterministic random vectors in
dimensions seven and eight, at every admissible incidence and tight-frame
cosparsity. It checked 1,172 vector instances and 35,318 optimum/projection
comparisons with no failure. This is not a proof; it checks the recurrence,
reconstruction, zero counts, tight-frame branch, repeated-value cases, and
zero-mean cases against an independent exhaustive specification.

\section{Novelty check and limitations}

Tillmann, Gribonval, and Pfetsch prove strong NP-hardness of general cosparse
projection and exclude an FPTAS unless $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$
\cite{TillmannEtAl}. They explicitly cite the source oracle assumption and
leave general practical approximation and further structured cases open.
The source and that supporting paper already record unitary, one-dimensional
finite-difference, and fused-Lasso tractable cases.

The run indexes and bounded arXiv/web searches through August 13, 2026 used
combinations of complete graphs, incidence operators, graph cosparsity, tight
frames, and dynamic programming. No statement of the result in this packet was
found. Novelty confidence is moderate rather than exhaustive.

This packet does not give a general-operator approximation, does not address
all tight frames, and does not prove new $\Omega$-RIP bounds. Human review
should focus on Lemma~\ref{lem:contiguous}, the exact zero-count state, and
the zero-DC union formula in Theorem~\ref{thm:tight}.
