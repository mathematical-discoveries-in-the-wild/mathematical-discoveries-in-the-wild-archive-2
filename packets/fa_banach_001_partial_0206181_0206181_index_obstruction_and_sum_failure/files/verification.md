## Divisibility obstruction

- If `ell_q` embeds into a `p`-saturated representative, its image is an
  infinite-dimensional subspace and must contain `ell_p`.
- Distinct classical sequence spaces `ell_p` and `ell_q` do not embed into
  one another in the required direction; equivalently, use classical
  saturation together with Pitt's theorem.
- The statement deliberately treats only finite `p`. The source places an
  endpoint `infinity` in the index but does not make fully consistent whether
  its saturation theorem there means an `ell_infinity`- or `c_0`-sum.

## Finite-block erasure

- Both directions of finite representability use finite outer-coordinate
  truncation. Repetition of every exponent and every finite block prevents a
  multiplicity mismatch.
- The displayed decomposition of `Z` is an FDD with finite-dimensional
  coordinate projections.
- Weak convergence makes every fixed initial projection tend to zero, so the
  gliding-hump subsequence can be chosen with successively disjoint supports
  and summable perturbation error.
- Disjoint normalized blocks in an outer `ell_2` sum are exactly the `ell_2`
  basis.
- The canonical basis of `ell_q`, `1<q<infinity`, is weakly null and every
  subsequence is equivalent to the whole basis.
- The growth comparison `N^(1/q)` versus `N^(1/2)` rules out equivalence for
  `q!=2`.

- The source's ambiguous endpoint convention does not affect the proved
