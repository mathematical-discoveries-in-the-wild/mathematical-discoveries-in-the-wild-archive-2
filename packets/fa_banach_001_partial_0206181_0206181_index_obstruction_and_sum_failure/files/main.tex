\large Partial progress on Problem 1 of arXiv:0206181}
\author{Candidate solution packet}

\begin{abstract}
Tokarev asked whether a finite-equivalence class $X^f$ can have infinitely
many exponents $p$ for which every representative contains $\ell_p$.  We do
not settle the problem, but prove two useful reductions.  First, no finite
exponent can belong to the divisibility index of a positive example.  Second, the most direct
countable-sum construction fails sharply: a sum containing prescribed
$\ell_{q_n}$ coordinates is finitely equivalent to an $\ell_2$-sum of
finite-dimensional blocks containing none of those $\ell_{q_n}$ spaces.
\end{abstract}

\section*{1. Exact problem and notation}

For a Banach space $Y$, the source writes
\[
 T(Y)=\{p:\ell_p\text{ is isomorphic to a subspace of }Y\}.
\]
If $X^f$ denotes the class of all Banach spaces finitely equivalent to $X$,
Problem 1 asks verbatim:
\begin{quote}
``Whether there exists such a class $X^f$ that
$\cap\{T(Y):Y\in X^f\}$ is infinite?''
\end{quote}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The source's divisibility index is
\[
 \ind(X^f)=\{r\in[1,\infty]:Y\in X^f\Longrightarrow
 \ell_r(Y)\in X^f\}.
\]
It also proves a saturation theorem.  Every $r$-divisible class contains a
representative $Y_r$ such that each infinite-dimensional subspace of $Y_r$
contains an isomorphic copy of $\ell_r$.

\section*{2. A finite-divisibility obstruction}

\begin{proposition}[Finite divisibility obstruction]\label{prop:index}
If $r\in\ind(X^f)$ for some $1\le r<\infty$, then
\[
 \left|\bigcap_{Y\in X^f}T(Y)\right|\le1.
\]
In particular, the divisibility index of any positive answer to Problem 1
contains no finite exponent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the saturation theorem quoted above, there is an
$\ell_r$-saturated $Y_r\in X^f$.  If
$\ell_q\hookrightarrow Y_r$, then the infinite-dimensional subspace
$\ell_q$ must itself contain $\ell_r$.  Classical total incomparability of
the sequence spaces $\ell_q$ and $\ell_r$ for $q\ne r$ makes this impossible.
Thus $T(Y_r)\subseteq\{r\}$ and the common intersection is contained in
$\{r\}$.

\end{proof}

This removes every self-similar construction such as a repeated
$\ell_r$-sum: once reindexing gives $\ell_r(X)\simeq_f X$, the class is
$r$-divisible and Proposition~\ref{prop:index} applies.

\section*{3. Why the obvious countable direct sum fails}

The next result records a stronger, explicit failure.  Fix distinct
exponents $q_n\in(1,\infty)\setminus\{2\}$.  Let
\begin{align*}
 X&=\left(\bigoplus_{(n,k)\in\N^2}\ell_{q_n}\right)_{\!2},\\
 Z&=\left(\bigoplus_{(n,k,m)\in\N^3}\ell_{q_n}^{,m}\right)_{\!2}.
\end{align*}
Every finite block type is repeated infinitely often in $Z$.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-block erasure]\label{prop:erase}
The spaces $X$ and $Z$ are finitely equivalent.  The space $X$ contains an
isometric copy of every $\ell_{q_n}$, whereas $Z$ contains no isomorphic copy
of any $\ell_{q_n}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every finite-dimensional subspace of $X$ is, up to arbitrarily small
distortion, supported on finitely many outer coordinates.  Its projection
into each of those $\ell_{q_n}$ coordinates is finite dimensional and can,
again with arbitrarily small distortion, be moved into some coordinate copy
of $\ell_{q_n}^{m}$ in $Z$.  Distinct outer coordinates are sent to distinct
blocks, so the outer $\ell_2$ norm is preserved.  Hence $X<_f Z$.

Conversely, a finite-dimensional subspace of $Z$ is approximated by finitely
many of its finite-dimensional blocks.  Each block $\ell_{q_n}^{m}$ embeds
isometrically into a fresh copy of $\ell_{q_n}$ in $X$; the infinitely many
copies indexed by $k$ let us keep different blocks in different outer
coordinates.  Thus $Z<_f X$.

The coordinate inclusion shows $\ell_{q_n}\hookrightarrow X$.  To see that
none embeds in $Z$, use the finite-dimensional decomposition of $Z$ into its
displayed finite blocks.  Every normalized weakly null sequence in an
$\ell_2$-sum of finite-dimensional blocks has, by the usual gliding-hump
argument, a subsequence arbitrarily close to a normalized block sequence.
Disjoint normalized blocks are isometrically equivalent to the unit vector
basis of $\ell_2$.

If $J:\ell_{q_n}\to Z$ were an isomorphic embedding, then
$(Je_j)$ would be a seminormalized weakly null sequence, because
$1<q_n<\infty$.  A subsequence would therefore be equivalent to the
$\ell_2$ basis.  It is also equivalent to a subsequence of the symmetric
$\ell_{q_n}$ basis, hence to the $\ell_{q_n}$ basis itself.  This is
impossible when $q_n\ne2$, as the norm of the sum of the first $N$ unit
vectors grows as $N^{1/q_n}$ rather than $N^{1/2}$.
\end{proof}

Thus local finite-dimensional data can retain every prescribed
$\ell_{q_n}^m$ while erasing every corresponding infinite-dimensional
$\ell_{q_n}$ copy.  A full positive solution must force infinitely many
infinite types by a mechanism substantially stronger than repeated direct
sums or finite representability alone.

\section*{4. What remains}

The reductions leave a sharp residual problem:
\begin{quote}
Construct a finite-equivalence class with no finite divisibility exponent in
which infinitely many
distinct $\ell_p$ types are unavoidable in every representative, or prove
that no such class exists.
\end{quote}
An attempted isolated-exponent route would require a realization theorem
turning isolated local $\ell_p$ data into an actual subspace in every stable
representative.  Complemented copies in ultrapowers do not descend in this
generality, so that route presently has a genuine gap.

Bounded searches through August 11, 2026 found no later source stating an
answer to Tokarev's problem.  The two propositions above are elementary but
appear not to be recorded in the source.  Priority is not asserted, and
specialist review is recommended.
