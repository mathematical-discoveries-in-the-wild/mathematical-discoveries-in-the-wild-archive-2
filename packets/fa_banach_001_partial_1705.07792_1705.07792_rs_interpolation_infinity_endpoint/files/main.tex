\section{Source statement and endpoint left out}

For a complex Banach space \(Y\), a bounded interval \(J\), and
\(1\leq s<\infty\), Amenta--Lorist--Veraar define an \(R^s(J;Y)\)-atom to
be a function
\[
 a=\sum_{I\in\cI}c_I\1_I,
 \qquad
 \Big(\sum_{I\in\cI}\|c_I\|_Y^s\Big)^{1/s}\leq1,
 \tag{1}
\]
where \(\cI\) is a family of mutually disjoint subintervals of \(J\).
The space \(R^s(J;Y)\) consists of the \(L^\infty\)-convergent sums
\(f=\sum_k\lambda_k a_k\), with norm the infimum of
\(\sum_k|\lambda_k|\) over all such representations.  Their endpoint
convention is
\[
 R^\infty(J;Y):=L^\infty(J;Y).
 \tag{2}
\]

Writing
\(q_\theta=[q_0,q_1]_\theta\), Theorem 4.4 of the source proves
\[
 R^{q_\theta}(J;Y)
 \hookrightarrow
 [R^{q_0}(J;Y),R^{q_1}(J;Y)]_\theta
 \tag{3}
\]
and its ordered multi-interval \(R_0^s\) analogue, but in both statements
adds the restriction \(q_1\ne\infty\) \cite{ALV}.  The page reproduced
below also records the paper's separate open questions about the
\(V^s\)-scale and reverse inclusions.

\section{Result}

\begin{theorem}[single-interval endpoint]\label{thm:single}
Let \(Y\) be a complex Banach space, let \(J\subset\mathbb R\) be a
bounded interval, let \(1\leq q<\infty\), and let
\(0<\theta<1\).  Put
\[
 \frac1r=\frac{1-\theta}{q},
 \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad r=\frac{q}{1-\theta}.
\]
Then the missing endpoint inclusion holds:
\[
 R^r(J;Y)
 \hookrightarrow
 [R^q(J;Y),L^\infty(J;Y)]_\theta.
 \tag{4}
\]
With the standard norms for the complex method and vector-valued sequence
spaces, the inclusion in \((4)\) is contractive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[ordered multi-interval endpoint]\label{thm:multi}
Let \(\cJ=(J_k)_{k\in\N}\) be an ordered family of mutually disjoint
bounded intervals.  Under the hypotheses and notation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:single},
\[
 R_0^r(\cJ;Y)
 \hookrightarrow
 [R_0^q(\cJ;Y),R_0^\infty(\cJ;Y)]_\theta.
 \tag{5}
\]
Here \(R_0^\infty\) is the \(c_0\)-sum of the intervalwise
\(L^\infty\) spaces, exactly as prescribed by the source definition.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}
The two \(R^s\) assertions (4.2) and (4.4) of Theorem 4.4 in
\cite{ALV} remain true for every \(1\leq q_0\leq q_1\leq\infty\); the
displayed condition \(q_1\ne\infty\) can be deleted.
\end{corollary}

\section{Proof intuition}

An atom in \(R^r\) already comes with precisely the data needed for
interpolation: its coefficient vector lies in \(\ell^r(Y)\), while the
same step-function synthesis map sends \(\ell^q(Y)\) into \(R^q\) and
\(\ell^\infty(Y)\) into \(L^\infty\).  Interpolating this one linear map
places every \(R^r\)-atom in the desired complex interpolation space with
uniform norm.  The outer atomic norm is an \(\ell^1\)-sum, so summing the
atoms completes the proof.  The multi-interval statement then follows by
the standard fact that complex interpolation commutes with \(c_0\)-sums.

The only point requiring care is identification of the limit: the atomic
series is given to converge in \(L^\infty\), while the proof also makes it
converge in the interpolation space.  Both limits agree because the
interpolation space embeds continuously into \(L^\infty\).

\section{Proofs}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:single}]
Fix a family \(\cI\) of mutually disjoint subintervals of \(J\), and
define its synthesis operator
\[
 T_{\cI}c:=\sum_{I\in\cI}c_I\1_I.
 \tag{6}
\]
For \(c\in\ell^q(\cI;Y)\), the normalized function
\(T_{\cI}c/\|c\|_{\ell^q}\) is an \(R^q\)-atom (unless \(c=0\)).
Consequently
\[
 \|T_{\cI}:\ell^q(\cI;Y)\to R^q(J;Y)\|\leq1.
 \tag{7}
\]
Disjointness also gives the endpoint estimate
\[
 \|T_{\cI}c\|_{L^\infty(J;Y)}
 \leq \|c\|_{\ell^\infty(\cI;Y)},
 \tag{8}
\]
so \(T_{\cI}:\ell^\infty(\cI;Y)\to L^\infty(J;Y)\) has norm at most
one.

The complex interpolation theorem for operators, together with the
standard vector-valued sequence-space identity
\[
 [\ell^q(\cI;Y),\ell^\infty(\cI;Y)]_\theta
 =\ell^r(\cI;Y),
 \qquad \frac1r=\frac{1-\theta}{q},
 \tag{9}
\]
therefore yields
\[
 \|T_{\cI}c\|_{[R^q(J;Y),L^\infty(J;Y)]_\theta}
 \leq \|c\|_{\ell^r(\cI;Y)}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Identity \((9)\) is valid for arbitrary counting index sets; alternatively,
each \(c\in\ell^r\) has countable support and is the \(\ell^r\)-limit of
finite-support vectors, so the usual finite-dimensional identity and
completion suffice.  In particular, every \(R^r\)-atom has interpolation
norm at most one.

Now take \(f\in R^r(J;Y)\) and an atomic representation
\[
 f=\sum_{k=1}^\infty\lambda_k a_k,
 \qquad (\lambda_k)\in\ell^1,
 \tag{11}
\]
converging in \(L^\infty(J;Y)\).  By \((10)\), the partial sums in
\((11)\) are Cauchy in
\(X_\theta:=[R^q(J;Y),L^\infty(J;Y)]_\theta\) and converge there to some
\(g\), with
\[
 \|g\|_{X_\theta}\leq\sum_{k=1}^\infty|\lambda_k|.
 \tag{12}
\]
The identity maps both \(R^q(J;Y)\) and \(L^\infty(J;Y)\) contractively
into \(L^\infty(J;Y)\), so operator interpolation gives a contractive
embedding \(X_\theta\hookrightarrow L^\infty(J;Y)\).  Thus the same
partial sums converge in \(L^\infty\) to \(g\).  Their prescribed
\(L^\infty\)-limit is \(f\), hence \(g=f\).  Taking the infimum in
\((12)\) over all atomic representations proves \((4)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:multi}]
Affine reparametrization of each \(J_k\) onto a fixed bounded interval
\(J_*\) is an isometry for every \(R^u\)-norm, including \(u=\infty\).
Therefore
\[
 R_0^u(\cJ;Y)
 \cong c_0\bigl(\N;R^u(J_*;Y)\bigr)
 \qquad(1\leq u\leq\infty)
 \tag{13}
\]
isometrically.  Complex interpolation commutes with \(c_0\)-sums
\cite[Remark 3, Section 1.18.1]{Triebel}.  Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:single} coordinatewise, with its constant independent of
the interval, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R_0^r(\cJ;Y)
 &\cong c_0\bigl(\N;R^r(J_*;Y)\bigr)\\
 &\hookrightarrow
 c_0\bigl(\N;[R^q(J_*;Y),L^\infty(J_*;Y)]_\theta\bigr)\\
 &=
 [c_0(\N;R^q(J_*;Y)),c_0(\N;L^\infty(J_*;Y))]_\theta\\
 &\cong [R_0^q(\cJ;Y),R_0^\infty(\cJ;Y)]_\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is \((5)\).
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and scope}

The proof uses only three exact ingredients: the two endpoint bounds
\((7)\)--\((8)\), interpolation of vector-valued \(\ell^p\) spaces, and
interpolation of \(c_0\)-sums.  Infinite atomic families cause no
measurability or convergence problem: an \(\ell^r\) coefficient vector
has countable support, and the synthesis operator is bounded on the whole
compatible endpoint couple.  The outer atomic series is controlled in the
interpolation norm by the same \(\ell^1\) coefficient sum used in the
definition of \(R^r\).

This packet does \emph{not} prove
\(V^{q_\theta}\hookrightarrow[V^{q_0},V^{q_1}]_\theta\) without an
epsilon loss.  It also does not prove
\([R^{q_0},R^{q_1}]_\theta\hookrightarrow R^{q_\theta}\).
The reverse \(V^s\) inclusion, which was described as unclear in the 2017
source, is explicitly proved by an increment-operator argument in a 2026
follow-up \cite[Lemma 2.4]{DLV}; it is therefore not claimed here.

\section{Novelty audit and reviewer focus}

The run's registry, solution, attempt, and proof-gap indexes were searched
for arXiv:1705.07792, the exact paper title, \(R^s\), bounded variation,
the \(q_1=\infty\) endpoint, and complex interpolation.  Exact and close
full-text searches of the local arXiv source corpus found only the source
theorem.  Bounded web searches on 11 August 2026 used the same terms and
inspected the 2026 follow-up by Deng--Lorist--Veraar.  That paper proves the
reverse \(V^s\) inclusion and continues to cite the 2017 paper for the
opposite-direction \(R^s\) result; no statement of the endpoint theorem
\((4)\)--\((5)\) was located.

Novelty confidence is moderate rather than high because the proof is a
short direct consequence of the atomic definition and standard complex
interpolation.  The most useful human check is whether the original
restriction concealed a convention not visible in Definition 4.2.  Under
the printed definitions, the synthesis argument applies verbatim.  A
reviewer should also check the standard \(c_0\)-sum identity at the
\(L^\infty\) endpoint; this is exactly the identity already invoked in the
source's proof of its multi-interval statements.
