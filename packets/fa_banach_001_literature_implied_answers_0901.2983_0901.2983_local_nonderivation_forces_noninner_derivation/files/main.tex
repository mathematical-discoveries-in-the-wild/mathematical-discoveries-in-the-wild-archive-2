\paragraph{Status.}
Full affirmative answer implied by 2013--2014 literature. The deduction is
recorded here for clarity and is not claimed as a new theorem.

\section*{Source question}

For a von Neumann algebra $\M$ with faithful normal semifinite trace $\tau$,
Albeverio--Ayupov--Kudaybergenov--Nurjanov ask whether, for either
\[
                     \A=S(\M)\quad\text{or}\quad
                     \A=S(\M,\tau),
\]
the existence of a local derivation which is not a derivation implies the
existence of a non-inner derivation.

\section*{Later results}

Two post-question theorems eliminate all non-derivational local maps on a
von Neumann algebra without abelian direct summands.

\begin{itemize}
\item Hadwin--Li--Li--Ma, Theorem~1(1), applies to $S(\M)$: a ring containing
$\M$ whose nonabelian type summands form a separating central family has only
derivational local derivations.
\item Mukhamedov--Kudaybergenov, Theorem~3.1, applies to $S(\M,\tau)$: this
is a solid $*$-subalgebra containing every finite-trace projection.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Affirmative deduction}

\begin{theorem}
Let $\M$ be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal semifinite trace
$\tau$, and let $\A$ denote either $S(\M)$ or $S(\M,\tau)$. If $\A$ admits
a local derivation which is not a derivation, then $\A$ admits a non-inner
derivation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $z$ be the maximal central projection such that $z\M$ is abelian, and
put $\N=(1-z)\M$. Then $\N$ has no abelian direct summand and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split}
            \A=z\A\oplus(1-z)\A.
\end{equation}

First recall the elementary central-homogeneity fact. If $e$ is a central
projection and $\Delta$ is a local derivation on $\A$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:homogeneous}
                         \Delta(ex)=e\Delta(x).
\end{equation}
Indeed, choose a derivation $D$ with $\Delta(ex)=D(ex)$. Every derivation
annihilates a central idempotent, so
$\Delta(ex)=eD(x)\in e\A$. Applying the same argument to $(1-e)x$ and using
linearity gives~\eqref{eq:homogeneous}.

Consequently any local derivation $\Delta$ decomposes as
\[
                  \Delta=\Delta_z\oplus\Delta_{1-z}
\]
along~\eqref{eq:split}, and both restrictions are local derivations. If
$\A=S(\M)$, Hadwin--Li--Li--Ma's theorem makes $\Delta_{1-z}$ a
derivation on $S(\N)$. If $\A=S(\M,\tau)$,
Mukhamedov--Kudaybergenov's theorem makes it a derivation on
$S(\N,\tau|_{\N})$.

Now suppose $\Delta$ is not a derivation. Its Leibniz defect splits along
\eqref{eq:split}, while the $(1-z)$ component has zero defect. Hence $\Delta_z$ is not a
derivation. But $z\M$ is commutative, and Theorem~3.8 of the source paper
says that the existence of such a local derivation on $z\A$ is equivalent
to the existence of a non-inner derivation $d_z$ there.

Extend $d_z$ by zero:
\[
                 d(x)=d_z(zx),\qquad x\in\A.
\]
Centrality of $z$ makes $d$ a derivation on $\A$. It is not inner: every
inner derivation restricts to zero on the commutative central ideal $z\A$,
whereas $d|_{z\A}=d_z\ne0$. This proves the implication for both choices of
$\A$.
\end{proof}

\section*{Classification and scope}

For $S(\M)$ the decisive later input appeared in arXiv:1311.0030 (2013); for
$S(\M,\tau)$ it appeared in arXiv:1410.1619 (2014). The final central-summand
argument combines those results with the source's own commutative Theorem~3.8.
Thus the appropriate classification is \emph{literature-implied full answer},
not a new solution. The argument assumes exactly the source setting of a
faithful normal semifinite trace.
