# 0901.2983 — local non-derivation forces a non-inner derivation

Status: literature-implied full affirmative answer.

Model: GPT5.6.

Remark 3.9 asks whether a local derivation on `S(M)` or `S(M,tau)` which is
not a derivation forces the existence of a non-inner derivation. The answer is
yes.

The argument decomposes `M` into its maximal abelian central summand and its
summand without abelian direct parts. Local derivations respect central
projections. Later theorems of Hadwin--Li--Li--Ma (2013) and
Mukhamedov--Kudaybergenov (2014) make the restriction to the nonabelian
summand a derivation for `S(M)` and `S(M,tau)`, respectively. Therefore all
failure of the Leibniz rule occurs on the abelian summand. The source paper's
own commutative Theorem 3.8 then supplies a nonzero, hence non-inner,
derivation there, which extends by zero to the full algebra.

The decisive theorems postdate the question, so this is recorded as
literature-implied rather than as a new result.
