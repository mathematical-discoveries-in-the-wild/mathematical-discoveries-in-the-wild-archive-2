\noindent\textbf{Status.} \emph{Literature already answered}.  This is an
exact later theorem, not a new run proof.

\section{Source question}

Paulsen and Raghupathi, \emph{Representations of Logmodular Algebras}
(arXiv:0806.0284), prove in Theorem 2.2 that a logmodular subalgebra
$\mathcal A\subseteq M_n$ containing the diagonal is, after permuting the
basis, a block upper-triangular algebra.  Immediately after Corollary 2.3 on
PDF page 4 they state that they do not know whether every logmodular
subalgebra of $M_n$ has this form, but believe that it does.

Equivalently, the question is whether the diagonal-containment hypothesis can
be removed, up to unitary equivalence.

\section{Exact later theorem}

Juschenko's \emph{A description of the logmodular subalgebras in the finite
dimensional $C^*$-algebras} (arXiv:1003.3123) explicitly says in its abstract
and introduction that it proves the conjecture from Paulsen--Raghupathi.  Its
main result is:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Theorem 2.6 (Juschenko, PDF page 5).}
If $\mathcal A\subseteq M_n(\mathbb C)$ is logmodular, then $\mathcal A$ is
unitarily equivalent to an algebra of block upper-triangular matrices.
\end{quote}
This is precisely the missing classification.  The supporting paper then
deduces in Corollary 2.7 that every contractive unital homomorphism from such
an algebra into $B(H)$ is completely contractive.

The supporting author knew she was resolving the source conjecture: the
abstract says the classification ``was conjectured'' in the cited
Paulsen--Raghupathi paper, and the introduction repeats that attribution
before announcing Theorem 2.6.

\section{Scope and search record}

The four run indexes and the parsed corpus contained no earlier packet for
arXiv:0806.0284.  Exact arXiv searches for logmodular subalgebras of $M_n$ and
block upper-triangular classification returned arXiv:1003.3123 as a direct
answer.  Both source PDFs were inspected locally.

This result is finite-dimensional.  It does not settle the source's separate
questions about $H^\infty(\mathbb D)$, infinite upper-triangular operator
algebras, completely distributive CSL algebras, or the final abstract operator
algebra $\mathcal C$.
