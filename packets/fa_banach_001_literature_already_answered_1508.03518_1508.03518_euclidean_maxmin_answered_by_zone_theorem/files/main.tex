\fbox{\parbox{0.91\textwidth}{\textbf{Status: literature\_already\_answered (one explicit subproblem).}
For arbitrary Euclidean norm-one functionals $f_1,\ldots,f_m$ on
$\mathbb R^n$, $n\geq2$, one has the sharp universal estimate
\[
 \max_{\|x\|_2=1}\min_{1\leq i\leq m}|f_i(x)|
 \ \geq\ \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2m}\right).
\]
The constant cannot be increased: equality holds when the normals are
coplanar and equally spaced as unoriented lines.  This follows immediately
from Jiang and Polyanskii's 2017 proof of Fejes T\'oth's zone conjecture.}}
\end{center}

\section*{The source question}

Kobos asks for estimates of
\[
 M(f_1,\ldots,f_m;\|\cdot\|)
 :=\max_{\|x\|=1}\min_{1\leq i\leq m}|f_i(x)|
\]
for norm-one linear functionals on $\mathbb R^n$.  The paper explicitly
singles out the Euclidean norm as a first case and, by a measure argument,
obtains the lower bound $1/(\sqrt n\,m)$ there
\cite[Section~3 and Problem~4]{source}.  The general-norm and
projection-constant questions in the paper are separate and are not claimed
to be settled here.

\section*{The later theorem}

A zone of width $\omega$ on the unit sphere is the set of points within
spherical distance $\omega/2$ of a great sphere.  Jiang and Polyanskii prove
that any finite collection of zones covering a unit sphere has total width at
least $\pi$; their theorem holds in every sphere dimension and also
characterizes equality \cite[Theorem~1]{zones}.

Represent $f_i$ by a unit normal $u_i$, so
$f_i(x)=\langle u_i,x\rangle$.  Put
\[
 q:=\max_{x\in S^{n-1}}\min_i|\langle u_i,x\rangle|.
\]
For each $i$, define
\[
 Z_i:=\{x\in S^{n-1}:|\langle u_i,x\rangle|\leq q\}.
\]
The spherical distance of $x$ from the great sphere
$u_i^\perp\cap S^{n-1}$ is
$\arcsin |\langle u_i,x\rangle|$.  Consequently $Z_i$ is a zone of
width $2\arcsin q$.  The definition of $q$ says exactly that the $m$ zones
$Z_i$ cover $S^{n-1}$.  The zone theorem therefore gives
\[
  2m\arcsin q\geq\pi,
  \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
  q\geq\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2m}\right).
\]

\section*{Sharpness}

Fix a two-dimensional subspace $L\subset\mathbb R^n$, and choose unit
normals $u_1,\ldots,u_m\in L$ whose unoriented directions are equally spaced
around the projective circle, with successive angle $\pi/m$.  For any unit
$x$, write $r=\|\operatorname{proj}_Lx\|_2$.  If $r=0$, all the functionals
vanish.  Otherwise, some great-line direction $u_i^\perp\cap L$ lies within
angle $\pi/(2m)$ of $\operatorname{proj}_Lx/r$.  For that $i$,
\[
 |f_i(x)|
 =r\,\bigl|\langle u_i,\operatorname{proj}_Lx/r\rangle\bigr|
 \leq r\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2m}\right)
 \leq\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2m}\right).
\]
Thus $q$ is at most the asserted constant for this configuration, while the
preceding lower bound applies to every configuration.  Equality follows.

Equivalently, if one minimizes $M(f_1,\ldots,f_m;\|\cdot\|_2)$ over all
Euclidean norm-one $m$-tuples, its exact value is
$\sin(\pi/(2m))$, independently of $n\geq2$.  Compared with the source
estimate, this is asymptotic to $\pi/(2m)$ and removes the dimension loss.

\section*{Scope and verification}

The source problem and its Euclidean estimate were checked in the official
arXiv source and PDF.  The covering theorem and its all-dimensional scope
were checked in the official PDF of arXiv:1703.10550v2, especially
Theorem~1.  The argument above is a direct translation between absolute
functional values and angular distances from great spheres; no additional
regularity or general-position hypothesis is used.
