\section{Source question}

For a positive decreasing summable sequence $x=(x_n)$ write
\[
  (\Tp x)_n=(x_{a_\infty})_n:=\frac1n\sum_{j>n}x_j,
\]
and let $(x)$ denote the principal $B(H)$-ideal generated by $x$.
Kaftal and Weiss define the arithmetic mean at infinity of an ideal $I$ by
\[
 \Sigma(I_{a_\infty})=
 \{z\in\co:z=O(y_{a_\infty})\text{ for some }
 y\in\Sigma(I\cap\mathcal L_1)\}.
\]
For summable $x$ they prove the useful identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:principal-identity}
                  (x)_{a_\infty}=(x_{a_\infty}).
\end{equation}
An ideal $J$ is am-$\infty$ stable precisely when
$J\subset\mathcal L_1$ and $J=J_{a_\infty}$
\cite[Section~2 and Theorem~5.2]{KWdensity}; see also
\cite[Lemma~4.7 and Corollary~4.10]{KWtraces}.

The source proves that stability of $I_{a_\infty}$ forces stability of $I$
when $I$ is principal, and asks whether this remains true for arbitrary
ideals \cite[p.~23]{KWdensity}:

\section{Idea of the proof}

The tail operator has a convenient slowly varying scale.  Put, up to harmless
finite initial terms,
\[
 u^{(p)}_n=\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\log n}}(\log n)^{p/2}}{n}
 \qquad(p=0,1,2,\ldots).
\]
Integration shows that $\Tp u^{(p)}\asymp u^{(p+1)}$.  Hence the directed
union $S=\bigcup_p(u^{(p)})$ is am-$\infty$ stable.

To obtain a proper ideal with arithmetic mean at infinity equal to $S$, we
flatten every $u^{(p)}$ on a common sparse collection of intervals
$[a_k,b_k]$.  The intervals are enormous in multiplicative length,
$b_k/a_k\asymp(\log a_k)^{2k}$, so at their left endpoints every fixed finite sum
of flattened sequences is far too small to dominate $u^{(0)}$, even after any
fixed ampliation.  At the same time they are tiny on the natural logarithmic
tail scale:
\[
 \frac{\log(b_k/a_k)}{\sqrt{\log a_k}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus flattening removes only a summably small fraction of each tail, and it
does not change the principal ideal generated by the tail mean.  The ideal
generated by the flattened sequences is therefore proper in $S$, while one
application of $a_\infty$ recovers all of $S$.

\section{Construction and estimates}

For $p\ge0$ and $n\ge3$ set
\[
 v^{(p)}_n:=\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\log n}}(\log n)^{p/2}}{n},
 \qquad
 u^{(p)}_n:=\sup_{j\ge n}v^{(p)}_j.
\]
Then $u^{(p)}\in(\ell^1)^*$, it is decreasing by construction, and it
coincides with $v^{(p)}$ from some index onward.  Changing the first two terms
in any decreasing positive way is understood.  Let
\[
 A_p(n):=\sum_{j>n}u^{(p)}_j.
\]

\begin{lemma}[tail scale]\label{lem:tail}
For every fixed $p\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-asymptotic}
 A_p(n)\sim
 2e^{-\sqrt{\log n}}(\log n)^{(p+1)/2},
 \qquad
 \Tp u^{(p)}\asymp u^{(p+1)}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, for fixed integers $p,k\ge0$, if
$b=\lceil a(\log a)^{2k}\rceil$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-ratio}
                    \frac{A_p(b)}{A_p(a-1)}\longrightarrow1
                    \quad(a\to\infty).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The integral test, followed by $t=\log x$ and $r=\sqrt t$, gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{j>n}v^{(p)}_j
 &\sim \int_n^\infty
       \frac{e^{-\sqrt{\log x}}(\log x)^{p/2}}{x}\,dx \\
 &=2\int_{\sqrt{\log n}}^\infty e^{-r}r^{p+1}\,dr
 \sim2e^{-\sqrt{\log n}}(\log n)^{(p+1)/2}.
\end{align*}
The finite monotone-envelope correction does not affect this asymptotic.
Dividing by $n$ proves the second assertion in
\eqref{eq:tail-asymptotic}.  Finally,
\[
 \sqrt{\log b}-\sqrt{\log a}
 =O\!\left(\frac{k\log\log a}{\sqrt{\log a}}\right)=o(1),
 \qquad \frac{\log b}{\log a}\to1,
\]
so the ratio of the two expressions in \eqref{eq:tail-asymptotic} tends to
one.  This proves \eqref{eq:tail-ratio}.
\end{proof}

Let $\varepsilon_k=2^{-k-4}$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}, choose integers
$a_k$ inductively and put
\[
 R_k=(\log a_k)^{2k},\qquad b_k=\lceil a_kR_k\rceil,
\]
so that the intervals $[a_k,b_k]$ are disjoint, $\log b_k\le2\log a_k$,
all $u^{(p)}$ with $p\le k$ already equal their displayed formulas after
$a_k$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-loss}
 A_p(b_k)\ge(1-\varepsilon_k)A_p(a_k-1)
 \qquad(0\le p\le k).
\end{equation}
There are only finitely many requirements at stage $k$, so the induction is
possible.

For every $p\ge0$, define the decreasing summable sequence $\xi^{(p)}$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flatten}
 \xi^{(p)}_n:=
 \begin{cases}
  u^{(p)}_{b_k},&a_k\le n\le b_k\text{ for some }k\ge p,\\
  u^{(p)}_n,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Monotonicity is immediate: on a flattened interval the sequence drops to its
right-endpoint value and rejoins $u^{(p)}$ there.

\begin{lemma}[flattening preserves every tail ideal]\label{lem:flatten}
For each $p\ge0$,
\[
        \sum_{j>n}\xi^{(p)}_j\asymp A_p(n),
        \qquad
        \Tp\xi^{(p)}\asymp u^{(p+1)},
        \qquad
        (\xi^{(p)})_{a_\infty}=(u^{(p+1)}).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The mass removed from the $k$th interval is at most
\[
 L_{p,k}:=\sum_{j=a_k}^{b_k}
       (u^{(p)}_j-u^{(p)}_{b_k})
 \le A_p(a_k-1)-A_p(b_k)
 \le\varepsilon_kA_p(a_k-1).
\]
If $n$ is outside a flattened interval, every interval contributing to the
tail starts after $n$, and hence the total removed tail is at most
\[
 A_p(n)\sum_{k\ge1}\varepsilon_k\le\frac1{16}A_p(n).
\]
If $n\in[a_k,b_k]$, discard the nonnegative remainder of the current plateau.
The tail after $b_k$, less all later losses, is at least
\[
 A_p(b_k)\left(1-\sum_{q>k}\varepsilon_q\right)
 \ge (1-\varepsilon_k)\frac{15}{16}A_p(a_k-1)
 \ge (1-\varepsilon_k)\frac{15}{16}A_p(n).
\]
The reverse inequality follows from $\xi^{(p)}\le u^{(p)}$.  Thus the tails
are comparable uniformly in $n$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} now gives
$\Tp\xi^{(p)}\asymp u^{(p+1)}$, and
\eqref{eq:principal-identity} gives the principal-ideal identity.
\end{proof}

\section{The counterexample}

Define the two countably generated ideals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:I-S}
 I:=\sum_{p\ge0}(\xi^{(p)})
   =\bigcup_{P\ge0}\left(\sum_{p=0}^{P}\xi^{(p)}\right),
 \qquad
 S:=\sum_{p\ge0}(u^{(p)})=\bigcup_{p\ge0}(u^{(p)}).
\end{equation}
The sums in \eqref{eq:I-S} are ideal sums; finite sums of principal ideals
are principal.  Since $\xi^{(p)}\le u^{(p)}$, one has $I\subset S\subset
\mathcal L_1$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:counterexample}
The ideal $I$ in \eqref{eq:I-S} is not am-$\infty$ stable, but
\[
                         I_{a_\infty}=S
\]
and $S$ is am-$\infty$ stable.  In particular, am-$\infty$ stability of
$I_{a_\infty}$ does not imply am-$\infty$ stability of $I$, even for a
countably generated ideal $I\subset\mathcal L_1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First, $(u^{(p)})\subset(u^{(p+1)})$ for every $p$, because
$u^{(p+1)}_n/u^{(p)}_n\sim\sqrt{\log n}$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} and \eqref{eq:principal-identity},
\[
             (u^{(p)})_{a_\infty}=(u^{(p+1)}).
\]
It follows directly from the definition of $I_{a_\infty}$ (or by applying it
to the directed union) that
\[
                  S_{a_\infty}
 =\bigcup_{p\ge0}(u^{(p)})_{a_\infty}
 =\bigcup_{p\ge0}(u^{(p+1)})=S.
\]
Since $S\subset\mathcal L_1$, this is am-$\infty$ stability.

Lemma~\ref{lem:flatten} and monotonicity give, for every $p$,
\[
 (u^{(p+1)})=(\xi^{(p)})_{a_\infty}\subset I_{a_\infty}.
\]
The union of the left sides is $S$, so $S\subset I_{a_\infty}$.  Conversely,
$I\subset S$ and monotonicity, followed by stability of $S$, give
$I_{a_\infty}\subset S_{a_\infty}=S$.  Therefore $I_{a_\infty}=S$.

It remains to prove that $I\ne S$.  We show that $u^{(0)}\notin\Sigma(I)$.
Put $g_P=\sum_{p=0}^{P}\xi^{(p)}$.  If $u^{(0)}\in(g_P)$, then for some
$m\in\N$ one would have $u^{(0)}=O(D_mg_P)$, where
$(D_mx)_n=x_{\lceil n/m\rceil}$.  Take $k\ge\max\{P,m\}$ and evaluate at
$n=ma_k$.  All the first $P+1$ sequences are flat at $a_k$, so
\[
 (D_mg_P)_{ma_k}=g_P(a_k)=\sum_{p=0}^{P}u^{(p)}_{b_k}
 \le(P+1)u^{(P)}_{b_k}.
\]
For large $k$, using $b_k/a_k\ge R_k$, $b_k>ma_k$, and
$\log b_k\le2\log a_k$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 \frac{u^{(0)}_{ma_k}}{(D_mg_P)_{ma_k}}
 &\ge
 \frac{R_k}{m(P+1)(\log b_k)^{P/2}}
 \exp\!\left(\sqrt{\log b_k}-\sqrt{\log(ma_k)}\right)\\
 &\ge
 \frac{(\log a_k)^{2k-P/2}}
      {m(P+1)2^{P/2}}\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{align*}
Thus $u^{(0)}\notin(g_P)$ for every $P$, and hence
$u^{(0)}\notin I$.  But $u^{(0)}\in S=I_{a_\infty}$, so $I\ne I_{a_\infty}$.
As $I\subset\mathcal L_1$, the stability criterion quoted above shows that
$I$ is not am-$\infty$ stable.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty audit}

The construction is exact and has no computational dependency.  The main
review points are: (1) the standard principal identity
\eqref{eq:principal-identity}; (2) the uniform accounting of future tail loss
in Lemma~\ref{lem:flatten}; and (3) the use of one common family of flattened
intervals for all $p\le k$, which is what makes the final diagonal
noncontainment work simultaneously for every finite sum and every fixed
ampliation.

The result answers only the specific weakest question displayed in the source:
stability of $I_{a_\infty}$ need not descend to $I$.  It therefore also shows
that the full seven-ideal equivalence of Theorem 5.2 cannot extend verbatim to
arbitrary ideals.  It does not classify which additional completeness,
softness, or lattice hypotheses restore rigidity.

A bounded literature audit searched the exact source question, the phrases
``am-$\infty$ stability'' and ``arithmetic mean at infinity'', the source
authors, arXiv:0707.3115, and the closely related papers arXiv:0707.3169,
0707.3217, 0707.3271, and 1201.4826.  No later paper explicitly answering this
question was located.  The source paper and the run's lightweight result,
attempt, and proof-gap indexes contained no prior solution packet for this
target.  Novelty is therefore plausible but remains pending specialist review.
