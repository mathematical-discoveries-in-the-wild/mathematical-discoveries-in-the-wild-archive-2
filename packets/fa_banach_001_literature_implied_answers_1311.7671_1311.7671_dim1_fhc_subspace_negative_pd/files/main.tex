\section*{Source question}

Muro, Pinasco and Savransky prove that, under their standing holomorphy-type
hypotheses, every nontrivial convolution operator on
$\mathcal H_{b\mathfrak A}(E)$ has a frequently hypercyclic subspace when
$\dim E>1$.  Immediately before Theorem~4.3, in Section~4.1 on arXiv PDF
page~13, they write:
\begin{quote}
The corresponding problem for $\dim(E)=1$ is open, up to our knowledge.
\end{quote}
Thus the question is whether the universal existence theorem remains true in
one complex dimension \cite{MPS}.

\section*{Decisive later theorem}

Let $D$ be differentiation on the Fr\'echet space $H(\mathbb C)$ of entire
functions.  Bayart, Ernst and Menet prove the following on supporting PDF
page~3:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Theorem 1.2.} Let $P$ be a polynomial. Then $P(D)$ does not possess
any frequently hypercyclic subspace.
\end{quote}
See \cite{BEM}, Theorem~1.2 (and Theorem~3.2 for the full proof).

\section*{Identification and scope}

Take $P(z)=z$.  Then $P(D)=D$.  Differentiation is a continuous nontrivial
convolution operator on $H(\mathbb C)$: it commutes with every translation and
is not a scalar multiple of the identity.  In one dimension, the source
holomorphy-type construction gives the usual entire-function space
$H(\mathbb C)$.  Hence $D$ belongs to the source question's class but has no
frequently hypercyclic subspace.  The universal one-dimensional extension is
therefore false.

This identification is an implication rather than an explicitly acknowledged
answer.  Bayart--Ernst--Menet state that they answer Bonilla and
Grosse-Erdmann's earlier Problem~2; their paper does not cite Muro, Pinasco or
Savransky.  Accordingly this packet records a literature-implied answer, not
a new counterexample and not an explicitly acknowledged literature answer.

\section*{Search evidence}

The run's registry, solution and attempt indexes were searched for the source
arXiv id/title and the phrases ``frequently hypercyclic subspace'',
``convolution operator'', $H(\mathbb C)$ and differentiation.  A bounded web
and arXiv search found the 2016 theorem above, the 2015 upper-frequent result,
and later surveys confirming that $P(D)$ is frequently hypercyclic but admits
no frequently hypercyclic subspace.  The supporting PDF was checked directly.
