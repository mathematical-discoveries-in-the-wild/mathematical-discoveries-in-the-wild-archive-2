\section{Source problem and scope}

Cerjan, Lauric, and Loring prove that the Clifford spectrum of any three
$2\times2$ Hermitian matrices is nonempty \cite{CLL2026}.  Their abstract
describes a conjecture for three $n\times n$ matrices of arbitrary size, while
the introduction says that general nonemptiness of the Clifford spectrum
remains open and calls their $2\times2$ theorem ``the smallest nontrivial
case'' (source PDF, page 3).  The earlier paper of Cerjan and Loring records
the broader all-tuples conjecture explicitly \cite[Conjecture 8.1]{CL2024}.

The relevant passage from page 3 of the source paper is reproduced below.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

For the three-matrix case, fix the Pauli matrices
\[
 \sigma_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \sigma_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \sigma_3=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Given Hermitian matrices $A_1,A_2,A_3\in M_n(\C)$ and
$\lambda=(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)\in\R^3$, their localizer is
\[
 L_\lambda(A)=\sum_{j=1}^3(A_j-\lambda_j\1_n)\otimes\sigma_j.
\]
The standard Pauli Clifford spectrum is
\[
 \Lambda^{\mathrm C}(A)=
 \{\lambda\in\R^3:L_\lambda(A)\text{ is singular}\}.
\]
The source problem in the present scope asks whether this set is nonempty for
every finite $n$, not only for $n=2$.

\section{Proof intuition}

Far from the matrices, the localizer is governed by the universal Pauli term
$-\sum_j\lambda_j\1_n\otimes\sigma_j$.  On a large sphere this term has a
uniform spectral gap, so the actual localizer can be deformed to it without
crossing zero.  The negative eigenspaces of the universal term form $n$ copies
of the Hopf line bundle over $S^2$.  Their determinant has winding number
$\pm n$ and is therefore nontrivial.

If the localizer had no singularity inside the sphere, however, its negative
spectral subspaces would make a vector bundle over the whole ball.  Every
bundle over a ball is trivial, so its determinant would restrict to a trivial
line bundle on the boundary.  The nonzero Hopf winding gives the
contradiction.  The size of the universal gap also locates a singularity in a
closed ball determined by the norm of the localizer at the origin.

\section{The theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrary finite size, with a norm bound]
\label{thm:main}
Let $n\geq1$ and let $A_1,A_2,A_3\in M_n(\C)$ be Hermitian.  Set
\[
 K=\sum_{j=1}^3 A_j\otimes\sigma_j,
 \qquad C=\lVert K\rVert.
\]
Then there is a $\lambda\in\R^3$ such that
\[
 \lVert\lambda\rVert_2\leq C
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 L_\lambda(A)\text{ is singular}.
\]
In particular, $\Lambda^{\mathrm C}(A)$ is nonempty for every finite $n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[The boundary Hopf class]
\label{lem:hopf}
For $\omega\in S^2$, let $H_+(\omega)$ be the $+1$ eigenspace of
$\omega\cdot\sigma=\sum_{j=1}^3\omega_j\sigma_j$.  The line bundle $H_+$
over $S^2$ has a transition function of winding number $-1$.  Consequently,
the determinant line of $H_+^{\oplus n}$ has winding number $-n$ and is
nontrivial.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 \omega=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta).
\]
On the complement of the south pole and the complement of the north pole,
respectively, normalized $+1$ eigenvectors are
\[
 u_N(\theta,\phi)=
 \begin{pmatrix}\cos(\theta/2)\\ e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2)\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 u_S(\theta,\phi)=
 \begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\phi}\cos(\theta/2)\\ \sin(\theta/2)\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct multiplication verifies
$(\omega\cdot\sigma)u_N=u_N$ and
$(\omega\cdot\sigma)u_S=u_S$.  On the overlap,
$u_S=e^{-i\phi}u_N$.  Thus the equatorial transition function is
$e^{-i\phi}$, of winding $-1$.  For $n$ direct summands the transition matrix
is $e^{-i\phi}\1_n$, whose determinant is $e^{-in\phi}$.  Its nonzero
winding proves that the determinant line, and hence the vector bundle, is
nontrivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
If $C=0$, then $L_0(A)=K=0$ and the assertion holds with $\lambda=0$.
Assume henceforth that $C>0$.

Fix any $R>C$.  We first prove that $L_\lambda(A)$ is singular at some point
of the closed ball $B_R=\{\lambda:\lVert\lambda\rVert_2\leq R\}$.  Suppose,
to the contrary, that it is invertible everywhere on $B_R$.  Because the
localizer is Hermitian and depends continuously on $\lambda$, the negative
spectral projections
\[
 P(\lambda)=\mathbf 1_{(-\infty,0)}(L_\lambda(A))
\]
vary continuously and define a complex vector bundle $E\to B_R$.

For $\omega\in S^2$ and $t\in[0,1]$, consider the boundary homotopy
\[
 F_t(\omega)=tK-R\1_n\otimes(\omega\cdot\sigma).
\]
Its endpoint at $t=1$ is the actual boundary localizer $L_{R\omega}(A)$.
Since $(\omega\cdot\sigma)^2=\1_2$, the operator
$R\1_n\otimes(\omega\cdot\sigma)$ has inverse norm $1/R$.  More directly,
for every vector $v$,
\begin{align*}
 \lVert F_t(\omega)v\rVert
 &\geq R\lVert v\rVert-t\lVert Kv\rVert\\
 &\geq (R-tC)\lVert v\rVert
 \geq (R-C)\lVert v\rVert>0.
\end{align*}
Thus $F_t(\omega)$ is invertible throughout $S^2\times[0,1]$.

At $t=0$, the negative eigenspace of
$F_0(\omega)=-R\1_n\otimes(\omega\cdot\sigma)$ is
\[
 \C^n\otimes H_+(\omega)\cong H_+(\omega)^{\oplus n}.
\]
It has rank $n$.  The negative spectral projections of $F_t$ give a homotopy
of boundary bundles, so
\[
 E|_{\partial B_R}\cong H_+^{\oplus n}
\]
up to bundle homotopy; in particular their determinant lines have the same
winding number.  Lemma~\ref{lem:hopf} says this number is $-n\neq0$.

On the other hand, $B_R$ is contractible, so the vector bundle $E\to B_R$ is
trivial.  Its determinant line is therefore trivial, and its restriction to
$\partial B_R\cong S^2$ must have winding number zero.  This contradicts the
preceding paragraph.  Hence every $R>C$ contains a point $\lambda_R$ at which
$L_{\lambda_R}(A)$ is singular.

Choose $R_k=C+1/k$.  The points $\lambda_{R_k}$ lie in the fixed compact ball
$B_{C+1}$, so a subsequence converges to some $\lambda$ with
$\lVert\lambda\rVert_2\leq C$.  Since the determinant is continuous,
\[
 \det L_\lambda(A)=
 \lim_{k\to\infty}\det L_{\lambda_{R_k}}(A)=0.
\]
This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Other three-generator representations]
Every finite-dimensional complex representation of the three-generator
Clifford relations is a direct sum of irreducible chiral representations.  An
orientation reversal changes the sign of the Hopf winding but not its
nonvanishing.  Hence each irreducible summand has a nonempty spectrum by the
same proof, and a direct-sum localizer is singular wherever one summand is.
The theorem was stated with the source paper's Pauli convention to keep the
scope exact.
\end{remark}

\section{Verification and limitations}

The proof is finite-dimensional and does not address triples of unbounded or
infinite-dimensional self-adjoint operators.  It also does not settle the
broader conjecture for an arbitrary number of matrices; the obstruction used
here is specifically the nontrivial boundary class of the Pauli map on
$S^2$.

The boundary gap, negative rank, explicit transition function, extension
contradiction, and limiting norm bound were audited separately in
\path{VERIFICATION.md}.  The optional script
\path{code/verify_boundary_obstruction.py} checks the gap and winding on
random finite examples, but no numerical statement is used in the proof.

\section{Novelty check}

A bounded search on 9 August 2026 covered the run's four lightweight indexes,
the exact arXiv identifier and title, and arXiv/web combinations of
``Clifford spectrum'' with ``Hopf line bundle,'' ``negative spectral
subspace,'' ``Chern class,'' ``Pauli,'' and ``nonempty.''  It found the source
$2\times2$ theorem \cite{CLL2026} and the earlier general conjecture
\cite{CL2024}, but no proof of the arbitrary-$n$ three-matrix case and no
appearance of the argument above.  Novelty confidence is therefore moderate,
not definitive, pending specialist review and broader citation-database
searches.
