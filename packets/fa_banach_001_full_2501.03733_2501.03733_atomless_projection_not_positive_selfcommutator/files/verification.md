## Source and convention audit

The source's Question 5.3 asks whether, on the Hilbert lattice
`ell^2 direct-sum L^2[0,1]`, the block projection `diag(0,I)` can be written
as `A^*A-AA^*` for a positive operator `A`.  The packet uses the same order
of the commutator and the same atomic/atomless block ordering.

## Regularity audit

A regular operator is a linear combination of positive operators.  The
coordinate inclusions and projections of the lattice direct sum are
positive, so every block of a regular `A` is regular.  If `T=T_1-T_2` with
positive `T_i`, then `T^*=T_1^*-T_2^*`, and adjoints of positive operators
are positive.  Hence all adjoint blocks used in the proof are regular.

Positive operators are regular, so exclusion of every regular `A` is a
genuine strengthening of the source question.

## Block multiplication and sign audit

For

```text
A = [[P,Q],[R,S]],
A^* = [[P^*,R^*],[Q^*,S^*]],
```

the lower-right blocks are

```text
(A^*A)_22 = Q^*Q + S^*S,
(AA^*)_22 = RR^* + SS^*.
```

Thus `A^*A-AA^*=diag(0,I)` forces exactly

```text
I = Q^*Q - RR^* + S^*S - SS^*.
```

No other block equation is used.

## Regular-kernel ideal audit

Let `J=I^r(L^2[0,1])`, the band generated by regular finite-rank operators.
Since `L^2` has order-continuous norm, the standard composition properties
make `J` a two-sided algebra ideal of `L^r(L^2)`.  A band in this Banach
lattice is closed in the regular norm.

Blanco, Theorem 2(ii), states that a regular operator which regularly
factors through an order-continuous purely atomic Banach lattice belongs to
the band generated by the regular finite-rank operators.  The intermediate
lattice here is `ell^2`, which satisfies both hypotheses.  Therefore

```text
Q^*Q = Q^* o Q in J,
RR^* = R o R^* in J.
```

The factor directions and domains match: both compositions start and end
on `L^2[0,1]`.

## Properness audit

The identity on atomless `L^2[0,1]` is not a regular kernel operator.  If it
had a measurable integral kernel `k(t,x)`, then testing indicators from a
countable generating algebra would say that, for almost every fixed `x`,
the absolutely continuous measure `k(t,x)dt` agrees with the point mass
`delta_x` on that algebra, and hence everywhere.  This is impossible.
Consequently `I notin J`, and `L^r(L^2)/J` is a nonzero unital Banach
algebra whose unit is `I+J`.

## Quotient and Wintner audit

Modulo `J`, the block equation becomes

```text
1 = [S^*+J,S+J].
```

If `[t,s]=1` in a unital Banach algebra, the derivation identity gives
`[t,s^n]=n s^(n-1)`.  Therefore

```text
||s^n|| >= n!/(2||t||)^n,
```

while submultiplicativity gives `||s^n||<=||s||^n`.  Since factorial growth
beats every exponential, the two inequalities contradict one another.
This proof works over both the real and complex scalars.
