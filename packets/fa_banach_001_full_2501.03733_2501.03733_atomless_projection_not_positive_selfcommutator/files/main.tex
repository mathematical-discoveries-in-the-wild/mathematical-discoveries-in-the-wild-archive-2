\section{Source question}

The source is R.~Drnov\v{s}ek and M.~Kandi\'c \cite{DK}.  Their article,
\emph{Positive self-commutators of positive operators} (arXiv:2501.03733),
appeared in \emph{Positivity} 29 (2025), Article~43.  Its concluding
Question~5.3 asks whether, on the Hilbert lattice
\[
 H=\ellTwo\oplus L^2[0,1],
\]
the positive operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C}
 C=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
is a self-commutator $A^*A-AA^*$ of a positive operator $A$.

\section{Regular kernel operators}

We isolate the standard Banach-lattice input.  For a Banach lattice $E$,
write $\Lr(E)$ for the unital Banach algebra of regular operators on $E$,
equipped with the regular norm.  On $L=L^2[0,1]$, let
\[
 J=\Kern(L)
\]
be the band of regular kernel operators, equivalently the band generated by
the regular finite-rank operators.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ideal}
The set $J$ is a proper closed two-sided ideal of $\Lr(L)$.  Moreover, every
regular operator on $L$ that regularly factors through $\ellTwo$ belongs to
$J$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The band $J$ is norm closed.  Because $L$ has order-continuous norm, the
standard composition properties of regular kernel operators show that it is
a two-sided algebra ideal in $\Lr(L)$.

For the factorization assertion, use Blanco's Theorem~2(ii) \cite{Blanco}:
if a regular operator between Banach lattices regularly factors through an
order-continuous purely atomic Banach lattice, then it belongs to the band
generated by the regular finite-rank operators.  The lattice $\ellTwo$ is
order continuous and purely atomic, so the theorem applies.

For completeness, $J$ is proper because $I_L$ is not a kernel operator.
Indeed, if a measurable kernel $k(t,x)$ represented $I_L$, then, after
testing the constant function, $k(\mathord\cdot,x)$ would be integrable for
almost every $x$.  Testing the indicators of a countable algebra generating
the Lebesgue measurable sets would give
\[
 \int_A k(t,x)\,dt=\mathbf 1_A(x)
\]
for all sets $A$ in that algebra and almost every fixed $x$.  By uniqueness
of finite measures this says that the absolutely continuous measure
$k(t,x)\,dt$ is the point mass $\delta_x$, impossible on the atomless
Lebesgue space.  Hence $I_L\notin J$.
\end{proof}

\section{The negative answer}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is no regular operator $A$ on $\ellTwo\oplus L^2[0,1]$ such that
\[
 A^*A-AA^*=0\oplus I.
\]
Consequently, the answer to Question~5.3 is negative.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose such a regular operator exists and write it in blocks as
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}P&Q\\R&S\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 Q:L\longrightarrow\ellTwo,\quad
 R:\ellTwo\longrightarrow L.
\]
The coordinate inclusions and projections are positive, so all four blocks
are regular.  Adjoints of regular operators are regular as well.  Comparing
the lower-right corners of $A^*A-AA^*$ and \eqref{eq:C} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
 I_L=Q^*Q-RR^*+S^*S-SS^*.
\end{equation}

Both cross terms in \eqref{eq:block} regularly factor through $\ellTwo$:
\[
 L\xrightarrow{Q}\ellTwo\xrightarrow{Q^*}L,
 \qquad
 L\xrightarrow{R^*}\ellTwo\xrightarrow{R}L.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal} therefore puts $Q^*Q$ and $RR^*$ in $J$.  Since
$I_L\notin J$, the quotient
\[
 \mathcal A=\Lr(L)/J
\]
is a nonzero unital Banach algebra.  If $s=S+J$ and $t=S^*+J$, equation
\eqref{eq:block} descends to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comm}
 1_{\mathcal A}=ts-st=[t,s].
\end{equation}

No unit of a Banach algebra is a commutator.  Here is the short Wintner
argument.  From $[t,s]=1$ one obtains inductively
\[
 [t,s^n]=n s^{n-1}\qquad(n\geq1).
\]
Thus
\[
 n\,\|s^{n-1}\|\leq 2\|t\|\,\|s^n\|,
\]
and iteration yields
\[
 \|s^n\|\geq \frac{n!}{(2\|t\|)^n}.
\]
This contradicts $\|s^n\|\leq\|s\|^n$ as $n\to\infty$.  Hence
\eqref{eq:comm}, and therefore the assumed $A$, cannot exist.

Every positive operator is regular, so the stronger statement implies the
negative answer requested by the source.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The proof uses only the lower-right block equation; no unproved assertion
about the other three blocks is needed.  The signs in \eqref{eq:block}
follow from
\[
 (A^*A)_{22}=Q^*Q+S^*S,
 \qquad
 (AA^*)_{22}=RR^*+SS^*.
\]
The imported factorization theorem applies to arbitrary regular factors, so
positivity is not needed for the strengthening.  A detailed adversarial
audit appears in the companion \texttt{verification.md}.

The local run indexes were searched using the arXiv identifier and exact
title, as well as terms for the mixed atomic/atomless projection, positive
self-commutators, and regular kernel factorization.  Bounded web searches on
11 August 2026 used the exact question and the kernel-ideal quotient route.
They found the source and the background factorization theorem but no later
answer to Question~5.3 and no matching quotient proof.  Novelty confidence
is moderate because citation and web coverage is bounded.
