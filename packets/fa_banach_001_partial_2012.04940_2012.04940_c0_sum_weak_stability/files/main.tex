\section{Source question}

A unit ball is \emph{weakly stable} if every finite convex combination of
relatively weakly open subsets of the ball is again relatively weakly open.
This is property CWO in the terminology of Abrahamsen et al. \cite{Aetal}.

L\'opez-P\'erez and Medina prove that $B_{C_0(K,X)}$ is weakly stable when
$K$ is scattered and $B_X$ is both norm stable and weakly stable.  Conversely,
weak stability of $B_{C_0(K,X)}$ forces weak stability of both $B_{C_0(K)}$
and $B_X$; the first condition forces $K$ to be scattered.  They then ask
whether these necessary conditions are sufficient without the norm-stability
hypothesis \cite[p.~13]{LM}.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

We prove the full converse for every discrete $K$, and in fact for arbitrary
$c_0$-sums with varying fibers.

\section{Claimed contribution}

For a family $(X_\gamma)_{\gamma\in\G}$, write
\[
 Z=\left(\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\G}X_\gamma\right)_{c_0}
 =\left\{z=(z_\gamma):\sup_\gamma\|z_\gamma\|<\infty,
 \ \{\gamma:\|z_\gamma\|\geq\varepsilon\}\text{ finite for all }
 \varepsilon>0\right\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $\G$ be nonempty and $(X_\gamma)_{\gamma\in\G}$ be arbitrary real or
complex Banach spaces.  Then $B_Z$ is weakly stable if and only if every
$B_{X_\gamma}$ is weakly stable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:discrete}
If $K$ is any discrete locally compact space and $X$ any Banach space, then
\[
 B_{C_0(K,X)}\text{ is weakly stable}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 B_X\text{ is weakly stable}.
\]
Equivalently, for every nonempty set $\G$ and every weakly stable $X$,
\[
 c_0(\G)\widehat\otimes_\varepsilon X
 \cong c_0(\G,X)
\]
has a weakly stable unit ball.
\end{corollary}

\section{Idea of the proof}

The dual of a $c_0$-sum is an $\ell_1$-sum.  Hence finitely many weak tests can
be approximated simultaneously by tests supported on one finite coordinate
set $F$.  Shrink each original weak neighborhood so that controlling weak
coordinates on $F$ is sufficient to remain inside it.

Now split a nearby target independently in every coordinate of $F$, using weak
stability of the corresponding fiber.  No continuous selection is required:
$F$ is finite and discrete.  Outside $F$, put the target coordinate itself in
every component of the convex combination.  The resulting component vectors
still vanish at infinity, because they differ from the target at only finitely
many coordinates.  This gives the desired weak neighborhood of the convex
combination.

\section{Proof}

We first isolate the finite-coordinate reduction.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:finite}
Let $z\in B_Z$ and let $U$ be a relatively weakly open neighborhood of $z$ in
$B_Z$.  There are a finite set $F\subset\G$ and, for every $\gamma\in F$, a
relatively weakly open neighborhood $U_\gamma$ of $z_\gamma$ in
$B_{X_\gamma}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:box}
 w\in B_Z,\quad w_\gamma\in U_\gamma\ (\gamma\in F)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad w\in U.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since
\[
 Z^*=\left(\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\G}X_\gamma^*\right)_{\ell_1},
\]
choose $f_1,\dots,f_r\in Z^*$ and $\eta>0$ such that the basic neighborhood
\[
 V=\{w\in B_Z:|f_q(w-z)|<\eta,\ 1\leq q\leq r\}
\]
is contained in $U$.  Approximate every $f_q$ in dual norm by a finitely
supported $g_q$ so that $2\|f_q-g_q\|<\eta/2$, and let $F$ contain all their
supports.  If $F$ is empty there is nothing to prove.  Otherwise, writing
$g_q=(g_{q,\gamma})_{\gamma\in F}$, put
\[
 U_\gamma=\left\{u\in B_{X_\gamma}:
 |g_{q,\gamma}(u-z_\gamma)|<\frac{\eta}{2|F|}
 \text{ for }1\leq q\leq r\right\}.
\]
If $w$ satisfies the left side of \eqref{eq:box}, then
\[
 |g_q(w-z)|\leq\sum_{\gamma\in F}
 |g_{q,\gamma}(w_\gamma-z_\gamma)|<\eta/2,
\]
while $|(f_q-g_q)(w-z)|\leq2\|f_q-g_q\|<\eta/2$.
Thus $w\in V\subset U$.
\end{proof}

Assume first that every $B_{X_\gamma}$ is weakly stable.  Let
$U_1,\dots,U_m$ be relatively weakly open subsets of $B_Z$, let
$\lambda_j>0$ with $\sum_j\lambda_j=1$, and fix
\[
 z=\sum_{j=1}^m\lambda_jz^{,j},\qquad z^{,j}\in U_j.
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:finite} to every pair $(z^{,j},U_j)$ and enlarge the
finitely many resulting supports to a common finite set $F$.  Taking
$U_{j,\gamma}=B_{X_\gamma}$ on any newly added coordinate, we obtain weak
neighborhoods $U_{j,\gamma}$ of $z^{,j}_\gamma$ such that coordinate
membership on $F$ forces membership in $U_j$.

For $\gamma\in F$, weak stability of $B_{X_\gamma}$ makes
\[
 W_\gamma=\sum_{j=1}^m\lambda_jU_{j,\gamma}
\]
relatively weakly open in $B_{X_\gamma}$ and containing $z_\gamma$.  Therefore
\[
 W=\{w\in B_Z:w_\gamma\in W_\gamma\text{ for every }\gamma\in F\}
\]
is a relatively weakly open neighborhood of $z$ in $B_Z$, because the finite
coordinate projections are weak-to-weak continuous.

Take $w\in W$.  For every $\gamma\in F$, choose
$u^{,j}_\gamma\in U_{j,\gamma}$ with
$w_\gamma=\sum_j\lambda_ju^{,j}_\gamma$.  Define
\[
 w^{,j}_\gamma=
 \begin{cases}
 u^{,j}_\gamma,&\gamma\in F,\\
 w_\gamma,&\gamma\notin F.
 \end{cases}
\]
Each $w^{,j}$ belongs to $B_Z$: every coordinate has norm at most one, and
$w^{,j}$ differs from the $c_0$ vector $w$ on only finitely many coordinates.
The finite-coordinate reduction gives $w^{,j}\in U_j$, and
$w=\sum_j\lambda_jw^{,j}$.  Hence
$W\subset\sum_j\lambda_jU_j$, proving weak stability of $B_Z$.

Conversely, suppose $B_Z$ is weakly stable and fix $\gamma_0\in\G$.  Let
$J:X_{\gamma_0}\to Z$ be the coordinate embedding and
$P:Z\to X_{\gamma_0}$ the coordinate projection.  Both have norm one and
$PJ=I$.  If $A_j\subset B_{X_{\gamma_0}}$ are relatively weakly open, then
\[
 \widetilde A_j=\{z\in B_Z:Pz\in A_j\}
\]
are relatively weakly open.  Given
$x=\sum_j\lambda_ja_j$ with $a_j\in A_j$, weak stability of $B_Z$ says that
$Jx$ is an interior point of $\sum_j\lambda_j\widetilde A_j$ in $B_Z$.
Intersect a witnessing neighborhood with $J(B_{X_{\gamma_0}})$ and pull it
back by $J$.  If $Jy=\sum_j\lambda_jz^j$ with
$z^j\in\widetilde A_j$, applying $P$ gives
$y=\sum_j\lambda_jPz^j$ with $Pz^j\in A_j$.  Thus $x$ is an interior point of
$\sum_j\lambda_jA_j$.  The coordinate unit ball is weakly stable, completing
the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

For Corollary~\ref{cor:discrete}, use
$C_0(K,X)=c_0(K,X)$ when $K$ is discrete.  On finite tensors
$\sum_{\gamma\in F}e_\gamma\otimes x_\gamma$, the injective tensor norm is
$\max_{\gamma\in F}\|x_\gamma\|$ (one inequality is immediate, and the other
uses a coordinate functional and a norming functional for $x_\gamma$).
Completion yields the stated isometry.

\section{Scope, comparison, and novelty}

Theorem~5.2 of Abrahamsen et al. proves a predecessor: $c_0(X_n)$ has CWO
when the fibers are finite-dimensional and satisfy the stronger norm-selection
property (co) \cite[Theorem~5.2]{Aetal}.  Their proof uses finite-dimensionality
to turn weak coordinate control into norm control and then invokes continuous
norm selections.  Lemma~\ref{lem:finite} keeps the coordinate conditions weak,
so the present proof removes finite-dimensionality and (co), permits arbitrary
fibers, and permits an arbitrary index set.

This does not settle the source question for a non-discrete scattered $K$.
At a non-isolated point, pointwise decompositions must assemble into
norm-continuous $X$-valued functions.  The source obtains that assembly from
norm stability and Michael selection.  Already for the one-point
compactification of a sequence, the missing step is the norm lower
semicontinuity of the inverse midpoint map, closely tied to the separate open
question whether weak stability implies norm stability.

A bounded novelty search on 11 August 2026 covered the four lightweight run
indexes and the two source papers.  Exact searches combined arbitrary
$c_0$-sums with weak stability and property CWO; further searches used the
vector-valued converse and injective-tensor question.  The search found the
cited finite-dimensional (co) theorem, but no arbitrary weakly
stable-coordinate version.  This supports, but cannot certify, novelty.
