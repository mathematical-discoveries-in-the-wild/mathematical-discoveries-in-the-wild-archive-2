\section{Original question}

Jameson Cahill and Peter G. Casazza,
\emph{The Paulsen Problem in Operator Theory}, arXiv:1102.2344,
published in \emph{Operators and Matrices} \textbf{7} (2013), 117--130,
poses Problem~1.1 on arXiv PDF page~1~\cite{source}.

\begin{question}
Find a function $h(\varepsilon,M,N)$ such that every
$\varepsilon$-nearly equal-norm, $\varepsilon$-nearly Parseval frame
$\{f_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ in an $M$-dimensional Hilbert space admits an
equal-norm Parseval frame $\{g_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ satisfying
\[
  \sum_{i=1}^{N}\lVert f_i-g_i\rVert^{2}
  \leq h(\varepsilon,M,N).
\]
\end{question}

The displayed source has a harmless index typo, printing $M$ rather than
$N$ on the family $\{g_i\}$; the sum and the frame cardinality make the
intended indexing clear.  Immediately after the problem, the authors call
it fundamental to decide whether $h$ can be independent of $N$.

\section{Separate later answer}

Linus Hamilton and Ankur Moitra,
\emph{The Paulsen Problem Made Simple}, arXiv:1809.04726, later published
in \emph{Israel Journal of Mathematics} \textbf{246} (2021), 299--313,
explicitly states in its abstract and introduction that the Paulsen problem
was resolved by Kwok--Lau--Lee--Ramachandran~\cite{kllr}, and gives a simpler
proof with a stronger estimate~\cite{answerpaper}.  Its bibliography cites
the Cahill--Casazza source paper by title.

On arXiv PDF page~3, Theorem~1 states:

\begin{answer}
For every $\varepsilon$-nearly equal-norm Parseval frame $V$ in
$\mathbb{R}^{d}$, there is an equal-norm Parseval frame $W$ such that
\[
  \operatorname{dist}^{2}(V,W)
  =\sum_i\lVert v_i-w_i\rVert_2^2
  \leq 20\varepsilon d^{2}.
\]
\end{answer}

Taking $d=M$ gives an admissible Paulsen function independent of the number
$N$ of frame vectors.  Thus it resolves the source's central quantitative
existence and $N$-independence question.  The relation is author-explicit,
not an agent-inferred reformulation.

\section*{Scope and search status}

This packet records the standard real finite-dimensional formulation proved
in the supporting paper.  It does not claim that the bound
$20\varepsilon d^{2}$ has optimal dependence on $d$: Hamilton--Moitra record
a lower bound of order $\varepsilon d$ and leave closing that gap open.  It
also does not identify an answer to the prescribed-norm Generalized Paulsen
Problem~5.3, Generalized Projection Problem~5.4, or the prescribed frame
operator Problem~5.7 in the source paper.

A bounded search on 11 August 2026 checked the run registry, the local arXiv
sources, the exact title and problem wording, and later Paulsen-problem papers.
The decisive match is Hamilton--Moitra's arXiv:1809.04726, whose abstract says
the problem was resolved and whose Theorem~1 supplies the explicit bound.
The earlier resolving paper arXiv:1710.02587 gives
$O(\varepsilon d^{13/2})$.  No new mathematical result is claimed here.
