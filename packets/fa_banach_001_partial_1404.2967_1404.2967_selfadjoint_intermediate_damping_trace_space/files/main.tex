\section{Source question}

Batty, Chill, and Srivastava study Besov maximal regularity for
\[
 u''+Bu'+Au=f,
 \qquad u(0)=u_0,\quad u'(0)=u_1,
\]
on a Banach space $X$ \cite{BCS}.  For $1\le p<\infty$,
$1\le q\le\infty$, and $0<\theta<1/p$, their trace space is
\begin{align*}
 (X,\Dom(B),\Dom(A))_{B^\theta_{pq}}
 :=\{(u(0),u'(0)):\;&u\in W^{2,p}(0,T;X),\\
 &u'',\ Bu',\ Au\in B^\theta_{pq}(0,T;X)\}.
\end{align*}
At the end of Section 5, Example 5.3, printed page 13, they write:

\begin{quote}
``The identification of the trace space
$(X,\Dom(B),\Dom(A))_{B^\theta_{pq}}$, even for particular choices of
$X$, $B$ and $A$, is left as an open problem.''
\end{quote}

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\caption{The open problem in the original source PDF, printed page 13.}
\end{figure}

The earlier version of this packet identified the critical self-adjoint case
$B=\alpha A^{1/2}$ for $p=q=2$.  The present upgrade classifies a much larger
Hilbert class and, in particular, every fractional damping
$B=\alpha A^\varepsilon$ with $0\le\varepsilon\le1$.

\section{Definitions}

Let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space, $T>0$, and $0<\theta<1/2$.  Let $A$ and
$B$ be densely defined closed operators on $H$, and assume throughout the
general theorem that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:embedding}
 \Dom(A)\hookrightarrow\Dom(B)
 \quad\text{continuously for their graph norms.}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{align*}
 \cE_\theta(0,T;A,B):=\{u\in W^{2,2}(0,T;H):
 u'',\ Bu',\ Au\in H^\theta(0,T;H)\}.
\end{align*}
Here $H^\theta=B^\theta_{22}$, with equivalent norms.  The trace range
\[
 \Tr\cE_\theta:=\{(u(0),u'(0)):u\in\cE_\theta(0,T;A,B)\}
\]
has the quotient norm induced by the graph norm of $\cE_\theta$.

For $\tau\in\R$, let $Q_\tau$ be the positive self-adjoint operator associated
with the closed quadratic form on $\Dom(A)$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formpencil}
 q_\tau[x]
 :=(\tau^4+\tau^2+1)\|x\|^2+\tau^2\|Bx\|^2+\|Ax\|^2.
\end{equation}
The embedding \eqref{eq:embedding} makes this a closed form with a fixed form
domain.  The fixed-domain form-resolvent identity and
$\|Bx\|\lesssim\|Ax\|+\|x\|$ show that $\tau\mapsto Q_\tau^{-1}$ is
norm-continuous.  Since $Q_\tau\ge(1+\tau^2+\tau^4)I$, the positive Bochner
operator integrals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Kj}
 K_j:=\int_{\R}
 \tau^{2j}(1+\tau^2)^{-\theta}Q_\tau^{-1}\,d\tau,
 \qquad j=0,1,
\end{equation}
converge in operator norm.  Both $K_j$ are bounded, positive, and injective.
For such a $K$, the operator range $\Ran K^{1/2}$ carries the norm
$\|x\|_{\Ran K^{1/2}}:=\|K^{-1/2}x\|$.

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[Exact Hilbert operator-range classification]\label{thm:range}
Under \eqref{eq:embedding},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rangeanswer}
 \boxed{\quad
 \Tr\cE_\theta
 =\Ran K_0^{1/2}\times\Ran K_1^{1/2}.
 \quad}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
 \|(u_0,u_1)\|_{\Tr\cE_\theta}^2
 \asympC
 \|K_0^{-1/2}u_0\|^2+\|K_1^{-1/2}u_1\|^2.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:Kj} is a complete trace-space identification for this Hilbert
class, without assuming that $A,B$ commute or are self-adjoint.
\end{theorem}

The formula becomes elementary spectral multiplier theory when $A$ and $B$
are strongly commuting nonnegative self-adjoint operators.  Write
$\alpha,\beta\ge0$ for their joint spectral variables and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scales}
 a=(1+\alpha^2)^{1/2},\qquad b=(1+\beta^2)^{1/2},\qquad
 S=(a+b^2)^{1/2},\qquad r=\frac aS.
\end{equation}
Then $0<r\le S$.  Define joint spectral multipliers $M_0,M_1$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multipliers}
 M_0^2=aS(1+r^2)^\theta,
 \qquad
 M_1^2=S(1+S^2)^\theta.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Closed formula for commuting self-adjoint pairs]\label{thm:spectral}
Suppose, in addition to \eqref{eq:embedding}, that $A,B$ are nonnegative
self-adjoint and strongly commute.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectralanswer}
 \boxed{\quad
 \Tr\cE_\theta=\Dom(M_0)\times\Dom(M_1),
 \quad}
\end{equation}
with equivalence between the quotient norm and the two graph norms.
\end{theorem}

The source paper's fractional damping family now has a complete exponent
diagram.

\begin{corollary}[All self-adjoint fractional dampings]\label{cor:phase}
Let $G$ be nonnegative self-adjoint on $H$, let $c>0$, and put
\[
 A=G,\qquad B=cG^\varepsilon,\qquad 0\le\varepsilon\le1.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phaseanswer}
 \Tr\cE_\theta
 =\Dom((I+G)^{\gamma_0})
 \times\Dom((I+G)^{\gamma_1}),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase}
 (\gamma_0,\gamma_1)=
 \begin{cases}
 \bigl(\frac34+\frac\theta2,\ \frac14+\frac\theta2\bigr),
   &0\le\varepsilon\le\frac12,\\[1mm]
 \bigl(\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}+\theta(1-\varepsilon),\
       \varepsilon(\frac12+\theta)\bigr),
   &\frac12\le\varepsilon\le1.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The formulas agree at $\varepsilon=1/2$.  In particular:
\begin{align*}
 \varepsilon=\tfrac12:&\quad
 \Dom((I+G)^{3/4+\theta/2})
 \times\Dom((I+G)^{1/4+\theta/2}),\\
 \varepsilon=1:&\quad
 \Dom(I+G)\times\Dom((I+G)^{1/2+\theta}).
\end{align*}
\end{corollary}

\section{Proof intuition}

The domain embedding turns the finite-interval graph space into an ordinary
intersection of three Hilbert-valued Bessel-potential spaces, so one common
time extension is available.  Fourier transformation in time produces the
positive form pencil $Q_\tau$.  The displacement and velocity evaluations
are bounded maps from this Fourier energy space to $H$.  Their mixed Gram
operator is zero by parity, while the two diagonal Gram operators are exactly
$K_0$ and $K_1$.  The Hilbert operator-range identity then proves
Theorem \ref{thm:range}, including surjectivity and the quotient norm.

For a commuting self-adjoint pair, the form pencil is scalar on every joint
spectral fiber.  Its fourth-order polynomial has a slow scale $r$ and a fast
scale $S$.  The displacement trace sees the slow scale; the velocity trace
sees the fast scale.  A two-scale integral lemma converts that observation
into the exact multipliers \eqref{eq:multipliers}.  Substituting
$B=cG^\varepsilon$ yields the two phase regimes in \eqref{eq:phase}.

\section{Proof of the operator-range theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Common whole-line extension]\label{lem:extension}
The graph space $\cE_\theta(0,T;A,B)$ is, with equivalent norms,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intersection}
 H^{2+\theta}(0,T;H)
 \cap H^{1+\theta}(0,T;\Dom(B))
 \cap H^\theta(0,T;\Dom(A)).
\end{equation}
One scalar time-extension operator maps this intersection boundedly into the
corresponding whole-line intersection.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $u\in\cE_\theta$, then $u''\in H^\theta$ and $u\in W^{2,2}$ imply
$u\in H^{2+\theta}(0,T;H)$.  Also $u\in H^\theta(0,T;\Dom(A))$.
By \eqref{eq:embedding}, $Bu\in H^\theta$; together with
$Bu'\in H^\theta$, this yields $Bu\in H^{1+\theta}$ and hence the middle
space in \eqref{eq:intersection}.  The converse follows immediately by
differentiation and the closed graph norms.

A finite reflection matching the function and its first derivative, followed
by a fixed cutoff, is bounded on $H^s(0,T;Y)$ for every Hilbert space $Y$ and
$0\le s<5/2$ in a fixed compact range.  The same scalar formula applies with
$Y=H,\Dom(B),\Dom(A)$, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathfrak X_\theta$ denote the resulting whole-line Hilbert space.  With
the unitary Fourier transform, its squared norm is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourierenergy}
 \|u\|_{\mathfrak X_\theta}^2
 =\int_{\R}(1+\tau^2)^\theta
 q_\tau[\widehat u(\tau)]\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
Fourier inversion defines the bounded trace map
\[
 T:\mathfrak X_\theta\longrightarrow H\times H,
 \qquad Tu=(u(0),u'(0)).
\]
The bounds follow by Cauchy--Schwarz in the form norm and
$Q_\tau^{-1}\le(1+\tau^2+\tau^4)^{-1}I$.

Relative to the inner product in \eqref{eq:fourierenergy}, the adjoints of
the two coordinate maps are, up to the unitary Fourier constant,
\begin{align*}
 (T_0^*h)(\tau)&=(1+\tau^2)^{-\theta}Q_\tau^{-1}h,\\
 (T_1^*h)(\tau)&=-i\tau(1+\tau^2)^{-\theta}Q_\tau^{-1}h.
\end{align*}
Since $Q_{-\tau}=Q_\tau$, the mixed Gram operators vanish, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gram}
 TT^*=(2\pi)^{-1}
 \begin{pmatrix}K_0&0\\0&K_1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For a bounded operator between Hilbert spaces,
$\Ran T=\Ran((TT^*)^{1/2})$, with equality of the natural quotient/operator-
range norms; this is the standard Hilbert range identity behind Douglas'
range theorem \cite{Douglas}.  Equation \eqref{eq:gram} proves the whole-line
version of \eqref{eq:rangeanswer}.  Lemma \ref{lem:extension} and restriction
transfer both inclusions to $(0,T)$.  Since restriction is bounded and the
common extension is a bounded right inverse to restriction, their induced
quotient norms are equivalent.  This proves Theorem \ref{thm:range}.

\section{The two-scale spectral calculation}

\begin{lemma}[Weighted two-scale integrals]\label{lem:twoscale}
Let $0<\theta<1/2$ and $0<r\le S$.  Then, uniformly in $r,S$,
\begin{align}
 \int_{\R}\frac{(1+\tau^2)^{-\theta}}
 {(\tau^2+r^2)(\tau^2+S^2)}\,d\tau
 &\asympC\frac1{rS^2(1+r^2)^\theta},\label{eq:J0}\\
 \int_{\R}\frac{\tau^2(1+\tau^2)^{-\theta}}
 {(\tau^2+r^2)(\tau^2+S^2)}\,d\tau
 &\asympC\frac1{S(1+S^2)^\theta}.\label{eq:J1}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $\delta=r/S\le1$.  After $\tau=rs$, the first integral equals
\[
 \frac1{rS^2}\int_{\R}
 \frac{(1+r^2s^2)^{-\theta}}
 {(1+s^2)(1+\delta^2s^2)}\,ds.
\]
Dropping the last denominator gives an upper bound.  If $r\le1$, the remaining
integral is uniformly bounded.  If $r\ge1$, split at $|s|=1/r$: the inner
piece has length $O(r^{-1})=O(r^{-2\theta})$, and on the outer piece use
$(1+r^2s^2)^{-\theta}\le r^{-2\theta}|s|^{-2\theta}$.  The latter majorant is
integrable because $\theta<1/2$.  A lower bound of order
$(1+r^2)^{-\theta}$ follows by restricting to $1/2\le|s|\le1$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:J0}.

After $\tau=Ss$, the second integral is
\[
 \frac1S\int_{\R}
 \frac{s^2(1+S^2s^2)^{-\theta}}
 {(s^2+\delta^2)(s^2+1)}\,ds.
\]
For the upper bound use $s^2/(s^2+\delta^2)\le1$ and repeat the preceding
split with $S$.  Restriction to $1\le|s|\le2$ gives the matching lower bound.
This proves \eqref{eq:J1}.
\end{proof}

On a joint spectral fiber, \eqref{eq:formpencil} is
\[
 \tau^4+b^2\tau^2+a^2.
\]
With $S,r$ from \eqref{eq:scales},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factorcompare}
 \tau^4+b^2\tau^2+a^2
 \asympC(\tau^2+r^2)(\tau^2+S^2).
\end{equation}
Indeed, $a\tau^2\le(\tau^4+a^2)/2$, so adding $a\tau^2$ changes the
polynomial by only a universal factor; moreover
$S^2+r^2$ is between $S^2$ and $2S^2$ and $r^2S^2=a^2$.

Lemma \ref{lem:twoscale} says that the two scalar values of $K_0,K_1$ are
comparable to the reciprocals of $M_0^2,M_1^2$ in
\eqref{eq:multipliers}.  Joint spectral calculus therefore turns
Theorem \ref{thm:range} into \eqref{eq:spectralanswer}, proving
Theorem \ref{thm:spectral}.

For Corollary \ref{cor:phase}, let $\lambda$ be the spectral variable of $G$.
Up to constants depending only on $c$,
$a\asympC1+\lambda$ and $b\asympC(1+\lambda)^\varepsilon$.  If
$\varepsilon\le1/2$, then
$S\asympC a^{1/2}$ and $r\asympC a^{1/2}$.  If
$\varepsilon\ge1/2$, then
$S\asympC a^\varepsilon$ and $r\asympC a^{1-\varepsilon}$.
Substitution in \eqref{eq:multipliers}, followed by taking square roots,
gives exactly \eqref{eq:phase}.  This proves the corollary.

\section{Why the unrestricted problem is not claimed solved}

The embedding \eqref{eq:embedding} is used only at the finite-interval
extension step, but it is substantive.  Without it, cutting off a reflected
extension differentiates the cutoff and produces $Bu$, whereas the source
space assumes only $Bu'$.  A diagonal example shows that a decaying
whole-line formula cannot simply be imposed.

Take $H=\ell_2$, let $A e_n=n e_n$, and let $B e_n=n^3 e_n$.  On a fixed
finite interval, the constant path $u(t)=u_0$ shows that every
$u_0\in\Dom(A)$ is an admissible displacement trace with zero velocity,
independently of $B$.  By contrast, the decaying whole-line two-scale weight
would impose a displacement weight of order $n^4$ in the squared norm, i.e.
a strictly smaller domain.  Thus the unrestricted finite-interval problem
retains joint-domain geometry that the present whole-line pencil does not
encode.  This packet does not claim that no other general description is
possible; it explains why the proved formula is scoped by
\eqref{eq:embedding}.
