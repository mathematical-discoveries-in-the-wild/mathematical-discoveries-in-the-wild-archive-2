\section*{Status}

This packet records a candidate partial result, likely valid, toward the
conjectural extension stated after Theorem~1 of Naor and Sheffield
\cite{NS}.  The source proves existence and uniqueness of tree-valued
absolutely minimal Lipschitz extensions when the domain is locally compact
and the target tree comes from a finite graph.  It conjectures that local
compactness can be removed and that the target may instead be a bounded
$\R$-tree.  We remove the finiteness restriction on the target for compact
domains, and prove existence.  We do not prove uniqueness for the enlarged
target class or remove local compactness of the domain.

\section*{Question and Answered Part}

The source defines a metric tree as the one-dimensional simplicial complex
of a finite graph-theoretical tree, with arbitrary positive edge lengths.  It
then writes:

\begin{quote}
We conjecture that the assumption that $X$ is locally compact can be dropped,
and that $T$ need not correspond to a finite graph-theoretical tree, but
rather can belong to the more general class of bounded $\R$-trees.
\end{quote}

We establish the target-tree extension when $X$ is compact.  In fact the
ambient target need not be bounded: compactness of the boundary data confines
the construction to a compact subtree.

\section*{Definitions}

Let $Y$ be closed in a metric space $X$ and let $f:Y\to T$ be Lipschitz.  A
Lipschitz extension $u:X\to T$ is an \emph{absolutely minimal Lipschitz
extension} (AMLE) if, for every open $U\subseteq X\setminus Y$ and every
Lipschitz $h:X\to T$ agreeing with $u$ on $X\setminus U$,
\[
 \Lip_U(h)\geq \Lip_U(u).
\]

For a map $u:X\to T$, say that $u$ satisfies \emph{$T$-comparison} on
$X\setminus Y$ when, for every $t\in T$, the real-valued function
$x\mapsto d_T(t,u(x))$ satisfies comparison with distance functions from
above in the sense of \cite[Section~2]{NS}.  Proposition~7 of \cite{NS}
shows that $T$-comparison implies the AMLE property.  Importantly, the source
notes that this implication works for an arbitrary metric target; the finite
tree structure is needed only for the converse direction.

\section*{Proof Intuition}

The finite-tree theorem can be used as an approximation engine.  The boundary
image is compact, and in a complete $\R$-tree its closed convex hull is still
compact.  Approximate that hull from inside by finite subtrees and project the
boundary values onto them.  The source theorem produces AMLEs for all the
finite problems.  These maps are equi-Lipschitz and live in one compact tree,
so a subsequence converges uniformly.

The key point is that the property used to recognize the limit is stable.
If $K$ is a finite approximating subtree and $p(t)$ is the gate projection of
$t$ onto $K$, then for $z\in K$,
\[
 d(t,z)=d(t,p(t))+d(p(t),z).
\]
Thus comparison for target points in $K$ automatically gives comparison for
every target point in the full compact hull.  Uniform limits preserve these
scalar comparison inequalities, and comparison implies absolute minimality.

\section*{Compact Convex Hulls in $\R$-Trees}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hull}
Let $T$ be a complete $\R$-tree and let $A\subseteq T$ be compact.  Then the
closed convex hull
\[
 C=\overline{\conv}(A)
\]
is a compact $\R$-tree.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and choose a finite $\varepsilon$-net
$F\subseteq A$.  In an $\R$-tree the convex hull of a set is the union of
the geodesic segments joining pairs of its points.  The set $\conv(F)$ is a
finite union of compact segments and is therefore compact.

Let $z\in\conv(A)$, so that $z\in[a,b]$ for some $a,b\in A$.  Choose
$a',b'\in F$ with $d(a,a')<\varepsilon$ and
$d(b,b')<\varepsilon$.  Parameterize $[a,b]$ and $[a',b']$ proportionally
and let $z'\in[a',b']$ have the same parameter as $z$.  Geodesics in an
$\R$-tree are Busemann convex, hence
\[
 d(z,z')\leq (1-s)d(a,a')+s d(b,b')<\varepsilon
\]
for the relevant $s\in[0,1]$.  Thus $\conv(A)$ lies in the
$\varepsilon$-neighborhood of the compact set $\conv(F)$.  It follows that
$\conv(A)$ is totally bounded.  Its closure $C$ is complete, being closed in
the complete space $T$, and hence is compact.  A closed convex subset of an
$\R$-tree is again an $\R$-tree.
\end{proof}

\section*{The Compact-Domain Extension Theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $X$ be a compact length space, let $\varnothing\neq Y\subseteq X$ be
closed, let $T$ be an arbitrary complete $\R$-tree, and let
$f:Y\to T$ be Lipschitz.  Then $f$ admits an AMLE $u:X\to T$.
Moreover, writing
\[
 C=\overline{\conv}(f(Y)),
\]
one may choose $u(X)\subseteq C$ and
\[
 \Lip_X(u)=\Lip_Y(f).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $L=\Lip_Y(f)$.  Because $Y$ is compact, $f(Y)$ is compact; hence
$C$ is compact by Lemma~\ref{lem:hull}.

Choose increasing finite sets $F_n\subseteq C$ such that $F_n$ is a
$1/n$-net in $C$, and set
\[
 K_n=\conv(F_n).
\]
Each $K_n$ is the realization of a finite graph-theoretical tree.  Let
$p_n:C\to K_n$ be the nearest-point, or gate, projection.  Closed convex
subtrees of an $\R$-tree admit a $1$-Lipschitz gate projection, and the net
property gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection}
 \sup_{z\in C} d(z,p_n(z))\leq \frac1n.
\end{equation}
Define $f_n=p_n\circ f:Y\to K_n$.  Then
$\Lip_Y(f_n)\leq L$, and $f_n\to f$ uniformly.

Theorem~1 of \cite{NS}, applied to the compact length space $X$ and finite
metric tree $K_n$, supplies an AMLE $u_n:X\to K_n$ of $f_n$.  These maps
may be taken with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lipbound}
 \Lip_X(u_n)=\Lip_Y(f_n)\leq L.
\end{equation}
For completeness, the upper bound also follows from absolute minimality:
the injectivity of a metric tree gives an $L$-Lipschitz extension competitor;
apply absolute minimality on $X\setminus Y$, and then use the length-space
property and continuity to control pairs with one endpoint in $Y$.

We next regard every $u_n$ as a map into the fixed compact tree $C$.  We
claim that $u_n$ satisfies $C$-comparison, not merely $K_n$-comparison.
Fix $t\in C$ and put $q=p_n(t)$.  For every $z\in K_n$, the gate property
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gate}
 d(t,z)=d(t,q)+d(q,z).
\end{equation}
By Proposition~7 of \cite{NS}, the scalar function
$x\mapsto d(q,u_n(x))$ satisfies comparison with distance functions from
above.  Adding the constant $d(t,q)$ preserves comparison, so
\eqref{eq:gate} shows that $x\mapsto d(t,u_n(x))$ also satisfies comparison.
Since $t\in C$ was arbitrary, the claim follows.

The family $(u_n)$ is equi-Lipschitz by \eqref{eq:lipbound}, and it consists
of maps from compact $X$ into compact $C$.  The Arzel\`a--Ascoli theorem
\cite[Theorem~6.1]{Munkres}
therefore gives a uniformly convergent subsequence; write
$u_n\to u:X\to C$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:projection} implies
$u|_Y=f$, and uniform convergence preserves the Lipschitz bound
$\Lip_X(u)\leq L$.  The reverse inequality follows by restriction to $Y$.

We verify $C$-comparison.  Fix $t\in C$.  The real-valued functions
\[
 g_n(x)=d(t,u_n(x))
 \quad\hbox{converge uniformly to}\quad
 g(x)=d(t,u(x)).
\]
Comparison with distance functions from above is closed under uniform
limits.  Indeed, if $g$ is bounded above by a distance cone on the boundary
of an open set, then for every $\delta>0$ and all sufficiently large $n$,
$g_n$ is bounded there by the cone plus $\delta$.  Comparison for $g_n$
propagates that bound to the open set; let $n\to\infty$ and then
$\delta\downarrow0$.  Hence $u$ satisfies $C$-comparison.  The
comparison-implies-AMLE direction of Proposition~7 in \cite{NS} now shows
that $u$ is a $C$-valued AMLE of $f$.

Finally, $C$ is a closed convex subtree of $T$, so its gate projection
$P_C:T\to C$ is $1$-Lipschitz.  If a $T$-valued competitor $h$ agreed with
$u$ off an open set and had smaller Lipschitz constant there, then
$P_C\circ h$ would be a $C$-valued competitor with no larger Lipschitz
constant.  This contradicts absolute minimality in $C$.  Thus $u$ is also
an AMLE with ambient target $T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
If $Y=\varnothing$, any constant map is an AMLE after choosing a point of
$T$.  The nonempty assumption only avoids this harmless choice.
\end{remark}

\section*{What Remains Open}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} proves existence for the target-tree extension of the
source conjecture when the domain is compact.  It is stronger on the target
side than the stated bounded-$\R$-tree conjecture, because $T$ itself may be
unbounded.  It does not prove:
\begin{itemize}
\item existence for arbitrary length spaces after local compactness is
removed;
\item existence for locally compact noncompact domains when the boundary
range has noncompact convex hull in a bounded nonproper $\R$-tree;
\item uniqueness of AMLEs for arbitrary $\R$-tree targets.
\end{itemize}
The last point is kept explicit because the source's proof that AMLE implies
tree comparison is written for finite metric trees.  Our proof only uses the
reverse implication, whose target generality the source explicitly records.
