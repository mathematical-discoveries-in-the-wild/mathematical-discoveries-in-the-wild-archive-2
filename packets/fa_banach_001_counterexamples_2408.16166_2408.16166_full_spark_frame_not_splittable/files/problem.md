# Problem statement

Let `F={f_1,...,f_n}` span `R^d` and define the atomic norm

```text
||z||_F = min { ||q||_1 : Fq=z }.
```

Let `Sigma_{F,s}` be the union of the spans generated by at most `s` frame
vectors, and choose a best `s`-term approximant

```text
z_s in argmin { ||z-v||_F : v in Sigma_{F,s} }.
```

The frame is `s`-splittable with constant `beta>0` if, for every `x,y`,

```text
||x+y||_F >= ||x_s||_F - ||y_s||_F
             + beta ( ||y-y_s||_F - ||x-x_s||_F ).
```

Chen, Kummerle, and Wang (arXiv:2408.16166, Definition 3.5 and the paragraph
following it) conjecture that every full-spark frame is splittable for a
sufficiently small positive `beta`.

The packet gives a negative answer: there is a unit-norm full-spark frame of
three vectors in `R^2` which is not 1-splittable for any positive `beta`.
The witness points have unique best one-term approximants, eliminating any
choice-of-minimizer ambiguity.
