\fbox{\parbox{0.91\textwidth}{
\textbf{Status: full counterexample to the direct density analogue.}
There is a normalized faithful cocycle action of the finite discrete quantum
group dual to $S_3$ on $M_2(\mathbb C)$ for which
\[
 \overline{\spn}\,\alpha(\B)(\A\otimes1)\ne\A\otimes\B.
\]
Thus the density condition equivalent to faithfulness for ordinary actions
in Lemma~2.2 of the source is not equivalent to faithfulness for cocycle
actions.  An exact stabilizer-twisted replacement is also proved below.
}}
\end{center}

\section{The source question and precise scope}

Adam and Zacharias~\cite{source}, immediately after Definition~5.1 on arXiv
PDF pages 15--16, state the counit characterization of faithfulness for a
cocycle action $(\alpha,U)$ and ask whether it is equivalent to ``some
natural density conditions on the image of $\alpha$,'' referring to their
ordinary-action Lemma~2.2.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/source_question.png}
  \caption{The question at the top of source PDF page~16.}
\end{figure}

The displayed formula (5.3) in the published paper is typographically
incomplete.  This causes no ambiguity here: our example is normalized in the
strongest standard sense,
\[
 (\varepsilon\otimes\id_{\B})\alpha=\id_{\B},
 \qquad (\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon\otimes\id_{\B})(U)=1.
\]
The phrase ``some natural density conditions'' is not itself a formal
yes--no proposition.  The theorem below settles the canonical concrete
interpretation suggested by Lemma~2.2: the same Podle\'s density equality is
\emph{false}.  We do not claim to exclude every condition that might be
described informally as natural.

\section{Result}

Let $\A=C^*(S_3)$, equipped with
$\Delta(\lambda_g)=\lambda_g\otimes\lambda_g$ and
$\varepsilon(\lambda_g)=1$.  As a finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebra,
\[
 \A\cong\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C\oplus M_2(\mathbb C),
\]
where the counit is the trivial-representation scalar block.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:counterexample}
For $\B=M_2(\mathbb C)$ there is a unitary
$U\in\A\otimes\A\otimes\B$ and a unital faithful $*$-homomorphism
$\alpha:\B\to\A\otimes\B$ satisfying
\begin{align*}
 (\id_{\A}\otimes\alpha)\alpha(b)
   &=U(\Delta\otimes\id_{\B})\alpha(b)U^*,\\
 (\id_{\A}\otimes\id_{\A}\otimes\alpha)(U)
   (\Delta\otimes\id_{\A}\otimes\id_{\B})(U)
   &=(1\otimes U)(\id_{\A}\otimes\Delta\otimes\id_{\B})(U),
\end{align*}
and the normalization identities above, but
\[
 \overline{\spn}\,\alpha(\B)(\A\otimes1)
 \ne \A\otimes\B.
\]
In fact, on the $M_2$ summand of $\A$, the left-hand side has block exactly
$M_2\otimes\mathbb C1$ rather than $M_2\otimes M_2$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Idea of the proof}

The ordinary density condition remembers a distinguished first tensor leg.
Exterior perturbation by a unitary preserves faithfulness and turns an
ordinary action into a cocycle action, but it need not preserve that tensor
leg.  On the two-dimensional irreducible block of $C^*(S_3)$, choose the
tensor flip.  It rotates the trivial action $b\mapsto1\otimes b$ into
$b\mapsto b\otimes1$.  The latter map remains faithful, while multiplication
on the right by the first-leg algebra can never create a non-scalar second
leg.  The cocycle is the explicit coboundary of the flip.

\section{Exterior perturbation lemma}

We record the calculation so that no cocycle-existence assertion is hidden.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:gauge}
Let $(\A,\Delta)$ be a $C^*$-bialgebra and let
$\beta:\B\to M(\A\otimes\B)$ be an ordinary action.  For a unitary
$X\in M(\A\otimes\B)$ put
\begin{align}
 \alpha(b)&=X^*\beta(b)X,\label{eq:alpha}\\
 U&=X_{23}^*(\id_{\A}\otimes\beta)(X^*)
        (\Delta\otimes\id_{\B})(X).
 \label{eq:U}
\end{align}
Then $(\alpha,U)$ satisfies both cocycle-action equations.  If $\A$ has a
counit, $\beta$ is counital, and
$(\varepsilon\otimes\id_{\B})(X)=1$, then
$(\varepsilon\otimes\id_{\B})\alpha=\id_{\B}$ and
$(\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon\otimes\id_{\B})(U)=1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Coassociativity of $\beta$ gives
$(\id\otimes\beta)\beta=(\Delta\otimes\id)\beta$.  Using
\[
 (\id\otimes\alpha)(X^*)
  =X_{23}^*(\id\otimes\beta)(X^*)X_{23},
\]
one obtains
\begin{align*}
 (\id\otimes\alpha)\alpha(b)
  =X_{23}^*(\id\otimes\beta)(X^*)
   (\Delta\otimes\id)\beta(b)
   (\id\otimes\beta)(X)X_{23}.
\end{align*}
Inserting \eqref{eq:U} and
$(\Delta\otimes\id)\alpha(b)
=(\Delta\otimes\id)(X^*)(\Delta\otimes\id)\beta(b)
(\Delta\otimes\id)(X)$ gives the same expression, proving the first
cocycle equation.

For completeness, write $q=\id_{\A}\otimes\beta$ and
$d=\Delta\otimes\id_{\B}$.  Expanding the left side of the second cocycle
equation from \eqref{eq:U}, cancelling adjacent unitary factors, and using
$q\beta=d\beta$ reduces it to
\[
 X_{34}^*q(X^*)_{234}
 (\id\otimes\Delta\otimes\id)q(X^*)
 (\Delta\otimes\id\otimes\id)d(X).
\]
The right side reduces to the same expression, with its last factor initially
$(\id\otimes\Delta\otimes\id)d(X)$; these agree by coassociativity of
$\Delta$.  This proves the cocycle identity.  The final two assertions follow
by applying the counit to \eqref{eq:alpha} and \eqref{eq:U}.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the counterexample}

Take the trivial ordinary action
\[
 \beta(b)=1_{\A}\otimes b \qquad(b\in\B).
\]
Let $p$ be the central projection of the $M_2$ summand of $\A$, identify
$p\A\otimes\B$ with $M_2\otimes M_2$, and let
$F(\xi\otimes\eta)=\eta\otimes\xi$ be the tensor-flip unitary.  Define
$X\in\A\otimes\B$ blockwise to be $1$ on both scalar summands and $F$ on
the $M_2$ summand.  In particular,
$(\varepsilon\otimes\id)(X)=1$.

Define $\alpha$ and $U$ by \eqref{eq:alpha}--\eqref{eq:U}.  Since $\beta$
is an action, Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge} proves both cocycle identities and both
normalization identities.  The map $\alpha$ is faithful because it is a
unitary conjugate of the faithful map $\beta$.

On the distinguished block,
\[
 p\alpha(b)=F^*(1_{M_2}\otimes b)F=b\otimes1_{M_2}.
\]
Consequently, if
$\mathcal D=\overline{\spn}\,\alpha(\B)(\A\otimes1)$, then
\[
 p\mathcal D
 =\spn\{(b\otimes1)(a\otimes1):a,b\in M_2\}
 =M_2\otimes\mathbb C1,
\]
which is a proper subspace of
$p(\A\otimes\B)=M_2\otimes M_2$.  This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:counterexample}.

\section{Upgrade: the exact stabilizer-twisted density condition}

The example also identifies the missing term in the density condition.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:corrected}
Let $(\alpha,U)$ be a cocycle action of a discrete quantum group on $\B$,
and suppose a unitary $X\in M(\A\otimes\B)$ stabilizes it in the sense that
\[
 \beta(b)=X\alpha(b)X^*
\]
is an ordinary action.  Then
\[
 \alpha\text{ is faithful}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \overline{\spn}\{\alpha(b)X^*(a\otimes1):
                   a\in\A,\ b\in\B\}=\A\otimes\B.
 \tag{$\dagger$}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Unitary conjugation shows that $\alpha$ is faithful exactly when $\beta$ is.
The ordinary-action Lemma~2.2 of the source says this is equivalent to
\[
 \overline{\spn}\,X\alpha(\B)X^*(\A\otimes1)=\A\otimes\B.
\]
Left multiplication by $X^*$ is a bijective isometry of $\A\otimes\B$ and
turns the last display exactly into $(\dagger)$.
\end{proof}

For the flip example, $(\dagger)$ does hold: on the $M_2$ block,
\[
 (b\otimes1)F(a\otimes1)=F(a\otimes b),
\]
whose span is all of $M_2\otimes M_2$.  Thus the failure of the untwisted
condition is not an accident; it is exactly repaired by the stabilizer.
Whenever $X$ preserves the first-leg module (for example under an appropriate
normalizer hypothesis), $(\dagger)$ reduces to the usual condition.

\section{Verification and novelty boundary}

All identities are finite-dimensional, so there are no strict-topology or
one-legged-multiplier issues.  The decisive checks are: the exterior
perturbation formula, counitality of $X$, the flip identity, and the strict
dimension drop
$\dim(M_2\otimes\mathbb C1)=4<16=\dim(M_2\otimes M_2)$.

The run's registry, solution, attempt, and proof-gap indexes were searched by
arXiv id, title, and the terms ``cocycle action,'' ``density condition,''
``Podle\'s,'' ``faithful,'' and ``exterior equivalence.''  Targeted searches
of later literature located no paper resolving the quoted question or this
finite-dimensional construction.  The new claim is deliberately limited to
the canonical Lemma~2.2 analogue and Proposition~\ref{prop:corrected}; the
source's broader informal wording is not overinterpreted.
