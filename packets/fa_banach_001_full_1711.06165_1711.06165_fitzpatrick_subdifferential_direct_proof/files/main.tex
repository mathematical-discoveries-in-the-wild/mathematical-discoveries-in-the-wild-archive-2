\section{Source problem and scope}

Let $E$ be a real Banach space, with canonical embedding
$x\mapsto\widehat x$ into $E^{**}$.  For a multifunction
$S:E\rightrightarrows E^*$, its Fitzpatrick function is
\[
 \varphi_S(x,x^*)
 :=\sup_{(s,s^*)\in\gra S}
 \bigl(\pair{s}{x^*}+\pair{x}{s^*}-\pair{s}{s^*}\bigr).
\]
When $S$ is closed, monotone, and quasidense, its Fitzpatrick extension
$S^{\mathbb F}:E^*\rightrightarrows E^{**}$ is defined by
\[
 (y^*,y^{**})\in\gra S^{\mathbb F}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \varphi_S^*(y^*,y^{**})=\pair{y^*}{y^{**}}.
\]

Simons's Theorem 4.8 states that, for $f\in\PCLSC(E)$,
\[
                 (\partial f)^{\mathbb F}=\partial(f^*),
\]
and Problem 4.10 asks for a simple direct proof.  Here $\partial(f^*)$ is
taken on $E^*$, so its values lie in $E^{**}$.  The contribution below is a
new proof of the already-known statement, not a new theorem statement.

\section{Proof intuition}

The separable Fenchel--Young function
\[
             H(x,x^*):=f(x)+f^*(x^*)
\]
majorizes the Fitzpatrick function of $\partial f$.  Conjugating reverses
this order and turns $H$ into the equally simple function
$H^*(y^*,y^{**})=f^*(y^*)+f^{**}(y^{**})$.  At a point of the Fitzpatrick
extension, the outside terms of the resulting inequality chain coincide.
Consequently equality holds in Fenchel--Young for $f^*$, which is precisely
membership in $\partial f^*$.  This yields one graph inclusion.  Maximality of
the Fitzpatrick extension supplies the reverse inclusion for free.

\section{Direct proof}

\begin{lemma}[The sandwich]
For $f\in\PCLSC(E)$ and $S=\partial f$,
\[
 \varphi_S(x,x^*)\le f(x)+f^*(x^*)
 \qquad ((x,x^*)\in E\times E^*).
\]
Consequently, for every $(y^*,y^{**})\in E^*\times E^{**}$,
\[
 \varphi_S^*(y^*,y^{**})
 \ge f^*(y^*)+f^{**}(y^{**})
 \ge \pair{y^*}{y^{**}}.                       \tag{1}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $(s,s^*)\in\gra\partial f$, Fenchel--Young equality says
$f(s)+f^*(s^*)=\pair{s}{s^*}$.  Applying Fenchel--Young to the two crossed
pairs gives
\begin{align*}
 \pair{s}{x^*}+\pair{x}{s^*}-\pair{s}{s^*}
 &\le f(s)+f^*(x^*)+f(x)+f^*(s^*)-\pair{s}{s^*}\\
 &= f(x)+f^*(x^*).
\end{align*}
Taking the supremum over $\gra\partial f$ proves the first inequality.
Convex conjugation reverses pointwise order, while separation of the two
variables gives
\[
 H^*(y^*,y^{**})=f^*(y^*)+f^{**}(y^{**}).
\]
The first inequality in (1) follows, and the second is Fenchel--Young for
$f^*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Direct answer to Problem 4.10]
If $f\in\PCLSC(E)$, then
\[
                    (\partial f)^{\mathbb F}=\partial(f^*).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $S=\partial f$.  If $(y^*,y^{**})\in\gra S^{\mathbb F}$, then by
definition and (1),
\[
 \pair{y^*}{y^{**}}
 =\varphi_S^*(y^*,y^{**})
 \ge f^*(y^*)+f^{**}(y^{**})
 \ge\pair{y^*}{y^{**}}.
\]
Thus equality holds in Fenchel--Young for $f^*$, equivalently
$y^{**}\in\partial f^*(y^*)$.  Hence
\[
                    S^{\mathbb F}\subseteq\partial f^*.       \tag{2}
\]

By Theorem 4.3 of the source survey, $S=\partial f$ is closed, monotone,
and quasidense.  Theorem 4.6 of the survey therefore says that
$S^{\mathbb F}$ is maximally monotone.  The subdifferential $\partial f^*$ is
monotone.  The monotone extension (2) of a maximally monotone graph cannot be
proper, so equality follows.
\end{proof}

\section{Checks, limitations, and novelty}

The proof does not assume that $E$ is reflexive.  It also avoids any
attainment assumption: if a point belongs to $\gra S^{\mathbb F}$, the
displayed equality makes every relevant extended-real quantity finite.
The identity $H^*=f^*\oplus f^{**}$ uses the standard dual pairing between
$E\times E^*$ and $E^*\times E^{**}$.

There is no circularity in invoking maximality.  Theorem 4.6 is a general
result about the Fitzpatrick extension of every closed, monotone, quasidense
operator.  The proof cited in Simons's earlier paper, arXiv:1407.1100,
Section 11 (the theorem labelled \texttt{AFMAXthm}), proceeds through marker
functions and the intrinsic characterization of the extension; it does not
use the subdifferential identity proved here.

A bounded search using the exact problem wording, the displayed identity,
and combinations of ``Fitzpatrick extension'' with ``subdifferential'' found
the source, arXiv:1407.1100, and the later arXiv:1907.07278, but not this
sandwich-plus-maximality proof.  This supports only moderate novelty
confidence pending a specialist literature review.  Human review should also
decide whether ``direct'' is intended to allow the two general theorems stated
immediately before the problem.
