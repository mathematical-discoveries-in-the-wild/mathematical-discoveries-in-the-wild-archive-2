\begin{abstract}
We give a negative answer to the final open problem of arXiv:2103.10289.
There is an enriched almost contraction \(T:\R\to\R\) for which no
\(T\)-invariant partition can have enriched-contractive restrictions; the
same example also rules out nonexpansive restrictions.  The construction is
explicit: start from a two-valued almost contraction \(S\), then undo a
Krasnosel'ski\u\i{} average by setting \(T=3S-2I\).  A three-point orbit
segment gives an immediate parameter-free contradiction on its forced
partition cell.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definitions and interpretation}

A self-map \(A\) of a normed space is a \((\delta,L)\)-almost contraction if
\[
 \|Ax-Ay\|\le \delta\|x-y\|+L\|y-Ax\|,
 \qquad 0<\delta<1,\qquad L\ge0.                      \tag{1}
\]
A map \(T\) is an enriched \((b,\theta,L)\)-almost contraction if
\[
 \|b(x-y)+Tx-Ty\|
 \le \theta\|x-y\|+L\|b(x-y)+Tx-y\|,
 \quad b\ge0,\qquad 0<\theta<b+1.                    \tag{2}
\]
It is a \((k,\eta)\)-enriched contraction if
\[
 \|k(x-y)+Tx-Ty\|\le\eta\|x-y\|,
 \qquad k\ge0,\qquad 0\le\eta<k+1.                  \tag{3}
\]

The open problem asks for a partition \(X=\bigcup_iX_i\) such that each
restriction \(T|_{X_i}\) is an enriched contraction (or a nonexpansive map).
As in the motivating Picard-operator partitions, ``restriction'' must mean a
self-map: \(T(X_i)\subseteq X_i\).  Without this invariance, the partition
into singletons makes every two-point inequality vacuous, so the question
would have a trivial affirmative answer unrelated to its motivation.

\section{Construction}

Define \(S:\R\to\R\) by
\[
 Sx=\begin{cases}
 -1,&x<0,\\
  1,&x\ge0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad\text{and set}\qquad
 Tx=3Sx-2x.                                             \tag{4}
\]

\begin{lemma}
The step map \(S\) is a \((1/2,2)\)-almost contraction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(x,y\) lie on the same side of zero, then \(Sx=Sy\) and (1) is
immediate.  If \(x<0\le y\), then
\[
 |Sx-Sy|=2,\qquad
 \tfrac12|x-y|+2|y-Sx|
 =\tfrac12(y-x)+2(y+1)\ge2.
\]
If \(y<0\le x\), the same calculation gives
\[
 \tfrac12|x-y|+2|y-Sx|
 =\tfrac12(x-y)+2(1-y)\ge2.
\]
Thus (1) holds for every ordered pair \((x,y)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
The map \(T\) in (4) is an enriched \((2,3/2,2)\)-almost contraction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The identities
\[
 2(x-y)+Tx-Ty=3(Sx-Sy)
\]
and
\[
 2(x-y)+Tx-y=3(Sx-y)
\]
show that (2), with \(b=2\), \(\theta=3/2\), and \(L=2\), is exactly three
times the \((1/2,2)\)-almost-contraction inequality for \(S\).  Notice that
\(3/2<3=b+1\), as required.  Equivalently, the Krasnosel'ski\u\i{} average
\((2I+T)/3\) is precisely \(S\).
\end{proof}

\section{No admissible invariant partition}

\begin{theorem}
The enriched almost contraction \(T\) from (4) admits neither
\begin{enumerate}
\item a \(T\)-invariant partition into enriched-contractive restrictions, nor
\item a \(T\)-invariant partition into nonexpansive restrictions.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, the final open problem of arXiv:2103.10289 has a negative
answer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The following orbit segment is exact:
\[
 u:=\frac85
 \quad\longmapsto\quad
 v:=Tu=-\frac15
 \quad\longmapsto\quad
 Tv=-\frac{13}{5}.                                     \tag{5}
\]
Suppose \(\{X_i\}\) were a \(T\)-invariant partition.  The point \(u\)
belongs to one cell, say \(X_i\), and invariance forces \(v=Tu\in X_i\).
For this pair,
\[
 |u-v|=\frac95,\qquad |Tu-Tv|=\frac{12}{5}.             \tag{6}
\]
Equation (6) already contradicts nonexpansiveness on \(X_i\).

Now suppose that \(T|_{X_i}\) were a \((k,\eta)\)-enriched contraction,
where \(k\ge0\) and \(\eta<k+1\).  Both differences in (6) have the same
sign before absolute values, so applying (3) to \(u,v\) gives
\[
 \frac{9k+12}{5}
 =|k(u-v)+Tu-Tv|
 \le \eta|u-v|=\frac{9\eta}{5}.
\]
Hence
\[
 \eta\ge k+\frac43>k+1,
\]
contrary to the defining condition \(\eta<k+1\).  Thus the forced cell is
neither enriched contractive nor nonexpansive, for every possible choice of
its parameters.
\end{proof}

\section{Why the construction is robust}

The mechanism is not tied to the displayed numbers.  If \(S\) is any almost
contraction and \(b\ge0\), then
\[
 T=(b+1)S-bI
\]
is an enriched almost contraction, because \((bI+T)/(b+1)=S\).  The target
question concerns invariant pieces for \(T\), not for its averaged map
\(S\).  De-averaging can create a positive expanding secant along a
\(T\)-orbit even though \(S\) has the standard almost-contractive partition
properties.  Such a secant is incompatible with (3), since in one dimension
every positive secant slope of an enriched contraction must be strictly less
than one.  Formula (5) realizes this obstruction with slope \(4/3\).
