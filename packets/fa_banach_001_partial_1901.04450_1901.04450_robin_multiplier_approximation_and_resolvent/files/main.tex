\section{Source problem}

Butzer and Stens write the Robin boundary trace for the exterior unit ball as
a spherical-harmonic multiplier family.  Unlike the Dirichlet and Neumann
families, it is not a semigroup.  They ask for a direct and inverse
approximation theorem, a replacement for the missing infinitesimal generator,
and an analogue of the resolvent interconnection formula when the operator is
not a semigroup generator \cite[Section~3, PDF p.~8]{ButzerStens}.

The results below give all three objects at the level needed for the
approximation question.  The key point is that the semigroup law is
unnecessary: the Robin multipliers can be compared coefficient by coefficient
with the Poisson multipliers.

\section{Claimed contribution}

Let $H=L^2(S^2)$ and write its orthogonal spherical-harmonic decomposition as
\[
 H=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}H_k,\qquad h=\sum_{k\geq0}h_k.
\]
Define the positive self-adjoint operator $N$ by
\[
 Nh_k=(k+1)h_k,\qquad
 \D(N)=\left\{h:\sum_{k\geq0}(k+1)^2\|h_k\|_2^2<\infty\right\},
\]
and let $P_t=e^{-tN}$ be the exterior Poisson semigroup.  Fix real Robin
parameters $\alpha,\beta$, not both zero, satisfying the nonresonance
condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonres}
 \alpha-\beta n\neq0\qquad(n=1,2,\ldots).
\end{equation}
The source's boundary-trace family, with $r=e^t$, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Vdef}
 V_th=\sum_{k\geq0}b_{k+1}(t)h_k,\qquad
 b_n(t)=e^{-nt}\frac{\alpha-\beta n e^{-t}}{\alpha-\beta n}.
\end{equation}
This formula is initially read on finite harmonic sums; the uniform estimate
proved below extends every $V_t$, $0<t\leq t_0$, to a bounded operator on $H$.

For the usual $K$-functional we use the equivalent homogeneous graph norm
\[
 K(t,h;H,\D(N))=
 \inf_{g\in\D(N)}\bigl(\|h-g\|_2+t\|Ng\|_2\bigr).
\]
This is equivalent to the conventional full graph norm because $N\geq I$.

\begin{theorem}[Robin direct and inverse theorem]\label{thm:main}
Under \eqref{eq:nonres}, there are $t_0>0$ and constants $0<c\leq C<\infty$,
depending only on $\alpha,\beta$, such that for every $h\in H$ and
$0<t\leq t_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threeway}
 c\,K(t,h;H,\D(N))
 \leq \|(I-V_t)h\|_2
 \leq C\,K(t,h;H,\D(N)).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the Robin error, the Poisson error
$\|(I-P_t)h\|_2$, and this $K$-functional are uniformly comparable.

Consequently, for every $0<s<1$,
\[
 \|(I-V_t)h\|_2=O(t^s)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 K(t,h;H,\D(N))=O(t^s).
\]
At the saturation order,
\begin{align*}
 \|(I-V_t)h\|_2=O(t)&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h\in\D(N),\\
 \|(I-V_t)h\|_2=o(t)&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h=0.
\end{align*}

Moreover the strong right derivative of $V_t$ at zero on $\D(N)$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C}
 Ch:=\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{V_th-h}{t}
 =\left[-N+\beta N(\alpha I-\beta N)^{-1}\right]h.
\end{equation}
The second term is bounded, $\D(C)=\D(N)$,
$\|Ch\|_2\asymp\|Nh\|_2$, and $C$ is a bounded perturbation of $-N$.
Thus $C$ is an explicit replacement infinitesimal operator and itself
generates a strongly continuous semigroup.
\end{theorem}

The source's separate resolvent question has an algebraic answer that is
independent of the Robin setting.

\begin{proposition}[Reciprocal resolvents need no semigroup]\label{prop:res}
Let $A:\D(A)\subset X\to X$ be a closed injective operator on a complex
Banach space and regard $A^{-1}$ as the closed operator with domain
$\operatorname{Ran}A$.  If $\lambda\neq0$ and
$\lambda^{-1}\in\rho(A)$, then $\lambda\in\rho(A^{-1})$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:res}
 \lambda\Rop(\lambda;A^{-1})
 =I-\lambda^{-1}\Rop(\lambda^{-1};A).
\end{equation}
No generator or semigroup assumption is used.

If instead $A$ is a bounded generalized-Drazin invertible operator, let $P$
be its spectral idempotent at zero, $Q=I-P$, and $A^D$ its generalized-Drazin
inverse.  At reciprocal nonzero resolvent points,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drazin}
 \lambda\Rop(\lambda;A^D)
 =P+Q\bigl[I-\lambda^{-1}\Rop(\lambda^{-1};A)\bigr]Q.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\section{Proof intuition}

There are two short mechanisms.  First, after dividing by the Poisson error,
the Robin error multiplier becomes
\[
 \frac{1-b_n(t)}{1-e^{-nt}}
 =\frac{nq_n(t)-a}{n-a},\qquad
 q_n(t)=\frac{1-e^{-(n+1)t}}{1-e^{-nt}},\quad a=\frac\alpha\beta.
\]
Concavity of $x\mapsto1-e^{-tx}$ gives
$1<q_n(t)<1+1/n$.  Large degrees are therefore uniformly harmless.  Only
finitely many small degrees remain, and their zero-time limits are nonzero
exactly because the Robin problem is nonresonant.

Second, the reciprocal-resolvent formula is simply the factorization
\[
 (\lambda I-A^{-1})A=\lambda(A-\lambda^{-1}I).
\]
The semigroup representation of a resolvent is one way to discover the
identity, but is not required to prove it.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

If $\beta=0$, then $b_n(t)=e^{-nt}$ and the Robin and Poisson errors are
identical.  Assume $\beta\neq0$ and put $a=\alpha/\beta$.  Condition
\eqref{eq:nonres} is precisely $a\notin\{1,2,\ldots\}$.

For $t>0$ define
\[
 q_n(t)=\frac{1-e^{-(n+1)t}}{1-e^{-nt}}.
\]
The function $x\mapsto1-e^{-tx}$ is increasing and concave on $[0,\infty)$
and vanishes at zero.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qbound}
 1<q_n(t)<\frac{n+1}{n}=1+\frac1n.
\end{equation}
A direct calculation from \eqref{eq:Vdef} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
 \frac{1-b_n(t)}{1-e^{-nt}}=\frac{nq_n(t)-a}{n-a}.
\end{equation}

Choose $n_0>2|a|+2$.  For $n\geq n_0$, the two quantities $n-a$ and
$nq_n(t)-a$ are positive, and \eqref{eq:qbound} bounds their ratio above and
away from zero by absolute constants independent of $n,t$.  For each of the
finitely many $1\leq n<n_0$, the right side of \eqref{eq:ratio} extends
continuously to $t=0$ with value
\[
 \frac{n+1-a}{n-a}.
\]
Neither numerator nor denominator vanishes because
$a\notin\{1,2,\ldots\}$.  Shrinking
$t_0$ and taking the minimum and maximum over these finitely many degrees
therefore yields constants $m,M>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalarcomp}
 m(1-e^{-nt})\leq |1-b_n(t)|\leq M(1-e^{-nt})
 \quad(n\geq1,\ 0<t\leq t_0).
\end{equation}
Orthogonality now gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poissoncomp}
 m\|(I-P_t)h\|_2\leq\|(I-V_t)h\|_2
 \leq M\|(I-P_t)h\|_2.
\end{equation}

It remains to identify the Poisson error.  Introduce the quadratic functional
\[
 K_2(t,h)^2=\inf_{g\in\D(N)}
 \bigl(\|h-g\|_2^2+t^2\|Ng\|_2^2\bigr).
\]
Minimizing independently on every $H_k$ gives the exact formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K2}
 K_2(t,h)^2=
 \sum_{k\geq0}\frac{t^2(k+1)^2}{1+t^2(k+1)^2}\|h_k\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
The inequalities
\[
 K_2(t,h)\leq K(t,h;H,\D(N))\leq\sqrt2K_2(t,h)
\]
are immediate.  Finally, $1-e^{-x}$ is uniformly comparable on
$[0,\infty)$ with $x/\sqrt{1+x^2}$.  Comparing the orthogonal sums for
$\|(I-P_t)h\|_2^2$ and \eqref{eq:K2} proves \eqref{eq:threeway}.

The $O(t^s)$ statement follows immediately.  Also
$K(t,h)\leq t\|Nh\|_2$ for $h\in\D(N)$.  Conversely, if
$K(t,h)=O(t)$, divide \eqref{eq:K2} by $t^2$ and use Fatou's lemma as
$t\downarrow0$ to obtain $\sum(k+1)^2\|h_k\|_2^2<\infty$.  If
$K(t,h)=o(t)$, the same formula applied to each fixed $k$ forces every
$h_k=0$.  This proves the saturation assertions.

For the derivative, differentiating \eqref{eq:Vdef} at zero gives the
multiplier
\[
 c_n=-n+\frac{\beta n}{\alpha-\beta n}.
\]
The rational second term is bounded under \eqref{eq:nonres}, while
$|c_n|\asymp n$: after division by $n$ its absolute value is
$|(n+1-a)/(n-a)|$, a nonzero sequence converging to one.  The scalar bound
\eqref{eq:scalarcomp} also gives
$|(b_n(t)-1)/t|\leq Mn$.  Dominated convergence in the orthogonal sum now
proves \eqref{eq:C} for $h\in\D(N)$.  The domain and norm assertions follow,
and the bounded perturbation theorem gives the final generator statement.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:res}}

Put $\mu=\lambda^{-1}$.  On $\D(A)$,
\[
 (\lambda I-A^{-1})A=\lambda(A-\mu I).
\]
Since $\mu\in\rho(A)$, this factorization shows that
$\lambda I-A^{-1}:\operatorname{Ran}A\to X$ is bijective.  Solving the
factorization and using $(A-\mu I)^{-1}=-\Rop(\mu;A)$ gives
\[
 \Rop(\lambda;A^{-1})
 =\lambda^{-1}I-\lambda^{-2}\Rop(\lambda^{-1};A),
\]
which is bounded and is equivalent to \eqref{eq:res}.

For the generalized-Drazin statement, use the reducing decomposition
$X=PX\oplus QX$.  On $PX$, $A^D=0$, so the left side of
\eqref{eq:drazin} equals $P$.  On $QX$, $A$ is invertible and
$A^D=A^{-1}$, so \eqref{eq:res} applies.  Combining the two blocks proves
\eqref{eq:drazin}.

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The separate report \texttt{verification.md} audits the resonance condition,
uniformity in the harmonic degree, the $K$-functional converse, and the
closed-operator domains.  The script in \texttt{code/} checks the scalar
factorization, derivative, resolvent identity, and representative numerical
ratios; computation is not used as proof.

The theorem gives the requested direct/inverse approximation counterpart for
all real nonresonant Robin parameters, not merely the opposite-sign example
mentioned in the source.  Proposition~\ref{prop:res} answers the literal
injective resolvent question and gives the standard bounded
generalized-Drazin modification.  We do not claim counterparts of every
inverse-ergodic assertion in the source, nor an unbounded $a$-Drazin theorem.

A bounded search on 11 August 2026 covered the run's four cheap indexes,
exact-title and arXiv searches for arXiv:1901.04450, the displayed resolvent
formula, the source authors' later exterior-boundary work, and combinations
of ``Robin'', ``K-functional'', and ``spherical harmonic multiplier''.  A
2022 follow-up found in the search treats exterior Dirichlet and Neumann
problems, not this Robin comparison.  No later source stating the theorem
above was found.  This supports, but cannot certify, novelty.  The resolvent
part itself should be regarded as an elementary clarification, not as a new
operator identity.
