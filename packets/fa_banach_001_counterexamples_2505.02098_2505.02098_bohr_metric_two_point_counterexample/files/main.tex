\section{Source question}

Sameer Chavan and Chaman Kumar Sahu study contractive interpolation by
functions in $H^\infty(\Hh_0)\cap\cD$, where $\cD$ denotes the functions
representable by a convergent Dirichlet series in some right half-plane
\cite{CS}.  Their Theorem~2.1 says that interpolation data
$\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n\in\Hh_{1/2}$ and
$w_1,\ldots,w_n\in\D$ must satisfy
\begin{align}
 \zetaP&=\big[(1-\overline w_iw_j)
       \zeta(\overline\lambda_i+\lambda_j)\big]_{i,j=1}^n\succeq0,
 \label{eq:zeta-pick}\\
 \classP&=\left[\frac{1-\overline w_iw_j}
       {\overline\lambda_i+\lambda_j}\right]_{i,j=1}^n\succeq0,
 \qquad \operatorname{rank}\classP=n.
 \label{eq:classical-pick}
\end{align}
Condition \eqref{eq:classical-pick} makes the classical contractive
$H^\infty(\Hh_0)$ solution family $\mathcal S$ non-singleton.  On PDF
page~8, the authors ask whether, if \eqref{eq:zeta-pick} also holds, there
must be a solution in $\mathcal S\cap\cD$.

\section{Definitions and the additional necessary inequality}

For $a,b\in\D$, write
\[
 \rho(a,b)=\left|\frac{a-b}{1-\overline a b}\right|
\]
for the pseudohyperbolic distance.  Let $p_1=2,p_2=3,\ldots$ enumerate the
primes, and for $s>0$ put
\[
 z(s)=(p_j^{-s})_{j\geq1}\in\B_{c_0}.
\]
The standard Bohr-transform theorem identifies the Banach algebra of bounded
Dirichlet series isometrically with $H^\infty(\B_{c_0})$ and carries
evaluation at $s$ to evaluation at $z(s)$; see
Defant--Garc\'ia--Maestre--Sevilla-Peris \cite{DGMS}.  We use the following
direct consequence.

\begin{lemma}[Bohr-metric contraction]
\label{lem:metric}
If $\varphi\in H^\infty(\Hh_0)\cap\cD$ and
$\lVert\varphi\rVert_\infty\leq1$, then for $s,t>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \rho\bigl(\varphi(s),\varphi(t)\bigr)
 \leq \sup_{j\geq1}\rho(p_j^{-s},p_j^{-t}).
 \label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Phi\in H^\infty(\B_{c_0})$ be the Bohr lift of $\varphi$.  For
$a\in\B_{c_0}$, the coordinatewise M\"obius map
\[
 \Theta_a(x)_j=\frac{x_j-a_j}{1-\overline{a_j}x_j}
\]
is an automorphism of $\B_{c_0}$ sending $a$ to zero.  Compose $\Phi$ on the
source with $\Theta_{z(s)}^{-1}$ and on the target with the disk automorphism
sending $\Phi(z(s))$ to zero.  The resulting contractive holomorphic scalar
function $G$ on $\B_{c_0}$ satisfies $G(0)=0$.  The Schwarz lemma on the unit
ball of a complex Banach space gives $|G(u)|\leq\lVert u\rVert_\infty$.
Applying it to $u=\Theta_{z(s)}(z(t))$ yields
\[
 \rho\bigl(\Phi(z(s)),\Phi(z(t))\bigr)
 \leq \lVert\Theta_{z(s)}(z(t))\rVert_\infty
 =\sup_j\rho(p_j^{-s},p_j^{-t}),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:metric}.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof intuition}

The classical Pick matrix sees only the hyperbolic distance between the two
points $1$ and $2$ in the half-plane.  A bounded Dirichlet series has much
more structure: under the Bohr lift, those evaluation points become the two
infinite-prime vectors $(p^{-1})_p$ and $(p^{-2})_p$.  Holomorphic contraction
on the $c_0$ ball therefore imposes a second metric bound.  The closest
bottleneck is the prime $2$, and its exact pseudohyperbolic distance is
$2/7$.  The classical condition allows target distance all the way up to
$1/3$, leaving a nonempty interval of counterexamples.

The same prime-$2$ coordinate also supplies the sharp positive construction:
a disk automorphism applied to $2^{-s}$ realizes the endpoint $2/7$.

\section{Counterexample and exact threshold}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:counterexample}
Let
\[
 \lambda_1=1,\qquad \lambda_2=2,\qquad w_1=0,\qquad w_2=r.
\]
For every $r\in\D$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \frac27<|r|<\frac13,
 \label{eq:window}
\end{equation}
both matrices \eqref{eq:zeta-pick} and \eqref{eq:classical-pick} are positive
definite (so the latter has rank two), but
\[
 \mathcal S\cap\cD=\varnothing.
\]
Consequently, the concluding-remarks question of Chavan--Sahu has a negative
answer.  In particular, one may take $r=3/10$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For these data, the classical matrix is
\[
 \classP=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \frac12&\frac13\\[2pt]
  \frac13&\frac{1-|r|^2}{4}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det\classP=\frac{1-9|r|^2}{72}.
\]
Thus $\classP$ is positive definite whenever $|r|<1/3$.

The zeta matrix is
\[
 \zetaP=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \zeta(2)&\zeta(3)\\
  \zeta(3)&(1-|r|^2)\zeta(4)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The source establishes the strict estimate
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\zeta(3)^2}{\zeta(2)\zeta(4)}<\frac89.
 \label{eq:source-zeta-bound}
\end{equation}
Since $|r|<1/3$ implies $1-|r|^2>8/9$, we obtain
\[
 \det\zetaP
 =\zeta(2)\zeta(4)\left(
 1-|r|^2-\frac{\zeta(3)^2}{\zeta(2)\zeta(4)}\right)>0.
\]
Hence $\zetaP$ is also positive definite.

Suppose now that some $\varphi\in\mathcal S\cap\cD$ existed.  By the
definition of $\mathcal S$, it would belong to the closed unit ball of
$H^\infty(\Hh_0)$ and satisfy $\varphi(1)=0$, $\varphi(2)=r$.  Lemma
\ref{lem:metric} would give
\begin{align*}
 |r|=\rho(0,r)
 &\leq \sup_{p\ \mathrm{prime}}\rho(p^{-1},p^{-2})\\
 &=\sup_{p\ \mathrm{prime}}\frac{p}{p^2+p+1}=\frac27.
\end{align*}
Indeed, the real function $x/(x^2+x+1)$ has derivative
$(1-x^2)/(x^2+x+1)^2<0$ for $x>1$, so the prime supremum occurs at $p=2$.
This contradicts \eqref{eq:window}.

For the advertised rational choice $r=3/10$, the classical determinant is
exactly
\[
 \frac{1-(3/10)^2}{8}-\frac19
 =\frac{91}{800}-\frac19=\frac{19}{7200}>0,
\]
and \eqref{eq:source-zeta-bound} gives the strict positivity of $\zetaP$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharpness on the two-point family]
\label{cor:sharp}
For $r\in\D$, there is a $\varphi\in H^\infty(\Hh_0)\cap\cD$ with
$\lVert\varphi\rVert_\infty\leq1$, $\varphi(1)=0$, and
$\varphi(2)=r$ if and only if $|r|\leq2/7$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Necessity is the calculation in Theorem~\ref{thm:counterexample}.  For
sufficiency, let
\[
 B(z)=\frac{\frac12-z}{1-\frac12z}.
\]
This is a disk automorphism satisfying $B(1/2)=0$ and $B(1/4)=2/7$.  If
$|r|\leq2/7$, define
\[
 \varphi_r(s)=\frac{7r}{2}\,B(2^{-s}).
\]
Then $\lVert\varphi_r\rVert_\infty\leq1$ on $\Hh_0$ and the two required
values hold.  Moreover,
\[
 B(z)=\frac12-3\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^k}{2^{k+1}},
\]
so $B(2^{-s})$ is a Dirichlet series (supported on powers of $2$) converging
absolutely throughout $\Hh_0$.  Thus $\varphi_r\in\cD$.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and scope}

The included script \texttt{code/verify\_two\_point.py} checks the exact
rational determinant, evaluates the zeta ratio at 80 decimal digits, verifies
the first 25 prime-coordinate distances, and samples the explicit disk
automorphism.  The calculation is only a sanity check; the proof above is
analytic.  Full commands and outputs are recorded in
\texttt{verification.md}.

The counterexample answers the source question exactly as asked.  It does not
claim that conditions \eqref{eq:zeta-pick}--\eqref{eq:classical-pick} are
never sufficient for other configurations.  Corollary~\ref{cor:sharp} is an
exact classification only for the displayed two-point family.

\section{Human-review recommendation}

This packet is suitable for mathematical review as a candidate full negative
answer.  The highest-value check is that the standard Bohr-transform
isometry applies to the source's precise class
$H^\infty(\Hh_0)\cap\cD$.  Once that standard identification is accepted,
the coordinatewise automorphism proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:metric}, both strict
Pick-matrix inequalities, and the sharp explicit interpolant are elementary.
