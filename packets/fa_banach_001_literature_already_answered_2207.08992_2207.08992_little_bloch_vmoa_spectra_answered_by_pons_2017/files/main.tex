\fbox{\parbox{0.9\textwidth}{\textbf{Status: literature already answered.}
Both source questions have the same answer: for every parabolic or hyperbolic

\section*{Original questions}

Allen, Le, and Pons ask at the end of Section 5 of their 2016 paper
\cite{ALP} (arXiv:2207.08992, PDF page 12):
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Question 1.} For $\varphi$ a parabolic or hyperbolic automorphism,
what is the spectrum of $C_\varphi$ on the little Bloch space?

\textbf{Question 2.} For $\varphi$ a parabolic or hyperbolic automorphism,
what is the spectrum of $C_\varphi$ on $VMOA$?
\end{quote}

\section*{Separate answer paper}

Matthew A. Pons's 2017 paper \cite{Pons} explicitly cites \cite{ALP}, says in
Section 4 that these are the remaining spectra to compute, and proves:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Theorem 4} (printed page 10 of 13): if $\varphi$ is parabolic or
hyperbolic, then
\[
  \sigma_{\mathcal B_0}(C_\varphi)=\partial\D.
\]
\item \textbf{Theorem 5} (printed pages 10--12): if $\varphi$ is parabolic,
then
\[
  \sigma_{VMOA}(C_\varphi)=\partial\D.
\]
The proof constructs approximate eigenfunctions for every unimodular value.
\item \textbf{Theorem 6} (printed page 12): if $\varphi$ is hyperbolic, then
\[
  \sigma_{VMOA}(C_\varphi)=\partial\D.
\]
Here Pons uses the canonical identification $VMOA^{**}=BMOA$, identifies the
bidual of the restricted composition operator with $C_\varphi$ on $BMOA$, and
uses equality of the spectra of an operator and its bidual.
\end{itemize}

Thus the supporting author knew he was answering precisely the questions left
by the source paper.  No part of Questions 1 or 2 remains open for parabolic
or hyperbolic disk automorphisms.
