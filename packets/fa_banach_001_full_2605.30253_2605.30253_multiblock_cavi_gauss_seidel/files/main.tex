\section*{Status and source question}

This is a \texttt{candidate\_full\_likely\_valid} answer to the multiblock
extension left for future work by Rocco Caprio, Adrien Corenflos, and Sam
Power, \emph{Wasserstein Contraction of Coordinate Ascent Variational
Inference}, arXiv:2605.30253 \cite{CCP}.  After their two-block contraction
theorem, the authors ask for the dynamics when the number of blocks is
$m>2$ (source PDF page~5).

The theorem below gives the systematic-scan multiblock extension under the
natural matrix-valued version of the source's Fisher-smoothness hypothesis.
It also identifies the convergence rate with the spectral radius of a
Gauss--Seidel comparison matrix.

\section*{Setting and proof intuition}

Let $m\ge2$, let $\mathcal X_i$ be smooth complete Riemannian manifolds, and
let $T_i$ be the $i$th CAVI optimality map: $T_i[\mu_{-i}]$ is the minimizing
$i$th marginal when all other marginals are fixed.  Fix a product fixed
point $\mu^*=(\mu_1^*,\ldots,\mu_m^*)$, so
$\mu_i^*=T_i[\mu_{-i}^*]$.

Write $\W_i$ for the Wasserstein-$p$ distance on $\Pp(\mathcal X_i)$ and
$\I_i$ for relative Fisher information.  Suppose $\mu_i^*$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TI}
 \lambda_i\W_i(\eta_i,\mu_i^*)
 \le \I_i(\eta_i\Vert\mu_i^*)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The multiblock Fisher-smoothness matrix $(L_{ij})$, with $L_{ii}=0$, means
that throughout a product neighborhood $\mathcal N$ of $\mu^*$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth}
 \I_i\bigl(T_i[\eta_{-i}]\Vert T_i[\mu_{-i}^*]\bigr)^{1/2}
 \le \sum_{j\ne i}L_{ij}\W_j(\eta_j,\mu_j^*).
\end{equation}
For $m=2$, this is exactly the source's Fisher-smoothness condition.  A
stronger all-pairs version of \eqref{eq:smooth} may of course be used.

The intuition is finite-dimensional.  Combining \eqref{eq:TI} and
\eqref{eq:smooth} makes each new Wasserstein error at most a weighted sum of
the other errors.  In a sequential sweep, already-updated blocks occur on
the left and not-yet-updated blocks on the right.  Solving this triangular
inequality produces the usual Gauss--Seidel matrix.  Perron--Frobenius
weighting then converts spectral radius below one into a contracting norm.

\section*{Main result}

Put $a_{ij}=L_{ij}/\lambda_i$ for $i\ne j$ and $a_{ii}=0$, and let
$A=(a_{ij})$.  Write $A_-$ and $A_+$ for the strict lower and strict upper
triangular parts of $A$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G}
 G=(I-A_-)^{-1}A_+.
\end{equation}
The inverse exists because $A_-$ is nilpotent, and it is entrywise
nonnegative.

\begin{theorem}[systematic-scan multiblock contraction]\label{thm:main}
Assume \eqref{eq:TI} and \eqref{eq:smooth}.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral}
 \rhoSp(G)<1,
\end{equation}
then for every $q\in(\rhoSp(G),1)$ there is a vector $w\in(0,\infty)^m$
such that one full systematic CAVI sweep satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contract}
 \max_i\frac{\W_i(\mu_i^{k+1},\mu_i^*)}{w_i}
 \le q\max_i\frac{\W_i(\mu_i^k,\mu_i^*)}{w_i}.
\end{equation}

For a local statement, suppose
$\mathcal N=\prod_i\{\eta_i:\W_i(\eta_i,\mu_i^*)<\varepsilon_i\}$.
Then \eqref{eq:contract} and invariance of $\mathcal N$ hold whenever
\[
 \max_i\frac{\W_i(\mu_i^0,\mu_i^*)}{w_i}<R,
 \qquad R<\min_i\frac{\varepsilon_i}{w_i}.
\]
If the assumptions are global, the conclusion holds for every initialization.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
One systematic sweep updates blocks in the order $1,\ldots,m$:
\[
 \mu_i^{k+1}=T_i[\mu_1^{k+1},\ldots,\mu_{i-1}^{k+1},
                    \mu_{i+1}^{k},\ldots,\mu_m^k].
\]
Set $e_i^k=\W_i(\mu_i^k,\mu_i^*)$.  Since
$T_i[\mu_{-i}^*]=\mu_i^*$, first \eqref{eq:TI} and then
\eqref{eq:smooth} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row}
 e_i^{k+1}\le
 \sum_{j<i}a_{ij}e_j^{k+1}+\sum_{j>i}a_{ij}e_j^k.
\end{equation}
In vector form,
\[
 e^{k+1}\le A_-e^{k+1}+A_+e^k.
\]
Because $(I-A_-)^{-1}=I+A_-+\cdots+A_-^{m-1}$ is nonnegative,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison}
 e^{k+1}\le Ge^k
\end{equation}
componentwise.

Fix $q\in(\rhoSp(G),1)$ and let $\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)^\mathsf T$.  The
Neumann series
\[
 w=(I-G/q)^{-1}\mathbf1
   =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(G/q)^n\mathbf1
\]
is finite and strictly positive.  It satisfies
$Gw=q(w-\mathbf1)\le qw$.  If
$r=\max_i e_i^k/w_i$, then $e^k\le rw$, and \eqref{eq:comparison} yields
$e^{k+1}\le rGw\le qrw$.  This is \eqref{eq:contract}.

For the local assertion, the assumed initial bound places every old block in
$\mathcal N$.  During the first sweep, apply \eqref{eq:row} successively.
The already-updated errors are bounded by the corresponding components of
$Ge^0\le qRw$, while the remaining old errors are bounded by $Rw$; hence all
hybrid inputs stay in $\mathcal N$.  The same argument closes the induction
over sweeps and proves local invariance.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[parallel/Jacobi update]\label{cor:jacobi}
If all blocks are updated from the previous iterate in parallel, then
$e^{k+1}\le Ae^k$.  Consequently $\rhoSp(A)<1$ gives the analogue of
\eqref{eq:contract}, with any $q\in(\rhoSp(A),1)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
There are no already-updated errors in \eqref{eq:row}, so the comparison
matrix is $A$.  Repeat the Neumann-series weighting argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[recovery of the source theorem]
For $m=2$,
\[
 G=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{12}\\0&a_{21}a_{12}\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \rhoSp(G)=a_{12}a_{21}
 =\frac{L_{12}L_{21}}{\lambda_1\lambda_2}.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:spectral} is precisely the source's small-interaction
condition and its one-sweep rate.
\end{corollary}

\section*{Verification, scope, and novelty status}

The included script tests 25,000 random nonnegative interaction matrices,
100,000 error vectors, and 20,000 two-block reductions.  It verifies the
triangular recursion, the Neumann weight inequality, weighted contraction,
and the exact two-block product formula.  This is a regression check, not a
substitute for the proof.

Eight focused proof passes are recorded in the accompanying attempt note.
They replace an insufficient cycle-product guess by the full comparison
matrix, distinguish systematic and parallel scans, establish local
invariance, recover the two-block theorem exactly, and audit the spectral
criterion against linear comparison dynamics.

The theorem assumes smooth blocks and a combined multiblock
Fisher-smoothness bound of the form \eqref{eq:smooth}.  It does not prove that
arbitrary pairwise Fisher-information estimates tensorize into that bound,
nor does it cover random-scan CAVI or the source's one-nonsmooth-block
data-augmentation refinement.  These are separate extensions.

Cheap run indexes and bounded web/arXiv searches on 17 August 2026 used
arXiv:2605.30253 and the phrases \emph{multiblock Fisher-smoothness CAVI},
\emph{transport-information multi-block variational inference}, and
\emph{Wasserstein contraction Gauss--Seidel CAVI}.  They found the source and
known multiblock results under strong log-concavity and block smoothness, but
no extension based on a matrix of Fisher-smoothness constants and
transport-information inequalities that gives direct Wasserstein contraction.
Novelty confidence is moderate-high pending expert review, especially because
the source is recent.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Check that the intended application supplies the combined bound
\eqref{eq:smooth}; conditional on it, the transport-information and matrix
argument is self-contained.  Also verify the desired scan convention before
using $G$ rather than $A$.
