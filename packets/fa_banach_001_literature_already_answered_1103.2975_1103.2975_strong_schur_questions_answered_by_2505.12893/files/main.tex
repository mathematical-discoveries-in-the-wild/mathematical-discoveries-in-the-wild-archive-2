\paragraph{Status.}
Both focused questions at the end of arXiv:1103.2975 are answered by
arXiv:2505.12893.  This note records the exact correspondence and does not
claim a new mathematical result.

\paragraph{Definitions.}
For a bounded sequence $(x_n)$ in a Banach space $X$, write
\[
 \operatorname{ca}(x_n)=\inf_N\operatorname{diam}\{x_n:n\geq N\},
 \qquad
 \delta(x_n)=\operatorname{diam}\operatorname{clust}_{w^*}(x_n)
 \subset X^{**}.
\]
The space has the $1$-Schur property when
$\operatorname{ca}(x_n)\leq\delta(x_n)$ for every bounded sequence.  It has
the $1$-strong Schur property when every normalized $\delta$-separated
sequence has, for every $\varepsilon>0$, a subsequence
$(2/\delta+\varepsilon)$-equivalent to the standard basis of $\ell_1$.

\paragraph{The two source questions.}
In its final remarks, arXiv:1103.2975 proves for real Banach spaces that
\[
 1\text{-Schur}\Longrightarrow 1\text{-strong Schur}
 \Longrightarrow 5\text{-Schur}.
\]
It asks whether the first two properties are equivalent and conjectures that
they are not.  It separately notes that the proof of the first implication is
real and asks, in effect, whether it remains valid over the complex field.

\paragraph{Exact later answer in the real case.}
Section 8 of O. F. K. Kalenda, \emph{Quantitative Schur property and measures
of weak non-compactness}, arXiv:2505.12893, gives the following example.
\begin{answerA}
Let $M=\mathbb Z$ with the shortest-path metric for the graph in which distinct
$m,n$ are adjacent exactly when $m\neq -n$.  Thus
\[
 d(m,n)=\begin{cases}
 0,&m=n,\\
 2,&m=-n\neq0,\\
 1,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then the real Lipschitz-free space $\mathcal F(M)$ has the $2$-Schur property,
fails the $c$-Schur property for every $c<2$, and has the $1$-strong Schur
property.
\end{answerA}
In particular $\mathcal F(M)$ is $1$-strong Schur but not $1$-Schur.  This is
exactly the counterexample conjectured in arXiv:1103.2975.

\paragraph{Exact later answer in the complex case.}
The same paper also distinguishes the two notions in the opposite direction
over the complex field.
\begin{answerB}
Let $X$ be the canonical complexification of the real Banach space $\ell_1$
(with the complexification norm used in the paper).  Then $X$ has the
$1$-Schur property, but it fails the quantitative strong-Schur condition with
constant $c$ for every $c<\pi/2$.  In particular, it fails the $1$-strong
Schur property.
\end{answerB}
The witness is the canonical-vector sequence.  Its complex lower
$\ell_1$ constant is $2/\pi$, so no subsequence can have the constant required
for $c<\pi/2$.  Therefore the real implication
$1$-Schur $\Rightarrow$ $1$-strong Schur is false for complex Banach spaces.

\paragraph{Verification and conclusion.}
The later paper cites arXiv:1103.2975 for the original definitions and
implications, announces in its introduction that the first graph example
``witnesses that these two properties are different also within real Banach
spaces,'' and proves the two examples above in full.  The source's conjecture
and its complex-field uncertainty are therefore both resolved by existing
literature.  The target is classified as
\texttt{literature\_already\_answered}.
