\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{accent}{soft}{\parbox{0.91\textwidth}{
\textbf{Status: literature-implied full negative answer.}
For every complex dimension $n\geq1$ and every Hermite time $\tau>0$, there
is a symbol satisfying the source's condition $(*)$ whose Toeplitz operator
has a bounded extension, although its critical Gaussian regularization is
unbounded.  The source conjecture is therefore false.}}
\end{center}

\section*{Source conjecture}
Jotsaroop and Thangavelu define the Hermite--Bergman space
$\Hh_\tau(\C^n)$ using
\[
 U_\tau(x,y)=4^n(\sinh4\tau)^{-n/2}
 \exp\!\big(\tanh(2\tau)|x|^2-\coth(2\tau)|y|^2\big).
\]
For a measurable $g$ satisfying
\[
 gK_\tau(\,cdot\,,w)\in L^2(U_\tau,dv)
 \quad\text{for every }w\in\C^n, \tag{$*$}
\]
they define the densely defined Toeplitz operator $T_g=P_\tau M_g$.
The remark on source PDF page~12, immediately after the boundedness theorem
in Section~3.2, conjectures
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conj}
 T_g\text{ extends boundedly on }\Hh_\tau(\C^n)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 g*q_{\sinh(4\tau)/8}\in L^\infty(\C^n),
\end{equation}
where $q_s$ is the Euclidean heat kernel on $\C^n\simeq\mathbb R^{2n}$
\cite{JT}.

\section*{Supporting theorem}
In Looi's standard Fock normalization
\[
 d\mu(\zeta)=(2\pi)^{-n}e^{-|\zeta|^2/2},dv(\zeta),
 \qquad K(\zeta,a)=e^{\zeta\cdot\bar a/2},
\]
Theorem~1.2 on supporting PDF page~3 constructs, for every $n\geq1$, a
measurable $h$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $hK(\cdot,a)\in L^2(d\mu)$ for every $a$;
\item $T_h$ extends to a bounded operator on the Fock space;
\item $h*q_{1/4}$ is unbounded.
\end{enumerate}
The paper proves that the bounded extension agrees with the natural Toeplitz
operator on the dense span of kernel vectors, so its boundedness notion is
the one used in the source conjecture \cite{Looi}.

\section*{Exact normalization transport}
\begin{proposition}
For every $\tau>0$, Looi's symbol transports to a counterexample to
\eqref{eq:conj}.  Hence no part of the source conjecture remains open for
general measurable symbols satisfying $(*)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 S=\sinh(4\tau),\qquad c=\coth(4\tau),\qquad
 \lambda=\sqrt{2/S}.
\]
First remove the nonradial part of the Hermite--Bergman weight.  For an entire
function $f$, set
\[
 (M_\tau f)(z)=e^{-cz^2/2}f(z).
\]
The elementary identities
\[
 \coth(4\tau)-\tanh(2\tau)=S^{-1},\qquad
 \coth(2\tau)-\coth(4\tau)=S^{-1}
\]
give, pointwise for $z=x+iy$,
\[
 |e^{-cz^2/2}|^2U_\tau(x,y)
 =4^nS^{-n/2}e^{-|z|^2/S}.
\]
Thus $M_\tau$ is a unitary identification (up to the harmless normalizing
constant in the measure) from a radial Fock space $\F_S$ with Gaussian scale
$S$ onto $\Hh_\tau$.  Moreover,
\[
 \langle gM_\tau f,M_\tau k\rangle_{\Hh_\tau}
 =\langle gf,k\rangle_{\F_S}. \tag{1}
\]
Consequently the Toeplitz forms, their natural domains, and existence of a
bounded extension are preserved with the same symbol $g$.

Now dilate $\F_S$ to Looi's Fock space.  After multiplication by a scalar
chosen to normalize the norm, $f(\zeta)\mapsto f(\lambda z)$ is unitary,
because $|z|^2/S=|\lambda z|^2/2$.  If $h$ is Looi's symbol, define
\[
 g_\tau(z)=h(\lambda z).
\]
Equation (1) and the change of variables $\zeta=\lambda z$ show that the
Toeplitz operator with symbol $g_\tau$ is unitarily equivalent to $T_h$.
The reproducing kernels transform by the same holomorphic multiplier and
dilation (up to nonzero scalar factors), so Looi's condition
$hK(\cdot,a)\in L^2(d\mu)$ is exactly the source condition $(*)$ for
$g_\tau$.

Finally, heat flow commutes with dilation according to
\[
 (g_\tau*q_s)(z)=(h*q_{\lambda^2s})(\lambda z).
\]
At the source endpoint $s=S/8$ one has $\lambda^2s=(2/S)(S/8)=1/4$.
Therefore
\[
 (g_\tau*q_{S/8})(z)=(h*q_{1/4})(\lambda z),
\]
which is unbounded by Looi's theorem, whereas $T_{g_\tau}$ has a bounded
extension.  This violates the forward implication in \eqref{eq:conj}.
\end{proof}

\section*{Provenance and scope}
Looi explicitly disproves the standard Berger--Coburn conjecture, but the
supporting paper does not cite arXiv:0907.2844 or state its Hermite--Bergman
variant.  The full answer above depends on the direct multiplier-and-dilation
identification, so it is classified as a literature-implied answer rather
than an explicit literature answer or a new counterexample.  The conclusion
is restricted to the source's general measurable-symbol conjecture; its
positive theorems for structured symbols remain valid.

The run indexes were searched for the source id/title, Hermite--Bergman
terms, the Berger--Coburn conjecture, and arXiv:2601.20859.  They contained an
existing packet for Looi's answer to the standard Fock conjecture, but no
prior packet transporting it to this source conjecture.
