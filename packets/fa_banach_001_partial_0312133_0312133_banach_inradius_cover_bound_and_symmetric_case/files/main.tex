\begin{abstract}
Kadets proved that if a convex body \(A\) in a Hilbert space is covered by
convex bodies \(A_n\), then
\[
  \sum_n r(A_n)\ge r(A),
\]
and asked whether the same statement holds in arbitrary Banach spaces. We
prove the exact inequality in every real Banach space when all covering
bodies \(A_n\) are centrally symmetric. We also prove a comparison theorem:
if the norm is within distortion \(D\) of a Hilbertian norm, then
\[
  \sum_n r(A_n)\ge \frac1D r(A).
\]
John's ellipsoid theorem consequently gives the universal factor
\(1/\sqrt d\) in dimension \(d\), improving the elementary factor
\(2/(d+1)\) obtained here by converting covering bodies into containing
planks and invoking Ball's Banach-space plank theorem. The unrestricted
dimension-free problem remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source question}

Kadets's paper \cite{Kadets} proves the inequality in real and complex Hilbert
spaces. On PDF page~1, at the end of the Introduction, it states:

\begin{quote}
\emph{An analogous question for general Banach spaces remains open.}
\end{quote}

Thus the extracted question is whether, for every Banach space \(X\), every
closed convex body \(A\subset X\), and every sequence of closed convex bodies
\((A_n)\) covering \(A\), one necessarily has
\[
  \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} r_X(A_n)\ge r_X(A).
\]
Here is the page-one source passage; the crop retains the full readable page
width and the complete open statement.

\end{center}

The same question is still recorded as open for general Banach spaces in the
published version of Verreault's survey \cite[Section~4.4]{Verreault}.

\section{Definitions and the plank input}

Throughout, \(X\) is a real Banach space with closed unit ball \(\Ball\). For
a closed convex set \(C\subset X\) with nonempty interior, its inradius is
\[
  \rin(C)=\sup\{t\ge0: x+t\Ball\subset C\text{ for some }x\in X\}.
\]
We allow the value \(+\infty\). A norm plank is a set
\[
  P(f;a,b)=\{x\in X:a\le f(x)\le b\},
  \qquad f\in X^*,\quad \|f\|=1,
\]
and its width is \(b-a\).

We use Ball's solution of the plank problem for symmetric bodies
\cite{Ball}: if a finite or countable family of planks covers a norm ball
\(x+q\Ball\), then the sum of their widths is at least \(2q\). This countable
form is the one quoted by Kadets \cite[p.~1]{Kadets}. In finite dimensions it
also follows from the finite theorem: widen the \(n\)-th closed plank by total
width \(\varepsilon 2^{-n}\). The interiors of the widened planks cover the
compact ball, hence have a finite subcover. Apply the finite theorem and let
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

\section{Proof intuition}

The source problem asks for an inside measurement of arbitrary covering
bodies, whereas Ball's theorem controls an outside measurement of planks. We
bridge the two measurements.

For a centrally symmetric body \(C\), any ball contained off-center has its
reflection contained as well; averaging the two balls puts a ball of the same
radius at the symmetry center. A supporting direction that nearly realizes
this centered inradius traps \(C\) in a plank of width nearly \(2r_X(C)\).
Replacing every \(A_n\) by such a plank loses no constant, so Ball gives the
exact Kadets inequality.

For a nonsymmetric compact body \(C\) in dimension \(d\), the active supporting
functionals at an incenter balance: zero lies in their convex hull. By
Carath\'eodory, at most \(d+1\) active functionals are needed. One has weight
at least \(1/(d+1)\), and the balancing identity bounds the opposite support
in that direction. The resulting containing plank has width at most
\((d+1)r_X(C)\). Applying this to the intersections \(A_n\cap U\), where
\(U\subset A\) is an inscribed norm ball, and then applying Ball yields the
factor \(2/(d+1)\).

There is also a stronger global comparison. If a Hilbert ellipsoid \(E\)
satisfies \(E\subset B_X\subset D E\), then the two inradii satisfy
\(r_E(C)/D\le r_X(C)\le r_E(C)\). Applying Kadets's Hilbert theorem in the
\(E\)-norm immediately transfers the exact Hilbert inequality with loss
only \(D\). John's theorem supplies \(D\le\sqrt d\). These results solve a
broad structured subcase and give a universal finite-dimensional
approximation, but neither resolves the unrestricted dimension-free question.

\section{The exact centrally symmetric covering-body case}

For a closed convex set \(K\subset X\) containing \(0\), write its support
function in the extended sense as
\[
  h_K(f)=\sup_{x\in K} f(x)\in\R\cup\{+\infty\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Symmetric bodies fit their inradius plankwise]
\label{lem:symmetric-plank}
Let \(C=c+K\) be a closed centrally symmetric convex body in \(X\), where
\(K=-K\). If \(\rin(C)<\infty\), then for every \(\eta>0\) there is a norm
plank containing \(C\) whose width is at most \(2\rin(C)+\eta\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose \(x+t\Ball\subset K\). By symmetry,
\(-x+t\Ball\subset K\). Convexity and pointwise averaging imply
\(t\Ball\subset K\). Thus every admissible radius can be realized at the
symmetry center and
\[
  \rin(C)=\sup\{t:t\Ball\subset K\}
          =\inf_{\|f\|=1}h_K(f).
\]
For the second equality, one implication follows by applying \(f\) to
\(t\Ball\subset K\). Conversely, if
\(t<\inf_{\|f\|=1}h_K(f)\), then \(t\Ball\subset K\) by the Hahn--Banach
separation representation of a closed convex set as the intersection of its
supporting half-spaces.

Choose \(f\in X^*\), \(\|f\|=1\), with
\(h_K(f)\le\rin(C)+\eta/2\). Since \(K=-K\),
\(h_K(-f)=h_K(f)\), and hence \(C\) is contained in
\[
  \{y:f(c)-h_K(f)\le f(y)\le f(c)+h_K(f)\}.
\]
This plank has width \(2h_K(f)\le2\rin(C)+\eta\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact inequality for symmetric covering bodies]
\label{thm:symmetric}
Let \(X\) be any real Banach space. Suppose a closed convex body \(A\subset X\)
is covered by a sequence of closed centrally symmetric convex bodies
\((A_n)_{n\ge1}\). Their symmetry centers may be different. Then
\[
  \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rin(A_n)\ge\rin(A).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \(S:=\sum_n\rin(A_n)=+\infty\), there is nothing to prove. Otherwise every
summand is finite. Fix \(\varepsilon>0\), choose positive \(\eta_n\) with
\(\sum_n\eta_n<\varepsilon\), and use Lemma~\ref{lem:symmetric-plank} to put
each \(A_n\) in a plank \(P_n\) of width at most
\(2\rin(A_n)+\eta_n\). The planks cover \(A\).

For every \(q<\rin(A)\), some translate of \(q\Ball\) lies in \(A\), hence is
covered by the \(P_n\). The ball plank theorem gives
\[
  2q\le\sum_n \operatorname{width}(P_n)
      \le 2S+\varepsilon.
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) and then \(q\uparrow\rin(A)\) proves the
claim. The same argument covers \(\rin(A)=+\infty\), because \(q\) may then
be arbitrarily large.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:symmetric} includes covers by translates of norm balls and
by planks. It does not require the covered body \(A\) itself to be symmetric.
\end{remark}

\section{A Hilbertian-distortion bound}

For the unit ball \(E\) of a Hilbertian norm on \(X\), write \(r_E(C)\) for
the corresponding inradius.

\begin{theorem}[Transfer from a Hilbertian norm]
\label{thm:distortion}
Suppose that \(X\) admits a Hilbertian norm whose closed unit ball \(E\)
satisfies
\[
  E\subset \Ball\subset D E
\]
for some \(D\ge1\). If a closed convex body \(A\subset X\) is covered by a
sequence of closed convex bodies \((A_n)\), then
\[
  \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rin(A_n)\ge \frac1D\rin(A).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every closed convex set \(C\), the inclusions of the unit balls give
\[
  \frac1D r_E(C)\le \rin(C)\le r_E(C).                 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, an \(E\)-ball of radius \(t\) contains an \(X\)-ball of radius
\(t/D\), while every \(X\)-ball of radius \(t\) contains the corresponding
\(E\)-ball. Kadets's theorem applied in the Hilbertian norm gives
\[
  \sum_n r_E(A_n)\ge r_E(A).
\]
Combining this with (2) yields
\[
  \sum_n\rin(A_n)
  \ge \frac1D\sum_n r_E(A_n)
  \ge \frac1D r_E(A)
  \ge \frac1D\rin(A),
\]
including the extended-value cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Universal finite-dimensional bound]
\label{cor:john}
If \(X\) is a \(d\)-dimensional real normed space and \((A_n)\) covers
\(A\), then
\[
  \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rin(A_n)\ge\frac1{\sqrt d}\rin(A).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
John's ellipsoid theorem for the centrally symmetric unit ball \(\Ball\)
provides an ellipsoid \(E\) with
\(E\subset\Ball\subset\sqrt d\,E\) \cite{John}. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:distortion} with \(D=\sqrt d\).
\end{proof}

\section{An elementary containing-plank bound}

\begin{lemma}[A \((d+1)\)-inradius containing plank]
\label{lem:finite-plank}
Let \(X\) be a \(d\)-dimensional normed space, \(d\ge1\), and let
\(C\subset X\) be a nonempty compact convex set. Then \(C\) is contained in a
norm plank of width at most
\[
  (d+1)\rin(C).
\]
Here \(\rin(C)=0\) when \(C\) has empty interior.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(C\) is lower-dimensional, its affine hull is contained in a hyperplane,
which is a plank of width \(0=(d+1)\rin(C)\). Assume henceforth that \(C\) has
nonempty interior. Compactness gives an incenter \(c\); translating, take
\(c=0\), and set \(\rho=\rin(C)>0\). For \(f\in S_{X^*}\), put
\(h(f)=h_C(f)\). Then
\[
  \rho=\min_{f\in S_{X^*}}h(f).
\]
Let \(F=\{f\in S_{X^*}:h(f)=\rho\}\) be the nonempty compact set of active
supporting functionals.

We first show that
\[
  0\in\conv F. \tag{1}
\]
If not, strict separation in the finite-dimensional dual gives a vector
\(v\in X\) and \(\alpha>0\) such that \(f(v)\ge\alpha\) for every \(f\in F\).
By continuity, \(f(v)\ge\alpha/2\) on a neighborhood \(N\) of \(F\) in
\(S_{X^*}\). On the compact complement there is \(\delta>0\) such that
\(h(f)\ge\rho+\delta\). For all sufficiently small \(t>0\), therefore,
\[
  h(f)-f(-tv)=h(f)+t f(v)>\rho
  \qquad(f\in S_{X^*}).
\]
This says that a ball of radius strictly larger than \(\rho\), centered at
\(-tv\), is contained in \(C\), contradicting maximality of the inball. Thus
(1) holds.

Carath\'eodory's theorem now gives \(f_1,\ldots,f_m\in F\), positive numbers
\(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m\), and \(m\le d+1\) such that
\[
  \sum_{j=1}^m\lambda_j=1,
  \qquad \sum_{j=1}^m\lambda_j f_j=0.
\]
Choose \(i\) with \(\lambda_i\ge1/m\ge1/(d+1)\). For every \(x\in C\), all
\(f_j(x)\le h(f_j)=\rho\), and the balancing identity gives
\[
  -f_i(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda_i}
      \sum_{j\ne i}\lambda_j f_j(x)
  \le \rho\frac{1-\lambda_i}{\lambda_i}.
\]
Together with \(f_i(x)\le\rho\), this places \(C\) in the plank
\[
  -\rho\frac{1-\lambda_i}{\lambda_i}
  \le f_i(x)\le\rho,
\]
whose width is
\[
  \frac{\rho}{\lambda_i}\le(d+1)\rho.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Elementary dimension-dependent inequality]
\label{thm:dimension}
Let \(X\) be a \(d\)-dimensional real normed space. If a closed convex body
\(A\subset X\) is covered by a sequence of closed convex bodies \((A_n)\), then
\[
  \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rin(A_n)
  \ge \frac{2}{d+1}\rin(A).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(q<\rin(A)\) and choose a closed norm ball
\(U=x_0+q\Ball\subset A\). Put \(C_n=A_n\cap U\). Each nonempty \(C_n\) is
compact and convex, the \(C_n\)'s cover \(U\), and
\(\rin(C_n)\le\rin(A_n)\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-plank}, choose a plank
\(P_n\supset C_n\) with
\[
  \operatorname{width}(P_n)\le(d+1)\rin(C_n).
\]
The \(P_n\)'s cover \(U\). The countable ball plank theorem yields
\[
  2q\le\sum_n\operatorname{width}(P_n)
      \le(d+1)\sum_n\rin(C_n)
      \le(d+1)\sum_n\rin(A_n).
\]
Let \(q\uparrow\rin(A)\). If \(\rin(A)=+\infty\), the same inequality for
arbitrarily large \(q\) forces the sum on the right to be infinite.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
The distortion bound gives guaranteed factors \(1,1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt3\) in
dimensions \(1,2,3\). The elementary bound is never stronger, since
\(2/(d+1)\le1/\sqrt d\), with equality only when \(d=1\).
\end{corollary}

\section{Verification and bounded computational check}

The proof has five independently checkable obligations.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Central symmetry really centers the inradius: reflecting and averaging
an arbitrary inscribed ball preserves its radius.
\item The active functionals at an incenter must balance. The proof above
includes the compactness step needed to turn strict separation into a uniform
improvement of every support slack.
\item The Carath\'eodory calculation is exact: the selected coefficient
\(\lambda_i\ge1/(d+1)\) produces plank width \(\rho/\lambda_i\), not twice
that quantity.
\item Intersecting with \(U\) is used only in the nonsymmetric
finite-dimensional proof. It is deliberately avoided in the symmetric proof,
since intersections of symmetric bodies with different centers need not be
symmetric.
\item The norm comparison has the correct direction: from
\(E\subset B_X\subset DE\) one gets
\(r_E(C)/D\le r_X(C)\le r_E(C)\). Thus only one factor \(D\), rather than
\(D^2\), is lost when Kadets's Hilbert theorem is transferred.
\end{enumerate}

Before the plank argument was identified, the script
\texttt{code/check\_cube\_triangulations.py} used linear programming to
compute the largest axis-parallel cube in each cell of two standard
tetrahedral partitions of \([0,1]^3\) in the \(\ell_\infty^3\) norm. The
five-tetrahedron partition has total inradius \(5/6\), and the six-cell
Freudenthal partition has total inradius \(1\), compared with ambient cube
inradius \(1/2\). Thus neither is a counterexample. This bounded computation
is not used in either proof.

\section{Limitations and novelty check}

Theorem~\ref{thm:symmetric} assumes symmetry of every covering body, not only
of \(A\). Corollary~\ref{cor:john} loses the factor \(1/\sqrt d\), so it does
not settle the exact question for any nonsymmetric finite-dimensional normed
space of dimension at least two. Theorem~\ref{thm:dimension} gives the weaker
factor \(2/(d+1)\) by an independent elementary route. Kadets's sharper
constant \(1\) remains available in Hilbert spaces \cite{Kadets}. Akopyan and
Karasev prove exact
relative-inradius results for arbitrary planar partitions and for inductive
partitions in higher dimension \cite{AK}; a general cover need not be a
partition.

Before promotion, the run's registry and solution indexes were searched for
arXiv:math/0312133 and the core phrases \emph{convex-body cover inradius},
\emph{centrally symmetric covering bodies}, \emph{Hilbertian distortion},
\(1/\sqrt d\), \(2/(d+1)\), and
\emph{containing plank \(d+1\) inradius}. A bounded web/arXiv search used the
same phrases and located Kadets \cite{Kadets}, Ball \cite{Ball},
Akopyan--Karasev \cite{AK}, and Verreault's current survey
\cite{Verreault}, but no exact match for either theorem above. Novelty
confidence is therefore moderate: these short syntheses of classical tools
may be folklore, and no priority claim is made.
