# Problem and exact scope

For a convex body `Omega` in `R^n`, the source considers two inequalities.
Writing averages in the first one, it asks whether the least constants `A_n`
such that

`average_Omega f <= A_n average_boundary(Omega) f`

for admissible convex functions can remain bounded independently of `n`.

Its Theorem 1 proves, for nonnegative subharmonic functions, an inequality of
the form

`integral_Omega f <= B_n |Omega|^(1/n) integral_boundary(Omega) f`

with a universal upper bound, and asks whether the optimal `B_n` might actually
decay as `n` grows.

The queue's literal `we do not know` hit is at source line 228 and only says
that the proof does not track the positions of Brownian particles surviving to
a fixed time.  It is not an unresolved mathematical statement.
