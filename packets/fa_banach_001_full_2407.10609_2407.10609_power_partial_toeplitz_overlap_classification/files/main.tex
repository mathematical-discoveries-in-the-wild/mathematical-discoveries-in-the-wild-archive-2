\section{The question}

For a Hilbert space $\mathcal E$, let $H^2_{\mathcal E}(\D^n)$ be the
vector-valued Hardy space on the polydisc.  Sarkar \cite{Sarkar} ends with:
``Characterize Toeplitz operators, which are power partial isometries.''  Here
$T$ is a \emph{power partial isometry} (PPI) when $T^m$ is a partial isometry
for every integer $m\geq1$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\caption{The complete closing question block in arXiv:2407.10609, current PDF
page 35.}
\end{figure}

The key input from the source is its classification of partially isometric
Toeplitz operators.  Apart from the trivial zero operator, such an operator
has a factorization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sourcefactor}
 T_\Phi=M_\Gamma M_\Psi^*,
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma,\Psi\in H^\infty_{\B(\mathcal F,\mathcal E)}(\D^n)$ are inner
and obey the source's necessary and sufficient Toeplitz-product condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sourcecondition}
 \bigl(\Gamma(\lambda)-\Gamma_k(\lambda)\bigr)
 \bigl(\Psi(\mu)-\Psi_k(\mu)\bigr)^*=0
\end{equation}
for every $\lambda,\mu\in\D^n$ and $k=1,\ldots,n$; here the subscript $k$
means that the $k$th variable is set equal to zero.  Equivalently, the
corresponding positive Fourier coefficients in the same variable have
orthogonal products.

\section{Full overlap classification}

Write $S$ for the unilateral shift, $J_q$ for the nilpotent truncated shift
of length $q$, and allow arbitrary Hilbert-space multiplicities in tensor
products below.

\begin{theorem}[Classification]\label{thm:classification}
Let $0\ne T_\Phi$ be a Toeplitz operator on
$H^2_{\mathcal E}(\D^n)$.  Then $T_\Phi$ is a power partial isometry if and
only if there are a Hilbert space $\mathcal F$ and inner functions
$\Gamma,\Psi\in H^\infty_{\B(\mathcal F,\mathcal E)}(\D^n)$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T_\Phi=M_\Gamma M_\Psi^*$ and \eqref{eq:sourcecondition} holds;
\item the overlap Toeplitz operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:overlap}
 C:=M_\Psi^*M_\Gamma=T_{\Psi^*\Gamma}
 \quad\hbox{on }H^2_{\mathcal F}(\D^n)
\end{equation}
is unitarily equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HW}
 U\ \oplus\ (S\otimes I_{\mathcal K_s})\ \oplus\
 (S^*\otimes I_{\mathcal K_b})\ \oplus\
 \bigoplus_{q\geq1}(J_q\otimes I_{\mathcal K_q}),
\end{equation}
where $U$ is unitary and any of the multiplicity spaces may be zero.
\end{enumerate}
The zero Toeplitz operator is a PPI.  If $T_\Phi=I_{H^2(\D^n)}\otimes A$ is
constant, the criterion reduces exactly to: $A$ has the decomposition
\eqref{eq:HW}, or equivalently $A$ is a PPI.
\end{theorem}

The criterion is independent of the chosen inner factorization: for every
factorization \eqref{eq:sourcefactor}, the associated overlap is a PPI exactly
when $T_\Phi$ is.  Thus nonuniqueness of $\Gamma,\Psi$ creates no ambiguity.

\begin{lemma}[Isometric outer factors reflect partial isometries]
\label{lem:outer}
Let $V,W:\mathcal K\to\mathcal H$ be isometries and
$A\in\B(\mathcal K)$.  Then $VAW^*$ is a partial isometry if and only if
$A$ is a partial isometry.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
One has
\[
 (VAW^*)^*(VAW^*)=W(A^*A)W^*.
\]
If $A^*A$ is a projection, so is the right side.  Conversely, if the right
side is a projection, compressing by $W^*$ and $W$ shows
$(A^*A)^2=A^*A$.  Since $A^*A\geq0$, this is equivalent to $A$ being a
partial isometry.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Power identity]\label{lem:powers}
For $T=M_\Gamma M_\Psi^*$ and $C=M_\Psi^*M_\Gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:powers}
 T^m=M_\Gamma C^{m-1}M_\Psi^*\qquad(m\geq1).
\end{equation}
Consequently, $T^m$ is a partial isometry if and only if $C^{m-1}$ is.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The formula is immediate for $m=1$ and induction inserts the middle product
$M_\Psi^*M_\Gamma=C$.  Both $M_\Gamma$ and $M_\Psi$ are isometries because
$\Gamma$ and $\Psi$ are inner.  Lemma~\ref{lem:outer} applies to
\eqref{eq:powers}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:classification}]
Suppose first that $T_\Phi$ is a nonzero PPI.  It is in particular a partial
isometry, so the source factorization theorem gives \eqref{eq:sourcefactor}
and \eqref{eq:sourcecondition}.  Lemma~\ref{lem:powers}, applied for all
$m\geq2$, says that every positive power of $C$ is a partial isometry.  The
Halmos--Wallen decomposition theorem \cite{HalmosWallen} gives precisely
\eqref{eq:HW}.

Conversely, \eqref{eq:sourcecondition} makes $M_\Gamma M_\Psi^*$ Toeplitz,
while innerness makes it a partial isometry.  Every summand in \eqref{eq:HW}
is a PPI, hence so is $C$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:powers} now makes every power of
$T_\Phi$ a partial isometry.

It remains only to justify the stated edge cases.  The zero operator is
immediate.  A constant symbol gives $T_\Phi=I\otimes A$ and
$T_\Phi^m=I\otimes A^m$, so partial-isometry status is preserved and
reflected term by term.  Alternatively, if $A$ is a partial isometry, take
$\mathcal F=(\ker A)^\perp$, let $\Psi:\mathcal F\hookrightarrow\mathcal E$
be inclusion, and let $\Gamma=A|_{\mathcal F}$.  These are constant
isometries and $A=\Gamma\Psi^*$, so the overlap proof also covers constants.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the overlap is again Toeplitz]
For $f,g\in H^2_{\mathcal F}(\D^n)$,
\[
 \langle M_\Psi^*M_\Gamma f,g\rangle
 =\int_{\T^n}\langle\Psi(\zeta)^*\Gamma(\zeta)f(\zeta),g(\zeta)\rangle
 \,d\zeta.
\]
Thus $C=T_{\Psi^*\Gamma}$, with no extra product hypothesis.  The
classification therefore stays entirely inside the Toeplitz category.
\end{remark}

\section{A direct analytic-symbol criterion}

The source also proves that an analytic multiplier $M_G$ is a partial
isometry exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:analyticPI}
 G(\zeta)^*G(\zeta)=P\quad\text{a.e. on }\T^n
\end{equation}
for one constant orthogonal projection $P$ on the coefficient space.  Indeed,
this is the boundary form of the source factorization $G=\Theta V^*$ with
$\Theta$ inner and $V$ a constant isometry.

\begin{corollary}[Analytic symbols]\label{cor:analytic}
For $\Phi\in H^\infty_{\B(\mathcal E)}(\D^n)$, the analytic Toeplitz
operator $M_\Phi$ is a power partial isometry if and only if, for every
$m\geq1$, there is a constant orthogonal projection $P_m$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:analyticcriterion}
 \Phi(\zeta)^{*m}\Phi(\zeta)^m=P_m
 \quad\text{for a.e. }\zeta\in\T^n.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since multiplication respects pointwise products,
$M_\Phi^m=M_{\Phi^m}$.  Apply \eqref{eq:analyticPI} to $G=\Phi^m$ for every
$m$.
\end{proof}

This is a symbol-only criterion.  It also shows why merely asking for
$\Phi(\zeta)$ to be a partial isometry a.e. is insufficient: the initial
projection in every power must be independent of the boundary point, and the
requirement must persist through all powers.

\section{Partial isometry does not imply PPI}

The operator-valued case differs sharply from the scalar case.

\begin{proposition}[A $2\times2$ nonconstant counterexample]
Let
\[
 A=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \Phi(z)=z_1A\quad(z\in\D^n).
\]
Then $\Phi(\zeta)$ is a partial isometry a.e., and $M_\Phi$ is a nonconstant
partially isometric Toeplitz operator, but $M_\Phi^2$ is not a partial
isometry.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $u=e_1$ and $v=(e_1+e_2)/\sqrt2$.  Then $A=uv^*$,
$A^*A=vv^*$, and $AA^*=uu^*$, so $A$ is a partial isometry.  But
\[
 A^2=\langle v,u\rangle A=\frac1{\sqrt2}A,
 \qquad (A^2)^*A^2=\frac12 vv^*,
\]
and the last operator is not a projection.  Under
$H^2_{\mathbb C^2}(\D^n)=H^2(\D^n)\otimes\mathbb C^2$,
$M_\Phi=M_{z_1}\otimes A$.  Hence it is a partial isometry, whereas its
square is $M_{z_1^2}\otimes A^2$ and is not.
\end{proof}

More generally, $z_1A$ is a PPI exactly when the coefficient partial
isometry $A$ is a PPI.  This embeds every Halmos--Wallen possibility, and
every obstruction, into a nonconstant analytic Toeplitz symbol.

\section{Focused proof and upgrade audits}

Eight focused checks were completed before promotion:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The current 36-page arXiv PDF was checked; Question IV remains verbatim
on page 35.
\item The source factorization was checked with domains and codomains written
explicitly, including a direct treatment of constant and zero symbols.
\item Formula \eqref{eq:powers} was checked inductively for all powers, rather
than inferred from the first few cases.
\item Lemma~\ref{lem:outer} was checked in both directions; no surjectivity of
the outer isometries is used.
\item The overlap identity $C=T_{\Psi^*\Gamma}$ was checked directly through
Hardy-space inner products.
\item The Halmos--Wallen theorem was applied only after the overlap was proved
to be a PPI; all unitary, shift, co-shift, and truncated-shift summands and
multiplicities are retained.
\item The analytic criterion was independently derived by applying the
source's analytic partial-isometry theorem to every pointwise power.
\item The $2\times2$ counterexample was verified exactly and by a small
symbolic check; its square has nonzero singular value $1/\sqrt2$ and hence is
not a partial isometry.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Novelty and review recommendation}

A bounded search on 13 August 2026 covered the exact wording of Question IV,
the source title and citations, overlap/compressed-inner-product formulations,
the scalar-valued classification \cite{DeepakPradhanSarkar}, the 2025 papers
of Babbar--Maji on PPIs and characteristic functions
\cite{BabbarMajiPPI,BabbarMajiCF}, and arXiv records through 2026.  The 2025
characteristic-function paper advances Question III in one variable but does
not state the overlap reduction or answer Question IV.  No later occurrence
of this classification was found.  Novelty is plausible, not certified, and
priority is not claimed.

The principal human-review point is conceptual: decide whether the overlap
canonical form is accepted as the requested ``characterization.''  It is a
necessary-and-sufficient parametrization with no unresolved mathematical
condition, because \eqref{eq:HW} is the complete Halmos--Wallen canonical
form.  The core new proof itself is the two-line power identity plus the fact
that isometric outer factors preserve and reflect partial-isometry status.
