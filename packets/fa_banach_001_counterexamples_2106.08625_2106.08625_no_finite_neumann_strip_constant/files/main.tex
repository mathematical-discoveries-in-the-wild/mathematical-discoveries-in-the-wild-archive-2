\section{Source estimate and open question}

The source is B.~Sorowen, \emph{Estimation of the number of negative
eigenvalues of magnetic Schrödinger operators in a strip},
arXiv:2106.08625v1 \cite{Sorowen}.  On PDF page~40 (printed page~31),
Theorem~3.2.1 asserts the following estimate on
$S=\R\times(0,d)$:
\[
 \Nminus(H_{B,V})\le
 \left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}'
 \frac{1}{\sqrt{16|k+\varphi|^2+1}}\right)
 \lVert V\rVert_X,
 \qquad X=L^1(\R;L^\infty(0,d)).
\]
The prime is described as neglecting summands below one.

\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figures/source_estimate_crop.png}
\end{center}

On PDF page~42 (printed page~33), Section~4.2 asks for the optimal constant in
this estimate.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The natural precise interpretation of that question is whether there is a
finite $C=C(d,\varphi)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:homogeneous}
 \Nminus(H^N_{A_\varphi,V})\le C\lVert V\rVert_X
 \quad\text{for every nonnegative }V\in X,
\end{equation}
where $H^N$ denotes the standard gauge-covariant magnetic Neumann
realization.  The result below disproves \eqref{eq:homogeneous} for every
$\varphi$, including the noninteger values singled out in the source.

\section{Definitions}

Put
\[
 S=\R\times(0,d),\qquad
 \theta(x,y)=\operatorname{Arg}(x+iy)\in(0,\pi),
\]
and use the source's Aharonov--Bohm potential
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
 A_\varphi(x,y)=\varphi\left(
 -\frac{y}{x^2+y^2},\frac{x}{x^2+y^2}\right)
 =\varphi\nabla\theta(x,y).
\end{equation}
The pole $(0,0)$ is not in $S$: it lies on its boundary.  Consequently,
$\theta$ is a globally single-valued smooth function on $S$.

The standard magnetic Neumann operator is associated with the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:form}
 q_{A_\varphi,V}[u]
 =\int_S|(-i\nabla+A_\varphi)u|^2
  -\int_SV|u|^2,
\end{equation}
with the gauge-transformed Neumann form domain.  Equivalently, if
\[
 (U_\varphi f)(x,y)=e^{-i\varphi\theta(x,y)}f(x,y),
\]
then $U_\varphi$ is unitary and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge}
 (-i\nabla+A_\varphi)U_\varphi f
 =U_\varphi(-i\nabla f),
 \qquad
 H^N_{A_\varphi,V}=U_\varphi(-\Delta_N-V)U_\varphi^{-1}.
\end{equation}
This is the gauge-covariant magnetic Neumann convention used in the waveguide
literature; compare Borisov--Ekholm--Kovařík \cite{BEK}.

\section{Proof intuition}

An Aharonov--Bohm flux has a spectral effect only when the accessible domain
retains nontrivial topology around its pole.  Here the pole is on the boundary
of an open, simply connected strip.  Formula \eqref{eq:gauge} therefore
removes the potential completely.  The Neumann strip has a zero-energy
transverse mode, so along that mode the problem is one-dimensional.  A long
tent function costs only $2/L$ in kinetic energy while a unit interval of
attractive potential gains $\varepsilon$.  Taking $L>2/\varepsilon$ produces
a bound state at arbitrarily small $X$-norm, which is incompatible with any
homogeneous counting bound.

\section{Counterexample}

\begin{theorem}[No finite best constant]\label{thm:counterexample}
Let $d>0$ and $\varphi\in\R$.  For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a bounded,
compactly supported, nonnegative $V_\varepsilon\in
L^1(\R;L^\infty(0,d))$ such that
\[
 \lVert V_\varepsilon\rVert_X=\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 \Nminus(H^N_{A_\varphi,V_\varepsilon})\ge1.
\]
Consequently, no finite constant $C(d,\varphi)$ can satisfy
\eqref{eq:homogeneous}; the optimal homogeneous constant asked for in the
source is infinite (equivalently, it does not exist as a finite constant).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and choose $L>2/\varepsilon$.  Let
\[
 I_L=[L+1,L+2],\qquad
 V_\varepsilon(x,y)=\varepsilon\mathbf 1_{I_L}(x).
\]
Since $I_L$ has length one,
\[
 \lVert V_\varepsilon\rVert_X
 =\int_\R\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{0<y<d}
   V_\varepsilon(x,y)\,dx=\varepsilon.
\]
Define the longitudinal tent function
\[
 g_L(x)=
 \begin{cases}
  (x-1)/L,&1\le x\le L+1,\\
  1,&L+1\le x\le L+2,\\
  (2L+2-x)/L,&L+2\le x\le2L+2,\\
  0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
It belongs to $H^1(\R)$, is supported away from the boundary pole, and obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tent}
 \int_\R|g_L'(x)|^2\,dx=\frac2L,
 \qquad
 \int_{I_L}|g_L(x)|^2\,dx=1.
\end{equation}
Use the constant transverse Neumann mode and undo the gauge:
\[
 u_L(x,y)=e^{-i\varphi\theta(x,y)}\frac{g_L(x)}{\sqrt d}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:gauge} and \eqref{eq:tent}, its exact form value is
\begin{align*}
 q_{A_\varphi,V_\varepsilon}[u_L]
 &=\int_\R|g_L'|^2
   -\varepsilon\int_{I_L}|g_L|^2\\
 &=\frac2L-\varepsilon<0.
\end{align*}
The unperturbed operator is unitarily equivalent to the Neumann Laplacian on
the strip and hence has essential spectrum $[0,\infty)$.  The bounded,
compactly supported potential is a relatively compact form perturbation, so
the essential spectrum remains $[0,\infty)$.  The min--max principle now
turns the negative trial value into a discrete negative eigenvalue.  Thus
$\Nminus\ge1$.

If a finite $C$ in \eqref{eq:homogeneous} existed, choose
$0<\varepsilon<1/C$ (with the conclusion immediate when $C=0$).  Then
\[
 1\le\Nminus(H^N_{A_\varphi,V_\varepsilon})
 \le C\varepsilon<1,
\]
a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and diagnosis of the source estimate}

The counterexample is an exact form computation.  Its four checkable inputs
are: $A_\varphi=\varphi\nabla\theta$ on the whole open strip; the gauge identity
\eqref{eq:gauge}; $\int|g_L'|^2=2/L$; and
$\int V_\varepsilon|g_L|^2=\varepsilon$.  No numerical or asymptotic step is
used.

The same geometry diagnoses the source derivation.  The claimed magnetic
improvement depends on a noninteger flux, but no closed curve in $S$ winds
around the boundary pole.  Therefore the flux is gauge-trivial on this
domain.  The source's transverse decomposition into modes indexed by
$k\in\mathbb Z$ is also incompatible with the constant transverse Neumann
ground mode after the global gauge removal.  Irrespective of how the primed
sum in Theorem~3.2.1 is interpreted, Theorem~\ref{thm:counterexample} rules out
every finite coefficient multiplying $\lVert V\rVert_X$ alone.

\section{Novelty check, limitations, and reviewer focus}

A bounded search was performed on 13 August 2026.  The run's registry,
solution, attempt, and proof-gap indexes were searched for arXiv:2106.08625,
the exact title, ``negative eigenvalues magnetic strip,'' and Aharonov--Bohm
Neumann terms.  Web and arXiv searches used the exact title and id, the exact
``best constant'' phrase, and combinations of ``magnetic Schrödinger,''
``strip,'' ``Neumann,'' and ``Aharonov--Bohm.''  They found the thesis,
conference abstracts repeating its estimate, standard waveguide literature
such as \cite{BEK}, and later work on poles approaching a boundary, but no
paper correcting this estimate or answering its optimal-constant question.
This is a bounded search, not a proof of novelty.

The conclusion concerns the standard gauge-covariant magnetic Neumann
realization.  If ``Neumann'' were instead intended to mean the ordinary,
non-gauge-covariant condition $\partial_\nu u=0$ while $A\cdot\nu\ne0$, that is
not the standard magnetic Neumann problem and the source does not give a
precise self-adjoint realization; the present packet makes no claim for that
different boundary problem.  The packet also does not address a pole lying
strictly inside the strip, a punctured or multiply connected waveguide,
Dirichlet/mixed boundary conditions, curved guides, or estimates with an
additive term or stronger norms.

Review should focus on the domain/gauge convention at the boundary pole and
on the relative-compactness step for the bounded compactly supported
potential.  The trial support lies in $\{x\ge1\}$, so it avoids every local
domain subtlety at $(0,0)$.
