\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Candidate substantial partial result, likely valid, pending human review.
For every discrete group, the finite-dimensional part of an arbitrary
sub-semidual is automatically closed under conjugation.  Consequently the
source's Open Problem~3 has an affirmative answer whenever the associated
quotient group $C^*$-algebra is residually finite-dimensional (RFD).  The same
gives RFD-qualified affirmative cases of Problems~3' and~2.  No unrestricted
answer is claimed.

\section{Source questions and setup}

Lau and Pham ask whether every weak$^*$-closed translation-invariant
subalgebra of $B(G)$ is conjugation-closed when $G$ is discrete; equivalently,
whether every sub-semidual of a discrete group is a subdual
\cite[Open Problem 3, p.~34]{LauPham}.  They also formulate the locally compact
Eberlein version as Open Problem~3' and note that an affirmative answer would
resolve their Open Problem~2.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Let $S\subseteq\widehatG$ be a sub-semidual in the source's sense.  Thus $S$ is
Fell closed,
\[
 \operatorname{supp}\!\left(\bigoplus_{\pi\in S}\pi\right)\subseteq S,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{supp}(\pi\otimes\rho)\subseteq S
 \quad(\pi,\rho\in S).
\]
Write
\[
 U_S=\bigoplus_{\pi\in S}\pi,\qquad
 I_S=\ker U_S,\qquad
 A_S=C^*(G)/I_S.
\]
Representations of $G$ that factor through $A_S$ are precisely those weakly
contained in $U_S$.  In particular, every irreducible representation factoring
through $A_S$ belongs to $S$.

\section{Proof intuition}

Finite-dimensional representations carry their own duals algebraically.  If
$\pi$ has dimension $d$, then $\det\pi$ occurs in $\pi^{\otimes d}$ and
\[
 \overline\pi\cong \Lambda^{d-1}\pi\otimes(\det\pi)^{-1}.
\]
Only the inverse determinant character appears to be missing.  But the positive
powers of any character form a semigroup in a compact group, and the closure of
that semigroup contains its inverse.  Fell closedness therefore supplies
$(\det\pi)^{-1}$.

If $A_S$ is RFD, its defining ideal is the intersection of kernels of its
finite-dimensional representations.  The preceding argument makes this family
of kernels invariant under group inversion.  Hence inversion descends to
$A_S$, after which every representation factoring through $A_S$ brings its
conjugate with it.  This upgrades a statement about individual finite-dimensional
representations to all of $S$.

\section{The finite-dimensional theorem}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:recurrence}
Let $\chi:G\to\T$ be a character of a discrete group.  In the Fell topology on
$\widehatG$, $\chi^{-1}$ belongs to the closure of
$\{\chi^n:n\geq1\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a finite set $F=\{g_1,\ldots,g_m\}\subseteq G$, put
$z=(\chi(g_1),\ldots,\chi(g_m))\in\T^m$.  The closure of the positive powers of
$z$ is a compact subgroup of $\T^m$; in particular, it contains $z^{-1}$.
Equivalently, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $n\geq1$ such that
\[
 \max_{g\in F}|\chi(g)^n-\chi(g)^{-1}|<\varepsilon.
\]
Ordering the pairs $(F,\varepsilon)$ by refinement gives a net of positive
powers converging pointwise on $G$ to $\chi^{-1}$.  For one-dimensional
representations of a discrete group, this is Fell convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:finite}
Let $G$ be any discrete group and $S\subseteq\widehatG$ any sub-semidual.  If
$\pi\in S$ is finite-dimensional, then $\overline\pi\in S$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $d=\dim\pi$ and $\chi=\det\pi$.  The character $\chi$ is the exterior-power
summand $\Lambda^d\pi$ of $\pi^{\otimes d}$.  Tensor closure of $S$ therefore
gives $\chi\in S$, and then $\chi^n\in S$ for every $n\geq1$.  Lemma
\ref{lem:recurrence} and Fell closedness give $\chi^{-1}\in S$.

Likewise $\Lambda^{d-1}\pi$ is a subrepresentation of
$\pi^{\otimes(d-1)}$, so its irreducible support lies in $S$.  The canonical
finite-dimensional duality isomorphism
\[
 \overline\pi\cong\pi^*
 \cong \Lambda^{d-1}\pi\otimes\chi^{-1}
\]
and one final application of tensor closure show that $\overline\pi\in S$.
For $d=1$, the same conclusion is already Lemma~\ref{lem:recurrence}.
\end{proof}

\section{The RFD upgrade}

Let $\vartheta:C^*(G)\to C^*(G)$ be the canonical linear involutive
$*$-anti-automorphism determined by
$\vartheta(u_g)=u_{g^{-1}}$.  If $\rho$ is a unitary representation of $G$ and
$J:H_\rho\to\overline{H_\rho}$ is the canonical antiunitary, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conjugate}
 \overline\rho(a)=J\rho\!\left(\vartheta(a^*)\right)J^{-1}
 \qquad(a\in C^*(G)).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rfd}
Let $G$ be a discrete group and $S$ a sub-semidual.  If
$A_S=C^*(G)/I_S$ is residually finite-dimensional, then $S$ is a subdual.
Equivalently, $B_S(G)$ is conjugation-closed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal F$ be the family of finite-dimensional representations of $G$
that factor through $A_S$.  Decomposing any member of $\mathcal F$ into
irreducibles and applying Theorem~\ref{thm:finite} shows that $\mathcal F$ is
closed under $\rho\mapsto\overline\rho$.

Residual finite-dimensionality says exactly that
\[
 I_S=\bigcap_{\rho\in\mathcal F}\ker\rho.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:conjugate} and conjugation stability of $\mathcal F$ now give
$\vartheta(I_S)=I_S$.  Hence $\vartheta$ descends to a bounded
$*$-anti-automorphism of $A_S$.

Let $\pi\in S$.  Since $I_S\subseteq\ker\pi$, equation~\eqref{eq:conjugate}
and $\vartheta(I_S)=I_S$ imply $I_S\subseteq\ker\overline\pi$.  Thus
$\overline\pi$ factors through $A_S$, or equivalently
$\overline\pi\prec U_S$.  The defining support condition for a sub-semidual
then yields $\overline\pi\in S$.  This is precisely the missing axiom for $S$
to be a subdual.  The coefficient-space formulation gives conjugation closure
of $B_S(G)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:local}
Let $G$ be locally compact and let
$B=B_S^c(G)=B_S(G_d)\cap C(G)$ be a closed translation-invariant Eberlein
subalgebra as in Theorem~3.11 of the source.  If $C_S^*(G_d)$ is RFD, then $B$
is conjugation-closed.  If moreover $B$ separates points, then
$A(G)\subseteq B$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:rfd}, applied to the discrete group $G_d$, makes $S$ a
subdual.  Conjugation closure survives intersection with $C(G)$.  In the
point-separating case $S$ is thick, and Theorem~7.1 of the source gives
$B_r(G)\subseteq B$; hence $A(G)\subseteq B$.
\end{proof}

In particular, Open Problems~3 and~3' are affirmative for all associated
RFD quotients, and Problem~2 is affirmative in the RFD-associated subclass.
The conclusion includes all finite-dimensional and subhomogeneous quotients,
and more generally every quotient separated by finite-dimensional
representations.

\section{Why the apparent antipode proof is not a full solution}

Lemma~3.13 of the source descends the group comultiplication to $A_S$, and its
Lemma~3.14 observes that for discrete $G$ the resulting object satisfies the
compact-quantum-group cancellation laws.  It is tempting to invoke a bounded
unitary antipode $R$ with $R(q(u_g))=q(u_{g^{-1}})$ and conclude immediately.

That step is exactly the unresolved point for an arbitrary exotic completion.
On the dense Hopf $*$-algebra the algebraic coinverse exists, while Woronowicz's
bounded polar-decomposition result is stated for the faithful-Haar form
\cite[Theorem 1.6]{Woronowicz}.  In the present cocommutative quotient, a bounded
$*$-anti-automorphism with the displayed generator formula exists if and only if
$\vartheta(I_S)=I_S$; by~\eqref{eq:conjugate}, this is equivalent to the desired
conjugation closure.  Invoking its descent without a separate norm argument is
therefore circular.  The RFD hypothesis in Theorem~\ref{thm:rfd} supplies that
missing norm argument by a separating family of finite-dimensional quotients.

\section{Upgrade attempts, scope, and novelty}

The initial full-solution attempt used the unitary antipode of the quotient
compact quantum group.  A deeper audit isolated the completion issue above and
prevented a false promotion.  A second route through determinant recurrence
proved Theorem~\ref{thm:finite}; the RFD kernel-intersection argument then
upgraded it to Theorem~\ref{thm:rfd}.  Attempts to obtain a counterexample from
one-sided tensor powers of a finite-dimensional representation collapse by
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite}.  A possible infinite-dimensional route through
one-sided tensor families (for example holomorphic versus antiholomorphic
representations) remains obstructed by the need to control weak containment
after passing to the underlying discrete group.

The result does not prove that every $A_S$ is RFD, nor that inversion is bounded
on a general exotic completion.  Thus it does not settle any of the three source
problems without the stated RFD qualification.
