\section{Source question}

Petrov, Torr, and Bibi prove in \emph{Prompting a Pretrained Transformer Can Be
a Universal Approximator}, arXiv:2402.14753 [1], that a specially constructed
fixed attention head is universal under prefix-tuning.  For Lipschitz maps on
$\Sph^m$ their construction uses
\[
 N=O\!\left(\epsilon^{-10-14m-4m^2}\right)
\]
prefix positions, whereas they quote $O(\epsilon^{-m})$ trainable parameters
as the usual benchmark for trained networks.  They ask whether a tighter
Jackson bound exists, or whether prefix-tuning and prompting inherently lose
asymptotic efficiency.

\centering

\end{figure}

We prove that the tighter bound exists in the same idealized fixed-head model.
The proof is direct: use a geodesic net as the keys, sampled target values as
the values, and make softmax localize near a closest net point.  This avoids
both the source's spherical convolution and its uniform Riemann-sum
approximation.

\section{Model and statement}

Let $\rho(x,y)=\arccos\ip{x}{y}$ be geodesic distance on the unit sphere
$\Sph^m\subset\R^{m+1}$.  Write $A_j$ for the surface area of $\Sph^j$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Cm}
 C_m=\frac{2m\pi^{m-1}A_m}{A_{m-1}}.
\end{equation}
For a sequence $X=(x_1,\ldots,x_T)$ and prefix
$P=(p_1,\ldots,p_N)$, the source's classical attention head is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:head}
 [u(X;P)]_k=
 \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N e^{x_k^\top Hp_i}W_Vp_i+
       \sum_{j=1}^T e^{x_k^\top Hx_j}W_Vx_j}
      {\sum_{i=1}^N e^{x_k^\top Hp_i}+
       \sum_{j=1}^T e^{x_k^\top Hx_j}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Benchmark-rate fixed-head Jackson bound]\label{thm:main}
Fix $m\ge1$ and $T\ge1$.  There are matrices
$H,W_V\in\R^{3(m+1)\times3(m+1)}$, independent of the target, accuracy,
prefix length, and input sequence, with the following property.

Let $f:\Sph^m\to\R^{m+1}$ satisfy
\[
 \norm{f(x)-f(y)}_2\le L\rho(x,y),\qquad x,y\in\Sph^m,
\]
where $L>0$, and put $B=\norm{f}_\infty$.  For every $\epsilon>0$ define
\[
 \delta=\min\left\{\frac12,\frac{\epsilon}{6L}\right\}.
\]
There is a prefix of length
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Nbound}
 N\le C_m\delta^{-m}
\end{equation}
such that, after the fixed embedding
$\Pi x=(x,0,0)\in\R^{3(m+1)}$ and projection onto the first block,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform}
 \sup_{(x_1,\ldots,x_T)\in(\Sph^m)^T}
 \max_{1\le k\le T}
 \norm{\Pi^{-1}[u(\Pi X;P)]_k-f(x_k)}_2\le\epsilon.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for fixed $m,L,T$ and $f$,
\[
 N=O_m(\epsilon^{-m})\qquad(\epsilon\downarrow0).
\]
Each prefix position has $3(m+1)$ coordinates, so the number of trainable
prefix coordinates has the same $\epsilon$-exponent $m$.
\end{theorem}

If $L=0$, the map is constant.  It is represented exactly with one prefix:
take zero key, value $(T+1)f$, and note that all $T+1$ attention scores are
zero.  Thus this harmless case is omitted from the quantitative statement.

\section{A small intrinsic net}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:net}
For $0<\delta\le1/2$, the sphere has a geodesic $\delta$-net
$\{b_1,\ldots,b_N\}$ satisfying \eqref{eq:Nbound}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a maximal geodesic $\delta$-separated set.  Maximality makes it a
$\delta$-net, while its open caps of radius $\delta/2$ are disjoint.  A cap of
radius $\delta/2$ has area
\[
 A_{m-1}\int_0^{\delta/2}\sin^{m-1}t\,dt.
\]
Since $\sin t\ge2t/\pi$ on $[0,\pi/2]$, this is at least
\[
 A_{m-1}\left(\frac2\pi\right)^{m-1}
 \frac{(\delta/2)^m}{m}
 =\frac{A_{m-1}\delta^m}{2m\pi^{m-1}}.
\]
The disjoint caps fit inside a sphere of area $A_m$.  Rearrangement gives
$N\le C_m\delta^{-m}$.
\end{proof}

The exponent is $m$, the intrinsic dimension of the domain, rather than the
ambient dimension $m+1$.

\section{Softmax is a local interpolant}

Fix the net in Lemma~\ref{lem:net}.  For $\lambda>0$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g}
 g(x)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^N e^{\lambda\ip{x}{b_i}}f(b_i)}
 {\sum_{i=1}^N e^{\lambda\ip{x}{b_i}}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local}
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda-local}
 \lambda\ge 2\delta^{-2}\log\left(\frac{\pi N}{\delta}\right),
\end{equation}
then $\sup_{x\in\Sph^m}\norm{g(x)-f(x)}_2\le3L\delta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x$ and choose $b_j$ with $\rho(x,b_j)\le\delta$.  A net point with
$\rho(x,b_i)\ge2\delta$ has score at most $\lambda\cos(2\delta)$, whereas the
denominator in \eqref{eq:g} is at least $e^{\lambda\cos\delta}$.  Moreover,
\begin{align*}
 \cos\delta-\cos(2\delta)
 &=2\sin(3\delta/2)\sin(\delta/2)\\
 &\ge \frac{6}{\pi^2}\delta^2\ge\frac12\delta^2,
\end{align*}
where both sine arguments lie in $[0,\pi/2]$.  Hence the total softmax mass
$q_x$ assigned outside the $2\delta$ cap obeys
\[
 q_x\le N e^{-\lambda\delta^2/2}\le\frac{\delta}{\pi}.
\]
Inside the cap, Lipschitzness gives error at most $2L\delta$; everywhere on
the sphere it gives error at most $\pi L$.  Since \eqref{eq:g} is a convex
combination,
\[
 \norm{g(x)-f(x)}_2
 \le2L\delta+\pi Lq_x\le3L\delta.
\]
The estimate is uniform in $x$.
\end{proof}

\section{Realization by one finite fixed head}

Use three blocks of size $m+1$ and fix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrices}
 H=\begin{bmatrix}0&I&0\\0&0&0\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad
 W_V=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&I\\0&0&0\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For each net point put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix}
 p_i=\begin{bmatrix}0\\ \lambda b_i\\ f(b_i)\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Then, for every $x_k$, the projected prefix score is
$\lambda\ip{x_k}{b_i}$ and the projected prefix value is $f(b_i)$.  Every
embedded input token has score zero and projected value zero.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-head}
 \Pi^{-1}[u(\Pi X;P)]_k
 =\frac{\sum_i e^{\lambda\ip{x_k}{b_i}}f(b_i)}
        {\sum_i e^{\lambda\ip{x_k}{b_i}}+T}.
\end{equation}
This identity uses ordinary finite matrices; no limit is hidden in it.

Let $D(x)=\sum_i e^{\lambda\ip{x}{b_i}}$.  Since some $b_j$ is within
$\delta$ of $x$, $D(x)\ge e^{\lambda\cos\delta}$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:g} and
the convexity bound on its numerator give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:context-error}
 \norm{\Pi^{-1}u(\Pi X;P)_k-g(x_k)}_2
 \le \frac{BT}{D(x_k)}
 \le BT e^{-\lambda\cos\delta}.
\end{equation}
Choose $\lambda$ satisfying \eqref{eq:lambda-local} and, in addition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda-context}
 \lambda\ge \frac{1}{\cos\delta}
 \max\left\{0,\log\left(\frac{2BT}{\epsilon}\right)\right\},
\end{equation}
with the second term read as zero when $B=0$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:local}, the
definition of $\delta$, and \eqref{eq:context-error} now give
\[
 \norm{\Pi^{-1}u(\Pi X;P)_k-f(x_k)}_2
 \le3L\delta+\frac\epsilon2\le\epsilon.
\]
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\paragraph{Arbitrary continuous targets.}
The same proof also removes the source's harmonic-coefficient hypothesis and
restriction $m\ge8$.  If $f$ is merely continuous, let
$\omega_f(r)=\sup_{\rho(x,y)\le r}\norm{f(x)-f(y)}_2$.  The near contribution
in Lemma~\ref{lem:local} is $\omega_f(2\delta)$ and the far contribution is at
most $2Bq_x$.  First choose $\delta$ from the modulus and then $\lambda$; this
gives density for every $m\ge1$ with the same finite head.  Lipschitzness is
needed only to turn this into the explicit Jackson rate \eqref{eq:Nbound}.

For fixed $m$, the number of trainable prefix coordinates is a constant
multiple of $N$, hence $O(\epsilon^{-m})$.  This answers the affirmative
branch of the source question: the much larger exponent is an artefact of the
particular proof, not an inherent prefix-length penalty in its constructed
fixed-head model.  Via the source's reduction from prefix-tuning to soft
prompting, the same conclusion applies to that idealized soft-prompt setting.

There is also a small repair to the source's conversion.  Its proof of exact
split-to-classical realization takes a block constant $M$ ``tending to
$-\infty$''.  A finite $M$ does not erase the input denominator exactly.
Equations~\eqref{eq:matrices}--\eqref{eq:lambda-context} instead retain those
terms, calculate them exactly, and absorb them without increasing $N$.

\section{Novelty check and limitations}

A bounded search through 11 August 2026 found two close later results.  Hu et
al. [2] prove prompt-tuning universality for a one-head, one-attention-layer
transformer, but use quantization, additional feed-forward layers, and a long
prompt indexing a discretized function class; they do not give this uniform
intrinsic-dimensional prefix Jackson bound.  Liu et al. [3, Theorem A.1] give
an $O(dnN_X)$ covering-number parameter bound, but their target-dependent
parameters occur in a trainable linear layer and trainable attention matrices,
not solely in prefixes of a frozen head.  Exact-phrase, title-citation, and
architecture-keyword searches found no later proof of the statement here.

The theorem concerns the specially designed frozen head in [1], not an
arbitrary transformer obtained from real-world pretraining.  Prefix magnitudes
are not bounded: \eqref{eq:lambda-local} permits
$\lambda=O(\epsilon^{-2}\log(1/\epsilon))$.  The result counts real trainable
coordinates, as the source question does, and does not measure their bit
complexity or optimization difficulty.  It also supplies no minimax lower
bound for the complete unbounded-prefix hypothesis class.  Thus the rate is
called \emph{benchmark-rate}, not formally minimax-optimal.
