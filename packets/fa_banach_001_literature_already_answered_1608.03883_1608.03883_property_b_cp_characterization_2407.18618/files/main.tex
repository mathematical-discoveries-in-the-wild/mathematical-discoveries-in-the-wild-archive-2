\noindent\textbf{Status.} \texttt{literature\_already\_answered}.

\section*{Original question}
Krupski and Marciszewski ask in Problem 5.10 on PDF page 12 of
\emph{On the weak and pointwise topologies in function spaces II}
\cite{KM2017}:
\begin{quote}
Characterize the property (B) of $\Cp(X)$ in terms of the topology of a
Tychonoff space $X$.
\end{quote}
A space has property (B) when it admits a countable family of closed nowhere
dense sets which absorbs every compact subset.

\section*{Explicit supporting answer}
Krupski, Kucharski, and Marciszewski explicitly cite the source as
``[4, Problem 5.10]'', say that they settle the question, and prove the
following as Theorem 1.1 on PDF page 2 of \cite{KKM2024}:
\begin{quote}
For every Tychonoff space $X$, the space $\Cp(X)$ fails property (B) if and
only if $X$ has property $(\kappa)$.
\end{quote}
Here property $(\kappa)$ means that every pairwise disjoint sequence of finite
subsets of $X$ has an infinite subsequence with a point-finite open expansion.
Thus the requested characterization is exact:
\[
  \Cp(X)\text{ has property (B)}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  X\text{ fails property }(\kappa).
\]
The supporting authors recognize the identification themselves, so this is an
explicit later literature answer rather than a new implication found in the
present run.

\section*{Scope limitation}
The result settles Problem 5.10 only.  It does not settle the source paper's
Problems 1.1 and 1.2 concerning homeomorphisms between $\Cp(K)$ and $C_w(L)$,
or the concrete Hilbert-cube and Cech--Stone cases listed in Section 4.
