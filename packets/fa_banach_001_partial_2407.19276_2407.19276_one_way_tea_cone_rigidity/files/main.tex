\section{Source question and exact scope}

Let $\F$ be $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$, and let $\Lambda$ be an infinite
index set.  Vectors $x,y$ in a normed space form a
\emph{triangle-equality-attaining pair}, abbreviated TEA, if
\[
 \|x+y\|=\|x\|+\|y\|.
\]
Li, Tsai, Wang and Wong characterize preservers of TEA and norm-parallel
pairs on finite-dimensional $\ell_p$ spaces and obtain infinite-dimensional
results under bidirectional preservation hypotheses~\cite{LTWW24}.  Their
Problem~3.7 asks:
\begin{quote}
Can one replace the two direction preserver conditions by that the linear map
$T$ sends parallel/TEA pairs to parallel/TEA pairs in Theorem~3.4 and
Corollary~3.6, as in the finite dimensional case?
\end{quote}
The question occurs on page 15 of the arXiv version:
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}
The authors' expanded final manuscript retains it as Problem~3.10, with the
corresponding results numbered Theorem~3.7 and Corollary~3.9~\cite{LTWW26}.

This packet settles both TEA questions affirmatively and more strongly: the
map need not be bounded, and in the $\ell_\infty$ case it need not be
bijective.  The slash in the source also poses a distinct parallel-pair
question.  Its literal $c_0$ formulation is false because every rank-one map
has parallel range.  The remaining rank-greater-than-one or bijective
parallel question is outside the claim here.  For that reason the packet is
classified as a substantial partial solution to the combined source problem,
although the TEA branch itself is completely solved.

\section{Idea of the proof}

First show that a nonzero one-way TEA preserver is injective.  The pullback
\[
 q(x):=\|Tx\|_\infty
\]
is then a genuine norm, possibly unrelated a priori to the original topology.
The preservation hypothesis becomes a statement purely about two norms:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pullback}
 \|x+y\|_\infty=\|x\|_\infty+\|y\|_\infty
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 q(x+y)=q(x)+q(y).
\end{equation}

Fix a point at which functions attain their sup norm, and rotate values so
that the value there is nonnegative real.  All functions normalized in this
way form a convex cone.  Equation~\eqref{eq:pullback} says that $q$ is
additive on each such cone, hence extends to a real-linear functional on the
real span of the cone.  Comparing the extensions at two points, first with
phase $1$ and then with phase $-1$, kills every possible ``tail'' functional.
A third point forces the cone functional to be a scalar multiple of point
evaluation.  The scalar is independent of the point, so
$q=\gamma\|\cdot\|_\infty$.

The decompositions are exact and algebraic.  Thus no continuity assumption,
finite-support approximation, or closed-graph argument is hidden in the
proof.

\section{A kernel lemma}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:kernel}
Let $X=c_0(\Lambda)$ or $X=\ell_\infty(\Lambda)$, where $\Lambda$ is
infinite.  If a nonzero linear map $T:X\to X$ sends TEA pairs to TEA pairs,
then $T$ is injective.  No boundedness is required.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $0\ne v\in\ker T$ and normalize $\|v\|_\infty=1$.  First assume
that $|v_\alpha|<1$ for some $\alpha\in\Lambda$.  Choose $r>0$ so large that
$r|v_\alpha|+1<r$.  The two vectors
\[
 rv+e_\alpha,\qquad rv-e_\alpha
\]
both have norm $r$, while their sum has norm $2r$; hence they form a TEA
pair.  Their images are $Te_\alpha$ and $-Te_\alpha$.  Opposite vectors can
form a TEA pair only when they vanish, so $Te_\alpha=0$.

Now take any $x\in X$ with $x_\alpha=0$ and choose $r>\|x\|_\infty$.
The vectors $re_\alpha+x$ and $re_\alpha-x$ form a TEA pair.  Their images
are $Tx$ and $-Tx$, so $Tx=0$.  Decomposing an arbitrary vector into its
$\alpha$-coordinate and a vector with zero $\alpha$-coordinate gives $T=0$,
a contradiction.

For $c_0(\Lambda)$ the first case always occurs.  It remains to treat an
$\ell_\infty(\Lambda)$ kernel vector with $|v_\alpha|=1$ for every $\alpha$.
After precomposing with a diagonal surjective isometry, we may assume that
$v=\mathbf 1$, so $T\mathbf 1=0$.  For distinct $\alpha,\beta$, the vectors
\[
 \mathbf 1-e_\beta,\qquad e_\alpha+e_\beta
\]
form a TEA pair.  Applying $T$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel1}
 \|Te_\alpha\|_\infty
 =\|Te_\beta\|_\infty+\|Te_\alpha+Te_\beta\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
Interchanging $\alpha$ and $\beta$ yields the same identity with the first
two norms exchanged.  Hence $Te_\alpha+Te_\beta=0$.  Using three distinct
indices shows $Te_\alpha=0$ for every $\alpha$.  The preceding
$re_\alpha\pm x$ argument, now for arbitrary $x$ with $x_\alpha=0$, again
gives $T=0$.  This contradiction proves injectivity.
\end{proof}

\section{Rigidity on \texorpdfstring{$c_0(\Lambda)$}{c0(Lambda)}}

All linear functionals in the next proof are real-linear.  This is essential
over $\mathbb C$, where the norm-attaining cone spans a real hyperplane.

\begin{lemma}[$c_0$ cone rigidity]\label{lem:c0}
Let $q$ be any norm on the vector space $c_0(\Lambda)$, with no topological
relation to $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ assumed.  Suppose~\eqref{eq:pullback} holds for
all $x,y\in c_0(\Lambda)$.  Then there is $\gamma>0$ such that
\[
 q(x)=\gamma\|x\|_\infty\qquad(x\in c_0(\Lambda)).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $i\in\Lambda$, set
\[
 C_i=\{x:x_i=\|x\|_\infty\ge0\},\qquad
 H_i=\{x:x_i\in\mathbb R\}.
\]
If $u,v\in C_i$, then $u,v$ form a TEA pair and $u+v\in C_i$.
Consequently $q|_{C_i}$ is additive and positively homogeneous.
Furthermore $C_i-C_i=H_i$.  Indeed, if $d_i=0$ and
$R>\|d\|_\infty$, then
\[
 d=Re_i-(Re_i-d),
\]
and both terms on the right belong to $C_i$; real multiples of $e_i$ are
also differences of elements of $C_i$.  Hence $q|_{C_i}$ extends uniquely to
a real-linear functional $\Phi_i$ on $H_i$.

Fix $j\ne i$ and let $d_i=d_j=0$.  For any unimodular scalar
$\zeta\in\Torus$ (only $\zeta=\pm1$ is needed), choose
$R>\|d\|_\infty$ and put
\[
 a=R(e_i+\zeta e_j),\qquad b=a-d.
\]
Both $a,b$ lie in $C_i$, while $\overline\zeta a,\overline\zeta b$ lie in
$C_j$.  Since $d=a-b$ and norms are invariant under unimodular scalars,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phasecompare}
 \Phi_i(d)=\Phi_j(\overline\zeta d).
\end{equation}
Taking $\zeta=1$ and $\zeta=-1$ gives
$\Phi_i(d)=\Phi_j(d)=-\Phi_j(d)$, and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tailzero}
 \Phi_i(d)=0\qquad\text{whenever }d_i=d_j=0.
\end{equation}

Choose distinct $j,k\ne i$.  If $d_i=0$, write
\[
 d=(d-d_je_j)+d_je_j.
\]
The first summand vanishes at $i,j$, and the second at $i,k$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:tailzero} shows $\Phi_i(d)=0$.  Thus
\[
 \Phi_i(x)=\gamma_i x_i\quad(x\in H_i),
 \qquad \gamma_i=q(e_i)>0.
\]
Since $e_i+e_j\in C_i\cap C_j$, evaluating $q(e_i+e_j)$ in the two cones
gives $\gamma_i=\gamma_j$.  Write the common value as $\gamma$.

Every nonzero $x\in c_0(\Lambda)$ attains its norm at some coordinate $i$.
Choose $\zeta\in\Torus$ such that $(\zeta x)_i=\|x\|_\infty$.  Then
$\zeta x\in C_i$, and
\[
 q(x)=q(\zeta x)=\Phi_i(\zeta x)
 =\gamma\|x\|_\infty.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Rigidity on \texorpdfstring{$C(K)$ and $\ell_\infty(\Lambda)$}{C(K) and ell-infinity(Lambda)}}

\begin{lemma}[$C(K)$ cone rigidity]\label{lem:CK}
Let $K$ be a compact Hausdorff space with at least three points, and let $q$
be any norm on the vector space $C(K,\F)$.  If~\eqref{eq:pullback} holds for
the sup norm, then $q=\gamma\|\cdot\|_\infty$ for some $\gamma>0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $t\in K$, define
\[
 C_t=\{f:f(t)=\|f\|_\infty\ge0\},\qquad
 H_t=\{f:f(t)\in\mathbb R\}.
\]
As before, $q$ is additive and positively homogeneous on $C_t$.  Also
$C_t-C_t=H_t$.  To see the only nontrivial part, let $d(t)=0$ and choose
$R>\|d\|_\infty$.  With $\mathbf1$ the constant-one function, put
\[
 v=(R-|d|)\mathbf1,\qquad u=v-d.
\]
Both $u$ and $v$ have norm $R$ and value $R$ at $t$, and $d=v-u$.
Together with differences of nonnegative constants, this proves the span
claim.  Thus $q|_{C_t}$ extends uniquely to a real-linear functional
$\Phi_t$ on $H_t$.

Let $s\ne t$ and suppose $d(t)=d(s)=0$.  For
$\zeta\in\Torus$, Urysohn's lemma gives a continuous
$h_\zeta:K\to\{z:|z|\le1\}$ such that
$h_\zeta(t)=1$ and $h_\zeta(s)=\zeta$: if
$\rho:K\to[0,1]$ has $\rho(t)=0,\rho(s)=1$, take
$h_\zeta=(1-\rho)+\rho\zeta$.
For $R>\|d\|_\infty$, set
\[
 v=(R-|d|)h_\zeta,\qquad u=v-d.
\]
Both functions have norm $R$; they lie in $C_t$, and multiplication by
$\overline\zeta$ puts both in $C_s$.  Since $d=v-u$, the comparison used in
\eqref{eq:phasecompare} gives
\[
 \Phi_t(d)=\Phi_s(\overline\zeta d).
\]
Again $\zeta=1,-1$ imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CKtail}
 \Phi_t(d)=0\qquad(d(t)=d(s)=0).
\end{equation}

Choose distinct $s,r\ne t$.  If $d(t)=0$, Urysohn's lemma supplies
$\rho:K\to[0,1]$ with $\rho(s)=1$ and $\rho(r)=0$.  Then
\[
 d=(1-\rho)d+\rho d.
\]
The first summand vanishes at $t,s$, and the second at $t,r$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:CKtail} therefore gives $\Phi_t(d)=0$.  Consequently
\[
 \Phi_t(f)=f(t)\Phi_t(\mathbf1)=\gamma f(t)\qquad(f\in H_t),
 \quad \gamma:=q(\mathbf1)>0.
\]
The scalar is independent of $t$.  Finally, every $f\in C(K)$ attains its
sup norm.  Rotate $f$ by a unimodular scalar so that a norming value at some
$t$ is positive real.  The rotated function lies in $C_t$, whence
$q(f)=\gamma\|f\|_\infty$.
\end{proof}

\section{Main theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $\Lambda$ be an infinite set, let
$X=c_0(\Lambda)$ or $X=\ell_\infty(\Lambda)$ over $\mathbb R$ or
$\mathbb C$, and let $T:X\to X$ be a nonzero linear map.  Assume only that
\[
 x,y\text{ TEA in }X\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Tx,Ty\text{ TEA in }X.
\]
Then there exists $\gamma>0$ such that
\[
 \boxed{\ \|Tx\|_\infty=\gamma\|x\|_\infty\quad(x\in X).\ }
\]
Thus $T/\gamma$ is an isometric embedding and $T$ is automatically bounded.
If $T$ is bijective, then $T/\gamma$ is a surjective linear isometry.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel} makes $q(x)=\|Tx\|_\infty$ a norm.  The hypothesis
gives~\eqref{eq:pullback}.  Lemma~\ref{lem:c0} proves the assertion when
$X=c_0(\Lambda)$.  For $X=\ell_\infty(\Lambda)$, use the standard isometric
identification
\[
 \ell_\infty(\Lambda)=C(\beta\Lambda),
\]
where $\beta\Lambda$ is the Stone--\v Cech compactification of the discrete
space $\Lambda$~\cite{GJ}.  Since $\Lambda$ is infinite,
$\beta\Lambda$ has at least three points, and Lemma~\ref{lem:CK} applies.
The final assertions are immediate.
\end{proof}

\section{Parallel pairs, checks, and limitations}

The theorem is exactly the desired one-way replacement for the TEA clauses
of both source results.  It is stronger than the $c_0$ result because it does
not assume reflection of TEA pairs, and stronger than the $\ell_\infty$
result because it assumes neither reflection nor bijectivity.

For clarity, it does not prove the analogous assertion from preservation of
\emph{parallel} pairs alone.  Indeed, if $0\ne\varphi\in c_0(\Lambda)'$ and
$0\ne v\in c_0(\Lambda)$, then
\[
 Tx=\varphi(x)v
\]
has rank one and sends every pair to a parallel pair, yet it is not an
isometric embedding.  This disposes of the literal one-way parallel version
of the $c_0$ theorem.  The source's bijective $\ell_\infty$ parallel question,
or a rank-greater-than-one reformulation, remains open in this packet.

The proof has been checked against the following edge cases.
\begin{itemize}
\item Zero vectors cause no problem; the nonzero hypothesis is used only to
      turn the kernel lemma into injectivity and to ensure $\gamma>0$.
\item No sequence or net is used, so uncountable $\Lambda$ is covered.
\item The function $|d|$ is continuous in both scalar fields.
\item The disk-valued Urysohn interpolation is explicit and works at
      non-isolated points of $\beta\Lambda$.
\item The use of phases $1$ and $-1$ is valid over both $\mathbb R$ and
      $\mathbb C$ and is precisely what eliminates non-evaluation terms.
\item No extension from finitely supported vectors, and hence no unproved
      continuity of $T$, occurs.
\end{itemize}

Human review should focus on the well-defined real-linear extension of
$q|_{C_t}$ and on the two exact cone decompositions in
Lemmas~\ref{lem:c0} and~\ref{lem:CK}.  These are the structural heart of the
argument.  The kernel lemma and final reduction are elementary.
