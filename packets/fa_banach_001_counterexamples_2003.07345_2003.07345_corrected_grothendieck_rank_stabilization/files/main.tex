\fcolorbox{accent}{soft}{\parbox{0.91\linewidth}{
\textbf{Result.}  The rank bound preceding Conjecture 2.18 is one unit too
large.  The exact universal stabilization ranks for both symmetric
Grothendieck norms are
\[
 \rho_{\R}(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{8n+1}-1}{2}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad \rho_{\C}(n)=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor.
\]
Consequently, every real parameter in
$d(d+1)/2\le n<(d+1)(d+2)/2$ and every complex parameter in
$d^2\le n<(d+1)^2$ is already an ordinary semidefinite program, although
the conjecture places it in its NP-hard range.  Block symmetrization gives
the same unconditional polynomial-time overlap for the rectangular norm.
Thus the conjectured frontier, under its natural uniform-parameter reading,
cannot be correct unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.  The strict range below
the corrected threshold remains open here.}}
\end{center}

\section{Source conjecture and the issue}

Friedland and Lim introduce real and complex Grothendieck $d$-norms and prove
a rank-stabilization result before asking whether the complementary parameter
range is NP-hard \cite{FL}.  Their Conjecture 2.18, on page 15, asserts
NP-hardness for
\begin{align*}
 &\|\cdot\|_{\gamma,d}^{\R},\ \|\cdot\|_{\Gamma,d}^{\R}
       &&\text{when }d(d+1)/2\le n,\\
 &\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm G,d}^{\R}
       &&\text{when }d(d+1)/2\le m+n,\\
 &\|\cdot\|_{\gamma,d}^{\C},\ \|\cdot\|_{\Gamma,d}^{\C}
       &&\text{when }d^2\le n,\\
 &\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm G,d}^{\C}
       &&\text{when }d^2\le m+n.
\end{align*}

The preceding Lemma 2.16 uses
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{8n+1}-1}{2}\right\rfloor+1
 \quad\text{and}\quad \lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor+1
\]
as upper bounds for extreme ranks.  The final $+1$ is unnecessary.  The
proof below supplies the exact bounds and records the resulting correction
to the complexity frontier.

\section{Definitions}

Let $\mathbb K$ be $\R$ or $\C$, and let $\Herm_r(\mathbb K)$ be the real
vector space of real symmetric or complex Hermitian $r\times r$ matrices.
For $n\ge1$ define the elliptope and subelliptope
\[
 \Ecal_n^{\mathbb K}=\{G\succeq0:g_{ii}=1\},\qquad
 \Ccal_n^{\mathbb K}=\{G\succeq0:0\le g_{ii}\le1\}.
\]
The subscript $d$ denotes the subset of matrices of rank at most $d$.
For $A=A^*$, the source definitions are equivalently
\begin{align*}
 \|A\|_{\gamma,d}^{\mathbb K}
   &=\max_{G\in\Ecal_{n,d}^{\mathbb K}}|\operatorname{tr}(AG)|,\\
 \|A\|_{\Gamma,d}^{\mathbb K}
   &=\max_{G\in\Ccal_{n,d}^{\mathbb K}}|\operatorname{tr}(AG)|.
\end{align*}
Removing $d$ means that the rank constraint is absent.  For a rectangular
$B\in\mathbb K^{m\times n}$ the source proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
 \|B\|_{\mathrm G,d}^{\mathbb K}
 =\frac12\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0&B\\B^*&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{
 \gamma,d}^{\mathbb K}
 =\frac12\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0&B\\B^*&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{
 \Gamma,d}^{\mathbb K}.
\end{equation}

\section{Proof intuition}

Factor a rank-$r$ correlation matrix as a Gram matrix of spanning vectors
$v_1,\ldots,v_n\in\mathbb K^r$.  A small Hermitian perturbation $H$ of the
inner product preserves the diagonal precisely when
$v_i^*Hv_i=0$ for every $i$.  An extreme point cannot admit a nonzero such
perturbation, so the rank-one matrices $v_iv_i^*$ must span the whole space
of symmetric or Hermitian matrices.  That space has real dimension
$r(r+1)/2$ over $\R$ and $r^2$ over $\C$.  Hence an extreme rank $r$ obeys
exactly the two displayed dimension inequalities.

Conversely, elementary vector configurations with those cardinalities have
rank-one projectors spanning the full matrix space, so the bounds are sharp.
Linear optimization over a compact convex set only needs its extreme points;
therefore every rank constraint at least as large as the maximum extreme rank
is redundant.  This is the one-step correction missed by the source.

\section{Exact symmetric stabilization}

Set
\[
 \rho_{\R}(n)=\max\{r:r(r+1)/2\le n\},\qquad
 \rho_{\C}(n)=\max\{r:r^2\le n\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Extreme-rank bound]
Every extreme point of $\Ecal_n^{\mathbb K}$ or $\Ccal_n^{\mathbb K}$ has
rank at most $\rho_{\mathbb K}(n)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $G=VV^*$ have rank $r$, where the rows of $V$ are spanning vectors
$v_1,\ldots,v_n\in\mathbb K^r$.  Suppose first that $G$ is extreme in
$\Ecal_n^{\mathbb K}$.  If the real span of
$v_1v_1^*,\ldots,v_nv_n^*$ is not all of $\Herm_r(\mathbb K)$, choose
$0\ne H\in\Herm_r(\mathbb K)$ orthogonal to every $v_iv_i^*$ in the trace
inner product.  For all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$,
$I\pm\varepsilon H\succeq0$, and
\[
 G_{\pm}=V(I\pm\varepsilon H)V^*
\]
are distinct positive semidefinite matrices with unit diagonal whose average
is $G$.  This contradicts extremality.  The projectors therefore span, so
\[
 \dim_{\R}\Herm_r(\R)=\frac{r(r+1)}2\le n,
 \qquad
 \dim_{\R}\Herm_r(\C)=r^2\le n.
\]

For $G$ extreme in $\Ccal_n^{\mathbb K}$, apply the same argument using only
the active indices $i$ for which $g_{ii}=1$.  If their projectors do not span,
the active diagonal entries stay fixed under the perturbation above, while
all inactive inequalities remain strict for sufficiently small
$\varepsilon$.  Again $G$ splits nontrivially.  There are at most $n$ active
indices, which gives the same bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Sharpness]
For every $r$ satisfying $r(r+1)/2\le n$ in the real case, or $r^2\le n$ in
the complex case, $\Ecal_n^{\mathbb K}$ has an extreme point of rank $r$.
The same matrix is extreme in $\Ccal_n^{\mathbb K}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Over $\R$, take the $r(r+1)/2$ unit vectors
\[
 e_i\quad(1\le i\le r),\qquad
 \frac{e_i+e_j}{\sqrt2}\quad(1\le i<j\le r).
\]
Their rank-one projectors span every real symmetric matrix.  Over $\C$, add
the vectors $(e_i+\mathrm i e_j)/\sqrt2$ for $i<j$; the resulting $r^2$
projectors span $\Herm_r(\C)$ over $\R$.  If necessary, append copies of
$e_1$ to obtain $n$ vectors.  To verify the converse perturbation criterion,
suppose the Gram matrix $G$ is the midpoint of $G_+$ and $G_-$ in the
elliptope.  Positivity implies $\ker G\subseteq\ker G_+\cap\ker G_-$, so
$G_\pm=V(I\pm H)V^*$ for a Hermitian $H$.  Unit diagonals give
$v_i^*Hv_i=0$ for every $i$, and projector spanning forces $H=0$.  Thus the
Gram matrix is extreme and has rank $r$.

If this matrix were a nontrivial convex combination of two subcorrelation
matrices, every diagonal coordinate---an average of numbers at most one---
could equal one only if both summands had unit diagonal.  Extremality in the
elliptope then makes the decomposition trivial.  Thus it is also extreme in
the subelliptope.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact universal threshold]
For $\mathbb K\in\{\R,\C\}$ and $d,n\ge1$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $d\ge\rho_{\mathbb K}(n)$;
\item $\|A\|_{\gamma,d}^{\mathbb K}=\|A\|_{\gamma}^{\mathbb K}$ for every
      $A\in\Herm_n(\mathbb K)$;
\item $\|A\|_{\Gamma,d}^{\mathbb K}=\|A\|_{\Gamma}^{\mathbb K}$ for every
      $A\in\Herm_n(\mathbb K)$.
\end{enumerate}
If $d<\rho_{\mathbb K}(n)$, there is an $A$ with a strict gap in (2), and
there is (possibly another) $A$ with a strict gap in (3).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The extreme-rank lemma and finite-dimensional Krein--Milman theorem imply
\[
 \Ecal_n^{\mathbb K}=\conv(\Ecal_{n,d}^{\mathbb K}),\qquad
 \Ccal_n^{\mathbb K}=\conv(\Ccal_{n,d}^{\mathbb K})
\]
whenever $d\ge\rho_{\mathbb K}(n)$.  Maximizing either sign of a linear
functional gives (2) and (3).

Conversely, suppose $d<\rho_{\mathbb K}(n)$ and choose, by sharpness, an
extreme correlation matrix $G$ of rank larger than $d$.  It is not in
$\conv(\Ecal_{n,d}^{\mathbb K})$.  In fact it is not in
$\conv(\Ecal_{n,d}^{\mathbb K}\cup-\Ecal_{n,d}^{\mathbb K})$: taking
diagonals in such a representation would force every negatively signed term
to have zero weight, after which extremality gives a contradiction.  Strict
separation of compact convex sets therefore produces a Hermitian $A$ for
which the full absolute support function is strictly larger than its
rank-$d$ counterpart.  This is the required $\gamma$ gap.

The same argument applies to $\Ccal_{n,d}^{\mathbb K}$.  Indeed, a convex
combination of signed subcorrelation matrices can have every diagonal entry
equal to one only when every used term is positively signed and itself has
unit diagonal.  Hence $G$ is also outside the symmetrized rank-$d$
subelliptope, and strict separation gives the $\Gamma$ gap.
\end{proof}

The extreme correlation rank characterization is classical
\cite{GPW,LiTam}; the proof is included to make both the strict threshold and
the subcorrelation extension self-contained.

\section{Consequences for Conjecture 2.18}

The theorem is equivalent to the following corrected stabilization conditions:
\begin{align}
 \|\cdot\|_{\gamma,d}^{\R}=\|\cdot\|_{\gamma}^{\R},\quad
 \|\cdot\|_{\Gamma,d}^{\R}=\|\cdot\|_{\Gamma}^{\R}
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n<\frac{(d+1)(d+2)}2,\label{eq:realstab}\\
 \|\cdot\|_{\gamma,d}^{\C}=\|\cdot\|_{\gamma}^{\C},\quad
 \|\cdot\|_{\Gamma,d}^{\C}=\|\cdot\|_{\Gamma}^{\C}
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n<(d+1)^2.\label{eq:complexstab}
\end{align}
Here each equality means equality for every input matrix.  The unrestricted
quantities are support functions of spectrahedra and can be computed to
arbitrary precision in polynomial time by two semidefinite programs, one for
each sign of the objective.

For $B\in\mathbb K^{m\times n}$ let $N=m+n$.  Combining \eqref{eq:block} with the
sufficient direction of the theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rectstab}
 d\ge\rho_{\mathbb K}(N)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \|B\|_{\mathrm G,d}^{\mathbb K}=\|B\|_{\mathrm G}^{\mathbb K}
 \quad\text{for every }B.
\end{equation}
This block argument is sufficient for the correction; no claim of an exact
rectangular threshold is made.

\begin{corollary}[Polynomial-time overlap]
All three real Grothendieck $d$-norms are semidefinite-programming quantities
throughout the uniform parameter band
\[
 \frac{d(d+1)}2\le N<\frac{(d+1)(d+2)}2,
\]
where $N=n$ for the symmetric norms and $N=m+n$ for the rectangular norm.
All three complex norms are likewise semidefinite-programming quantities
throughout
\[
 d^2\le N<(d+1)^2.
\]
These are infinite families lying inside all four clauses of Conjecture 2.18.
Thus, under the standard assumption $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$ and the
natural uniform reading in which $d$ may vary with the matrix dimension, the
conjecture is false as stated.
\end{corollary}

For example, the symmetric norms already stabilize at $(d,n)=(2,3)$ over
$\R$ and at $(d,n)=(2,4)$ over $\C$, exactly on the conjecture's claimed
hard boundary.  The correction is larger than these equality cases: it fills
the entire bands above.

\section{Complexity and scope caveats}

There are three necessary qualifications.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A problem in polynomial time can also be NP-hard if
      $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.  The unconditional statement proved here is
      SDP computability and the exact stabilization threshold; the word
      ``counterexample'' uses the conventional $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$
      interpretation also implicit in contrasting easy and hard ranges.
\item If ``for fixed $d$'' was intended, and only $n\to\infty$ supplies an
      input family, the overlap contains only finitely many $n$ for that
      particular $d$.  Then this is a precise correction of the printed
      parameter frontier, not a refutation of possible fixed-$d$ hardness in
      the remaining strict range.  The paper's own discussion allows $d$ to
      scale with $n$, which is why the uniform reading is natural.
\item The proof gives arbitrary-precision polynomial-time SDP computation,
      matching the source paper's formulation.  It does not claim a rational
      closed form or exact bit-model output for generally algebraic optima.
\end{enumerate}

The remaining possible hardness region is therefore
\[
 n\ge\frac{(d+1)(d+2)}2\quad(\R),\qquad
 n\ge(d+1)^2\quad(\C)
\]
for the symmetric norms, with analogous corrected sufficient boundaries in
$m+n$ for the rectangular norms.  No unconditional hardness result in that
strict range is proved here.

\section{Verification, novelty, and review recommendation}

The proof was checked independently in four places: the perturbation preserves
positive semidefiniteness and every active diagonal; the real dimensions are
$r(r+1)/2$ and $r^2$; the explicit projector families span the required
spaces; and diagonal coordinates exclude negative terms in the sharpness
separation.  Substitution at triangular and square boundary values confirms
the one-step difference from Proposition 2.17.

A bounded novelty search covered the local run indexes, the exact paper title,
``Conjecture 2.18'', ``Grothendieck $d$-norms NP-hard'', ``stabilization rank
correlation matrices'', and later copies of the source paper.  It found the
classical extreme-correlation rank results and work on extreme bipartite
correlations \cite{GDL}, but no published erratum or explicit correction of
this conjectured frontier.  Since the core rank theorem is classical and the
correction is an arithmetic tightening of the source argument, novelty
confidence is moderate, not high.

Human review should focus first on the intended complexity quantifiers in
Conjecture 2.18, then on the exact-threshold converse using symmetrized convex
hulls.  The mathematical stabilization theorem itself is recommended as
likely valid.
