\section*{Source question and status}

Let $\Hn=\Cn\times\R$ be the Heisenberg group, let
$T=\partial_t$ be its central derivative, and let $\cL$ be its
sublaplacian.  Under the Schr\"odinger representation $\pi_\lambda$,
$\cL$ corresponds to the Hermite operator
\[
 H(\lambda)=-\Delta_{\R^n}+\lambda^2|x|^2.
\]
Bagchi--Ganguly--Sarkar--Thangavelu \cite{BGST} consider an integrable
function $f$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermite-decay}
 \widehat f(\lambda)^*\widehat f(\lambda)
 \le C\exp\!\left[-2\Thetaf(\sqrt{H(\lambda)})\sqrt{H(\lambda)}\right],
 \qquad \lambda\ne0,
\end{equation}
where $\Thetaf:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ decreases to zero and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ingham-divergence}
 \int_1^\infty \frac{\Thetaf(r)}r\,dr=\infty.
\end{equation}
Their Theorem 1.5 proves $f=0$ if, for every $\lambda\ne0$, the central
Fourier fiber
\[
 f^\lambda(z)=\int_{\R}e^{i\lambda t}f(z,t)\,dt
\]
vanishes on some open subset of $\Cn$.  Remark 4.1 asks whether it is enough
to assume instead that $f$ vanishes on a nonempty open subset of $\Hn$.

The full question remains open.  We prove it under an independent
Denjoy--Carleman condition in the central direction.

\section*{Proof intuition}

Ordinary open-set vanishing does not survive Fourier transformation in the
central variable: a function can vanish for $t$ in an interval while
$f^\lambda(z)$ is nonzero for every $\lambda$.  The added Carleman condition
repairs exactly this loss.  Pair $f(z,t)$ against an arbitrary horizontal test
function $\eta(z)$.  The resulting scalar function of $t$ vanishes on an
interval, and its even-derivative norms inherit the Carleman divergence from
$T^{2m}f$.  The one-dimensional Chernoff theorem makes the pairing vanish for
every $t$.  Varying $\eta$ turns the local zero set into an entire vertical
cylinder.  The source's fiberwise theorem then applies without modification.

\begin{theorem}[vertical Carleman subcase]\label{thm:main}
Assume \eqref{eq:hermite-decay} and \eqref{eq:ingham-divergence}.  Suppose in
addition that $f\in L^1(\Hn)\cap L^2(\Hn)$, that $T^{2m}f\in L^2(\Hn)$ for
every $m\ge1$, and that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertical-carleman}
 \sum_{m=1}^\infty
 \|T^{2m}f\|_{L^2(\Hn)}^{-1/(2m)}=\infty.
\end{equation}
If $f$ vanishes on a nonempty open subset of $\Hn$, then $f=0$ almost
everywhere.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every nonempty open subset of $\Cn\times\R$ contains a product $U\times I$,
where $U\subset\Cn$ and $I\subset\R$ are nonempty and open.  Fix a nonzero
$\eta\in C_c^\infty(U)$ and define, initially as an $L^2$ function,
\[
 g_\eta(t)=\int_{\Cn}f(z,t)\overline{\eta(z)}\,dz.
\]
For every $m\ge1$, distributional differentiation, Cauchy--Schwarz in $z$,
and Fubini give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairing-bound}
 \|g_\eta^{(2m)}\|_{L^2(\R)}
 \le \|\eta\|_{L^2(\Cn)}\,
       \|T^{2m}f\|_{L^2(\Hn)}.
\end{equation}
Thus $g_\eta$ belongs to every even Sobolev space and has a smooth
representative.  Moreover, \eqref{eq:pairing-bound} and
\eqref{eq:vertical-carleman} imply
\[
 \sum_{m=1}^\infty
 \|g_\eta^{(2m)}\|_2^{-1/(2m)}=\infty;
\]
the additional factor $\|\eta\|_2^{-1/(2m)}$ tends to one.  Since $f=0$ on
$U\times I$, the function $g_\eta$ vanishes on $I$, so it and all its
derivatives vanish at any point of $I$.  Chernoff's one-dimensional theorem
\cite{Chernoff}, applied to the Laplacian $-d^2/dt^2$, yields
$g_\eta\equiv0$ on $\R$.

This holds for every $\eta\in C_c^\infty(U)$.  Consequently
$f=0$ on $U\times\R$ in the distributional, hence almost-everywhere, sense.
Fubini now shows that, for every central frequency for which the fiber is
defined,
\[
 f^\lambda(z)=0\qquad\text{for almost every }z\in U.
\]
The fiberwise Ingham theorem, Theorem 1.5 of \cite{BGST}, applies under
\eqref{eq:hermite-decay}--\eqref{eq:ingham-divergence} and gives $f=0$.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis has a concrete analytic-vector sufficient condition.

\begin{corollary}[central analytic vectors]\label{cor:analytic}
Under the other hypotheses of Theorem \ref{thm:main}, condition
\eqref{eq:vertical-carleman} holds whenever
\[
 e^{a|T|}f\in L^2(\Hn)
\]
for some $a>0$, where $|T|$ is defined by Fourier transformation in the
central variable.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Spectral calculus and
$\sup_{s\ge0}s^{2m}e^{-as}=(2m/(ae))^{2m}$ give
\[
 \|T^{2m}f\|_2
 \le \left(\frac{2m}{ae}\right)^{2m}\|e^{a|T|}f\|_2.
\]
After taking $-1/(2m)$ powers, the resulting series dominates a constant
multiple of $\sum_m m^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\section*{Why this does not settle the full question}

The added condition is not a formal consequence of the Hermite-only bound.
The lowest Hermite eigenvalue gives $H(\lambda)\ge n|\lambda|$, so
\eqref{eq:hermite-decay} controls central frequency only at the scale
\[
 \exp\!\big[-c\sqrt{|\lambda|}\,
       \Thetaf(c\sqrt{|\lambda|})\big].
\]
Even if $\Thetaf$ were replaced by a positive constant, the associated
$2m$-th central derivative norm has a root of quadratic order in $m$, and
the reciprocal Carleman series need not diverge.  The actual hypothesis
$\Thetaf(r)\to0$ is weaker still.  Thus the proof isolates an independent
vertical quasi-analyticity mechanism; it does not hide a proof of the general
sublaplacian Chernoff theorem.

\section*{Verification and novelty status}

The proof uses only Cauchy--Schwarz/Fubini, the classical one-dimensional
Chernoff theorem, and the source's proved fiberwise Theorem 1.5.  Exact-title,
author, sublaplacian-Chernoff, and open-set searches were performed in the
local parsed arXiv corpus and in arXiv-indexed web results through
2026-08-11.  The closest sources checked were arXiv:2009.14230,
arXiv:2106.02704, and arXiv:2011.09940; no later answer to Remark 4.1 was
found.  Novelty confidence for this modest added-Carleman subcase is medium,
not high.  Human review should focus on the passage from scalar test pairings
to the common vertical cylinder and on the precise regularity convention in
the source's Theorem 1.5.
