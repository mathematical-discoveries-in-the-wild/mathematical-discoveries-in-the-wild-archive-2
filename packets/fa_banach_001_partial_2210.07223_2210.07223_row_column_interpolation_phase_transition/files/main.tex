\title{An exact column--row phase transition on the row--column interpolation scale}
\author{Candidate solution packet for arXiv:2210.07223}
\date{17 August 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Ray and Sarkar ask which operator spaces have the column--row property.  For the
canonical Hilbertian interpolation scale
\(
  H_\theta=(\Rspace,\Cspace)_\theta\ (0\leq\theta\leq1),
\)
we compute every finite column-to-row norm exactly:
\[
 \gamma_n(H_\theta)=n^{\max\{(1-2\theta)/2,0\}}.
\]
Consequently, the infinite-dimensional space \(H_\theta\) has the column--row
property if and only if \(\theta\geq\tfrac12\), and in that range its optimal
constant is \(1\).  The reverse norm is
\(n^{\max\{(2\theta-1)/2,0\}}\).  We also record that the property, with the
expected geometric-mean bound on constants, is stable under complex
interpolation.  This is a complete result for this natural one-parameter
family, not a classification of all operator spaces.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source question and transcription}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.97\textwidth]{source_question_crop.png}
\end{center}

The displayed crop is from page 1 of Ray--Sarkar, arXiv:2210.07223v2.  The
source asks:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Which operator spaces satisfy the column--row property?}
\end{quote}
For a concrete operator space \(E\subseteq B(\mathcal H)\), the source calls
\(E\) column--row if one constant \(K\) satisfies
\[
 \left\|[x_1\ \cdots\ x_n]\right\|_{M_{1,n}(E)}
 \leq K\left\|[x_1\ \cdots\ x_n]^t\right\|_{M_{n,1}(E)}
 \quad(n\geq1)
\]
for all finite families in \(E\).  The paper proves classifications for
\(C^*\)-algebras and semifinite noncommutative \(L_p\)-spaces and notes that
\(\Cspace\) and \(\OH\) have constant \(1\), whereas \(\Rspace\) fails the
property.  It does not analyze the interpolation continuum joining these
three spaces.

\section{Definitions and result}

Let \(\Rspace\) and \(\Cspace\) be the row and column operator-space structures
on \(\ell_2\).  We fix the convention
\[
 H_\theta=(\Rspace,\Cspace)_\theta,
 \qquad 0\leq\theta\leq1,
\]
for complex interpolation of operator spaces.  Thus
\(H_0=\Rspace\), \(H_{1/2}=\OH\), and \(H_1=\Cspace\), completely isometrically.

\begin{definition}
For an operator space \(E\) and \(n\geq1\), put
\[
 \gamma_n(E)=
 \left\|t_n:M_{n,1}(E)\longrightarrow M_{1,n}(E)\right\|,
\]
where \(t_n([x_1\ \cdots\ x_n]^t)=[x_1\ \cdots\ x_n]\).
Thus \(E\) has the column--row property exactly when
\(\sup_n\gamma_n(E)<\infty\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact phase transition]\label{thm:main}
For every \(0\leq\theta\leq1\) and every \(n\geq1\),
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \gamma_n(H_\theta)=n^{\frac12(1-2\theta)_+},
 \quad (s)_+=\max\{s,0\}.\quad}
\]
Hence \(H_\theta\) has the column--row property if and only if
\(\theta\geq\tfrac12\).  Whenever it has the property, the optimal uniform
constant is \(1\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Reverse direction]\label{cor:reverse}
If
\(
 \rho_n(E)=\|t_n:M_{1,n}(E)\to M_{n,1}(E)\|,
\)
then
\[
 \rho_n(H_\theta)=n^{\frac12(2\theta-1)_+}.
\]
In particular, both directions are uniformly bounded only at the symmetric
point \(H_{1/2}=\OH\).
\end{corollary}

\section{Idea and proof intuition}

There are two complementary parts.

First, interpolation gives the upper bound.  At the row endpoint the
column-to-row transpose has norm \(\sqrt n\); at the symmetric midpoint
\(\OH\) it is an isometry; and at the column endpoint it is a contraction.
Reiteration then gives exactly the exponent in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

Second, the first \(n\) coordinate vectors detect the bound below the
midpoint.  Their column and row norms in \(H_\theta\) are exactly
\(n^{\theta/2}\) and \(n^{(1-\theta)/2}\).  The upper estimates follow by
interpolating their endpoint norms.  A trace-type functional attains the
matching lower estimates.  This dual witness is what turns a soft
interpolation bound into an exact formula.

\section{Proof}

We use only standard exactness and reiteration for complex interpolation of
operator spaces.  In particular,
\[
 M_{a,b}((E_0,E_1)_s)
   =(M_{a,b}(E_0),M_{a,b}(E_1))_s
\tag{4.1}\label{eq:matrix-interp}
\]
isometrically.

\subsection*{Endpoint norms}

For every concrete operator space \(E\subseteq B(\mathcal K)\),
\[
 \left\|[x_1\ \cdots\ x_n]\right\|^2
 =\left\|\sum_i x_ix_i^*\right\|
 \leq \sum_i\|x_i\|^2
 \leq n\left\|\sum_i x_i^*x_i\right\|.
\]
Thus \(\gamma_n(E)\leq\sqrt n\).  In \(E=\Rspace\), take the first \(n\)
canonical row vectors \(e_i=E_{1i}\).  Their column norm is \(1\), while their
row norm is \(\sqrt n\).  Therefore
\[
 \gamma_n(\Rspace)=\sqrt n.\tag{4.2}
\]

For \(E=\Cspace\), regard \(x_i\) as Hilbert-space columns.  The row is their
synthesis operator, whose operator norm is bounded by its Hilbert--Schmidt
norm; the latter is exactly the norm of the stacked column.  Hence
\(\gamma_n(\Cspace)\leq1\).  Equality follows by taking only \(x_1\neq0\), so
\[
 \gamma_n(\Cspace)=1.\tag{4.3}
\]

Finally, \(\OH\) is completely isometric to its opposite through the identity.
Equivalently, transposition preserves the norm of every \(\OH\)-valued
matrix.  (For a column this also follows directly from the usual Gram-matrix
formula
\(
 \|[x_i]^t\|=(\sum_{i,j}|\langle x_i,x_j\rangle|^2)^{1/4}.
\))  Consequently,
\[
 \gamma_n(\OH)=1.\tag{4.4}
\]

\subsection*{Interpolation upper bounds}

If \(0\leq\theta\leq\tfrac12\), reiteration gives
\(H_\theta=(\Rspace,\OH)_{2\theta}\).  Apply the interpolation theorem to the
same transpose map \(t_n\), using \eqref{eq:matrix-interp}, (4.2), and (4.4):
\[
 \gamma_n(H_\theta)
 \leq (\sqrt n)^{1-2\theta}\,1^{2\theta}
 =n^{(1-2\theta)/2}.\tag{4.5}
\]
If \(\tfrac12\leq\theta\leq1\), then
\(H_\theta=(\OH,\Cspace)_{2\theta-1}\), and (4.3)--(4.4) give
\[
 \gamma_n(H_\theta)\leq1.\tag{4.6}
\]

\subsection*{The exact coordinate witness}

It remains to prove equality in (4.5).  Work first in the \(n\)-dimensional
coordinate spaces \(\Rspace_n,\Cspace_n\); their inclusions and coordinate
projections are complete contractions at both endpoints, so interpolation
embeds their interpolants completely isometrically in \(H_\theta\).

Let \(e_1,\dots,e_n\) be the common canonical basis and set
\[
 u=[e_1\ \cdots\ e_n]^t,
 \qquad v=[e_1\ \cdots\ e_n].
\]
The concrete endpoint identifications give
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & \Rspace_n & \Cspace_n\\ \hline
 \|u\| & \|I_n\|_{M_n}=1 & \sqrt n\\
 \|v\| & \sqrt n & \|I_n\|_{M_n}=1.
\end{array}\tag{4.7}
\]
Log-convexity of interpolation norms therefore yields
\[
 \|u\|_{M_{n,1}(H_\theta)}\leq n^{\theta/2},
 \qquad
 \|v\|_{M_{1,n}(H_\theta)}\leq n^{(1-\theta)/2}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

For completeness, both inequalities are equalities.  Define the common
coordinate functional
\[
 \varphi([x_i])=\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i)_i.
\]
On the two endpoint realizations of the column \(u\), this is respectively
the trace functional on \(M_n\) and the inner product with the vector having
\(n\) unit coordinates in \(\ell_2^{n^2}\).  Its functional norms are therefore \(n\) and
\(\sqrt n\).  Applying the interpolation theorem directly to the compatible
functional \(\varphi\) gives
\[
 \|\varphi\|_{(M_{n,1}(H_\theta))^*}
 \leq n^{1-\theta}(\sqrt n)^\theta=n^{1-\theta/2}.
\]
Since \(\varphi(u)=n\), this forces
\(\|u\|\geq n^{\theta/2}\), proving equality in the first part of (4.8).

For the row \(v\), the same functional has endpoint dual norms \(\sqrt n\)
and \(n\), in that order.  Hence
\[
 \|\varphi\|_{(M_{1,n}(H_\theta))^*}
 \leq(\sqrt n)^{1-\theta}n^\theta=n^{(1+\theta)/2}.
\]
The identity \(\varphi(v)=n\) now gives
\(\|v\|\geq n^{(1-\theta)/2}\), proving equality in the second part of
(4.8).  Thus
\[
 \gamma_n(H_\theta)\geq\frac{\|v\|}{\|u\|}
 =n^{(1-2\theta)/2} \qquad(0\leq\theta\leq\tfrac12).
\]
Together with (4.5), this is equality.  Above the midpoint, (4.6) and a family
with only one nonzero member give \(\gamma_n(H_\theta)=1\).  This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

Since \(H_\theta^{\op}\) is completely isometric to \(H_{1-\theta}\), the
row-to-column norm on \(H_\theta\) is the column-to-row norm on
\(H_{1-\theta}\).  Corollary~\ref{cor:reverse} follows.

\section{A general interpolation consequence}

\begin{proposition}[Interpolation stability]\label{prop:stability}
Let \((E_0,E_1)\) be a compatible couple of operator spaces.  If \(E_j\) has
column--row constant \(K_j\) for \(j=0,1\), then
\[
 E_s=(E_0,E_1)_s
 \quad\text{has column--row constant at most}\quad
 K_0^{1-s}K_1^s.
\]
More precisely,
\(
 \gamma_n(E_s)\leq\gamma_n(E_0)^{1-s}\gamma_n(E_1)^s
\)
for every \(n\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:matrix-interp}, the transpose \(t_n\) acts between the
interpolated column and row couples.  The ordinary interpolation theorem for
operators gives the displayed finite-level estimate.  Taking the supremum in
\(n\) gives the uniform assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty limits}

The proof has three independent checks: endpoint norms are computed in
concrete matrix models; the upper estimate follows from exact interpolation;
and the coordinate/trace witness supplies a matching lower estimate.  The
formulas recover all source-paper endpoints:
\[
 \theta=0:\ \gamma_n=\sqrt n,
 \qquad
 \theta=\tfrac12:\ \gamma_n=1,
 \qquad
 \theta=1:\ \gamma_n=1.
\]
Opposite-space symmetry reproduces the reverse formula and is an additional
consistency check.

This theorem completely classifies the row--column interpolation scale, but
the source question ranges over all operator spaces and remains much broader.
A bounded search of arXiv and web indexes on 17 August 2026 found the source
paper and the complete-Pick-space literature, but no statement matching the
exact formula above.  That is evidence only, not a definitive novelty claim.
Independent expert review should focus on the standard interpolation
identifications in \eqref{eq:matrix-interp} and on the endpoint norms of the
trace witness.
