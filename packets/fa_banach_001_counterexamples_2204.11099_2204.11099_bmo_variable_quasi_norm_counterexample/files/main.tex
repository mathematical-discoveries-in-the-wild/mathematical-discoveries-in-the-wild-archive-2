\section*{Source questions and answer}

For every axis-parallel cube $Q\subset\R^n$, the source assigns a local
quasi-Banach function space $X_Q$ with
\(\|\chi_Q\|_{X_Q}\simeq1\), and defines
\[
 \|f\|_{\BMO_X}=\sup_Q\|f-\avg f_Q\|_{X_Q},\qquad
 \|f\|_{\BMO_X^*}=\sup_Q\inf_{c\in\R}\|f-c\|_{X_Q}.
\]
Question 6.1 asks whether \(\BMO_X\hookrightarrow\BMO\) can fail.
Question 6.2 asks whether \(\BMO\hookrightarrow\BMO_X\) forces that
converse; its stronger version replaces \(\BMO_X\) by \(\BMO_X^*\) in the
conclusion \cite{source}.  The appended source crop reproduces the questions.

\begin{theorem}[Answers to Questions 6.1 and 6.2]\label{thm:main}
For every $n\ge1$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item There is a normalized family of Banach function spaces $X_Q$ and a
locally integrable $f\in\BMO_X\setminus\BMO$.
\item There is a normalized family of quasi-Banach function spaces $X_Q$
such that
\[
 \BMO\hookrightarrow\BMO_X,
 \qquad
 \BMO_X^*\not\hookrightarrow\BMO.
\]
Indeed $f(x)=x_1$ belongs to \(\BMO_X^*\) in the second construction.
Consequently both the ordinary and stronger forms of Question 6.2 have
negative answers.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section*{Proof intuition}

The family in the definition may depend on the cube, with no compatibility
between different cubes.  For Question 6.1, a weighted $L^1$ norm can look
only near the central hyperplane of each cube.  The affine function $x_1$
then has bounded weighted oscillation although its ordinary oscillation grows
linearly with the side length.

The additional embedding in Question 6.2 makes this harder: concentrating a
fixed $L^1$ weight would detect the logarithmic drift of BMO averages
between a large cube and its tiny centre.  The remedy is a power mean with an
exponent $p_Q\downarrow0$.  Such a mean approaches a geometric mean.  We
balance a diffuse scale $L$ and a tiny central scale $s$ so their weighted
geometric mean is exactly constant.  The average drift of any BMO function is
only logarithmic, and the same vanishing exponent absorbs it uniformly.

\section*{A Banach-space counterexample for Question 6.1}

Let $Q$ have centre $c_Q$ and side length $L$.  Define the probability
measure
\[
 d\nu_Q(x)=Z_Q^{-1}e^{-|x_1-(c_Q)_1|}\,dx\quad(x\in Q),
 \qquad X_Q=L^1(Q,\nu_Q).
\]
These are Banach function spaces over Lebesgue measure because the densities
are strictly positive; moreover \(\|\chi_Q\|_{X_Q}=1\).

Take $f(x)=x_1$.  Its Lebesgue average on $Q$ is $(c_Q)_1$.  Writing
\(A=L/2\), integration in the first coordinate gives
\[
 \|f-\avg f_Q\|_{X_Q}
 =\frac{\int_0^A t e^{-t}\,dt}{\int_0^A e^{-t}\,dt}
 =1-\frac{A}{e^A-1}\le1.
\]
Thus $f\in\BMO_X$.  On the other hand,
\[
 \frac1{|Q|}\int_Q|f-\avg f_Q|\,dx=\frac{L}{4},
\]
which is unbounded as $L\to\infty$.  Hence $f\notin\BMO$, proving the
first assertion of Theorem \ref{thm:main}.

\section*{A stronger quasi-Banach counterexample for Question 6.2}

Fix $n\ge1$, and put $L_0=\exp(\exp(n+4))$.  If a cube $Q$ has side
length $L\le L_0$, let
\[
 X_Q=L^1\bigl(Q,dx/|Q|\bigr).
\]
Suppose now that $L>L_0$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
 a=\log L,\qquad b=\log a,\qquad q=\frac1b,
 \qquad p=\frac1{a^2b},\qquad s=L^{-(b-1)}.
\end{equation}
Let $J_Q$ be the cube concentric with $Q$ and of side length $s$.  Since
\(b>1\), it is a proper subcube.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:measure}
 \mu_Q=(1-q)\frac{\chi_Q\,dx}{|Q|}
       +q\frac{\chi_{J_Q}\,dx}{|J_Q|},
 \qquad X_Q=L^p(Q,\mu_Q).
\end{equation}
The measure is a probability and its density is positive almost everywhere
on $Q$.  Thus $X_Q$ is a quasi-Banach function space over $Q$, with the
lattice and Fatou properties and weak order unit \(\chi_Q\).  Also
\(\|\chi_Q\|_{X_Q}=1\) exactly.  Hence the whole family is normalized.

We use the standard nested-average estimate.  If $U\subset V$ are
concentric cubes with side-length ratio at most two, then
\[
 |\avg h_U-\avg h_V|
 \le \frac{|V|}{|U|}\frac1{|V|}\int_V|h-\avg h_V|
 \le2^n\|h\|_{\BMO}.
\]
Iterating through concentric doublings from $J_Q$ to $Q$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nested}
 \frac1{|J_Q|}\int_{J_Q}|h-\avg h_Q|
 \le C_n\bigl(1+\log(L/s)\bigr)\|h\|_{\BMO}.
\end{equation}
For definiteness, the doubling chain above permits
\(C_n=1+2^n(1+1/\log2)<2^{n+2}\).

\begin{lemma}[The forward embedding]\label{lem:forward}
The family \eqref{eq:measure} satisfies
\(\BMO\hookrightarrow\BMO_X\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $N=\|h\|_{\BMO}$.  The assertion is immediate on cubes with
$L\le L_0$.  On a large cube, the monotonicity of probability power means
for $0<p<1$, followed by \eqref{eq:nested}, gives
\[
 \left(\frac1{|Q|}\int_Q|h-\avg h_Q|^p\right)^{1/p}\le N,
 \quad
 \left(\frac1{|J_Q|}\int_{J_Q}|h-\avg h_Q|^p\right)^{1/p}
 \le RN,
\]
where $R=C_n(1+D)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D}
 D=\log(L/s)=ab,\qquad pD=\frac1a.
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixfactor}
 \|h-\avg h_Q\|_{X_Q}
 \le N\bigl((1-q)+qR^p\bigr)^{1/p}.
\end{equation}

It remains only to see that the last factor is uniform.  Since
\(D=ab>1\), \(1+D\le2ab\).  The displayed choice of $C_n$, together with
\(b\ge n+4\), gives
\[
 \log R\le\log(2C_n)+\log a+\log b\le3b.
\]
Consequently
\[
 q\log R\le3,
 \qquad v:=p\log R\le\frac3{a^2}<1.
\]
For $0\le v\le1$, $e^v-1\le2v$.  Hence
\[
 \frac1p\log\bigl((1-q)+qR^p\bigr)
 \le\frac1p\log(1+2qv)
 \le2q\log R\le6.
\]
The factor in \eqref{eq:mixfactor} is at most $e^6$, independently of
$Q$.  Taking the supremum proves the embedding.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The affine witness]\label{lem:affine}
For the same family, $f(x)=x_1$ belongs to \(\BMO_X^*\), but
$f\notin\BMO$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $c=(c_Q)_1=\avg f_Q$.  On cubes with $L\le L_0$,
\(\|f-c\|_{X_Q}=L/4\le L_0/4\).  On a large cube, direct integration of the
first coordinate gives, with $A=L/2$ and $B=s/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affineexact}
 \|f-c\|_{X_Q}
 =\frac1{(p+1)^{1/p}}
   \bigl((1-q)A^p+qB^p\bigr)^{1/p}.
\end{equation}
The parameter choice makes the weighted geometric mean constant:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geomean}
 A^{1-q}B^q=\frac12L^{1-q}s^q=\frac12.
\end{equation}
To compare the positive power mean with this limit, let
\(u=p\log(A/B)=pD=1/a\).  Factoring the right side of
\eqref{eq:affineexact} by the geometric mean produces
\[
 H=e^{qu}\bigl((1-q)+qe^{-u}\bigr).
\]
The elementary inequalities $e^{-u}\le1-u+u^2/2$ and
\(\log(1+t)\le t\) imply
\[
 \log H
 \le qu+\log(1-qu+qu^2/2)
 \le\frac{qu^2}{2}.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:parameters},
\[
 \frac{qu^2}{2p}=\frac12.
\]
Since $(p+1)^{-1/p}<1$, equations \eqref{eq:affineexact}--
\eqref{eq:geomean} give
\[
 \|f-c\|_{X_Q}\le\frac{e^{1/2}}2
\]
on every large cube.  The supremum over all cubes is finite, and choosing
this $c$ in the best-constant infimum proves $f\in\BMO_X^*$.  Finally,
the classical mean oscillation is $L/4$ on every cube of side $L$, so
$f\notin\BMO$.
\end{proof}

Lemmas \ref{lem:forward} and \ref{lem:affine} prove the second assertion of
Theorem \ref{thm:main}.  Notice that the same witness also lies in
\(\BMO_X\), because the chosen constant is its Lebesgue cube average.

\section*{Scope, checks, and literature boundary}

The first construction strengthens Question 6.1 by using Banach spaces.  The
second fully answers Question 6.2 as written and refutes its strongest stated
variant.  It uses exponents $p_Q<1$; the analogous Banach-only version of
Question 6.2 is not claimed here.  Fixed weighted $L^1$ norms, intersections,
and normalized sums with \(\exp L\) were also examined, but either fail the
forward embedding or lose uniform normalization.

The script \path{code/verify_parameters.py} evaluates the exact power means
stably at scales as large as \(\log L=10^6\).  It verifies
\(D=ab\), $pD=1/a$, the geometric balance \eqref{eq:geomean}, and generous
forms of both uniform estimates.  The proof above is analytic and has no
computational dependency.

The source appeared in \emph{Mathematische Annalen} in 2024.  Exact-ID,
exact-question, citation, and core-keyword searches through 11 August 2026
found no prior matching counterexample.  The directly related later preprint
\cite{followup} develops additional sufficient median and sparse testing
criteria and still presents characterization directions as open; it does not
contain either construction above.  This is a bounded novelty audit, not an
exhaustive priority claim.
