\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Candidate partial result, likely valid.  The theorem below settles a broad
sufficient regime of the source problem and exactly characterizes a stronger
uniform small-support property.  It is not a full characterization of all DSS
fixed-weight inclusions.

\section{Source question}

Bernal-Gonz\'alez, Rodr\'iguez-Vidanes, Seoane-Sep\'ulveda, and Tag ask in
Section~5, item~4 of \cite{source}:
\begin{quote}
What is the characterization of inclusion operators between Orlicz--Lorentz
spaces to be disjointly strictly singular when the weight function $w$ is
fixed?
\end{quote}
They emphasize that the Orlicz-space proof does not transfer plainly because
of the weight and decreasing rearrangement.  The exact source passage is shown
below (source PDF page~34).

\section{Definitions and statement}

Let $w$ be a positive decreasing locally integrable weight on $[0,1]$ and
$W(t)=\int_0^t w(s)\,ds$.  For an Orlicz function $\varphi$, write
\[
 \rho_{\varphi,w}(f)=\int_0^1\varphi(f^*(s))w(s)\,ds,
 \qquad
 \|f\|_{\varphi,w}
 =\inf\{a>0:\rho_{\varphi,w}(f/a)\leq1\}.
\]
The source says that $\varphi_2$ satisfies
$\Delta_{\varphi_1}(\infty)$ when, for every $b>0$, there is $u_b$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reldelta}
 \varphi_1(u)\leq \varphi_2(bu)\qquad(u\geq u_b).
\end{equation}

Suppose the identity is a continuous inclusion
$J:\Lam_{\varphi_2,w}\to\Lam_{\varphi_1,w}$.  Define its small-support modulus
by
\[
 \omega_J(t)=\sup\left\{
 \frac{\|f\|_{\varphi_1,w}}{\|f\|_{\varphi_2,w}}:
 0\neq f\in\Lam_{\varphi_2,w},\ \meas(\supp f)\leq t
 \right\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact uniform small-support criterion]\label{thm:main}
For Orlicz functions $\varphi_1,\varphi_2$ and a fixed weight $w$ on $[0,1]$,
the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\varphi_2$ satisfies $\Delta_{\varphi_1}(\infty)$;
\item $\displaystyle\lim_{t\downarrow0}\omega_J(t)=0$.
\end{enumerate}
Condition \textup{(i)} itself guarantees that $J$ is a continuous inclusion.
Whenever these conditions hold, $J$ is disjointly strictly singular.
\end{theorem}

Thus the growth condition completely characterizes a property stronger than
DSS: the embedding constant vanishes uniformly on all sufficiently small
supports.  It is independent of the chosen fixed weight.

\section{Proof intuition}

Normalize a function in the domain norm and split its decreasing
rearrangement at one amplitude $A$.  Relative $\Delta$ controls the high
amplitudes after asking for the condition at the scale $b=\eta/2$; convexity
then supplies a factor $1/2$.  The low amplitudes contribute at most a fixed
constant times $W(t)$, which vanishes with the support size.  This proves the
uniform estimate.  Conversely, normalized characteristic functions read off
the ratio of the inverse Orlicz functions exactly, and that inverse ratio is
equivalent to relative $\Delta$.  Disjoint supports in $[0,1]$ necessarily
shrink, so the uniform estimate rules out an isomorphic disjoint restriction.

The unresolved part is necessity for DSS itself.  If uniform decay fails,
characteristic blocks have individual norm ratios bounded below, but arbitrary
linear combinations of those blocks undergo a new decreasing rearrangement.
That prevents the individual estimates from producing an isomorphism on the
whole disjoint span.

\section{Proof}

We first record the elementary inverse-function form of relative $\Delta$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse}
 \varphi_2\in\Delta_{\varphi_1}(\infty)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \lim_{s\to\infty}
 \frac{\varphi_2^{-1}(s)}{\varphi_1^{-1}(s)}=0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, if \eqref{eq:reldelta} holds and $s=\varphi_1(u)$, then for all large
$u$,
$\varphi_2^{-1}(s)\leq bu=b\varphi_1^{-1}(s)$.  Since $b>0$ is arbitrary,
the ratio tends to zero.  Conversely, if the ratio tends to zero, then for a
fixed $b>0$ and all large $u$,
$\varphi_2^{-1}(\varphi_1(u))\leq bu$, which is \eqref{eq:reldelta}.

Assume (i), and fix $0<\eta<1$.  Apply \eqref{eq:reldelta} with
$b=\eta/2$, obtaining a threshold $u_0$.  Put $A=\eta u_0/2$.  If $r\geq A$,
then convexity and $\varphi_1(0)=0$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:high}
 \varphi_1(r/\eta)
 \leq \tfrac12\varphi_1(2r/\eta)
 \leq \tfrac12\varphi_2(r).
\end{equation}
Choose $\delta>0$ so small that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:low}
 \varphi_1(A/\eta)W(\delta)\leq\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
This is possible because $W(\delta)\downarrow0$.

Take $0\neq f\in\Lam_{\varphi_2,w}$ with
$\meas(\supp f)\leq\delta$, and put
$g=f/\|f\|_{\varphi_2,w}$.  The Luxemburg normalization gives
$\rho_{\varphi_2,w}(g)\leq1$, while $g^*(s)=0$ for $s\geq\delta$.  Splitting
where $g^*<A$ and $g^*\geq A$, and using \eqref{eq:low} and
\eqref{eq:high}, yields
\begin{align*}
 \rho_{\varphi_1,w}(g/\eta)
 &=\int_0^\delta\varphi_1(g^*(s)/\eta)w(s)\,ds\\
 &\leq \varphi_1(A/\eta)W(\delta)
 +\tfrac12\int_0^\delta\varphi_2(g^*(s))w(s)\,ds\\
 &\leq \tfrac12+\tfrac12=1.
\end{align*}
Therefore $\|f\|_{\varphi_1,w}\leq
\eta\|f\|_{\varphi_2,w}$.  Since $\eta$ was arbitrary,
$\omega_J(t)\to0$.  The same relative condition implies
$\varphi_1\prec_\infty\varphi_2$, so continuity of $J$ also follows from
Theorem~3.7 of \cite{source}.

Conversely, assume (ii).  The characteristic-function calculation (Lemma~3.1
of \cite{source}) gives
\[
 \|\chi_{[0,t]}\|_{\varphi,w}
 =\frac{1}{\varphi^{-1}(1/W(t))}.
\]
Hence
\[
 \frac{\|\chi_{[0,t]}\|_{\varphi_1,w}}
      {\|\chi_{[0,t]}\|_{\varphi_2,w}}
 =\frac{\varphi_2^{-1}(1/W(t))}
       {\varphi_1^{-1}(1/W(t))}
 \leq\omega_J(t)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Because $W$ is continuous and strictly increasing from zero, $1/W(t)$ ranges
through all sufficiently large values as $t\downarrow0$.  Thus
\eqref{eq:inverse} holds, proving (i).

Finally, let $(f_n)$ be any sequence of nonzero pairwise disjoint functions in
the domain.  The support measures tend to zero because their sum is at most
one.  Condition (ii) therefore gives
\[
 \frac{\|Jf_n\|_{\varphi_1,w}}{\|f_n\|_{\varphi_2,w}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
If $J$ were an isomorphism on the closed span of $(f_n)$, the displayed ratios
would have a uniform positive lower bound.  This contradiction proves that
$J$ is DSS. \qed

\section{The opposite endpoint and examples}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:endpoints}
If $\varphi_2\in\Delta_{\varphi_1}(\infty)$, the fixed-weight inclusion is
DSS.  If instead both $\varphi_1\prec_\infty\varphi_2$ and
$\varphi_2\prec_\infty\varphi_1$, the two Orlicz--Lorentz spaces have
equivalent norms and the inclusion is not DSS.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  In the second case,
Theorem~3.7 of \cite{source} applies in both directions, so the identity is an
isomorphism on the whole infinite-dimensional space and hence on the span of
any disjoint sequence of nonzero characteristic functions.
\end{proof}

For example, with $\varphi_1(u)=u^p$ and $\varphi_2(u)=u^q$,
$1\leq p<q<\infty$, the inclusion
$\Lam_{q,w}[0,1]\hookrightarrow\Lam_{p,w}[0,1]$ is DSS for every fixed
weight $w$.  If the two generating functions are equivalent at infinity up to
dilations, the inclusion is not DSS.

\section{Verification, limitations, and novelty status}

The proof uses only four checkable facts: Luxemburg normalization,
$f^*=0$ beyond the measure of $\supp f$, convexity
$\varphi(\theta u)\leq\theta\varphi(u)$ for $0\leq\theta\leq1$, and the exact
characteristic-function norm.  No numerical evidence or ordinary
$\Delta_2$ hypothesis is used.

This is a partial answer.  The implication ``DSS $\Rightarrow$
$\Delta_{\varphi_1}(\infty)$'' is not claimed.  Failure of the small-support
condition produces disjoint functions whose individual norm ratios stay
positive, but that does not control all scalar combinations after decreasing
rearrangement.  The full oscillatory/intermediate-growth regime therefore
remains open.

A bounded literature search on 12 August 2026 used arXiv:2312.13903, the exact
title, and combinations of ``fixed weight'', ``Orlicz--Lorentz'', and
``disjointly strictly singular''.  It located the source, the classical
Orlicz/Lorentz results cited there, Astashkin's general symmetric-space gap
theory \cite{astashkin}, and later variable-Lebesgue work, but no explicit
later solution of the fixed-weight question.  Astashkin's results make the
criterion conceptually adjacent to known gap theorems, and it may be implicit
in broader almost-compact embedding theory; originality confidence is
therefore moderate rather than high.
