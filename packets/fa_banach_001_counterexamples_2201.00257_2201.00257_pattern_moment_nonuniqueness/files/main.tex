\section*{Source question}

The source is Tapesh Yadav, \emph{Wigner type laws for structured random
matrices} \cite{yadav}, available as
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00257}{arXiv:2201.00257}.  A pattern is a
set $S\subset[0,1]^2$ with null boundary.  The source fills the corresponding
grid cells with centered iid entries of variance one, scaled by $N^{-1/2}$,
and proves convergence of all normalized starred moments.  Section 5.3 asks:

\begin{quote}
If two patterns are not similar, must their collections of limiting starred
moments be different?
\end{quote}

Here ``similar'' means that their approximating matrices are similar for
infinitely many sizes.  The answer is no.

\section*{Result}

For $S\subset[0,1]^2$, write $K_S=\1_S$.  Call $S$
\emph{$p$-biregular} if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:biregular}
 \int_0^1K_S(x,y)\,dy=p\quad\text{for a.e. }x,
 \qquad
 \int_0^1K_S(x,y)\,dx=p\quad\text{for a.e. }y.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Biregular moment collapse]\label{thm:general}
Let $S$ satisfy \eqref{eq:biregular}.  For every starred word $C$ of
length $2n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapse}
 m_C(S)=p^n m_C([0,1]^2),
\end{equation}
where $m_C(S)$ is the limiting normalized expected starred moment of the
source.  The corresponding odd and unbalanced moments vanish.  Consequently,
all $p$-biregular patterns have the same complete collection of limiting
starred moments.
\end{theorem}

This gives an infinite-dimensional failure of injectivity, not merely an
isolated coincidence.  The following two elementary patterns make it a full
counterexample to the source question:
\begin{align}
 S_= &:=([0,\tfrac12]^2)\cup([\tfrac12,1]^2),\label{eq:equal}\\
 S_{\ne}&:=([0,\tfrac12]\times[\tfrac12,1])
 \cup([\tfrac12,1]\times[0,\tfrac12]).\label{eq:unequal}
\end{align}
Changing their boundary points is immaterial.  In block notation their
indicators are
\[
 K_{S_=}\sim\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 K_{S_{\ne}}\sim\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both are $1/2$-biregular and hence have identical limiting starred moments.
Nevertheless, their approximating matrices are almost surely never similar
from size two onward when the allowed iid entry law is continuous.

\section*{Why all moments collapse}

The proof is the tree structure hidden in the source's path-counting formula.

\begin{lemma}[Oriented tree integral]\label{lem:tree}
Let $T$ be a tree with $n$ edges, each oriented arbitrarily.  If $S$ is
$p$-biregular, then
\[
 \int_{[0,1]^{V(T)}}
 \prod_{u\to v\in E(T)}K_S(x_u,x_v)\prod_{u\in V(T)}dx_u=p^n.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a leaf.  If its incident edge points away from the leaf, integration
in the leaf variable uses the second identity in \eqref{eq:biregular}; if it
points toward the leaf, it uses the first.  In either case this removes the
leaf and contributes $p$.  Iterate over all $n$ edges.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:general}]
Expand $N^{-1}\E\Tr C(X_{N,S},X_{N,S}^*)$.  Centering and independence
leave, at leading order, pairings in which $n$ distinct entries occur once
unconjugated and once conjugated.  A leading pairing has $n+1$ free index
classes.  Its quotient graph is connected because the cyclic trace word
walks through all its vertices.  It therefore has $n+1$ vertices and $n$
edges, hence is a tree.

For a fixed leading pairing, the normalized sum over its free indices tends
to the integral in Lemma \ref{lem:tree}; the orientation records which of the
two occurrences is unstarred.  Its value is $p^n$, while the same pairing
has weight one for the full-square pattern.  Summing over the leading
pairings proves \eqref{eq:collapse}.  If the word is odd or has unequal
numbers of starred and unstarred letters, no leading conjugate pairing exists,
so its limit is zero.  This is also a direct specialization of the source's
polar constraint-path formula: a constraint path of length $2n$ on $n+1$
colors has precisely such a tree as its underlying graph.
\end{proof}

For \eqref{eq:equal}--\eqref{eq:unequal}, Lemma \ref{lem:tree} has a discrete
interpretation.  Every tree has exactly two two-colorings in which adjacent
labels are equal (the two constant labelings), and exactly two in which they
are unequal (the two bipartite colorings).  Since there are $2^{n+1}$ block
assignments, either pattern gives each leading tree the same weight
$2/2^{n+1}=2^{-n}$.

\section*{Why the two patterns are not similar}

The source declares matrix entry $(i,j)$ active when the cell with horizontal
index $j$ and vertically reversed row index $i$ meets $S$ in nonempty
interior.  This vertical reversal interchanges the two block pictures above:
$S_{\ne}$ yields diagonal-block support, while $S_=$ yields off-diagonal-
block support.

Every diagonal entry of $X_{N,S_{\ne}}$ is therefore active.  No diagonal
entry of $X_{N,S_=}$ is active for even $N$.  For odd $N$, only the
unique central grid cell that straddles $1/2$ is active.  Choose standard
complex Gaussian entries, which satisfy all assumptions of the source.  For
each $N\ge2$, the two traces are different linear combinations of
continuous independent variables; their equality has probability zero.
Taking the countable intersection over $N$, almost surely
\[
 \Tr X_{N,S_=}\ne\Tr X_{N,S_{\ne}}\qquad(N\ge2).
\]
Similar matrices have equal trace.  Hence the two approximating matrices are
not similar for any $N\ge2$, almost surely, and the patterns are not similar
in the source's sense.  Combined with Theorem \ref{thm:general}, this proves
the claimed full counterexample.

\section*{Verification and scope}

The accompanying script exhaustively enumerates complex-Gaussian leading
pairings for every balanced word through length ten.  It checked 350 words
and 1618 leading pairings; in every term both block patterns have weight
$2^{-n}$.  This is a finite audit of the combinatorics, while the leaf
argument above proves all orders.

Searches on 2026-08-12 for the exact title and exact moment-uniqueness wording,
as well as searches for biregular/doubly-stochastic variance profiles and
starred moments, found literature on circular laws for variance profiles but
no later paper answering this pattern-injectivity question.  The run's four
cheap indexes likewise contained no result for arXiv:2201.00257.  This is a
bounded novelty check rather than a claim to have exhausted non-arXiv sources.

The theorem fully answers the yes/no injectivity question negatively and
identifies a large moment-equivalence class.  It does not classify every pair
of patterns with the same starred moments.  Note also that the proof of
non-similarity uses only the necessary trace invariant, so it does not depend
on any convention about whether ordinary or unitary similarity was intended.

\section*{Human review recommendation}

Review as a likely-valid full counterexample.  The two useful independent
checks are: (i) every leading pairing quotient really is a tree, and (ii) the
vertical row-index reversal makes $S_{\ne}$, rather than $S_=$, the
diagonal-block approximant.  Neither point affects the equality-of-moments
proof, but both make the non-similarity witness transparent.
