\section*{Source question}

The source is David Mu\~noz-Lahoz, \emph{Band projections and order
idempotents in Banach lattice algebras}, arXiv:2407.09149v2 (2024).
Question~1.1 on page~2 (quoting Wickstead's Question~5.3) asks:
\begin{quote}
Is every Banach lattice algebra isometrically (isomorphic) to a closed
subalgebra and sublattice of some space of regular operators?
\end{quote}
Here the intended concrete targets are $\mathcal L^r(X)$ for Dedekind
complete Banach lattices (or vector lattices) $X$, and a representation
preserves both lattice and algebra operations.

\section*{Separate later answer}

The same author subsequently answered this question explicitly:

\begin{quote}
David Mu\~noz-Lahoz, \emph{Wickstead's conjecture on positive projections
and non-representable Banach lattice algebras}, arXiv:2604.14697v3,
10 August 2026.
\end{quote}

Its abstract states that there are two-dimensional Banach lattice algebras
with no faithful representation as regular operators on any Dedekind complete
Banach lattice.  The introduction, page~2, says verbatim that this ``answers
[9, Question 5.3] and [10, Problem 2] in the negative.''  Thus the supporting
authors---indeed, the source and supporting author are the same---explicitly
identify the resolved problem.

The decisive result is Proposition~4.3 on pages~14--15.  If a
lattice-ordered algebra contains nonzero positive idempotents $e,p$ with
$ep=pe=p$ and
\[
 p=\alpha e+x,\qquad x\wedge e=0,
 \qquad
 \alpha\notin\{0\}\cup\{1/n:n\in\mathbb N\},
\]
then it has no faithful representation on $\mathcal L^r(X)$ for any Dedekind
complete vector lattice $X$.

Example~4.4, pages~15--16, applies this to $\R^2$ with pointwise
multiplication and cone
\[
 P^1_\beta=\{(s,t)\in\R^2:0\le s,\ \beta s\le t\le s\},
 \qquad -1\le\beta\le0.
\]
This is normable as a unital Banach lattice algebra.  Whenever
\[
 \beta\notin\{0\}\cup
 \{-1/(n-1):n\in\mathbb N,\ n\ge2\},
\]
it admits no faithful representation, whether or not the representation is
required to be unital.  For example, $\beta=-2/3$ gives a concrete
two-dimensional counterexample.

\section*{Status and scope}

The answer to Question~1.1 of arXiv:2407.09149 is therefore \textbf{no}.
This is an exact later-paper resolution, not a new result of the present run.
The later theorem is stronger than merely disproving isometric
representability: it rules out every faithful lattice-and-algebra
representation of the displayed examples on any Dedekind complete vector
lattice.
