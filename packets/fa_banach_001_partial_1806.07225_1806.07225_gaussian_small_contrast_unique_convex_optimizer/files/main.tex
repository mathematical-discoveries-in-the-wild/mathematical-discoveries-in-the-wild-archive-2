\section{Source conjecture}

Osting and Simanek consider
\[
 E[\rho]=\frac12\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}k(x,y)\rho(x)\rho(y)\,dx\,dy
\]
over densities of mass one satisfying
$\rho_-\leq\rho\leq\rho_+$.  Their Conjecture 3.8 asks whether convexity of
$\Omega$ and positivity of $\rho_-$ force uniqueness of the maximizer and
convexity of its high-density region $B_+$ \cite{OS}.

\section{Proof intuition}

At small contrast the energy is led by a linear ``bathtub'' problem.  Its
potential is the convolution of the convex body's indicator with a Gaussian,
whose level sets have strictly positive curvature.  The nonlinear correction
does not merely give convergence to this reference set: the KKT condition
defines a threshold map on fixed-volume sets, and that map is a contraction.
The unique fixed point is therefore the unique maximizer.  Its threshold
surface is a small $C^2$ perturbation of a strictly convex surface, hence stays
convex.  The argument does not control arbitrary contrast.

\section{The partial theorem}

Let $\Omega\subset\R^d$ be a bounded convex body, let $M=|\Omega|$ and
$c=M^{-1}$, and fix $\tau>0$ and $\theta\in(0,1)$.  Put
\[
 G_\tau(z)=(4\pi\tau)^{-d/2}e^{-|z|^2/(4\tau)},\qquad
 k_\tau(x,y)=G_\tau(x-y).
\]
For $\varepsilon>0$ define
\[
 \rho_-^{\varepsilon}=c-\theta\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 \rho_+^{\varepsilon}=c+(1-\theta)\varepsilon.
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every bounded convex body $\Omega$, $\tau>0$, and
$\theta\in(0,1)$, there is $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that, whenever
$0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$, the Gaussian energy problem with bounds
$\rho_-^{\varepsilon},\rho_+^{\varepsilon}$ has a unique maximizer modulo
null sets.  It has the form
\[
 \rho_\varepsilon=c+\varepsilon(\1_{B_\varepsilon}-\theta),
 \qquad |B_\varepsilon|=\theta M,
\]
and $B_\varepsilon$ is convex modulo null sets.
\end{theorem}

The Gaussian is explicitly listed among the source's model kernels.  There is
a wording caveat: $e^{-r^2/(4\tau)}$ is completely monotone in $r^2$, not in
$r$.  Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:main} addresses the source's explicitly intended
Gaussian subclass even if its preceding condition is read literally.

\section{Proof}

Define the container potential
\[
 V(x)=(G_\tau*\1_\Omega)(x)=\int_\Omega G_\tau(x-y)\,dy.
\]

\subsection*{Step 1: strict curvature of Gaussian-potential levels}

Let $\mu_x$ be the probability measure on $\Omega$ with density
$G_\tau(x-y)/V(x)$.  Differentiation under the integral gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hess}
 \nabla^2\log V(x)
 =-\frac1{2\tau}I+\frac1{4\tau^2}\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_x}(Y).
\end{equation}
The Brascamp--Lieb covariance inequality gives
$\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_x}(Y)\preceq2\tau I$ \cite{BL}.  Equality in a
nonzero direction would require an untruncated Gaussian direction; this is
impossible because $\Omega$ is bounded.  Hence the inequality in
\eqref{eq:hess} is negative definite.  On every compact level surface of
$V$, compactness gives a uniform negative bound.

In particular, $V$ has a unique maximum, every other level is noncritical,
and every positive superlevel of $V$ has strictly convex smooth boundary.
For the unique number $t_\theta$ satisfying
\[
 |\Omega\cap\{V>t_\theta\}|=\theta M,
\]
the reference bathtub set
$B_0=\Omega\cap\{V>t_\theta\}$ is convex.

\subsection*{Step 2: the threshold map}

Let $\mathcal B_\theta$ be the measurable subsets of $\Omega$ of measure
$\theta M$, modulo null sets, with metric
$d(B,C)=|B\symdiff C|$.  This metric space is complete.  For
$B\in\mathcal B_\theta$ set
\[
 \rho_B=c+\varepsilon(\1_B-\theta),\qquad
 P_B=G_\tau*\rho_B=cV+\varepsilon G_\tau*(\1_B-\theta\1_\Omega).
\]
Let $\lambda_B$ be the fixed-volume quantile of $P_B$ and define
\[
 T(B)=\Omega\cap\{P_B>\lambda_B\},\qquad |T(B)|=\theta M.
\]
Gaussian convolution is analytic and nonconstant, so its level sets have zero
Lebesgue measure and the definition is unambiguous.

For $B,C\in\mathcal B_\theta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sup}
 \|P_B-P_C\|_\infty
 \leq \varepsilon\|G_\tau\|_\infty |B\symdiff C|.
\end{equation}
Fixed-volume quantiles are $1$-Lipschitz under uniform perturbations, so
$|\lambda_B-\lambda_C|$ is bounded by the right side of
\eqref{eq:sup}.  Uniformly in $B$, $P_B$ is $C^2$-close to $cV$ as
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$.  The selected levels therefore stay in a compact
noncritical band around $ct_\theta$.  The coarea formula supplies constants
$A,s_0>0$, independent of $B$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:band}
 \big|\{x\in\Omega:|P_B(x)-\lambda_B|\leq s\}\big|\leq As
 \quad(0<s<s_0).
\end{equation}
If $x\in T(B)\symdiff T(C)$, the two threshold inequalities and
\eqref{eq:sup} place $x$ in one of the bands in \eqref{eq:band} with width at
most twice the right side of \eqref{eq:sup}.  Consequently
\[
 |T(B)\symdiff T(C)|
 \leq 2A\varepsilon\|G_\tau\|_\infty |B\symdiff C|.
\]
For sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, $T$ is a strict contraction and has a
unique fixed point $B_\varepsilon$.

\subsection*{Step 3: the fixed point is the unique maximizer}

The Gaussian kernel is strictly positive definite, so the quadratic energy is
strictly convex as a functional of $\rho$.  A maximizer over the bounded mass
class is therefore bang--bang, hence is $\rho_B$ for some
$B\in\mathcal B_\theta$.  The usual mass-transfer first variation says that
high density can occur only on the top fixed-measure values of $P_B$;
otherwise moving equal mass from a lower-potential high-density piece to a
higher-potential low-density piece raises the energy.  Thus every maximizer
satisfies $B=T(B)$ modulo null sets.  The contraction has only one fixed
point, proving uniqueness.

Finally, $P_B$ is uniformly $C^2$-close to $cV$.  The full selected
superlevel surface $\{P_B=\lambda_B\}$ is a small normal perturbation of the
strictly convex surface $\{V=t_\theta\}$.  Its principal curvatures remain
positive.  Hence $\{P_B>\lambda_B\}$ is convex, and so is its intersection
with the convex body $\Omega$.  Applying this to the fixed point proves that
$B_\varepsilon$ is convex and completes the proof. \qed

\section{Scope, checks, and novelty}

The theorem proves both conclusions of Conjecture 3.8 for every fixed volume
fraction in a sufficiently small Gaussian density-contrast regime.  It does
not settle arbitrary contrast or the source's entire kernel class.  The same
contraction proof applies to any smooth kernel for which the selected
container-potential level has uniform positive curvature and convolution
gives a uniform $C^2$ perturbation bound.

A bounded search through 13 August 2026 used the exact title and conjecture,
together with combinations of \emph{Gaussian kernel}, \emph{bang--bang
density}, \emph{small contrast}, \emph{unique optimizer}, \emph{convex
superlevel}, and \emph{threshold map}.  It found the standard log-concavity
and nonlocal-perimeter literature but no exact result for this problem.
Novelty confidence is moderate because the ingredients are classical.  The
main reviewer targets are the uniform coarea estimate at a nonsmooth container
boundary and strictness in the truncated-Gaussian covariance inequality.
