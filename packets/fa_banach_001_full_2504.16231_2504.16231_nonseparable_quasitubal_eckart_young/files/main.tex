\begin{abstract}
The separability assumption in the quasitubal construction of
Mor--Avron is unnecessary.  For an arbitrary orthonormal-basis index set
$I$, the correct pair is $\ell_2(I)\triangleleft\ell_\infty(I)$.  We identify
the quasitube algebra, construct the qSVD pointwise, and prove the exact
global Eckart--Young formula for finite matrices whose entries lie in the
possibly nonseparable Hilbert space.  The key fact is that every individual
$\ell_2(I)$ tensor has countable coordinate support.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source question and answer}

Section 9 of Mor--Avron asks how to ``define and establish optimality results
for non separable Hilbert spaces'' (pages 32--33 of the arXiv PDF).

We answer the nonseparable-Hilbert branch completely: replace the countable
coordinate set by an arbitrary index set.  The algebraic qSVD works on all
coordinates, while square summability automatically makes the approximation
problem countable for each concrete tensor.  We treat complex Hilbert spaces;
the real version is identical with orthogonal in place of unitary matrices.

\section{Setup}

Let $\Hh$ be an arbitrary Hilbert space, let $(e_i)_{i\in I}$ be an
orthonormal basis, and let
\[
 F:\Hh\longrightarrow\ell_2(I),\qquad
 (Fx)_i=\langle x,e_i\rangle
\]
be the coordinate unitary.  Here sums over arbitrary sets mean suprema of
finite partial sums.  Define
\[
 x\star y=F^{-1}((Fx)(Fy)),\qquad x,y\in\Hh,
\]
where the product on the right is coordinatewise.  This is well-defined
because $\ell_2(I)\ell_2(I)\subset\ell_1(I)\subset\ell_2(I)$.
Put $\A=\ell_\infty(I)$, acting diagonally on $\ell_2(I)$ and hence on
$\Hh$.  Thus $\Hh\cong\ell_2(I)$ is a two-sided $*$-ideal in the commutative
unital $C^*$-algebra $\A$.

For $X=(x_{ab})\in\A^{m\times p}$, write
\[
 X(i)=\bigl(x_{ab}(i)\bigr)_{a,b}\in\mathbb C^{m\times p}.
\]
The quasitubal product and adjoint are characterized by
$(XY)(i)=X(i)Y(i)$ and $X^*(i)=X(i)^*$.  We use
\[
 \|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{i\in I}\|X(i)\|_2.
\]
If $X\in\Hh^{m\times p}$, its Hilbert--Frobenius norm is
\[
 \|X\|_{\Hh,F}^2
 =\sum_{a,b}\|x_{ab}\|_{\Hh}^2
 =\sum_{i\in I}\|X(i)\|_F^2.
\]
Finally define the implicit rank
\[
 \irank(Y)=\sum_{i\in I}\rank Y(i)
\]
as a cardinal-valued sum.  For finite $q$, $\irank(Y)\le q$ says precisely
that only finitely many slices are nonzero and their matrix ranks have total
at most $q$; it therefore agrees with the scalar dimension of the range of
the induced quasitubal multiplier in the only case used below.

\section{Idea of Proof}

There are three short observations.
First, module endomorphisms of $\ell_2(I)$ are diagonal: the coordinate
idempotents force this, and boundedness makes their diagonal an element of
$\ell_\infty(I)$.  Second, a qSVD is just an ordinary matrix SVD of every
$X(i)$; no measurability or continuity selection is required because
$\ell_\infty(I)$ contains all bounded families.  Third, if the entries of
$X$ lie in $\ell_2(I)$, then
$\sum_{i,j}s_j(i)^2<\infty$.  Hence the positive scalar singular values have
countable support and can be sorted.  Ordinary matrix Eckart--Young on every
slice reduces the global problem to allocating $q$ retained scalar singular
values, whose optimum is plainly to retain the largest $q$.

\section{Algebra and qSVD without separability}

\begin{lemma}[The arbitrary-index quasitube algebra]
Every $\Hh$-module endomorphism of $(\Hh,\star)$ is automatically bounded,
and these endomorphisms form a commutative
unital $C^*$-algebra canonically and isometrically $*$-isomorphic to
$\ell_\infty(I)$.  Under this identification, $a\in\ell_\infty(I)$ acts by
$M_a x=F^{-1}(aFx)$ and $\|M_a\|=\|a\|_\infty$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Work in $\ell_2(I)$ and denote the coordinate vector by $\delta_i$.  If $T$
is a module map, then
\[
 T\delta_i=T(\delta_i^2)=\delta_iT\delta_i=a_i\delta_i.
\]
For arbitrary $x$, module linearity gives
$\delta_iTx=T(\delta_i x)=a_ix_i\delta_i$; hence $Tx=(a_ix_i)_i$.
If $(a_i)$ were unbounded, we could choose distinct $i_n$ with
$|a_{i_n}|\ge n^2$ and take $x=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-1}\delta_{i_n}\in\ell_2(I)$.
Then $Tx\notin\ell_2(I)$, a contradiction.  Thus $a\in\ell_\infty(I)$.
Conversely every bounded family
$a$ defines such a module map, and testing on $\delta_i$ gives
$\|M_a\|=\sup_i|a_i|$.  Composition and adjoint become coordinatewise
multiplication and conjugation, proving the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Arbitrary-index qSVD]
Every $X\in\A^{m\times p}$ has a factorization
\[
 X=U\,S\,V^*,
\]
where $U\in\A^{m\times m}$ and $V\in\A^{p\times p}$ are unitary and
$S\in\A^{m\times p}$ is diagonal with
$s_1(i)\ge\cdots\ge s_r(i)\ge0$ for $r=\min(m,p)$ and every $i\in I$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every $i$, choose an ordinary full matrix SVD
$X(i)=U(i)S(i)V(i)^*$.  Every entry of a unitary matrix has modulus at most
one, and $s_j(i)\le\|X(i)\|_2\le\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}$.  Thus all chosen
entries define elements of $\ell_\infty(I)$, and the pointwise identities are
exactly the asserted identities over $\A$.
\end{proof}

\section{Global Eckart--Young theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Nonseparable quasitubal Eckart--Young]
Let $X\in\Hh^{m\times p}$ and let $s_j(i)$ be the singular values in an
arbitrary-index qSVD.  List the positive multiset
\[
 \{s_j(i):i\in I,\ 1\le j\le r\}
\]
with multiplicity in nonincreasing order
$\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\ge\cdots>0$, padding by zeros if the list is finite.
For $q\ge0$, let $X_{[q]}$ be obtained by retaining singular rank-one terms
corresponding to the first $q$ entries.  Ties may be resolved arbitrarily,
subject to retaining earlier singular directions within each slice.  Then
\begin{align*}
 \irank(X_{[q]})&\le q,\\
 \inf_{\substack{Y\in\A^{m\times p}\\\irank(Y)\le q}}
 \|X-Y\|_{\Hh,F}^2
 &=\|X-X_{[q]}\|_{\Hh,F}^2
 =\sum_{n>q}\sigma_n^2,\\
 \|X-X_{[q]}\|_{\mathrm{op}}&=\sigma_{q+1}\longrightarrow0.
\end{align*}
Moreover $X_{[q]}$ has entries in the span of at most $q$ members of the
chosen orthonormal basis of $\Hh$, so every truncation is finitely
representable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Unitary invariance of the Frobenius norm in every finite slice gives
\[
 \sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j=1}^r s_j(i)^2
 =\sum_{i\in I}\|X(i)\|_F^2
 =\|X\|_{\Hh,F}^2<\infty.                                      \tag{1}
\]
An arbitrary square-summable family has countable support: for each $k\ge1$
the set on which its absolute value is at least $1/k$ is finite, and the
support is the countable union of these sets.  This proves that the displayed
multiset is countable.  Equation (1) also implies $\sigma_n\to0$ and permits
the nonincreasing enumeration.

The construction of $X_{[q]}$ uses $q$ scalar rank-one terms, so its implicit
rank is at most $q$ and only finitely many basis coordinates occur.  Direct
orthogonality yields the tail identity
\[
 \|X-X_{[q]}\|_{\Hh,F}^2=\sum_{n>q}\sigma_n^2.                 \tag{2}
\]

Now take $Y$ with $\irank(Y)\le q$ and put $r_i=\rank Y(i)$.  Such a $Y$ has
finite support and belongs automatically to $\Hh^{m\times p}$.  The ordinary
matrix Eckart--Young theorem gives
\[
 \|X(i)-Y(i)\|_F^2\ge\sum_{j>r_i}s_j(i)^2.
\]
Summing in $i$, the right side deletes from (1) at most $q$ members of the
singular-value multiset.  The largest possible deleted squared mass is
$\sum_{n\le q}\sigma_n^2$, proving from (2) that no $Y$ improves on
$X_{[q]}$.

With the stated tie convention, the spectral norm of each residual slice is
its largest omitted singular value.  Taking the supremum over $i$ therefore
gives $\|X-X_{[q]}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sigma_{q+1}$, which tends to zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Separable reduction of each tensor]
For every finite $X\in\Hh^{m\times p}$ there is a separable reducing
subspace $\Hh_X\subseteq\Hh$, spanned by a countable subset of the fixed
basis, containing $X$ and all its optimal finite-rank truncations.  Thus the
nonseparable theory is globally defined on $\ell_\infty(I)$ but every
individual Hilbert-valued approximation problem is automatically separable.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take the union of the coordinate supports of the finitely many entries of
$X$.  Each support is countable, and all nonzero slice singular vectors and
truncations remain on this union.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, novelty, and scope}

A deterministic finite-model verifier enumerates every allocation of rank
budgets $0$ through $8$ among seven random complex matrix slices and confirms
that the minimum slice-wise Eckart--Young tail equals the tail after globally
sorting all scalar singular values.  It also checks the coordinate ideal and
diagonal multiplier inequalities.  The LaTeX source, verifier output, and
bounded novelty-search record accompany this packet.

The result fully answers the nonseparable-Hilbert-space branch for the
orthonormal-basis tubal product used by the source.  It does not address the
source's separate suggestion concerning Banach spaces with a semidefinite
form, nor does it optimize the choice of orthonormal basis for a given data
set.
