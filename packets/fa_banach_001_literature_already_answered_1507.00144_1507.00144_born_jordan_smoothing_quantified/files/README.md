# Born--Jordan smoothing was quantified microlocally

Status: `literature_already_answered`

## Original source

- Elena Cordero, Maurice de Gosson, and Fabio Nicola, *On the Invertibility
  of Born-Jordan Quantization*, J. Math. Pures Appl. 105 (2016), 537--557,
  [arXiv:1507.00144](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.00144).
- On source PDF page 2, the authors call it a challenging open problem to
  quantify analytically the numerically evident smoothing produced by the
  Born--Jordan kernel relative to Weyl/Wigner quantization.

## Separate answering paper

- Elena Cordero, Maurice de Gosson, and Fabio Nicola, *On the reduction of
  the interferences in the Born-Jordan distribution*, Appl. Comput. Harmon.
  Anal. 44 (2018), 230--245,
  [arXiv:1601.03719](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.03719),
  [doi:10.1016/j.acha.2016.04.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acha.2016.04.007).

## Identification

The later paper explicitly says it supplies the previously missing rigorous
quantitative explanation. Theorem 1.2 proves a gain of two microlocal
Fourier--Lebesgue derivatives in every direction
`zeta_1 dot zeta_2 != 0`. Corollary 1.3 specializes this to microlocal `H^2`
regularity for every `L^2` signal. Theorem 1.4 supplies the complementary
negative result: there is no uniform global gain of the modulation-space
smoothness exponent, explaining why horizontal and vertical directions are
exceptional.

Thus the source problem is answered in the natural sharp form: two-derivative
smoothing away from the characteristic axes, and no general global smoothing
gain across those axes.
