\noindent\textbf{Status.}
\texttt{literature\_already\_answered}.  A separate paper by the same
authors gives a quantitative microlocal smoothing theorem and a complementary
global obstruction.

\section*{Original problem}
For a signal $f$, its Born--Jordan distribution is
\[
  Qf=Wf*\Theta_\sigma,
  \qquad
  \Theta(\zeta_1,\zeta_2)
   =\frac{\sin(\pi\zeta_1\cdot\zeta_2)}
          {\pi\zeta_1\cdot\zeta_2}.
\]
On source PDF page 2, Cordero--de Gosson--Nicola observe that multiplication
by $\Theta$ visibly damps ghost frequencies but state that analytically
quantifying this smoothing effect remains a challenging open problem
\cite{CGN15}.  The intended comparison is between the Wigner distribution
$Wf$ and the Born--Jordan distribution $Qf$.

\section*{The separate answer}
The later paper \cite{CGN16} explicitly presents itself as a rigorous
quantitative explanation of this phenomenon for arbitrary tempered signals.
Let $WF_{\mathcal{F}L_s^q}$ denote the Fourier--Lebesgue wave-front set.  Its
Theorem 1.2 states that, if
\[
  f\in\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^d),\qquad
  Wf\in M^{\infty,q}(\mathbb R^{2d}),
\]
then for every $(z,\zeta)$ with
$\zeta=(\zeta_1,\zeta_2)$ and
$\zeta_1\cdot\zeta_2\ne0$,
\[
  (z,\zeta)\notin WF_{\mathcal{F}L_2^q}(Qf).
\]
Thus $Qf$ has two additional microlocal derivatives in every
non-characteristic direction.  Corollary 1.3 gives the particularly concrete
case
\[
  f\in L^2(\mathbb R^d)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  (z,\zeta)\notin WF_{H^2}(Qf)
  \quad(\zeta_1\cdot\zeta_2\ne0).
\]
These statements occur on supporting PDF page 6.

The result is also sharp in the essential geometric sense.  Theorem 1.4
proves that an estimate
\[
  \|Qf\|_{M^{p,q_1}}\le C\|Wf\|_{M^{p,q_2}}
  \qquad(f\in\mathcal S)
\]
forces $q_1\ge q_2$.  Hence there is no uniform global improvement of the
modulation-space smoothness exponent.  The exceptional set
$\zeta_1\cdot\zeta_2=0$ contains the horizontal and vertical directions that
retain interference because the Born--Jordan transform preserves marginals.

\section*{Conclusion}
The 2016/2018 paper answers the source's open problem in the natural sharp
form: Born--Jordan provides a two-derivative microlocal gain away from the
characteristic axes, while no general global smoothness gain can cross those
axes.  This is an explicit later-literature answer, not a new agent result.
