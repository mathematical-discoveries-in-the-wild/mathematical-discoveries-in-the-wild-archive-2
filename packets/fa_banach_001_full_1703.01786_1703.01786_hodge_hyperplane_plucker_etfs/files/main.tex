\begin{abstract}
Question 6.3 asks whether Pl\"ucker equiangular tight frames (ETFs) exist
with parameters different from the paper's single $(16,6)$ example, lifted
from a tight $(16,2,4)$ fusion frame.  We prove a transfer principle: every
real unit-norm $(n,t)$ ETF, for $t\geq3$, is the lifted Pl\"ucker image of the
tight rank-$(t-1)$ hyperplane fusion frame
$Q_j=I-u_ju_j^{\mathsf T}$.  The reason is that Hodge star is an isometry
$\R^t\to\bigwedge^{t-1}\R^t$ and identifies a normal vector with the
Pl\"ucker vector of its orthogonal hyperplane.  Applying this to regular
simplices gives, for every $t\geq3$, a Pl\"ucker ETF with parameters
$(n,m,l,t)=(t+1,t,t-1,t)$.  Thus the question has an affirmative answer by
an infinite family, already beginning with a $(4,3)$ ETF lifted from a tight
$(4,2,3)$ fusion frame.
\end{abstract}

\section{The source question}

The source defines an equiangular $(n,m)$-frame to be a \emph{Pl\"ucker ETF}
when it is the lifted Pl\"ucker image of a tight $(n,l,t)$ fusion frame,
where $l\geq2$ and $m=\binom{t}{l}$.  After constructing one example with
$(n,m,l,t)=(16,6,2,4)$, it asks whether examples with different parameters
exist.

\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_question_crop.png}

The answer below uses $l=t-1$.  This is not the degenerate orthonormal-basis
case: regular simplices have $n=t+1>t=m$ and nonzero common angle $1/t$.

\section{The hyperplane transfer theorem}

We use the Euclidean Hodge isometry
\[
 *:\R^t\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{t-1}\R^t.
\]
After fixing the standard ordered basis, the coordinates of a decomposable
vector $a_1\wedge\cdots\wedge a_{t-1}$ are precisely the maximal minors of
the matrix whose rows are $a_1^{\mathsf T},\ldots,a_{t-1}^{\mathsf T}$.
They are therefore precisely its Pl\"ucker coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[ETF-to-hyperplane Pl\"ucker transfer]\label{thm:transfer}
Let $t\geq3$, and let $\cH=\{u_j\}_{j=1}^n\subset\R^t$ be a real
unit-norm $(n,t)$ ETF.  Put
\[
 Q_j=I_t-u_ju_j^{\mathsf T}\qquad(1\leq j\leq n).
\]
Then $\cQ=\{Q_j\}_{j=1}^n$ is a tight $(n,t-1,t)$ fusion frame, and its
lifted Pl\"ucker vectors form an $(n,t)$ ETF.  Consequently that ETF is a
Pl\"ucker ETF in the sense of the source paper.  Moreover, $\cQ$ is itself
chordally equiangular.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $u_j$ is a unit vector, $Q_j$ is the orthogonal projection onto
$u_j^\perp$ and has rank $t-1$.  Tightness of $\cH$ says
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^n u_ju_j^{\mathsf T}=\frac nt I_t.
\]
Hence
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^n Q_j
 =nI_t-\frac nt I_t
 =\frac{n(t-1)}t I_t,
\]
which is exactly the required tight fusion-frame identity.

For each $j$, choose an orthonormal basis
$a_{j,1},\ldots,a_{j,t-1}$ of $u_j^\perp$, and let $A_j$ be the
$(t-1)\times t$ matrix having the $a_{j,r}^{\mathsf T}$ as rows.  Then
$A_jA_j^{\mathsf T}=I_{t-1}$ and $A_j^{\mathsf T}A_j=Q_j$, so $A_j$ is a
generator of $Q_j$ in the terminology of the source.  Its Pl\"ucker vector
is the coordinate vector of
\[
 a_{j,1}\wedge\cdots\wedge a_{j,t-1}.
\]
This is a unit generator of the one-dimensional space
$\bigwedge^{t-1}(u_j^\perp)$.  Therefore, for some sign
$\varepsilon_j\in\{\pm1\}$,
\[
 \overline\Phi(Q_j)=\varepsilon_j *u_j.                 \tag{1}
\]
The sign records the selected orientation class of the generator.  A
different allowed choice only changes $\varepsilon_j$, which has no effect
on tightness or on absolute inner products.

Hodge star is an isometry, and multiplication of individual frame vectors
by signs preserves the frame operator.  Thus (1) gives
\[
 \bigl|\ip{\overline\Phi(Q_j)}{\overline\Phi(Q_k)}\bigr|
 =|\ip{u_j}{u_k}|
 \quad(j\ne k)
\]
and
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^n \overline\Phi(Q_j)\overline\Phi(Q_j)^{\mathsf T}
 =\frac nt I_{\binom{t}{t-1}}
 =\frac nt I_t.
\]
The lifted vectors are consequently a unit-norm $(n,t)$ ETF.  Since
$t-1\geq2$ and $\binom{t}{t-1}=t$, this is a Pl\"ucker ETF under the
source definition.

Finally, if the common ETF coherence is $c$, then for $j\ne k$,
\[
 \tr(Q_jQ_k)
 =\tr\bigl((I-u_ju_j^{\mathsf T})(I-u_ku_k^{\mathsf T})\bigr)
 =t-2+|\ip{u_j}{u_k}|^2=t-2+c^2.
\]
Thus the fusion frame is chordally equiangular as well.
\end{proof}

\section{An infinite family with new parameters}

\begin{corollary}[Regular-simplex family]\label{cor:simplex}
For every integer $t\geq3$, there exists a real Pl\"ucker ETF with
\[
 (n,m,l,t)=(t+1,t,t-1,t).
\]
In particular these give infinitely many parameter sets different from
$(16,6,2,4)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $V=\mathbf1^\perp\subset\R^{t+1}$, identified isometrically with
$\R^t$, and set
\[
 u_j=\sqrt{\frac{t+1}{t}}
 \left(e_j-\frac1{t+1}\mathbf1\right),
 \qquad 1\leq j\leq t+1.
\]
These vectors have norm one, satisfy
$\ip{u_j}{u_k}=-1/t$ for $j\ne k$, and obey
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{t+1}u_ju_j^{\mathsf T}=\frac{t+1}{t}I_V.
\]
They are the regular-simplex $(t+1,t)$ ETF.  Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}
now applies, with $l=t-1\geq2$ and
$m=\binom{t}{t-1}=t$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The first member: a tetrahedral $(4,3)$ Pl\"ucker ETF}

In $\R^3$, take
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 u_1&=3^{-1/2}(1,1,1),&
 u_2&=3^{-1/2}(1,-1,-1),\\
 u_3&=3^{-1/2}(-1,1,-1),&
 u_4&=3^{-1/2}(-1,-1,1).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Every off-diagonal inner product is $-1/3$.  The four rank-two projections
$Q_j=I_3-u_ju_j^{\mathsf T}$ satisfy
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^4Q_j=\frac83 I_3.
\]
For example, an oriented orthonormal generator of $u_1^\perp$ has rows
\[
 a=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1,0),\qquad
 b=\frac1{\sqrt6}(1,1,-2).
\]
In the Pl\"ucker coordinate order $(12,13,23)$, its three maximal minors are
\[
 (\det[a_1\ a_2;b_1\ b_2],
   \det[a_1\ a_3;b_1\ b_3],
   \det[a_2\ a_3;b_2\ b_3])
 =\frac1{\sqrt3}(1,-1,1).
\]
This is $*u_1$ under the standard coordinate identification
$*(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(x_3,-x_2,x_1)$.  The other three generators give
$\pm *u_j$, so their Pl\"ucker images are exactly a tetrahedral $(4,3)$ ETF.
This is lifted from a tight $(4,2,3)$ fusion frame and is already a concrete
parameter set distinct from the source example.

\section{Checks, scope, and novelty guardrail}

The proof uses only two identities: complementation converts the frame
operator into the tight fusion-frame operator, and Hodge star identifies the
normal line with the top exterior power of its hyperplane.  The determinant
coordinates in the source definition are exactly the coordinates of that
exterior product.  Orientation ambiguity produces only independent signs.

The result answers Question 6.3 as literally posed, and more strongly gives
an infinite family with $n>m$ and nonzero common angle.  It does not claim
that these examples reproduce the source's chordally \emph{biangular}
$(16,2,4)$ precursor; rather, their precursors are chordally equiangular,
which is permitted by Definition 6.2 and follows from the final calculation
in Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}.

\paragraph{Review focus.}
The only convention-sensitive point is (1): a reader should check the source's
chosen ordering of maximal minors against the chosen Hodge-star coordinate
ordering.  Any discrepancy is a fixed signed permutation, hence an ambient
orthogonal map, and any generator-orientation discrepancy is an individual
sign.  Neither changes the ETF conclusion.
