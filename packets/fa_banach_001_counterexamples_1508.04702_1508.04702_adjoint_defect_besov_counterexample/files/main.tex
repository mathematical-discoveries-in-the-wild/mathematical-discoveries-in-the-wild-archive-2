\fbox{\parbox{0.9\textwidth}{
\textbf{Status: candidate counterexample, likely valid.}
There are bounded self-adjoint almost commuting operators $A,B$ and a
real-valued $\varphi\in B^1_{\infty,1}(\R^2)$ for which
$\varphi(A,B)^*-\varphi(A,B)$ is not trace class.  This answers Problem~2 of
Aleksandrov--Peller negatively.
}}
\end{center}

\section{Source problem}

Aleksandrov and Peller define, for bounded self-adjoint $A,B$, the ordered
double-operator-integral calculus
\[
 \varphi(A,B)=\iint_{\R^2}\varphi(x,y)\,dE_A(x)\,dE_B(y).
\]
Their Problem~2, in Section~6 on PDF pages 18--19 of
arXiv:1508.04702 \cite{AP}, asks whether
\[
 \big(\varphi(A,B)\big)^*-\overline\varphi(A,B)\in\Sone.
\]
for every $\varphi\in\B$, whenever $A,B$ are bounded self-adjoint and
$[A,B]\in\Sone$.  They had proved this for the smaller projective tensor
class $B^1_{\infty,1}(\R)\widehat\otimes B^1_{\infty,1}(\R)$.

\section{Idea of the proof}

For two spectral values in each variable, reversing the order of the two
spectral projections detects the rectangular four-point difference of
$\varphi$.  If both spectral gaps have size $h$, the commutator of the two
operators costs $h^2$, whereas a $B^1_{\infty,1}$ atom at that scale can have
a rectangular difference of order $h$.  One such block is harmless.  The
separation appears after taking many copies: at scale $h_k\asymp2^{-k}$, use
$k2^k$ identical blocks.  Their commutator costs sum to a convergent series,
but their adjoint defects sum to the harmonic series.  A coefficient
$1/k^2$ on the $k$-th Besov atom keeps the Besov norm summable.

\section{Construction and proof}

Put
\[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 C=[Q,P]=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both $P$ and $Q$ are rank-one orthogonal projections, and
\begin{equation}
 \|C\|_{\Sone}=1. \label{eq:C}
\end{equation}

For $k\geq1$, set
\[
 h_k=2^{-k-4},\qquad a_k=4h_k,\qquad N_k=k2^k,
\]
and define two self-adjoint $2\times2$ matrices
\[
 A_k=(a_k-h_k/2)I+h_kP,\qquad B_k=-h_kI/2+h_kQ.
\]
Their two spectral values are $a_k\pm h_k/2$ and $\pm h_k/2$, respectively,
and
\begin{equation}
 [A_k,B_k]=h_k^2[P,Q],\qquad
 \|[A_k,B_k]\|_{\Sone}=h_k^2. \label{eq:blockcomm}
\end{equation}
On
\[
 \mathcal H=\bigoplus_{k\geq1}(\mathbb C^2)^{\oplus N_k}
\]
let $A$ and $B$ be the direct sums of $N_k$ copies of $A_k$ and $B_k$.
They are bounded and self-adjoint.  By \eqref{eq:blockcomm},
\begin{equation}
 \|[A,B]\|_{\Sone}
 =\sum_{k\geq1}N_kh_k^2
 =2^{-8}\sum_{k\geq1}k2^{-k}
 =\frac1{128}<\infty. \label{eq:globalcomm}
\end{equation}

Choose a real $\eta\in C_c^\infty((-3/4,3/4))$ such that
$\eta(-1/2)=0$ and $\eta(1/2)=1$, and put $F(u,v)=\eta(u)\eta(v)$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \varphi(x,y)=\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{h_k}{k^2}
 F\left(\frac{x-a_k}{h_k},\frac{y}{h_k}\right). \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
The $x$-supports of the summands are disjoint: the $k$-th lies in
$((13/4)h_k,(19/4)h_k)$, while the next lies strictly to its left.

\begin{lemma}
The real function $\varphi$ in \eqref{eq:phi} belongs to $B^1_{\infty,1}(\R^2)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Delta_j$ be any fixed homogeneous Littlewood--Paley resolution used to
define $B^1_{\infty,1}$.  Translation does not change the norm.  Since
$h_k=2^{-(k+4)}$, dyadic scaling merely shifts the Littlewood--Paley index,
and therefore
\[
 \left\|h_kF\left(\frac{\,\cdot-z_k}{h_k}\right)
 \right\|_{B^1_{\infty,1}}
 \leq C_F,
 \qquad z_k=(a_k,0),
\]
with a constant independent of $k$.  (Equivalently, the factor $h_k$ exactly
cancels the degree-one scaling factor $h_k^{-1}$.)  Hence the $k$-th summand
of \eqref{eq:phi} has Besov norm at most $C_F/k^2$.  The series converges in
$B^1_{\infty,1}$ because $\sum k^{-2}<\infty$.  Its pointwise sum is the
displayed continuous real function (the disjoint supports also make this
identification immediate).
\end{proof}

We next record the finite-block identity.  Write $P_+=P$, $P_-=I-P$,
$Q_+=Q$, and $Q_-=I-Q$.  Given four real numbers $t_{\varepsilon\delta}$,
the ordered double operator integral on one block is
\[
 T=\sum_{\varepsilon,\delta\in\{+,-\}}
 t_{\varepsilon\delta}P_\varepsilon Q_\delta.
\]
Using $P_-=I-P$ and $Q_-=I-Q$ gives
\begin{equation}
 T^*-T=
 (t_{++}-t_{+-}-t_{-+}+t_{--})[Q,P]. \label{eq:blockidentity}
\end{equation}

At the four spectral pairs of $(A_k,B_k)$, all summands of \eqref{eq:phi} except the
$k$-th vanish.  The choice of $\eta$ gives
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &h_k/2&-h_k/2\\ \hline
 a_k+h_k/2&h_k/k^2&0\\
 a_k-h_k/2&0&0
 \end{array}
\]
for the four values of $\varphi$.  Applying \eqref{eq:blockidentity} and
\eqref{eq:C}, the trace norm of
the adjoint defect on every copy of the $k$-th block is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \left\|\varphi(A_k,B_k)^*-\varphi(A_k,B_k)\right\|_{\Sone}
 =\frac{h_k}{k^2}. \label{eq:blockdefect}
\end{equation}
The common block decomposition reduces both spectral measures, so the global
double operator integral is the direct sum of these block integrals.  Thus
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \left\|\varphi(A,B)^*-\varphi(A,B)\right\|_{\Sone}
 &=\sum_{k\geq1}N_k\frac{h_k}{k^2}\\
 &=\frac1{16}\sum_{k\geq1}\frac1k=\infty.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:globaldefect}
\end{equation}
Since $\varphi$ is real, $\overline\varphi(A,B)=\varphi(A,B)$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:globalcomm} and \eqref{eq:globaldefect} prove the promised
counterexample.

\begin{theorem}
There exist bounded self-adjoint operators $A,B$ with $[A,B]\in\Sone$ and a
real-valued $\varphi\in B^1_{\infty,1}(\R^2)$ such that
\[
 \varphi(A,B)^*-\overline\varphi(A,B)\notin\Sone.
\]
Consequently, Problem~2 of Aleksandrov--Peller has a negative answer.
\end{theorem}

\section{Verification and novelty status}

The included script \texttt{code/verify\_blocks.py} checks
\eqref{eq:blockidentity}, including its trace norm, on 1,000 random four-point
tables and evaluates the partial sums in \eqref{eq:globalcomm} and
\eqref{eq:globaldefect}.  This computation is only a regression check; the
proof above is exact.

A bounded novelty search covered arXiv and exact-web queries for the paper
title, ``Problem 2,'' the adjoint formula, ``almost multiplicative,'' and
$B^1_{\infty,1}(\R^2)$, plus the citing literature visible from the journal
record and Peller's 2024 survey \cite{PellerSurvey}.  It found the original
problem and repeated accounts of the proved commutator/trace formula, but no
later proof or counterexample to this adjoint-defect question.  Novelty is
therefore plausible, not certified.

\section{Limitations and review recommendation}

This theorem completely negates Problem~2, but does not decide the separate
product-defect question in Problem~1.  Human review
should focus first on the degree-one dyadic scaling in the Besov lemma and on
the order/sign in \eqref{eq:blockidentity}.  The finite-block identity is
independently checked by the included script.
