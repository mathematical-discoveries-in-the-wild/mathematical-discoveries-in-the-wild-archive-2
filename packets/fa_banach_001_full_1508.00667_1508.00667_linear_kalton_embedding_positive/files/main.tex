\section{Source problem}

Dikranjan, Shakhmatov, and Sp\v{e}v\'ak define the integer Kalton map
\[
   \KA:\bigoplus_{a\in A}\langle a\rangle\longrightarrow E
\]
and its real-linear extension
\[
   \lKA:\bigoplus_{a\in A}\R a\longrightarrow E.
\]
In Section 10, Question 10.6, they ask whether a topologically isomorphic
embedding $\KA$ forces $\lKA$ to be a topologically isomorphic embedding as
well \cite{DSS}.

Equivalently, their preceding results already give that $\lKA$ is an
injective continuous linear map, and the remaining issue is whether it is
open onto $\spanR A$.

\section{Definitions and source reductions}

All topological vector spaces below are Hausdorff and real. For a complex
space, one may pass to the underlying real space.

A set $A\subset E\setminus\{0\}$ is \emph{topologically independent} if for
every neighbourhood $W$ of $0$ there is a neighbourhood $V$ of $0$ such that,
for every finitely supported family $(z_a)_{a\in A}$ of integers,
\[
   \sum_{a\in A}z_a a\in V
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   z_a a\in W\quad(a\in A).
\]
It is \emph{absolutely Cauchy summable} if every neighbourhood $U$ of $0$
contains the subgroup generated by $A\setminus F$ for some finite
$F\subset A$.

We use four results proved in the source \cite{DSS}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\KA$ is a topologically isomorphic embedding if and only if $A$ is
topologically independent and absolutely Cauchy summable (Theorem 5.1).
\item A topologically independent subset of a real topological vector space
is linearly independent (Proposition 4.11(i)).
\item If $A$ is absolutely Cauchy summable, then for every neighbourhood
$U$ of $0$ there is a finite $F\subset A$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
   \spanR(A\setminus F)\subset U
\end{equation}
(Proposition 9.2(i)), and $\lKA$ is continuous (Proposition 10.1).
\item For linearly independent $A$, the linear Kalton map is open onto its
image if and only if every canonical coordinate projection
\[
  \pi^A_a:\spanR A\longrightarrow \R a
\]
is continuous (Proposition 10.2).
\end{enumerate}

Thus it suffices to prove continuity of every $\pi^A_a$.

\section{Proof intuition}

The obstruction exhibited by a discontinuous real coordinate is a sequence
of very small real combinations whose distinguished coefficient stays equal
to one. Topological independence only tests integer combinations, so the key
is to round a suitable multiple of such a real combination.

The rounding is split into two regions. Absolute Cauchy summability puts the
entire real span of a cofinite tail inside a prescribed small neighbourhood,
so all tail rounding errors are harmless regardless of their size. Only a
finite head remains. Simultaneous Dirichlet approximation rounds those finitely
many coefficients with a uniformly bounded denominator. The allegedly
discontinuous vector is chosen only after that denominator bound is known, so
every possible multiple remains small. The rounded integer combination then
contradicts topological independence at the distinguished coordinate.

\section{A simultaneous rounding lemma}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:dirichlet}
Let $r_1,\ldots,r_m\in\R$ and let $Q\ge 2$ be an integer. There exist an
integer $q$ with $1\le q\le Q^m$ and integers $z_1,\ldots,z_m$ such that
\[
   |q r_j-z_j|\le \frac1Q\qquad(1\le j\le m).
\]
For $m=0$, take $q=1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $m\ge1$, place the $Q^m+1$ points
\[
  (\{k r_1\},\ldots,\{k r_m\}),\qquad 0\le k\le Q^m,
\]
in the $Q^m$ half-open boxes of side length $1/Q$ partitioning
$[0,1)^m$. Two points lie in the same box. Subtract their indices and the
corresponding integer parts. The positive index difference is at most $Q^m$
and has the asserted property.
\end{proof}

\section{Main theorem and proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $E$ be a Hausdorff real topological vector space and let
$A\subset E\setminus\{0\}$. If the integer Kalton map $\KA$ is a
topologically isomorphic embedding, then the linear Kalton map $\lKA$ is a
topologically isomorphic embedding.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the source reductions, $A$ is topologically independent, absolutely
Cauchy summable, and linearly independent; moreover, $\lKA$ is an injective
continuous linear map. It remains to prove that it is open onto $\spanR A$.

Fix $a_0\in A$. Write
\[
   \pi^A_{a_0}(x)=\varphi(x)a_0\qquad(x\in\spanR A),
\]
where $\varphi$ is the coefficient functional at $a_0$. Suppose for a
contradiction that $\varphi$ is discontinuous.

The cyclic subgroup $\Z a_0$ is discrete in the one-dimensional subspace
$\R a_0$. Hence there is a neighbourhood $W$ of $0$ in $E$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclic}
   W\cap\Z a_0=\{0\}.
\end{equation}
Let $V$ be a $W$-witness for the topological independence of $A$. Choose
neighbourhoods $U_1,U_2,U_3$ of $0$, with $U_1$ balanced, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sum}
   U_1+U_2+U_3\subset V.
\end{equation}

Apply \eqref{eq:tail} to $U_3$. After adjoining $a_0$ to the exceptional
set, we obtain a finite $F\subset A$ with $a_0\in F$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tailsmall}
   \spanR(A\setminus F)\subset U_3.
\end{equation}
Put $H=F\setminus\{a_0\}$ and $m=|H|$. The finite-dimensional map
\[
   \R^H\longrightarrow E,\qquad (t_b)_{b\in H}\longmapsto
   \sum_{b\in H}t_b b,
\]
is continuous. Therefore there is $\eta>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:headsmall}
   |t_b|<\eta\ (b\in H)
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   \sum_{b\in H}t_b b\in U_2.
\end{equation}
Choose an integer $Q\ge2$ with $1/Q<\eta$, and set $N=Q^m$ (with
$N=1$ when $m=0$).

A discontinuous nonzero linear functional takes every real value on every
neighbourhood of zero: otherwise its image on some neighbourhood would be a
proper balanced subset of $\R$ and hence bounded, forcing continuity. Apply
this to the neighbourhood $(N^{-1}U_1)\cap\spanR A$. There is a finitely
supported real combination
\begin{equation}\label{eq:x}
   x=\sum_{b\in A}r_b b\in N^{-1}U_1,
   \qquad r_{a_0}=\varphi(x)=1.
\end{equation}

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:dirichlet} to $(r_b)_{b\in H}$. We obtain
$1\le q\le N$ and integers $z_b$ for $b\in H$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:headround}
   |z_b-q r_b|<\eta\qquad(b\in H).
\end{equation}
Set $z_{a_0}=q$. For each $b\in A\setminus F$ in the finite support of $x$,
choose an integer $z_b$ nearest to $q r_b$; set all other $z_b$ equal to zero.
The integer combination $y=\sum_b z_b b$ has finite support, and
\begin{align*}
 y
 &=qx+
   \sum_{b\in H}(z_b-q r_b)b+
   \sum_{b\in A\setminus F}(z_b-q r_b)b.
\end{align*}
Because $x\in N^{-1}U_1$, $q\le N$, and $U_1$ is balanced, the first term
belongs to $U_1$. The second belongs to $U_2$ by
\eqref{eq:headsmall}--\eqref{eq:headround}. The third is an element of
$\spanR(A\setminus F)$ and hence belongs to $U_3$ by
\eqref{eq:tailsmall}. Thus \eqref{eq:sum} gives $y\in V$.

Since $V$ is a $W$-witness, topological independence forces
$z_{a_0}a_0=q a_0\in W$. This contradicts \eqref{eq:cyclic}, because
$q\ge1$. Therefore $\varphi$, and hence $\pi^A_{a_0}$, is continuous.

The choice of $a_0$ was arbitrary, so every canonical coordinate projection
is continuous. Proposition 10.2 of the source now makes $\lKA$ open onto its
image. Together with its injectivity and continuity, this proves that $\lKA$
is a topologically isomorphic embedding.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification notes}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The order of choices is essential. The tail neighbourhood and finite
set $F$ are fixed first; the denominator bound $N$ is then determined by the
finite head; only afterward is $x$ chosen in $N^{-1}U_1$. Thus no circular
dependence is hidden in the proof.
\item The tail estimate controls the full real span, so the tail rounding
errors need no coefficient bound. This is the precise role of absolute Cauchy
summability beyond continuity of $\lKA$.
\item All combinations are finitely supported. No convergence or completeness
of $E$ is used.
\item The distinguished coefficient is rounded without error because
$r_{a_0}=1$ and $z_{a_0}=q$.
\item No computational verification is relevant. The separate
\texttt{verification\_report.md} records an adversarial step check.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Novelty check and limitations}

The local run indexes were searched for arXiv:1508.00667, the exact wording of
Question 10.6, \emph{linear Kalton map}, \emph{topologically independent}, and
\emph{absolutely Cauchy summable}. A bounded external search used the exact
question, paper title and authors, and close variants involving integer and
linear Kalton embeddings. It returned the source records but no later paper
claiming to answer Question 10.6. The search was not an exhaustive citation
audit, so novelty confidence is moderate.

The theorem answers Question 10.6. It does not settle Question 9.8 of the
source for arbitrary absolutely Cauchy summable sets lacking topological
independence.
