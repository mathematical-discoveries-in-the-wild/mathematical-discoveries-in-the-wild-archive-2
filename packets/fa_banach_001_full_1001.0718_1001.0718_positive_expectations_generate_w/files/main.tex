\section*{Source Question and Status}

Douglas and Nowak work over the real scalars and put
$\C=\ell_\infty(G)$.  They define $\W$ as a weak-* closed subspace of
\[
 \mathcal L\bigl(\mathcal L(\ellU,\C),\C\bigr)
\]
and let $\M_+$ be the set of normalized expectations in $\W$ whose
restriction to $\ell_\infty(G,\C)$ is positive.  Their Question 5.2 asks
whether every $\Xi\in\W$ can be represented as
\[
 \Xi=cM-c'M',\qquad M,M'\in\M_+,quad c,c'\geq0
\]
\cite[p.~17]{DN}.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

This packet gives an affirmative answer for every finitely generated group.
The proof uses only the definitions of $\W$ and $\M_+$; exactness and group
invariance are not needed.  Status: \emph{candidate full solution, likely
valid}, subject to human review.

\section*{Definitions}

An element $\xi\in\ellU$ is written as a family
$\xi=(\xi_g)_{g\in G}$ with $\xi_g\in\C$, and
\[
 \|\xi\|_u=\left\|\sum_{g\in G}|\xi_g|\right\|_{\C}.
\]
It induces the evaluation operator
\[
 \widehat\xi(T)=T(\xi),
 \qquad T\in\mathcal L(\ellU,\C),
\]
and the source proves that $\xi\mapsto\widehat\xi$ is isometric.  Let
\[
 \W_{00}=\left\{\xi:\#\operatorname{supp}\xi<\infty,
 \ \sum_g\xi_g=c1_G\text{ for some }c\in\mathbb R\right\}.
\]
Then $\W$ is the weak-* closure of $\widehat{\W}_{00}$.  An expectation is
an $M\in\W$ with $M(\onefam)=1_G$, and $M\in\M_+$ when, additionally,
its restriction to $\ell_\infty(G,\C)$ is positive.  Here
$\onefam$ denotes the constant coefficient family used in the source.

\section*{Proof Intuition}

Pointwise Jordan decomposition nearly settles the question.  For finite
$\xi$, its positive and negative coefficient families $a,b$ satisfy
$\xi=a-b$, but their total masses $A=\sum a_g$ and $B=\sum b_g$ can vary
over $G$ and hence cannot be normalized by scalar coefficients.  Since
$A-B$ is scalar, the same positive slack $R1_G-A$ can be added to both
families at one group coordinate.  Their difference is unchanged, while
their new masses become the scalars $R$ and $R-c$.  A uniform choice of $R$
works along a weak-* approximating net, so compactness passes the finite
decompositions to $\W$.

\section*{Main Result}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $\Xi\in\W$ there exist $M,M'\in\M_+$ and
$c,c'\in[0,\infty)$ such that
\[
 \Xi=cM-c'M'.
\]
Consequently, the answer to Question 5.2 of \cite{DN} is yes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a net $\xi_\alpha\in\W_{00}$ such that
$\widehat\xi_\alpha\to\Xi$ weak-*.  Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:augmentation}
 \sum_g(\xi_\alpha)_g=c_\alpha1_G.
\end{equation}
A weak-* convergent net in a dual Banach space is norm bounded.  Since the
evaluation embedding is isometric, there is $K<\infty$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bound}
 \|\xi_\alpha\|_u\leq K\qquad\text{for every }\alpha.
\end{equation}
Fix once and for all a scalar $R>K$.

Take pointwise positive and negative parts in the real Banach lattice $\C$:
\[
 a_{\alpha,g}=((\xi_\alpha)_g)^+,
 \qquad b_{\alpha,g}=((\xi_\alpha)_g)^-.
\]
Set $A_\alpha=\sum_ga_{\alpha,g}$ and
$B_\alpha=\sum_gb_{\alpha,g}$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:masses}
 A_\alpha-B_\alpha=c_\alpha1_G,
 \qquad
 0\leq A_\alpha\leq A_\alpha+B_\alpha
 =\sum_g|({\xi_\alpha})_g|\leq K1_G.
\end{equation}
Thus $r_\alpha=R1_G-A_\alpha$ is positive.  If $e$ is the identity of
$G$, define finite coefficient families
\begin{align*}
 p_{\alpha,g}&=a_{\alpha,g}+\mathbf 1_{\{e\}}(g)r_\alpha,\\
 q_{\alpha,g}&=b_{\alpha,g}+\mathbf 1_{\{e\}}(g)r_\alpha.
\end{align*}
They are positive and their difference is $\xi_\alpha$.  Moreover,
using \eqref{eq:masses},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-masses}
 \sum_gp_{\alpha,g}=R1_G,
 \qquad
 \sum_gq_{\alpha,g}=(R-c_\alpha)1_G.
\end{equation}
The inequality $|c_\alpha|\leq\|\xi_\alpha\|_u\leq K<R$ shows that both
normalizing scalars are positive.  Therefore
\[
 M_\alpha=R^{-1}\widehat p_\alpha,
 \qquad
 M'_\alpha=(R-c_\alpha)^{-1}\widehat q_\alpha
\]
belong to $\M_+$.  Indeed, they lie in $\widehat\W_{00}$, are normalized by
\eqref{eq:scalar-masses}, and are positive on
$\ell_\infty(G,\C)$ because all their coefficients are positive.  Their
norms are one: for a positive coefficient family the $\ell_u$-norm is its
total scalar mass.  We have the exact finite decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-decomp}
 \widehat\xi_\alpha=RM_\alpha-(R-c_\alpha)M'_\alpha.
\end{equation}

The pair $(M_\alpha,M'_\alpha)$ lies in the product of two weak-* compact
unit balls.  Pass to a simultaneous subnet converging weak-* to $(M,M')$.
Since $\W$ is weak-* closed, $M,M'\in\W$; evaluation at
$\onefam$ shows that both remain normalized.  Positivity also passes to
the limit: the positive cone of $\C=\ell_\infty(G)$ is weak-* closed, so
$M(f),M'(f)\geq0$ for every positive
$f\in\ell_\infty(G,\C)$.  Hence $M,M'\in\M_+$.

Finally, \eqref{eq:augmentation} and weak-* convergence show that
$c_\alpha\to c$ for a scalar $c$ and
$\Xi(\onefam)=c1_G$.  Taking limits in
\eqref{eq:finite-decomp} gives
\[
 \Xi=RM-(R-c)M'.
\]
Since $|c|\leq K<R$, both coefficients are nonnegative.  This is the
required representation.
\end{proof}

\section*{Scope, Novelty Check, and Verification}

The theorem proves algebraic generation, which is stronger than merely
placing $\W$ in the weak-* closed linear span of $\M_+$.  It does not answer
the source's separate, open-ended question about uniqueness of invariant
expectations.  The notation matters: immediately before Question 5.2 the
source defines $\M$ using only conditions (a) and (b) of invariant
expectations, so members of $\M_+$ need not themselves be group invariant.

The cheap run indexes contained no answer.  A bounded search through
17 August 2026 used the exact question, the title and authors, and the
phrases \emph{positive expectations generate $\W$} and \emph{invariant
expectations exact groups}.  It found the source, its published version, and
later cohomological applications, but no answer to Question 5.2.

The companion script performs 500 randomized finite-dimensional checks of
the common-slack identities.  These are consistency checks only; the weak-*
argument above is the proof.
