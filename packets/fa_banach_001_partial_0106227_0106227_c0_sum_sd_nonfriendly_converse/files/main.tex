\section*{Status and source question}

This packet gives a likely valid partial answer to the question following
Proposition 4.3(c) of Bilik--Kadets--Shvidkoy--Sirotkin--Werner
\cite{BKSSW}.  For a compact Hausdorff space \(K\), a Banach space \(E\),
and an arbitrary range space \(W\), that proposition says
\[
 \bigl[\text{every }T\in\SD(C(K,E),W)\text{ is \(C\)-narrow}\bigr]
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \SD(E)=\{0\}.
\]
The authors ask whether the converse holds.  Their May 2025 monograph
\cite{KMRW} repeats the result as Proposition 8.3.4 and still lists the
converse as Question 8.2.

We do not settle the unrestricted converse.  We prove it for every Banach
space assembled as an arbitrary \(c_0\)-sum of separable USD-nonfriendly
spaces.  The resulting space need not be separable.  The proof is an
\(M\)-summand permanence argument which extends the isolated \(E=c_0\)
observation in the source.

\section*{Notation and source facts}

Write \(\mathsf P_K(E)\) for the following property:
\begin{quote}
For every Banach space \(W\), every strong Daugavet operator
\(T:C(K,E)\to W\) is \(C\)-narrow.
\end{quote}
Write \(\mathsf P(E)\) if \(\mathsf P_K(E)\) holds for every compact
Hausdorff \(K\).

We use four results proved in \cite{BKSSW} (also reproduced in Chapter 8 of
\cite{KMRW}).
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(X=X_1\oplus_\infty X_2\) and \(T\in\SD(X,W)\), then each
restriction \(T|_{X_j}\) is a strong Daugavet operator.
\item If \(T:C(K,E)\to W\), then \(T\) is \(C\)-narrow if and only if, for
every \(x\in E\),
\[
 T_x:C(K)\to W,\qquad T_xg=T(g\otimes x),
\]
is \(C\)-narrow.
\item Finite sums of scalar \(C\)-narrow operators on \(C(K)\) are
\(C\)-narrow.
\item Every separable USD-nonfriendly space has \(\mathsf P(E)\), and every
USD-nonfriendly space is SD-nonfriendly.
\end{enumerate}

Recall that \(E_0\subset E\) is an \(M\)-summand when
\(E=E_0\oplus_\infty F\) for some closed \(F\subset E\).

\section*{Two elementary closure lemmas}

\begin{lemma}[Norm closure]
\label{lem:closed}
For fixed \(K\) and \(W\), the scalar \(C\)-narrow operators
\(C(K)\to W\) form a norm-closed class.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(R_n\to R\) in operator norm, with every \(R_n\) \(C\)-narrow.  Use the
local unit-bump characterization from \cite{BKSSW}.  Given a nonempty open
\(U\subset K\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(n\) with
\(\|R-R_n\|<\varepsilon/2\).  There is a nonnegative \(g\in C(K)\) with
\(\|g\|=1\), \(\supp g\subset U\), and
\(\|R_ng\|<\varepsilon/2\).  Then
\(\|Rg\|<\varepsilon\).  Thus \(R\) is \(C\)-narrow.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite \(\ell_\infty\)-stability]
\label{lem:finite}
If \(\mathsf P_K(E_j)\) holds for \(j=1,\dots,m\), then it holds for
\(E=E_1\oplus_\infty\cdots\oplus_\infty E_m\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(T:C(K,E)\to W\) be strong Daugavet.  Since
\[
 C(K,E)=C(K,E_1)\oplus_\infty\cdots\oplus_\infty C(K,E_m),
\]
the restriction \(T_j=T|_{C(K,E_j)}\) is strong Daugavet, hence
\(C\)-narrow by \(\mathsf P_K(E_j)\).

For \(x=(x_1,\dots,x_m)\in E\),
\[
 T_xg=\sum_{j=1}^m T_j(g\otimes x_j).
\]
Each summand is a scalar \(C\)-narrow operator, and their finite sum is
\(C\)-narrow.  Therefore every \(T_x\) is \(C\)-narrow, so the scalar
restriction criterion gives that \(T\) is \(C\)-narrow.
\end{proof}

\section*{Dense \(M\)-summand permanence}

\begin{theorem}[Dense \(M\)-summand principle]
\label{thm:skeleton}
Let \(E\) have a family \((E_\alpha)_{\alpha\in A}\) of \(M\)-summands whose
union is norm dense in \(E\).  If every \(E_\alpha\) has
\(\mathsf P_K(E_\alpha)\), then \(E\) has \(\mathsf P_K(E)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(T:C(K,E)\to W\) be strong Daugavet.  If
\(E=E_\alpha\oplus_\infty F_\alpha\), then pointwise decomposition gives
\[
 C(K,E)=C(K,E_\alpha)\oplus_\infty C(K,F_\alpha).
\]
Hence \(T|_{C(K,E_\alpha)}\) is strong Daugavet and, by hypothesis,
\(C\)-narrow.  Consequently \(T_x\) is scalar \(C\)-narrow for every
\(x\in\bigcup_\alpha E_\alpha\).

For all \(x,y\in E\),
\[
 \|T_x-T_y\|\leq \|T\|\,\|x-y\|.
\]
The union of the \(E_\alpha\)'s is dense, so Lemma \ref{lem:closed} implies
that \(T_x\) is \(C\)-narrow for every \(x\in E\).  The scalar restriction
criterion now shows that \(T\) is \(C\)-narrow.
\end{proof}

There is a parallel observation for SD-nonfriendliness.

\begin{lemma}[Dense SD-nonfriendly \(M\)-summands]
\label{lem:sd}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem \ref{thm:skeleton}, if every \(E_\alpha\)
is SD-nonfriendly, then \(E\) is SD-nonfriendly.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(S:E\to Y\) is strong Daugavet, then every restriction
\(S|_{E_\alpha}\) is strong Daugavet and hence zero.  Density of the union
and continuity of \(S\) give \(S=0\).
\end{proof}

\section*{The \(c_0\)-sum theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrary \(c_0\)-sums]
\label{thm:c0}
Let \((E_\gamma)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\) be any family of Banach spaces and put
\[
 E=\Bigl(\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_\gamma\Bigr)_{c_0}.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if every \(E_\gamma\) has \(\mathsf P_K(E_\gamma)\), then \(E\) has
\(\mathsf P_K(E)\);
\item if every \(E_\gamma\) is SD-nonfriendly, then \(E\) is
SD-nonfriendly.
\end{enumerate}
The index set \(\Gamma\) is unrestricted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a finite \(F\subset\Gamma\), let
\[
 E_F=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in F}^{\ell_\infty}E_\gamma.
\]
It is an \(M\)-summand of \(E\), and the union of all \(E_F\) is the finitely
supported subspace, which is norm dense in the \(c_0\)-sum.  Lemma
\ref{lem:finite} gives \(\mathsf P_K(E_F)\), so Theorem
\ref{thm:skeleton} proves (1).  For (2), repeated restriction to the
coordinate \(M\)-summands shows that every finite \(E_F\) is SD-nonfriendly;
Lemma \ref{lem:sd} then applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A broad positive class for the source question]
\label{cor:usd}
If every \(E_\gamma\) is separable and USD-nonfriendly, then
\[
 E=\Bigl(\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_\gamma\Bigr)_{c_0}
\]
is SD-nonfriendly and has \(\mathsf P(E)\).  In particular, this holds when
every \(E_\gamma\) is finite-dimensional.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each component is SD-nonfriendly and has \(\mathsf P(E_\gamma)\) by the
source results.  Apply Theorem \ref{thm:c0} for every compact \(K\).
\end{proof}

Thus Proposition 4.3(c) is an equivalence throughout this class, including
nonseparable examples obtained from uncountable \(\Gamma\).

\section*{Why the full converse is not claimed}

The proof uses a feature absent from a general SD-nonfriendly space: every
vector is norm approximable inside \(M\)-summands on which a strong Daugavet
operator restricts as a strong Daugavet operator.  A general separable
subspace does not have this restriction property.

Two natural full-solution routes stop at the same point.  First, quotienting
the scalar maps \(T_x:g\mapsto T(g\otimes x)\) by the space of \(C\)-narrow
operators would settle the question if the induced operator on \(E\) were
strong Daugavet.  A strong Daugavet witness only controls \(Tf\), however,
and does not control \(T(gf)\) for scalar cutoffs \(g\).  Second, localization
and ultrapower arguments need exactly the same cutoff stability to turn
vector-valued witnesses into approximately strong Daugavet maps on \(E\).
No such implication follows from the definitions.

The packet therefore promotes Corollary \ref{cor:usd} as a partial result and
leaves the unrestricted Question 8.2 open.

\section*{Novelty and review notes}

The cheap run indexes were searched for the exact arXiv id and for the terms
SD-nonfriendly, USD-nonfriendly, \(C\)-narrow, strong Daugavet,
\(M\)-summand, direct sum, and \(c_0\)-sum.  A bounded primary-source web
search found \cite{BKSSW} and the 2025 monograph \cite{KMRW}, but no later
answer or explicit version of Theorems \ref{thm:skeleton}--\ref{thm:c0}.

Human review should focus on the use of the strong-Daugavet restriction
theorem for \(\ell_\infty\)-summands and on the scalar-restriction
characterization of \(C\)-narrowness.  Once those source results are granted,
the remaining argument is elementary.
