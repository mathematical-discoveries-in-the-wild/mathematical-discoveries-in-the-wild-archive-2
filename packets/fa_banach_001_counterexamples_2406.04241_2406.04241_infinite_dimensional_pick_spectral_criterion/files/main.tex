\begin{abstract}
Schober asks whether the finite-dimensional criteria for regular
operator-valued Pick functions extend to infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces.
They do not extend verbatim.  On $\mathcal H=\ell^2(\mathbb N)$ let
$De_n=n^{-1}e_n$ and $F(z)=zD$.  The associated boundary group is the direct
sum of rescaled standard shifts.  An explicit coordinatewise dilation
conjugates it to the standard shift of multiplicity $\ell^2$, so $F$ is
regular and its multiplicity space is $\ell^2$.  Nevertheless
\[
  0\in\spec(F(z))\quad(z\in\Cplus),
  \qquad D(F,0)=\mathbb R.
\]
Thus conditions (b), (d), and (e) of source Theorem~3.1.7 and the zero-set
condition of Theorem~3.1.10 fail for a regular infinite-dimensional Pick
function.  Condition (c), involving actual kernels, still holds; the precise
obstruction is continuous spectrum accumulating at zero.
\end{abstract}

We use the natural unchanged definitions for an infinite-dimensional complex
Hilbert space $\mathcal H$.  Thus a norm-holomorphic
$F:\Cplus\to B(\mathcal H)$ is Pick if $\operatorname{Im}F(z)\geq0$, it has
self-adjoint boundary values $F_*(x)$ almost everywhere, and it is regular if
\[
 \bigcap_{t\in\mathbb R}e^{itF_*}H^2(\Cplus,\mathcal H)=\{0\},
 \qquad
 \overline{\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb R}e^{itF_*}
 H^2(\Cplus,\mathcal H)}=L^2(\mathbb R,\mathcal H).
\]

\section{Proof intuition}

Finite dimension does not distinguish spectrum from approximate point
spectrum in the way needed here: a spectral point on the real axis is an
eigenvalue and produces a fixed Hardy-space direction.  In infinite
dimension, strictly positive eigenvalues may accumulate at zero.  Taking the
orthogonal sum of the scalar regular functions $z/n$ preserves regularity,
but zero enters the spectrum without creating any zero eigenvector.  This
breaks every condition in the source that tests the full spectrum while
leaving the kernel condition intact.

\section{A diagonal family}

The counterexample is a special case of a transparent family.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:family}
Let $(a_n)_{n\geq1}$ be a bounded sequence of strictly positive real numbers,
let $\mathcal H=\ell^2(\mathbb N)$, and set
\[
 De_n=a_ne_n,\qquad F(z)=zD\quad(z\in\Cplus).
\]
Then $F$ is an operator-valued Pick function with self-adjoint boundary
values and is regular.  More precisely,
\[
 (L^2(\mathbb R,\mathcal H),H^2(\Cplus,\mathcal H),U^F)
 \cong
 (L^2(\mathbb R,\ell^2),H^2(\Cplus,\ell^2),S),
\]
so its multiplicity space is $\ell^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The function $F$ is norm-holomorphic and
\[
 \operatorname{Im}F(z)=(\operatorname{Im}z)D\geq0.
\]
Its boundary values are $F_*(x)=xD$, which are self-adjoint.  Hence the
associated group acts coordinatewise by
\[
 (U_t^Ff)_n(x)=e^{ita_nx}f_n(x).
\]

For each $n$ define a positive-dilation unitary on $L^2(\mathbb R)$ by
\[
 (W_nf)(y)=a_n^{-1/2}f(y/a_n).
\]
Positive dilation preserves $H^2(\Cplus)$, and a direct calculation gives
\[
 W_nM_{e^{ita_nx}}=M_{e^{ity}}W_n.
\]
Therefore $W=\bigoplus_{n\geq1}W_n$ is a unitary on
$L^2(\mathbb R,\ell^2)$, maps $H^2(\Cplus,\ell^2)$ onto itself, and satisfies
\[
 WU_t^FW^{-1}=S_t=M_{e^{ity}}\otimes\mathbf 1_{\ell^2}
 \qquad(t\in\mathbb R).
\]
The standard shift triple on the right is a regular one-parameter group with
multiplicity space $\ell^2$.  Hence so is the triple generated by $F$.
\end{proof}

\section{Counterexample to both finite-dimensional criteria}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:counterexample}
On $\mathcal H=\ell^2(\mathbb N)$ let
\[
 De_n=\frac1n e_n,\qquad F(z)=zD.
\]
Then $F$ is regular, but:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\spec(F(z))\not\subseteq\Cplus$ for every $z\in\Cplus$;
\item $D(F,0)=\mathbb R$, so the singleton and general zero-set criteria of
  source Theorems 3.1.7 and 3.1.10 fail;
\item nevertheless,
\[
 \ker(\lambda\mathbf1-M_{F_*})=\{0\}
 \quad\text{on }L^2(\mathbb R,\ell^2)
 \quad(\lambda\in\mathbb R).
\]
Thus both equivalences fail in infinite dimension.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Regularity and the multiplicity statement follow from
Lemma~\ref{lem:family}.  The operator $D$ is compact, positive, and injective,
with
\[
 \spec(D)=\{0\}\cup\left\{\frac1n:n\geq1\right\}.
\]
For $z\in\Cplus$ the scalar $z$ is nonzero, so
\[
 \spec(F(z))=z\spec(D)
 =\{0\}\cup\left\{\frac zn:n\geq1\right\}.
\]
Because $0\notin\Cplus$, condition (b) of source Theorem~3.1.7 fails.

For every $y\in\mathbb R$, zero belongs to $\spec(yD)$.  Recalling the source
notation
\[
 D(F,M)=\{y\in\mathbb R:M\cap\spec(F_*(y))\neq\varnothing\},
\]
we therefore have
\[
 D(F,0)=D(F,\{0\})=\mathbb R.
\]
Thus $\mathbb R\setminus D(F,0)$ has zero measure and $D(F,0)$ is not a
Lebesgue zero-set.  This disproves conditions (d) and (e) of Theorem~3.1.7.
Since $\{0\}$ itself is a Lebesgue zero-set, it also disproves the implication
from regularity to the zero-set condition in Theorem~3.1.10.

Finally, suppose $(\lambda\mathbf1-M_{F_*})f=0$ for some
$f=(f_n)_{n\geq1}\in L^2(\mathbb R,\ell^2)$.  The $n$th coordinate gives
\[
  (\lambda-y/n)f_n(y)=0\quad\text{for almost every }y\in\mathbb R.
\]
Outside the singleton $\{n\lambda\}$ the scalar coefficient is nonzero.
Hence $f_n=0$ as an $L^2$ function for every $n$, and therefore $f=0$.
This proves the kernel assertion and shows that the failed spectral point is
continuous rather than point spectrum.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, limitations, and novelty}

The dilation formula gives an explicit isomorphism of regular
one-parameter groups, so regularity does not rest on an informal direct-sum
argument.  For the chosen $a_n=1/n$ it reads
\[
  (W_nf)(y)=\sqrt n\,f(ny),
\]
which is readily checked to be unitary and to preserve the Hardy half-plane.
The spectrum computation uses only the standard spectrum of a compact
diagonal operator.  The multiplication-kernel calculation is stronger than
the source's condition (c), because it proves triviality on all of $L^2$, not
only on its Hardy subspace.

The source's phrase ``similar results'' is broad.  This packet gives a full
negative answer to the verbatim extension of the two explicitly cited
classification theorems.  It does not preclude corrected infinite-dimensional
criteria formulated with point spectrum or spectral measures.  Nor does it
contradict a possible infinite-cardinal version of the multiplicity formula:
the example has explicitly computed multiplicity $\ell^2$.

A bounded novelty search on August 13, 2026 checked all four lightweight run
indexes for the arXiv id, exact title, regular operator-valued Pick functions,
the spectrum condition, and the infinite-dimensional question.  External
searches used the exact title/id and the phrases ``infinite-dimensional
Hilbert'', ``regular Pick function'', and ``$\operatorname{Spec}(F(z))$''.
They found arXiv v2 and copies of the thesis, but no later paper stating this
counterexample.  Priority remains subject to specialist review.

This packet is recommended for expert review as a likely valid full scoped
counterexample.  The main audit points are the natural infinite-dimensional
extension of the source definitions, preservation of vector-valued Hardy
space by the coordinatewise dilation $W$, and the distinction between point
and continuous spectrum at zero.
