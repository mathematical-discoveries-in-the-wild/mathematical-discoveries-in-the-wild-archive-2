\section{Source question}

Rueda Zoca defines the Daugavet thickness indices
\begin{align*}
\Tidx(X)
&=\inf\left\{r>0:
\begin{array}{l}
\text{some nonempty relatively weakly open }W\subset B_X\\
\text{is covered by finitely many }B(x_i,r),\ x_i\in S_X
\end{array}\right\},\\
\Tidx_{w^*}(X^*)
&=\inf\left\{r>0:
\begin{array}{l}
\text{some nonempty relatively weak-star open }W\subset B_{X^*}\\
\text{is covered by finitely many }B(x_i^*,r),\ x_i^*\in S_{X^*}
\end{array}\right\}.
\end{align*}
Proposition 4.3 gives the lower bound
\[
 \|T+I\|\ge
 \max\{\Tidx(X),\Tidx_{w^*}(X^*)\}
\]
for norm-one weakly compact \(T\). Question 2 on PDF page 16 then asks
whether, for a weakly compact operator \(T:X\to X\),
\[
 \|T+I\|=\max\{\Tidx(X),\Tidx_{w^*}(X^*)\}.
\tag{Q2}
\]
See \cite{source}.

\section{Proof intuition}

The right side of (Q2) measures whether some weakly open piece of the unit
ball can be covered by small balls. In a Hilbert space, a slice very near a
unit vector is an arbitrarily small spherical cap, so both thickness indices
vanish. This has no reason to force \(\|I+T\|\) to vanish. Taking \(T=I\)
makes the mismatch maximal while retaining norm one and weak compactness.

\section{Counterexample}

\begin{theorem}
The equality \emph{(Q2)} is false. It remains false if \(X\) is required to
be separable and infinite-dimensional and \(T\) is required to have norm one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(H=\ell_2\) over the real scalars and let \(e=e_1\). For
\(0<\delta<1\), consider
\[
 W_\delta=\{x\in B_H:\langle x,e\rangle>1-\delta\}.
\]
This is a nonempty relatively weakly open subset of \(B_H\), and
\(e\in W_\delta\). For every \(x\in W_\delta\),
\[
 \|x-e\|^2
 =\|x\|^2+\|e\|^2-2\langle x,e\rangle
 <2\delta.
\]
Consequently
\[
 W_\delta\subset B(e,\sqrt{2\delta})
 \qquad\text{and hence}\qquad
 \Tidx(H)\le\sqrt{2\delta}.
\]
Letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) gives \(\Tidx(H)=0\).

The Hilbert space \(H\) is reflexive. Thus the weak-star topology
\(\sigma(H^*,H)\) and the weak topology \(\sigma(H^*,H^{**})\) coincide
under the canonical identification \(H^{**}=H\). Applying the same cap
argument in the Hilbert space \(H^*\) gives
\[
 \Tidx_{w^*}(H^*)=0.
\]

Now take \(T=I_H\). It has norm one. It is weakly compact because the closed
unit ball of the reflexive space \(H\) is weakly compact. But
\[
 \|T+I_H\|=\|2I_H\|=2,
 \qquad
 \max\{\Tidx(H),\Tidx_{w^*}(H^*)\}=0.
\]
This contradicts (Q2).
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and novelty}

The source's Question 2 omits the norm-one hypothesis present in its
immediately preceding proposition. The example above deliberately uses a
norm-one operator, so adding that hypothesis does not repair the equality.
The same proof works on any nonzero reflexive Banach space whose primal and
dual balls have arbitrarily small weak slices; Hilbert space is the cleanest
instance.

This packet does not address Question 1 on projective tensor products of
\(L\)-embedded Daugavet spaces.

A bounded primary-source search through 12 August 2026 used the exact arXiv
id and title, the displayed equality, and combinations of \emph{Daugavet
thickness}, \emph{weakly compact operator}, and \emph{counterexample}. It
found later work on other thickness-index questions but no explicit prior
answer to (Q2). Since the contradiction is elementary and the printed
question may be an unintended formulation, novelty confidence is moderate;
the appropriate claim is a correction to the literal statement.
