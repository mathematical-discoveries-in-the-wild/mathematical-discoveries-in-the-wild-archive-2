\section{Source problem}

Jaming and Komornik study moving observations of the periodic beam equation
\cite{JK}.  Their Open Question (iv), on PDF page 19, asks whether their
inverse inequality (4.8) holds for any two distinct non-integer rationals
$a_1,a_2$.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{Open Question (iv) and its context in \cite[p.~19]{JK}.}
\end{figure}

For reference, a solution has the Fourier representation
\[
 u(t,x)=c_0^++c_0^-t+
 \sum_{k\in\Z\setminus\{0\}}
 \left(c_k^+e^{i(k^2t+kx)}+c_k^-e^{i(-k^2t+kx)}\right).
\]
Given $T>0$, base points $(t_j,x_j)$, and velocities $a_j$, the requested
inverse inequality is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obs}
 \sum_{k\in\Z}\bigl(|c_k^+|^2+|c_k^-|^2\bigr)
 \le C\sum_{j=1}^2\int_0^T
 |u(t_j+t,x_j-a_jt)|^2\,dt.
\end{equation}

\section{Proof intuition}

The only obstruction left by the source's nonharmonic Fourier estimates is a
finite cycle of coincident frequencies.  Such coincidences are encoded by
lattice points on the circles
\[
 S_a=\{(x,y)\in\R^2:x^2-ax+y^2+ay=0\}.
\]
In a cycle between two non-integral parameters $a\ne b$, the lattice points
can be written alternately as $(x_i,y_i)\in S_a$ and
$(x_{i+1},y_i)\in S_b$.  Subtracting the paired circle equations and summing
once around the cycle makes all quadratic terms telescope.  This forces the
sums of the two coordinates to agree.  Summing just the $S_a$ equations then
forces a sum of squares to be zero, which is impossible because the origin is
not a graph vertex.

\section{The finite collision graph}

Put
\[
 A_a=(S_a\cap\Z^2)\setminus\{(0,0)\},
\]
and let $A_a^+$ and $A_a^-$ be its first- and second-coordinate projections.
The set $A_a$ is finite.  Moreover, it is empty if $a$ is irrational, because
every $(x,y)\in A_a$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameter}
 a=\frac{x^2+y^2}{x-y}\in\mathbb Q.
\end{equation}

For distinct parameters $a,b$, form a graph whose vertices are
$A_a\cup A_b$, joining two vertices when they share a coordinate.  The
source's estimate (4.9), with the harmless observation phases left explicit,
has the form
\begin{align}\label{eq:source-reduction}
 \int_0^T|u(t_j+t,x_j-a_jt)|^2\,dt\asymp{}&
 \sum_{k\notin A_{a_j}^+}|c_k^+|^2+
 \sum_{m\notin A_{a_j}^-}|c_m^-|^2\\
 &+\sum_{(k,m)\in A_{a_j}}
 |\alpha_{j,k}c_k^++\beta_{j,m}c_m^-|^2,
 \nonumber
\end{align}
where all $\alpha_{j,k},\beta_{j,m}$ are unimodular.  Thus the only
finite-dimensional obstruction to \eqref{eq:obs} is a closed chain of
collision relations.

\begin{lemma}[coordinate injectivity]\label{lem:injectivity}
If $a\notin\Z$, no two distinct points of $A_a$ share a first coordinate or
share a second coordinate.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $(x,y),(x',y)\in A_a$ with $x\ne x'$, then $x,x'$ are the two roots of
\[
 X^2-aX+y^2+ay=0.
\]
Hence $a=x+x'\in\Z$, a contradiction.  If $(x,y),(x,y')\in A_a$ with
$y\ne y'$, the corresponding quadratic in $Y$ gives
$-a=y+y'\in\Z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the graph reduction]\label{prop:graph}
Let $a,b$ be distinct non-integers.  If the coordinate-sharing graph on
$A_a\cup A_b$ has no cycle, then \eqref{eq:obs} holds with
$(a_1,a_2)=(a,b)$ for arbitrary base points and every $T>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If either lattice set is empty, the one-observation result
\cite[Theorem~4.4(ii)]{JK} already proves the claim.  Otherwise,
Lemma~\ref{lem:injectivity} and \eqref{eq:parameter} show that the graph is
bipartite, has degree at most two, and hence is a disjoint union of paths when
it has no cycle.

Consider the sum of the two quadratic forms in
\eqref{eq:source-reduction}.  If it vanishes, an unshared coordinate at an
endpoint of any path is zero because it occurs in one of the first two sums.
The endpoint collision relation then forces the next coordinate variable to
vanish; induction propagates zero along the whole path.  Isolated vertices
are handled in the same way.  All variables in the finite collision block
therefore vanish, while the first two sums already control every variable
outside that block.  The resulting finite-dimensional quadratic form is
positive definite, hence coercive.  Together with
\eqref{eq:source-reduction}, this is \eqref{eq:obs}.
\end{proof}

\section{No coordinate cycle can occur}

\begin{lemma}[telescoping cycle obstruction]\label{lem:no-cycle}
For distinct $a,b\in\R\setminus\Z$, the coordinate-sharing graph on
$A_a\cup A_b$ has no cycle.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose a cycle exists.  A nonzero lattice point determines its parameter
uniquely by \eqref{eq:parameter}, so $A_a\cap A_b=\varnothing$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:injectivity} forces both the parameter labels and the
horizontal/vertical directions to alternate around the cycle.  After choosing
an orientation, its vertices may therefore be labeled cyclically as
\[
 P_i=(x_i,y_i)\in A_a,
 \qquad Q_i=(x_{i+1},y_i)\in A_b,
 \qquad i=1,\ldots,r,
\]
where $x_{r+1}=x_1$.  Their circle equations are
\begin{align}
 x_i^2-a x_i+y_i^2+a y_i&=0, \label{eq:a-circle}\\
 x_{i+1}^2-b x_{i+1}+y_i^2+b y_i&=0. \label{eq:b-circle}
\end{align}
Subtract \eqref{eq:a-circle} from \eqref{eq:b-circle} and sum over $i$.
The square terms telescope cyclically, leaving
\[
 (a-b)\left(\sum_{i=1}^r x_i-\sum_{i=1}^r y_i\right)=0.
\]
Since $a\ne b$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sums}
 \sum_{i=1}^r x_i=\sum_{i=1}^r y_i.
\end{equation}
Finally, sum \eqref{eq:a-circle} over $i$ and use \eqref{eq:sums}:
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^r(x_i^2+y_i^2)
 =a\left(\sum_{i=1}^r x_i-\sum_{i=1}^r y_i\right)=0.
\]
Every $x_i$ and $y_i$ is zero.  This says every $P_i$ is the excluded origin,
a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every two distinct $a_1,a_2\in\R\setminus\Z$, every $T>0$, and arbitrary
base points $(t_1,x_1),(t_2,x_2)\in\R^2$, the periodic beam solutions satisfy
the inverse observability inequality \eqref{eq:obs}.  In particular, Open
Question (iv) of \cite{JK} has an affirmative answer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Proposition~\ref{prop:graph} with Lemma~\ref{lem:no-cycle}.
\end{proof}

\section{Checks, scope, and novelty}

The proof uses rationality nowhere.  If a parameter is irrational, its lattice
set is empty; otherwise \eqref{eq:parameter} makes it rational automatically.
Thus the theorem strictly strengthens the source's rational question.  It
does not address integer velocities, for which coordinate injectivity fails.

The arXiv source prints ``integers'' in Lemma 4.7 and Theorem 4.8.  This is
inconsistent with the lemma's proof, which derives integrality as a
contradiction, with the subsequent non-integral rational example, and with
Open Question (iv).  Proposition~\ref{prop:graph} states the intended
non-integer reduction directly from the source's estimate (4.9), so the result
does not rely on that printed word.

As a non-proof regression check, the included script exhaustively found no
cycle for all parameters of absolute value at most $120$ (21,178 parameter
classes and 3,044 candidate pairs).  A bounded search on 13 August 2026 used
the exact question, title, arXiv id, graph notation, and core cycle and
observability phrases.  The current arXiv version still states the question as
open, and no later explicit answer or matching telescoping argument was found.
Novelty confidence is moderate.
