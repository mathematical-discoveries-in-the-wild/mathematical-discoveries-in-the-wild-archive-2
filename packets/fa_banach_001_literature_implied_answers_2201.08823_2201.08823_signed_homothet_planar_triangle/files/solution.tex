\begin{center}
  {\Large\bfseries Signed homothetic coverings: two planar classes}\\[3pt]
  {\small arXiv:2201.08823, Conjecture 4 (G. Fejes T\'oth)}\\[7pt]
  \badge{LITERATURE-IMPLIED ANSWER --- PARTIAL SUBCASE}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Source question.}
Let $K\subset\R^d$ be a convex body.  If translates of $\lambda_iK$, with
$-1<\lambda_i<1$, cover $K$, must
\[
       \sum_i |\lambda_i|\ge d?
\]
This is Conjecture 4 on page 6 of Ambrus's paper.

\paragraph{Exact result identified.}
The answer is affirmative in each of the following complete planar classes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item every nondegenerate triangle;
  \item every centrally symmetric planar convex body.
\end{enumerate}
The first class follows from a mixed-orientation theorem of Dumitrescu--Jiang
that is not cited in the source discussion.  The second follows by reducing
signs to the positive planar theorem already cited there.  No claim is made
for a general noncentrally symmetric planar body or for $d\ge3$.

\paragraph{Proof intuition.}
A signed homothet of a triangle is still a triangle with the same three edge
directions; a negative coefficient merely reverses its orientation.
Dumitrescu--Jiang's extend/shrink argument was designed for both orientations
and converts any such cover into three vertex triangles without increasing
the total side length.  Their final three-triangle geometry forces total
length $2$.  Affine maps preserve signed homothety coefficients, so the
equilateral result is already the result for every triangle.

\paragraph{The decisive literature theorem.}
Dumitrescu and Jiang, Theorem 1, prove that if a unit equilateral triangle
$T$ is covered by finitely many smaller equilateral triangles $T_i$ whose
sides are parallel to those of $T$, and $t_i$ is the side length of $T_i$,
then
\[
       \sum_i t_i\ge2.
\]
Their definitions immediately below the theorem distinguish positive and
negative homothetic copies, and the proof explicitly handles both cases.
Thus $t_i=|\lambda_i|$ and this is precisely the signed conjecture for an
equilateral triangle.

\section*{The two bridge proofs}

\paragraph{1. Every planar triangle.}
Let $K$ be a nondegenerate triangle and let $A(x)=Lx+b$ be an affine
bijection taking $K$ to a unit equilateral triangle $T$.  For every scalar
$\lambda$ and translation vector $t$,
\[
  A(\lambda K+t)
   =\lambda A(K)+\bigl(Lt+(1-\lambda)b\bigr).
\]
Consequently a cover
$K\subset\bigcup_i(\lambda_iK+t_i)$ is carried to a signed homothetic cover
of $T$ with exactly the same coefficients.  Discard coefficients $0$:
the union of the remaining finitely many closed homothets contains the dense
set obtained by deleting the finitely many zero-homothet points from $T$,
hence contains all of $T$.  Dumitrescu--Jiang now gives
\[
       \sum_i|\lambda_i|\ge2=d.
\]

\paragraph{2. Every centrally symmetric planar body.}
Suppose $K$ is centrally symmetric about $c$, so $-K=K-2c$.  If
$\lambda=-\mu<0$, then
\[
       \lambda K+t=-\mu K+t=\mu K+(t-2\mu c).
\]
Thus every negative homothet is a translate of a positive homothet of the
same magnitude.  Replacing all negative members this way turns the signed
cover into a positive homothetic cover with coefficients $|\lambda_i|$.
The positive Soltan--V\'as\'arhelyi theorem in dimension two (summarized on
the same source page) yields $\sum_i|\lambda_i|\ge2$.

\paragraph{Why this is only a partial answer.}
For a noncentrally symmetric planar body with more than three support
directions, reflection is not a translate and the triangle's finite
extend/shrink reduction has no known analogue.  Exact linear-programming
searches during this investigation found no counterexample among triangles,
asymmetric trapezoids, or pentagons with two to four mixed-sign homothets and
total coefficient $1.8$--$1.9$, but those finite searches do not settle the
general case.
