\section{Source problem}

Davydov, Moldavskaya, and Zitikis~\cite{DMZ} define, for
\(h\in C^2(G)\), a global convexity index
\[
 \Conv(h;G)=
 \frac{\int_G\tr(H_h(x))_+\,dx}
      {\int_G\|H_h(x)\|_*\,dx},
\]
where \(H_h\) is the Hessian, \(A_+\) is the spectral positive part of a
symmetric matrix, and \(\|\cdot\|_*\) is the nuclear norm.  Their Theorem~3.1
identifies \(\tr(A_-)\) with the nuclear distance from \(A\) to the positive
semidefinite cone.  On page 15, Section~5, they ask for a modification that
accommodates functions without second partial derivatives, specifically the
limits
\[
 \min\{g(x),g(y)\},\qquad \max\{g(x),g(y)\}
\]
of their power means as \(\beta\to-\infty\) and \(\beta\to+\infty\).

\section{Idea of the proof}

The ordinary Hessian is replaced by the distributional Hessian
\(M=\Hess h\), a symmetric matrix-valued Radon measure.  Write a polar density
\(M=A\rho\) against any positive control measure and take the spectral parts
\(A_+\) and \(A_-\) pointwise.  This produces canonical positive-semidefinite
matrix measures \(M^+=A_+\rho\) and \(M^-=A_-\rho\).  Integrating the source
paper's finite-dimensional matrix theorem proves that \(M^-\) is exactly the
nuclear-total-variation distance from \(M\) to the cone of positive
semidefinite matrix measures.

For a maximum or minimum of two smooth branches, the BV chain rule makes the
new feature explicit.  In addition to the ordinary Hessians on the two smooth
regions, the switching hypersurface carries a rank-one measure
\[
 \pm |\nabla(u-v)|\,n\otimes n\,\mathcal H^{d-1}.
\]
The sign is positive for \(\max\) and negative for \(\min\).  Thus the ridge
has local index one for a maximum and zero for a minimum.  Finally, the
rank-one term is recovered as the concentration limit of the Hessians of the
smooth power means, so the new index is the actual \(\beta\to\pm\infty\)
limit of the source's indices.

\section{The measure-valued index}

Let \(G\subset\mathbb R^d\) be open.  Denote by
\(\M(G;\Sym_d)\) the finite symmetric matrix-valued Radon measures.  Its
nuclear total-variation norm is
\[
 \|M\|_{*,\M}
 :=\sup\left\{\int_G \Phi:dM:
 \Phi\in C_c(G;\Sym_d),\ \sup_G\|\Phi\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1\right\}.
\]
If \(M=A\rho\) for a positive control measure \(\rho\), then
\(\|M\|_{*,\M}=\int_G\|A(x)\|_*\,d\rho(x)\).  This is the \(p=1\)
Hessian--Schatten variation used in the modern nonsmooth framework
of~\cite{ACU}; see also the standard polar decomposition of vector measures
in~\cite{AFP}.

Let \(\Pos(G)\) be the cone of positive-semidefinite matrix measures: a
measure \(P\) belongs to \(\Pos(G)\) when \(z^TPz\) is a positive scalar
measure for every \(z\in\mathbb R^d\).

\begin{theorem}[Measure-cone projection and nonsmooth convexity index]
\label{thm:measure}
Let \(M\in\M(G;\Sym_d)\).  Choose a positive measure \(\rho\) with
\(M=A\rho\), and define
\[
 M^+=A_+\rho,\qquad M^-=A_-\rho.
\]
These measures do not depend on the choice of \(\rho\), and
\[
 M=M^+-M^-,\qquad
 \|M\|_{*,\M}=\tr M^+(G)+\tr M^-(G).                 \tag{3.1}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \inf_{P\in\Pos(G)}\|M-P\|_{*,\M}
   =\|M^-\|_{*,\M}=\tr M^-(G).
 \quad}                                               \tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently, if \(h\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(G)\) and
\(\Hess h\in\M(G;\Sym_d)\), define
\[
 \Loc_{\M}(h;G)=\tr(\Hess h)^-(G),\qquad
 \Conv_{\M}(h;G)=
 \frac{\tr(\Hess h)^+(G)}
 {\tr(\Hess h)^+(G)+\tr(\Hess h)^-(G)}.             \tag{3.3}
\]
When the denominator vanishes, \(h\) is affine on each connected component;
we set \(\Conv_{\M}=1\).  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\Conv_{\M}(h;G)\in[0,1]\);
\item \(\Conv_{\M}(h;G)=1\) if and only if \(h\) has a convex
representative on convex \(G\);
\item \(\Conv_{\M}(h;G)=0\) if and only if \(h\) has a concave
representative and is not affine;
\item if \(h\in W^{2,1}(G)\), formula (3.3) is exactly the source's global
index, with \(\Hess h=H_h(x)\,dx\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Changing the control measure multiplies the density by a nonnegative scalar,
and spectral positive and negative parts are positively homogeneous.  Hence
\(M^\pm\) are intrinsic.  The identities in (3.1) follow pointwise from
\(A=A_+-A_-\) and
\(\|A\|_*=\tr A_++\tr A_-\).

Fix \(P\in\Pos(G)\), and dominate both \(M\) and \(P\) by
\(\sigma=|M|_*+\tr P\).  Write \(M=B\sigma\) and \(P=C\sigma\).  Then
\(C(x)\succeq0\) for \(\sigma\)-almost every \(x\).  The source paper's
finite-dimensional theorem, applied pointwise, yields
\[
 \|B(x)-C(x)\|_*\ge \tr B_-(x).
\]
After integration,
\[
 \|M-P\|_{*,\M}\ge\int_G\tr B_-\,d\sigma
 =\tr M^-(G).
\]
Equality is attained by \(P=M^+\), because \(M-M^+=-M^-\).  This proves
(3.2) and (3.3).

For the calibration statement, the classical distributional characterization
says that a locally integrable function on a convex open set is convex exactly
when its distributional Hessian is positive semidefinite.  One quick proof is
to mollify: a positive-semidefinite distributional Hessian gives convex smooth
mollifications on interior subdomains, and their local \(L^1\) limit has a
convex representative.  The converse follows by restricting a convex
function to lines and taking nonnegative second directional derivatives.
Thus \((\Hess h)^-=0\) is equivalent to convexity.  Applying the same argument
to \(-h\) proves the concave statement.  The \(W^{2,1}\) specialization is
immediate from the absolutely continuous density \(H_h(x)\).
\end{proof}

The pointwise index also has a precise measure analogue.  With
\[
 \kappa_h=\tr(\Hess h)^++\tr(\Hess h)^-,
\]
set
\[
 \Conv_{\M}(h,x)=
 \frac{d\,\tr(\Hess h)^+}{d\kappa_h}(x)
 \quad\text{for }\kappa_h\text{-a.e. }x.             \tag{3.4}
\]
At Lebesgue points of a classical Hessian this is the source's eigenvalue
ratio.  Formula (3.4) remains meaningful on singular ridges, where Lebesgue
measure sees nothing.

\section{Exact formulas for maxima and minima}

\begin{theorem}[Switching-interface formula]
\label{thm:switch}
Let \(u,v\in C^2\) on a neighborhood of \(\overline G\), put
\(\phi=u-v\), and suppose \(0\) is a regular value of \(\phi\).  Let
\[
 \Sigma=G\cap\{u=v\},\qquad n=\frac{\nabla\phi}{{|\nabla\phi|}}.
\]
Then, as symmetric matrix-valued measures,
\begin{align}
 \Hess\max\{u,v\}
 &=\mathbf1_{\{u>v\}}\Hess u\,dx
   +\mathbf1_{\{v>u\}}\Hess v\,dx
   +|\nabla\phi|\,n\otimes n\,\mathcal H^{d-1}\!\restriction\Sigma,
                                                               \tag{4.1}\\
 \Hess\min\{u,v\}
 &=\mathbf1_{\{u<v\}}\Hess u\,dx
   +\mathbf1_{\{v<u\}}\Hess v\,dx
   -|\nabla\phi|\,n\otimes n\,\mathcal H^{d-1}\!\restriction\Sigma.
                                                               \tag{4.2}
\end{align}
The singular term has nuclear mass
\[
 J_\Sigma=\int_\Sigma|\nabla(u-v)|\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}.          \tag{4.3}
\]
It contributes \(J_\Sigma\) entirely to the positive curvature mass of the
maximum and entirely to the negative curvature mass of the minimum.
Consequently (3.3) gives explicit indices by adding (4.3) to the appropriate
bulk spectral mass.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since \(\max\{u,v\}=v+\phi_+\), the BV chain rule and the Gauss--Green
formula give
\[
 \nabla\max\{u,v\}=\nabla v+\mathbf1_{\{\phi>0\}}\nabla\phi
\]
and
\[
 D\mathbf1_{\{\phi>0\}}
 =\frac{\nabla\phi}{|\nabla\phi|}\,
   \mathcal H^{d-1}\!\restriction\Sigma.
\]
Differentiating once more produces (4.1), because
\(\nabla\phi\otimes\nabla\phi/|\nabla\phi|
=|\nabla\phi|n\otimes n\).  The minimum identity follows either in the same
way or from \(\min\{u,v\}=u+v-\max\{u,v\}\).  The rank-one matrix
\(n\otimes n\) is positive semidefinite and has nuclear norm one, proving
(4.3) and the sign assertions.
\end{proof}

The regular-value assumption is a transparent generic hypothesis.  The same
formula holds whenever the switching set is countably rectifiable and its
critical part has \(\mathcal H^{d-1}\)-measure zero, which covers isolated
critical crossings by approximation.

\begin{corollary}[Closed one-dimensional branch formulas]
\label{cor:branch}
Let \(I=(a,b)\), let \(g\in C^2(I)\) be strictly monotone with \(g'\ne0\), and
set \(Q=I^2\).  Define
\[
 J=2\int_a^b|g'(t)|\,dt,
\]
and
\[
 W_{\max}(t)=
 \begin{cases}t-a,&g\text{ increasing},\\ b-t,&g\text{ decreasing},\end{cases}
 \qquad
 W_{\min}(t)=b-a-W_{\max}(t).
\]
For \(h_{\max}(x,y)=\max\{g(x),g(y)\}\), its positive and negative masses are
\begin{align*}
 P_{\max}&=2\int_a^b W_{\max}(t)(g''(t))_+\,dt+J,\\
 N_{\max}&=2\int_a^b W_{\max}(t)(g''(t))_-\,dt.
\end{align*}
For \(h_{\min}(x,y)=\min\{g(x),g(y)\}\), they are
\begin{align*}
 P_{\min}&=2\int_a^b W_{\min}(t)(g''(t))_+\,dt,\\
 N_{\min}&=2\int_a^b W_{\min}(t)(g''(t))_-\,dt+J.
\end{align*}
In both cases \(\Conv_{\M}=P/(P+N)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Strict monotonicity makes \(\Sigma\) the diagonal.  Along it,
\(|\nabla(g(x)-g(y))|=\sqrt2|g'(t)|\) and
\(d\mathcal H^1=\sqrt2\,dt\), yielding \(J\).  Integrating the selected
diagonal Hessian \(\operatorname{diag}(g''(x),0)\) or
\(\operatorname{diag}(0,g''(y))\) over the two triangles gives the weights
shown above.
\end{proof}

\section{Recovery as the smooth power-mean limit}

\begin{theorem}[No large-\(|\beta|\) surrogate is needed]
\label{thm:limit}
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:switch}, with \(u,v\ge m>0\), and
assume \(\mathcal H^{d-1}(\Sigma\cap\partial G)=0\).  For \(\beta\ne0\), put
\[
 h_\beta=\left(\frac{u^\beta+v^\beta}{2}\right)^{1/\beta}.
\]
Then on \(G\), in the weak-star sense of matrix measures and strictly in
nuclear total variation,
\begin{align*}
 \Hess h_\beta\,dx&\longrightarrow \Hess\max\{u,v\}
     &&(\beta\to+\infty),\\
 \Hess h_\beta\,dx&\longrightarrow \Hess\min\{u,v\}
     &&(\beta\to-\infty).
\end{align*}
The positive and negative spectral masses converge separately.  Hence,
whenever the limiting denominator is nonzero,
\[
 \Conv(h_\beta;G)\longrightarrow
 \begin{cases}
 \Conv_{\M}(\max\{u,v\};G),&\beta\to+\infty,\\
 \Conv_{\M}(\min\{u,v\};G),&\beta\to-\infty.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{5.1}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(a=\log u\), \(b=\log v\), and
\[
 p_\beta=\frac{e^{\beta a}}{e^{\beta a}+e^{\beta b}},\qquad
 m_\beta=p_\beta\nabla a+(1-p_\beta)\nabla b.
\]
A direct differentiation gives
\begin{align}
 \Hess h_\beta
 =h_\beta\bigl[&m_\beta\otimes m_\beta
 +p_\beta\Hess a+(1-p_\beta)\Hess b\notag\\
 &+\beta p_\beta(1-p_\beta)
 (\nabla a-\nabla b)\otimes(\nabla a-\nabla b)\bigr].    \tag{5.2}
\end{align}
Away from \(\Sigma\), the first line converges to the Hessian of the selected
branch.  In the second line,
\(\beta p_\beta(1-p_\beta)\) is the derivative of the logistic function in
the normal variable \(a-b\), with integral one.  The coarea formula therefore
gives, for \(\beta\to+\infty\),
\[
 \beta h_\beta p_\beta(1-p_\beta)
 (\nabla a-\nabla b)^{\otimes2}\,dx
 \stackrel{*}{\rightharpoonup}
 |\nabla(u-v)|n\otimes n\,
 \mathcal H^{d-1}\!\restriction\Sigma.
\]
Indeed, on \(\Sigma\), where \(u=v=s\), one has
\(s|\nabla(a-b)|=|\nabla(u-v)|\).  For \(\beta\to-\infty\) the same logistic
layer has negative sign and selects the minimum.

To obtain strict nuclear convergence, split \(G\) into a thin tubular
neighborhood of \(\Sigma\) and its complement.  On the complement, dominated
convergence applies to (5.2).  In the tube, the first line is uniformly
bounded, while the rank-one last line has the indicated sign and its nuclear
mass converges by coarea to (4.3).  The integral of the bounded term over the
tube vanishes with its width.  This proves convergence of total nuclear mass.
For symmetric matrices,
\[
 \tr A_+=\tfrac12(\|A\|_*+\tr A),\qquad
 \tr A_-=\tfrac12(\|A\|_*-\tr A),
\]
so nuclear convergence together with weak convergence of the trace measures
gives separate convergence of the positive and negative masses.  Formula
(5.1) follows.
\end{proof}

\section{The source paper's concrete function}

For
\[
 g(t)=\tfrac12(1-t)^2+0.01t^{1/4},
\]
one has
\[
 g'(t)=t-1+0.0025t^{-3/4},\qquad
 g''(t)=1-0.001875t^{-7/4}.
\]
On \(I=[0.1,0.9]\), \(g\) is strictly decreasing and strictly convex.
Corollary~\ref{cor:branch} gives
\[
 J=0.7917667515752844,qquad
 P_{\min}=2\int_{0.1}^{0.9}(t-0.1)g''(t)\,dt
 =0.6360956571149691,
\]
and therefore
\[
 \boxed{\Conv_{\M}(\min\{g(x),g(y)\};I^2)
 =0.4454880619053803.}
\]
The maximum is convex on this square, so its index is exactly one.  A midpoint
quadrature of the classical Hessian on an \(800\times800\) grid gives
\(0.4462763224\) at \(\beta=-100\) and \(1.0000000000\) at \(\beta=100\),
consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:limit}.  The reproducible checker is
\texttt{code/verify\_formulas.py}.

The source's \(g\) is not globally monotone: it has two critical points, and
\(g''\) is singular at the endpoint zero.  The general switching formula
(4.1)--(4.3) handles every regular diagonal or off-diagonal equality branch.
On the full open unit square the Hessian measure is locally finite; global
finite indices should be taken on compact interior subdomains (as the source
does for its expanding squares) or defined by exhaustion when the limiting
ratio exists.

\section{Verification, novelty, and review focus}

\paragraph{Checks.}
The script tests the finite-dimensional distance inequality against 2,000
random symmetric/PSD matrix pairs, verifies equality at the spectral positive
part, evaluates the closed interface formula, and compares it with smooth
power means.  These computations are sanity checks, not ingredients of the
proof.

\paragraph{Bounded novelty search.}
On 11 August 2026 we searched the exact source title and future-problem terms,
as well as ``distributional Hessian matrix-valued measure distance positive
semidefinite cone nuclear norm'', ``Hessian-Schatten total variation
nonsmooth convexity'', and close variants.  We found the later
Hessian--Schatten framework of Aziznejad--Campos--Unser~\cite{ACU} and related
2023--2024 work, but no paper stating the signed PSD-measure projection
identity (3.2), the resulting convexity index, the \(\min/\max\) ridge formulas,
or the power-mean index limit (5.1).  Novelty is therefore plausible, not
certified.  The framework itself is known; the signed convexity construction
and its application to the source problem are the candidate contribution.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Promote for expert review as a candidate full solution.  The most important
checks are: (i) the intrinsic spectral decomposition of a matrix measure;
(ii) strict nuclear convergence in Theorem~\ref{thm:limit}, especially if the
regular-value hypothesis is weakened; and (iii) treatment by exhaustion near
the source function's endpoint singularity.  None of these affects the exact
interior formulas under the stated hypotheses.
