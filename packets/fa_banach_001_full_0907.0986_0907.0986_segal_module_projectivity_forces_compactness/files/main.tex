\section*{Source question}
Forrest, Lee, and Samei study operator projectivity of natural modules over the Lebesgue--Fourier algebra $\Sone$ and Feichtinger's algebra $\Szero$ \cite{Source}.  Both are operator Segal algebras of $\A$.  The source proves projectivity of $\A$ over either algebra when $G$ is compact and asks in its introduction, on source PDF page 2, whether the converse holds:
\begin{quote}
``\dots it remains open whether or not the projectivity of $A(G)$ as either an $S^1A(G)$ or an $S_0(G)$-module forces $G$ to be compact.''
\end{quote}

\section*{Proof intuition}
The splitting criterion for an essential projective module normally appears to say only that multiplication lands back in the ambient algebra.  Here it says more.  If $S$ is a Segal ideal in a commutative Banach algebra $A$, then multiplication
\[
 S\opten A\longrightarrow S
\]
is bounded in the stronger Segal norm.  Consequently, any right inverse $A\to S\opten A$ to multiplication forces every element of $A$ to lie in $S$.  Thus a proper essential Segal ideal cannot have its ambient algebra as a projective module.

For the two source algebras, equality $S=\A$ forces every Fourier-algebra function to be integrable.  On a noncompact group, however, one may place arbitrarily many disjoint translates of a fixed compactly supported regular coefficient.  Disjointness makes their $L^1$ norms add, whereas the coefficient realization in the left regular representation makes their $\A$ norm grow only like the Hilbert norm of an orthogonal sum.

\section*{The abstract range lemma}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:range}
Let $A$ be a commutative completely contractive Banach algebra and let $S$ be an operator Segal algebra in $A$ in the sense of \cite{Source}: $S$ is dense in $A$, is an ideal, is a completely contractive $A$-module in its own norm, and $AS$ is dense in $S$ in that norm.  If $A$ is operator projective as a left $S$-module, then $S=A$ as sets and the two norms are equivalent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First $A$ is an essential $S$-module.  Indeed, $AS$ is dense in $S$ in the $S$-norm.  The inclusion $j:S\to A$ is continuous, so
\[
 S\subseteq \overline{AS}^{\,\|\cdot\|_A}.
\]
Since $S$ is dense in $A$, it follows that $\overline{SA}^{\,\|\cdot\|_A}=A$.

The essential-module projectivity criterion \cite[Proposition 2.5]{Source} therefore supplies a completely bounded $S$-module map
\[
 \rho:A\longrightarrow S\opten A
\]
such that $\pi_A\rho=\operatorname{id}_A$, where $\pi_A(s\otimes a)=sa$ is viewed as taking values in $A$.  But the Segal-module axiom and commutativity give a completely bounded map
\[
 \pi_S:S\opten A\longrightarrow S,\qquad \pi_S(s\otimes a)=sa,
\]
and $j\pi_S=\pi_A$.  Hence the bounded map $J=\pi_S\rho:A\to S$ satisfies
\[
 jJ=\operatorname{id}_A.
\]
Because $j$ is the set-theoretic inclusion, every $a\in A$ equals the element $J(a)\in S$.  Thus $A=S$, and boundedness of $J$ together with boundedness of $j$ makes their norms equivalent.
\end{proof}

\section*{The integrability obstruction}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:l1}
If the inclusion $\A\hookrightarrow L^1(G)$ is well-defined and bounded for a locally compact group $G$, then $G$ is compact.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Assume that $G$ is noncompact and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:embed}
 \|v\|_1\le C\|v\|_{\A}\qquad(v\in\A).
\end{equation}
Choose a relatively compact measurable neighborhood $V$ of the identity with positive Haar measure, put $\xi=1_V\in L^2(G)$, and define
\[
 u(s)=\langle\lambda(s)\xi,\xi\rangle.
\]
Then $u\in\A$ is nonzero, nonnegative, continuous, and supported in the compact set $VV^{-1}$; in particular $0<\|u\|_1<\infty$.

Choose a compact set $K$ containing $V\cup VV^{-1}$.  Noncompactness permits, for every $n$, elements $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ whose left translates $x_iK$ are pairwise disjoint: inductively choose $x_{m+1}$ outside the finite union $\bigcup_{i\le m}x_iKK^{-1}$.  Put
\[
 u_i(s)=u(x_i^{-1}s).
\]
Their supports are disjoint, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:l1linear}
 \left\|\sum_{i=1}^n u_i\right\|_1=n\|u\|_1.
\end{equation}
On the other hand,
\[
 u_i(s)=\langle\lambda(s)\xi,\lambda(x_i)\xi\rangle,
\]
and the vectors $\lambda(x_i)\xi$ are orthogonal because the sets $x_iV$ are disjoint.  The coefficient definition of the Fourier-algebra norm yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Asqrt}
 \left\|\sum_{i=1}^n u_i\right\|_{\A}
 \le \|\xi\|_2\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda(x_i)\xi\right\|_2
 =\sqrt n\,\|\xi\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:embed}--\eqref{eq:Asqrt} gives
$n\|u\|_1\le C\sqrt n\,\|\xi\|_2^2$ for every $n$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\section*{Full answer}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $G$ be a locally compact group and let $S=\Sone$ or $S=\Szero$.  Then $\A$ is operator projective as a left $S$-module if and only if $G$ is compact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $\A$ is operator projective over $S$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:range} gives $\A=S$ with equivalent norms.  For $S=\Sone$, the definition $\Sone=\A\cap L^1(G)$ immediately gives a bounded inclusion $\A\hookrightarrow L^1(G)$.  For $S=\Szero$, the source recalls that $\Szero$ is also an operator Segal algebra of $L^1(G)$, so its inclusion into $L^1(G)$ is bounded; equality $\A=\Szero$ gives the same conclusion.  Lemma~\ref{lem:l1} implies that $G$ is compact.

Conversely, the compact case is the positive result already recorded in \cite{Source}: $\A$ is operator projective over both $\Sone$ and $\Szero$.
\end{proof}

\section*{Verification, scope, and novelty check}
The proof uses only the source's definitions and projectivity criterion, the standard coefficient description of $\A$, and elementary Haar-measure geometry.  It proves the exact converse for both named Segal algebras and, abstractly, shows that an ambient commutative algebra cannot be an essential projective module over a proper operator Segal ideal.
