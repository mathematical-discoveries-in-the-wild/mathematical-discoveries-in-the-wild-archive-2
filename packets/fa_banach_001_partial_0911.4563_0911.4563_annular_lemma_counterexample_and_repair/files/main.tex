\section{Source statement and question}

Let $Q(a,s)$ denote the cube centered at $a$ with side length $s$.  For a
unit-scale covering cube $Q(x_j^t,\sqrt t)$, the source subtracts the
Lebesgue mean of $f$ on $Q(x_j^t,2\sqrt t)$:
\[
 m^{j,t}=\frac{1}{|Q(x_j^t,2\sqrt t)|}
 \int_{Q(x_j^t,2\sqrt t)}f(y)\,dy,
 \qquad g^{j,t}=f-m^{j,t}.
\]
It then restricts $g^{j,t}$ to
\[
 C_k^{j,t}=Q(x_j^t,2^{k+1}\sqrt t)
 \setminus Q(x_j^t,2^k\sqrt t),\qquad
 g_k^{j,t}=g^{j,t}{\bf1}_{C_k^{j,t}}.
\]
Assertion B of Lemma 3.4 claims an estimate with the large-cube
normalization $(2^k\sqrt t)^{-n}$.

\section{Counterexample to the printed annular estimate}

\begin{theorem}[failure of Lemma 3.4(B)]\label{thm:counter}
Assertion B of the source's Lemma 3.4 is false, even when $n=1$, $t=1$,
and
\[
 d\muM(x)=M(x)\,dx=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}\,dx.
\]
In fact, for the standard unit tiling and a sequence of smooth compactly
supported functions, the left-hand side stays bounded away from zero while
the asserted right-hand side tends to zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the tiling centers $x_j^1=j$.  For $k\ge3$, set $R=2^k$ and choose the
center $x_j^1=R$.  Then
\[
 Q(R,2^{k+1})=[0,2R],\qquad
 C_k^{j,1}=[0,R/2]\cup[3R/2,2R],
\]
whereas the mean cube is $Q(R,2)=[R-1,R+1]$.

Choose $f_k\in C_c^\infty(\R)$, $0\le f_k\le1$, so that
\[
 f_k=0\quad\hbox{on }[R-1,R+1],
 \qquad
 f_k=1\quad\hbox{on }[0,2R]\setminus[R-2,R+2].
\]
The cutoff to compact support can be placed outside $[0,2R]$.  Its subtracted
mean is exactly zero, and $f_k=1$ on $C_k^{j,1}$.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:left}
 \lVert g_k^{j,1}\rVert_{L^2(C_k^{j,1},\muM)}^2
 \ge \muM([0,R/2])\longrightarrow\frac12.
\end{equation}

Let $B_R=[R-2,R+2]$ and write $Q_R=[0,2R]$.  If $x\in Q_R\setminus B_R$,
then $f_k(x)=1$ and a difference can occur only for $y\in B_R$; for
$x\in B_R$, use the crude inner bound $|Q_R|=2R$.  Thus
\begin{align}
 N_k&:=\int_{Q_R}\int_{Q_R}|f_k(x)-f_k(y)|^2\,dy\,d\muM(x)\notag\\
 &\le |B_R|\muM(Q_R\setminus B_R)+2R\muM(B_R)
 \le 4+2R\muM(B_R).
\end{align}
The right-hand side asserted in B, without its fixed constant, is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:right}
 \frac{N_k}{2^k}\le \frac4R+2\muM([R-2,R+2])\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:left} and \eqref{eq:right} contradict the existence of
a uniform constant $\bar C$.

Finally, the Gaussian is fully admissible in the source: for
$V(x)=x^2/2+\tfrac12\log(2\pi)$,
$(1-\varepsilon)|V'|^2/2-V''\to\infty$.  Thus the failure is not caused by
leaving the paper's measure class.
\end{proof}

\section{The correct annular estimate}

The problem is exactly the scale of the mean.  It is taken on the small cube,
so no factor $2^{-kn}$ can be gained by ``arguing similarly.''

\begin{proposition}[corrected B]\label{prop:correct}
With the source's notation, for every $k\ge1$,
\begin{align}
 \lVert g_k^{j,t}\rVert_{L^2(C_k^{j,t},\muM)}^2
 \le \frac{C_n}{t^{n/2}}
 \int_{Q(x_j^t,2^{k+1}\sqrt t)}
 \int_{Q(x_j^t,2^{k+1}\sqrt t)}
 |f(x)-f(y)|^2\,dy\,d\muM(x).             \label{eq:correct}
\end{align}
The same argument gives, for $1\le p<\infty$,
\begin{align}
 \lVert g_k^{j,t}\rVert_{L^p(C_k^{j,t},\muM)}^p
 &\le \frac{C_n}{t^{n/2}}
 \int_{Q(x_j^t,2^{k+1}\sqrt t)}
 \int_{Q(x_j^t,2^{k+1}\sqrt t)}
 |f(x)-f(y)|^p\,dy\,d\muM(x).             \label{eq:correctp}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $x\in C_k^{j,t}$, Jensen's inequality on the actual mean cube
$Q_0=Q(x_j^t,2\sqrt t)$ gives
\[
 |g^{j,t}(x)|^p
 \le \frac1{|Q_0|}\int_{Q_0}|f(x)-f(y)|^p\,dy
 =\frac{C_n}{t^{n/2}}\int_{Q_0}|f(x)-f(y)|^p\,dy.
\]
Integrate in $x$ over $C_k^{j,t}$ and enlarge both domains to the large
cube.  This proves \eqref{eq:correctp}; $p=2$ gives \eqref{eq:correct}.
\end{proof}

There is also a literal square-difference local $L^p$ form, valid for every
$1\le p<\infty$:
\[
 \lVert g_k^{j,t}\rVert_p^p
 \le C_nt^{-np/4}\int_{C_k^{j,t}}
 \left(\int_{Q_0}|f(x)-f(y)|^2\,dy\right)^{p/2}d\muM(x).
\]
Thus the elementary local averaging step itself is not the difficulty in the
intended global $L^p$ problem.

\section{Repair of the source's main \texorpdfstring{$L^2$}{L2} proof}

\begin{theorem}[the polynomial loss is harmless]\label{thm:repair}
Replacing Assertion B by Proposition \ref{prop:correct} leaves the conclusion
of the source's Lemma 3.3, and hence its main $L^2$ theorem, valid.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert \eqref{eq:correct} into the source's $t$-integral.  For fixed $t,x,y$,
the number of enlarged cubes of side $2^{k+1}\sqrt t$ containing both points
is at most $C_n2^{kn}$.  Such a cube can exist only when
$|x-y|\le C_n2^k\sqrt t$.  Therefore the $k$th annular contribution is at
most
\[
 C2^{kn}\int\!\!\int |f(x)-f(y)|^2\,d\muM(x)\,dy
 \int_{|x-y|^2/(C_n4^k)}^A t^{-1-(n+\alpha)/2}\,dt,
\]
and hence at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
 C2^{k(2n+\alpha)}
 \int\!\!\int_{|x-y|\le C_n2^k\sqrt A}
 \frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^2}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}\,d\muM(x)\,dy.
\end{equation}
This deliberately safe exponent also absorbs the normalization and covering
factor discrepancies visible in the printed equations (3.20)--(3.21).

The source's resolvent estimate already multiplies the $k$th contribution by
$e^{-c2^k}$.  Put $\beta=2n+\alpha$.  The elementary tail bound
\[
 \sum_{\substack{k\ge1\\ r\le C_n2^k\sqrt A}}
 2^{\beta k}e^{-c2^k}\le C e^{-c'r},\qquad r\ge0,
\]
follows by comparing the sum with its first admissible dyadic scale (enlarging
$C$ for bounded $r$).  Summing \eqref{eq:loss} therefore gives exactly the
exponentially damped fractional difference integral asserted in Lemma 3.3.
No later part of the paper needs the false factor $2^{-kn}$.
\end{proof}

\section{What can and cannot yet be upgraded to \texorpdfstring{$L^p$}{Lp}}

One useful part of the $L^p$ extension is automatic.  Let
$R_t=(I+tL)^{-1}$.  It is a positive contraction on $L^1(\muM)$ and
$L^\infty(\muM)$, while the source proves, for disjoint closed $E,F$ at
distance $d$,
\[
 \|{\bf1}_FR_t{\bf1}_E\|_{2\to2}\le8e^{-C_1d/\sqrt t}.
\]
Riesz--Thorin interpolation yields the following.

\begin{lemma}[scalar $L^p$ off-diagonal decay]\label{lem:lp}
For $1<p<\infty$, $p'=p/(p-1)$, and
$\theta_p=2\min\{1/p,1/p'\}$,
\[
 \|{\bf1}_FR_t{\bf1}_E\|_{p\to p}
 \le8^{\theta_p}e^{-\theta_p C_1d/\sqrt t}.
\]
The same bound holds for $tL(I+tL)^{-1}$ between disjoint $E$ and $F$,
because its identity term vanishes there.
\end{lemma}

What remains is not scalar off-diagonal decay.  The intended conclusion is a
vector-valued estimate of the form
\[
 \left\|\left(\int_0^A t^{-1-\alpha/2}
 |tL(I+tL)^{-1}f|^2\,dt\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^p(\muM)}
 \lesssim \|D_{\alpha,c}f\|_{L^p(\muM)}.
\]
For $p=2$, the source squares local estimates, sums disjoint cubes, and uses
Fubini.  For $p\ne2$, scalar interpolation does not justify the corresponding
$L^p(L^2(dt/t))$ localized estimate.  Equivalently, one needs an appropriate
vector-valued or $R$-bounded off-diagonal family.  The embeddings between
$L^p(\ell^2)$ and $\ell^2(L^p)$ point in opposite directions on the two sides
of $p=2$, so neither Minkowski shortcut covers all $p$.

This packet therefore gives a full negative answer to the \emph{literal}
printed annular assertion, a complete repair of the $L^2$ theorem, and two
genuine $L^p$ ingredients, but it does not claim to settle the intended global
square-function comparison.

\section{Novelty, scope, and human review}

A bounded run-index and web/arXiv search on 17 August 2026 used arXiv id
0911.4563, the title and authors, ``Lemma 3.4'', the displayed annular
normalization, and erratum/correction terms.  It found copies of the same
statement and later adjacent nonlocal $L^p$ Poincar\'e inequalities, but no
erratum, counterexample, or matching repair.  Novelty confidence is moderate
to high, not conclusive.

Human review should focus on three checks: the drifting-center Gaussian
construction in Theorem \ref{thm:counter}, the corrected mean-cube
normalization in Proposition \ref{prop:correct}, and the safe exponent
$2n+\alpha$ in Theorem \ref{thm:repair}.  The distinct global $L^p$ question
is explicitly left unresolved.
