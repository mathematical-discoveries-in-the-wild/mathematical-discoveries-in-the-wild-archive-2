\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Substantial partial resolution of Conjecture 80 in Boche--M\"onich
\cite{source}: the conjectured pointwise \(c\log N\) lower bound holds for
every periodic nonuniform perturbation of the integer sampling lattice.
The general aperiodic complete-interpolating-sequence case remains open.

\section*{Source conjecture}

Holger Boche and Ullrich J.\ M\"onich,
\emph{Signal and System Approximation from General Measurements},
arXiv:1402.1092 (2014) \cite{source}.  In Conjecture 80, following Remark 26,
they ask whether every ordered complete interpolating sequence
\(\{t_k\}_{k\in\mathbb Z}\) for \(\PW_\pi^2\), with cardinal functions
\(\phi_k\), satisfies
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
 \left|\sum_{k=-N}^{N}e^{i\omega t_k}\widehat\phi_k(x)\right|\,dx
 \geq C\log N
\]
for every \(\omega\in[-\pi,\pi]\) and \(N\in\mathbb N\).  Their proved
estimate has instead the maximum over all earlier truncation indices
\(1\leq M\leq N\).

The cached TeX was faithfully compiled with a substitute article class
because its publisher class was absent; the conjecture appears on page 18
of that local source PDF, where automatic numbering labels it Conjecture
5.3 rather than 80.

\section*{Definitions and theorem}

Fix an integer \(q\geq2\) and real numbers
\[
 0=\alpha_0<\alpha_1<\cdots<\alpha_{q-1}<q.
\]
For \(m\in\mathbb Z\) and \(0\leq r<q\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nodes}
 t_{qm+r}=qm+\alpha_r.
\end{equation}
These nodes are strictly increasing and \(t_0=0\).  Let
\(\phi_k\in\PW_\pi^2\) be their cardinal functions, normalized by
\[
 \phi_k(t_\ell)=\delta_{k\ell},\qquad
 \phi_k(t)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
 \widehat\phi_k(x)e^{ixt}\,dx.
\]
Put
\[
 L_N(\omega)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
 \left|\sum_{k=-N}^{N}e^{i\omega t_k}\widehat\phi_k(x)\right|\,dx.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Periodic nonuniform case]\label{thm:main}
The sequence in \eqref{eq:nodes} is a complete interpolating sequence for
\(\PW_\pi^2\).  There is a constant
\(c=c(q,\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{q-1})>0\) such that
\[
 L_N(\omega)\geq c\log(N+1)
\]
for every \(N\geq1\) and every \(\omega\in[-\pi,\pi]\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Alternating nonuniform grids]\label{cor:q2}
For every \(0<a<2\), the nonuniform sequence
\[
 t_{2m}=2m,\qquad t_{2m+1}=2m+a
\]
satisfies Conjecture 80.  Except when \(a=1\), its consecutive gaps alternate
between \(a\) and \(2-a\), so this is a genuinely non-equidistant family.
\end{corollary}

\section*{Proof intuition}

Split the frequency interval into \(q\) equal cells.  Under this splitting,
the exponential basis associated with \eqref{eq:nodes} becomes the ordinary
Fourier basis on one cell, multiplied pointwise by a fixed Vandermonde
matrix and harmless unimodular phases.  Inverting that finite matrix gives
the biorthogonal functions explicitly.

For a fixed evaluation frequency \(\omega\), restrict the Lebesgue integral
to the same cell as \(\omega\).  All residue classes modulo \(q\) have a
large common block of truncation indices.  Their contribution factors as
a scalar Dirichlet kernel times a continuous coefficient whose value on
the diagonal is exactly \(q\).  The non-common endpoints contribute only
a uniformly bounded remainder.  The local \(L^1\)-norm of the Dirichlet
kernel is logarithmic, and the bounded remainder cannot cancel that growth.

\section*{Proof}

Partition \([-\pi,\pi)\) into cells
\[
 I_j=I_0+s_j,\qquad
 I_0=[-\pi,-\pi+2\pi/q),\qquad s_j=\frac{2\pi j}{q},
 \quad 0\leq j<q.
\]
Set
\[
 z_r=e^{2\pi i\alpha_r/q},\qquad
 V=(z_r^j)_{\substack{0\leq j<q\\0\leq r<q}}.
\]
The \(z_r\) are distinct, so the Vandermonde matrix \(V\) is invertible.

\subsection*{The exponential system is a Riesz basis}

Use the cell-splitting map
\[
 (\mathcal Jf)(y)=\bigl(f(y+s_0),\ldots,f(y+s_{q-1})\bigr),
 \qquad y\in I_0.
\]
For \(k=qm+r\),
\[
 \mathcal J(e^{it_k\cdot})(y)
 =e^{iqmy}e^{i\alpha_r y}v_r,
 \qquad v_r=(1,z_r,\ldots,z_r^{q-1})^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Thus the split system is obtained from the orthogonal vector Fourier basis
\(\{e^{iqmy}e_r:m\in\mathbb Z,0\leq r<q\}\) by multiplication with
\[
 W(y)=V\,\operatorname{diag}
 \bigl(e^{i\alpha_0y},\ldots,e^{i\alpha_{q-1}y}\bigr).
\]
Both \(W(y)\) and \(W(y)^{-1}\) are uniformly bounded on \(I_0\).
Consequently \(\{e^{it_kx}\}_{k\in\mathbb Z}\) is a Riesz basis of
\(L^2[-\pi,\pi]\).  By the complete-interpolation/Riesz-basis equivalence
recalled in the source \cite[Theorem 11]{source}, the nodes form a complete
interpolating sequence for \(\PW_\pi^2\).

\subsection*{Explicit biorthogonal functions}

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C}
 C=q(V^{\mathsf T})^{-1}=(c_{jr})_{0\leq j,r<q}.
\end{equation}
We claim that the Fourier transforms of the cardinal functions are
characterized cellwise by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
 \widehat\phi_{qm+r}(y+s_j)
 =e^{-i(qm+\alpha_r)y}c_{jr},
 \qquad y\in I_0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, for \(t_{qn+s}=qn+\alpha_s\), cell splitting gives
\begin{align*}
 &\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
 e^{it_{qn+s}x}\widehat\phi_{qm+r}(x)\,dx\\
 &\quad=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{I_0}
 e^{iq(n-m)y}e^{i(\alpha_s-\alpha_r)y}
 \sum_{j=0}^{q-1}z_s^jc_{jr}\,dy.
\end{align*}
By \eqref{eq:C}, the sum is \(q\delta_{sr}\).  When \(s=r\),
orthogonality of \(\{e^{iqmy}\}\) on the interval of length \(2\pi/q\)
then makes the last display \(\delta_{nm}\); when \(s\neq r\), it is zero
before integration.  Hence \eqref{eq:dual} is the unique biorthogonal
family.

\subsection*{Dirichlet core of every truncation}

Fix \(\omega\in I_j\), write \(\omega=y_\omega+s_j\), and restrict the
integration variable to \(x=y+s_j\in I_j\).  Put \(u=\omega-x\) and
\[
 b_{jr}=z_r^jc_{jr},\qquad
 A_j(u)=\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}b_{jr}e^{i\alpha_ru}.
\]
From \eqref{eq:dual},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 K_N(\omega,x):=\sum_{k=-N}^{N}
 e^{i\omega t_k}\widehat\phi_k(x)
 =
 \sum_{r=0}^{q-1}b_{jr}e^{i\alpha_ru}
 \sum_{\substack{m\in\mathbb Z\\|qm+r|\leq N}}e^{iqmu}.
\end{equation}
Since \(C^{\mathsf T}=qV^{-1}\),
\[
 A_j(0)=\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}V_{jr}C_{jr}
 =(VC^{\mathsf T})_{jj}=q.
\]
There are finitely many \(j\), so some \(\eta>0\), chosen also with
\(\eta\leq\pi/q\), satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Aaway}
 |A_j(u)|\geq q/2
 \quad(0\leq j<q,\ |u|\leq\eta).
\end{equation}

Write \(N=q\ell+s\), \(0\leq s<q\).  The integer ranges in the inner sums
of \eqref{eq:kernel} all contain the same consecutive block
\[
 J_N=
 \begin{cases}
 \{-\ell,\ldots,\ell-1\},&s\leq q-2,\\
 \{-\ell,\ldots,\ell\},&s=q-1.
 \end{cases}
\]
Each residue class has at most two further endpoint indices.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomp}
 K_N(\omega,x)=A_j(u)P_N(u)+R_{N,j}(u),
 \qquad
 P_N(u)=\sum_{m\in J_N}e^{iqmu},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R}
 |R_{N,j}(u)|\leq
 B:=2\max_j\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}|b_{jr}|
\end{equation}
for every \(N,j,u\).

\subsection*{Local logarithmic lower bound}

If a consecutive integer set has cardinality \(d\), then
\[
 \left|\sum_{m=a}^{a+d-1}e^{iqmu}\right|
 =\frac{|\sin(dqu/2)|}{|\sin(qu/2)|}.
\]
The elementary Dirichlet-kernel estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dir}
 \int_0^\eta
 \frac{|\sin(dqu/2)|}{|\sin(qu/2)|}\,du
 \geq c_0\log(d+1)-C_0
\end{equation}
holds with constants depending only on \(q,\eta\).  For completeness,
\(|\sin(qu/2)|\leq q|u|/2\), and after the change of variables
\(v=dqu/2\), the lower bound follows by summing
\(\int_{m\pi+\pi/6}^{m\pi+5\pi/6}|\sin v|v^{-1}\,dv\)
over \(m\leq d\eta q/(2\pi)\).

For every \(\omega\), one of the two one-sided intervals of length \(\eta\)
starting at \(\omega\) lies in the same cell \(I_j\); at a cell boundary,
choose either adjacent cell.  On that interval, \eqref{eq:Aaway}--\eqref{eq:R}
and the triangle inequality give, for all sufficiently large \(N\),
\begin{align*}
 2\pi L_N(\omega)
 &\geq \int_0^\eta
 \bigl(|A_j(u)||P_N(u)|-|R_{N,j}(u)|\bigr)\,du\\
 &\geq \frac q2\bigl(c_0\log(|J_N|+1)-C_0\bigr)-B\eta\\
 &\geq c_1\log(N+1)-C_1,
\end{align*}
uniformly in \(\omega\).  This is at least \(c_2\log(N+1)\) once \(N\)
is large.

For each of the finitely many remaining \(N\), the function
\(\omega\mapsto L_N(\omega)\) is continuous and strictly positive: a
finite nontrivial linear combination of the biorthogonal functions cannot
vanish in \(L^2\).  Shrinking \(c_2\) covers those \(N\) as well and proves
Theorem \ref{thm:main}.  Corollary \ref{cor:q2} is the case
\(q=2,\alpha_0=0,\alpha_1=a\).
\qed

\section*{Verification notes}

\begin{itemize}
\item The identity \(VC^{\mathsf T}=qI\) is the key noncancellation check:
      it forces the coefficient of the Dirichlet core to equal \(q\) on
      the diagonal, independently of the cell.
\item Every residue-class truncation differs from the common block \(J_N\)
      by at most two indices.  This is why the remainder in
      \eqref{eq:decomp} is bounded independently of \(N\).
\item The optional checker in the packet's code directory verifies the
      Vandermonde/dual identities and numerically evaluates sample Lebesgue
      functions for alternating and three-periodic grids.  No numerical
      output is used in the proof.
\item The proof gives constants depending on the fixed periodic pattern.
      It does not assert a uniform constant over degenerating patterns
      where two \(\alpha_r\)'s approach one another modulo \(q\).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Upgrade attempts, novelty, and limitations}

The source's first two named conjectures are proved later in the same paper
and were rejected as extraction noise.  Conjecture 80 is genuine.  Direct
attempts to upgrade the source's Szarek lemma fail because that lemma is
intrinsically a maximal-partial-sum statement.  General lower bounds for
projection norms control a supremum over \(\omega\), whereas Conjecture 80
requires every \(\omega\) and every truncation.  Finite or summable
perturbations of a periodic pattern were also considered, but the dual
Riesz basis changes nonlocally, so the uniformly bounded endpoint remainder
used here no longer follows.

A bounded search on 11 August 2026 used the paper title, Conjecture 80,
complete interpolating sequences, periodic nonuniform sampling, Lebesgue
constants, and logarithmic lower bounds.  It found the authors' later
\emph{Strong Divergence for System Approximations} \cite{strong}, which
strengthens equidistant and adaptive-kernel results but does not establish
the arbitrary nonuniform kernel estimate above.  It also found literature
on periodic nonuniform reconstruction and on complete interpolating
sequences, but no exact pointwise \(L^1\) lower bound matching this theorem.
Novelty confidence is therefore moderate, not definitive.

The unresolved step is genuinely aperiodic: for a general complete
interpolating sequence, splitting into finitely many cells does not produce
a fixed finite matrix, and no common Dirichlet core with bounded remainder
is available.  The full Conjecture 80 remains open in this packet.

\section*{Human-review recommendation}

Validity confidence is high.  A reviewer should check the transpose
convention in \eqref{eq:C}, the normalization \(q\) in the biorthogonality
calculation, and the common-block indexing for \(N=q\ell+s\).  Those are the
only places where a phase or endpoint error could affect the theorem.
Specialist literature review is recommended before a public novelty claim.
