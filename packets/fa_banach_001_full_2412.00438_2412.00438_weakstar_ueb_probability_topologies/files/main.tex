\section{Source and open question}

The source is V.~Alekseev, H.~Ando, F.~M.~Schneider, and A.~Thom,
\emph{Amenability and skew-amenability of actions of topological groups},
arXiv:2412.00438v2 (24 October 2025)~\cite{source}.  After Remark 3.12 on
page 9, the authors ask whether, for a locally compact group $G$ with left
Haar measure $\nu$, the topology
\[
  \sigma(C_0(G)^*,C_0(G))
\]
on the probability densities
\[
  \Prob(G,\nu)=\{\varphi\in L^1(G,\nu):\varphi\geq0,
      \ \|\varphi\|_1=1\}
\]
agrees with the topology inherited from the UEB topology on
$\Meanu(G_{\mathrm R})$, and likewise on $\Meanu(G_{\mathrm L})$.
Equivalently, they ask whether the integration maps $\Xi_\nu^{\mathrm R}$
and $\Xi_\nu^{\mathrm L}$ are weak-star-to-UEB continuous.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
  \caption{The open question in arXiv:2412.00438v2, page 9.}
\end{figure}

The source's Appendix Lemma A.2 proves the needed equivalence for maps whose
entire family of supports has relatively compact union.  The argument below
removes that support assumption by deriving a common compact concentration
set locally from the weak-star topology itself.

\section{Definitions}

We identify $\varphi\in\Prob(G,\nu)$ with the Radon probability measure
$\varphi\,d\nu$.  A family $B$ of bounded continuous functions on a uniform
space is \emph{UEB} if it is uniformly bounded and uniformly
equicontinuous.  On the dual of the corresponding bounded uniformly
continuous functions, the UEB topology is generated by
\[
  p_B(\lambda)=\sup_{f\in B}|\lambda(f)|
\]
as $B$ ranges over UEB families.  We apply this definition to the right and
left uniformities of $G$.

We use two standard facts.  First,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c0-inclusion}
  C_0(G)\subseteq \RUCB(G)\cap\LUCB(G).
\end{equation}
Indeed, compactly supported continuous functions are uniformly continuous
for both group uniformities, and $C_0(G)$ is their uniform closure.  Second,
the restriction of a UEB family to a compact subset $L\subseteq G$ is
relatively compact, hence totally bounded, in $C(L)$ by the compact-space
Arzela-Ascoli theorem~\cite{ascoli}.

\section{Proof intuition}

The apparent mismatch is that weak-star convergence tests one function at a
time, whereas a UEB seminorm tests a whole equicontinuous family at once.
Probability mass prevents escape at a weak-star limit which is still a
probability measure.  Near a fixed probability $\mu$, a single compactly
supported cutoff forces every nearby probability to put almost all of its
mass in one compact set.  On that compact set, Arzela-Ascoli reduces a UEB
family to finitely many test functions up to a small uniform error.  The
cutoff turns those finitely many tests into elements of $C_0(G)$, exactly the
functions controlled by the weak-star topology.  The reverse implication is
immediate from \eqref{eq:c0-inclusion}.

\section{The full result}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $G$ be a locally compact Hausdorff group and let $\nu$ be a left Haar
measure.  On $\Prob(G,\nu)$, the topology
$\sigma(C_0(G)^*,C_0(G))$ agrees with the topology pulled back by
$\Xi_\nu^{\mathrm R}$ from the UEB topology on $\Meanu(G_{\mathrm R})$.
It also agrees with the topology pulled back by $\Xi_\nu^{\mathrm L}$ from
the UEB topology on $\Meanu(G_{\mathrm L})$.

Consequently, both $\Xi_\nu^{\mathrm R}$ and $\Xi_\nu^{\mathrm L}$ are
$\sigma(C_0(G)^*,C_0(G))$-to-UEB continuous.  Thus the question after
Remark 3.12 of arXiv:2412.00438v2 has an affirmative answer, without a
second-countability assumption.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The same argument applies to the right and left uniformities.  Fix one of
them, write $\Xi$ for the associated integration map, and let $B$ be a UEB
family in the associated function algebra.  Put
\[
  M=\sup_{f\in B}\|f\|_\infty<\infty.
\]
If $M=0$, there is nothing to prove.  Fix $\mu\in\Prob(G,\nu)$ and
$\varepsilon>0$.  Choose positive numbers $\eta,\delta$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
  3M\eta+3\delta<\varepsilon.
\end{equation}

The measure $\mu$ is a Radon probability measure.  By inner regularity and
the locally compact Urysohn lemma, there is a function
$\chi\in C_c(G)$ such that
\[
  0\leq\chi\leq1,
  \qquad \mu(\chi)>1-\eta.
\]
Let $L=\operatorname{supp}\chi$, which is compact.  The restricted family
$B|_L$ is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous.  Arzela-Ascoli therefore
gives $f_1,\ldots,f_n\in B$ such that for every $f\in B$ some $j$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-net}
  \sup_{x\in L}|f(x)-f_j(x)|<\delta.
\end{equation}
Each $\chi f_j$ belongs to $C_c(G)\subseteq C_0(G)$.

Consider the following weak-star neighborhood of $\mu$ inside
$\Prob(G,\nu)$:
\[
  V=\left\{\lambda\in\Prob(G,\nu):
  |\lambda(\chi)-\mu(\chi)|<\eta,\quad
  |\lambda(\chi f_j)-\mu(\chi f_j)|<\delta
  \ (1\leq j\leq n)\right\}.
\]
For $\lambda\in V$, positivity and normalization give
\[
  \lambda(1-\chi)<2\eta,
  \qquad \mu(1-\chi)<\eta.
\]
Given $f\in B$, choose $f_j$ as in \eqref{eq:finite-net}.  Splitting with
the cutoff and using that $\chi$ is supported in $L$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 |\lambda(f)-\mu(f)|
 &\leq |\lambda((1-\chi)f)|+|\mu((1-\chi)f)| \\
 &\quad +|\lambda(\chi(f-f_j))|+|\mu(\chi(f-f_j))| \\
 &\quad +|\lambda(\chi f_j)-\mu(\chi f_j)| \\
 &< 3M\eta+3\delta<\varepsilon.
\end{align*}
The bound is uniform over $f\in B$.  Hence
\[
  \sup_{f\in B}|\Xi(\lambda)(f)-\Xi(\mu)(f)|<\varepsilon
  \qquad(\lambda\in V).
\]
Every UEB seminorm is therefore continuous for the weak-star topology on
$\Prob(G,\nu)$.  This proves that the weak-star topology is at least as
strong as the pulled-back UEB topology.

Conversely, take $h\in C_0(G)$.  By \eqref{eq:c0-inclusion}, the singleton
$\{h\}$ is a UEB family for either group uniformity.  Moreover,
\[
  \Xi(\lambda)(h)=\lambda(h)
  \qquad(\lambda\in\Prob(G,\nu)).
\]
Thus every weak-star coordinate is continuous for the pulled-back UEB
topology.  The two topologies agree.  Applying the argument once to
$\RUCB(G)$ and once to $\LUCB(G)$ proves both assertions.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification notes}

The proof works directly with neighborhoods, so there is no sequentiality or
metrizability assumption hidden in the argument.  Equivalently, for a net
$\mu_i\to\mu$ in the weak-star topology within the probability subspace, the
cutoff $\chi$ shows eventual uniform tightness of the net; the finite-net
estimate then yields convergence in every UEB seminorm.

The normalization to probability measures is essential to the tightness
step.  In the full positive cone of $C_0(G)^*$, vague convergence can lose
mass at infinity (for example, escaping point masses), so the corresponding
statement must not be asserted there.  No such escape is possible when both
the approximants and their weak-star limit lie in $\Prob(G,\nu)$.

The proof in fact applies to all Radon probability measures on $G$, not only
those absolutely continuous with respect to Haar measure.  The narrower
formulation above exactly answers the source question.

were searched for arXiv:2412.00438, the paper title, and the core phrase
``weak-star UEB probability topology''; no duplicate was found.  A bounded

current arXiv v2, dated 24 October 2025, still contains the question on page 9.

This packet answers only that topology/continuity question.  It does not
claim to settle the paper's separate questions about non-second-countable
amenable actions, well-placed subgroups, or extensions of exact groups.
