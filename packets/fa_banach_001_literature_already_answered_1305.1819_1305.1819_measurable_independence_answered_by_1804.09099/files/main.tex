\section{Question in the source}

The final section, ``Future work,'' of Dobre, D\"ur, Frerick, and
Vallentin~\cite{source} asks for a copositive formulation of the measurable
stability problem for graphs admitting infinite measurable stable sets.  Its
central example is the graph on the unit sphere in which two vertices are
adjacent exactly when they are orthogonal.  Thus the requested parameter is
\[
 m_0(\Sph)=\sup\{\omega(I): I\subseteq\Sph\text{ measurable and containing
 no orthogonal pair}\},
\]
where $\omega$ is surface measure.  This is Witsenhausen's problem.  The source
does not ask for the still-open numerical evaluation of $m_0(\Sph)$; it asks
for an exact copositive optimization formulation from which stronger bounds
could be derived.

\section{Exact later answer}

DeCorte, de Oliveira Filho, and Vallentin~\cite{answer} address precisely this
setting.  The match is explicit at four levels.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Section~2, example~(ii), identifies Witsenhausen's problem with the
measurable independence number $\alpha_\omega(G(\Sph,\{\pi/2\}))$.

\item Their general convex program (equation~(3.3) in the published numbering;
the arXiv source label is \texttt{theta-problem}) maximizes
\[
 \langle J,A\rangle
\]
over continuous kernels $A$ with unit diagonal integral, vanishing on graph
edges, and lying in a chosen cone.  For the cone $\mathcal C(V)$ of completely
positive kernels, it is the infinite measurable analogue of the standard
finite completely-positive stability-number program.

\item Section~4 defines the dual cone $\mathcal C^*(V)$ and explicitly proves
that it is the cone of copositive kernels:
\[
 \mathcal C^*(V)=\{Z:\langle Z,f\otimes f^*\rangle\ge0
 \text{ for every }f\in L^2(V),\ f\ge0\}.
\]
Thus the program has exactly the copositive/completely-positive conic duality
requested in the 2013 paper.

\item Theorem~5.1 (labelled \texttt{thm:cp-exactness}) proves
\[
 \vartheta(G,\mathcal C(V))=\alpha_\omega(G)
\]
for locally independent compact vertex-transitive graphs under the stated Haar
measure and density-metric hypotheses.  The paragraph immediately following
the theorem applies it to every single-angle graph
$G(\Sph,\{\theta\})$.  Taking $\theta=\pi/2$ is exactly the sphere
orthogonality graph.
\end{enumerate}

The paper also states the specialization directly as Theorem~1.1: replacing
positive type by completely positive type in its spherical optimization
problem makes the optimal value exactly $m_0(\Sph)$.  In radial form this is
\begin{equation*}
\begin{array}{rl}
\text{maximize}&\displaystyle
 \int_{\Sph}\int_{\Sph} f(x\cdot y)\,d\omega(y)d\omega(x)\\[1ex]
\text{subject to}&f(1)=\omega(\Sph)^{-1},\quad f(0)=0,\\
&f\text{ continuous and of completely positive type for }\Sph.
\end{array}
\end{equation*}
This is an exact formulation, not merely an upper bound.  The numerical value
of Witsenhausen's parameter (and the double-cap conjecture) remains open; that
does not weaken the answer to the formulation request.

\section{Conclusion and verification}

The 2018 arXiv preprint, later published in \emph{Mathematical Programming},
therefore resolves the future-work question in arXiv:1305.1819 in the intended
sense.  It is especially strong evidence of continuity of purpose that Frank
Vallentin is a coauthor of both papers and that the later paper cites the 2013
copositive-kernel work while extending it to measurable independence.

The identification was checked against the official PDFs and TeX sources of
both papers, including the 2013 ``Future work'' section; the 2018 Section~2
Witsenhausen example; the general program; Section~4's copositive dual cone;
Theorem~5.1 and its immediate spherical application; and Theorem~1.1.
