For each \(s\in\{1,2,\infty\}\) and each \(\varepsilon>0\), there is a
polynomial \(f\), nonvanishing on the closed bidisk, whose degree-one optimal
polynomial approximant \(p\) to \(1/f\) has
\[
 \inf\bigl\{\|z\|_s:p(z)=0\bigr\}<\varepsilon.
\]
Thus the sharp universal zero-exclusion radius is zero for all three norms.
The construction concentrates \(|f|^2\) near two almost co-rotating points
of the distinguished boundary; the OPA normal equations then converge to a
two-point interpolation system with coefficients of order \(m\).
\end{minipage}}

\section{The question and the later numerical benchmark}

B\'en\'eteau, Khavinson, and Seco ask how far inside the bidisk zeros of an
optimal polynomial approximant (OPA) can get, explicitly distinguishing the
\(\ell_1\), \(\ell_2\), and \(\ell_\infty\) distances in \(\C^2\)
\cite[pp.~13--14]{BKS}. Their complete statement is reproduced on the next

\section{Setup and proof intuition}

The Hardy norm on \(H^2(\D^2)\) is the \(\ell_2\)-norm of Taylor
coefficients, equivalently the boundary \(L^2(\T^2)\)-norm. The degree-one
OPA for \(1/f\) is the polynomial
\[
 p_f=p_0+p_1z_1+p_2z_2
\]
minimizing \(\|1-pf\|_{H^2}\).

The key is to view its normal equations through the probability measure
\[
 d\mu_f=\frac{|f|^2}{\|f\|_{H^2}^2}\,dm
 \quad\text{on }\T^2.
\]
After dividing by \(p_0\), the two nonconstant normal equations depend only
on the first moments of \(\mu_f\). If these measures converge to equal mass
at two points \((1,1)\) and \((a,b)\), the normalized OPA converges to the
affine polynomial that vanishes at both points.

We choose \(a\) and \(b\) so that both rotate by about \(2\pi/m\), but their
relative rotation is only \(2\pi/m^2\). The two interpolation rows are
therefore almost proportional while asking for incompatible constant
values. The affine coefficients grow like \(m\), and its zero hyperplane
passes within distance \(O(1/m)\) of the origin.

\section{OPA normal equations as moments}

\begin{lemma}[moment form and continuity]\label{lem:moment}
Let \(0\ne f\in H^2(\D^2)\) with \(f(0)\ne0\), and let \(p_f\) be its
degree-one OPA. Then \(p_0\ne0\). Writing
\[
 p_f=p_0(1+q_1z_1+q_2z_2),
\]
the vector \(q=(q_1,q_2)^T\) is the unique solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal}
 \left(\int_{\T^2} z_i\overline{z_j}\,d\mu_f\right)_{j,i=1}^2 q
 =
 -\left(\int_{\T^2}\overline{z_j}\,d\mu_f\right)_{j=1}^2.
\end{equation}
If probability measures \(\mu_\nu\) converge weakly and the limiting
\(2\times2\) matrix in \eqref{eq:normal} is invertible, their normalized OPA
coefficient vectors converge to the limiting solution.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Gram matrix of \(f,z_1f,z_2f\) is positive definite: an analytic
identity
\[
 c_0f+c_1z_1f+c_2z_2f=0
\]
forces the affine factor to vanish identically. The right side of the full
normal system is \((\overline{f(0)},0,0)^T\). Positivity and the Schur
complement show that the first coordinate \(p_0\) is nonzero.

For \(j=1,2\), the right side is zero because \(z_jf\) has zero constant
term. Hence
\[
 0=\langle p_ff,z_jf\rangle
 =p_0\left\langle(1+q_1z_1+q_2z_2)f,z_jf\right\rangle.
\]
Division by \(p_0\|f\|_{H^2}^2\) gives \eqref{eq:normal}. Weak convergence
gives convergence of all displayed continuous moments, and inversion is
continuous at an invertible matrix.
\end{proof}

\section{A boundary concentration codebook}

Fix an integer \(m\ge3\), put \(D=m^2\), and let
\[
 \zeta=e^{2\pi i/D}.
\]
Define the analytic polynomials
\begin{align}
 G_m(z_1,z_2)&=z_1^m+z_1^{m-1}z_2^m,\label{eq:G}\\
 L_\omega(z)&=\frac1D\sum_{r=0}^{D-1}(z/\omega)^r,\\
 H_m(z_1,z_2)&=-1+L_1(z_2)+L_{\zeta^{m+1}}(z_2).\label{eq:H}
\end{align}

\begin{lemma}[two-point concentration]\label{lem:concentration}
For \(t,M>0\), set
\[
 f_{m,t,M}(z)=\exp\bigl(tG_m(z)+MH_m(z)\bigr).
\]
These functions are entire and zero-free. Their boundary probability
measures satisfy
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}\mu_{f_{m,t,M}}
 =
 \frac12\delta_{(1,1)}
 +\frac12\delta_{(a_m,b_m)},
\]
where
\[
 a_m=\zeta^m=e^{2\pi i/m},
 \qquad
 b_m=\zeta^{m+1}=e^{2\pi i(1/m+1/m^2)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On \(\T^2\), \(\operatorname{Re}G_m\le2\). Equality holds precisely when
\[
 z_1^m=1,
 \qquad
 z_1^{m-1}z_2^m=1.
\]
The exponent matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}m&0\\m-1&m\end{pmatrix}
\]
has determinant \(m^2\), so the maximum set consists of exactly
\[
 K_m=\bigl\{(\zeta^{mk},\zeta^{(m+1)k}):0\le k<D\bigr\}.
\]
All maxima are nondegenerate and have the same Hessian: in local angular
coordinates the quadratic loss is
\[
 \frac12\bigl[(m u)^2+((m-1)u+mv)^2\bigr].
\]

Because \(\gcd(m+1,D)=1\), the second coordinates in \(K_m\) run through all
\(D\)-th roots of unity. The root-of-unity filter \(L_\omega\) equals one
at \(\omega\) and zero at the other such roots. Hence \(H_m\) equals zero
at \((1,1)\) and \((a_m,b_m)\), and equals \(-1\) at every other point of
\(K_m\).

The elementary Laplace method at finitely many equal, nondegenerate maxima
now gives, for fixed \(M\), limiting masses proportional to
\(e^{2M H_m(x)}\). Thus the two selected points have weight one and each
other point has weight \(e^{-2M}\). Sending \(M\) to infinity proves the
claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact limiting zero depths}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:depth}
For the equal two-point measure in Lemma~\ref{lem:concentration}, the limiting
normalized OPA is
\[
 \ell_m(z)=1+q_{1,m}z_1+q_{2,m}z_2,
\]
where
\[
 q_{1,m}=\frac{b_m-1}{a_m-b_m},
 \qquad
 q_{2,m}=\frac{1-a_m}{a_m-b_m}.
\]
If \(\rho_s(\ell)=\inf\{\|z\|_s:\ell(z)=0\}\), then
\[
 \rho_s(\ell_m)=\frac1{\|(q_{1,m},q_{2,m})\|_{s^*}},
\]
where \(s^*\) is the dual exponent. In particular,
\begin{align}
 \rho_\infty(\ell_m)
 &=\frac{\sin(\pi/m^2)}
 {\sin(\pi/m)+\sin(\pi/m+\pi/m^2)}
 \sim\frac1{2m},\label{eq:rinf}\\
 \rho_2(\ell_m)
 &\sim\frac1{\sqrt2\,m},
 \qquad
 \rho_1(\ell_m)\sim\frac1m.\label{eq:rother}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two feature rows \((1,1)\) and \((a_m,b_m)\) are linearly independent
because \(a_m\ne b_m\). They therefore span all functions on the two-point
space. The limiting normal equations say exactly that \(\ell_m\) vanishes
at both points:
\[
 1+q_{1,m}+q_{2,m}=0,
 \qquad
 1+q_{1,m}a_m+q_{2,m}b_m=0.
\]
Solving gives the displayed coefficients.

H\"older's inequality, with equality in the dual direction, gives
\[
 \inf\bigl\{\|z\|_s:q_{1,m}z_1+q_{2,m}z_2=-1\bigr\}
 =\frac1{\|q_m\|_{s^*}}.
\]
Finally,
\begin{align*}
 |a_m-b_m|&=2\sin(\pi/m^2),\\
 |1-a_m|&=2\sin(\pi/m),\\
 |1-b_m|&=2\sin(\pi/m+\pi/m^2).
\end{align*}
Substitution proves \eqref{eq:rinf}; the \(\ell_2\) and \(\ell_1\) formulas
follow by replacing the sum of the last two chord lengths by their Euclidean
norm or maximum. Their asymptotics are immediate.
\end{proof}

\section{The full polynomial result}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every \(\varepsilon>0\) and each
\(s\in\{1,2,\infty\}\), there exists a polynomial \(F\) with no zeros on
\(\overline{\D}^2\) such that its degree-one OPA \(p_F\) has a zero \(z\)
with \(\|z\|_s<\varepsilon\). Consequently,
\[
 \inf_F \inf\bigl\{\|z\|_s:p_F(z)=0\bigr\}=0,
\]
even when \(F\) ranges only over polynomials nonvanishing on the closed
bidisk.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(m\) so large that the three quantities in
\eqref{eq:rinf}--\eqref{eq:rother} are below \(\varepsilon/2\). By
Lemmas~\ref{lem:moment} and~\ref{lem:concentration}, first choosing \(M\)
large and then \(t\) large makes the normalized OPA coefficients of the
zero-free entire function \(f_{m,t,M}\) arbitrarily close to those of
\(\ell_m\). The corresponding affine zero depths are continuous functions
of the two nonconstant coefficients, so all three depths are below
\(\varepsilon\).

It remains to obtain a polynomial. The Taylor polynomials \(F_N\) of
\(f_{m,t,M}\) converge uniformly on the closed bidisk. Since the limiting
entire function has a positive minimum modulus there, \(F_N\) is
nonvanishing on the closed bidisk for all sufficiently large \(N\).
Uniform convergence also gives \(H^2\)-convergence, hence convergence of the
finite Gram matrices and of the degree-one OPA coefficients. Taking \(N\)
large preserves the strict zero-depth inequalities.
\end{proof}

\section{Checks, upgrade attempts, and scope}

\paragraph{Exact finite check.}
The packet-local script \texttt{code/verify\_two\_point\_construction.py}
checks the finite group relations, solves the two-point interpolation
system, verifies the chord-length formula, and shows numerically the
\(1/m\) decay for all three norms. It is confirmatory only; every step is
proved above.

\paragraph{Deep upgrades.}
The first attempt reduced the source's symmetric two-parameter family to
one-dimensional positive sums and reproduced its reported minimum
\(0.9757663352595\). A second search over zero-free exponential families
found explicit values below \(0.15\). The decisive upgrade replaced
numerical search by the two-point concentration codebook, proving that the
infimum is zero. A final upgrade used uniform Taylor approximation to obtain
zero-free polynomials, rather than only zero-free entire functions.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The theorem completely answers the zero-depth extremal question for
degree-one OPAs with the standard total-degree space
\(\operatorname{span}\{1,z_1,z_2\}\), simultaneously for the three norms
asked in the source. It does not address higher-degree OPAs, eventual escape
of zeros, other monomial orderings, or Dirichlet-type spaces.

\paragraph{Novelty check.}
Bounded primary-source searches through 11 August 2026 covered the exact
question, degree-one OPAs, zero-free polynomials on the bidisk, and zeros
arbitrarily close to the origin. They recovered the two source papers and
one-variable zero-exclusion results, but no primary source stating the
theorem above. Novelty confidence is moderate because the search was
bounded.

\paragraph{Human review.}
Audit the two-stage Laplace limit, the moment-to-interpolation passage, and
the preservation of zero-freeness under uniform Taylor approximation.
