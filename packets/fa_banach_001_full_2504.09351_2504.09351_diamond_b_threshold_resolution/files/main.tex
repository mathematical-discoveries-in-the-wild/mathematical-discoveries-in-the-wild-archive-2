\section{Source question and result}

Mart\'inez-Celis and Morawski prove under $\Diamond(\operatorname{non}(\cM))$
that there is a Radon--Nikod\'ym (RN) compact space of weight $\aleph_1$ with a
non-RN continuous image \cite{MC}.  Their construction necessarily has weight
at least $\operatorname{non}(\cM)$, and they ask:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Question 1.} Is it consistent to have
$\operatorname{non}(\cM)>\aleph_1$, but have a Radon--Nikod\'ym compact space
of weight $\aleph_1$ with a continuous image that is not Radon--Nikod\'ym?
\end{quote}

They specifically suggest changing their condition (5) so that the space can
be constructed from $\Diamond(\mathfrak b)$.  The relevant passage is reproduced
below from page 9 of the source PDF.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

We carry out that suggestion.

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]\label{thm:main}
Assume $\Diamond(\mathfrak b)$.  There are compact spaces $L_0,L_1$ of weight
$\aleph_1$ and a continuous surjection $\pi:L_0\to L_1$ such that $L_0$ is
Radon--Nikod\'ym and $L_1$ is not Radon--Nikod\'ym.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:consistency}
It is relatively consistent with ZFC that
$\operatorname{non}(\cM)>\aleph_1$ and that an RN compactum of weight
$\aleph_1$ has a non-RN continuous image.  Hence Question 1 has an affirmative
consistency answer.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, Minami's Corollary 4.7(ii) gives a model in which
\[
 \mathfrak c=\operatorname{non}(\cM)=\operatorname{cov}(\cM)=\aleph_2
 \quad\text{and}\quad \Diamond(\mathfrak b)
\]
hold simultaneously \cite[Corollary 4.7(ii), p.~762]{Minami}.  Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} in that model proves the corollary.

\section{Proof intuition}

The source construction assigns to each dyadic cell $t\in2^n$ a continuous
Cantor map whose image is the dyadic interval determined by $t$.  Exact
guessing was used to arrange that one small cylinder in each relevant fiber is
sent to the two endpoints of that interval.  Exact strings on a whole level
lead naturally to $\operatorname{non}(\cM)$.

Only the \emph{length} of the small cylinder is actually needed.  If $S_m$
deletes the first $m$ bits, then
\[
             \sigma\longmapsto t\concat S_m(\sigma)
\]
maps every cylinder of length at most $m$ onto the full Cantor cylinder $[t]$.
Thus an integer threshold replaces an exact-string guess.

There are two simultaneous requirements.  For each useful level $n$, one
integer $m_\beta(n)$ must work for every $t\in2^n$; and for every such $t$ we
need infinitely many base points, so they converge to the resolution point
$\beta$.  A single $\Diamond(\mathfrak b)$ coordinate does both jobs.  Its value
sets the threshold for its own level, while that same successful coordinate
adds one new good ladder point to every earlier cell.  Infinitely many
successful coordinates therefore populate every cell infinitely often.

Crucially, maps belonging to level $n$ still have image diameter at most
$2^{-n}$.  That shrinking-diameter property is exactly what preserves the
Reznichenko metric on the Cantor resolution $L_0$.  On the interval resolution
$L_1$, lower semicontinuity gives an estimate $4^{-n}$ across every dyadic
cell, and chaining $2^n$ cells forces the distance between the two endpoints
to be at most $2^{-n}$ on infinitely many levels.

\section{The threshold ladder fan}

We use the following standard form of $\Diamond(\mathfrak b)$.  For every
Borel map $F:2^{<\omega_1}\to\ww$ there is a sequence
$\langle u_\beta:\beta<\omega_1\rangle$, $u_\beta\in\ww$, such that for every
$\gamma\in2^{\omega_1}$, stationarily many $\beta$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diamond}
       F(\gamma\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\beta)(j)<u_\beta(j)
       \quad\text{for infinitely many }j.
\end{equation}
This is the Borel relational presentation used in \cite{MHD,Minami,MC}.

Let $T=2^{<\omega}$ and, for $t\in T$, put
$A_t=\{t\}\times\omega_1$ and $A=\bigcup_{t\in T}A_t$.  Let $B$ be the set of
nonzero countable limit ordinals.  For every $\beta\in B$, fix an increasing
cofinal sequence $\langle\lambda_\beta(j):j\in\omega\rangle$ in $\beta$.
For $n\le j$, $t\in2^n$, fix an enumeration
\[
 e_{\beta,j,n,t}:\omega\longrightarrow
 \beta\setminus(\lambda_\beta(j)+1).
\]
Repetitions are harmless, although the enumerations may be chosen bijective.

\begin{lemma}[Threshold ladder lemma]\label{lem:threshold}
Under $\Diamond(\mathfrak b)$ there are countable sets $C_\beta\subset A$
and integers $m_\beta(n)$, for $\beta\in B$ and $n\in\omega$, such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $C_\beta\cap C_\eta$ is finite whenever $\beta\ne\eta$;
\item $C_\beta\cap A_t$ is infinite for every $t\in T$;
\item for every $\ell:A\to\omega$ there are $\beta\in B$ and an infinite
set $W\subset\omega$ such that, whenever $n\in W$ and $t\in2^n$,
\[
 \bigl\{a\in C_\beta\cap A_t:\ell(a)\le m_\beta(n)\bigr\}
 \quad\text{is infinite.}\tag{$\ast$}
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We describe the Borel target before applying the diamond.  A branch can use a
fixed standard code for a countable family
$\langle G_t:t\in T\rangle$ of subsets of $\omega_1$ and integers
$\langle H(t):t\in T\rangle$.  At a countable stage $\beta$, for $j,n,t$ with
$n\le j$ and $t\in2^n$, let
\[
 p_{\beta,j,n,t}=min\{k:e_{\beta,j,n,t}(k)\in G_t\cap\beta\},
\]
when this set is nonempty.  On correctly formed codes for which all the needed
minima exist, set
\[
 F(\gamma\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\beta)(j)
 =\max\Bigl(
   \{H(t):t\in2^n,\ n\le j\}
   \cup
   \{p_{\beta,j,n,t}:n\le j,\ t\in2^n\}
 \Bigr).
\tag{1}
\]
Set the value equal to $0$ on malformed codes.  For each fixed $\beta$ and
$j$, (1) takes a maximum over a finite set, and every displayed minimum is a
first-hit search in a fixed enumeration.  Hence each coordinate is Borel.
The usual pairing of $\omega_1\times\omega$ with $\omega_1$ makes this one
Borel map on $2^{<\omega_1}$.

Let $\langle u_\beta\rangle$ be the $\Diamond(\mathfrak b)$ sequence for $F$,
and put
\[
 m_\beta(n)=u_\beta(n),
\]
\[
 C_\beta[j]=
 \left\{(t,e_{\beta,j,n,t}(k)):
 n\le j,\ t\in2^n,\ k\le u_\beta(j)\right\},
 \qquad C_\beta=\bigcup_{j<\omega}C_\beta[j].
\tag{2}
\]
Every stage in (2) is finite.  If $\beta<\eta$, then $C_\beta$ has all second
coordinates below $\beta$, whereas all but finitely many stages of $C_\eta$
have all second coordinates above $\beta$.  Thus $C_\beta\cap C_\eta$ is
finite, proving (i).  For fixed $t\in2^n$, every stage $j\ge n$ contributes a
point of $A_t$ above $\lambda_\beta(j)$; cofinality of the latter sequence
gives (ii).

Now fix $\ell:A\to\omega$.  Since $A_t$ has size $\aleph_1$ and
$A_t=\bigcup_H\{a:\ell(a)\le H\}$, choose $H(t)$ so that
\[
             G_t=\{\xi<\omega_1:\ell(t,\xi)\le H(t)\}
\]
is uncountable.  There is a club of $\beta$ for which every $G_t\cap\beta$ is
cofinal in $\beta$ and the branch code restricts correctly.  Choose in this
club a diamond stage $\beta$ satisfying \eqref{eq:diamond}, and let $J$ be the
infinite set of successful coordinates $j$.

If $j\in J$, then $u_\beta(j)>H(t)$ for every $t$ on every level at most $j$.
Moreover, (2) includes, for each such $t$, a point whose second coordinate is
$e_{\beta,j,n,t}(p_{\beta,j,n,t})\in G_t$.  Fix $n\in J$ and $t\in2^n$.
Every sufficiently large $j\in J$ therefore contributes a point of
$C_\beta\cap A_t$ with $\ell$-value at most $H(t)<u_\beta(n)=m_\beta(n)$.
As these points lie above the cofinal lower bounds $\lambda_\beta(j)$, infinitely
many are distinct.  Taking $W=J$ proves (iii).
\end{proof}

\section{The two resolutions}

Give $A\cup B$ the locally compact topology in which points of $A$ are isolated
and
\[
 \bigl\{\{\beta\}\cup(C_\beta\setminus F):F\subset C_\beta\text{ finite}\bigr\}
\]
is a neighborhood base at $\beta\in B$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold}(i) makes
this space Hausdorff.  Let $K=A\cup B\cup\{c\}$ be its one-point
compactification.  This is the source paper's ``pre-basic'' space, and
$w(K)=|K|=\aleph_1$ \cite[Lemma 2.3]{MC}.

Blow up each isolated $a\in A$ to a Cantor fiber:
\[
                  L=(A\times2^\omega)\cup B\cup\{c\}.
\]
The topology has the fiber cylinders $\{a\}\times[s]$ and, for $U$ open in
$K$, the sets $((U\cap A)\times2^\omega)\cup(U\setminus A)$.  Thus $L$ is
zero-dimensional compact and has weight $\aleph_1$.

Write $S_m(\sigma)(i)=\sigma(m+i)$ for the tail shift and let
$\rho$ be the usual Cantor metric.  For $\beta\in B$, define the continuous
map $g_\beta:L\setminus\{\beta\}\to2^\omega$ by
\[
 g_\beta(a,\sigma)=t\concat S_{m_\beta(n)}(\sigma)
 \quad\text{if }a\in C_\beta\cap A_t, |t|=n,
\tag{3}
\]
and put $g_\beta=\mathbf 0$ off $(C_\beta\times2^\omega)\cup\{\beta\}$.
Continuity on an isolated fiber is clear.  At $\eta\in B\setminus\{\beta\}$,
it follows from finiteness of $C_\eta\cap C_\beta$; at $c$, take a neighborhood
avoiding the compact set $(C_\beta\times2^\omega)\cup\{\beta\}$.  Notice the
uniform range estimate
\[
 \operatorname{diam}_\rho g_\beta(\{a\}\times2^\omega)\le 2^{-n}
 \quad(a\in C_\beta\cap A_t, |t|=n).
\tag{4}
\]

Let $q:2^\omega\to[0,1]$ be
$q(x)=\sum_i x(i)2^{-(i+1)}$, and put $h_\beta=q\circ g_\beta$.  Define graph
resolutions
\begin{align*}
L_0&=\{(u,v)\in L\times(2^\omega)^B:
       \beta\ne u\Rightarrow v(\beta)=g_\beta(u)\},\\
L_1&=\{(u,w)\in L\times[0,1]^B:
       \beta\ne u\Rightarrow w(\beta)=h_\beta(u)\}.
\end{align*}
They are compact, and applying $q$ coordinatewise gives a continuous
surjection $\pi:L_0\to L_1$.  Both weights are $\aleph_1$.

\section{The Cantor resolution is RN}

We adapt Proposition 4.1 of Avil\'es--Koszmider \cite{AK} and Theorem 2.6 of
the source paper \cite{MC}.  Define a metric $d$ on $L_0$ as follows.  It is
zero on the diagonal; over one $\beta\in B$ use the Cantor distance between
the two free $\beta$-coordinates; over one isolated base point $a\in A$ use
the Cantor distance between the two fiber parameters; and set $d=1$ in every
remaining off-diagonal case.

Exactly as in \cite[Proposition 4.1]{AK}, $d$ fragments $L_0$.  Indeed, a
nonempty subset meeting an isolated Cantor fiber has a relatively open piece
of arbitrarily small $d$-diameter.  If it meets no such fiber, project it to
the scattered space $B\cup\{c\}$, isolate one projected point, and restrict
the corresponding free Cantor coordinate.

It remains to verify the Reznichenko separation property.  Distinct points
with distinct base points, or two distinct points in one isolated fiber, are
handled by disjoint base or Cantor neighborhoods.  Consider two distinct
points over the same $\beta\in B$, and choose disjoint clopen Cantor
neighborhoods of their free $\beta$-coordinates.  If these lifted neighborhoods
had $d$-distance zero, there would be pairs over common isolated base points
$a_i=(t_i,\xi_i)\in C_\beta$ with fiber parameters $\sigma_i,\tau_i$ such that
$\rho(\sigma_i,\tau_i)\to0$, while
$g_\beta(a_i,\sigma_i)$ and $g_\beta(a_i,\tau_i)$ remain in the two separated
coordinate neighborhoods.

If $|t_i|\to\infty$ along a subsequence, (4) makes the two images converge
together.  Otherwise pass to a subsequence on which $t_i=t$ is fixed.  Formula
(3) then uses one fixed continuous shift $S_{m_\beta(|t|)}$, so again the two
images converge together.  Both alternatives contradict separation of the
coordinate neighborhoods.  Thus $d$ is a fragmenting Reznichenko metric.

It follows that $L_0$ is quasi-RN.  It is zero-dimensional, and every
zero-dimensional quasi-RN compactum is RN by Arvanitakis
\cite[Theorem 3.6]{Arv}.  Hence $L_0$ is RN.

\section{The interval resolution is not RN}

Suppose toward a contradiction that an l.s.c. metric $\delta$ fragments
$L_1$.  For $a\in A$, write $a+\sigma$ for the unique point of $L_1$ over
$(a,\sigma)\in L$.  Fix $n\ge1$, $t\in2^n$, and $a\in A_t$.  Fragment the
Cantor fiber $\{a+\sigma:\sigma\in2^\omega\}$ at scale $4^{-n}$.  A nonempty
relatively open piece contains a cylinder $[s(a)]$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{diam}_\delta\{a+\sigma:\sigma\in[s(a)]\}<4^{-n}.
\tag{5}
\]
Put $\ell(a)=|s(a)|$ (define it arbitrarily on the level $n=0$), and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold}.  Obtain $\beta$ and an infinite $W$ satisfying
$(\ast)$.  Discard $0$ from $W$.

Fix $n\in W$ and $t\in2^n$.  There are infinitely many
$a\in C_\beta\cap A_t$ with $|s(a)|\le m=m_\beta(n)$.  For each such $a$, choose
two points of $[s(a)]$ by filling with zeroes up to coordinate $m$ and then
using respectively the constant-zero and constant-one tails.  Call them
$x_a^0,x_a^1$.  Then
\[
 S_m(x_a^0)=\mathbf0,\qquad S_m(x_a^1)=\mathbf1,
\]
so (3) gives
\[
 h_\beta(a,x_a^0)=q(t\concat\mathbf0),\qquad
 h_\beta(a,x_a^1)=q(t\concat\mathbf1).
\tag{6}
\]

Enumerate infinitely many such $a$ without repetition.  They converge to
$\beta$ because they lie in $C_\beta$.  In the graph resolution, continuity
of every $h_\eta$ at $\beta$ for $\eta\ne\beta$, together with (6), gives
\[
 a+x_a^0\longrightarrow \beta\oplus q(t\concat\mathbf0),\qquad
 a+x_a^1\longrightarrow \beta\oplus q(t\concat\mathbf1),
\]
where $\beta\oplus r$ denotes the point over $\beta$ with free
$\beta$-coordinate $r$.  From (5) and lower semicontinuity,
\[
 \delta\bigl(\beta\oplus q(t\concat\mathbf0),
              \beta\oplus q(t\concat\mathbf1)\bigr)\le4^{-n}.
\tag{7}
\]

Order the $2^n$ words $t\in2^n$ lexicographically.  The right endpoint of one
dyadic interval equals the left endpoint of the next:
$q(t\concat\mathbf1)=q(t^+\concat\mathbf0)$.  Chaining (7) over all words gives
\[
             \delta(\beta\oplus0,\beta\oplus1)
             \le 2^n4^{-n}=2^{-n}.
\]
This holds for infinitely many, hence unbounded, $n\in W$.  Therefore the two
distinct points $\beta\oplus0$ and $\beta\oplus1$ have $\delta$-distance zero,
a contradiction.  Thus $L_1$ is not RN, completing the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Verification notes and limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The almost-disjointness check is insensitive to the diamond: all points
added at stage $j$ lie above the cofinal lower bound $\lambda_\beta(j)$, and
each stage is finite.
\item In Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold}, one successful coordinate $j$ sets the
threshold for level $j$ and simultaneously adds a point from every $G_t$ on
all levels at most $j$.  Infinitely many successful coordinates are therefore
enough to make every cell on each successful level infinite.
\item The shift is fixed on a given $\beta$ and dyadic level.  This is essential
in the bounded-level branch of the Reznichenko argument.
\item No preservation theorem for RN compacta under forcing is used.  The
spaces are constructed directly inside Minami's $\Diamond(\mathfrak b)$ model.
\item The packet claims a relative-consistency answer, exactly matching the
source question.  It does not prove in ZFC that such a weight-$\aleph_1$
example exists.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Verify the uniform Borel coding leading to (1), then audit the same-$\beta$
Reznichenko case and the graph-resolution convergence used before (7).  These
are the only nonroutine interfaces between the new combinatorial lemma and the
published resolution argument.
