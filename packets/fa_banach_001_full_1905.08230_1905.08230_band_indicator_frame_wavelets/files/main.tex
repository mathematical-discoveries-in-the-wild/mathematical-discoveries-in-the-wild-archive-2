\section{The question and answer}

Bownik and Rzeszotnik ask the following question in Problem 2.7 of
\cite{BR}.  For $0<b<1$, define $\psi_b\in L^2(\R)$ by
\[
 \widehat\psi_b=\ind_{E_b},\qquad
 E_b=(-1,-b)\cup(b,1).
\]
For which $1/8<b\leq1/6$ is $\psi_b$ a frame wavelet?  The neighboring
parameter ranges were determined by Bownik and Weber \cite{BW}, who also
expressed heuristic confidence that the missing interval should be positive.

\begin{figure}[ht]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/problem_crop.png}
 \caption{Problem 2.7 in the source survey, PDF page 7.}
\end{figure}

We use
\[
 \widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\R}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}\,dx,
 \qquad
 \psi_{j,k}(x)=2^{j/2}\psi(2^jx-k).
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $1/8<b\leq1/6$ and every $f\in L^2(\R)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frame}
 \frac1{54}\|f\|_2^2
 \leq \sum_{j,k\in\Z}|\langle f,(\psi_b)_{j,k}\rangle|^2
 \leq 6\|f\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
Consequently every parameter in the interval asked about in Problem 2.7 gives
a frame wavelet.
\end{theorem}

\section{Exact periodization and the alias graph}

Put $F=\widehat f$.  At a fixed scale $P=2^j$, Parseval's identity for
Fourier series on $[0,P)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:periodization}
 \sum_{k\in\Z}|\langle f,(\psi_b)_{j,k}\rangle|^2
 =\int_0^P\left|
 \sum_{n\in\Z}F(x+nP)\ind_{PE_b}(x+nP)
 \right|^2dx.
\end{equation}
Since $PE_b\subset(-P,P)$, only $n=0,-1$ can occur.  Hence the right-hand
side is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threepieces}
 \int_0^{bP}|F(x-P)|^2dx
 +\int_{bP}^{(1-b)P}|F(x)+F(x-P)|^2dx
 +\int_{(1-b)P}^{P}|F(x)|^2dx.
\end{equation}

This formula has a useful graph interpretation.  Take two signed copies of
the positive half-line,
\[
 X=\{+,-\}\times(0,\infty),
 \qquad d\mu(\sigma,t)=dt,
\]
and define
\[
 g(+,t)=F(t),\qquad g(-,t)=-F(-t).
\]
For every dyadic power $P$, join $(+,t)$ to $(-,P-t)$ whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edgecondition}
 bP<t<(1-b)P.
\end{equation}
At either signed vertex of magnitude $t$, put one \emph{pin} whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pincondition}
 (1-b)P<t<P.
\end{equation}
Edges carry Lebesgue measure in $t$; reflection $t\mapsto P-t$ preserves that
measure.  Summing \eqref{eq:threepieces} over $j$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \sum_{j,k\in\Z}|\langle f,(\psi_b)_{j,k}\rangle|^2
 =\E(g):=int_{v\sim w}|g(v)-g(w)|^2,d\nu
 +\int_{v\text{ pinned}}|g(v)|^2,d\mu.
\end{equation}
The sign change on the negative copy is the reason that the middle term in
\eqref{eq:threepieces} becomes a difference across an edge.

\section{The graph has uniform measured expansion}

All statements below ignore countably many dyadic boundary points, which form
a $\mu$-null set.  Write a magnitude uniquely as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mantissa}
 t=2^m r,\qquad m\in\Z,\quad 1<r<2,
\end{equation}
and set
\[
 c=8b\in(1,4/3],\qquad a=2(1-b)\in[5/3,7/4).
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:degrees}
The total incidence of a vertex (graph edges plus pins) is as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{mantissa}&\text{graph degree}&\text{number of pins}\\ \hline
1<r<c&2&0\\
c<r<a&3&0\\
a<r<2&2&1.
\end{array}
\]
Moreover, no two vertices in the first row are adjacent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a vertex of magnitude $2^mr$, the only possible active powers are
$2^{m+1},2^{m+2},2^{m+3}$.  Indeed, the first two are always active, the
third is active exactly when $r>8b=c$, and all later powers are inactive
because $16b>2>r$.  The $2^{m+1}$ incidence is an edge for $r<a$ and a pin
for $r>a$; all other active incidences are edges.  This proves the table.

If $1<r<c$, its neighbors along the first two active powers have dyadic
mantissas
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neighbors}
 4-2r\qquad\text{and}\qquad 2-r/2.
\end{equation}
Since $c\leq4/3$, both quantities are strictly larger than $c$ when $r<c$.
Thus the two-edge, unpinned class is independent.
\end{proof}

Orient each graph edge from the endpoint of larger magnitude toward the
endpoint of smaller magnitude.  A vertex of magnitude $t$ has at most one
outgoing edge.  In fact, if $P=t+s$ and $s<t$, then the overlap condition at
$t$ implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oneout}
 \frac{t}{1-b}<P<2t.
\end{equation}
The ratio of the endpoints in this interval is $2(1-b)<2$, so it contains at
most one power of two.

Let $A\subset X$ be measurable with finite measure.  Write
\[
 \ell=\mu(A\cap L),\qquad h=\mu(A\cap H),
\]
where $L$ is the first row of Lemma~\ref{lem:degrees} and $H=X\setminus L$.
Let $B(A)$ be the measure of graph edges crossing from $A$ to $X\setminus A$,
plus the measure of pins based in $A$.  Finally, let $e(A)$ denote the measure
of graph edges having both endpoints in $A$.

Because the edge orientation has out-degree at most one and each reflection
preserves measure,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:internal}
 e(A)\leq\mu(A)=\ell+h.
\end{equation}
The incidence table and the handshake identity now give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:firstboundary}
 B(A)=2\ell+3h-2e(A)\geq h.
\end{equation}
Every $L$-vertex has two graph neighbors, both in $H$.  Counting these
incidences inside $A$, and charging the rest to crossing edges, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:secondboundary}
 2\ell\leq3h+B(A).
\end{equation}
If $\ell\leq2h$, then \eqref{eq:firstboundary} implies
$B(A)\geq(\ell+h)/3$.  If $\ell>2h$, then
\eqref{eq:secondboundary} implies the same inequality.  We have proved the
measured isoperimetric estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cheeger}
 B(A)\geq\frac13\mu(A).
\end{equation}

\section{Cheeger inequality and proof of the theorem}

First take $g$ bounded with support of finite measure and put
$A_s=\{v:|g(v)|^2>s\}$.  Integrating \eqref{eq:cheeger} over $s>0$ and using
the layer-cake formula gives
\begin{align}
 \frac13\|g\|_2^2
 &\leq
 \int_{v\sim w}\big||g(v)|^2-|g(w)|^2\big|,d\nu
 +\int_{v\text{ pinned}}|g(v)|^2,d\mu.\label{eq:coarea}
\end{align}
By Cauchy--Schwarz and the elementary inequality
$\big||z|-|w|\big|\leq|z-w|$, the right-hand side is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cs}
 \E(g)^{1/2}
 \left(
 \int_{v\sim w}(|g(v)|+|g(w)|)^2d\nu
 +\int_{v\text{ pinned}}|g(v)|^2d\mu
 \right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The second factor squared is bounded by $6\|g\|_2^2$: each graph edge is
charged twice at each endpoint after using $(u+v)^2\leq2(u^2+v^2)$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:degrees} gives
\[
 2\deg_{\rm graph}(v)+\deg_{\rm pin}(v)\leq6.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \E(g)\geq\frac1{54}\|g\|_2^2.
\]
Truncation and density extend this estimate to every $g\in L^2(X)$.

Conversely, $|z-w|^2\leq2(|z|^2+|w|^2)$ and the same incidence table give
\[
 \E(g)\leq6\|g\|_2^2.
\]
Since \eqref{eq:energy} is exact and Plancherel gives
$\|g\|_{L^2(X)}=\|F\|_2=\|f\|_2$, these are precisely the two inequalities
in \eqref{eq:frame}.  This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Endpoint and sharp-threshold checks}

At $b=1/6$, the exceptional mantissa $r=4/3$ supports a degree-two
cancellation chain.  It is a countable null fiber, so it does not affect the
$L^2$ estimate.  On either side, Lemma~\ref{lem:degrees} forces the extra
branching used by \eqref{eq:cheeger}.

The mechanism breaks at exactly the known negative threshold.  If $b>1/6$,
then $c=8b>4/3$, and the two quantities in \eqref{eq:neighbors} can again lie
below $c$.  Adjacent unpinned degree-two vertices can then form arbitrarily
long cancellation corridors, in agreement with the construction in
\cite{BW}.  Thus the graph proof also explains why $1/6$ is the endpoint.

\section{Novelty status}

A bounded search of the run indexes, exact phrases from Problem 2.7, the
source survey, the original Bownik--Weber paper, and later web/arXiv records
located no published solution of the missing interval.  Priority is not
asserted.  The result should be checked by a wavelet/frame specialist,
especially the measured edge-counting formulation, before dissemination.
