\section{The two source conjectures}

Let $T$ be the free binary system on one generator, let $T_m$ be its terms
with $m$ leaves, and let $A$ be the union of the finite-dimensional probability
simplices supported on the $T_m$.  Moore's Conjecture~1.3 proposes a
nonassociative Hindman theorem for every coloring $c:T\to[0,1]$.  Conjecture~1.4
proposes that every compact convex subsystem of the finitely additive
probability measures on an arbitrary binary system contains an idempotent.
Both appear together on official PDF page~4:

\section{The later literature answer}

Moore's later paper arXiv:1807.05469 states explicitly that free binary systems
do not support idempotent means and that both source conjectures are refuted:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=.93\textwidth]{figures/later_refutation_statement_crop.png}
\end{center}

This is an exact ask/answer match, not merely an obstruction to one proposed
proof.  A free binary system is a binary system, so failure there is a
counterexample to the universal quantifier in Conjecture~1.4.  The same free
system on one generator is the object of Conjecture~1.3.

\section{Counterexample mechanism}

For completeness, here is the core of the later proof.  If $(S,*)$ is freely
generated, let $\#(s)$ be the number of generators in the unique term for $s$,
write $S_n=\{s:\#(s)=n\}$, and recursively define
\[
  T_0=S,\qquad T_{p+1}=(S\setminus Z)*T_p,
  \qquad s\in Z\Longleftrightarrow s=a*b\text{ with }b\in T_{\#(a)}.
\]
Suppose that $\mu$ were idempotent and put $r=\mu(Z)$.  Freeness and finite
additivity give $\mu(S_n)=0$ and $\mu(T_n)=(1-r)^n$ for every $n$.  If $r>0$,
choose $n$ with $(1-r)^n<r$; decomposing $Z$ by the left size forces
$r\le(1-r)^n$, a contradiction.  If $r=0$, every section
$\{t:s*t\in Z\}=T_{\#(s)}$ has $\mu$-measure one, so
$\mu*\mu(Z)=1\ne0=\mu(Z)$.  The later paper records the conclusion and then
turns to the second conjecture:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=.93\textwidth]{figures/later_two_refutations_crop.png}
\end{center}

For Conjecture~1.3, take the coloring of probability measures
$c(\nu)=\nu(Z)$ and any $0<\varepsilon<1/2$.  Given every proposed increasing
sequence of measures, the later proof recursively constructs admissible terms
whose $Z$-values are arbitrarily close to zero and arbitrarily close to one.
No single $r\in[0,1]$ can put both within $\varepsilon$ of $r$:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=.93\textwidth]{figures/later_hindman_conclusion_crop.png}
\end{center}

Thus Conjectures~1.3 and~1.4 both have full negative literature answers.

\section{Scope boundary}

The source's concluding Questions~7.1--7.4 are broader than those two yes/no
conjectures.  The counterexample does not by itself decide every fixed number
of summands, classify the binary systems for which an Ellis-type statement is
true, or decide preservation under products.  Nor does it settle the finite
copy statement of Question~7.4: the source identifies that statement with
amenability of Thompson's group $F$, and arXiv:1807.05469 explicitly says that
the amenability question remains open.  This packet therefore claims exactly
the two central refutations and no more.
