\paragraph{Status.}
Candidate full counterexample to Conjecture~1 of
Champagne--Johnston--MacDonald--Pipes \cite{CJMP}, subject to expert review.
All certificates below are exact integers.

\section{Source question}
Champagne, Johnston, MacDonald, and Pipes characterize absolute symmetric PPT
spectra using symmetric matricizations.  Their Conjecture~1 asks whether, for
an ordered spectrum $\lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{10}\geq0$ on
$\C^4\vee\C^4$, it suffices to check just the four displayed matrices on PDF
page~14 \cite{CJMP}.  They report that a few million random examples supported
the conjecture, while a proof remained elusive.  The later lane-source paper
of Xiong and Sze again describes the symmetric $m\geq4$ case as open
\cite[p.~2]{XS}.

\paragraph{Transcription.}
If $\rho\in\mathcal L(\C^4\vee\C^4)$ has eigenvalues
$\lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{10}\geq0$, the conjecture asserts that
$\rho$ is absolutely symmetric PPT if and only if the following four matrices
are positive semidefinite:
\begin{align*}
M_6&=\begin{bmatrix}
2\lambda_{10}&\lambda_3&\lambda_2&\lambda_1\\
\lambda_3&2\lambda_4&\lambda_5&\lambda_6\\
\lambda_2&\lambda_5&2\lambda_7&\lambda_8\\
\lambda_1&\lambda_6&\lambda_8&2\lambda_9
\end{bmatrix},&
M_{13}&=\begin{bmatrix}
2\lambda_{10}&\lambda_3&\lambda_2&\lambda_1\\
\lambda_3&2\lambda_4&\lambda_5&\lambda_7\\
\lambda_2&\lambda_5&2\lambda_6&\lambda_8\\
\lambda_1&\lambda_7&\lambda_8&2\lambda_9
\end{bmatrix},\\[2mm]
M_{11}&=\begin{bmatrix}
2\lambda_{10}&\lambda_6&\lambda_2&\lambda_1\\
\lambda_6&2\lambda_5&\lambda_4&\lambda_3\\
\lambda_2&\lambda_4&2\lambda_7&\lambda_8\\
\lambda_1&\lambda_3&\lambda_8&2\lambda_9
\end{bmatrix},&
M_{12}&=\begin{bmatrix}
2\lambda_{10}&\lambda_7&\lambda_2&\lambda_1\\
\lambda_7&2\lambda_5&\lambda_4&\lambda_3\\
\lambda_2&\lambda_4&2\lambda_6&\lambda_8\\
\lambda_1&\lambda_3&\lambda_8&2\lambda_9
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{align*}

\section{Definitions and source reduction}
For $x\in\mathbb R^{10}$, an upper-triangular matricization places the ten
coordinates of $x$, in any order, in the upper triangle of a $4\times4$
matrix $X$.  The corresponding \emph{symmetric matricization} is $X+X^T$;
thus diagonal entries are doubled and off-diagonal entries are not.
Theorem~1 of \cite{CJMP} states that a symmetric state is absolutely symmetric
PPT if and only if every symmetric matricization of its spectrum is positive
semidefinite.  Consequently, to disprove the reverse implication of the
conjecture it is enough to make its four matrices positive semidefinite while
one other symmetric matricization is indefinite.

\section{Proof intuition}
The four proposed tests differ only by interchanging
$\lambda_5,\lambda_6,\lambda_7$ in a few slots.  Making those three
eigenvalues equal collapses the four tests to two.  A second pair of repeated
eigenvalues, $\lambda_8=\lambda_9$, leaves enough freedom to keep those two
matrices barely positive definite while changing the placement of
$\lambda_8$ and $\lambda_9$ in another matricization produces a negative
determinant.  Strict inequalities on both sides make the failure robust, not
a boundary artifact.

\section{Exact counterexample}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:counterexample}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectrum}
(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{10})
=\frac1{8023}(1000,1000,1000,1000,703,703,703,660,660,594).
\end{equation}
Then $M_6,M_{13},M_{11},M_{12}$ are positive definite, but a symmetric
matricization of this spectrum is not positive semidefinite.  Hence any
symmetric quantum state with spectrum \eqref{eq:spectrum} is not absolutely
symmetric PPT, and Conjecture~1 of \cite{CJMP} is false.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The ten displayed integers are nonincreasing, positive, and sum to $8023$,
so \eqref{eq:spectrum} is an ordered density-matrix spectrum.  Multiplying
all matricizations by the positive number $8023$ does not affect definiteness.
The equalities among the weights give
\[
8023M_6=8023M_{13}=A:=
\begin{bmatrix}
1188&1000&1000&1000\\
1000&2000&703&703\\
1000&703&1406&660\\
1000&703&660&1320
\end{bmatrix}
\]
and
\[
8023M_{11}=8023M_{12}=B:=
\begin{bmatrix}
1188&703&1000&1000\\
703&1406&1000&1000\\
1000&1000&1406&660\\
1000&1000&660&1320
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Their leading principal minors, in orders $1,2,3,4$, are respectively
\begin{align*}
\Delta(A)&=(1188,\ 1{,}376{,}000,\ 753{,}535{,}708,\
293{,}705{,}189{,}448),\\
\Delta(B)&=(1188,\ 1{,}176{,}119,\ 465{,}623{,}314,\
137{,}338{,}080).
\end{align*}
Every entry in both lists is positive, so Sylvester's criterion makes $A$ and
$B$ positive definite.

Now place the spectrum entries in the upper triangle, read row by row, in the
order
\[
(10,9,3,1,7,4,2,5,6,8).
\]
(This is also ordering 4 in the source authors' list of 26 relevant orders.)
The resulting symmetric matricization, again scaled by $8023$, is
\[
C:=\begin{bmatrix}
1188&660&1000&1000\\
660&1406&1000&1000\\
1000&1000&1406&703\\
1000&1000&703&1320
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Direct exact elimination gives
\[
\det C=-337{,}300{,}392<0.
\]
A positive-semidefinite matrix has nonnegative determinant, so $C/8023$ is
not positive semidefinite.  Theorem~1 of \cite{CJMP} therefore shows that a
state with spectrum \eqref{eq:spectrum} is not absolutely symmetric PPT,
despite passing all four proposed tests.  Such a state exists, for example as
the diagonal operator with these eigenvalues in any orthonormal basis of
$\C^4\vee\C^4$.
\end{proof}

\section{Independent verification}
The included script \texttt{code/check\_counterexample.py} uses only exact
integer arithmetic.  It verifies the order and normalization of
\eqref{eq:spectrum}, constructs the four matrices directly from the source
formulas, confirms the two pairwise equalities and all eight leading minors,
constructs $C$ from its placement order, and confirms its negative
determinant.  It implements fraction-free Bareiss elimination rather than
floating-point eigensolvers.  Running it prints:
\begin{verbatim}
M6=M13 leading principal minors: (1188, 1376000, 753535708, 293705189448)
M11=M12 leading principal minors: (1188, 1176119, 465623314, 137338080)
ordering-4 determinant: -337300392
all exact checks passed
\end{verbatim}

The construction was discovered by constrained semidefinite numerical search,
then rationalized and rechecked independently in exact arithmetic.  Two failed
proof routes---coefficientwise domination of principal-minor polynomials and
direct Loewner domination by the four tests---were also checked before the
counterexample search succeeded.

\section{Scope, novelty, and review recommendation}
This packet refutes exactly the four-matrix sufficiency assertion.  It does
not identify a minimal replacement subfamily among the 26 relevant
matricizations, and it does not decide whether absolutely symmetric separable
and absolutely symmetric PPT states coincide in dimension four.

A bounded novelty search through 11 August 2026 covered the run's four cheap
indexes; the exact arXiv id and title; the phrases ``four matrices'',
``absolutely symmetric PPT'', ``Conjecture 1'', ``symmetric matricization'',
and ``counterexample''; the current arXiv record; and the 2022 journal
metadata.  It recovered the source conjecture and no later proof or
counterexample.  This is not a claim of priority.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
High priority.  Check the transcription of the four source matrices and the
upper-triangular placement defining $C$.  After that, the certificate consists
only of the displayed integer minors and determinant and is readily auditable.
