\section{The question and the answer}

For Hilbert-valued $X,Y$, the source uses the uncentred covariances
$C_{YX}=\E[Y\otimes X]$ and $C_{XX}=\E[X\otimes X]$. In its concluding
discussion it asks whether a nonlinear model $Y=f(X)+\varepsilon$ admits,
under reasonable regularity assumptions, a finite $\beta$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 C_{XY}C_{YX}\leq \beta C_{XX}^{,2}.
\end{equation}
This is equivalent to the relevant covariance range inclusion and to
existence of a bounded linear least-squares minimiser.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/source_question_crop.png}
\end{center}

The following shows that even very strong smoothness, boundedness, and tail
assumptions do not imply \eqref{eq:target}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There are a compactly supported random variable $X$ in $\ell_2$ with
injective covariance and a globally Lipschitz Fr\'echet-$\cC^\infty$ map
$f:\ell_2\to\R$, all of whose derivatives are globally bounded, such that,
for the bounded noiseless response $Y=f(X)$, no finite $\beta$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:target}. The same remains true after adding any independent,
centred, square-integrable scalar noise to $Y$.
\end{theorem}

Thus bounded (hence sub-Gaussian) data, moments of every order, smoothness of
every order, and exogenous noise do not by themselves force the desired
range inclusion.

\section{A smooth map with nonsquare-summable sampled slopes}

Let $(e_n)_{n\geq2}$ be the standard basis of $\ell_2$, and set
\[
 p_n=2^{1-n},\qquad a_n=\frac1{\log(n+1)},\qquad
 r_n=\frac1{\sqrt n},\qquad \rho_n=\frac{a_n}{4}.
\]
Then $\sum_{n\geq2}p_n=1$, $a_n\downarrow0$, and $(r_n)\notin\ell_2$.
Choose $\eta\in\cC_c^\infty(\R)$ equal to one near zero and supported in
$(-1,1)$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f}
 f(x)=\sum_{n\geq2}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \sigma a_nr_n\,
 \eta\!\left(\frac{\|x-\sigma a_ne_n\|^2}{\rho_n^2}\right).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth}
The supports in \eqref{eq:f} are pairwise disjoint, and $f$ is globally
Lipschitz and Fr\'echet-$\cC^\infty$ on $\ell_2$. For every $k\geq1$,
$D^kf$ is globally bounded and $D^kf(0)=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Opposite centres at index $n$ are distance $2a_n$ apart. If $n\ne m$, the
distance between any two centres is $(a_n^2+a_m^2)^{1/2}$, whereas the sum
of their support radii is
\[
 \frac{a_n+a_m}{4}\leq\frac{\sqrt2}{4}(a_n^2+a_m^2)^{1/2}.
\]
Thus the supports are disjoint. They are locally finite away from their only
accumulation point, zero.

On the pair of supports indexed by $n$, the chain rule gives, for constants
$M_k$ depending only on $k$ and $\eta$,
\[
 \|D^kf(x)\|\leq M_k a_nr_n\rho_n^{-k}
 \leq 4^kM_k r_na_n^{1-k}
 =4^kM_k\frac{(\log(n+1))^{k-1}}{\sqrt n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence every derivative on $\ell_2\setminus\{0\}$ extends continuously by
zero at the origin. Induction using the Banach-space fundamental theorem of
calculus makes these extensions the Fr\'echet derivatives of $f$ at zero.
For each fixed $k$ the displayed sequence is bounded, so $D^kf$ is globally
bounded. The case $k=1$ implies global Lipschitz continuity.
\end{proof}

In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sample-values}
 f(\sigma a_ne_n)=\sigma a_nr_n.
\end{equation}

\section{Covariances and the divergent test vectors}

Define the symmetric atomic design by
\[
 \mathbb P(X=\sigma a_ne_n)=\frac{p_n}{2}
 \qquad(n\geq2,\ \sigma\in\{-1,1\})
\]
and put $Y=f(X)$. Both random variables are centred and bounded. Writing
$\lambda_n=p_na_n^2>0$, direct calculation from
\eqref{eq:sample-values} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covariances}
 C_{XX}e_n=\lambda_ne_n,
 \qquad
 b:=\E[YX]=\sum_{n\geq2}\lambda_nr_ne_n.
\end{equation}
For scalar $Y$, $C_{YX}$ is represented by the vector $b$, and therefore
$C_{XY}C_{YX}=b\otimes b$. Notice that $C_{XX}$ is injective.
The support closure $\{0\}\cup\{\pm a_ne_n:n\geq2\}$ is compact because
$a_n\to0$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Suppose \eqref{eq:target} holds. For $N\geq2$, take the finite vector
\[
 x^{(N)}=\sum_{n=2}^N\frac{r_n}{\lambda_n}e_n.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:covariances},
\[
 \langle b,x^{(N)}\rangle=\sum_{n=2}^N r_n^2,
 \qquad
 \|C_{XX}x^{(N)}\|^2=\sum_{n=2}^N r_n^2.
\]
Evaluation of the positive-operator inequality on $x^{(N)}$ yields
\[
 \left(\sum_{n=2}^N r_n^2\right)^2
 \leq\beta\sum_{n=2}^N r_n^2,
 \quad\text{so}\quad
 \sum_{n=2}^N\frac1n\leq\beta.
\]
The harmonic partial sums diverge, a contradiction. Equivalently, the formal
preimage of $b$ under $C_{XX}$ is $(r_n)$, which is not in $\ell_2$.

If independent centred noise $\varepsilon$ is added, then
$\E[\varepsilon X]=0$, so $C_{YX}$ and the same contradiction are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\section{Audit, novelty, and scope}

The accompanying script checks probability normalisation, support separation,
the derivative scales through order eight, and the exact finite-dimensional
quadratic-form ratios. The proof itself is exact and does not rely on these
computations.

A bounded search on 13 August 2026 used the exact title, the displayed
inequality, and the phrases ``covariance bounds,'' ``range inclusion,'' and
``nonlinear infinite-dimensional regression.'' It found the source and nearby
operator-learning literature, but no later answer to this question or this
construction. The novelty verdict is therefore \emph{apparently new within
the bounded search}, not an exhaustive bibliographic claim.

Because the source intentionally leaves ``reasonable assumptions'' open,
the theorem does not exclude tailored sufficient hypotheses such as a
finite-rank design or an explicit source/range condition. It gives a sharp
negative result: arbitrary-order smoothness of $f$, bounded/sub-Gaussian
inputs and outputs, injective covariance, and independent noise are still
insufficient.
