\section{Source question}

The source is Bickel, Gorkin, Greenbaum, Ransford, Schwenninger, and Wegert,
\emph{Crouzeix's Conjecture and Related Problems}, arXiv:2006.04901
\cite{BGGRSW}.  If $\Theta$ is a finite Blaschke product with distinct zeros
$z_1,\dots,z_n\in\D$, the compressed shift $S_\Theta$ has an $n\times n$
matrix representation $M_\Theta$.  For a matrix $A$ with distinct eigenvalues,
the source defines
\[
 \eta(A)=\inf\{\|X\|_2\|X^{-1}\|_2:A=X\Lambda X^{-1},\ \Lambda
 \text{ diagonal}\}.
\]
Question 1.1 on source page 4 asks:
\begin{quote}
\emph{What is the minimal condition number of an eigenvector matrix $X$ of
$M_\Theta$ for a general finite Blaschke product $\Theta$ with distinct
zeros?}
\end{quote}

\section{Statement of the result}

For $z_1,\dots,z_n\in\D$, let $G=G(z_1,\dots,z_n)$ be the normalized
Szeg\H{o}-kernel Gram matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G}
 G_{ij}=\frac{\sqrt{(1-|z_i|^2)(1-|z_j|^2)}}
 {1-\overline{z_i}z_j}.
\end{equation}
It is positive definite when the points are distinct.  With the opposite
inner-product convention, the column Gram matrix is $\overline G$ instead;
complex conjugation preserves all eigenvalues and the Loewner inequalities
below because the comparison matrices are real diagonal.  For positive Hermitian
matrices $B,C$, write $B\preceq C$ for the Loewner order, and write
$\cond(B)=\lambda_{\max}(B)/\lambda_{\min}(B)$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact diagonal SDP]\label{thm:sdp}
Let $\Theta$ be a finite Blaschke product with distinct zeros
$z_1,\dots,z_n$.  Then
\begin{align}
 \eta(M_\Theta)^2
 &=\min_{D>0\ \mathrm{diagonal}}\cond(DGD) \label{eq:scale}\\
 &=\min\left\{t\geq1:\text{ there is a positive diagonal $P$ with }
 P\preceq G\preceq tP\right\}. \label{eq:sdp}
\end{align}
Both minima are attained.  Thus $\eta(M_\Theta)^2$ is the optimum of a
one-parameter quasiconvex semidefinite feasibility problem depending only on
the zero set, not on its ordering.
\end{theorem}

Let
\[
 \rho(z,w)=\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\overline zw}\right|
\]
denote pseudohyperbolic distance.

\begin{theorem}[Degree two and a pairwise lower bound]\label{thm:two}
If $n=2$, put $\rho=\rho(z_1,z_2)$ and
$c=\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two}
 \eta(M_\Theta)=\sqrt{\frac{1+c}{1-c}}=\frac{1+c}{\rho}.
\end{equation}
For arbitrary $n$, the sharp pairwise obstruction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-bound}
 \eta(M_\Theta)\geq
 \max_{i<j}\sqrt{\frac{1+c_{ij}}{1-c_{ij}}},
 \qquad c_{ij}=\sqrt{1-\rho(z_i,z_j)^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Call a zero set a \emph{regular pseudohyperbolic $n$-gon of radius $r$} if it
is the image under one disk automorphism of
\[
 \{r,r\omega,\dots,r\omega^{n-1}\},\qquad
 \omega=e^{2\pi i/n},\quad 0<r<1.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Regular-polygon formula]\label{thm:regular}
If the zeros of $\Theta$ form a regular pseudohyperbolic $n$-gon of radius
$r$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular}
 \boxed{\eta(M_\Theta)=r^{-(n-1)}.}
\end{equation}
Equal norms for the eigenvectors are optimal; no unequal column scaling can
improve the condition number.
\end{theorem}

The source observes that $\eta(A)\leq2$ is sufficient for Crouzeix's
conjecture for $A$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:regular} therefore makes that test exact
for this family:
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:threshold}
For a regular pseudohyperbolic $n$-gon, the condition-number test
$\eta(M_\Theta)\leq2$ holds exactly when
\[
 r\geq 2^{-1/(n-1)}.
\]
This is a statement about the sufficient test, not a failure assertion for
Crouzeix's conjecture below the threshold.
\end{corollary}

\section{Idea of the proof}

The source diagonalizes $M_\Theta$ using an explicit triangular matrix, but
that formula depends on an ordering of the zeros and obscures the minimization
over eigenvector lengths.  Passing to the adjoint removes both issues.  The
eigenvectors of $S_\Theta^*$ are precisely the normalized Szeg\H{o} kernels at
the zeros, so every choice of eigenvector matrix differs only by diagonal
column scaling.  Its squared singular values are the eigenvalues of $DGD$.
This gives the exact diagonal-scaling problem, and a congruence turns it into
the SDP in \eqref{eq:sdp}.

For a regular polygon, $G$ is circulant and its Fourier eigenvectors are flat:
every coordinate has modulus $n^{-1/2}$.  Pull the largest- and
smallest-eigenvalue Fourier vectors back by $D^{-1}$.  Their Rayleigh
quotients for $DGD$ have exactly the same denominator, regardless of $D$.
Their quotient therefore certifies that diagonal scaling cannot beat
$\cond(G)$.  A geometric-series computation of the Fourier eigenvalues then
gives $r^{-2(n-1)}$ for the squared condition number.

\section{Proofs}

\subsection{Kernel eigenvectors and the diagonal SDP}

Let
\[
 k_z(w)=\frac1{1-\overline zw},\qquad
 e_z=(1-|z|^2)^{1/2}k_z.
\]
If $\Theta(z_j)=0$, then $k_{z_j}\in K_\Theta=H^2\ominus\Theta H^2$ because
$\langle \Theta h,k_{z_j}\rangle=(\Theta h)(z_j)=0$.  Since $K_\Theta$ is
backward-shift invariant,
\[
 S_\Theta^*k_{z_j}=S^*k_{z_j}=\overline{z_j}k_{z_j}.
\]
The eigenvalues are distinct, so these $n$ kernel vectors form an eigenbasis
of the $n$-dimensional model space.

If $A=X\Lambda X^{-1}$, then
$A^*=X^{-*}\Lambda^*X^*$ and
$\cond(X^{-*})=\cond(X)$.  Applying the same observation to $A^*$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjoint}
 \eta(A)=\eta(A^*).
\end{equation}
Unitary changes of basis also preserve $\eta$.  It follows that every
eigenvector matrix relevant to $\eta(M_\Theta)$ can be represented by columns
$d_je_{z_j}$, where $d_j>0$: phases and permutations have no effect on the
singular values.  Its Gram matrix is $DGD$ or $D\overline GD$, according to
inner-product convention, with $D=\diag(d_1,\dots,d_n)$.  The two matrices
have the same eigenvalues, so we use $DGD$.  Since
\[
 \cond(X)^2=\cond(X^*X),
\]
this proves \eqref{eq:scale}.

Fix $t\geq1$.  The inequality $\cond(DGD)\leq t$ holds if and only if there is
an $s>0$ such that
\[
 sI\preceq DGD\preceq tsI.
\]
Congruencing by $D^{-1}$ gives
\[
 sD^{-2}\preceq G\preceq tsD^{-2}.
\]
Thus $P=sD^{-2}$ is feasible in \eqref{eq:sdp}.  Conversely, if
$P\preceq G\preceq tP$ for a positive diagonal $P$, then
$D=P^{-1/2}$ gives
\[
 I\preceq DGD\preceq tI.
\]
This proves equality of the two optima.

For completeness, the second optimum is attained.  It is feasible at
$t_0=\cond(G)$ by taking $P=\lambda_{\min}(G)I$.  If $t\leq t_0$ is feasible,
then comparison of diagonal entries in $P\preceq G\preceq tP$ and $G_{ii}=1$
gives
\[
 t_0^{-1}\leq p_i\leq1.
\]
The relevant feasible set is therefore compact, and the Loewner inequalities
are closed.  This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:sdp}.

\subsection{Automorphisms of the disk}

We record the invariance needed below.  It suffices to consider
\[
 \phi(z)=e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\overline az}.
\]
The identities
\begin{align*}
 1-|\phi(z)|^2&=\frac{(1-|a|^2)(1-|z|^2)}{|1-\overline az|^2},\\
 1-\overline{\phi(z_i)}\phi(z_j)
 &=\frac{(1-|a|^2)(1-\overline{z_i}z_j)}
 {(1-a\overline{z_i})(1-\overline az_j)}
\end{align*}
show that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mobius}
 G(\phi(z_1),\dots,\phi(z_n))=U^*G(z_1,\dots,z_n)U,
 \quad
 U_{jj}=\frac{1-\overline az_j}{|1-\overline az_j|}.
\end{equation}
The diagonal unitary $U$ commutes with every positive diagonal $D$, so
\eqref{eq:scale} is invariant under applying one disk automorphism to all
zeros.

\subsection{Two points and the pairwise obstruction}

For two points, a diagonal unitary changes $G$ to
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&c\\c&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 c=|G_{12}|.
\]
A direct calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-rho}
 c^2=\frac{(1-|z_1|^2)(1-|z_2|^2)}
 {|1-\overline{z_1}z_2|^2}=1-\rho(z_1,z_2)^2.
\end{equation}
Let $D=\diag(a,b)$.  Overall scalar multiplication does not change a condition
number, so normalize $ab=1$.  Then the determinant of $DGD$ is $1-c^2$, while
its trace is $a^2+b^2\geq2$, with equality exactly when $a=b=1$.  For a
positive $2\times2$ matrix with fixed determinant, the ratio of its two
eigenvalues is strictly increasing with its trace.  Equal scaling is therefore
optimal, and
\[
 \eta(M_\Theta)^2=\frac{1+c}{1-c}.
\]
Using $\rho^2=1-c^2$ gives both forms in \eqref{eq:two}.

For general $n$ and any positive diagonal $D$, the $(i,j)$ principal block of
$DGD$ is a scaled two-point Gram matrix.  Eigenvalue interlacing implies
\[
 \cond(DGD)\geq
 \cond\big((DGD)_{\{i,j\}\times\{i,j\}}\big)
 \geq\frac{1+c_{ij}}{1-c_{ij}}.
\]
Taking square roots, then the maximum over pairs and the infimum over $D$,
proves Theorem~\ref{thm:two}.

\subsection{Regular polygons}

By \eqref{eq:mobius}, it is enough to treat $z_j=r\omega^j$ for
$j=0,\dots,n-1$.  Put $q=r^2$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circulant}
 G_{ij}=\frac{1-q}{1-q\omega^{j-i}},
\end{equation}
so $G$ is circulant.  For $0\leq\ell<n$, let
\[
 f_\ell=n^{-1/2}(1,\omega^{-\ell},\dots,
 \omega^{-(n-1)\ell})^T.
\]
Expanding $(1-q\omega^k)^{-1}$ as a geometric series and using the
root-of-unity filter gives
\begin{align*}
 \lambda_\ell
 &=(1-q)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\omega^{-\ell k}}
 {1-q\omega^k}\\
 &=n(1-q)\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}q^{\ell+hn}
 =\frac{n(1-q)q^\ell}{1-q^n}.
\end{align*}
Thus $Gf_\ell=\lambda_\ell f_\ell$, with largest eigenvalue $\lambda_0$
and smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_{n-1}$.

It remains to prove that a diagonal scaling cannot reduce their ratio.  Fix a
positive diagonal $D=\diag(d_0,\dots,d_{n-1})$, put $H=DGD$, and set
$x_\ell=D^{-1}f_\ell$.  Because all Fourier coordinates have modulus
$n^{-1/2}$,
\[
 \|x_\ell\|^2=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}d_j^{-2}=:\alpha_D
 \quad\text{for every }\ell.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \frac{x_\ell^*Hx_\ell}{\|x_\ell\|^2}
 =\frac{f_\ell^*Gf_\ell}{\alpha_D}
 =\frac{\lambda_\ell}{\alpha_D}.
\]
The variational characterization of extreme eigenvalues now yields
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(H)\geq\frac{\lambda_0}{\alpha_D},
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(H)\leq\frac{\lambda_{n-1}}{\alpha_D}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \cond(DGD)\geq\frac{\lambda_0}{\lambda_{n-1}}
 =q^{-(n-1)}=r^{-2(n-1)}.
\]
Equality holds when $D$ is scalar.  Taking square roots proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:regular}; Corollary~\ref{cor:threshold} is immediate.

\section{Verification and limitations}

\paragraph{Independent checks.}
The accompanying script constructs the kernel Gram matrices directly and
optimizes over logarithmic diagonal scales.  For $2\leq n\leq8$ and
$r\in\{0.2,0.5,0.8\}$, it reproduces $r^{-(n-1)}$ to numerical precision and
returns scalar optimal scaling.  It also checks the degree-two expression on
100 random pairs and verifies disk-automorphism invariance.  A generic
five-point example exhibits a strict improvement from unequal scaling,
confirming that the regular-polygon argument is genuinely special.  These
computations are sanity checks, not ingredients of the proof.

\paragraph{Scope.}
Equation \eqref{eq:sdp} is an exact general characterization and supports
certified numerical computation by bisection in $t$.  It does not simplify the
answer for an arbitrary nonsymmetric zero set to a scalar expression in the
pairwise pseudohyperbolic distances.  Random tests show that simply taking
$D=I$ is false as a general optimality rule.  Thus the broad closed-form part
of Question 1.1 remains open outside the solved families.

\paragraph{Bounded novelty check.}
On 12 August 2026, searches covered the run's registry, solution, attempt, and
proof-gap indexes; exact phrases involving ``minimal condition number'',
``finite Blaschke product'', ``compressed shift'', and $M_\Theta$; the
OpenAlex-indexed citation descendants of \cite{BGGRSW}; and the most relevant
later primary sources found in that search, including arXiv:2306.12183
\cite{OV}, arXiv:2312.04537 \cite{BCGGV}, and arXiv:2506.15444 \cite{Renard}.
No exact diagonal-SDP reduction or regular-polygon formula was located.  This
is a bounded search, not a proof of novelty; novelty confidence is medium.

\paragraph{Human-review focus.}
The most useful checks are: (i) the passage from $M_\Theta$ to the kernel
eigenvectors of $S_\Theta^*$ and equality \eqref{eq:adjoint}; (ii) the
congruence equivalence in Theorem~\ref{thm:sdp}; (iii) the diagonal-unitary
factor in \eqref{eq:mobius}; and (iv) the common-denominator Rayleigh quotient
that proves optimality for the regular polygon.
