\large A qualitative operator-norm answer to Remark 4.3}

\begin{abstract}
Ofek--Pandey--Shalit ask whether their comparison of finite Drury--Arveson
quotients and multiplier algebras remains valid with ordinary operator norm
in place of completely bounded norm.  We answer the qualitative form
positively.  For a fixed finite set $X$ and arbitrary equal-cardinality sets
$Y_k$, ordinary algebra distortion tending to one is equivalent to
automorphism-invariant geometric convergence, RKHS Banach--Mazur convergence,
and cb multiplier convergence.  Consequently an ordinary-norm local modulus
exists.  The proof uses scalar multiplier extremals to prevent degeneration,
compactness of normalized finite Gram matrices, continuity of multiplier
norms, exact isometric rigidity, and Procrustes alignment.  No explicit
power-type modulus is obtained, so the source's detailed numerical estimates
are not all replaced.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source question}

The source is D.~Ofek, S.~K.~Pandey, and O.~M.~Shalit,
\emph{Distance between reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and geometry of
finite sets in the unit ball}, J. Math. Anal. Appl. 500 (2021), 125140;
arXiv:2011.06578v3 \cite{OPS}.  Its Remark~4.3 asks:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_question.png}
\end{center}

For a finite set $X\subset\B_d$, write
$\Hh_X=H_d^2|_X$ and $\M_X=\operatorname{Mult}(\Hh_X)$.  Define the ordinary
multiplier distortion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dop}
 \dop(\M_X,\M_Y)=
 \inf_\varphi\|\varphi\|\,\|\varphi^{-1}\|,
\end{equation}
where $\varphi:\M_X\to\M_Y$ ranges over algebra isomorphisms.  For finite
sets, every isomorphism is composition with a bijection \cite{OPS}.

\section{Proof intuition}

Ordinary norm does not directly control the row amplification used in the
source's cb proof.  Instead, scalar multiplier extremals already recover each
pseudohyperbolic distance.  An ordinary near-isometry therefore prevents the
moving point set from colliding or escaping to the boundary after one point
is normalized to zero.  Its finite Gram matrices are compact.  At any Gram
limit, continuity of multiplier norms turns ``nearly isometric'' into an
exact isometric algebra isomorphism; exact rigidity then makes the limit
congruent to the fixed set.  Thus every Gram limit is the correct one, and a
finite-dimensional Procrustes argument aligns the configurations.

\section{Qualitative operator-norm theorem}

Let $\widetilde\rho_s$ denote the automorphism-invariant symmetric distance
induced by $\ph$, and let $\delta_{RK}$ and $\delta_M$ be the source's RKHS
and cb multiplier distortions.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Fix $n\ge2$ and a set $X=\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}$ of distinct points in the open
unit ball of a complex Hilbert space.  For any sequence
$Y_k=\{y_1^k,\ldots,y_n^k\}$ of $n$-point sets, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $\widetilde\rho_s(X,Y_k)\to0$;
 \item $\delta_{RK}(\Hh_X,\Hh_{Y_k})\to1$;
 \item $\delta_M(\M_X,\M_{Y_k})\to1$;
 \item $\dop(\M_X,\M_{Y_k})\to1$.
\end{enumerate}
The assertion remains valid for an infinite-dimensional ambient Hilbert
ball.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[local modulus]\label{cor:modulus}
For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is an $\eta>0$, depending on $X$ and
$\varepsilon$, such that
\[
 \dop(\M_X,\M_Y)<1+\eta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \widetilde\rho_s(X,Y)<\varepsilon,
 \quad\delta_{RK}(\Hh_X,\Hh_Y)<1+\varepsilon,
 \quad\delta_M(\M_X,\M_Y)<1+\varepsilon
\]
for every $n$-point set $Y$.  In particular, ordinary near-isometry upgrades
to complete near-isometry.  Also, $\dop(\M_X,\M_Y)=1$ if and only if $X$ and
$Y$ are congruent.
\end{corollary}

\section{Proof of the hard implication}

We use the scalar extremal identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extremal}
 \ph(u,v)=\sup\{|f(u)|:\|f\|_{\M_Z}\le1, f(v)=0\},\qquad u,v\in Z.
\end{equation}
Indeed, contractive multipliers on a finite Drury--Arveson restriction extend
contractively to the ball, giving ``$\le$'' by Schwarz--Pick; a scalar
component of the ball automorphism taking $v$ to zero attains equality.

Assume (4).  Choose isomorphisms $\varphi_k:\M_X\to\M_{Y_k}$ induced by
bijections and put $C_k=\|\varphi_k\|$, $D_k=\|\varphi_k^{-1}\|$, so that
$C_kD_k\to1$.  The maps are unital, hence $C_k,D_k\ge1$ and both tend to
one.  Label $Y_k$ according to the bijections.  Applying separate ball
automorphisms to the matched first points preserves all relevant norms and
distances, so assume $x_1=y_1^k=0$.

From \eqref{eq:extremal},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metriccompare}
 C_k^{-1}\ph(x_i,x_j)\le \ph(y_i^k,y_j^k)
 \le D_k\ph(x_i,x_j)\qquad(1\le i,j\le n).
\end{equation}
Thus all $y_i^k$ lie in a fixed smaller ball and remain uniformly separated.
Let
\[
 G_k=(\langle y_i^k,y_j^k\rangle)_{i,j=1}^n.
\]
Every subsequence has a further subsequence with $G_k\to G$.  The positive
semidefinite matrix $G$ is the Gram matrix of a configuration
$Z=\{z_1,\ldots,z_n\}$ in a Hilbert ball; \eqref{eq:metriccompare} makes its
points distinct and $z_1=0$.

The Drury--Arveson kernel matrices
\[
 K_k=\left(\frac1{1-\langle y_i^k,y_j^k\rangle}\right)_{i,j}
\]
converge to the positive definite kernel matrix $K_Z$.  If
$a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in\C^n$, then $\|a\|_{\M_Z}$ is the operator norm of
the diagonal map $k_{z_i}\mapsto\overline{a_i}k_{z_i}$ in the Hilbert norm
with Gram matrix $K_Z$.  Hence $K\mapsto\|a\|_{\M(K)}$ is continuous on
positive definite matrices.  On the other hand, the ordinary distortion
gives, under the value-vector identifications,
\[
 D_k^{-1}\|a\|_{\M_X}\le \|a\|_{\M_{Y_k}}
 \le C_k\|a\|_{\M_X}.
\]
Passing to the limit shows that the labeled identity
$\M_X\to\M_Z$ is an isometric algebra isomorphism.

The exact rigidity theorem for Drury--Arveson multiplier varieties says that
an isometric multiplier-algebra isomorphism is induced by a ball
automorphism, and the varieties are congruent \cite{DRS,OPS}.  Here the
implementing automorphism respects the labels and sends $z_1=0$ to $x_1=0$;
it is therefore unitary.  Consequently $G=G_X$.  Every convergent
subsequence of $(G_k)$ has the same limit, so $G_k\to G_X$.

Finally apply the Procrustes lemma from \cite{OPS} in the span of the two
$n$-tuples.  That span has dimension at most $2n$, even if the ambient ball
is infinite-dimensional.  There are ambient unitaries $U_k$ with
\[
 \max_i\|x_i-U_ky_i^k\|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Euclidean and pseudohyperbolic metrics are uniformly equivalent on the fixed
smaller ball, so $\widetilde\rho_s(X,Y_k)\to0$.  This proves (4)$\Rightarrow$(1).

The implications (1)$\Rightarrow$(2)$\Rightarrow$(3) are the quantitative
results of \cite{OPS}, while (3)$\Rightarrow$(4) follows from
$\|\varphi\|\le\|\varphi\|_{cb}$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} follows.
Corollary~\ref{cor:modulus} follows by contradiction: failure of any asserted
modulus would give a sequence violating the theorem.

\section{Scope and verification}

This resolves the qualitative content of the source's main finite-set
equivalence with ordinary norms and supplies a local $\eta$--$\varepsilon$
modulus.  It does not produce the explicit power bounds in the source's
Proposition~4.7: ordinary norm does not directly control the vector-valued
row multiplier used there.

The proof was audited at the scalar-extremal, nondegeneracy, kernel-norm
continuity, labeled rigidity, and ambient-dimension steps.  Exact-question,
title/citation, ordinary-norm, multiplier Banach--Mazur, and later deformation
searches on 13 August 2026 found later work using the cb formulation but no
operator-norm answer.  Novelty is plausible, not certified.  Human review
should focus on the labeled use of exact rigidity and on whether the source
intended an explicit quantitative replacement rather than its qualitative
main theorem.
