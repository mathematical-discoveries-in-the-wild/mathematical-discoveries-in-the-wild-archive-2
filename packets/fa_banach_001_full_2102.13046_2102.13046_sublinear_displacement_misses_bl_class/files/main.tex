\section{Source question}

Dymond and Kaluža introduce $\phi$-displacement equivalence of separated
nets in $\R^d$ \cite{DymondKaluza}.  If $h:X\to Y$ is a bijection, write
\[
 \disp_R(h)=\sup\{|h(x)-x|:x\in X,\ |x|\le R\}.
\]
The nets are $\phi$-displacement equivalent if some bijection has
$\disp_R(h)=O(\phi(R))$.  After proving that the $\phi$-class of $\Z^d$
meets uncountably many bi-Lipschitz (BL) classes for every unbounded,
increasing, concave $\phi$, they ask:

\begin{quote}
Does every such $\phi$-displacement equivalence class intersect every BL
equivalence class?
\end{quote}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

The answer is negative for every sublinear $\phi$, and positive exactly at
linear scale.

\section{Idea of the proof}

Burago and Kleiner constructed a bounded positive density $\rho$ on a cube
that is not the Jacobian density of any bi-Lipschitz map
\cite{BuragoKleiner}.  We build a separated net $X$ whose normalized counting
measures on rapidly growing cubes converge to $\rho\,dx$.

Suppose a BL image $Y$ of $X$ could be matched to $\Z^d$ with sublinear
displacement.  Blow up the BL map on those cubes.  Compactness produces a
bi-Lipschitz limit $F$.  The sublinear matching disappears under rescaling,
so the images of the sampled points become the full lattice inside $F(Q)$,
up to a vanishing boundary layer.  Hence
\[
 F_\#(\rho\,dx)=dx|_{F(Q)},
\]
contradicting the defining property of $\rho$.  Importantly, the net $X$ is
independent of $\phi$, so one BL class is missed simultaneously by all
sublinear lattice-displacement classes.

\section{A net with a prescribed blow-up density}

A \emph{separated net} (or Delone set) is a subset of $\R^d$ with positive
minimum separation and finite covering radius.

Fix $d\ge2$ and $Q=[0,1]^d$.  By the Burago--Kleiner obstruction, in the form
used in the proof of \cite[Theorem 6.1]{DymondKaluza}, there is a measurable
$\rho:Q\to(0,\infty)$ with $\int_Q\rho\,dx=1$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho-bounds}
 0<m\le\rho\le M<\infty
\end{equation}
such that no bi-Lipschitz $F:Q\to\R^d$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forbidden}
 F_\#(\rho\,dx)=dx|_{F(Q)}.
\end{equation}
The normalization is harmless: multiplying a density by a positive constant
is compensated by a scalar dilation of the target, so realizability is
unchanged.

\begin{lemma}[Nested-shell sampling]\label{lem:sampling}
There are a separated net $X\subset\R^d$ and integers $R_n\to\infty$ such
that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:measure-conv}
 \mu_n:=\frac1{R_n^d}\sum_{x\in X\cap R_nQ}\delta_{x/R_n}
 \ ightharpoonup\ \rho\,dx|_Q.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $R_n$ so fast that $R_{n-1}/R_n\to0$.  The finite sampling lemma
\cite[Lemma 6.4]{DymondKaluza} gives, for each $n$, a uniformly separated and
uniformly relatively dense set $\Xi_n\subset R_nQ$ whose normalized counting
measure, after the dilation $x\mapsto x/R_n$, tends weakly to $\rho\,dx$.
The separation and covering constants depend only on the two bounds in
\eqref{eq:rho-bounds}.

On the shell $R_nQ\setminus R_{n-1}Q$, use $\Xi_n$.  Near every interface,
delete points in a fixed-width strip and fill a slightly wider strip with a
fixed translated lattice.  The widths can be chosen once, from the uniform
separation and covering constants, so the union over all shells is a
separated net $X$.

At scale $R_n$, replacing $\Xi_n$ by $X$ changes the normalized measure only
inside the old cube $R_{n-1}Q$ and fixed-width interface strips.  Uniform
point-count bounds for Delone sets show that their masses are respectively
\[
 O\bigl((R_{n-1}/R_n)^d\bigr)\quad\text{and}\quad O(R_n^{-1}).
\]
Both tend to zero.  The weak limit is therefore unchanged, proving
\eqref{eq:measure-conv}.
\end{proof}

\section{The blow-up obstruction}

\begin{lemma}[Bi-Lipschitz blow-up and lattice filling]\label{lem:blowup}
Let $X$ and $R_n$ satisfy \eqref{eq:measure-conv}.  Suppose that a separated
net $Y$ admits an $L$-bi-Lipschitz bijection $f:X\to Y$ and that a bijection
$h:Y\to\Z^d$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sublinear}
 \disp_R(h)=o(R).
\end{equation}
Then, after passing to a subsequence, there is an $L$-bi-Lipschitz map
$F:Q\to\R^d$ satisfying \eqref{eq:forbidden}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x_0\in X$ and put $b=f(x_0)$.  On $R_n^{-1}X$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FnGn}
 F_n(x/R_n)=\frac{f(x)-b}{R_n},\qquad
 G_n(x/R_n)=\frac{h(f(x))-b}{R_n}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Compactness.}
On every fixed cube $P$, the domains $R_n^{-1}X\cap P$ become dense and the
$F_n$ are uniformly $L$-bi-Lipschitz.  Extend them to $P$ by Kirszbraun's
theorem.  Arzelà--Ascoli and a diagonal subsequence give local uniform
convergence to an $L$-Lipschitz map $F$ on $\R^d$ (a neighborhood of $Q$
would suffice).  To pass the lower bound to the limit, approximate any two
points of $P$ by points of $R_n^{-1}X$ and use the upper Lipschitz bound of
the extensions.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bilimit}
 L^{-1}|u-v|\le|F(u)-F(v)|\le L|u-v|,
\end{equation}
so $F$ is bi-Lipschitz.

For $x/R_n$ in a fixed $P$, bi-Lipschitzness of $f$ gives $|f(x)|=O(R_n)$.
Consequently \eqref{eq:sublinear} gives, uniformly on such points,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:close}
 |G_n(x/R_n)-F_n(x/R_n)|
 =\frac{|h(f(x))-f(x)|}{R_n}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Pushing \eqref{eq:measure-conv} forward and using local uniform convergence
therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-limit}
 \nu_n:=\frac1{R_n^d}\sum_{x\in X\cap R_nQ}
 \delta_{(h(f(x))-b)/R_n}
 \ \rightharpoonup\ F_\#(\rho\,dx|_Q).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Lattice filling.}
Every atom of $\nu_n$ lies in the shifted lattice
\[
 \cL_n:=R_n^{-1}(\Z^d-b),
\]
whose normalized counting measures converge to Lebesgue measure.  We show
that the atom set of $\nu_n$ differs from $\cL_n\cap F(Q)$ only near
$\partial F(Q)$.

First, \eqref{eq:close} and uniform convergence imply that images of points
in $R_nQ$ lie in an $o(1)$-neighborhood of $F(Q)$.

Conversely, suppose $z_n\in\cL_n$ stays in a compact subset of
$\operatorname{int}F(Q)$.  By bijectivity of $h\circ f$, its unscaled version
has the unique form $h(f(x_n))-b$.  Since that point has norm $O(R_n)$,
sublinearity forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reverse-close}
 |f(x_n)|=O(R_n),\qquad |h(f(x_n))-f(x_n)|=o(R_n).
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $|f(x_n)|/R_n\to\infty$, then
$|h(f(x_n))-f(x_n)|=o(|f(x_n)|)$ would imply
$|h(f(x_n))|\sim|f(x_n)|$, a contradiction.  The lower Lipschitz bound for
$f$ now gives $|x_n|=O(R_n)$.

After taking a subsequence, $x_n/R_n\to t$ in a fixed compact cube, and
\eqref{eq:reverse-close} gives
\[
 F(t)=\lim z_n\in\operatorname{int}F(Q).
\]
Because $F$ is a homeomorphism on a neighborhood of $Q$, invariance of domain
gives $F(\operatorname{int}Q)=\operatorname{int}F(Q)$.  Injectivity therefore
forces $t\in\operatorname{int}Q$, and hence $x_n\in R_nQ$ eventually.  By
compactness, this conclusion is uniform on every compact subset of
$\operatorname{int}F(Q)$.

It follows that the symmetric difference in question is contained in a
shrinking neighborhood of $\partial F(Q)$.  Since
$\partial F(Q)=F(\partial Q)$ and $F$ is Lipschitz, this boundary has
Lebesgue $d$-measure zero.  Counting unit cubes around points of the shifted
lattice shows that the normalized lattice mass of the shrinking boundary
layer tends to zero.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-limit}
 \nu_n\rightharpoonup dx|_{F(Q)}.
\end{equation}
Comparing \eqref{eq:first-limit} and \eqref{eq:second-limit} proves
\eqref{eq:forbidden}.
\end{proof}

\section{Full classification}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $d\ge2$ there is a separated net $X\subset\R^d$ such that no net
BL-equivalent to $X$ admits a sublinear-displacement bijection to $\Z^d$.
The same $X$ works for every sublinear displacement scale.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose the non-realizable $\rho$ and the net $X$ from Lemma
\ref{lem:sampling}.  If a BL-equivalent net $Y$ admitted a bijection
$h:Y\to\Z^d$ satisfying \eqref{eq:sublinear}, Lemma \ref{lem:blowup} would
produce a bi-Lipschitz solution of \eqref{eq:forbidden}, contrary to the
choice of $\rho$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:classification}
Let $d\ge2$ and let $\phi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ be increasing and
concave.  Every $\phi$-displacement class intersects every BL class if and
only if $\phi(R)\in\Omega(R)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $\phi=o(R)$, Theorem \ref{thm:main} says that the $\phi$-class of
$\Z^d$ misses the BL class of $X$.  If $\phi\in\Omega(R)$, source
Proposition 2.6 gives an $O(R)$-displacement bijection between any two
separated nets, so $\phi$-equivalence is indiscrete.  These cases exhaust
increasing concave $\phi$: their asymptotic slope exists.
\end{proof}

In dimension one every separated net is BL-equivalent to $\Z$, so the
$d\ge2$ hypothesis is essential.

\section{Verification, upgrades, and novelty}

Eight focused attempts are recorded in the accompanying attempt note.  The
main upgrades were from an ordinary density mismatch to a non-realizable
density, from scale-dependent far-away blocks to one global nested-shell net,
and from pointwise image containment to the two-sided boundary-filling
argument in Lemma \ref{lem:blowup}.  No computational assertion is used.

The published 2024 source still labels the question open.  A bounded search
on 11 August 2026 used the exact question, title, DOI, author names, and
displacement/BL-class terminology.  The later Dymond--Kaluža papers located
concern bi-Lipschitz extension, not displacement-class intersections.  No
later solution was found.  Novelty confidence is moderate pending a complete
citation audit.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Check Lemma \ref{lem:sampling}'s fixed-width shell gluing, the lower
bi-Lipschitz limit in \eqref{eq:bilimit}, and especially the reverse inclusion
in the lattice-filling paragraph.  If these pass, Corollary
\ref{cor:classification} fully answers the stated question.
