\title{The double-cone Sobolev interpolation question\\
was answered by Auscher--Badr}
\author{Literature-status note for arXiv:0705.2216}

\paragraph{Status.}
Literature already answered.  This note identifies an explicit later answer;
it does not claim a new theorem.

\paragraph{Source question.}
Let
\[
 X=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\mathbb R^n:
       x_1^2+\cdots+x_{n-1}^2\le x_n^2\},
 \qquad \Omega=\operatorname{int}X,
\]
the union of two closed cones meeting only at the vertex.  For finite $p$,
Badr defines $H_p^1(X)$ as the closure of $\operatorname{Lip}_0(X)$ for
\[
 \|f\|_{H_p^1(X)}=\|f\|_{L^p(\Omega)}+
                    \lVert |\nabla f|\rVert_{L^p(\Omega)}.
\]
The Appendix of \emph{Real interpolation of Sobolev spaces},
arXiv:0705.2216, asks on printed page~23 whether the $H_p^1$ interpolate for
all $1\le p\le\infty$.

\paragraph{Exact later answer.}
Pascal Auscher and Nadine Badr study the same double cone and the same spaces
in \emph{Sobolev spaces on multiple cones}, arXiv:0812.1146.  Their
Theorem~3.2 (printed page~5) states that, whenever
\[
 1\le p_0<p<p_1\le\infty,
 \qquad \frac1p=\frac{1-\theta}{p_0}+\frac{\theta}{p_1},
\]
one has, with equivalent norms,
\[
 (H_{p_0}^1(X),H_{p_1}^1(X))_{\theta,p}
 =
 \begin{cases}
 H_p^1(X),&p\ne n,\\
 \widehat H_n^1(X),&p=n.
 \end{cases}
\]
Here, if $f_r$ is the spherical radial mean of $f$ and
$f_a=f-f_r$ is its anti-radial part, then
\[
 \widehat H_n^1(X)
 =\left\{f\in H_n^1(X):\frac{f_a}{|x|}\in L^n(X)\right\},
 \qquad
 \|f\|_{\widehat H_n^1}
 =\|f\|_{H_n^1}+\left\|\frac{f_a}{|x|}\right\|_{L^n}.
\]
The paper proves that $\widehat H_n^1(X)$ is a strict dense subspace of
$H_n^1(X)$.  For example, in dimension two, a compactly supported function
equal near the vertex to
$\operatorname{sign}(x_2)|\log |x||^{-\beta}$ lies in $H_2^1(X)$ for every
$\beta>0$, but lies in $\widehat H_2^1(X)$ only when $\beta>1/2$.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The answer to the source question is \emph{no}: the family
$(H_p^1(X))_{1\le p\le\infty}$ fails to be a real interpolation scale at the
single critical exponent $p=n$.  The later paper also gives the exact
replacement space at that exponent.  Its introduction explicitly identifies
the previously unsettled multiple-cone question as its motivation.
