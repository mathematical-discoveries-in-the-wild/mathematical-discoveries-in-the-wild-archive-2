\section{Source question}

The source is Ingrid Belti\c{t}\u{a} and Daniel Belti\c{t}\u{a},
\emph{Faithful representations of infinite-dimensional nilpotent Lie
algebras}, arXiv:1108.5563 \cite{BB}.  On page 2 it recalls Wojty\'nski's
question:
\begin{quote}
Does every quasinilpotent Banach--Lie algebra admit a faithful continuous
representation on some Banach space?
\end{quote}
Here quasinilpotent means that $\spec(\ad x)=\{0\}$ for every $x\in\g$.
The source proves the affirmative answer when $\g$ is nilpotent.  The result
below covers a broad inverse-limit-like class without any finite bound on the
nilpotency classes of its quotients.

\section{Proof intuition}

There are two small ideas. First, the source represents an $N$-step
nilpotent Banach--Lie algebra on polynomials of degree at most $N$. Every
representing operator strictly lowers polynomial degree. Weighting the
degree-$m$ summand by a large factor $R^m$ therefore makes the whole
representation arbitrarily small in operator norm without changing its
faithfulness or Lie relations.

Second, take all nilpotent quotients that separate the original algebra,
make each quotient representation contractive, and place them on one
$\ell_2$-direct sum. The diagonal action is bounded because the coordinate
norms are uniformly bounded; it is faithful because some quotient detects
every nonzero element. The renorming step is what prevents the norms of the
nilpotent representations from diverging with their nilpotency classes.

\section{Definitions and statement}

Let $\K\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\}$. A representation of a Banach--Lie
algebra $\g$ is a bounded linear Lie homomorphism
$\rho:\g\to\B(E)$ for a Banach space $E$ over $\K$. We call $\g$
\emph{Banach-residually nilpotent} if there is a family of continuous Lie
homomorphisms $q_i:\g\to\h_i$ into nilpotent Banach--Lie algebras such that
$\bigcap_i\ker q_i=\{0\}$.

Define the closed lower-central series by
\[
 \Cen^1\g=\g,\qquad
 \Cen^{n+1}\g=\overline{[\g,\Cen^n\g]}.
\]
We say that $\g$ is \emph{topologically residually nilpotent} if
$\bigcap_{n\ge1}\Cen^n\g=\{0\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Residual nilpotence implies linearity]\label{thm:main}
Every Banach-residually nilpotent Banach--Lie algebra admits a faithful
bounded representation on a Banach space. More precisely, the representation
can be chosen contractive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:lower}
Every topologically residually nilpotent Banach--Lie algebra has a faithful
bounded representation. Consequently Wojty\'nski's question has an
affirmative answer for every quasinilpotent Banach--Lie algebra whose closed
lower-central series has zero intersection.
\end{corollary}

\section{Proof}

We first extract a quantitative form of the source's nilpotent construction.

\begin{lemma}[Arbitrarily small nilpotent representations]\label{lem:small}
Let $\h$ be a nilpotent Banach--Lie algebra. For every $\varepsilon>0$ there
are a Banach space $E$ and a faithful bounded representation
$\pi:\h\to\B(E)$ such that
\[
 \norm{\pi(x)}\le \varepsilon\norm{x}\qquad(x\in\h).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $\h$ has nilpotency class at most $N$. Write
\[
 \Pcal_N(\h,\K)=\bigoplus_{m=0}^N\Pcal^m(\h,\K)
\]
for the Banach space of continuous scalar polynomials of degree at most $N$,
decomposed into homogeneous degrees. The differentiated left-regular
construction in \cite[Lemmas 2.11--2.12 and Corollary 2.14]{BB} gives a
bounded Lie homomorphism
\[
 d\lambda:\h\longrightarrow\B(\Pcal_N(\h,\K))
\]
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lowering}
 d\lambda(x)\Pcal^m(\h,\K)
 \subseteq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{m-1}\Pcal^k(\h,\K).
\end{equation}
It is faithful already on the full polynomial space: for
$\xi\in\h^*\subset\Pcal_N$,
\[
 (d\lambda(x)\xi)(0)
 =\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\xi((-tx)*0)=-\xi(x),
\]
so $d\lambda(x)=0$ implies $x=0$ by Hahn--Banach.

Choose fixed Banach norms $\norm{\cdot}_m$ on the homogeneous summands. Since
there are only finitely many blocks in \eqref{eq:lowering}, there is a finite
constant $C_N$ such that, writing $P_k$ for homogeneous projection,
\[
 \sum_{0\le k<m\le N}
 \norm{P_kd\lambda(x)\phi_m}_k
 \le C_N\norm{x}\sum_{m=0}^N\norm{\phi_m}_m.
\]
For $R\ge1$, give the same vector space the equivalent norm
\[
 \norm{\phi}_R=\sum_{m=0}^N R^m\norm{\phi_m}_m.
\]
Every nonzero block drops degree by at least one, hence
\[
 \norm{d\lambda(x)\phi}_R
 \le \frac{C_N}{R}\norm{x}\norm{\phi}_R.
\]
Taking $R\ge C_N/\varepsilon$ proves the lemma. The underlying linear map
and Lie identities are unchanged by this equivalent renorming.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Let $(q_i:\g\to\h_i)_{i\in I}$ be a separating family. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:small}, choose a faithful representation
$\pi_i:\h_i\to\B(E_i)$ so small that
\[
 \norm{\pi_i}\,\norm{q_i}\le1
\]
(with no restriction needed when $q_i=0$). Let
\[
 E=\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}E_i\right)_{\ell_2}
\]
and define the diagonal operator
\[
 \rho(x)(v_i)_{i\in I}
   =\big(\pi_i(q_i x)v_i\big)_{i\in I}.
\]
Then
\[
 \norm{\rho(x)}=\sup_i\norm{\pi_i(q_i x)}\le\norm{x},
\]
so $\rho:\g\to\B(E)$ is bounded. It is a Lie homomorphism coordinatewise.
If $\rho(x)=0$, faithfulness of every $\pi_i$ gives $q_i(x)=0$ for all
$i$, and the separating property gives $x=0$. Thus $\rho$ is faithful.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:lower}]
For $n\ge1$, the quotient
\[
 q_n:\g\longrightarrow\g/\Cen^{n+1}\g
\]
is a continuous homomorphism into a nilpotent Banach--Lie algebra of class at
most $n$. The kernels intersect in $\bigcap_n\Cen^{n+1}\g=\{0\}$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} applies.
\end{proof}

\section{A strict nonnilpotent quasinilpotent example}

\begin{example}
Let $E=\ell_2(\mathbb N_0)$ with basis $(e_k)$ and define the weighted shift
\[
 De_k=2^{-k}e_{k+1}.
\]
Then
\[
 \norm{D^n}=2^{-n(n-1)/2},
 \qquad \norm{D^n}^{1/n}\longrightarrow0,
\]
so $D$ is quasinilpotent but not nilpotent. Form
\[
 \g_D=E\rtimes_D\K,\qquad
 [(x,a),(y,b)]=(aDy-bDx,0).
\]
Relative to $E\oplus\K$, every adjoint operator has block form
\[
 \ad_{(x,a)}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
   aD&-Dx\\
   0&0
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
and therefore has spectrum $\{0\}$. Thus $\g_D$ is quasinilpotent. It is
not nilpotent because $D^n\ne0$ for every $n$. On the other hand,
\[
 \Cen^{n+1}\g_D=\overline{D^nE}\oplus\{0\}
 =\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{e_n,e_{n+1},\ldots\}\oplus\{0\},
\]
whose intersection is zero. Corollary~\ref{cor:lower} therefore applies.
\end{example}

\section{Scope, verification, and novelty}

The result does not prove that every quasinilpotent Banach--Lie algebra is
topologically residually nilpotent. Individual quasinilpotence of each
$\ad x$ supplies no evident uniform joint-spectral-radius control on mixed
commutators, and the closed lower-central residual may be nonzero. Nor does
the diagonal construction separate an element lying in every nilpotent
quotient. Those are the precise remaining obstructions to upgrading this
packet to a full answer.

\paragraph{Verification notes.}
There is no computational dependency. Review should focus on three points:
(i) the source's degree-lowering inclusion \eqref{eq:lowering} on the full
polynomial space, (ii) the factor $R^{-1}$ in the weighted norm, and (iii) the
uniform boundedness of the diagonal $\ell_2$ action for an arbitrary index
set. The example's spectral assertion follows from the spectrum of a
triangular $2\times2$ operator matrix.

\paragraph{Novelty check.}
On 11 August 2026, the four run indexes were searched for the arXiv id,
``quasinilpotent Banach--Lie'', ``residually nilpotent Banach--Lie'', and
``faithful representation''. Bounded primary-source web searches used the
exact phrases ``residually nilpotent Banach--Lie algebra faithful
representation'', ``pro-nilpotent Banach Lie linear'', and the source and
Wojty\'nski titles. We also checked the 2026 arXiv version of Neeb's
infinite-dimensional Lie theory text, which records the nilpotent polynomial
representation theorem and Wojty\'nski's BCH theorem separately. No source
located the weighted-renorming plus diagonal-quotient theorem above. Because
the argument is short and may be folklore, novelty confidence is moderate
rather than high.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
Review as a substantial partial result. The proof is elementary once the
source's polynomial representation is imported, but it expands the known
positive class from a single nilpotency class to arbitrary separating towers
of nilpotent Banach quotients.
