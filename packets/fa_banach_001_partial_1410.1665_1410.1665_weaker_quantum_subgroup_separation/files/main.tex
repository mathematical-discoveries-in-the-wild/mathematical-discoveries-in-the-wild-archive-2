\section*{Source question and status}

Runde and Viselter \cite[Remark~6.8]{RV} ask whether their compact quantum
subgroup separation theorem remains true when a state
$\mu\in\Cu(\mathbb G)^*$ is assumed merely not to factor through
\[
 \Phi:\Cu(\mathbb G)\longrightarrow C^{\mathrm u}(\mathbb H),
\]
rather than assuming that its universal Fourier transform lies outside the
image of the dual subgroup morphism.  The exact question appears on printed
and PDF page~19:

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=.98\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

This packet gives a new affirmative answer in two broad regimes: when the
compact subgroup $\mathbb H$ is coamenable, with arbitrary locally compact
ambient $\mathbb G$, and when $\mathbb G$ itself is compact, with no
coamenability assumption.  The case in which $\mathbb G$ is noncompact and
$\mathbb H$ is noncoamenable remains open.

\section*{Definitions and proof intuition}

Write $\mathbb W_{\mathbb G}$ for the universal bicharacter,
$\widehat\Phi$ for the dual morphism, and
$\widehat\epsilon_{\mathbb H}$ for the dual counit.  The desired weaker
separation assertion says that if a state $\mu$ does not factor through
$\Phi$, then there is
$\widehat\omega\in\Cu(\widehat{\mathbb G})_+^*$ such that
\[
 \widehat\omega\circ\widehat\Phi=\widehat\epsilon_{\mathbb H},
 \qquad
 \mu\bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\widehat\omega)
              (\mathbb W_{\mathbb G})\bigr)\ne1.                 \tag{1}
\]

The key idea is to argue contrapositively.  If every admissible slice in
(1) has $\mu$-value one, the proof mechanism of \cite[Theorem~6.5]{RV}
forces $\mu$ to absorb the Haar idempotent of $\mathbb H$.  For a coamenable
subgroup, faithful Haar averaging immediately turns this into factorization.
For compact ambient $\mathbb G$, the polynomial coinvariant algebra replaces
faithful Haar averaging: Takeuchi correspondence says that its augmentation
ideal generates exactly the subgroup kernel.

\section*{The absorption lemma}

Let $h_{\mathbb H}^{\mathrm u}$ be the Haar state on the universal compact
quantum group algebra, put
\[
 \vartheta=h_{\mathbb H}^{\mathrm u}\circ\Phi,
 \qquad
 y=(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathbb W_{\mathbb G}),
\]
and let $p$ be the central minimal projection corresponding to the trivial
representation of $\mathbb H$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:absorb}
If equality holds in (1) with right-hand side (1) for every admissible
$\widehat\omega$, then
\[
 \mu*\vartheta=\vartheta=\vartheta*\mu.                         \tag{2}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Represent $M(\Cu(\widehat{\mathbb G}))$ faithfully and put
$P=\widehat\Phi(p)$.  For every unit vector $\xi\in P\mathcal K$, its
vector functional restricts through $\widehat\Phi$ to
$\widehat\epsilon_{\mathbb H}$.  The hypothesis gives
$\langle y\xi,\xi\rangle=1$.  Since $\|y\|\le1$, equality in
Cauchy--Schwarz gives $y\xi=\xi=y^*\xi$.  Hence
\[
 yP=P=Py.
\]
The standard subgroup-bicharacter identity gives
$P=(\vartheta\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathbb W_{\mathbb G})$.
Multiplication of universal Fourier transforms corresponds to convolution,
and that transform is injective.  The two displayed equalities are therefore
exactly (2).
\end{proof}

\section*{Main partial theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $\mathbb H$ be a compact quantum subgroup of a locally compact quantum
group $\mathbb G$.  The weaker separation assertion (1) holds, without the
additional structural hypothesis (6.2) of \cite{RV}, in either of the
following cases:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathbb H$ is coamenable;
\item $\mathbb G$ is compact.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently Remark~6.8 has an affirmative answer for every finite quantum
group pair and for every classical compact subgroup.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose, toward the contrapositive, that no $\widehat\omega$ separates
$\mu$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:absorb}, (2) holds.  Define the right translation
coaction
\[
 \delta=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Phi)\Delta_{\mathbb G}^{\mathrm u}.
\]

Assume first that $\mathbb H$ is coamenable.  If $a\in\ker\Phi$, then
\[
 z=(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta(a^*a)\in C^{\mathrm u}(\mathbb H)_+
\]
satisfies
\[
 h_{\mathbb H}^{\mathrm u}(z)
  =(\mu*\vartheta)(a^*a)
  =\vartheta(a^*a)
  =h_{\mathbb H}^{\mathrm u}(\Phi(a^*a))=0.                    \tag{3}
\]
Coamenability identifies the universal and reduced compact quantum group
algebras, so $h_{\mathbb H}^{\mathrm u}$ is faithful.  Thus $z=0$.
Applying the counit and using
$(\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon_{\mathbb H})\delta=\mathrm{id}$ gives
$\mu(a^*a)=0$.  Hence $\mu$ vanishes on $\ker\Phi$ and factors through
$\Phi$.

Now assume that $\mathbb G$ is compact.  Put
\[
 \mathcal P=\Pol(\mathbb G),\quad
 \mathcal K=\Pol(\mathbb H),\quad
 \pi=\Phi|_{\mathcal P},\quad
 \mathcal R=\{r\in\mathcal P:(\mathrm{id}\otimes\pi)\Delta(r)=r\otimes1\}.
\]
For $r\in\mathcal R$, Haar averaging fixes $r$, while
$\pi(r)$ is a right-invariant element of $\mathcal K$ and hence equals
$\epsilon(r)1$.  Therefore (2) implies
\[
 \mu(r)=(\mu*\vartheta)(r)=\vartheta(r)=\epsilon(r).            \tag{4}
\]

Let $\mathcal R^+=\mathcal R\cap\ker\epsilon$.  The Hopf
$*$-algebra $\mathcal K$ is cosemisimple.  Thus $\mathcal P$ is faithfully
coflat as a right $\mathcal K$-comodule: coflatness follows from
cosemisimplicity, and faithfulness follows because the surjection $\pi$
splits as a comodule map.  Takeuchi correspondence \cite{Takeuchi} now gives
the exact fiber identity
\[
 \ker\pi=\mathcal R^+\mathcal P.                               \tag{5}
\]
For $r\in\mathcal R^+$ and $x\in\mathcal P$, (4) and positivity yield
\[
 \mu(rr^*)=\epsilon(rr^*)=0,qquad
 \mu(rx)=\langle\pi_\mu(x)\xi_\mu,
                    \pi_\mu(r^*)\xi_\mu\rangle=0.             \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) show that $\mu$ vanishes on the polynomial kernel.
Peter--Weyl spectral truncations preserve $\ker\Phi$ and show that
$\ker\pi$ is norm dense in $\ker\Phi$.  Hence $\mu$ again factors through
$\Phi$.

In both cases, failure of (1) forces factorization.  The contrapositive proves
the theorem.
\end{proof}

\section*{Verification, scope, and novelty check}

The proof reuses only the vector-state step before the structural hypothesis
enters the proof of \cite[Theorem~6.5]{RV}; the new steps are the absorption
reformulation, the faithful-Haar argument, and the compact Hopf-fiber
argument.  The compact proof also rules out Kac--Paljutkin, Sekine, and all
other finite quantum groups as counterexamples.

A bounded current search covered the exact Remark~6.8 wording, the source
title and authors, compact-quantum-subgroup separation, idempotent states,
and finite quantum groups.  Krajczok--Skalski \cite{KS} is a later paper on
positive-definite separation and cites \cite{RV}, but studies approximation
and von Neumann algebra separation properties rather than the subgroup
factorization question.  No paper explicitly proving or disproving the two
statements above was found.  Novelty confidence is moderate because the
operator-algebraic target is specialized; mathematical confidence is high.

The unresolved regime is precisely: noncompact $\mathbb G$ and noncoamenable
compact $\mathbb H$.  In (3), Haar-nullity then controls only the reduced
image of $z$, whereas the desired value is
$\epsilon_{\mathbb H}(z)=\mu(a^*a)$ at the universal level.  A general
nonunital universal Hopf--Galois fiber theorem could close this gap, but it is
not assumed here.  Human review should focus on the Takeuchi identity (5) and
the polynomial-kernel density statement.
