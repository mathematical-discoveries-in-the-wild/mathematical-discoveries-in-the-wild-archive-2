\section{Source question and answer}

Yemon Choi's paper \cite{Choi2023}, arXiv:2012.14413, asks in Question~6.2
which nonabelian locally compact groups attain the minimum possible value of
the Fourier-algebra amenability constant.  Using his lower bound and his
classification of equality for the antidiagonal constant, the paper rewrites
the question as follows (physical PDF page~25):
\begin{quote}
Does $[G:Z(G)]=4$ imply $\AM(\A(G))=3/2$?
\end{quote}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/open_problem_crop.png}
\end{center}

Choi and Ghandehari subsequently proved this for discrete groups
\cite[Theorem 1.4(iii)]{ChoiGhandehari2026}, but explicitly left the general
locally compact extension open.  The theorem below proves that extension and,
together with Choi's necessary condition, answers Question~6.2 completely.

\section{Notation and proof intuition}

Write $\A(G)$ for Eymard's Fourier algebra and $\AM(\A(G))$ for its Banach
amenability constant.  The symbol $\widehat\otimes_\gamma$ denotes the Banach
projective tensor product, while $\widehat\otimes_\op$ denotes the operator
projective tensor product.  The canonical map identifies
$\A(G)\widehat\otimes_\op\A(G)$ completely isometrically with
$\A(G\times G)$.  Multiplication is diagonal restriction,
\[
 \Delta u(s)=u(s,s),
\]
and the bimodule actions are
$(a\cdot u)(s,t)=a(s)u(s,t)$ and
$(u\cdot a)(s,t)=u(s,t)a(t)$.

\subsection*{Proof intuition}

When $[G:Z(G)]=4$, the derived subgroup is $\{e,c\}$ for a central involution
$c$.  Translation by $c$ splits the Fourier algebra into two $\ell^1$-summands:
the even summand comes from the one-dimensional representations, and the odd
summand from the two-dimensional representations.

Start with a contractive \emph{operator} approximate diagonal and average it
under simultaneous translation by $c$ in both variables.  Only two tensor
blocks survive: even--even and odd--odd.  The diagonal equations force each
block to have asymptotic norm exactly $1/2$.  Passing from the operator tensor
product to the ordinary Banach projective tensor product costs $1$ on the
even--even block (it is the tensor square of an abelian quotient) and at most
$2$ on the odd--odd block (Runde's degree bound).  The resulting ordinary
approximate diagonal therefore has asymptotic norm
\[
 \frac12+2\frac12=\frac32.
\]
The main technical point is proving the exact half--half split; four matrix
coefficients of one two-dimensional irreducible provide the needed identity.

\section{The central spectral decomposition}

\begin{lemma}[Structure and spectral blocks]\label{lem:blocks}
Let $G$ be a locally compact nonabelian group with $[G:Z(G)]=4$.  There is a
central element $c\ne e$ such that
\[
 c^2=e,\qquad [G,G]=\{e,c\},
\]
and every continuous irreducible unitary representation of $G$ has degree one
or two.  If $\tau:\A(G)\to\A(G)$ is given by
$\tau a(s)=a(cs)$ and
\[
 \A_+=\ker(\tau-I),\qquad \A_-=\ker(\tau+I),
\]
then
\[
 \A(G)=\A_+\oplus_1\A_-.
\]
The plus block consists precisely of the one-dimensional representation
fields and is isometric to $\A(G/\langle c\rangle)$; the minus block consists
precisely of the two-dimensional fields.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The quotient $G/Z(G)$ is the Klein four group.  Commutators depend only on
cosets modulo the center, so there is a unique possible nonidentity
commutator $c$.  Comparing $[x,y]$ and $[y,x]$ gives $c=c^{-1}$, and hence
$[G,G]=\{e,c\}$.  The degree bound is the standard center-index-four
representation lemma; equivalently, one restricts to an abelian subgroup of
index two.  These facts, without countability assumptions, are also proved in
\cite[Theorem 4.6]{Choi2023}.

Since $c$ is central, $p_\pm=(1\pm\lambda(c))/2$ are central projections in
$\VN(G)$.  The predual decomposition of the two von Neumann summands gives
the asserted $\ell^1$-sum and identifies its projections with
$(I\pm\tau)/2$.  Every character kills $[G,G]$.  Conversely, an irreducible
representation on which $c$ acts as $I$ factors through the abelian group
$G/\langle c\rangle$ and is therefore one-dimensional.  Schur's lemma and
the degree bound then put every remaining irreducible in the two-dimensional
minus block.  Quotient pullback gives the isometric identification of the
plus block with $\A(G/\langle c\rangle)$.
\end{proof}

The same central projections in each coordinate give an additive four-block
decomposition of $\A(G\times G)$.  We write $u^{\varepsilon\delta}$ for the
block on which translation by $c$ in the first and second variables has signs
$\varepsilon,\delta\in\{+,-\}$.

\section{The half--half lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Equal mass in the two surviving blocks]\label{lem:half}
There is a contractive operator approximate diagonal $(v_\alpha)$ for
$\A(G)$ such that
\[
 v_\alpha=v_\alpha^{++}+v_\alpha^{--},\qquad
 \norm{v_\alpha^{++}}\longrightarrow\frac12,
 \quad
 \norm{v_\alpha^{--}}\longrightarrow\frac12.
\]
Here the norms are the $\A(G\times G)$, equivalently operator-projective,
norms.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The group is virtually abelian and hence amenable.  Ruan's operator
amenability theorem supplies a contractive operator approximate diagonal
$(u_\alpha)$; see also the proof of \cite[Theorem 2.7]{Runde2006}.  Let
$S=\tau\otimes I$, $U=I\otimes\tau$, and $T=SU$.  Put
\[
 v_\alpha=\frac12(u_\alpha+Tu_\alpha).
\]
The map $T$ is an isometry and $T^2=I$.  Moreover,
\[
 a\cdot Tu_\alpha-Tu_\alpha\cdot a
 =T\bigl((\tau a)\cdot u_\alpha-u_\alpha\cdot(\tau a)\bigr),
\]
while $\Delta(Tu_\alpha)=\tau(\Delta u_\alpha)$.  Since translating a bounded
approximate identity by the involutive algebra automorphism $\tau$ gives
another bounded approximate identity, $(v_\alpha)$ is still a contractive
operator approximate diagonal.  Its $T$-invariance kills the mixed blocks.

Set
\[
 e_\alpha=\Delta v_\alpha,
 \qquad q_\alpha=\Delta(Sv_\alpha).
\]
We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qzero}
 bq_\alpha\longrightarrow0\quad\hbox{in }\A(G)
 \qquad(b\in\A(G)).
\end{equation}
Indeed, translate the first variable in the module-commutator defect and then
restrict to the diagonal.  Both operations are contractive, and the result is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect}
 (\tau a-a)q_\alpha\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(a\in\A(G)).
\end{equation}
If $b\in\A_-$, taking $a=b$ proves \eqref{eq:qzero}.

Now let $b\in\A_+$.  Continuous irreducible representations separate the
points of $G$, so there is an irreducible $\pi$ with $\pi(c)\ne I$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:blocks}, $\pi$ is two-dimensional and $\pi(c)=-I$.  In an
orthonormal basis write its matrix coefficients as $u_{ij}$, $1\le i,j\le2$.
Each $u_{ij}$ is an odd element of $\B(G)$ of norm at most one, and
$bu_{ij}\in\A_-$.  Apply \eqref{eq:defect} to $a=bu_{ij}$ to obtain
\[
 bu_{ij}q_\alpha\longrightarrow0.
\]
Multiplication by $\overline{u_{ij}}\in\B(G)$ is contractive on $\A(G)$, and
unitarity of $\pi(s)$ gives
\[
 \sum_{i,j=1}^2\overline{u_{ij}(s)}u_{ij}(s)=2.
\]
Summing the four convergences yields $2bq_\alpha\to0$.  Decomposing an
arbitrary $b$ into its plus and minus parts proves \eqref{eq:qzero}.

Since $S$ has signs $+1$ and $-1$ on the two surviving blocks,
\[
 \Delta v_\alpha^{++}=\frac12(e_\alpha+q_\alpha),
 \qquad
 \Delta v_\alpha^{--}=\frac12(e_\alpha-q_\alpha).
\]
Thus multiplication by either expression converges on every $b\in\A(G)$ to
$b/2$.  Fixing $b$ of norm one and using contractivity of $\Delta$ gives
\[
 \liminf_\alpha\norm{v_\alpha^{++}}\ge\frac12,
 \qquad
 \liminf_\alpha\norm{v_\alpha^{--}}\ge\frac12.
\]
The central von Neumann algebra decomposition makes the norms additive, while
$\norm{v_\alpha}\le1$.  Hence both displayed norms converge to $1/2$.
\end{proof}

\section{Full solution}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every locally compact nonabelian group $G$,
\[
 \AM(\A(G))=\frac32
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 [G:Z(G)]=4.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The forward implication is \cite[Theorems 1.6 and 1.7]{Choi2023}: the
antidiagonal constant satisfies
$3/2\le \mathrm{AD}(G_d)\le\AM(\A(G))$, and equality in the first inequality
is equivalent to center index four.

For the converse, assume $[G:Z(G)]=4$ and take $(v_\alpha)$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:half}.  Let
\[
 J:\A(G)\widehat\otimes_\gamma\A(G)\longrightarrow\A(G\times G)
\]
be the canonical map.  Since $G$ is virtually abelian, $J$ is bijective and
its inverse has norm at most the maximal irreducible degree, which here is
two \cite[Corollary 2.6 and Theorem 2.7]{Runde2006}.

The map $J$ respects the central block projections.  On the $++$ block,
$J^{-1}$ is isometric: by Lemma~\ref{lem:blocks}, this is exactly the
canonical Banach tensor map for the locally compact abelian group
$G/\langle c\rangle$.  On the $--$ block the global bound gives norm at most
two.  Therefore
\[
 \norm{J^{-1}v_\alpha}_\gamma
 \le \norm{v_\alpha^{++}}+2\norm{v_\alpha^{--}}
 \longrightarrow \frac32.
\]
Because $J^{-1}$ is an $\A(G)$-bimodule map and intertwines multiplication,
$(J^{-1}v_\alpha)$ is an ordinary Banach approximate diagonal.  Consequently
$\AM(\A(G))\le3/2$.  Choi's universal nonabelian lower bound gives equality.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The proof uses no separability hypothesis and no Plancherel measure.  This is
why it applies to nondiscrete groups even though the 2026 discrete proof does
not.  It settles Question~6.2, but it does not prove the broader conjecture
$\AM(\A(G))=\mathrm{AD}(G_d)$ for arbitrary locally compact groups, nor the
full locally compact version of the sharper upper bound from
\cite{ChoiGhandehari2026}.

The formal audit checked: preservation of the operator diagonal under
simultaneous translation; the $A(G)$-norm convergence in the
matrix-coefficient argument; additivity of the central predual blocks; and
the exact identification of the abelian $++$ block.  The bounded novelty
search through 13 August 2026 found only the discrete theorem in
arXiv:2507.05243, whose concluding section still presents the locally compact
extension as open.  Novelty is plausible, not certified.

\paragraph{Human-review recommendation.}
High priority.  Check first the passage from \eqref{eq:defect} to the even
case of \eqref{eq:qzero}, and then the isometric $++$ tensor identification.
These are the two nonstandard steps; the remaining ingredients are standard
Fourier-algebra and operator-amenability facts.
