\noindent\textbf{Status.}
Candidate counterexample, likely valid, pending human review.  An explicit
infinite-order constant-coefficient differential operator preserves every
strictly positive polynomial and has strictly positive-definite moment
matrices, but its inverse sends the hyperbolic polynomial $x^6$ to a
polynomial with nonreal zeros.  Thus Problem~2 has a negative answer.

\section{Source question}

Let $D=d/dx$.  The source calls a linear operator on $\R[x]$ a
\emph{positivity-preserver} if it maps every polynomial positive on all of
$\R$ to another such polynomial, and a \emph{hyperbolicity-preserver} if it
maps every real-rooted polynomial to a real-rooted polynomial.  Problem~2 on
source PDF page~3 asks whether every positivity-preserver of the form
\[
 U=\alpha_0+\alpha_1D+\alpha_2D^2+\cdots
\]
has a hyperbolicity-preserver as its inverse~\cite{BGS}.

\section{Proof intuition}

Every probability measure with all moments gives a constant-coefficient
positivity-preserver by averaging translations:
\[
 (U_\mu p)(x)=\int_\R p(x+t)\,d\mu(t).
\]
The two-point average has symbol $\cosh z$, so its formal inverse has symbol
$\operatorname{sech}z$.  Already
\[
 \operatorname{sech}(D)x^6=x^6-15x^4+75x^2-61
\]
is not real-rooted.  To rule out any objection that this seed measure has
finite support and degenerate high-order moment matrices, add one percent of
a Gaussian.  Strict positive definiteness is restored, while the inverse
image of $x^6$ retains a pair of nonreal conjugate roots.

\section{The counterexample}

Let $\gamma$ be standard Gaussian measure and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mu}
 \mu=\frac{99}{200}(\delta_{-1}+\delta_1)+\frac1{100}\gamma.
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:U}
 (Up)(x)=\int_\R p(x+t)\,d\mu(t)
 =\left(\frac{99}{100}\cosh D+\frac{1}{100}e^{D^2/2}\right)p(x).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The operator $U:\R[x]\to\R[x]$ in \eqref{eq:U} is an invertible,
infinite-order, constant-coefficient positivity-preserver.  Every finite
Hankel moment form associated with $U$ is positive definite.  Nevertheless
$U^{-1}$ is not a hyperbolicity-preserver.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The translation identity $e^{tD}p(x)=p(x+t)$ and the Gaussian moment formula
give \eqref{eq:U}.  Thus $U$ is a constant-coefficient formal differential
operator with infinitely many nonzero coefficients.  If $p(x)>0$ for every
$x\in\R$, then every integrand $p(x+t)$ is positive, and hence
$(Up)(x)>0$ for every $x$.  Therefore $U$ preserves positivity.

The moments $m_k=\int t^k\,d\mu(t)$ determine its Hankel forms.  For every
nonzero polynomial $q$,
\[
 \sum_{i,j}m_{i+j}q_iq_j
 =\int_\R q(t)^2\,d\mu(t)
 \ge \frac1{100}\int_\R q(t)^2\,d\gamma(t)>0.
\]
The final inequality holds because a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on a
set of full Gaussian measure.  Thus all finite moment matrices are strictly
positive definite.

The formal symbol of $U$ is
\[
 M(z)=\frac{99}{100}\cosh z+\frac1{100}e^{z^2/2}.
\]
Since $M(0)=1$, it has a unique reciprocal formal power series $N(z)$, and
$V=N(D)$ is the two-sided inverse of $U$ on $\R[x]$: all series truncate on
each input polynomial.  Through degree six,
\begin{align*}
 M(z)&=1+\frac12z^2+\frac{17}{400}z^4
          +\frac{19}{12000}z^6+O(z^8),\\
 N(z)&=1-\frac12z^2+\frac{83}{400}z^4
          -\frac{1009}{12000}z^6+O(z^8).
\end{align*}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
 V(x^6)=x^6-15x^4+\frac{747}{10}x^2-\frac{3027}{50}.
\end{equation}

The input $x^6$ is hyperbolic.  To inspect the output, put $y=x^2$ and
multiply the resulting cubic by $50$:
\[
 R(y)=50y^3-750y^2+3735y-3027.
\]
Its discriminant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disc}
 \operatorname{Disc}(R)=-668917845000<0.
\end{equation}
A real cubic with negative discriminant has one real zero and a nonreal
conjugate pair.  Hence the polynomial in \eqref{eq:q} has nonreal zeros and
is not hyperbolic.  Thus $U^{-1}=V$ is not a
hyperbolicity-preserver.
\end{proof}

\section{Deep upgrade, novelty, and scope}

The first attempt used the transparent seed
$U_0=\cosh D=(e^D+e^{-D})/2$.  Its inverse already fails on $x^6$; writing
$y=x^2$ factors the corresponding cubic as
\[
 y^3-15y^2+75y-61=(y-1)(y^2-14y+61),
\]
whose quadratic factor has negative discriminant.  The deep upgrade in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} mixes in a Gaussian.  This forces
$\int q^2\,d\mu>0$ for every nonzero $q$, so the counterexample lies in the
strict interior of the moment cone rather than on a finite-support boundary.
The symbolic coefficient and discriminant calculations are independently
reproduced in \texttt{code/verify\_counterexample.py}.

A bounded primary-source search used the exact wording of Problem~2,
``positivity-preserver'', ``hyperbolicity-preserver'', inverse, moment
measure, $\cosh(D)$, and $\operatorname{sech}(D)$.  It found the source and
later moment/generator work, including arXiv:2308.10455~\cite{Dio}, but no
primary paper stating this counterexample.  Novelty confidence is moderate;
correctness confidence is high.

This disproves Problem~2 only.  It does not classify all positivity
preservers as requested in the much broader Problem~1.  The argument uses
the source's algebraic convention for infinite-order operators: formal power
series in $D$ act unambiguously on polynomials because only finitely many
derivatives survive.
