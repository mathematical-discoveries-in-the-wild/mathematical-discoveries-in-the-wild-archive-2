# Coordinate-axis points of the complex unit ball are not MDM points

Status: `candidate counterexample — likely valid; human review requested`

Source: Krystian Kazaniecki and Michał Wojciechowski, *On the analytic
version of the Mitiagin-DeLeeuw-Mirkhil non-inequality on bi-disc*,
arXiv:2301.09526, Definition 7 and the paragraph following it on PDF page 3.

## Result

Let

\[
\mathbb B^2=\{(z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb C^2:|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2<1\}.
\]

Every boundary point of the form \((e^{i\theta},0)\) or
\((0,e^{i\theta})\) is neither a local nor a global MDM point of
\(\mathbb B^2\).  At \(p=(1,0)\), the global estimate is particularly
simple: every polynomial satisfying the source's normalization obeys

\[
 |F_{12}(p)|\le 1+\frac{\pi}{2}.
\]

The proof actually uses only \(\|F_2\|_\infty\le1\) and
\(\|F_{11}\|_\infty\le1\).  A cap-local estimate, finite for every cap
radius, rules out local MDM behavior as well.

The closed Euclidean ball is polynomially convex, and these points lie on the
boundary rather than in the interior of its polynomial hull.  They therefore
counterexample the source conjecture that polynomial-hull interiority is the
only obstruction to the MDM property.

## Main mechanism

Put \(h(\zeta)=F_2(\zeta,0)\).  Cauchy's estimate on the transverse fiber
\(|w|<\sqrt{1-r^2}\) gives

\[
 |h''(r)|=|F_{112}(r,0)|\le(1-r^2)^{-1/2}.
\]

The right-hand side is integrable up to \(r=1\), which bounds
\(h'(1)=F_{12}(1,0)\).  The same full transverse fibers remain inside every
sufficiently thin cap near \((1,0)\), producing a radius-dependent local
bound.

## Scope

This packet disproves the conjectured *only obstruction* and gives a complete
classification of the coordinate-axis boundary orbit under phase rotations
and coordinate interchange.  It does **not** decide whether generic boundary
points with both coordinates nonzero are MDM points, nor whether the ball has
some MDM point.  For a generic point, the analogous coordinate fiber shrinks
linearly rather than as a square root, making the direct Cauchy singularity
nonintegrable.
