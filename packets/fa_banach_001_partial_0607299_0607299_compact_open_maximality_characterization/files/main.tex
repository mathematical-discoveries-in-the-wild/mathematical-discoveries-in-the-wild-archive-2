\section{Source conjecture}

The source is N. Spronk~\cite{Spronk}.  The paper is titled
\emph{Operator space structure on Feichtinger's Segal algebra}.
Its arXiv identifier is math/0607299v2.  On source p.~17, after
the Banach projective tensor formula, it conjectures that the canonical
operator space on $\Szero(G)$ is maximal exactly when $G$ admits an open
abelian subgroup.  The source statement and its then-known compact-abelian-
open case are reproduced below.

The source's operator space is intrinsically determined only up to complete
isomorphism~\cite[Introduction]{Spronk}.  Accordingly, ``maximal'' below
means completely isomorphic to the maximal operator space on the underlying
Banach space.

\section{Proof intuition}

A compact open subgroup $K$ turns the local question into a compact Fourier-
algebra question.  Restriction is a complete surjection
$\Szero(G)\to\Szero(K)$, so maximality of $\Szero(G)$ descends to
$\Szero(K)$.  Compactness identifies the latter with $A(K)$.  If $A(K)$ is
maximal, its operator projective tensor square is just its Banach projective
tensor square; but the source's functorial tensor theorem identifies the
operator square with $A(K\times K)$.  Losert's characterization then says
that $K$ is virtually abelian.  Since $K$ is open, the finite-index abelian
subgroup it contains is open in $G$.

For the converse, an open abelian subgroup $A$ meets $K$ in a compact
abelian open subgroup of $G$, exactly the sufficient case already proved in
the source.  The compact-open assumption is therefore used in a symmetric,
structural way rather than as a technical separability condition.

\section{Formal result}

We use two standard operator-space facts~\cite{EffrosRuan}: quotients of
maximal operator spaces are maximal, up to complete isomorphism; and
\[
 \MAX(E)\,\widehat\otimes\,\MAX(F)
 \cong \MAX(E\otimes_\gamma F),
\]
where $\widehat\otimes$ and $\otimes_\gamma$ denote the operator and Banach
projective tensor products.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $G$ be a locally compact group possessing a compact open subgroup.
Then the canonical operator space structure on $\Szero(G)$ is maximal if
and only if $G$ possesses an open abelian subgroup.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $K$ be a compact open subgroup of $G$.

Assume first that the canonical operator space on $\Szero(G)$ is maximal.
Source Theorem~3.3 says that restriction
\[
 R_K:\Szero(G)\longrightarrow\Szero(K),\qquad u\longmapsto u|_K,
\]
is a complete surjection.  In the source's terminology this means that the
induced map from $\Szero(G)/\ker R_K$ onto $\Szero(K)$ is a complete
isomorphism.  Since operator quotients of maximal spaces are maximal,
$\Szero(K)$ is maximal up to complete isomorphism.

Because $K$ is compact, source Corollary~2.5(ii) gives a complete
isomorphism
\[
 \Szero(K)\cong A(K).
\]
Thus $A(K)$ is maximal.  The maximal tensor identity and source
Theorem~3.1 now give, through the natural elementary-tensor map,
\begin{align*}
 A(K)\otimes_\gamma A(K)
 &\cong A(K)\,\widehat\otimes\,A(K)\\
 &\cong \Szero(K)\,\widehat\otimes\,\Szero(K)\\
 &\cong \Szero(K\times K)\\
 &\cong A(K\times K).
\end{align*}
Losert's tensor-product theorem~\cite{Losert} states that the natural map
$A(K)\otimes_\gamma A(K)\to A(K\times K)$ is an isomorphism only if $K$
has an abelian subgroup $B$ of finite index (equivalently, an open abelian
subgroup).  Since $B$ is open in $K$ and $K$ is open in $G$, $B$ is open in
$G$.  This proves necessity.

Conversely, suppose that $G$ has an open abelian subgroup $A$.  Then
$A\cap K$ is a subgroup that is abelian, compact (as a closed subgroup of
$K$), and open in $G$ (as the intersection of two open subgroups).  The
paragraph following the source conjecture establishes that the canonical
operator space on $\Szero(G)$ is maximal whenever $G$ has a compact abelian
open subgroup.  Applying that result to $A\cap K$ proves sufficiency.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:tdlc}
The source conjecture holds for every totally disconnected locally compact
group.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By van Dantzig's theorem~\cite{vanDantzig}, such a group has a compact open
subgroup, so Theorem~\ref{thm:main} applies.
\end{proof}

The same argument also records a useful necessary condition without a
compact-open assumption on $G$ itself.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:hereditary}
If the canonical operator space on $\Szero(G)$ is maximal, then every compact
subgroup of $G$ has an open abelian subgroup.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For any compact subgroup $K\leq G$, repeat the restriction-quotient and
Losert argument in the necessity half of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

\section{Upgrade attempts and remaining obstruction}

Eight focused routes were checked before stopping at the partial theorem.
First, the compact-open restriction route yielded Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
Second, quotienting by compact normal subgroups only reproduces the same
class.  Third, unrestricted closed-subgroup descent gives
Corollary~\ref{cor:hereditary}, but does not characterize groups such as the
affine or Heisenberg group, whose compact subgroups are abelian.  Fourth,
the source averaging map passes maximality to quotients, but available
quotients of these connected models remain noncompact.

Fifth, a Gleason--Yamabe reduction moves the problem toward connected Lie
groups but still leaves solvable nonabelian base cases.  Sixth, maximality
does give the Banach projective formula
$\Szero(G)\otimes_\gamma\Szero(G)\cong\Szero(G\times G)$; outside the compact
case there is no justified passage from this identity to Losert's identity
for $A(G)$.  Seventh, a compactly supported Fourier-algebra ideal would
transfer the obstruction if it were completely complemented in $\Szero(G)$;
an exact idempotent cutoff is available for compact open cosets, bringing the
argument back to Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, but not for a general connected
group.  Eighth, classical atomic decompositions in the abelian case do not
automatically identify the source's quotient matrix norms with maximal
matrix norms.

Thus the unresolved part is not a missing group-theoretic reduction: it is
the operator-space behavior of non-idempotent local ideals in groups with
noncompact identity component.  No claim is made for that range.

\section{Novelty audit and verification recommendation}

A bounded search on 13 August 2026 covered the exact title and arXiv id,
``Feichtinger Segal algebra maximal operator space'', ``open abelian
subgroup'', ``compact open subgroup'', and later work on $p$-Feichtinger
algebras and algebraic characterizations.  It located restatements of the
source and related functorial results, but no later proof of this compact-open
subcase or of the full conjecture.  Novelty confidence is provisional because
the proof is a short synthesis of source theorems and Losert's classical
characterization.

Human review should focus on two compatibility points: that ``complete
surjection'' in source Theorem~3.3 is the complete-isomorphic quotient notion
needed for maximality descent, and that the natural maps in the tensor chain
are the elementary-tensor map appearing in Losert's theorem.  The source's
definitions and Theorem~3.1 make both identifications explicit.
