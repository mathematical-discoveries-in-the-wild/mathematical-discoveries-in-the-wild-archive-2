\section*{Source question}

The source is R.~Tanaka, \emph{On Birkhoff--James orthogonality preservers
between real non-isometric Banach spaces}, arXiv:2108.00655
\cite{Tanaka}.  In the Remarks on PDF page 11, the author explains that the
complex analogue of the construction would require non-Hilbert Radon planes
and states that, to the best of his knowledge, it was not known whether a
complex Radon plane exists.  Since every two-dimensional complex Hilbert
space has symmetric Birkhoff--James orthogonality, the surrounding sentence
makes clear that the intended issue is a \emph{non-Hilbert} complex Radon
plane.

Recall that a two-dimensional normed space is a Radon plane when its
Birkhoff--James orthogonality is symmetric.

\section*{Supporting construction}

The supporting paper is A.~Guterman, B.~Kuzma, S.~Singla, and S.~Zhilina,
\emph{Birkhoff--James classification of norm's properties},
arXiv:2402.13416 \cite{GKSSZ}.  Example 3.17 on PDF pages 23--24 gives an
explicit positive answer.

Start with the absolute nonsmooth real Radon plane
$(\mathbb R^2,\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal R})$ constructed in Example 3.14 and put
\[
 \mathcal X=\mathbb C^2,
 \qquad
 \|(a,b)\|_{\mathcal X}
 =\|(|a|,|b|)\|_{\mathcal R}.
\]
For $(c,d)\in\mathbb C^2$, identify the functional
$\widehat{(-d,c)}$ by
\[
 \widehat{(-d,c)}(a,b)=-da+cb.
\]
The computation in Example 3.17 proves
\[
 \|\widehat{(-d,c)}\|_{\mathcal X^*}=\|(c,d)\|_{\mathcal X}.
\]
Thus
\[
 \Phi(c,d)=\widehat{(-d,c)}
\]
is an isometry from $\mathcal X$ to $\mathcal X^*$ and satisfies
$\Phi(x)(x)=0$ for every $x\in\mathcal X$.  By Lemma 3.15 of the supporting
paper, this property is equivalent in dimension two to being a Radon plane.
Hence $\mathcal X$ is a complex Radon plane.

The same example shows that $(1,0)$ is a nonsmooth point of $\mathcal X$.
Therefore $\mathcal X$ is not a Hilbert space, giving exactly the non-Hilbert
example required by the source context.  The supporting authors additionally
note that earlier complex Radon-plane examples already appeared in Oman's
work; their Example 3.17 is nonetheless a self-contained decisive source for
the present identification.

\section*{Provenance and scope}

The supporting paper cites Tanaka's source article for a different
orthogonality-preserver construction, but does not explicitly label Example
3.17 as an answer to Tanaka's Remarks.  The relation is therefore an
agent-identified direct implication and belongs in
\texttt{literature\_implied\_answers}.

This packet settles only existence of a non-Hilbert complex Radon plane.  It
does not settle the source's automatic-continuity question for preservers
between smooth complex Banach spaces, the nonreflexive and finite-dimensional
gaps in that classification, or the unrestricted implication
$X\sim_{BJ}Y\Rightarrow X\cong Y$ (and its complex conjugate variant).
