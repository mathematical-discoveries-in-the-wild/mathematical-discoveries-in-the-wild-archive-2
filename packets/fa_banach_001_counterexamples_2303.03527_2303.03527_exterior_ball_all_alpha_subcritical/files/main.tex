\section{Source question}

The source is U.~Das, Y.~Pinchover, and B.~Devyver,
\emph{On existence of minimizers for weighted $L^p$-Hardy inequalities on
$C^{1,\gamma}$-domains with compact boundary}, arXiv:2303.03527, published in
\emph{Journal of Spectral Theory} 15 (2025), 1089--1138\cite{source}.
Open Problem (3), on printed page 32, asks:
\begin{quote}
Let $\Omega$ be a $C^{1,\gamma}$-exterior domain and
$\alpha+p\in\{1,N\}$. Is $-\Delta_{\alpha,p}$ critical in $\Omega$?

\section{Definitions and proof intuition}

For $N\ge2$, $1<p<\infty$, and a domain $\Omega$, let
$\delta_\Omega(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)$ and
\[
 -\Delta_{\alpha,p}u
 :=-\operatorname{div}\!\left(
 \delta_\Omega^{-\alpha}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right).
\]
The source calls the associated nonnegative functional \emph{critical} when
no nonzero compactly supported nonnegative potential can be subtracted while
preserving nonnegativity; otherwise it is \emph{subcritical}.  It recalls the
equivalent criterion that a nonnegative operator is critical exactly when its
equation has, up to multiplication by a positive constant, a unique positive
supersolution (then a solution)\cite[Section 2]{source}.

\paragraph{Proof intuition.}
An exterior ball is radial, and a radial weighted $p$-harmonic function is
governed by conservation of flux.  Prescribing unit flux determines its
derivative explicitly.  One can integrate that derivative from whichever end
is integrable: from the boundary when $\alpha>1-p$, and from infinity when
$\alpha\le1-p$.  This always yields a positive nonconstant solution.  Since
the constant function is another positive solution, the uniqueness required
for criticality fails.  The endpoint cases in the question are merely the two
borderline logarithmic instances of this all-weight construction.

\section{The all-weight counterexample}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $N\ge2$, $1<p<\infty$, $R>0$, and
\[
 \Om:=\R^N\setminus\overline{B_R(0)},
 \qquad \delta_{\Om}(x)=|x|-R.
\]
For every $\alpha\in\R$, the operator $-\Delta_{\alpha,p}$ is subcritical in
$\Om$.  In particular, for every such $N,p,R$ it is subcritical at both
$\alpha+p=1$ and $\alpha+p=N$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $r=|x|$ and define, for $r>R$,
\[
 b_\alpha(r):=(r-R)^{\alpha/(p-1)}
                   r^{-(N-1)/(p-1)}.
\]
We construct a finite positive radial function by choosing the integrable end:
\[
 U_\alpha(r):=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\int_R^r b_\alpha(s)\,ds,
       &\alpha>1-p,\\[2.2ex]
 \displaystyle\int_r^\infty b_\alpha(s)\,ds,
       &\alpha\le 1-p.
 \end{cases}\tag{1}
\]
In the first branch, the integrand is asymptotic to a positive constant times
$(s-R)^{\alpha/(p-1)}$ near $R$, and the exponent is greater than $-1$;
therefore the integral is finite.  In the second branch,
\[
 b_\alpha(s)\asymp
 s^{(\alpha-N+1)/(p-1)}\qquad(s\to\infty).
\]
Here $\alpha\le1-p<N-p$, so the exponent is strictly less than $-1$ and the
tail integral is finite.  Thus $U_\alpha$ is finite and strictly positive on
$(R,\infty)$.  It is smooth locally in that interval and
\[
 U_\alpha'(r)=\sigma b_\alpha(r),
 \qquad
 \sigma:=
 \begin{cases}1,&\alpha>1-p,\\-1,&\alpha\le1-p.\end{cases}
\]

For a radial function, the weighted radial flux is
\[
 (r-R)^{-\alpha}r^{N-1}
 |U_\alpha'(r)|^{p-2}U_\alpha'(r).
\]
Since $b_\alpha(r)^{p-1}=(r-R)^\alpha r^{-(N-1)}$,
this flux is the constant $\sigma$.  Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{div}\!\left(
 (r-R)^{-\alpha}|\nabla U_\alpha|^{p-2}
 \nabla U_\alpha\right)
 =\frac1{r^{N-1}}\frac{d}{dr}(\sigma)=0
 \quad\text{in }\Om.\tag{2}
\]
The function $U_\alpha$ belongs to $W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Om)$, is positive,
and is nonconstant because its radial derivative never vanishes.

The constant function $1$ is also a positive solution.  In particular, the
Agmon--Allegretto--Piepenbrink criterion quoted in the source makes the
functional nonnegative.  If $-\Delta_{\alpha,p}$ were critical, the source's
criticality criterion would force all positive solutions (indeed all positive
supersolutions) to be proportional.  The functions $1$ and $U_\alpha$ are not
proportional, a contradiction.  Hence the operator is subcritical.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The two requested endpoints}

At $\alpha+p=N$, formula (1) uses the boundary end and gives
\[
 U_N(r)=\int_R^r
 (s-R)^{(N-p)/(p-1)}s^{-(N-1)/(p-1)}\,ds;
\]
its integrand is asymptotic to $s^{-1}$ at infinity, so $U_N$ has logarithmic
growth.  At $\alpha+p=1$, formula (1) uses the infinite end and gives
\[
 U_1(r)=\int_r^\infty
 (s-R)^{-1}s^{-(N-1)/(p-1)}\,ds;
\]
it has logarithmic blow-up only as $r\downarrow R$.  Both are nevertheless
positive local weak solutions in the open exterior domain, exactly as required
by the source definitions.

\section{Verification, scope, and novelty}

The accompanying script checks the constant-flux identity and both convergence
branches at 675 representative parameter/radius tuples, including $p<2$ and
both endpoint families.  It passes.  This is a regression check, not part of
the proof.

The result is a full negative answer if the source question asks whether
criticality holds in general for all $C^{1,\gamma}$ exterior domains: every
exterior ball is a counterexample.  It does not classify which nonradial
exterior domains might be critical if the question was intended as a
domain-by-domain classification.

A bounded search of the four run indexes and exact/near-exact web and arXiv
phrases found no later paper explicitly answering Open Problem (3), nor the
all-$\alpha$ exterior-ball theorem.  However, the downloaded arXiv source is
unusual: after the current document's first \verb|\end{document}|, a discarded
older document records the special case $(N,p,\alpha)=(2,2,0)$ with solutions
$1$ and $\log r$, and says a similar argument should work generally.  That
passage is absent from the final PDF and gives neither the two full endpoint
families nor the all-weight proof above, but it establishes prior author
awareness of the planar seed.  Mathematical confidence is therefore high and
novelty confidence moderate.  Human review should chiefly assess this
provenance caveat and the intended quantifier in the source question.
